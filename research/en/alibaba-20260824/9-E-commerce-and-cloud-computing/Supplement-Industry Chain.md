## Upstream: who captured the profit

The GPU has always been the most profitable position in this chain. Nvidia's data centre business has long run gross margins near 70%, because what it sells is not hardware but the "CUDA ecosystem + supply scarcity." When Alibaba builds a data centre, its single largest expense is buying cards—which is exactly why it must develop its own.

Pingtouge's position is therefore critical: the Zhenwu M890 already serves more than 650 external customers across over 20 industries through Alibaba Cloud. What it delivers is not performance leadership but "availability" and "20%–30% cheaper." As long as export controls do not ease, these two factors are hard barriers; the moment controls loosen, the advantage shrinks immediately.

Optical modules, servers and IDC all earn hard-won money: server gross margins are 8%–12%, IDC 25%–35%. As the buyer, Alibaba holds pricing power in these segments—one reason it can hold capex to RMB 67.68 billion per quarter while sustaining an 11.6% cloud profit margin.

## Midstream: Alibaba holds the heaviest position in the chain

Cloud sits at the hub of this chain: upstream it buys chips, power and data centres; downstream it sells compute and models. It combines two traits — "heavy assets" and "strong scale effects." The former makes it painful when demand falls; the latter makes it highly profitable when demand rises. This quarter is the upcycle: revenue +45%, EBITA +133%.

Alibaba's uniqueness lies in doing all three midstream layers: IaaS (Alibaba Cloud), chips (T-Head) and models (Qwen). This brings a benefit no one else has: it defines its own cost structure. Running its own models on its own chips and selling its own cloud, each layer retains some margin — 11.6% combined.

The cost is equally unique: no one shares its heavy assets. Tencent can buy cards and use external chips; ByteDance's Volcano Engine started late with less capex pressure; Huawei has carrier operations to dilute costs. Alibaba is the only company that must bear the full RMB 380 billion investment itself — which is the structural reason it had to place HKD 80 billion in shares.

## Downstream, value distribution differs completely between the two battlegrounds

E-commerce platform commission is the second most profitable position in this chain: customer management revenue of RMB 82.55 billion, gross margin above 70%, and almost no capital consumed. But its bargaining power is eroding — only +1% on a like-for-like basis and -7% as reported, which shows merchants are unwilling to pay more. Pinduoduo's low prices and Douyin's content-driven marketplace have turned "traffic must be bought" into "prices can be compared."

Instant retail sits at the other extreme: gross margin of 10%–15%, rigid fulfillment costs, low average order value. Taobao Shangou used subsidies to capture roughly a 40% share of order volume (Meituan 52%, JD 7%), a position where "no subsidy means losing share, subsidy means no profit." It thins the group's gross margin — a proactive choice and a forced response alike.

So the downstream's significance to Alibaba is not profit but "scenarios": e-commerce scenarios feed the cloud's customers (merchants moving to the cloud, AI shopping assistants), feed the models' data (Qwen's actual calls on Taobao), and feed payments and logistics. Trading profit for scenarios is the real logic of this game.

## Cycle position: the three curves are not at the same point

E-commerce at the tail of maturity. China's online retail penetration has peaked, industry growth has fallen to single digits and players are fighting for share. Alibaba's zero growth in customer management is the direct readout of this stage. Instant retail in the middle of a subsidy war. The battle that began in summer 2025 enters a "share stabilizes, losses narrow" phase in 2026, with a 5:4:1 structure among the three players largely taking shape. AI compute on the upswing of the capex cycle. Everyone is expanding capacity: Alibaba at RMB 67.68 billion in a single quarter, Tencent at RMB 52.8 billion in Q2 (+176%), and the four major North American cloud players raising guidance in tandem.

Together these three curves make up today's Alibaba: a non-growing base, a loss-making new battleground, and a high-growth new business that devours cash. At HKD 112.5, the market essentially prices the "third curve" while using the first two to compress the multiple.

The cycle call hinges on 2027: if capacity from industry-wide expansion is released in a concentrated wave, compute prices will loosen and cloud's 11.6% margin will peak. That is the real test of this story—not whether demand is sufficient, but whether supply will overshoot.
