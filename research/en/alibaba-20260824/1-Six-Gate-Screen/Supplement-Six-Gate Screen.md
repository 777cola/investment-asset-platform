## Gate 1: Circle of competence (8/10, pass)

Alibaba's three businesses can each be summed up in one sentence: transaction platform commissions (customer management revenue RMB 82.55 billion), selling compute (AI cloud and compute RMB 48.44 billion), and selling fulfillment (instant retail RMB 53.30 billion). **The hard part isn't understanding the business — it's understanding this new reporting structure.** Starting this quarter, Alibaba has split the company into four segments: e-commerce group, AI cloud and compute services, AI lab and applications, and all others. For the first time, AI's numbers are presented separately on a self-funding basis — this is the single most important development this quarter, more important than the 45% growth rate.

The 2-point deduction is for predictability. E-commerce revenue is forecastable: customer management ties to GMV and monetization rate, instant retail ties to order volume and subsidies, and international e-commerce ties to AliExpress and Trendyol's geographic expansion. Cloud revenue is also forecastable: AI-related products at RMB 12.376 billion, 12 consecutive quarters of triple-digit growth, and decent order visibility. What isn't forecastable is profit — it depends on how much Wu Yongming decides to burn this quarter. **A segment with an annualized loss of RMB 55.4 billion that contributes only RMB 3.34 billion in revenue can't be valued with DCF — only a subjective multiple.**

## Gate 2: Good business (7/10, pass)

The e-commerce group's adjusted EBITA margin was 19.3% (397.5 ÷ 2,058.6), the standard return for a rent-collecting business; it relies on merchant prepayments, payment terms and zero inventory, tying up very little capital. Taken on its own, this is a good business, with little room for debate.

Cloud is asset-heavy, but it has cleared the ugliest hurdle: segment EBITA of RMB 5.628 billion, up 133% YoY, with margin rising from 7.2% to 11.6%, **and it did so while growth accelerated from 26% to 45%**. The hardest thing for an asset-heavy business is improving growth and profitability at the same time; it achieved that this quarter, showing that depreciation on the previous round of GPU purchases has not yet fully weighed on results and scale effects have arrived first.

What drags down the score is instant retail and AI applications. Instant retail revenue rose 45% but was bought with subsidies, thinning the group's gross margin; the AI lab and applications lost RMB 13.86 billion, a loss ratio of -415%. These two are not bad businesses, but businesses where "it is not yet clear they are good businesses." The 7 points go to e-commerce and cloud; the 3 deducted go to the other two.

## Gate 3: Moat (8/10, pass)

Three moats, ranked by durability: **cloud and compute** is the hardest — Alibaba Cloud holds 38.1% of China's AI cloud market (Omdia) and is the only vendor to deploy in-house chips at scale in a public cloud; T-Head's Zhenwu M890 already serves more than 650 external customers across over 20 industries through Alibaba Cloud. **Shopping mindshare** comes second — 88VIP has about 64 million members, growing double digits year on year, the most valuable membership list in Chinese e-commerce. **In-house chips** rank third — GPUs, CPUs, storage and networking are all covered; the role is securing supply and cutting costs, not beating Nvidia.

An 8, not a 9, because the nature of the moat has changed. The old moat was shopping mindshare plus merchant tools — soft, and erodible by subsidies. The instant retail battle has already proved it: Taobao Shangou bought its way to roughly a 40% share of orders on subsidies, against Meituan's 52% and JD's 7%. The new addition is a hard-asset moat of "compute + chips + models", more durable but requiring continuous cash feeding. **The width of a moat is ultimately tested by whether it can raise prices, and Taobao and Tmall cannot right now** — customer management revenue rose just 1% on a like-for-like basis and fell 7% as reported.

## Gate 4: Management (6/10, questionable)

Capability scores 8. After Eddie Wu took over in 2023, he cut non-core businesses and concentrated resources on cloud and AI, delivering 45% cloud growth and an 11.6% segment margin in two and a half years — execution is not the issue. Joseph Tsai's capital-markets moves have been crisp too: of the RMB 380 billion AI infrastructure plan announced in February 2025, roughly RMB 190 billion had been invested by June 2026 — past the halfway mark.

Judgment and communication lose 4 points. **Results were released after the close on August 20, and a HKD 80 billion placement was launched on the evening of August 23** — two days, with only one trading session in between. The placement was priced at HKD 112.70, an 8.4% discount — not harsh on existing shareholders, and with nearly 3x oversubscription and sovereign and long-only funds taking over 40%, institutions clearly accept the price. But the sequence — "the earnings call had just finished explaining that AI spending is disciplined, and two days later the company put its hand out for money" — undercuts the word "disciplined."

On August 24, Joseph Tsai bought 720,000 shares (about HKD 80 million) and Eddie Wu bought 350,000 shares (about HKD 40 million), HKD 120 million combined. **That is 0.15% of the placement size — a gesture, not a hedge.** Worth noting, but not a bottom signal.

## Gate 5: Margin of safety (5/10, barely passes)

Sum-of-the-parts valuation: the e-commerce group's annualized adjusted EBITA of about RMB 159 billion (397.5 × 4) at 9x = RMB 1.43 trillion; cloud and computing annualized revenue of RMB 193.7 billion (484.4 × 4) at 6x = RMB 1.16 trillion; AI lab and applications at 3x the annualized loss of RMB 55.4 billion, carried as a negative = -RMB 0.17 trillion. **Total RMB 2.42 trillion, or HKD 129 per share on 20.46 billion post-placement shares at an exchange rate of 0.92.**

The current price of HKD 112.5 implies a 13% discount. Is that discount justified? A 13% discount means the market has already priced in part of the "cloud growth deceleration" story for you, but has not priced in "AI applications keep losing money for another three years." Free cash flow of -RMB 44.67 billion and a net cash position of only about RMB 35.1 billion (2026/3/31) mean the margin of safety can only be built on segment multiples, not on cash flow — **this is the fundamental difference between it and Tencent and Pinduoduo**.

## Level 6: Discipline (4/10, fail)

The discipline test asks only one question: can the buy thesis be written as "if A happens, I am wrong." Right now it cannot. **AI labs and applications lost RMB 13.86 billion in a single quarter, an annualized RMB 55.4 billion, a loss ratio of -415%, and the company says only that "investment increased and inference costs rose" — no path to narrowing losses, no timetable, no target loss ratio.** Assigning any positive valuation to the application layer under these information conditions is imagination, and using "imagination" as a margin of safety means zero discipline.

The second thing without evidence is cloud's speed. The 45% growth is real, but one quarter of data cannot prove "still above 40% two years from now." This can be resolved by tracking — watch growth and margins over the next two quarters, not the research reports.

A score of 4 is not for "should not buy," it is for "cannot buy now." The difference: should not buy means no conditions; cannot buy now means conditions exist and have not yet been met.

## Overall: 38/60, Watch

Viewed together, the six gates make Alibaba a combination of "8 for assets, 5 for price, 4 for visibility." **The two hardest assets (e-commerce cash flow, cloud and chips) are both improving; the price has fallen from a high of HKD 185.1 to HKD 112.5, a 39% drawdown, so valuation is no longer expensive; visibility is the weak link — for a new business that absorbs 100% of capex, the company itself has not laid out a path to profitability.**

So the answer lands in the middle: watch. Watching is not delay; it means writing the conditions down clearly and then waiting. If either of two conditions is met, this gate moves from 4 to 7: the price falls below HKD 95 (a 26% discount to neutral valuation), or the cloud segment margin stays above 12% for two consecutive quarters while AI application losses narrow quarter on quarter.
