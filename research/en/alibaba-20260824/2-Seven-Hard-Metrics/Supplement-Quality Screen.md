## The three that passed: not all of equal quality

Gross margin of 39.8% is the most robust of the three. FY2024 37.7%, FY2025 40.0%, FY2026 39.8%—a 2.3pp range over three years, showing that e-commerce monetisation has not collapsed and that cloud scale effects are offsetting gross margin dilution from instant retail. Clearing this metric confirms Alibaba retains platform pricing power, even as that power erodes at the margin.

Operating cash flow / net profit of 218% is the most easily overstated of the three. Quarterly operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion is genuinely healthy and up 11% YoY, but AI investment compressed net profit to RMB 10.54 billion, flattering the ratio through a small denominator. Reframed: operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion against capex of RMB 67.68 billion gives coverage of just 34%—that is the true cash position.

Net margin of 10.3% barely clears the line. FY2026 full-year figures leaned partly on investment gains and interest income, and the single quarter has already fallen to 3.9%. It is marked as cleared because it has held above 8% for several consecutive years, but it is not a metric to rely on under stress.

## Four points for But: two are attributes, two are flaws

Attribute 1: Revenue growth. FY2024 +8.34%, FY2025 +5.86%, FY2026 +2.74% — three years of steady deceleration, inevitable for a company with RMB 1,023.7 billion in revenue once China's e-commerce penetration peaked. This quarter's +9% came from instant retail +45% (RMB 53.3 billion) and cloud +45%, while core China e-commerce fell -8%. Growth has a new engine; that does not mean growth is back.

Attribute 2: Operating margin 6.2%. Retailization (direct sales, instant retail fulfillment) and asset-heavy expansion (cloud capex) both compress this number, across the industry. JD.com is at 1.3%, Meituan lower; Alibaba's 6.2% is not bad among peers. Failing this test only means it is no longer an "asset-light, high-margin" company — nothing more.

Ailment 1: Free cash flow -RMB 44.67 billion. Quarterly capex of RMB 67.68 billion, +75% YoY, is a deliberate choice and understandable; but negative free cash flow and net cash falling from RMB 179.3 billion a year ago to RMB 35.1 billion (as of 2026/3/31) force reliance on external financing — hence the HKD 80 billion placement. This is not a cyclical problem; it is a capital allocation problem.

Ailment 2: Share count. For years Alibaba was a positive case of buybacks and cancellation; August 2026 reverses that: 710 million shares, 3.47% dilution, effectively rolling back three years of buyback progress. Selling stock at HKD 112.70, 39% below October 2025's HKD 185.1 — issuing equity at a low is the one thing adverse-selection screening cares about most.

## But is it fatal: a tiered assessment

Four misses, but they differ in severity by three levels. Revenue growth is not fatal: for a company with RMB 1,023.7 billion in revenue and RMB 300 billion in cash, 2.7% growth on a Non-GAAP P/E just above 20x should never have commanded a growth multiple. Operating margin is not fatal: it reflects a business-model choice rather than eroding competitiveness, and Cloud's 11.6% segment margin shows that asset-heavy models can still generate returns.

Negative free cash flow is close to fatal, but not yet: cash and current investments of RMB 474.505 billion, plus HKD 79.7 billion in net placement proceeds, comfortably fund two years of capex. The real risk is "the year depreciation comes due"—RMB 67.68 billion of investment in a single quarter, amortised over 3–5 years, adds RMB 140–270 billion of annualised depreciation, and margins will compress when that flows through. Share dilution is the least fatal, but hits sentiment hardest: 3.47% dilution erodes intrinsic value by roughly 3%, but it signals that "management views its own stock at HKD 112 as too expensive to buy back."
