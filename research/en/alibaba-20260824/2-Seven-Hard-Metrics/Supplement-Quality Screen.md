## The three that passed: not all of equal quality

**Gross margin of 39.8% is the most solid of the three.** FY2024 37.7%, FY2025 40.0%, FY2026 39.8% — a range of 2.3 percentage points over three years, showing that e-commerce monetization has not collapsed and that cloud scale effects are offsetting gross margin dilution from instant retail. Clearing this metric confirms that "Alibaba is still a platform with pricing power," even as that pricing power thins.

**Operating cash flow / net profit of 218% is the most easily overstated of the three.** Quarterly operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion is genuinely healthy and up 11% year on year, but net profit was compressed to RMB 10.54 billion by AI investment, so the small denominator flatters the ratio. Reframed: operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion against capex of RMB 67.68 billion gives coverage of just 34% — that is the true cash position.

**Net margin of 10.3% is "just barely over the line."** FY2026 full-year figures leaned partly on investment gains and interest income, and the single quarter has already fallen to 3.9%. It is marked as cleared because it has held above 8% for several consecutive years, but it is not a metric to rely on in a stress test.

## Four points for But: two are attributes, two are flaws

**Attribute 1: Revenue growth.** FY2024 +8.34%, FY2025 +5.86%, FY2026 +2.74% — three years of steady deceleration, inevitable for a company with RMB 1,023.7 billion in revenue after China's e-commerce penetration peaked. This quarter's +9% came from instant retail +45% (RMB 53.3 billion) and cloud +45%, while the core China e-commerce business fell -8%. **Growth has a new engine; that does not mean growth is back.**

**Attribute 2: Operating margin 6.2%.** Retailization (direct sales, instant retail fulfillment) and asset-heavy expansion (cloud capex) both compress this number, across the industry. JD.com is at 1.3%, Meituan lower; Alibaba's 6.2% is not bad among peers. Failing this test only means it is no longer an "asset-light, high-margin" company — nothing more.

**Ailment 1: Free cash flow -RMB 44.67 billion.** Quarterly capex of RMB 67.68 billion, +75% YoY, is a deliberate choice and understandable; but negative free cash flow and net cash falling from RMB 179.3 billion a year ago to RMB 35.1 billion (as of 2026/3/31) force reliance on external financing — hence the HKD 80 billion placement. This is not a cyclical problem; it is a capital allocation problem.

**Ailment 2: Share count.** For years Alibaba was a positive case of buybacks and cancellation; August 2026 reverses that: 710 million shares, 3.47% dilution, effectively rolling back three years of buyback progress. Selling stock at HKD 112.70, 39% below October 2025's HKD 185.1 — **issuing equity at a low is the one thing adverse-selection screening cares about most.**

## But is it fatal: a tiered assessment

Four misses, but the severity differs by three levels. **Revenue growth is not fatal**: for a company with RMB 1,023.7 billion in revenue and RMB 300 billion-scale cash, 2.7% growth paired with a Non-GAAP PE just above 20x should never have been priced as a growth stock. **Operating margin is not fatal**: this is a business-model choice, not a loss of competitiveness; cloud's 11.6% segment margin shows that asset-heavy can still make money.

**Negative free cash flow is close to fatal, but not now**: cash and current investments on the books of RMB 474.505 billion, plus HKD 79.7 billion in net placement proceeds, can fund two years of capex without issue. The real risk is "the year depreciation starts to collect the bill" — RMB 67.68 billion of investment in a single quarter, amortized over 3–5 years, adds RMB 140–270 billion of annualized depreciation, and margins will be squeezed when that hits. **Share dilution is the least fatal, but hits sentiment hardest**: 3.47% dilution affects intrinsic value by about 3%, but it proves that "the company believes its own stock at HKD 112 is not cheap enough to be worth buying back."
