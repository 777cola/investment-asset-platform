## Why 0: expected-value arithmetic

Neutral HKD 129, bear case HKD 88, current price HKD 112.5: upside HKD 16.5 (+15%), downside HKD 24.5 (-22%), payoff ratio 1:1.5. **For positive expected value, the win rate must exceed 60%.** The neutral scenario requires two conditions to hold simultaneously (cloud sustaining 40%+ and AI losses no longer widening), which on independent probabilities comes to only about 33%.

A 33% win rate against a 1:1.5 payoff ratio gives expected value = 0.33 × 15% − 0.67 × 22% = **-9.8%**. That is negative expected value; no matter how bullish you are on the company, you should not take the bet. **This is the mathematical meaning of the "Watch" rating — not a stance, but arithmetic.**

At HKD 95, upside is HKD 34 (+36%) and downside HKD 7 (-7%), inverting the payoff ratio to 5:1; even with a 33% win rate, expected value is +7.2%. **Same company, same set of assumptions, a 15% difference in price turns negative expected value into positive — that is the definition of margin of safety.**

## Position-building discipline: three tranches, no chasing

**Tranche one (3%) at HKD 95.** This price implies a 26% discount to the neutral value, close to the bear-case HKD 88; the lower bound of HKD 71 (extreme scenario) implies maximum drawdown of 25%. The purpose of this position is to "build a real feel for tracking the name" — only with a position will you read the financials seriously. **Tranche two (6%) at HKD 88.** This is the bear-case price, equivalent to the market having already priced in "cloud growth decelerating + e-commerce continuing to retreat." **Tranche three (8%) after evidence emerges.** Add only when two conditions are met — cloud margin ≥12% for two consecutive quarters and AI losses narrowing quarter on quarter — regardless of price.

**What "no chasing" means in practice:** if the stock rebounds from 95 to 110, do not add; if it rebounds above 120, consider halving tranche one to lock in gains, but keep the base position. **All adds carry dual thresholds of "condition + price"; no "chart-based adding."**

Single-name cap of 10%, because this name has only one key variable (cloud) and concentration risk is already high; add placement and dilution uncertainty, and anything above 10% would push portfolio volatility to an uncomfortable level.

## Trimming and stop-losses: use conditions, not prices

**Set stop-losses on conditions, not prices.** If any of the three falsification triggers appears, exit the position regardless of cost: **1) cloud quarterly growth -RMB 50 billion.** All three point to the same conclusion: returns on the asset-heavy shift are below expectations, and the valuation center must move down one notch from "infrastructure + growth."

Why no price-based stop-loss? Because half of price volatility comes from a valuation-framework shift (185 to 90 is a shift, not fundamentals), **and a price stop-loss would be repeatedly shaken out by that shift, then miss the recovery.** A condition-based stop-loss means "I admit I was wrong on fundamentals," which is the only meaningful stop-loss.

**There are two cases for trimming:** 1) the stock rises above HKD 150 (close to 88% of the optimistic 171), cut by half; 2) a second placement occurs, regardless of price, cut one-third first—a second placement means the "capex discipline" assumption has collapsed, and the valuation should shift down permanently.

## Correlation: not a diversification tool

Alibaba is highly correlated with Chinese internet stocks as a whole. In 2026, the Hang Seng Tech Index fell more than 25%, Tencent and SMIC fell more than 30%, and Alibaba, Meituan and Xiaomi fell more than 20% — **this shows the common factors driving them (China asset risk premium, AI narrative shifts, USD liquidity) are stronger than individual stock fundamentals.**

Correlation assessment with specific holdings: **correlation with Tencent, Meituan, JD.com and Pinduoduo is above 0.7** (all Chinese internet, all affected by the AI narrative); **correlation with Nvidia, TSMC and SK hynix is moderate (0.4–0.5)** — all affected by the compute cycle, but one is a buyer and the others are sellers; **correlation with manufacturing names such as CATL and BYD is low (&lt;0.3)**.

The portfolio-level implication is clear: **if you already hold Tencent or Meituan, Alibaba's position cap should be cut further to 6%**, because they are essentially the same bet on "Chinese internet + AI." Genuine diversification comes from two asset classes: "compute sellers" (Nvidia, optical modules, memory) and "domestic consumption." **If you treat Alibaba as a "call option on Chinese AI," do not buy a second option of the same kind in the same account.**
