## Why 0: expected-value arithmetic

Neutral HKD 129, bear case HKD 88, current price HKD 112.5: upside HKD 16.5 (+15%), downside HKD 24.5 (-22%), a payoff ratio of 1:1.5. Positive expected value therefore requires a win rate above 60%. The neutral scenario needs two conditions to hold at once — cloud sustaining 40%+ growth and AI losses no longer widening — which, treating them as independent, implies a probability of only about 33%.

A 33% win rate against a 1:1.5 payoff ratio gives expected value = 0.33 × 15% − 0.67 × 22% = -9.8%. Negative expected value: however bullish you are on the company, the bet should not be taken. That is the arithmetic behind the "Watch" rating — not a stance, but maths.

At HKD 95, upside is HKD 34 (+36%) and downside HKD 7 (-7%), inverting the payoff ratio to 5:1; even on a 33% win rate, expected value is +7.2%. Same company, same assumptions, a 15% lower price turns negative expected value positive — that is the definition of margin of safety.

## Position-building discipline: three tranches, no chasing

Tranche one (3%) at HKD 95. That implies a 26% discount to neutral value, close to the bear case of HKD 88; the HKD 71 lower bound (extreme scenario) implies maximum drawdown of 25%. The purpose of this position is to "build a real feel for tracking the name" — only with a position will you read the financials seriously. Tranche two (6%) at HKD 88. This is the bear-case price, equivalent to the market having already priced in "cloud growth decelerating + e-commerce continuing to retreat." Tranche three (8%) after evidence emerges. Add only when two conditions are met — cloud margin ≥12% for two consecutive quarters and AI losses narrowing quarter on quarter — regardless of price.

What "no chasing" means in practice: if the stock rebounds from 95 to 110, do not add; above 120, consider halving tranche one to lock in gains, but keep the base position. All adds carry dual thresholds of "condition + price"; no "chart-based adding."

Single-name cap of 10%: the name has only one key variable (cloud) and concentration risk is already high; add placement and dilution uncertainty, and anything above 10% would push portfolio volatility to an uncomfortable level.

## Trimming and stop-losses: use conditions, not prices

Set stop-losses on conditions, not prices. Exit the position regardless of cost if any of the three falsification triggers appears: 1) cloud quarterly growth -RMB 50 billion. All three point to the same conclusion: returns on the asset-heavy shift are below expectations, and the valuation centre must move down one notch from "infrastructure + growth."

Why no price-based stop-loss? Because half of price volatility comes from a valuation-framework shift (185 to 90 is a shift, not fundamentals), and a price stop-loss would be repeatedly shaken out by that shift, then miss the recovery. A condition-based stop-loss means "I admit I was wrong on fundamentals" — the only meaningful stop-loss.

Two cases for trimming: 1) the stock rises above HKD 150 (close to 88% of the optimistic 171), cut by half; 2) a second placement occurs, regardless of price, cut one-third first — a second placement means the "capex discipline" assumption has collapsed, and the valuation should shift down permanently.

## Correlation: not a diversification tool

Alibaba is highly correlated with Chinese internet stocks as a whole. In 2026 the Hang Seng Tech Index fell more than 25%, Tencent and SMIC more than 30%, and Alibaba, Meituan and Xiaomi more than 20% — evidence that common factors (China asset risk premium, shifts in the AI narrative, USD liquidity) dominate individual stock fundamentals.

Correlation with specific holdings: above 0.7 with Tencent, Meituan, JD.com and Pinduoduo — all Chinese internet, all exposed to the AI narrative; moderate at 0.4–0.5 with Nvidia, TSMC and SK hynix — all exposed to the compute cycle, but Alibaba is a buyer and the others are sellers; below 0.3 with manufacturing names such as CATL and BYD.

The portfolio implication is clear: if you already hold Tencent or Meituan, cut Alibaba's position cap further to 6%, since the two are essentially the same bet on "Chinese internet + AI." Genuine diversification comes from two other exposures: "compute sellers" (Nvidia, optical modules, memory) and "domestic consumption." If you treat Alibaba as a call option on Chinese AI, do not buy a second option of the same kind in the same account.
