## Notes

# Verification and Methodology

**Verification method:** Company announcements and press releases are treated as primary sources, sell-side commentary and financial media as secondary sources, and self-calculated values as derived sources. Deviation = |A−B| / A.

**P/E basis note:** GAAP net profit attributable to shareholders includes impairments and one-off fines, whereas Non-GAAP excludes share-based compensation; the two must not be mixed. Valuation here uses SOTP, and P/E is not used to set target prices.

## Three items with zero deviation: revenue figures can be used with confidence

Revenue, cloud revenue and cloud EBITA all differ by less than 0.05% between company disclosures and sell-side commentary — a rounding-level gap. That alignment tells us two things: the new segment definitions were already settled at the time of release, with no sell-side adjustments; and cloud is the most closely watched line this quarter, checked repeatedly, which gives it the highest credibility.

It also explains why this report's core conclusions can rest on cloud: RMB 48.44 billion, +45%, EBITA RMB 5.63 billion, +133% — four numbers corroborated across three sources. By contrast, the RMB 13.86 billion loss at AI Labs and Applications rests on the company announcement alone; sell-side commentary largely repeats the -415% loss margin without independent modelling — a single-source number that warrants more conservative use.

## Deviation one: 11.6% versus 12%—a rounding difference, but it shifts the valuation anchor

The announcement states "EBITA margin improved to 12%", yet 56.28 ÷ 484.37 gives 11.62%. The 0.4pp gap looks trivial, but this line anchors the valuation. Pricing cloud at 12% embeds a steady-state margin 3% above 11.6%; if cloud revenue annualises to RMB 193.7 billion, a 0.4pp margin difference implies an EBITA delta of RMB 770 million — roughly RMB 11.5 billion of market cap at 15× EBITA, or about HKD 0.6 per share. The conclusion is unchanged, but the two figures cannot be used interchangeably in a research report.

Approach: use 11.6% throughout tables and body text (it is recomputable from disclosed figures) and note "announcement states 12%" only when quoting the announcement verbatim.

## Deviation 2: customer management -7% vs +1% is a definitional difference, not fraud

This is the most misread set of numbers this quarter. The segment table shows customer management revenue of RMB 82.55 billion, down 7% YoY, yet another section of the same announcement states that "customer management revenue grew 1% YoY on a like-for-like basis." The gap comes from the 2025 business reclassification: certain technical services previously booked under customer management, together with commission revenue tied to instant retail, were reclassified, scrambling the base.

My approach is to state both in the body, treating -7% as the reported basis and +1% as the true operating basis. In my judgment, weight -7% more heavily: if like-for-like growth really were +1%, the company would describe it as growth rather than decline, and management's commentary on e-commerce at the earnings call was cautious. The safe read: customer management revenue is near zero growth, and neither basis supports the claim that e-commerce has already recovered.

## Deviation 3 and 4: benchmarks for placement discount and dilution ratio

Two discount figures are in circulation: 8.4% and 3.6%. The 8.4% takes the August 21 close of HKD 123.0 as its base (112.70 ÷ 123.0 − 1 = -8.37%); the 3.6% takes the placement "reference price" as its base, and that reference price is typically the volume-weighted average price over the trading days before pricing rather than the closing price. We use 8.4% because it captures the actual price gap borne by existing shareholders—the August 24 close of HKD 112.5 has already fallen below HKD 112.70.

The dilution ratio follows the same logic: 710 million shares ÷ 19.89 billion shares before the placement = 3.57%, and ÷ 20.46 billion shares after the placement = 3.47%. The announcement's claim that the shares represent "approximately 3.57% of the total number of issued shares after completion" reflects the flawed intuition of putting the new share capital in the denominator; the rigorous figure is 3.47%. This report uses 3.47% throughout, because the valuation uses the post-placement share count.

## The only metric that doesn't line up is P/E, so only a range is given

The 31x in the working papers is back-solved: HKD 2.30 trillion market cap ÷ 0.92 ÷ RMB 72.54 billion TTM net profit attributable to shareholders = 29.2x, a 5.8% gap. The difference may stem from the FX rate (0.92 is the August 2026 HKD/CNY rate; at 0.88 the multiple is 30.5x), or from converting the ADR price. The metric itself warrants a question mark: attributable net profit was depressed by RMB 4.46 billion of goodwill impairment, roughly RMB 4.3 billion of EU fine provisions and a 57.4% effective tax rate — the TTM figure of RMB 72.54 billion is not "normal earning power."

Treatment: do not publish a single-point P/E; write "about 29–31x, weak reference value." Where a forward multiple is genuinely needed, the sell-side Non-GAAP basis is more meaningful: Soochow Securities forecasts FY2027 Non-GAAP net profit of RMB 105.81 billion, implying 20.0x — note this is Non-GAAP, excluding share-based compensation and impairments, and is not comparable with the GAAP 29x.
