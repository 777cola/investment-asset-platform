Four-role summary (Team Lead) — Alibaba

Points of divergence across four independent perspectives, and the verdict

Core view

Cross-validating the four perspectives leaves more agreement than disagreement. Agreement: cloud was the only segment this quarter that was both fast-growing and profitable; e-commerce monetisation rate is retreating; negative free cash flow reflects deliberate choice; AI applications remain a black box. Three points of disagreement remain, adjudicated one by one below.

The consensus conclusion: three facts, one black box

All four perspectives converge on three facts. First, cloud is genuinely strong: RMB 48.44 billion of revenue, +45%, EBITA of RMB 5.63 billion, +133%, an 11.6% margin — delivered in the same period as RMB 67.68 billion of capex, which shows scale effects arrived ahead of depreciation pressure. Second, e-commerce monetization is retreating: customer management revenue -7% as reported and +1% like-for-like; neither basis supports the word "recovery." Third, the cash flow hole is self-dug: operating cash flow +11% against capex +75%, a RMB 44.7 billion gap, with the HKD 80 billion placement covering only 1.6 quarters.

The AI lab and applications remain a black box: RMB 3.34 billion of revenue, a RMB 13.86 billion loss, a -415% loss margin, and management offers a single line of explanation — "higher investment, rising inference costs." All four analysts reach the same conclusion on this segment: no positive valuation; carry it as a negative at a loss multiple. They differ only on the multiple — 3x on the commercial perspective, 3–5x on the risk perspective — with 3x adopted in the end.

Disagreement one: should the cloud business get 6x or 9x PS

Business lens (Duan Yongping) assigns 6x: cloud is asset-heavy, with 1–3 year customer contracts against 5-year depreciation and a mismatch in between, so a higher multiple is unsafe. Financial lens (Buffett) supports 6x: segments with negative free cash flow should not command high multiples. Industry lens (Munger) wants 9x: 38.1% share, in-house chips and a "can't-buy-it" moat that is extremely hard to cross during the control period. Risk lens (Li Lu) pushes it down to 4x: compute is homogenising and 2027–2028 carries high risk of oversupply.

Verdict: 6x neutral, 4x bear case, 8x bull case. Three reasons — ① this quarter is a single data point; waiting for two quarters to confirm 40%+ growth and a 12% margin before raising the multiple is more prudent; ② globally, Azure traded at 8–10x PS when growing 40%, but Azure's customer mix and margins are far better than Alibaba Cloud's; ③ with an 11.6% EBITA margin, Alibaba Cloud now looks more like "wholesale compute + partial PaaS" than a pure platform. 6x PS implies roughly 52x EV/EBITA, which is already not cheap; the upside is left for evidence to earn.

Divergence 2: Is AI application an asset or an expense?

This is a genuine disagreement. The commercial view sees ecosystem value: over 3 billion global downloads of the Qwen series and more than 300,000 derivative models, Qwen3.8-Max open-sourced at 2.4 trillion parameters, and de facto standard status for Chinese open-source models — which over time converts into cloud revenue. The risk view sees cash burn: RMB 13.86 billion in a single quarter, RMB 55.4 billion annualized, equivalent to burning half an e-commerce group's EBITA every year.

The way to adjudicate is to value the two separately: the value of the open-source ecosystem is already reflected in cloud's 45% growth (customers buy Alibaba Cloud because they can use Qwen), so it cannot be valued a second time on a standalone basis without double-counting. What remains is only the path of "the application layer earning its own money," and it currently loses RMB 4.15 for every RMB 1 of revenue. We therefore assign a negative value at 3x annualized losses (-RMB 166.2 billion), which neither denies the ecosystem value nor pays for it twice.

This adjudication has a large impact on the target price: at zero valuation, HKD 136 neutral; at 5x losses, HKD 117. Using 3x is the middle path, and the only one that holds together.

Divergence 3: How to read the placement

Near term, the placement is a clear negative. On August 24 the stock fell 8.54% to HKD 112.5, breaking below the 112.70 placement price, while the Hang Seng Tech Index fell nearly 4% the same day. Breaking the placement price is symbolic: even the placees are underwater, and the market's first reaction is that the price was set too high.

Long term, the money is neutral: HKD 80 billion raised, 3.47% dilution, HKD 79.7 billion net, all of it going into AI infrastructure, with the deal nearly 3x oversubscribed and sovereign and long-only funds taking over 40%. Institutions voted with real money, a signal that professional money sees HKD 112.70 as fair or even cheap. Management bought HKD 120 million of stock on August 24 (Joseph Tsai 720,000 shares, Eddie Wu 350,000 shares), 0.15% of the placement — a gesture, not a hedge.

Verdict: near-term negative, long-term neutral, but add one line to the tracking sheet: "whether a second placement appears." Once is an accident, twice is a pattern, and a pattern permanently shifts the valuation down one notch.

Ruling and enforcement: monitor, with conditions clearly specified

Four perspectives converge on one conclusion: good assets (cloud, e-commerce, chips), a neutral price (HKD 129 neutral value, current price at a 13% discount), and missing evidence (the path to narrowing losses in AI applications, the sustainability of cloud growth). Rating: "Watch, wait for a pullback," consistent with the working papers.

On execution, set three hard conditions and act only if any one is met: ① share price ≤ HKD 95 (a 26% discount to neutral value, versus the HKD 88 pessimistic floor, where the risk-reward flips to 5:1); ② cloud segment adjusted EBITA margin ≥12% for two consecutive quarters, with next-quarter growth no lower than 40%; ③ quarterly losses at the AI lab and applications narrow sequentially, and the company provides a timeline for reducing losses. If none of the three is met, do nothing; sitting out a year is also a correct call.

If already holding, the approach differs: do not add at HKD 112, and do not cut here either—wait for Q2 (the October earnings report) to see the direction of cloud margins and AI losses, then decide whether to add or exit. Holding still is also a decision, provided you know why you hold.

Conclusion

Verdict: cloud at 6x PS (neutral), AI applications valued at negative 3x losses, placement a near-term negative but neutral long term. At the current price we would watch, not buy. Target price HKD 129, bear case 88, bull case 171, risk-reward 1:1.5. Three items on the tracking sheet: cloud margin, AI losses quarter on quarter, and whether a second placement follows.
