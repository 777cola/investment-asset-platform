Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Alibaba

Put "how much I could lose" ahead of "how much I could gain"

Core view

The risk is not whether Alibaba survives, but whether this money gets burned with no return. The biggest risk is the return on capital expenditure, followed by the path to narrowing losses in the AI application layer, and third is competition. The probability of all three risks hitting at once is low, but any one of them alone is enough to send the stock down another 20%.

Risk one: return on RMB 380 billion

RMB 190 billion already invested, cloud annualized EBITA of RMB 22.5 billion — an 11.8% static return. That number is acceptable for infrastructure, but it rests on one condition: compute prices do not fall. The entire industry is now expanding capacity (Tencent Q2 capex +176%, the four major North American cloud providers raising guidance), and with supply released in bulk in two to three years, unit compute prices will most likely decline. A 20% price drop takes cloud EBITA margin from 11.6% to below 9%, and the entire segment's valuation logic has to be recalculated.

The bigger problem is the depreciation lag. Depreciation has not yet fully ramped this quarter — part of the reason for the margin improvement. Once equipment purchased in FY2026–FY2027 begins amortizing over 5 years, incremental annual depreciation reaches the RMB 54 billion level. The result will be a combination of "revenue still rising, profit eaten by depreciation" — the most painful phase of a heavy-asset business.

Second risk: the path to narrowing losses in AI applications

AI Labs and Applications: revenue RMB 3.34 billion, loss RMB 13.86 billion, loss margin -415%. This is not a "investment phase" — it is an "unproven model." The company's explanation is "investment in AI capability building + rising inference costs for the Qwen App," which translates to "users pay nothing, we pay the electricity bill."

Li Lu would ask a more fundamental question: who pays for this business. On the enterprise side there is Qwen Office; on the consumer side there are paid value-added services for the Qwen App (launching in August). Both paths have just begun. Compare Tencent: WeChat's AI capabilities sit on top of existing paid scenarios (advertising, gaming, payments), with low marginal cost and a ready monetization path. Alibaba's Qwen must build a new entry point and fight Doubao and DeepSeek for users — the most expensive way to play this.

If no loss reduction is visible within two years, the market will reclassify this division from "investment" to "expense" — on valuation that means going from -3x loss to -5x or worse, a difference of more than HKD 12 per share.

Third risk: governance and dilution chain reaction

The HKD 80 billion placement is not itself the risk—3.47% dilution, 3x oversubscription, sovereign funds taking 40%, the market accepted it. The risk lies in its precedent effect: if FY2027 capex continues to exceed expectations, will there be a second, a third placement? Once is an accident, twice is a pattern. The market assigns a valuation discount to companies that habitually issue new shares—a rule repeatedly validated in Hong Kong stocks over the past decade.

Two governance points bear noting: first, the placement landed two days after the earnings call, showing that the company unilaterally controls the pace of disclosure and market communication, with no expectation management for minority shareholders; second, management's HKD 120 million increase in holdings against an HKD 80 billion placement is a ratio of 0.15%—more symbolic than substantive.

Geopolitics and regulation are tail risks: the roughly RMB 4.3 billion EU fine provision has already been booked; export controls affect chip supply (for Alibaba this is paradoxically the source of a "can still buy" moat—a double-edged sword); ADR policy risk persists long-term but is not the main thread near term.

The other side of risk: why not "avoid"

All five risks are real, but none is the "balance sheet collapse" kind. With RMB 474.51 billion in cash and liquid investments, positive operating cash flow and an unstressed debt structure—this is a company that may earn less, not one that may go to zero. That caps the rating at "Watch" rather than "Avoid".

In Li Lu's framework, the worst case must be calculable: cloud at 3x PS (RMB 0.58 trillion), e-commerce at 7x EBITA (RMB 0.98 trillion), AI applications at 2x losses (-RMB 0.11 trillion), for a total of RMB 1.45 trillion, or HKD 71 per share.This is the extreme case where everything happens at once, with probability below 10%, but it shows you where the floor is.

So the real discipline is: buy at the current price, worst case a 37% loss; buy at HKD 95, worst case a 25% loss; buy at HKD 80, worst case an 11% loss.The lower the price, the smaller the risk exposure—that is the quantitative meaning of "wait for a pullback".

Conclusion

Three risks ranked by importance: capex returns (-27%), e-commerce monetization rate (-13%), and narrowing losses in AI applications (-11%). None of the risks suggests "the company will collapse," but all three suggest "profits will be compressed for three to five years." Hence the rating is watch, not avoid; the trigger is price or evidence, not opinion.
