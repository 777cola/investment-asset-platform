Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Alibaba

Cash flow, capital allocation and shareholder returns
Research date 2026/08/24  ·  Price HK$112.50 (-8.54%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 2.30 trillion (approximately 20.46 billion shares after placement)  ·  PE (TTM, back-solved) ~31x (profit distorted by AI investment; weak reference value)

Core view

Reading the three statements, the conclusion in one line: cash generation is intact (operating cash flow RMB 22.95 billion, +11%), but capex swallowed all of it (RMB 67.68 billion, +75%), leaving free cash flow at negative RMB 44.67 billion — hence the HKD 80 billion placement. This is not a financial crisis; it is a capital allocation choice.

Cash generation is intact, but covers only a third

Operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion, up 11% YoY, is the most reassuring line in a report showing net profit down 76% — it proves the profit decline stems mainly from accounting and investment factors, not from an inability to collect cash. But bringing capex into the picture makes grim reading: RMB 22.95 billion against RMB 67.68 billion, a coverage ratio of 33.9%. In other words, for every RMB 1 of cash earned, the company spends RMB 3 on equipment.

This gap cannot be funded by financing over the long term. The HKD 80 billion placement, with net proceeds of HKD 79.7 billion ≈ RMB 73.3 billion, covers only 1.6 quarters at this quarter's gap rate of RMB 44.7 billion. If FY2027 capex sustains this pace (about RMB 270 billion), the full-year free cash flow gap will reach RMB 150–180 billion, which placements cannot fill — operating cash flow itself must accelerate, or capex must decelerate.

Income statement: how much of the -76% is one-off

Net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 10.54 billion, down 76% year on year, looks like a collapse. Broken down, only one piece is an operating deterioration: the AI lab and applications lost roughly RMB 10.6 billion (a loss of RMB 13.86 billion this quarter versus about RMB 3.2 billion a year earlier). That is deliberate investment — one may accept it or not, but it genuinely happened.

The other three items are one-off or definitional: goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion, an EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion, and the effective tax rate jumping from 17.6% to 57.4% (roughly RMB 6 billion of extra tax), plus interest and investment income falling from RMB 17.38 billion to RMB 9.00 billion (down RMB 8.38 billion, mainly because last year had a one-off investment gain). Together these four items total about RMB 19 billion, or 57% of the net profit decline (from 439.0 to 105.4, a drop of RMB 33.36 billion).

A Buffett-style read: strip out one-offs and the company's normal earning power is about RMB 25–30 billion per quarter, or RMB 100–120 billion annualized, broadly in line with the sell-side Non-GAAP forecast of RMB 105.81 billion for FY2027. On that number, the current price of 20x Non-GAAP PE is neither cheap nor expensive.

Capital allocation: where the money goes and how returns are measured

Of the three-year RMB 380 billion AI infrastructure plan announced in February 2025, about RMB 190 billion had been invested by June 2026 — past the halfway mark. How should the return on that money be calculated? Cloud revenue this quarter was RMB 48.44 billion and EBITA RMB 5.63 billion; annualized EBITA of RMB 22.5 billion against RMB 190 billion invested gives a static return of 11.8%. Not spectacular, but adequate for infrastructure.

The problem is incremental return. If another RMB 190 billion goes in, annualized cloud EBITA must rise from RMB 22.5 billion to RMB 45 billion just to break even — implying annualized cloud revenue of RMB 390 billion at an 11.6% margin, meaning growth must hold above 40% for another two years. This is the single fulcrum of the entire valuation case.

On shareholder returns the direction has reversed: buybacks and cancellations were a plus for Alibaba in past years, but the HKD 80 billion placement in August 2026 pushed progress backwards. Management made a gesture with HKD 120 million of personal purchases, but that is 0.15% of the placement size. Buffett would note it down: management is using its own share price as a signal — selling stock at HKD 112.70 says they believe it can be sold at that price, not that it is cheap enough to buy back.

Balance sheet: how much longer can it hold up?

Cash and liquid investments RMB 474.51 billion (2026/6/30); interest-bearing debt about RMB 281.7 billion on the 2026/3/31 basis; net cash about RMB 190 billion (the company's broad measure). On stockanalysis's narrow measure (cash and investments RMB 316.8 billion minus interest-bearing debt RMB 281.7 billion), net cash is only RMB 35.1 billion. The two figures differ by RMB 155 billion, and the difference is whether "liquid investments" are counted — use the narrow measure for a more conservative margin of safety.

Stress test: assuming capex holds at RMB 67.68 billion per quarter and operating cash flow holds at RMB 23 billion per quarter, the quarterly gap is RMB 44.7 billion. Narrow-measure net cash of RMB 35.1 billion plus placement proceeds of RMB 73.3 billion ≈ RMB 108.4 billion, enough for only 2.4 quarters. Conclusion: without slowing down or raising more capital, the broader cash pool gets tapped by the end of FY2027. That is why this placement is not a nice-to-have but a must-complete.

The good news is that the debt structure is not stretched: the overall debt ratio is low, ratings are solid and the cash pool is huge, so there is no near-term solvency risk. The bad news is that the "net cash company" label is gone, and that label was the floor under Alibaba's valuation for the past few years.

Conclusion

Financially this is a company with "healthy cash flow and aggressive capital allocation," not one with "deteriorating financials." The judgment Buffett would give: the business is decent, management is gambling, and the price is not cheap enough to hedge the risk of a wrong bet. Watch three numbers: whether the single-quarter FCF gap narrows to within -RMB 20 billion, whether capex growth falls below 30%, and whether the cloud segment's EBITA can cover the year's incremental depreciation.
