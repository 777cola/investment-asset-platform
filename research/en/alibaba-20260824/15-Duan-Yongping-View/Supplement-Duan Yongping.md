# Alibaba（09988）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/08/24
- Price：112.5 HKD
- Market cap：Approximately HKD 2.30 trillion (approximately 20.46 billion shares after placement)

## Notes

This piece is rewritten using the investment principles Duan Yongping has stated publicly (circle of competence, the nature of the business, benfen, the stop-doing list, margin of safety) as its framework; it is not his own view. All figures are taken from the FY2027 Q1 announcement and this report's SOTP.

## Q1: Is this a good business?

"To judge whether it is good, first see how hard it works to make money." E-commerce is not hard work: RMB 205.86 billion revenue, RMB 39.75 billion EBITA, a 19.3% margin, merchants pay first and the goods need not be stocked in-house — one of the best business models there is. Cloud is harder work: RMB 67.68 billion of capex in a single quarter, cash goes in first and rent is collected later, but an 11.6% segment margin shows it has moved past the "burning cash only" stage.

Instant retail and AI applications are less good: one buys share with subsidies (RMB 53.3 billion revenue, +45%, but it thins the group gross margin), the other loses RMB 4.15 for every RMB 1 of revenue (RMB 3.34 billion revenue, RMB 13.86 billion loss). "These two are not businesses right now; they are investments."

Conclusion: half is a good business, half is still on the way to becoming one. Overall it counts as "good", but not "very good".

## Q2: Will it still be around in ten years?

"Whether it is still here in ten years depends on whether it solves people's long-term needs." People will still buy things, so Taobao will most likely still be here; enterprises will still need computing power, so Alibaba Cloud will most likely still be here. Neither of these two things will disappear within ten years, so this company will most likely still be here in ten years, and at a larger scale.

But "still here" does not equal "still profitable." What worries me is this: in ten years, Taobao may become "a public utility that cannot raise prices" — 88VIP has 64 million members, yet customer management revenue shows zero growth, which means "people are there, but the money is not coming in." The end point of infrastructure-ization is a public utility, with returns compressed to the average by regulation and competition.

So the real question is not "will it still be here," but "while it is still here, is it earning good money."That cannot be seen right now; it depends on whether cloud can stabilize its margin above 12% within three years.

## Question three: Can you understand it?

"If you don't understand it, don't touch it — that's not modesty, it's survival." E-commerce is understandable, cloud is understandable, chips are half-understandable (I know it is cutting costs and securing supply, but not how large its performance gap is), AI applications are not understandable — RMB 3.34 billion in revenue, RMB 13.86 billion in losses, and the company only says "higher investment, rising inference costs" without saying when it will earn the money back.

How big is the part I don't understand? Annualized, AI applications burn RMB 55.4 billion a year, half of the group's annualized adjusted EBITA of RMB 109.2 billion. In other words, half the profit is being invested into something I cannot explain clearly. By my standards, that is "too big to ignore."

The treatment is simple: value the understandable parts (e-commerce + cloud) on the numbers, deduct for the part I don't understand (AI applications). That is how the sum-of-the-parts valuation of HKD 129 works — e-commerce RMB 1.43 trillion, cloud RMB 1.16 trillion, AI applications -RMB 0.17 trillion. Something I don't understand should not get a positive number; a negative number is the honest approach.

## Question 4: At what price to buy

"Price isn't about how far it has fallen; it's about how much you can make after you buy." At the current HKD 112.5, against a fair value of HKD 129, you make 15%; on the downside, the bear case of HKD 88 means a 22% loss. That is 1:1.5 odds — not enough.

What price is enough? Below HKD 95. At that level, upside to 129 is +36% and downside to 88 is -7%, or 5:1 odds. Even if there is a 2/3 chance I am wrong, the trade still makes money. That is the whole meaning of "margin of safety": it is not about buying good companies, it is about buying good odds on good companies.

The other entry point is "evidence appearing": cloud margins holding above 12% for two consecutive quarters, and AI application losses starting to narrow quarter on quarter. By then the price may already be 130, but the win rate rises from 33% to above 60%, so 1:1.5 odds become acceptable. Price or evidence — at least one has to be on my side. Right now neither is.

## Question 5: What worries you most?

"What I fear most is not the fall itself, but falling without knowing why." Three things worry me. First, the heavy-asset trap. Capital expenditure of RMB 67.68 billion in a single quarter — if compute prices collapse three years from now, that RMB 380 billion becomes a pile of depreciation. Second, "earning while leaking" becoming the norm. E-commerce earned RMB 39.75 billion, AI applications lost RMB 13.86 billion, all other segments lost RMB 3.34 billion, and instant retail is still subsidized — the money earned does not cover the money spent. Third, habitual financing. This HKD 80 billion is a one-off; if it happens again next year, it is a pattern, and a pattern permanently lowers the valuation.

These three share one thing: none is a market problem — all are the company's own choices. If the market falls, I can wait; if the company burns money, I cannot — because spent money is gone.

So the metric I care about most is not revenue growth, but whether capital expenditure is disciplined. Management that controls spending deserves a premium; management that treats spending as an achievement can only be bought at a discount.

## Question 6: What should those who already hold it do?

"If you already own it, ask yourself one question: if you were flat today, would you still buy at this price?" If the answer is "no," trim. If the answer is "yes, because I'm looking three years out," hold — but write down clearly what you expect to see in three years.

My advice splits into two cases. If your cost is below HKD 130, you can hold without moving — the progress in cloud is real; the risk-reward is unattractive but not bad enough to run, and trimming risks selling at the low. If your cost is above HKD 150 and your position is heavy, cut to below 5% — not because the company has deteriorated, but because your cost determines your mindset, and a heavy loss makes people make bad decisions.

One last old saying: "Not doing the wrong thing matters more than doing the right thing." At this level, there are three "wrong things" — chasing the high, holding a heavy position, and dumping everything because you're angry about the placement. Anger is not an investment reason; price is.
