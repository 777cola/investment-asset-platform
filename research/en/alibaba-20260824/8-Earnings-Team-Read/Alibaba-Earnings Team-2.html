Core view

If you could look at only one number, it would be free cash flow: -RMB 44.67 billion. That single figure explains the -76% net profit, the RMB 80 billion placement, and why an 11.6% cloud margin failed to lift the share price.

Income statement: a "clean bad sheet"

A 76% drop in net profit looks alarming, but the breakdown is clean. The only genuine operating deterioration is the roughly RMB 10.6 billion loss from AI labs and applications. The other four items — goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion, an EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion, the effective tax rate jumping from 17.6% to 57.4% (about RMB 6 billion more tax paid), and investment income falling from RMB 17.38 billion to RMB 9.00 billion (down RMB 8.38 billion) — total about RMB 23 billion and are one-off or accounting in nature.

Buffett would do one thing: strip these out and look at "normal earning power." Non-GAAP net profit of RMB 20.72 billion, down 38% YoY — that decline (about RMB 12.7 billion) almost equals the incremental AI spend (about RMB 10.6 billion) plus the incremental cloud depreciation. In other words, absent the deliberate AI investment, net profit this quarter would have been around RMB 31 billion, down 7% YoY. That is his basis for judging that "operations aren't broken — the company is deliberately spending."

Cash flow statement: the only statement that matters

Operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion, up 11% YoY, in a quarter where net profit fell 76%, shows the cash content of revenue has not deteriorated. But capital expenditure of RMB 67.68 billion swallowed all of it. Buffett would ask: how much cash will what this RMB 67.68 billion bought generate next year?

The answer lies in the cloud segment: annualised revenue of RMB 193.7 billion and annualised EBITA of RMB 22.5 billion. Against RMB 190 billion already invested, that is a static return of 11.8%. He would probably say an 11.8% return is acceptable, but it falls below Berkshire's hurdle, and depreciation is not yet fully loaded. Once it is, the return may be only 8–9%.

What would discomfort him more is the funding method: equity issuance rather than debt. Debt carries interest, a maturity date and covenants; equity issuance only requires existing shareholders to accept dilution. From a "protect shareholders" standpoint, this is the easiest and least dignified approach — it shifts the risk of capital expenditure from the company to its shareholders.

Balance sheet: the floor is still there, but it is lower

Cash and other current investments of RMB 474.51 billion provide a thick floor. But net cash on the narrow measure is just RMB 35.1 billion, down 80% from RMB 179.3 billion a year ago. The market underappreciates what the "net cash company" label is worth: in the worst case, the company does not need to ask anyone for help. That attribute has now weakened.

Stress test: a quarterly shortfall of RMB 44.7 billion against net placement proceeds of RMB 73.3 billion covers 1.6 quarters. Absent a capex slowdown, the company raises again within fiscal 2027 or draws heavily on its cash pool.

Buffett's response to this kind of situation is to wait, not to bet. Wait for what? For the quarter when free cash flow turns positive again — at that point the valuation framework switches from "infrastructure stock" back to "platform stock," a multiple gap of more than two times. Buy two years later, give up a stretch of gains, but avoid all the risk of the capex cycle.

Conclusion

Conclusion: a clean bad balance sheet paired with a stretched but good cash-flow statement. Operations are not broken; the company is spending deliberately. The spending itself is not wrong—funding it with a share issuance is. Buffett would not buy at this level, but would put it on the "wait for cash flow" list, and there is only one thing to do on that list: wait for free cash flow to turn positive.
