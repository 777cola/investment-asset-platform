Core view

Alibaba now reports AI as a standalone P&L line. The split tells two very different stories: cloud posted its best quarter in five years, while the application side booked a loss margin of -415%. One quarter, two numbers — only taken together do they tell the truth about this company.

1. First, the good news: cloud, the strongest in five years

Alibaba reported fiscal 2027 first-quarter results (April–June) after the close on August 20, 2026: revenue of RMB 268.95 billion, up 9% YoY, the fastest growth in nearly 4.0 years. The real good news was in cloud — AI cloud and compute services revenue of RMB 48.44 billion, up 45% YoY, the fastest growth in 22 quarters; adjusted EBITA of RMB 5.63 billion, up 133% YoY, with margin rising from 7.2% to 11.6%.

Asset-heavy businesses usually force a choice: growth at the expense of profit, or profit at the expense of growth. Cloud achieved both this time, for three reasons: depreciation on the previous round of GPU purchases has not yet fully run through, compute supply and demand remain tight, and in-house chips are cutting costs. T-Head's Zhenwu M890 already serves more than 650 external customers across over 20 industries — a hard advantage in an environment where Nvidia is restricted.

Another detail worth noting: AliExpress recorded operating profit this quarter. International business has gone from burning cash to making money, something that has not happened in the past 5.0 years. Alibaba Cloud holds a 38.1% share of China's AI cloud market, ranking first.

Second, the ugly part: net profit fell 76%. Where did the money go?

On the same table, net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 10.54 billion, down 76% YoY and the lowest in nine quarters.Broken down, however, only one item reflects genuine operating deterioration: the AI lab and applications segment lost about RMB 10.6 billion.

The other four items are one-off or accounting-related: goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion; an EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion; an effective tax rate jumping from 17.6% to 57.4%, adding about RMB 6 billion in tax; and interest and investment income falling from RMB 17.38 billion to RMB 9.00 billion, a drop of RMB 8.38 billion. Together these four items total about RMB 23 billion.

Non-GAAP is therefore closer to operating reality: adjusted net profit of RMB 20.72 billion, down 38% YoY.That 38% decline almost exactly equals the incremental AI investment plus the incremental cloud depreciation.In other words, absent AI investment, net profit this quarter would be roughly RMB 31 billion, down 7% YoY.The business isn't broken; the company deliberately switched lanes.

III. Black box: a bill with a loss ratio of -415%

From this quarter Alibaba reports four segments, with "AI Labs & Applications" broken out for the first time: revenue RMB 3.34 billion, +16% YoY, and a loss of RMB 13.86 billion — a -415% loss margin, with the loss widening 330% YoY.

In plain terms, the segment spends RMB 5.15 for every RMB 1 of revenue. Management attributes this to "investment in AI capability building plus rising inference costs for the Qwen App." The product is real: Qwen3.8-Max open-sourced at 2.4 trillion parameters, Qwen series global downloads above 3 billion, 300,000 derivative models, paid value-added services launched on the Qwen App in August, and Qwen Office aimed at enterprises.

Yet no disclosure indicates when the segment turns profitable. This is the biggest information gap in the entire report: management gave the loss margin but not its trend, a path to narrower losses, or a timeline. On that disclosure, any positive valuation assigned to the application layer is imagination.

IV. Cash: RMB 44.7 billion burned in a single quarter, then a RMB 80 billion ask

Operating cash flow came in at RMB 22.95 billion, up 11% YoY, so cash generation remains intact. Capex, however, was RMB 67.68 billion, up 75% YoY, and free cash flow was negative RMB 44.67 billion, with the shortfall widening 1.4x YoY.

Then came the evening of August 23 announcement: a placement of 710 million new shares at HKD 112.70 apiece, raising HKD 80 billion gross and about HKD 79.7 billion net, a 3.47% dilution, with 100% of proceeds earmarked for full-stack AI and AI infrastructure. CICC, HSBC, Morgan Stanley and UBS underwrote the deal, which carried a 90-day lock-up, was nearly 3x oversubscribed, and saw sovereign and long-only funds take more than 40%.

On August 24 the stock fell 8.54% to close at HKD 112.5, below the placement price, while the Hang Seng Tech Index fell nearly 4% the same day. Joseph Tsai bought 720,000 shares and Eddie Wu bought 350,000 shares, for a combined HKD 120 million — 0.15% of the placement size. That is a gesture, not a hedge.

V. How to price it: HKD 129 base case and 1:1.5 risk-reward

Sum-of-the-parts: e-commerce annualised EBITA of RMB 159 billion at 9x = RMB 1.43 trillion; cloud annualised revenue of RMB 193.7 billion at 6x = RMB 1.16 trillion; AI applications at 3x the annualised loss of RMB 55.4 billion, carried as a negative = -RMB 0.17 trillion. Total RMB 2.42 trillion, which at 20.46 billion shares post-allotment and an FX rate of 0.92 gives HKD 129 per share.

Bear case HKD 88 (-22%), base case 129 (+15%), bull case 171 (+52%). Payoff ratio 1:1.5, requiring a 60% win rate to justify a bet — and of the two propositions, "cloud holds 45%" and "AI applications narrow losses within two years," the latter has not a single data point.

The conclusion is therefore watch, not avoid: good assets, fair price, insufficient evidence. Two hard-coded buy conditions — the share price falls below HKD 95; or the cloud segment margin stays above 12% for two consecutive quarters and AI application losses narrow quarter on quarter. If neither is met, do nothing. That is the whole of the discipline.

Conclusion

How to read Alibaba's earnings: half is the best cloud business in five years, half is an experiment running a -415% loss ratio. The stock fell 8.54% that day — what fell was not the results but the dilution and uncertainty from the RMB 80 billion placement. HKD 112.5 is not expensive, but it is not cheap either: a "can wait" price, not a "must buy" one.
