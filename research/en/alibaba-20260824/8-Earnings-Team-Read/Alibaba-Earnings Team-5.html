Synthesis of the four masters' views — Alibaba

Four close reads, one consensus, two divergences
Research date 2026/08/24  ·  Price HK$112.50 (-8.54%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 2.30 trillion (approximately 20.46 billion shares post-placement)  ·  PE (TTM, back-solved) ~31x (earnings distorted by AI investment; limited read-across)

Core view

Read the four deep dives together and consensus outweighs disagreement: all four endorse the cloud business (+45%, 11.6% margin), all four reject the AI application numbers (-415% loss ratio), and all four judge the current price not cheap enough to buy.

Four analysts review the material independently and pool their findings afterwards. No cross-discussion session is held, so as to avoid convergence of views.
Consensus one: cloud was the only victory this quarter

All four reached the same conclusion on cloud, in similar wording: RMB 48.44 billion, +45%, EBITA RMB 5.63 billion, +133%, margin 11.6% — delivered in the same period as sustained capex expansion. Duan Yongping called it "the more you do, the more you earn"; Buffett said "an 11.8% static return is acceptable"; Munger said "this is the only segment that is improving"; Li Lu said "this is the position we bought into."

The value of this consensus is that it turns cloud valuation from a "story" into a recomputable multiple. Cloud annualised revenue is RMB 193.7 billion; at 6x that is RMB 1.16 trillion, or 48% of the neutral valuation of RMB 2.42 trillion. In other words, buying Alibaba at the current price means half the money is buying cloud.

Consensus view 2: AI applications are a black box and should not command a positive valuation

The four investors frame it differently but land in the same place. Duan Yongping: "if you can't understand it, don't touch it" — value it at cost. Buffett: "no premium for an asset with no cash flow" — carry it as a negative. Munger: "selective disclosure is itself a signal" — value it at the worst case. Li Lu: "the position has value but the rent is undetermined" — likewise assigns no positive number.

This report therefore books a uniform negative value of 3x annualised losses (RMB -166.2 billion).That is no small sum: -6.9% of the neutral valuation, equal to RMB -8.1 per share, or about HKD -8.8.If management lays out a path to narrower losses next quarter, this RMB -166.2 billion can shrink to RMB -55.4 billion (1x), adding HKD 5.9 per share; if losses widen to 5x, that takes another HKD 8.8 off each share.

Disagreement one: is this a "predictable business"

Duan Yongping and Buffett sit on the "predictable" side: e-commerce revenue, cloud revenue, capex and depreciation policy are all trackable, with error you can control — only the AI application piece is unmeasurable, so strip it out. The subtext: a company need not be understandable in every piece, so long as the piece you cannot understand is small enough.

Munger and Li Lu sit on the "unpredictable" side: AI applications account for 100% of where capex goes (the entire RMB 80 billion placement goes to AI); when an investment of that size is unpredictable, the whole company is unpredictable. Li Lu's addendum is harsher: ultimate pricing power over compute sits with the buyer, and a ceiling on Chinese buyers' willingness to pay could compress cloud returns to utility-like levels.

Verdict: a middle path — price the part you understand, deduct for the part you do not. That is precisely what SOTP does: 9x for e-commerce, 6x for cloud (both comparable multiples), and a negative value for AI applications. The disagreement does not affect method, only the level of the multiples. This report adopts the more conservative set, on the grounds that at this point precision is not required — safety is.

Disagreement two: how should the placement be penalized

Buffett and Munger would penalise this heavily: issuing shares at a low price shifts risk onto shareholders, and management lifting its stake by only 0.15% signals insufficient conviction. Duan Yongping would be more forgiving: financing is not itself a mistake, only the timing looks poor. Li Lu would barely mark it down at all: the infrastructure investment phase inherently requires external capital, global cloud providers are all raising debt to expand capacity, and Alibaba turned to equity financing only because Hong Kong-listed shares offer better liquidity.

Verdict: penalise the valuation multiple, not the rating. Specifically, score management 6/10 (rather than 7) and shave HKD 5 off the optimistic scenario—HKD 171 rather than HKD 176. Why not cut the rating? Because the placement itself does not change the quality of the assets, only the price you pay.

Synthesized conclusion: one sentence

Alibaba is half good assets, half experiments. The good-asset half — e-commerce at RMB 1.43 trillion plus cloud at RMB 1.16 trillion — is worth RMB 2.59 trillion; the experimental half (AI applications) is worth negative RMB 0.17 trillion. Together that is RMB 2.42 trillion, or HKD 129 per share. Against the current price of HKD 112.5, that implies a 13% discount.

None of the four would buy at this price, but for different reasons: Duan Yongping says "I don't understand AI applications," Buffett says "negative cash flow," Munger says "fighting three battles at once," and Li Lu says "wait for the investment phase to end." All four reasons point to the same action: wait. What they are waiting for differs, but the timing is the same — two quarters.

Conclusion

Synthesis: Rating "Watch, wait for pullback"; base case HKD 129, bear case 88, bull case 171; risk/reward of 1:1.5 is not enough. Disagreements are folded into multiple selection and do not change the conclusion. Next quarter (FY2027Q2, November), watch two numbers: cloud EBITA margin and the quarter-on-quarter loss at AI applications.
