## Key person: who actually makes the decisions

Alibaba's decision structure is a "chairman + CEO" dual core, not one person holding sway. **Joseph Tsai oversees capital and external affairs, Eddie Wu oversees strategy and technology**—a division established in 2023 and unchanged for two and a half years. On August 24, 2026, Joseph Tsai bought 720,000 shares (about HKD 80 million) and Eddie Wu bought 350,000 shares (about HKD 40 million). The simultaneous purchases signal coordinated division of labor and are also a gesture.

Three people actually drive results: Eddie Wu (the capex cadence for cloud and AI), Jiang Fan (e-commerce share and instant-retail subsidies), and Toby Xu (how the losses are framed). **Eddie Wu's annual compensation is about RMB 495 million (including equity incentives), among the highest for Hong Kong-listed tech companies**—the KPI behind that pay is most likely cloud revenue and AI progress, not free cash flow. This must be remembered: where the incentives are, there the behavior follows.

The partnership system remains Alibaba's implicit power center. The June 2026 reshuffle saw Shao Xiaofeng exit and Zhou Jingren and Toby Xu join, **bringing the CFO and the AI scientist into the partnership at the same time—giving "the one who spends" and "the one who controls the purse" equal decision-making seats.** That is a balance, and it also means capex discipline depends on these two checking each other rather than on the system.

## Capital allocation record: four major deals in four years

**① Buyback and cancellation (2024–2025): positive.** Alibaba is one of the most active buyback names in Hong Kong, repurchasing and cancelling large amounts over the past two fiscal years — a key support for the share price recovering from its lows to HKD 185 in 2025. **② Spinoffs and focus (2023–2025): positive.** Cutting non-core businesses and concentrating resources on cloud and AI is what produced today's 45% cloud growth. **③ RMB 380 billion AI infrastructure (from February 2025): to be validated.** About RMB 190 billion invested by June 2026, past the halfway mark; annualized cloud EBITA of RMB 22.5 billion implies a static return of 11.8%. **④ HKD 80 billion placement (August 2026): negative.** Issuing equity at 39% below the high diluted holders by 3.47%, rolling back part of the prior three years' buyback progress.

Two less conspicuous but important items: **the roughly USD 800 million investment in Kimi (Moonshot AI) for about a 36% stake** (disclosed in the FY2026 annual report), and ownership of its own properties (about 18.7 million square meters). The former is an option on the AI ecosystem; the latter is the other face of asset-heavy expansion — both push in a "heavier" direction.

Taken together, this management team's capital allocation preference is clear: **willing to pay for "position", unwilling to wait for "cash flow".** Buybacks mean buying its own stock (when cheap); placements mean selling its own stock (when it needs money). Doing both within 24 months shows decisions are opportunity-driven, not discipline-driven.

## Integrity and governance: no blemishes, but habits

There is no hard integrity problem: no financial fraud, no controlling-shareholder fund appropriation, no related-party transaction scandal. Audit opinions are standard, with dual disclosure in Hong Kong and the US, and the governance framework is among the more standardised in Hong Kong stocks — the independent directors include Jerry Yang, Shan Weijian, Irene Lee, Anthony Wu, Kabir Misra and Wan Ling Martello, a solid lineup.

But two "habits" are worth noting. **The first is surprise financing.** Results were released after market close on 20 August 2026, and the placement was announced on the evening of 23 August, with only one trading day in between. The placement itself is compliant (general mandate, no shareholder meeting required), but it is a sudden shock of information asymmetry to the secondary market — the -8.54% fall on 24 August, breaking below the placement price, was the market voting with its feet. **The second is flexibility in disclosure basis.** Customer management revenue was -7% as reported and +1% on a like-for-like basis, with both figures appearing in the same announcement; compliant, but it makes it hard for outsiders to judge the true trend.

A provision of about RMB 4.3 billion for the EU fine was booked this quarter; this settles a historical compliance issue and is not a new problem. **Overall judgement: governance passes, communication habits do not; no deduction on integrity, a deduction on communication.**

## Succession: how many more years can this leadership serve?

Joseph Tsai was born in 1964 and is 62 this year; Eddie Wu was born in 1975 and is 51; Jiang Fan is 40 and Xu Hong is 53. **The real question is "who comes after Eddie Wu," and right now there is no visible answer.** Jiang Fan is the successor candidate for the e-commerce line, but no number-two has surfaced on the cloud and AI main line — Zhou Jingren is the technology lead, not the business lead.

The risk in this structure is not that "people will leave" but that "the roadmap will break." **The RMB 380 billion capex plan and the full-stack AI roadmap depend heavily on Eddie Wu's personal judgment.** If he hands over around FY2028, the first thing the new CEO faces will be "keep investing or pull back" — a harder decision than the one today.

From an investment perspective, this is not a current-period variable but a valuation variable: **companies with high key-person dependence should command a lower multiple over the long term.** This is not deducted in the optimistic scenario, and is one reservation behind my HKD 171 target rather than a higher one.

## Management score: execution 8, capital allocation 5, communication 4

Scoring it across three items is fairer. **Execution 8**: lifting cloud from 26% growth to 45% and margin from 7.2% to 11.6%, while carving out AI into a separately accounted unit, is an organizational move that takes courage. **Capital allocation 5**: the RMB 380 billion is pointed in the right direction, but the pace is too aggressive and it is funded by share issuance rather than operating cash flow, shifting risk onto shareholders. **Communication 4**: the surprise placement, dual-basis disclosure and the absence of a timeline for narrowing AI losses all three make the company hard to model from the outside.

Weighted (execution 40%, capital allocation 40%, communication 20%), the score is 6.2, consistent with the 6/10 management judgment in the six gates. **What that score means: you can trust them to run the business, not to refrain from spending.**

The operational implication for investors is direct: **treat "capex discipline" as a standalone tracking metric**, not as a quality of management. Watch the numbers, not the statements: whether quarterly capex decelerates sequentially, whether a second placement appears, whether depreciation growth outpaces revenue growth. If two of those three deteriorate, cut the rating by one notch.
