Funnel conclusion

Run the six Chinese internet companies that can do "AI + transactions" through a funnel: Tencent, Alibaba, Pinduoduo, Meituan, JD.com, ByteDance (unlisted). The coarse screen eliminates three: JD.com (1.3% margin, no AI position), Meituan (loss-making this period, single battlefield), ByteDance (cannot be bought).

Three finalists: Tencent (steadiest cash flow), Pinduoduo (cheapest, highest ROE), Alibaba (the only full-stack AI position). Among the three, Alibaba has both the best and the worst odds—the good: cloud's high growth is not priced in; the bad: free cash flow is negative. At the current price, Alibaba is picked as the "watchlist name"—not because it is the best, but because it is the most likely to hold an "expectations gap."

First-pass screen: keep only companies with an AI position and cash flow

Among Chinese internet companies, only four combine "large-scale cash flow" and "an AI compute position": Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Huawei (unlisted). Add "extreme efficiency" as a dimension and Pinduoduo must also be included. Meituan and JD.com are screened out at the first layer: Meituan is loss-making in the current period (operating margin around -6.5%), while JD.com has an operating margin of 1.3% and no compute or models of its own.

ByteDance's problem is not quality but that it cannot be bought. The same goes for Huawei — an extremely strong compute position (Ascend), but outside the investable universe. Of the remaining five, Alibaba is unique in being "the only one that has built all four layers: chips, cloud, models and scenarios"; Tencent lacks chips, Pinduoduo lacks cloud and Meituan lacks models.

Layer two: three finalists, three ways to survive

Tencent is the "cash flow" play: WeChat advertising, gaming and payments are cash machines, and AI capabilities sit on top of existing paid scenarios with low marginal cost. 2026Q2 capex was RMB 52.8 billion, up 176% year on year — it is expanding capacity too, but free cash flow remains positive. Its risk is that "AI spending erodes gaming and advertising margins"; its opportunity is that "AI lifts advertising conversion by another notch."

Pinduoduo is the "efficiency" play: market cap of about RMB 839.9 billion and ROE in the 21% range, one of the highest revenue-per-employee e-commerce companies in commercial history. It has no cloud and no large-model narrative; it relies on extreme low prices and supply chain. Its risks are "Temu's geopolitics" and "the limits of domestic growth"; its opportunity is that "while everyone else burns cash, it still makes money."

Alibaba is the "positioning" play: the only full-stack player, and the only one burning cash. Its payoff depends on whether cloud can deliver. Ranking among the three: for stability, pick Tencent; for cheapness, pick Pinduoduo; for expectation gap, pick Alibaba.

Why Alibaba Is the Final Pick: Three Reasons and One Objection

Reason one: the position cannot be replicated. Chips (Pingtouge Zhenwu M890, 650+ external customers), cloud (38.1% share), models (Qwen3.8-Max with 2.4 trillion parameters, 300,000 derivative models), scenarios (88VIP 64 million) — all four layers complete, the only such company in China. Such assets cannot be priced on PE, only on "replacement cost." Reason two: the market gives it only a 13% discount. SOTP neutral value of HKD 129 versus the current price of HKD 112.5 — the discount is not deep, but it already embeds some expectation that cloud growth will slow. Reason three: the catalysts are clear. Cloud margins and the direction of AI losses over the next two quarters can both be verified in the November earnings report.

There is only one counterargument, but it is hard: free cash flow is negative. RMB -44.67 billion, with the shortfall widening 1.4x year on year, kept alive by an HKD 80 billion placement. Neither Tencent nor Pinduoduo needs to do this. So the answer to "why this one" is not "it is the best," but "it is the most likely to deliver an expectations gap, and its downside can be calculated clearly (bear case HKD 88, extreme case HKD 71)."

Ranking conclusion: where it sits on the list

If I could buy only one Chinese internet stock, at this point I would pick Tencent, on the grounds of positive free cash flow, AI investment that does not strain cash, and a valuation with a margin of safety. If I could pick only the one with the most upside elasticity, I would pick Alibaba — but only below HKD 95, or after cloud margins are confirmed for a second quarter.

PDD's problem is not that it is a bad business, but that it is unclear where growth comes from: the domestic market has plateaued, Temu is exposed to geopolitics, and the valuation is cheap but lacks a catalyst. Meituan is the hardest one: stable share and improving UE, but loss-making in the current period and squeezed by three rivals.

So Alibaba's position is second on the list, but the one most worth watching. Its earnings carry the most information (four segments, each verifiable), and it is the most likely to throw up an expectations gap over the next two quarters — up or down. For the researcher, it is the most efficient name; for the buyer, it is the name that requires patience.

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: six names screened → three finalists (Tencent, Pinduoduo, Alibaba) → Alibaba ranks second but is the one to watch most closely. The case for it: a unique position, clear catalysts, and a calculable downside; the case against: negative free cash flow. On execution: no purchase, but put it in the No. 1 watch slot, and build a one-third position first when the trigger conditions are met.
