## First-pass screen: retain only companies with an AI position and cash flow

Only four Chinese internet companies combine "large-scale cash flow" with "an AI compute position": Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Huawei (unlisted). Add "extreme efficiency" as a dimension and Pinduoduo must also be included. Meituan and JD.com are screened out at the first layer: Meituan is loss-making in the current period (operating margin around -6.5%), while JD.com has an operating margin of 1.3% and no compute or models of its own.

ByteDance's problem is not quality but that it cannot be bought. The same goes for Huawei — an extremely strong compute position (Ascend), but outside the investable universe. Of the remaining five, Alibaba is unique in being "the only one that has built all four layers: chips, cloud, models and scenarios"; Tencent lacks chips, Pinduoduo lacks cloud and Meituan lacks models.

## Layer two: three finalists, three ways to survive

Tencent is the cash flow play: WeChat advertising, gaming and payments are cash machines, and AI sits on top of existing paid scenarios at low marginal cost. 2026Q2 capex was RMB 52.8 billion, up 176% YoY — capacity is expanding here too, but free cash flow remains positive. The risk is that AI spending erodes gaming and advertising margins; the opportunity is that AI lifts advertising conversion by another notch.

Pinduoduo is the efficiency play: market cap of about RMB 839.9 billion and ROE in the 21% range, making it one of the highest revenue-per-employee e-commerce companies in commercial history. There is no cloud and no large-model narrative; it relies on extreme low prices and supply chain. The risks are Temu's geopolitics and the limits of domestic growth; the opportunity is that while everyone else burns cash, it still makes money.

Alibaba is the positioning play: the only full-stack player, and the only one burning cash. Its payoff depends on whether cloud can deliver. Ranking among the three: for stability, pick Tencent; for cheapness, pick Pinduoduo; for expectation gap, pick Alibaba.

## Why Alibaba Is the Final Pick: Three Reasons and One Objection

Reason one: the position cannot be replicated. Chips (Pingtouge Zhenwu M890, 650+ external customers), cloud (38.1% share), models (Qwen3.8-Max at 2.4 trillion parameters, 300,000 derivative models) and scenarios (88VIP at 64 million) — all four layers are complete, and no other company in China has all four. Such assets cannot be valued on P/E, only on replacement cost. Reason two: the market applies only a 13% discount. SOTP neutral value of HKD 129 against the current HKD 112.5 — not a deep discount, but it already embeds some expectation of slower cloud growth. Reason three: the catalysts are clear. Cloud margins and the direction of AI losses over the next two quarters can both be verified in the November earnings report.

There is only one counterargument, but it is a hard one: free cash flow is negative. RMB -44.67 billion, with the shortfall widening 1.4x YoY and an HKD 80 billion placement keeping the business afloat. Neither Tencent nor Pinduoduo needs to do this. So the answer to "why this one" is not "it is the best," but "it is the most likely to deliver an expectations gap, and its downside can be calculated clearly (bear case HKD 88, extreme case HKD 71)."

## Ranking conclusion: where it sits on the list

If I could own only one Chinese internet stock, it would be Tencent: positive free cash flow, AI investment that does not strain the balance sheet, and a valuation with a margin of safety. If I wanted the most upside elasticity, I would own Alibaba — but only below HKD 95, or once cloud margins have been confirmed for a second quarter.

PDD's problem is not a bad business but an unclear source of growth: the domestic market has plateaued, Temu carries geopolitical exposure, and the stock is cheap but catalyst-light. Meituan is the hardest call: stable share and improving UE, but loss-making in the current period and squeezed by three rivals.

That puts Alibaba second on the list, but first on the watchlist. Its earnings carry the most information — four segments, each verifiable — and it is the most likely to throw up an expectations gap over the next two quarters, in either direction. For the researcher, it is the most efficient name; for the buyer, it is the one that demands patience.
