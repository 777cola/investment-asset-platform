Core view

Moat, scored by durability: cloud and compute 8/10, transaction mindshare 8/10, in-house chips 7/10, instant retail 6/10. The first three were hard-won; the fourth was bought.

The most dangerous rival is not Meituan or Pinduoduo but ByteDance, which is weakening Alibaba on two fronts at once: Douyin e-commerce siphons off transactions and ad budgets—one direct cause of zero growth in customer management—while Volcano Engine siphons off AI compute demand. The second most dangerous is Alibaba itself: if the RMB 67.68 billion quarterly capex mistimes the cycle, heavy assets become a burden.

Position in the supply chain: three roles within the chain

Alibaba is the "landlord" of the e-commerce chain: customer management revenue of RMB 82.55 billion at gross margin above 70%, with merchants paying advertising fees and commissions in exchange for traffic. It is the "wholesaler" of the AI compute chain: RMB 48.44 billion in revenue at an 11.6% EBITA margin, buying chips and selling compute. It is the "fulfillment provider" of the instant retail chain: RMB 53.3 billion in revenue at a 10%–15% gross margin, using riders and front warehouses to deliver goods to customers' doors.Three identities, three business models, three competitive logics.

These multiple identities are synergistic in an upcycle (scenarios cultivate customers, customers cultivate compute) and a burden in a downcycle, when all three lines need money at once.This quarter is a downcycle sample: cash from e-commerce was drained to subsidize instant retail, drained again to build data centers, and finally had to be backfilled through a placement.

Moat breakdown: where the moat is widening, where it is narrowing

Two layers thickening: cloud and chips. Alibaba Cloud's 38.1% share of China's AI cloud market and Zhenwu M890's 650 external customers should thicken over time, because compute scale effects are cumulative: more customers mean lower unit costs, more room to undercut on price and win share. This is the only flywheel that can make Alibaba "stronger the more it does."

Two layers thinning: transaction mindshare and network effects. 64 million 88VIP members are an asset, but flat customer management revenue shows "members are there, ad spend is not." A moat's width is ultimately tested by whether you can raise prices — and Taobao Tmall cannot raise them now. Pinduoduo's low prices and Douyin's content-driven arena have turned "you must come to Taobao to buy" into "you can compare everywhere."

Two layers undecided: instant retail and switching costs. Taobao Shangou's roughly 41% share of order volume was bought with subsidies; how much survives once subsidies stop, no one knows. Multi-platform operation by merchants has become the norm, and switching costs are falling. If these two layers keep weakening, Alibaba degrades into "a company that sells compute + a mall that cannot raise prices."

The most dangerous rival: ByteDance

Meituan competes only in instant retail, Pinduoduo only in low-price e-commerce, and Tencent overlaps in cloud but not in scenarios.ByteDance is the only rival eroding Alibaba on two fronts at once. Douyin e-commerce siphons off transactions and ad budgets—visible directly in flat customer management revenue; Volcano Engine siphons off AI compute demand—the key variable determining whether cloud growth can hold above 40%. Douyin in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion in 2025, up nearly +60% YoY, demonstrating its ability to convert traffic into transactions.

ByteDance's edge is that "traffic does not need to be bought": Douyin's daily active users are themselves a distribution channel, with marginal customer acquisition cost near zero. Alibaba's handicap is that "traffic must be bought": Taobao's customer acquisition depends on subsidies, on 88VIP benefits and on the fulfilment experience of instant retail.For the same users, ByteDance's cost structure sits a tier below Alibaba's.

The second most dangerous rival is Alibaba itself.Capex of RMB 67.68 billion per quarter is its only way to counter ByteDance, and also its biggest source of risk. If cloud revenue growth falls to 25% in 2027, this RMB 380 billion of investment turns from a "moat" into a "depreciation burden"—self-inflicted damage more fatal than any rival.

Durability of the moat: which one lasts a decade

Apply one yardstick to everything: "will it still be here in ten years?" Cloud and chips: most likely yes (compute demand is a long-term trend and scale effects entrench the competitive landscape); transaction mindshare: most likely yes, but it will thin (Taobao will not disappear, but it becomes a public utility that cannot raise prices); instant retail share: uncertain (hinges on retention once subsidies end); AI application gateway position: least certain (Qwen must fight Doubao, DeepSeek and Yuanbao for users, and no player holds a clear advantage).

That is why the valuation uses SOTP rather than a blended multiple: durability differs threefold across assets, and forcing one multiple onto them is bound to be wrong. E-commerce at 9x, cloud at 6x PS, AI applications at a negative value: in essence, this prices the difference in durability.

Conclusion

Moat conclusion: the two hard moats (cloud, chips) are thickening, the two soft moats (transactional mindshare, network effects) are thinning, and one (instant retail) is being offset by subsidies before it is fully built. That combination warrants a sum-of-the-parts valuation, with each piece priced separately rather than through a blended multiple. ByteDance is the most dangerous competitor; the company's own capex cadence is the second.
