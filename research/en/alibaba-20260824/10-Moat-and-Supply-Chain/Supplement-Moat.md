## Position in the supply chain: three roles within the chain

Alibaba is the "landlord" of the e-commerce chain: customer management revenue of RMB 82.55 billion, gross margin above 70%, with merchants paying advertising fees and commissions in exchange for traffic. It is the "wholesaler" of the AI compute chain: RMB 48.44 billion in revenue, 11.6% EBITA margin, buying chips and selling compute. It is the "fulfillment provider" of the instant retail chain: RMB 53.3 billion in revenue, 10%–15% gross margin, using riders and front warehouses to deliver goods to customers' doors.**Three identities, three business models, three competitive logics.**

These multiple identities are synergistic in an upcycle (scenarios cultivate customers, customers cultivate compute) and a burden in a downcycle (all three lines need money at once).**This quarter is a downcycle sample: cash from e-commerce was drained to subsidize instant retail, then drained again to build data centers, and finally had to be backfilled through a placement.**

## Moat breakdown: which ones are thickening, which are thinning

**Two layers thickening: cloud and chips.** Alibaba Cloud's 38.1% share of China's AI cloud market and Zhenwu M890's 650 external customers will thicken over time — because the scale effect of compute is cumulative: the more customers, the lower the unit cost, and the more room to undercut on price and win customers. This is the only flywheel that can make Alibaba "stronger the more it does."

**Two layers thinning: transaction mindshare and network effects.** 64 million 88VIP members are an asset, but zero growth in customer management revenue shows "members are there, ad spend is not." **The width of a moat is ultimately tested by "can you raise prices" — and Taobao Tmall cannot raise them now.** Pinduoduo's low prices and Douyin's content-driven arena have turned "you must come to Taobao to buy" into "you can compare everywhere."

**Two layers undecided: instant retail and switching costs.** Taobao Shangou's roughly 41% share of order volume was bought with subsidies; how much remains after subsidies stop, no one knows. Multi-platform operation by merchants has become the norm, and switching costs are falling. **If these two layers keep weakening, Alibaba degrades into "a company that sells compute + a mall that cannot raise prices."**

## The most dangerous rival: ByteDance

Meituan only captures instant retail, Pinduoduo only captures low-price e-commerce, and Tencent is a comparable in cloud but its scenarios do not conflict.**ByteDance is the only rival weakening Alibaba on two fronts at once.** Douyin e-commerce takes away transactions and ad budgets—directly reflected in zero growth in customer management revenue; Volcano Engine takes away AI compute demand—the key variable determining whether cloud growth can sustain 40%+. Douyin in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion in 2025, up nearly +60% year on year, showing it can convert traffic into transactions.

ByteDance's advantage is that "traffic does not need to be bought": Douyin's daily active users are themselves a distribution channel, with marginal customer acquisition cost near zero. Alibaba's disadvantage is that "traffic must be bought": Taobao's customer acquisition depends on subsidies, on 88VIP benefits, and on the fulfillment experience of instant retail.**For the same users, ByteDance's cost structure is a tier lower than Alibaba's.**

The second most dangerous rival is Alibaba itself.**Capex of RMB 67.68 billion per quarter is its only way to counter ByteDance, and also its biggest source of risk.**If cloud revenue growth falls to 25% in 2027, this RMB 380 billion of investment will turn from a "moat" into a "depreciation burden"—this kind of self-harm is more fatal than any rival.

## Durability of the moat: which one lasts a decade

Measure everything against one yardstick: "will it still be here in ten years?" **Cloud and chips: most likely yes** (compute demand is a long-term trend, and scale effects entrench the competitive landscape); **transaction mindshare: most likely yes, but it will thin** (Taobao will not disappear, but it will become a public utility that cannot raise prices); **instant retail share: uncertain** (hinges on retention after subsidies end); **AI application gateway position: least certain** (Qwen must fight Doubao, DeepSeek and Yuanbao for users, and no player holds a clear advantage).

This is why the valuation uses SOTP rather than a blended multiple: **durability differs threefold across assets, and forcing one multiple onto them is bound to be wrong.** E-commerce at 9x, cloud at 6x PS, AI applications at a negative value: in essence, this prices the difference in durability.
