Core view

Alibaba just posted its fastest revenue growth in nearly four years (+9%) alongside its ugliest net profit print (-76%). Both stem from the same cause: cash pulled out of e-commerce and ploughed into cloud and AI. Revenue therefore carries 45% cloud growth, while profit is RMB 33.36 billion lighter.

The number to watch is not net profit but three structural metrics: cloud segment margin of 11.6% (double-digit for the first time), zero growth in customer management revenue (-7% as reported), and free cash flow of -RMB 44.67 billion (capex of RMB 67.68 billion). The first anchors the valuation, the second is the thermometer for the core business, and the third explains the capital raise.

Layer one: revenue mix — growth has a new engine

Breaking down the 9% growth, only two engines contributed: instant retail +45% (RMB 53.30 billion) and AI cloud and computing power +45% (RMB 48.44 billion). Together they added roughly RMB 29 billion of incremental revenue, almost equal to the group's entire increment. The core is retreating: China e-commerce RMB 110.90 billion, -8%, of which customer management RMB 82.55 billion, -7%; international e-commerce RMB 27.76 billion, -1%.

Customer management is the foundation of Alibaba's profit, and its weakening for several consecutive quarters shows merchant ad budgets on Taobao and Tmall have not returned. Management offered another measure: "customer management revenue +1% YoY on a like-for-like basis" — the 8-percentage-point gap between the two figures comes from the 2025 business reclassification. Either way, neither measure supports an "e-commerce recovery."

A notable structural shift: instant retail at RMB 53.30 billion has nearly doubled international e-commerce's RMB 27.76 billion, becoming the second-largest revenue line within the e-commerce group. Gross margin here sits far below platform commissions, so "better revenue mix, worse profit mix" is playing out at the same time.

Second layer: cloud — margins and growth rising together

Revenue of RMB 48.44 billion, +45%, the fastest growth in 22 quarters, with AI-related products contributing RMB 12.376 billion and growing triple digits for 12 consecutive quarters. The top line itself is unremarkable; the margin is what stands out: adjusted EBITA of RMB 5.628 billion, +133% YoY, with margin expanding from 7.2% to 11.6%.

Asset-heavy businesses usually face a binary choice: either trade price for growth and cede margin, or raise prices to protect profit and slow growth. Alibaba did both this time, for three reasons: ① depreciation on the previous round of GPU purchases has not yet fully run through, so the cost side has yet to pay the bill; ② compute supply and demand are tight, the industry is scrambling for capacity and pricing is firm; ③ in-house chips lower costs — T-Head's Zhenwu M890 already serves more than 650 external customers, displacing some high-priced external procurement.

All three have a shelf life: depreciation will arrive, supply will loosen and export controls will change. So 11.6% is not a steady state but a window-period peak. Whether the story holds depends on whether margin keeps rising or rolls over in the next two quarters — if Q2 and Q3 still hold above 12%, scale effects are real; if margin falls back to 9%, it was an illusion ahead of depreciation.

Layer three: where the profit went — breaking down the RMB 33.36 billion

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell from RMB 43.90 billion to RMB 10.54 billion, a decline of RMB 33.36 billion. Item by item: ① AI lab and applications lost roughly RMB 10.6 billion (a loss of RMB 13.86 billion this quarter vs about RMB 3.2 billion in the same period last year); ② All other segments swung from a profit of RMB 690 million to a loss of RMB 3.34 billion, a swing of RMB 4.0 billion; ③ Goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion; ④ EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion; ⑤ Interest and investment income fell from RMB 17.38 billion to RMB 9.00 billion, down RMB 8.38 billion; ⑥ Effective tax rate jumped from 17.6% to 57.4%, adding roughly RMB 6 billion in tax.

The six items sum to about RMB 37.8 billion, more than the RMB 33.36 billion decline, with the difference partly offset by resilient e-commerce EBITA and incremental cloud EBITA (+RMB 3.2 billion). Key conclusion: only item ①, at RMB 10.6 billion, represents genuine operating deterioration; the other five are one-off, definitional or accounting-related.

This also explains why Non-GAAP net profit of RMB 20.72 billion fell only 38% YoY while GAAP fell 76% — the 38-percentage-point gap reflects the combined impact of impairments, fines, the tax rate and investment income. Which number you use to look at the company determines the conclusion you reach. Our approach is to look at both: Non-GAAP to judge operating trends, GAAP to judge true returns.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — how deep is the hole

Operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion, +11% YoY, shows cash generation remains intact; capex of RMB 67.68 billion, +75% YoY, shows investment is accelerating. Net the two and free cash flow is negative RMB 44.67 billion, with the shortfall 1.4x wider YoY (prior-year period: negative RMB 18.82 billion).

The balance sheet still holds up, with cash and other current investments of RMB 474.505 billion (2026/6/30). But the "net cash company" label slipped this quarter: on a narrow basis (cash and investments of RMB 316.8 billion less interest-bearing debt of RMB 281.7 billion, 2026/3/31), net cash was just RMB 35.1 billion, down 80% from RMB 179.3 billion a year earlier. That is the financial backdrop to the HKD 80 billion placement: not a shortage of money, but a reluctance to plug this hole with operating cash flow.

A stress test: at this quarter's shortfall pace of RMB 44.7 billion, placement net proceeds of HKD 79.7 billion (about RMB 73.3 billion) cover only 1.6 quarters. If FY2027 capex holds this pace (RMB 270 billion annualized), the full-year shortfall is RMB 150–180 billion. Conclusion: either operating cash flow accelerates (cloud starts monetizing, instant retail narrows losses) or capex decelerates — otherwise a second financing is only a matter of time.

The three things to watch most closely

① Cloud EBITA margin. At 11.6%, this is the valuation anchor for the story: below 9%, cloud de-rates from 6x PS to 4x and the target price falls by HKD 19 outright. ② Sequential direction of AI lab and application losses. RMB 13.86 billion, loss ratio -415%; any sequential narrowing prompts the market to reprice the application layer, while continued expansion means another valuation cut. ③ The lag between capex and depreciation. RMB 67.68 billion in a single quarter, with depreciation amortised back through the income statement over the following 3–5 years; the current 11.6% margin is propped up by depreciation that "hasn't started yet."

All three numbers can be verified next quarter (FY2027Q2, expected in November), so there is no need to wait a year. This is not a company that "can't be seen clearly" — it is one that "can be seen clearly but requires two quarters of waiting."

Conclusion

Read this earnings report on three levels: revenue is the healthiest line (+9%, cloud +45%), profit the ugliest (-76%), and cash flow the tightest (RMB -44.67 billion). All three are facets of a single move—shifting e-commerce cash flow into building computing power. Two tests will settle whether the bet is right: does the cloud margin hold above 12%, and when does capex peak. If both trend favourably, this is a good investment; if either deteriorates, it is a heavy-asset trap.
