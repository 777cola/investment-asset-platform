## Layer one: revenue mix — growth has a new engine

Breaking down the 9% growth, only two engines contributed: **instant retail +45% (RMB 53.30 billion) and AI cloud and computing power +45% (RMB 48.44 billion)**. Together they contributed about RMB 29 billion of incremental revenue, almost equal to the entire group's increment. The core business is retreating: China e-commerce RMB 110.90 billion, -8%, of which customer management RMB 82.55 billion, -7%; international e-commerce RMB 27.76 billion, -1%.

Customer management is the foundation of Alibaba's profit. Its weakening for several consecutive quarters shows that merchants' ad budgets on Taobao and Tmall have not returned. The company offered another measure: "customer management revenue +1% year on year on a like-for-like basis" — **the 8-percentage-point gap between the two figures comes from the 2025 business reclassification. Either way, neither measure supports an "e-commerce recovery."**

There is a notable structural shift: instant retail at RMB 53.30 billion has nearly doubled international e-commerce's RMB 27.76 billion, becoming the second-largest revenue line within the e-commerce group. This business carries a gross margin far below platform commissions, so "better revenue mix, worse profit mix" is happening at the same time.

## Second layer: cloud — margins and growth rising together

Revenue of RMB 48.44 billion, +45%, the fastest growth in 22 quarters; AI-related products accounted for RMB 12.376 billion, growing triple digits for 12 consecutive quarters. The numbers themselves are unremarkable; what is remarkable is the margin over the same period: **adjusted EBITA of RMB 5.628 billion, +133% YoY, with margin rising from 7.2% to 11.6%.**

Asset-heavy businesses usually face a binary choice: either trade price for growth (margin falls) or raise prices to protect profit (growth slows). Alibaba achieved both this time, for three reasons: **① depreciation on the previous round of GPU purchases has not yet fully hit**, so the cost side has not started paying the bill; **② compute supply and demand are tight**, the industry is scrambling for capacity, and pricing is firm; **③ in-house chips lower costs** — T-Head's Zhenwu M890 already serves more than 650 external customers, replacing some high-priced external procurement.

All three reasons have a shelf life: depreciation will arrive, supply will loosen, and export controls will change. **So 11.6% is not a steady state but a window-period peak.** Whether this story can persist depends on whether the margin keeps rising or rolls over in the next two quarters — if Q2 and Q3 still hold above 12%, scale effects are real; if it falls back to 9%, it was an illusion before depreciation.

## Layer three: where the profit went — breaking down RMB 33.36 billion

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell from RMB 43.90 billion to RMB 10.54 billion, a decline of RMB 33.36 billion. Breaking it down item by item: **① AI lab and applications lost roughly RMB 10.6 billion** (a loss of RMB 13.86 billion this quarter vs about RMB 3.2 billion in the same period last year); **② All others swung from a profit of RMB 690 million to a loss of RMB 3.34 billion**, a gap of RMB 4.0 billion; **③ Goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion**; **④ EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion**; **⑤ Interest and investment income fell from RMB 17.38 billion to RMB 9.00 billion**, down RMB 8.38 billion; **⑥ Effective tax rate jumped from 17.6% to 57.4%**, adding roughly RMB 6 billion in tax.

The six items sum to about RMB 37.8 billion, more than the RMB 33.36 billion decline, with the difference partly offset by the resilience of e-commerce EBITA and the incremental cloud EBITA (+RMB 3.2 billion). **Key conclusion: only item ①, at RMB 10.6 billion, represents genuine "operating deterioration"; the other five are either one-off, definitional, or accounting-related.**

This also explains why Non-GAAP net profit of RMB 20.72 billion fell only 38% year on year while GAAP fell 76% — the 38-percentage-point gap is the combined impact of impairments, fines, tax rate and investment income. **Which number you use to look at the company determines what conclusion you reach. My approach is to look at both: Non-GAAP to judge operating trends, GAAP to judge true returns.**

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — how deep is the hole

Operating cash flow RMB 22.95 billion, +11% YoY — cash generation is intact; capex RMB 67.68 billion, +75% YoY — investment is accelerating. **Net the two and free cash flow is negative RMB 44.67 billion, with the shortfall 1.4x wider YoY (prior-year period: negative RMB 18.82 billion).**

The balance sheet still holds up: cash and other current investments of RMB 474.505 billion (2026/6/30). But the "net cash company" label slipped this quarter — on a narrow basis (cash and investments RMB 316.8 billion minus interest-bearing debt RMB 281.7 billion, 2026/3/31), net cash was only RMB 35.1 billion, down 80% from RMB 179.3 billion a year earlier. **That is the financial backdrop to the HKD 80 billion placement: not a shortage of money, but a reluctance to plug this hole with operating cash flow.**

A stress test: at this quarter's shortfall pace of RMB 44.7 billion, placement net proceeds of HKD 79.7 billion (about RMB 73.3 billion) cover only 1.6 quarters. If FY2027 capex holds this pace (RMB 270 billion annualized), the full-year shortfall is RMB 150–180 billion. **Conclusion: either operating cash flow accelerates (cloud starts monetizing, instant retail narrows losses) or capex decelerates — otherwise a second financing is only a matter of time.**

## The three things to watch most closely

**① Cloud segment EBITA margin.** 11.6% is the valuation anchor for this story; if it falls back below 9%, cloud de-rates from 6x PS to 4x, and the target price drops by HKD 19 outright. **② The quarter-on-quarter direction of AI lab and application losses.** RMB 13.86 billion, loss ratio -415%; any sequential narrowing will prompt the market to reprice the application layer; continued expansion means another valuation cut. **③ The lag between capex and depreciation.** RMB 67.68 billion in a single quarter; depreciation will be amortised back through the income statement over the following 3–5 years; the current 11.6% margin is propped up by depreciation that "hasn't started yet."

All three numbers can be verified next quarter (FY2027Q2, expected in November); no need to wait a year. **So this is not a company that "can't be seen clearly" — it is one that "can be seen clearly but requires two quarters of waiting."**
