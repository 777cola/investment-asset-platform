Core view

Condense the previous seven notes onto one page: what to buy (cloud + e-commerce), at what price (HKD 95, or on the appearance of evidence), how much (three tranches, 10% cap), and when to admit error (three falsification lines). There is no fifth item.

What to buy: only buy the two-thirds you understand

SOTP breakdown: e-commerce RMB 1.43 trillion (59%), cloud RMB 1.16 trillion (48%), AI applications -RMB 0.17 trillion (-7%). In other words, of what you pay, 59% buys e-commerce, 48% buys cloud, and -7% funds the losses of AI applications.

This structure defines the nature of the investment: you are not betting on AI; you are buying e-commerce cash flow plus a fast-growing cloud at a cheap price, and picking up the option cost of an AI application along the way. If the AI application ultimately succeeds, it comes free; if it fails, the cost is already in the price.

The benefit of this framework: you can decide whether to buy without a view on AI. You only need to judge two things — whether e-commerce's 19.3% margin holds, and whether cloud's 45% growth sustains.

At what price to buy: two triggers, no buy otherwise

Trigger one (price): HKD 95 or below. That is a 26% discount to neutral value and sits above the bear case of HKD 88. With HKD 34 of upside against HKD 7 of downside, the payoff is 5:1; even on a 33% win rate, expected value is +7.2%.

Trigger two (evidence): cloud segment EBITA margin of 12% or above for two consecutive quarters alongside growth of 40% or more, and the AI application's quarterly loss narrowing sequentially. The price may by then be above HKD 130, but the win rate rises from 33% to over 60%, making a 1:1.5 payoff acceptable.

If neither is met, wait. At the current HKD 112.5, the price is not low enough (1:1.5 payoff) and the evidence is not sufficient (one quarter). That is the entirety of "watch."

How much to buy: three tranches, cap 10%

Build 3% at HKD 95, scale to 6% at HKD 88 and to 8% once evidence emerges; cap at 10% on the dual trigger (price + evidence). Investors already holding at a cost above HKD 130 should cut to below 5% — not because the company has deteriorated, but because a heavy loss position leads to bad decisions.

Why cap at 10%? Cloud growth and margin are the only key variables here, so concentration risk is inherently high; add placement, dilution and policy uncertainty, and anything above 10% pushes portfolio volatility to an uncomfortable level. Correlation with Tencent, Meituan, JD and Pinduoduo is above 0.7 — holding them together requires a lower cap.

When to admit error: three falsification lines

① Cloud revenue growth <30% YoY; ② Cloud EBITA margin <9%; ③ Capex up QoQ for three consecutive quarters with the free cash flow gap wider than -RMB 50 billion. Any one of these triggers liquidation, regardless of cost basis.

Why no price stop-loss? Because the fall from 185 to 90 re-rated the valuation framework, not earnings; a price stop would simply be whipsawed repeatedly by such regime shifts. A condition-based stop is an admission that "I was wrong on fundamentals" — the only stop that carries meaning.

One final discipline: do not revise the judgment because the stock rebounds, and do not liquidate in anger over a placement. The data lands in November; the moves in the intervening two months are noise. I set this down in writing because I know I will make mistakes — committing the discipline to paper in advance is the only way to stop emotion rewriting it midway.

Conclusion

To close the decision framework: two triggers, three position tiers, one falsification line. At HKD 112.5, do nothing—wait for HKD 95 or wait for evidence. Not buying for a year is also right—the goal of investing is to make money, not to hold a position.
