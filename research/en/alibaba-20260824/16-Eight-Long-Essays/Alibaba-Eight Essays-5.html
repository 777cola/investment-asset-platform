Industry and competition: two battlegrounds, one absent winner-take-all — Alibaba

Core view

Alibaba ranks first or in the top tier in both battlegrounds, but neither battleground has a "winner-takes-all" structure—which means it will deliver decent growth, mediocre margins, and perpetual price wars.

E-commerce: a share fight among three players in a mature market

Incremental growth in Chinese e-commerce is over. Taobao Tmall, Pinduoduo and Douyin e-commerce split the market, each with a clear position: Taobao is "the mall that has everything" (88VIP 64 million), Pinduoduo is "the cheapest bazaar" (extreme low prices), Douyin is "the content venue for watching while buying" (GTV expanding rapidly).

The defining trait of a stock game is "whoever cuts prices loses profit, whoever doesn't loses share." Alibaba chose the middle path: no price increase in customer management (same-basis +1%), retaining users through instant retail and the AI shopping assistant. The result is revenue intact, profit compressed.

This structure will not change within two years. Implication for valuation: 9x EBITA for e-commerce is a reasonable ceiling; do not expect a return to the 20x PE of 2020.

AI cloud: an incremental market, but the increment has a shelf life

China's AI cloud is a growth market, and Alibaba Cloud ranks first with a 38.1% share. But three traits keep it from being a "winner-takes-all" market: customer concentration (large-model companies, internet platforms and government/enterprise clients all wield strong bargaining power), product homogeneity (compute has no brand; competition is on price and supply stability), and no ceiling on capex (anyone can buy cards; the only difference is how many they can afford).

Alibaba's differentiation lies in chips and models: T-Head's Zhenwu M890 (650+ external customers) plus the Tongyi open-source ecosystem (3 billion downloads, 300,000 derivative models). These two let Alibaba take more orders than peers in an environment where cards cannot be bought.

But the shelf life of growth depends on supply: as industry-wide capacity is released in 2027–2028, prices will soften. Cloud will then shift from "competing for capacity" to "competing for customers," with margin and growth under pressure at the same time—the most certain endpoint of this story.

Competitor: ByteDance is the most dangerous one

Meituan only contests instant retail (52% share), Pinduoduo only contests low-price e-commerce (market cap about RMB 839.9 billion), and Tencent is a comparable but non-conflicting player in cloud (capex RMB 52.8 billion, +176%). ByteDance is weakening Alibaba on two fronts at once: Douyin e-commerce siphons off transactions and advertising budgets, while Volcano Engine siphons off AI compute demand.

ByteDance has a better cost structure: it does not need to buy traffic (Douyin's daily active users are the channel), and marginal customer acquisition cost is near zero. Alibaba has to buy traffic (subsidies, 88VIP benefits, instant retail fulfillment). For the same user, ByteDance is a tier cheaper.

But ByteDance has soft spots: no in-house chips (it relies on external sourcing), no closed loop of payments and logistics, and weaker e-commerce fulfillment than Alibaba. The two are locked in a standoff of "strong traffic vs strong supply chain," and neither can knock the other out.

The impact of market structure on margins: a matter of arithmetic

Three players splitting the market + no winner-take-all = industry margins pinned slightly above the average line. Alibaba's group adjusted EBITA margin was 10.2% (273.0 ÷ 2,689.5), JD.com 1.3%, Meituan loss-making for the period, Pinduoduo 20%+ (extreme efficiency). Alibaba sits in the middle: better than JD.com, worse than Pinduoduo.

What this position means: it carries neither JD.com's heavy-asset burden nor Pinduoduo's extreme efficiency — it has a bit of everything and nothing at the extreme. Such a company keeps pace when the industry rises and is squeezed first when it falls.

So the valuation requirement for it should be higher, not lower: mediocre margins must be compensated for in the price. The current price of HKD 112.5 offers a 13% discount — insufficient compensation. That is the industry reason this is a "watch" rather than a "buy".

Conclusion

Industry conclusion: Alibaba is in the first tier in both battlegrounds, and neither battleground has a winner-takes-all outcome. This determines that Alibaba's revenue can grow, but its margin cannot. The appropriate valuation for this structure is "sum-of-the-parts, low multiples, with a discount retained," not "whole-company, high multiples, storytelling."
