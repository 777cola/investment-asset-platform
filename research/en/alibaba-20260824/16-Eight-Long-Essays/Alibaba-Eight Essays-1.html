Cognitive reset: Alibaba is no longer an e-commerce company — Alibaba

Part One · Out of the old labels

Core view

Most people still picture Alibaba as an "e-commerce platform" — Taobao, Tmall, Double 11, commissions and advertising. That picture was already outdated by 2026. From this quarter, Alibaba reports four segments: e-commerce group, AI cloud and computing power services, AI lab and applications, and all others. This is no accounting exercise; it is an admission that its second leg has grown strong enough to stand on its own.

The Cost of Legacy Labels

Look at Alibaba through the "e-commerce company" lens and the HKD 112.5 price of August 24, 2026 reads as "a retail company with stalled growth": FY2026 revenue up just 2.74%, customer management revenue flat, instant retail propped up by subsidies, net profit -76%. On that framework, even 20x P/E looks expensive.

Through the "compute infrastructure + e-commerce cash flow" lens, the same numbers tell a different story: cloud revenue RMB 48.44 billion, +45%, a 22-quarter high; segment EBITA RMB 5.63 billion, +133%; margin up from 7.2% to 11.6%. That business runs at an annualized RMB 193.7 billion — enough on its own to rank in the Hong Kong top twenty.

The two frameworks produce valuations more than a multiple apart. The old one gives HKD 90 (the June 25, 2026 low of 90.05 was its product); the new one gives HKD 129. At HKD 112.5 the price sits right in between — that is the real price of a "cognitive switch": half the market still runs the old framework, half has already switched.

Three outdated reference points that must be replaced

Old anchor one: net cash company. Buying Alibaba used to be as much about its RMB 300 billion-scale net cash as about the business itself. Narrow-basis net cash now stands at just RMB 35.1 billion versus RMB 179.3 billion a year ago — the "floor" is 80% lower. Where did the money go? Into GPUs in the data centers.

Old anchor two: buybacks and cancellation. In 2024–2025 Alibaba was one of Hong Kong's most active buyback names, and that underpinned the valuation. August 2026 marked a reversal: a 710 million share placement, 3.47% dilution. From "reducing share count" to "increasing share count" — a directional shift.

Old anchor three: asset-light, high gross margin. FY2026 gross margin of 39.8% still holds, but capex as a share of revenue has jumped from single digits to 25.2%. A company spending 25 yuan on equipment for every 100 yuan of revenue can no longer command an "asset-light platform" multiple.

The three pillars of the new framework

Pillar one: Cloud is a growth asset. Revenue of RMB 48.44 billion, +45%; AI-related products RMB 12.376 billion, with triple-digit growth for 12 consecutive quarters; customers span internet, financial services, autonomous driving, and government and enterprise. The ceiling here is not "Chinese e-commerce ad budgets" but "Chinese enterprise compute budgets" — and the latter has far more room to grow.

Pillar two: E-commerce is a cash cow, not a growth stock. Revenue of RMB 205.86 billion, EBITA of RMB 39.75 billion, 19.3% margin. Its proper role is to fund cloud and AI, not to grow itself again.Pricing it at 9x EBITA rather than 20x P/E is the right approach.

Pillar three: AI applications are an option, not an asset. Revenue of RMB 3.34 billion, loss of RMB 13.86 billion. The value lies in the entry point Alibaba gains if it works; the cost is RMB 55.4 billion of cash a year.An option must be priced as an option: assign negative or zero value, not a positive number.

The three pillars combine into an SOTP: 1.43 + 1.16 - 0.17 = RMB 2.42 trillion, or HKD 129.The method is imperfect, but it is the only one that can hold both "good assets" and "bad cash flow" at the same time.

Conclusion

The cognitive reset is complete: Alibaba has already changed from "an e-commerce company" into "a company that uses e-commerce cash flow to build computing-power infrastructure." The former is valued on P/E; the latter on SOTP and the capex cycle. At HKD 112.5, the stock is priced in the transition between the two frameworks — to make this money, you must believe in the new framework, and wait until a second quarter of data confirms it.
