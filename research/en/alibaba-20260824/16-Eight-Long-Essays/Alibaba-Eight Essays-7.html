Risk list: five risks, one downside floor — Alibaba

Part Seven · How Much Could Be Lost
Research date 2026/08/24  ·  Price HK$112.50 (-8.54%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 2.30 trillion (approximately 20.46 billion shares post-placement)  ·  PE (TTM, back-solved) ~31x (earnings distorted by AI investment; limited read-across)

Core view

Five risks, ranked by impact on per-share value: capex stall (-27%), cloud growth deceleration (-17%), e-commerce monetisation rate (-13%), AI application deterioration (-11%), regulation and geopolitics (-10% to -25%). All five are real, but none implies the company is collapsing.

Risk 1: return on RMB 380 billion

RMB 190 billion has already been invested, cloud annualised EBITA stands at RMB 22.5 billion and the static return is 11.8%. That number rests on one condition: compute prices do not fall. Yet the whole industry is adding capacity — Tencent spent RMB 52.8 billion in Q2, up 176%, and the four major North American cloud providers raised capex — and with supply concentrated in 2027–2028, prices will most likely decline.

A 20% price decline takes the cloud EBITA margin from 11.6% to below 9% and cloud from 6x PS to 4x, shaving HKD 19 per share (-17%). If growth also slows to 25%, cloud valuation drops from RMB 1.16 trillion to RMB 0.74 trillion, shaving more than HKD 19 per share.

The depreciation lag is worse: RMB 67.68 billion of quarterly investment amortised over 5 years adds roughly RMB 54 billion in annualised depreciation. The result is "revenue rising, profit eaten by depreciation" — the most painful phase of a capital-intensive business.

Risk 2 and Risk 3: Core business and experimental layer

E-commerce monetisation rate: customer management revenue -7% reported, +1% like-for-like. Two consecutive quarters of negative growth would drag e-commerce EBITA from an annualised RMB 159 billion toward RMB 140 billion, compressing the multiple from 9x to 7x and cutting value by HKD 15 per share (-13%). This is the highest-probability risk, given persistent pressure from Douyin and Pinduoduo.

AI applications: loss of RMB 13.86 billion, loss ratio -415%. A quarterly loss above RMB 16 billion would prompt the market to reclassify the segment from "investment" to "expense," shifting the valuation from -3x to -5x losses and cutting value by HKD 12 per share (-11%). This risk carries the least information — management has given no path to narrowing losses.

Together the two total -24%, implying a share price near HKD 86, close to the bear case of HKD 88. This is no "black swan" — it is where the stock lands if baseline assumptions are slightly off.

Risk 4 and Risk 5: Governance and geopolitics

Governance: The HKD 80 billion placement is not a risk in itself (3.47% dilution, 3x oversubscribed). The risk is the precedent: a second placement would prompt the market to apply a "habitual equity issuer" discount and de-rate the valuation permanently. The trigger to watch is clear: does the company raise again the next time capex exceeds expectations.

Geopolitics: The roughly RMB 4.3 billion EU fine provision is already booked. Export controls constrain chip supply—paradoxically a "can still buy" moat for Alibaba, though a double-edged sword. ADR policy risk persists long term but is not the main thread near term.

Tail risk needs a number: if all five occur simultaneously (cloud at 3x PS + e-commerce at 7x + AI applications at 2x losses), the total is RMB 1.45 trillion, or HKD 71 per share—37% downside, with probability below 10%.

The significance of the lower bound: why not "avoid"

All five risks are real, but none points to a balance-sheet collapse: cash and liquid investments of RMB 474.51 billion, positive operating cash flow (+11%) and a debt structure under no strain. This is a company that may earn less, not one that may go to zero.

That caps the rating at Watch, not Avoid. Avoid is reserved for companies whose business model has been disproven, that are burning cash and facing maturing debt; Alibaba fails all three tests.

The downside discipline: at the current price the worst case is a 37% loss, at HKD 95 it is 25% and at HKD 80 it is 11%. The lower the entry price, the smaller the risk — waiting for a pullback is not timidity, it is risk sizing.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: five risks, one floor (HKD 71 extreme case, HKD 88 bear case). What these risks have in common is three to five years of profit compression, not collapse. The answer is therefore to watch and wait for price, not to avoid.
