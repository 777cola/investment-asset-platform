Financial reality: a clean but weak balance sheet, alongside a stretched cash flow statement — Alibaba

Part Four · The Moves Behind the Numbers

Core view

Read this earnings report as two statements, two stories. The income statement tells a story of deliberate spending: net profit fell 76%, but only 38% excluding one-off items. The cash flow statement tells a story of not enough money: free cash flow was negative RMB 44.67 billion, plugged by an HKD 80 billion placement. Only the two statements together capture the company's true state today.

Income statement: unpacking that RMB 33.36 billion

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell from RMB 43.90 billion to RMB 10.54 billion, a decline of RMB 33.36 billion. The six-part breakdown: AI applications lost roughly RMB 10.6 billion more; all other segments swung from profit to loss, a RMB 4.0 billion gap; goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion; an EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion; investment income down RMB 8.38 billion; and the effective tax rate jumping from 17.6% to 57.4%, adding roughly RMB 6.0 billion in tax.

Together the six items total about RMB 37.8 billion, more than the decline itself, with e-commerce resilience and incremental cloud contribution (EBITA +RMB 3.2 billion) offsetting the difference. Only the RMB 10.6 billion of AI investment reflects genuine operating deterioration; the other five items will not recur next year.

That is why Non-GAAP net profit fell only 38%, to RMB 20.72 billion: strip out impairment, fines, tax-rate effects and investment-income noise, and what remains is a single action — deliberate investment in AI. Use Non-GAAP to read operations and GAAP to read returns; both are valid, but the two cannot be mixed.

Cash flow: a chain of three numbers

229.5 (operating cash flow) − 676.8 (capex) = -446.7 (free cash flow). These three numbers form the causal chain running through the entire earnings report: cash generation keeps growing (+11%), investment doubles (+75%), and the gap widens 1.4x.

How to close it? Three paths: slow capex, accelerate operating cash flow, external financing. The company chose the third, because the first two would hurt growth—slowing capex would drag on cloud growth, while accelerating cash flow would require narrowing losses in instant retail, which is not achievable in the short term. Financing is the least painful option, and the one that shifts risk to shareholders.

Stress test: at this quarter's gap pace of RMB 44.7 billion, the net placement proceeds of HKD 79.7 billion (about RMB 73.3 billion) last 1.6 quarters. If that pace holds for the full year (annualised capex of RMB 270 billion), the full-year gap is RMB 150–180 billion. Conclusion: this placement is not "icing on the cake"—it is "necessary but insufficient."

Balance sheet: the floor is 80% lower

Cash and other current investments totalled RMB 474.51 billion on the broad measure (2026/6/30). On the narrow measure — cash and investments of RMB 316.8 billion less interest-bearing debt of RMB 281.7 billion (2026/3/31) — just RMB 35.1 billion, against RMB 179.3 billion a year earlier. The RMB 155.0 billion gap hinges on whether "current investments" are counted.

Both measures are valid and serve different purposes: use the broad measure (RMB 474.51 billion) for solvency and the narrow measure (RMB 35.1 billion) for margin of safety. We use the latter for valuation because it is more conservative.

The "net cash company" label matters because, in the worst case, the company does not need to ask anyone for help. That attribute has now weakened — it remains "a company with money", but is no longer "a company that does not lack money." That distinction explains why the placement could be priced at an 8.4% discount and still be 3x oversubscribed: institutions know it needs money, and they also know it will not collapse.

Conclusion

The financial picture in one sentence: the income statement reflects deliberate spending, the cash flow statement reflects a funding shortfall, and the balance sheet reflects a falling floor. All three point to the same conclusion: the assets are not impaired, the valuation framework must change, and the price must be lower.
