Moat anatomy: three thickening, two thinning — Alibaba

Core view

A moat is not "the company is impressive"; it is "no matter how much a rival spends, it cannot catch up." Measured against that yardstick, Alibaba's five moats stack up as follows: cloud and compute are thickening, in-house chips are thickening, transaction mindshare is thinning, network effects are thinning, and instant retail has yet to be rebuilt.

The first thickening point: cloud and computing power

This moat rests on "scale effect + switching costs." Scale effect: the larger the customer base, the lower the unit compute cost, and the more room Alibaba Cloud has to undercut rivals on price to win share. Switching costs: enterprises run their data, models and orchestration software on Alibaba Cloud, and migrating means a "rewrite—test—downtime" cycle that takes 3–6 months for mid-sized customers.

The hard evidence: Alibaba Cloud ranks first in China's AI cloud market with a 38.1% share (Omdia), and held that share while the entire industry was scrambling for capacity. The soft evidence: many Alibaba Cloud customers buy the cloud "because they can use the Tongyi models"—models bound to the cloud, a stickiness rivals lack.

The moat's weakness: compute itself is commoditized and customers have strong price-comparison power, so its width depends on whether the "models and chips" layers can sustain their lead; IaaS alone is not enough. Score 8/10, trend thickening.

A thicker second curve: in-house chips

T-Head spans four chip categories: GPU, CPU, storage and networking. Its latest AI processor, Zhenwu M890, already serves more than 650 external customers across over 20 industries via Alibaba Cloud, from autonomous driving to internet and financial services. Its role is not to beat Nvidia but to be "available" and "20%–30% cheaper."

With export controls unlikely to ease, these two points become hard barriers: cloud providers without cards will lose share; those with in-house chips can keep taking orders. That is the structural reason Alibaba Cloud's share can rise to 38.1% in 2026.

The weaknesses are equally clear: constrained process nodes leave a generational performance gap versus Nvidia, and the barrier holds only while controls persist. Once controls ease, the value of "available" goes to zero, leaving only "cheaper." Score 7/10: the trend strengthens but is conditional.

Two thinning moats: transaction mindset and network effects

Shopping mindshare was once Alibaba's most valuable asset: to buy something, you opened Taobao first. The evidence is still there — roughly 64 million 88VIP members, growing double digits YoY, the most valuable roster in Chinese e-commerce. But "members are there, ad spend is not": customer management revenue posted zero growth, a sign merchants are unwilling to spend more on Taobao.

Network effects — the merchant-consumer two-sided effect — are fading too. Merchants once "had to open a store on Taobao"; now they can open stores simultaneously on Taobao, Douyin, Pinduoduo and Xiaohongshu, and multi-platform operation is the norm. When a platform goes from "a must" to "one of several", its ability to take commission inevitably declines.

How fast these two assets thin out determines whether e-commerce deserves 7x or 11x EBITA. Current data (+1% on a like-for-like basis) supports 9x — not decline, but stagnation. Scores of 8/10 and 7/10 respectively, with a thinning trend.

The unfinished one: instant retail

Delivery density is a real moat: the higher the order density in a given area, the lower the per-order fulfilment cost. Meituan built it over ten years and now holds 52% share. Taobao Shangou used subsidies to reach about 41% in one year (JD 7%), which shows the barriers in this business "can be bought with money"—itself a negation of the moat.

The real question is how much remains once subsidies stop. Three possibilities: ① retention above 35%, indicating genuine stickiness and that Alibaba has bought itself a ticket to the game; ② retention of 25%–35%, indicating the position is sustained by price and that it can only ever be the second player long term; ③ retention below 25%, indicating the subsidies were burned for nothing and it should exit.

No data currently exists to determine which case applies—the most unpredictable piece of investing in Alibaba. Score 6/10, trend undetermined.

Conclusion

Moat conclusion: Alibaba has five moats — two are thickening (cloud, chips), two are thinning (transaction mindshare, network effects) and one is undetermined (instant retail). The two thickening moats set the ceiling; the two thinning ones set the floor. That is also why the stock can only be valued on a sum-of-the-parts basis — letting the width of one moat stand for the whole company does a disservice to the other four.
