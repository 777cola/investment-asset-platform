Deliberation

The four arrive at different answers but the same conclusion. Duan Yongping says the nature of the business has changed—from collecting rent to heavy-asset investment, so the valuation framework must change; Buffett says a company with negative cash flow gets no premium; Munger says the most dangerous thing is fighting three battles at once; Li Lu says compute is the new infrastructure, and what Alibaba has secured is a ticket to entry, not the finish line.

All four agree "this company will not disappear," and all four disagree that "now is the time to buy." The disagreement is only over the odds: a neutral case of HKD 129 against the current price of HKD 112.5 implies 15% upside and 22% downside, odds of 1:1.5. Those odds demand a 60% win rate, and the half of the story beyond cloud (narrowing losses in AI applications) has not a single data point.

Duan Yongping: Has the Nature of Business Changed?

He would first ask: is this still the "rent-collecting" business? The answer is half yes, half no. E-commerce is — merchant prepayments, payment terms, zero inventory; RMB 205.9 billion of revenue earns RMB 39.75 billion of EBITA, and the 19.3% margin is the rent; cloud barely counts — an 11.6% segment margin already resembles an infrastructure return; instant retail and AI applications are not — both are now buying position with cash, and every subsidy in the RMB 53.3 billion of revenue is carved out of the e-commerce rent.

He would not buy just because "AI is the future," because in his framework he does not touch what he cannot understand: an application layer losing RMB 55.4 billion annualized and contributing only RMB 3.34 billion of revenue is not an asset but a liability. The other meaning of "benfen" is not gambling on a not-yet-formed future via share placement — HKD 80 billion, 3.47% dilution; the move itself admits that "operating cash flow cannot sustain the current pace of investment."

The positive mark he would give is cloud: 45% growth with margins rising at the same time — the kind of "the more it does, the more it earns" he is willing to pay for. So he would probably say, "I understand this business, but I'll wait until it squares the AI numbers." Understandable, holdable, reasonably priced — the third of the three is not met.

Buffett: Cash flow is the only language

He starts with the cash flow statement. Operating cash flow RMB 22.95 billion, up 11% year on year — cash generation is intact; capex RMB 67.68 billion, up 75% year on year; free cash flow negative RMB 44.67 billion. Then he looks at how management plugs the hole — an equity placement, not debt. Debt carries interest, a maturity schedule and covenants; a placement never has to be repaid, and the only cost is dilution for existing shareholders. From a capital-allocation standpoint, this shifts risk from the company to shareholders.

Credit where due: cash and current investments on the books total RMB 474.505 billion, so HKD 80 billion equals just 3.5% of market cap and 17% of cash — no damage to the balance sheet. The placement also drew nearly 3x oversubscription, with sovereign and long-only funds taking over 40%, a sign that professional money endorses Alibaba at this price.

There is really only one question: what return will the RMB 380 billion of compute investment over three years earn. Against annualized cloud revenue of RMB 193.7 billion and an 11.6% margin, the payback period is roughly 2–3 years and the math works; if cloud growth falls to 20%, it becomes another asset-heavy trap. His verdict: I won't pay a premium for a company with negative cash flow, but I will pay once it proves the return — buy later, give up some upside, but never lose principal.

Munger: invert the question — what would make it fall another 30%

At the current price of HKD 112.5, what would take the stock to HKD 80? Three paths, all specific. ① Cloud growth falls from 45% back below 25%, the valuation multiple drops from 6x revenue to 4x, cloud shrinks from RMB 1.16 trillion to RMB 0.77 trillion, shaving HKD 19 per share. ② Customer management revenue stays at -7%, e-commerce EBITA slides from an annualized RMB 159 billion to RMB 140 billion, the multiple goes from 9x to 7x, shaving HKD 15 per share. ③ Capex never stops and free cash flow stays negative for four consecutive quarters, and the market starts pricing it as a "heavy-asset utility," assigning cloud 3x PS.

The second of the three is already happening (even on a like-for-like basis it is only +1%); the first hinges on competition — Volcano Engine and Tencent Cloud are both grabbing share, and Huawei Cloud is more aggressive in government and enterprise; the third is management's own choice, and the placement has just extended that choice by another two years.

He would also point to a psychological factor: the market now treats "AI investment" as good news and "AI losses" as a necessary cost — a classic incentive mismatch — management gets credit for "strategic correctness" simply by keeping the spending going, while the real bill only comes due in three to five years. The odds of all three happening at once are low, but any one of them alone is enough to knock 20% off the stock.

Li Lu: Compute Is the New Infrastructure — Who Collects the Rent

His framework is civilizational evolution: computing power is becoming the electricity and railways of our time—used by every industry, requiring huge investment, delivering steady but unspectacular returns. Within this framework, Alibaba Cloud is well positioned: first in China's AI cloud market with a 38.1% share; T-Head's full-stack in-house development spans GPU to network chips, with Zhenwu M890 already serving more than 650 external customers; Tongyi Qianwen open-source models have surpassed 3 billion downloads and 300,000 derivative models.This is the only company in China with all four layers—model, chip, cloud, and scenario—at once.

But he would flag one thing: the rent on infrastructure is ultimately determined by users' willingness to pay. Chinese enterprises' willingness to pay for computing power is far below that of their US peers, which is why Alibaba Cloud's quarterly revenue of RMB 48.4 billion (about USD 6.8 billion) is only a fraction of Nvidia's quarterly data-center revenue. The position is right, but the ceiling must be recalculated—not the ceiling of a "Chinese Azure," but the ceiling of "Chinese utility computing."

So what he offers is a "ticket in," not a "destination": this business will not disappear in twenty years, but it will go through several rounds of capex reckoning along the way. He will not buy during the investment phase; he will buy after the reckoning, when cash flow turns positive again—growth may then be only 20%, but valuations will be cheaper too.

Odds assessment: 1:1.5, not enough to bet

Neutral HKD 129 implies HKD 16.5 upside; bear case HKD 88 implies HKD 24.5 downside; payoff ratio 1:1.5. To make money at this payoff ratio, the win rate must exceed 60%. The problem is the win rate cannot be calculated: whether cloud can sustain 40% growth, and whether this quarter is a peak or the start of a trend, rests on a single data point; the company has given no timetable for when AI applications will narrow losses.

Breaking the win rate down makes it clearer: assuming a 55% probability that "cloud sustains 40%+ growth" and a 60% probability that "AI application losses stop widening within two years", if the two are independent the neutral-scenario probability is only 33%. That means the 1:1.5 payoff ratio actually corresponds to a negative expected value. To turn it positive, either the price must come down (HKD 95 implies 36% neutral upside and 7% bear-case downside, flipping the payoff ratio to 5:1), or the evidence must improve (two quarters of cloud margin ≥12% with AI losses narrowing quarter on quarter, pushing the neutral-scenario probability above 55%).

This is also the mathematical meaning of the "Watch" rating: it is not a lack of conviction, it is that the expected value of this trade is not yet sufficient. If the payoff ratio is below 1:2, or the win rate is below 55%, you should not act — that is discipline, not opinion.

Conclusion

The four-person committee concluded "hold cash, hold the watchlist," not "hold the stock." There is only one point of disagreement: whether cloud should be valued at 6x or 9x PS. Duan Yongping and Buffett lean conservative (wait for cash flow), while Munger and Li Lu lean structural (compute position is scarce). The verdict method is to wait for data, not opinions: if the cloud segment's margin stays at ≥12% for two consecutive quarters and the AI application's quarterly loss narrows quarter on quarter, with both occurring simultaneously, the valuation multiple moves up one notch (6x→8x), the target price rises from HKD 129 to above HKD 150, and at that point the current price offers 30% upside.
