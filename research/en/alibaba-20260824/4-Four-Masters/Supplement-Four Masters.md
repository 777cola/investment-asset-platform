## Duan Yongping: Has the Nature of Business Changed?

His first question would be whether this is still a "rent-collecting" business. The answer is half yes, half no. E-commerce is — merchant prepayments, payment terms, zero inventory; RMB 205.9 billion of revenue earns RMB 39.75 billion of EBITA, and the 19.3% margin is the rent. Cloud barely counts — an 11.6% segment margin already resembles an infrastructure return. Instant retail and AI applications are not — both are now buying position with cash, and every subsidy within the RMB 53.3 billion of revenue is carved out of the e-commerce rent.

He would not buy simply because "AI is the future," since his framework does not touch what he cannot understand: an application layer losing RMB 55.4 billion annualized on just RMB 3.34 billion of revenue is not an asset but a liability. The other meaning of "benfen" is not gambling on a not-yet-formed future via share placement — HKD 80 billion, 3.47% dilution; the move itself concedes that "operating cash flow cannot sustain the current pace of investment."

The positive mark he would give is cloud: 45% growth with margins rising at the same time — the kind of "the more it does, the more it earns" he is willing to pay for. So he would probably say, "I understand this business, but I'll wait until it squares the AI numbers." Understandable, holdable, reasonably priced — the third of the three is not met.

## Buffett: Cash flow is the only language

He starts with the cash flow statement. Operating cash flow of RMB 22.95 billion, up 11% YoY, shows cash generation is intact; capex of RMB 67.68 billion, up 75% YoY, leaves free cash flow negative at RMB 44.67 billion. He then looks at how management plugs the hole — an equity placement, not debt. Debt carries interest, a maturity schedule and covenants; a placement never has to be repaid, and the only cost is dilution for existing shareholders. From a capital-allocation standpoint, that shifts risk from the company to shareholders.

Credit where due: cash and current investments on the books total RMB 474.505 billion, so HKD 80 billion equals just 3.5% of market cap and 17% of cash — no damage to the balance sheet. The placement also drew nearly 3x oversubscription, with sovereign and long-only funds taking over 40%, a sign that professional money endorses Alibaba at this price.

There is really only one question: what return will the RMB 380 billion of compute investment over three years earn. Against annualised cloud revenue of RMB 193.7 billion and an 11.6% margin, the payback period is roughly 2–3 years and the maths works; if cloud growth falls to 20%, it becomes another asset-heavy trap. His verdict: I won't pay a premium for a company with negative cash flow, but I will pay once it proves the return — buy later, give up some upside, but never lose principal.

## Munger's inversion: what would drive a further 30% de-rating?

From the current HKD 112.5, three specific paths could take the stock to HKD 80. ① Cloud growth decelerates from 45% to below 25%: the multiple contracts from 6x revenue to 4x, cloud value shrinks from RMB 1.16 trillion to RMB 0.77 trillion, removing HKD 19 per share. ② Customer management revenue stays at -7%: e-commerce EBITA slides from an annualised RMB 159 billion to RMB 140 billion, the multiple compresses from 9x to 7x, removing HKD 15 per share. ③ Capex continues unabated and free cash flow stays negative for four consecutive quarters: the market reprices the business as a "heavy-asset utility" and assigns cloud 3x P/S.

The second scenario is already playing out—even on a like-for-like basis growth is only +1%. The first hinges on competition: Volcano Engine and Tencent Cloud are both taking share, and Huawei Cloud is more aggressive in government and enterprise. The third is a management choice, and the placement has just extended that optionality by another two years.

Psychology also plays a part: the market now treats "AI investment" as good news and "AI losses" as a necessary cost—a classic incentive mismatch—management earns credit for "strategic correctness" simply by sustaining the spend, while the real bill only comes due in three to five years. The odds of all three materialising at once are low, but any one alone is enough to knock 20% off the stock.

## Li Lu: Compute Is the New Infrastructure — Who Collects the Rent

His framework is civilizational evolution: computing power is becoming the electricity and railways of our time—used by every industry, requiring huge investment, delivering steady but unspectacular returns. Within it, Alibaba Cloud is well positioned: first in China's AI cloud market with a 38.1% share; T-Head's full-stack in-house development spans GPU to network chips, with Zhenwu M890 already serving more than 650 external customers; Tongyi Qianwen open-source models have surpassed 3 billion downloads and 300,000 derivative models.It is the only company in China with all four layers—model, chip, cloud and scenario—at once.

He flags one constraint, however: infrastructure rent is ultimately determined by users' willingness to pay. Chinese enterprises' willingness to pay for computing power sits far below that of US peers, which is why Alibaba Cloud's quarterly revenue of RMB 48.4 billion (about USD 6.8 billion) is only a fraction of Nvidia's quarterly data-centre revenue. The position is right, but the ceiling must be recalculated—not the ceiling of a "Chinese Azure," but of "Chinese utility computing."

What he offers, then, is a "ticket in," not a "destination": this business will not disappear in twenty years, but it will go through several rounds of capex reckoning along the way. He will not buy during the investment phase; he will buy after the reckoning, when free cash flow turns positive again—growth may then be only 20%, but valuations will be cheaper too.

## Odds assessment: 1:1.5, not enough to bet

Neutral HKD 129 implies HKD 16.5 upside; bear case HKD 88 implies HKD 24.5 downside; payoff ratio 1:1.5. At that payoff ratio, the win rate must exceed 60% to make money. The problem is the win rate cannot be calculated: whether cloud can sustain 40% growth, and whether this quarter is a peak or the start of a trend, rests on a single data point, and the company has given no timetable for narrowing AI application losses.

Breaking the win rate down makes this clearer. Assume a 55% probability that cloud sustains 40%+ growth and a 60% probability that AI application losses stop widening within two years; if the two are independent, the neutral-scenario probability is only 33%. That means the 1:1.5 payoff ratio actually carries a negative expected value. To turn it positive, either the price must come down — HKD 95 implies 36% neutral upside and 7% bear-case downside, flipping the payoff ratio to 5:1 — or the evidence must improve: two quarters of cloud margin ≥12% with AI losses narrowing quarter on quarter, pushing the neutral-scenario probability above 55%.

This is also the mathematical meaning of the "Watch" rating: not a lack of conviction, but insufficient expected value in the trade. If the payoff ratio is below 1:2, or the win rate is below 55%, you should not act — that is discipline, not opinion.
