## Main text (ready to copy and publish)

**Alibaba's Earnings: Half Miracle, Half Bill**

The conclusion first: on August 24, 2026, Alibaba closed at HKD 112.5, down 8.54% in a single day. What fell was not the results but an HKD 80 billion placement. And the brightest part of the results — Cloud, with revenue up 45%, a 22-quarter high. One earnings report, two extremes.

**1. Cloud: the best scorecard in five years**

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (April to June), Alibaba Cloud was renamed "AI Cloud and Computing Power Services," with revenue of RMB 48.44 billion, up 45% year on year. That growth rate is the fastest in 22 quarters. AI-related products within it came to RMB 12.376 billion, growing triple digits for 12 consecutive quarters.

More critical is profit. Adjusted EBITA for this business was RMB 5.63 billion, up 133% year on year, with margin rising from 7.2% to 11.6%. Anyone in cloud knows what that means: asset-heavy businesses usually force a choice — sacrifice profit for growth, or growth for profit. Alibaba got both this time.

Why? Three reasons. First, depreciation on the previous round of chip purchases has not fully run its course, so the cost side has not started paying the bill. Second, the whole industry is scrambling for computing power, and pricing is firm. Third, in-house chips are cutting costs — T-Head's Zhenwu M890 already serves more than 650 external customers across over 20 industries through Alibaba Cloud.

One detail nobody mentions: AliExpress recorded operating profit this quarter. International e-commerce went from burning cash to making money, something that has not happened in the past five years.

**2. Net profit -76%: where the money went**

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell to just RMB 10.54 billion, down 76% year on year, the lowest in nine quarters. It looks like a collapse. But broken down, only one piece reflects genuine operating deterioration: AI Labs and Applications lost roughly RMB 10.6 billion more.

The rest is one-off or accounting-related: goodwill impairment of RMB 4.46 billion; an EU fine provision of about RMB 4.3 billion; an effective tax rate jumping from 17.6% to 57.4%, adding roughly RMB 6 billion in tax; and interest and investment income falling from RMB 17.38 billion to RMB 9 billion, a drop of RMB 8.38 billion. The four items total about RMB 23 billion.

So adjusted net profit is closer to the truth: RMB 20.72 billion, down only 38% year on year. That 38% roughly equals the incremental AI investment. In other words, without AI spending, net profit this quarter would have been about RMB 31 billion, down only 7% year on year.

The business is not broken; the company deliberately changed lanes.

**3. The number nobody dares examine closely: -415%**

This quarter Alibaba split the company into four segments, and AI Labs and Applications was broken out separately for the first time. Revenue was RMB 3.34 billion, up 16%; the loss was RMB 13.86 billion, a loss margin of -415%, with the loss widening 330% year on year.

In plain terms: for every RMB 1 of revenue, this division spends RMB 5.15.

The company's explanation is "investment in building AI capabilities + rising inference costs for the Qwen App." The product is real — Qwen3.8-Max with 2.4 trillion parameters was open-sourced, the Qwen series has surpassed 3 billion global downloads and 300,000 derivative models, and the Qwen App launched paid services in August. But no number can pin down when it will make money.

This is the biggest information gap in the entire report: it gives the loss margin but not the trend of that margin, nor a path to narrowing losses or a timeline.

**4. RMB 44.7 billion burned in one quarter, then a request for RMB 80 billion**

Operating cash flow was RMB 22.95 billion, still up 11% year on year — cash generation is intact. But capital expenditure was RMB 67.68 billion, up 75% year on year. Subtract the two, and free cash flow was -RMB 44.67 billion, with the shortfall widening 1.4 times year on year.

Then came the announcement on the evening of August 23: a placement of 710 million new shares at HKD 112.70 each, raising HKD 80 billion, net proceeds of about HKD 79.7 billion, 3.47% dilution, 100% invested in AI infrastructure. The underwriting syndicate was CICC, HSBC, Morgan Stanley and UBS, with a 90-day lock-up.

The placement was executed well: oversubscribed nearly 3x in under an hour, total subscriptions above HKD 200 billion, with sovereign and long-only funds taking over 40%. But the market latched onto one thing — this was Alibaba's first equity financing since its 2019 Hong Kong listing. A company long known for buybacks and cancellation suddenly sold stock with the share price 39% below its high.

On August 24, the stock fell below the placement price of 112.70 and closed at 112.5. Even the placees were underwater.

The same day, Joe Tsai bought 720,000 shares and Eddie Wu bought 350,000 shares, HKD 120 million combined. That sounds like a lot, but it is 0.15% of the HKD 80 billion placement. It is a gesture, not a hedge.

**V. What it is worth: HKD 129 neutral, and 1-to-1.5 odds**

Value the four pieces separately: the e-commerce group's annualized EBITA of about RMB 159 billion at 9x, RMB 1.43 trillion; cloud annualized revenue of RMB 193.7 billion at 6x, RMB 1.16 trillion; AI applications at 3x the annualized loss of RMB 55.4 billion as a negative value, minus RMB 17 billion. That adds up to RMB 2.42 trillion, or HKD 129 per share on 20.46 billion shares post-placement at an exchange rate of 0.92.

At the current HKD 112.5, that is a 13% discount. Upside of 15%, downside of HKD 88 in the bear case, or 22%. Odds of 1 to 1.5.

To make money at these odds, the win rate has to exceed 60%. Of the two conditions required, "cloud sustains above 40% growth" has one quarter of data, and "AI applications narrow losses within two years" has not a single data point.

So the conclusion is watch, not avoid. Good assets, neutral price, insufficient evidence.

**VI. At what price to buy, stated clearly**

Two triggers, either one suffices: first, the share price falls below HKD 95; second, the cloud segment margin holds above 12% for two consecutive quarters and AI application losses begin to narrow quarter on quarter.

Buying at HKD 95 gives 36% upside and only 7% downside, flipping the odds to 5 to 1 — even with a two-thirds chance of being wrong, the trade still makes money.

One last honest point: what is most valuable at Alibaba now is not e-commerce, it is that cloud. But part of what you pay is underwriting an experiment that loses RMB 55.4 billion a year. When that math breaks even, the company itself has not yet said.

Until it does, waiting is cheaper than guessing.
