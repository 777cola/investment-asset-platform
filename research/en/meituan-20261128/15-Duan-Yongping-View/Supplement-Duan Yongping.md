## Q: Is this a good business?

Answer: Good, but not the best kind. What makes it good? Gross margin of 34.7%, light capital expenditure, cash collected before it is paid out, and users opening the app over a hundred times a year. These are strengths of the business itself, regardless of who runs it. In 2024 this business earned RMB 45.1 billion in operating profit for the year, a 20.9% margin — that was the best state.

What makes it not good? It does not set its own price. See's Candies can raise prices 5% a year because no one can replace it; Meituan raises prices by RMB 1 and users immediately go to another platform. 2026Q3 revenue grew 21.6%, driven by order volume and category expansion, not by price increases — a business that cannot raise prices, no matter how deep its moat, cannot earn excess profit, only scale profit.

Duan Yongping would say: "The business this most resembles is not Coca-Cola, but airlines — demand is always there, but a portion of the money earned is always taken away by competition." The difference is that Meituan's competitive structure is better than airlines': three players, not ten.

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

Answer: Yes, and most likely still number one. The reason is not the brand, it is the assets. A dispatch network spanning 2,800 counties and cities, about 6 million riders with active orders, and about 8 million active merchants — these cannot be bought outright with money. JD.com and Alibaba each spent over RMB 100 billion and fought for two years, and their combined order-volume share has only just passed half.

Could something else replace it in ten years? Two possibilities: first, drones and autonomous vehicles drive fulfillment costs close to zero and technology erases the density advantage; second, the traffic gateway shifts and users no longer open the app, ordering instead through WeChat, Douyin or AI assistants. The first is good for Meituan (it is investing in unmanned delivery itself); the second is the real threat — 30% of Meituan's traffic currently comes from external gateways, and that part it cannot defend.

So the answer is "yes", but we cannot pay a premium for "certainly yes". That is also why the valuation uses 19x rather than 25x.

## Can you understand it?

Answer: The revenue is understandable; the profit is not. Revenue = order volume × average order value + merchant commission + advertising + self-operated goods; all five variables have historical ranges and can be modeled. Profit = revenue − delivery cost − rider cost − subsidies − R&D − administrative expenses, and the "subsidies" line depends on the budgets of three boards — Alibaba, JD.com and Douyin — not on Wang Xing.

Duan Yongping's circle-of-competence principle is: if you cannot understand it, do not touch it, or touch it only with a small position. Meituan's revenue side sits fully inside the circle of competence; its profit side sits outside it. That dictates the reasonable action — not abstaining from buying, but "after buying, do not forecast quarterly profit," looking only at the annual direction.

His own purchases of NetEase, Apple and Moutai were all companies whose rough earnings five years out could be calculated. Meituan cannot be calculated, only ranged: between RMB 11 billion and RMB 33 billion in 2027. The range is 3 times wide — that is the quantified expression of "cannot understand it."

## Q: At what price to buy

Answer: HKD 67.85 is a level to buy a little; below HKD 55, buy more. The math is simple: 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 23 billion, less net cash of RMB 120 billion, leaves the core business at RMB 265.5 billion, or 11.5x. For a business with 6 million riders that is still growing, 11.5x is cheap.

But "cheap" does not mean "should be a heavy position." Duan Yongping's position-sizing logic is: bet big when certainty is high, test with a small position when certainty is average. Meituan's certainty is that "the business will not disappear"; its uncertainty is "when profit comes back, and how much comes back." The first half is worth HKD 100, the second half only HKD 50 — together, that is roughly today's HKD 68.

So the move splits into three tranches: buy the first tranche now (30%), buy the second at HKD 58 (30%), and buy the third (40%) after the Q1 2027 report confirms Q4 profit held at RMB 5 billion. No forecasting, no all-in, tranche by price and data.

## Q: What is the biggest fear

Answer: The biggest fear is a competitor that ignores cost. Meituan held about 65% of order volume in early 2025; by February 2026 it held only about 51%, losing 14 percentage points in 20 months — this is not a moat failing, it is a competitor willing to lose money. A moat stops rational players; it does not stop someone using e-commerce profits to subsidize food delivery.

The second fear is "convincing yourself." The August 2026 report said "Q3 will be worse than Q2," and it was indeed slightly worse; had you treated management's words as pleasantries, you would have chased the stock higher in August. The most dangerous moment in this business is often when the subsidy war looks like it is ending — because the competitor will use one pulse of subsidies to tell you it is not over.

Duan Yongping's "stop-doing list" is especially useful here: do not go all-in because of one quarter of returning to profit, do not call it an opportunity because the stock fell 36%, do not believe the competitor thinks the same way just because management says "share does not matter." Do none of those three, and the remaining action is naturally "scale in."
