## Capital allocation: three lines, two good and one bad

First, buybacks. A USD 2 billion programme was approved in June 2024; following shareholder re-authorisation in June 2026, it has been executed only in small size within the HKD 66.5–69 range, with 2.9248 million shares bought over two days for about HKD 200 million.The problem is scale: USD 2 billion is just 0.4% of the HKD 419 billion market cap, and at the current pace it would take ten years to complete.For a company sitting on RMB 120 billion of net cash that considers itself undervalued, this is insufficient—our main reservation about management.

Second, R&D and AI. 2026Q3 R&D was about RMB 8.65 billion (estimated), +11.3% YoY and 7.5% of revenue, 10 points below revenue growth—the result of deliberate restraint.Wang Xing said in 2025 that the company would not "build a Token factory," and reiterated in 2026 that it would not join the compute arms race. With every major player scrambling for chips, that stance carries a cost (share, narrative, valuation), but it preserves cash flow.

Third, overseas expansion. Keeta reached unit-economic profitability in Hong Kong in October 2025 and in Saudi Arabia in July 2026, taking only 22 months; Saudi Arabia now accounts for about 25% of Keeta's daily orders.Brazil is different: a signed 5-year, USD 1 billion investment agreement makes it the only battlefield where "how much you lose is up to you, how much you earn is up to your rivals."Q3 new-business losses widened sequentially from RMB 1.74 billion to about RMB 2.16 billion, mainly on the Brazil launch. This is not a mistake; it is a bet.

## Integrity and governance: three tests

The first was the October 2021 antitrust penalty: RMB 3.442 billion and an order to refund merchants' exclusive-cooperation deposits. The company did not appeal, paid in full on schedule and subsequently scrapped "choose one of two." Its approach was to accept the penalty and change its structure, without delay. The second was rider rights in 2025: algorithm adjustments to satisfy regulators, social-insurance pilots, and nationwide coverage of "new occupational injury" insurance from July 1, 2026, with the platform bearing the full premium — compliance bought with real money, at an annual cost in the RMB 10 billion range. The third is the 2026 subsidy war. On the earnings call, management was asked how it would respond to a 14-point share loss and answered that "share is not the goal, unit economics is." Q3 numbers later validated that statement.

The governance concern is the dual-class share structure: Wang Xing holds more than 40% of voting power on roughly 9% of economic interest, which limits shareholders' checks on management. Fortunately, there has been no history of major-shareholder cash-outs or related-party disputes; Mu Rongjun and Chen Shaohui hold deeply committed stakes. This can only be observed, not quantified.

## Succession: not a problem, but no answer either

At 47, Wang Xing is a long way from retirement, so succession is not a risk today. But one thing needs to be clear: the company currently has no explicit "number two" arrangement. Mu Rongjun runs technology and compliance, Wang Puzhong runs core local commerce (69% of revenue) and Chen Shaohui runs capital — three people each own a piece, and none has been publicly positioned as the next CEO.

Compare with peers: Alibaba has an explicit partnership system and job rotation, JD.com has a tier of leaders behind Xu Lei, and Pinduoduo has Chen Lei. Meituan's organisation looks more like "Wang Xing plus a group of business unit heads" — high strategic concentration, high execution efficiency, but also concentrated succession risk. That is not a deduction today but a discount ten years out: if no clear leadership tier is visible by 2027–2028, valuation should take a further 5%–10% discount.

## Give management 8 out of 10 — where do the 2 deducted points come from?

The positives are specific: it defended UE in the worst quarter; when everyone was chasing compute, it said it would not be a "token factory"; in August it proactively guided that Q3 would be worse than Q2, and the November numbers matched. The last point is the most valuable — management giving bad guidance and delivering on it is rarer than giving good guidance.

Two negatives. First, shareholder returns are too conservative: RMB 120 billion in net cash and a 0.4% buyback mean capital-allocation authority stays in its own hands indefinitely. Second, overseas expansion is rushed: Saudi Arabia reaching profitability in 22 months is a strong result, but entering Brazil at the same point puts the money just earned into a harder battlefield. If Brazil's losses exceed RMB 5 billion in 2027, this goes from a negative to a deduction.
