## Duan Yongping: Do users still open it?

Duan Yongping does not start with profit when he looks at Meituan; he starts with "why users still open it." The Q3 report answers that: revenue +21.6% YoY and +10.4% QoQ, accelerating even as subsidies retreat. Subsidy retreat usually means order volume falls; here revenue is accelerating instead. There is only one explanation—users did not leave, and average order value came back. He would probably say: "That's right—users bought with subsidies are not users."

Second, he would ask whether management is doing the right things. At the worst point in 2025, Wang Xing publicly said he would "resolutely oppose involution," choosing to defend unit economics and cede order-volume share, at the cost of a full-year net loss of RMB 23.36 billion and share falling from 65% to 51%. On the "doing the right things" scorecard, that earns credit: knowing he would be criticized, he still refused a meaningless price war. The 2026Q3 report is the payoff—profit is back, and users did not leave.

Third is always price. He often says "a good business still needs a good price." At HKD 67.85 and a market cap of HKD 419 billion, that is 16.8x 2027 earnings and 11.5x after deducting net cash. At this price he would say "not expensive, but not a fire sale either," so the move is "buy a little," not "go all in." What he dislikes most is mistaking "cheap" for "safe"—Meituan's cheapness is conditional, and the conditions are written in competitors' budget sheets.

## Buffett: What Is a Moat Worth?

Buffett would file this under "has a moat, but the moat costs money to maintain," and reach for the See's Candies comparison. See's can raise prices every year and still run gross margin above 60%; Meituan cannot — pricing power sits with consumers and regulators, not fully with the company. The fair multiple should therefore be 15–20x, not the 30x of a growth stock.

He would work the unit economics. Q3 gross profit was RMB 40.06 billion, a 34.7% gross margin (derived); sales and marketing was RMB 26.5 billion, a 22.9% expense ratio (derived). Together they say one thing: of every RMB 100 of revenue, 34.7 is gross profit, 22.9 goes to marketing, and the remaining 11.8 must cover R&D, admin and rider incentives. For the same business in 2024 the marketing expense ratio was only 16% — those 7 points are the entire source of profit elasticity. Core local commerce operating margin was 20.9% in 2024 and 5.6% now — of the 15-point gap, 7 points are marketing and 8 points are delivery cost and other subsidy.

On capital allocation he would keep score: net cash of RMB 120 billion, interest-bearing debt near zero, and the USD 2 billion buyback authorization approved in June 2024 executed at HKD 66.5–69. But USD 2 billion is only 0.4% of the HKD 419 billion market cap — he would call that "too little": a company that believes its shares are undervalued should buy back far harder. Nor would he like the USD 1 billion commitment to Keeta Brazil: a business he does not understand, in someone else's country, buying share with losses.

## Munger: What would make it halve again?

Munger habitually inverts the question: what would have to happen for HKD 67.85 to fall to HKD 35? He would probably list four things.

- Subsidy war restarts. Alibaba diverts part of its HKD 80 billion allocation for 2026 into instant retail, pushing industry monthly subsidies back to the 2025 peak. This one is partly ruled out — Alibaba itself says the money goes to AI infrastructure.

- Regulatory costs step up permanently. From 1 July 2026, rider "new occupational injury" insurance covers the whole country, with premiums borne entirely by the platforms — an irreversible cost step-up; if the social-security scope widens further, every RMB 0.3 rise in per-order cost adds RMB 6 billion a year.

- Brazil spirals. Keeta repeats the 2018 Didi-style cash burn in Brazil: USD 1 billion is not enough, add another USD 2 billion.

- Shareholder returns stay absent. With RMB 120 billion of net cash on the books paying no dividend and buybacks at just 0.4%, cash sits idle and the market discounts it.

Of the four, the first is half resolved, the second is quantifiable, the third is controllable, and the fourth management can fix itself. His conclusion would be: "The risk list is not long, but every item has to be bought with money and time." That settles position sizing — you can bet, but not heavily.

## Li Lu: Is this a structural advantage?

Li Lu would take a longer view: delivering within three kilometers in thirty minutes rests on three conditions—high-density cities, low-cost labor and mature mobile payments. Only China has all three in full.So this is not efficiency built on subsidies; it is efficiency determined by urban form. Subsidies can shift the distribution of share, but they cannot shift this model's cost curve.

He would focus on the endgame: once all three players stop subsidizing and return to rational competition, does the industry become "one dominant player earning excess profit" or "three players splitting the market at average profit"? In the former, Meituan, at its 2024 margin of 20.9%, could earn more than RMB 50 billion a year and be worth HKD 200; in the latter, only HKD 80.Buying now is essentially a bet on the former, and the Q3 report gave that bet its first piece of evidence—share held at 50%, yet profit came back.

He would be slightly more positive on overseas expansion than the other three: Keeta reached unit-economic profitability in Hong Kong in October 2025 and in Saudi Arabia in July 2026, taking only 22 months, which shows the dispatch system can be replicated across cities.But he would also caution that Saudi Arabia is "high ticket size plus low competition" while Brazil is "low ticket size plus strong rivals"—the two are not the same thing. Extrapolating the Saudi experience to Brazil is the most easily overstated point in this report.
