Core view

Buffett would first ask: "Of this RMB 2.071 billion, how much was earned from operations?" The answer is RMB 1.71 billion (estimated); deduct capex of RMB 2.5 billion and free cash flow comes to about RMB 6 billion — better than net profit, because food delivery is a prepaid business.

Quality of profit: RMB 1.71 billion operating profit

Net profit of RMB 2.071 billion, adjusted net profit of RMB 1.850 billion, operating profit of RMB 1.71 billion (derived). Three declining figures point to two things: non-operating income contributed about RMB 360 million, and even after adding back share-based compensation, the figure remains below net profit. Buffett would like this kind of "conservatively confirmed" accounting — it does not count unrealized investment gains as profit.

Gross profit: RMB 40.06 billion, gross margin 34.7%, up 1.7 points year on year. That gross margin is mid-range for Chinese internet (Alibaba e-commerce about 40%, JD retail about 15%), but asset turnover is fast — it collects cash from users upfront and settles merchant accounts monthly, so working capital is negative.

What really makes him frown is the expense structure: sales and marketing RMB 26.5 billion, 22.9% of revenue; R&D RMB 8.65 billion, 7.5%. A company that spends 30% of revenue on "getting people to buy" and "researching how to get people to buy" shows it has not yet found a way to retain users without spending. Compare Coca-Cola, whose advertising spend is under 10% of revenue.

Cash flow: a truer measure than profit

Operating cash flow of about RMB 8.5 billion, capex of about RMB 2.5 billion, free cash flow of about RMB 6 billion (derived). Annualized at RMB 24 billion, that is a 6.2% FCF yield on a market cap of RMB 385.5 billion. This number is far more useful than PE — PE is distorted by how thin profits are; FCF is real.

But mind the seasonality: Q4 is the off-season for food delivery, order volume falls while rider costs stay fixed, so FCF will be clearly lower than in Q3. Operating cash flow in Q4 2025 was negative (part of the full-year -RMB 13.815 billion). So the annualized RMB 24 billion is overstated; RMB 12–15 billion for the full year would be a good outcome.

The balance sheet is what satisfied him most: cash of RMB 104.7 billion plus short-term wealth management products of RMB 63.6 billion, interest-bearing debt of almost zero, net cash of about RMB 120 billion, or 31% of market cap. That means one third of the current price is cash and two thirds is the business. The business portion is RMB 265.5 billion, which can earn RMB 23 billion in 2027, or 11.5x.

Capital allocation: will he vote against it?

Buffett has three criteria for capital allocation: each dollar of retained earnings must create more than a dollar of market value; buybacks only when the price is below intrinsic value; no drifting into new industries. Meituan passes two and a half of the three.

What it passes: no drifting into new industries (restraint on AI, no carmaking, no mining acquisitions); buyback price is reasonable (HKD 66.5–69 range, below his calculated intrinsic value). What it fails is scale: the USD 2 billion authorization is only 0.4% of the HKD 419 billion market cap, against RMB 120 billion of net cash on the balance sheet — at the current pace it would take ten years.

The "one-dollar test" also fails for now: retained earnings were negative in 2025 (a loss of RMB 23.355 billion) and have only just turned positive in 2026, so it is too early to speak of creating market value. This criterion cannot be scored until 2027.

He would be cautious on overseas expansion: Keeta turning profitable in Saudi Arabia in 22 months is a good result, but USD 1 billion over 5 years in Brazil is "using certain money to buy an uncertain market." His line would roughly be: "I'd be happier if that money went to buybacks."

What price will he offer

Using his algorithm: 2027 net profit of RMB 23 billion, at 18–20x (a company with 6 million riders, a defensible moat but no pricing power) = RMB 414–460 billion, plus net cash of RMB 120 billion = RMB 534–580 billion, or HKD 580.4–630.4 billion, or HKD 94–102 per share. Today's HKD 67.85 is 67%–72% of intrinsic value, a 28%–33% discount.

Is that discount enough? His standard is "pay 40 cents for a dollar," i.e. a 40% discount. Right now it is only 30%, 10 points short. That corresponds to a share price of HKD 58–62 — which is why he says "you can start buying, but hold most of your money for lower prices."

He would add: "I would rather own part of a good company than all of a mediocre one." Meituan is part of a good company; the only problem is that it has not yet proven it can earn money steadily.

Conclusion

Financial view: thin but real profits, cash flow better than profit, a clean balance sheet, and conservative capital allocation. Intrinsic value HKD 94–102 versus the current price of HKD 67.85, a 28%–33% discount, still 10 points short of the 40% he demands. Action: start buying, hold most of the capital for HKD 58–62.
