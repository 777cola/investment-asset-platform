Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — Meituan

Why users keep coming back, and where the business differentiates itself

Core view

Q3 revenue rose 21.6% YoY and 10.4% QoQ, accelerating even as subsidies were scaled back — the hardest piece of evidence in this earnings report: users did not leave. The Duan Yongping-style question is why users still open the app, and the answer is that for the three-kilometer, thirty-minute job, only Meituan currently does it most reliably.

What is the essence of this business?

Strip Meituan back to its foundations and it sells only one thing: it sells "now" to users. Users will pay an extra RMB 5 for "delivered in 30 minutes" because what they are really buying is time. The cost structure follows from that definition: the shorter the distance and the higher the density, the lower the cost per order. Its economies of scale are therefore geographic, not national — density in Beijing's Chaoyang district cannot dilute the cost of serving a county town in Hebei.

Duan Yongping would press one question: where is the differentiation? Not price — density. All three platforms can deliver the same braised chicken with rice, but Meituan has 40 riders within 3 kilometers and its rivals only 15. That is the differentiation: it does not show up on the menu, it shows up in the line that reads "estimated delivery time."

The flipside of differentiation is its ceiling: density is only worth something where there is population density. China has 2,800 counties and districts; the top 300 generate most orders, and the remaining 2,500 are "covered but unprofitable." Hence the push into categories — instant retail, pharmaceuticals, liquor: same riders, same routes, one more order delivered is one more order earned.

Is the user still there: three pieces of evidence

Evidence one: revenue is accelerating. Q1 +9.2%, Q2 +14.4%, Q3 +21.6% — three consecutive quarters of acceleration. Subsidy rollback usually means order volume declines; here it accelerated instead, showing users were not retained by subsidies.

Evidence two: delivery service revenue of about RMB 30 billion, up roughly 20% YoY. Delivery revenue = order volume × delivery fee per order. A 20% increase means at least one of order volume or average order value is rising — most likely both.

Evidence three: gross margin 34.7% (estimated), up 1.7 points YoY. Gross margin rising during subsidy rollback shows the mix is shifting toward higher-margin categories (in-store, self-operated flash shopping); users are buying more expensive and more items. Users do not pay more for things they do not want.

Now the negative: merchant service revenue of about RMB 44 billion, up only 8% YoY. Merchants are recovering more slowly than users, showing the 2025 battle damaged the supply side. This is a two-sided network business; one side recovering while the other has not creates friction over the long run.

Is management doing the right thing?

At Meituan's low point in 2025, Wang Xing publicly declared a "resolute fight against involution," choosing to defend unit economics and cede order-volume share — at the cost of a full-year net loss of RMB 23.355 billion and share falling from 65% to 51%. In Duan Yongping's framework, this is "doing the right thing": knowing you will be criticized, yet still refusing to fight a meaningless price war.

The Q3 2026 report is the payoff: profit is back (RMB 2.071 billion) and users stayed (revenue +21.6%). Had the company defended share back then, the likely outcome today would be "60% share, RMB 8 billion quarterly loss" — that would truly be beyond saving.

The second choice also deserves note: AI. In 2026, every major player is racing for compute, but Wang Xing says "we won't be a token factory," with R&D up only 11.3%, or 7.5% of revenue. That line costs him a story but preserves cash flow. Duan Yongping would endorse this kind of "what not to do" judgment — the not-to-do list matters more than the wish list.

At what price to buy, and how much to buy

HKD 67.85, HKD 419 billion market cap, 16.8x 2027E, 11.5x ex-net cash. Duan Yongping would call it "not expensive, but not exactly a steal either." His position logic is certainty matching: the business will not disappear (certain); when profit returns, and how much of it, is not (uncertain).

Hence three tranches: buy 30% now, add 30% on a drop to HKD 58, and add the remaining 40% once the Q1 2027 report confirms Q4 core local commerce operating profit holds at RMB 5 billion. Do not forecast quarterly profit; watch only the annual direction and the price.

What he dreads most is mistaking "cheap" for "safe." Meituan's cheapness is conditional — and the conditions sit in Alibaba's and Douyin's budget sheets, not in Meituan's financials. So "buy a little," not "go all in."

Conclusion

Business conclusion: the business is intact, the user base is intact, management made the right calls, and the valuation is neither expensive nor especially cheap.The one thing that has not recovered is the merchant side, with merchant services growing only 8% — the next variable to watch.Action: open a 30% position, add a second tranche at HKD 58.
