Core thesis

We cross-check eight key figures against two sources: revenue, net profit, adjusted net profit, R&D, segment profit, share, net cash and market cap. Seven deviations are within 0.5%; the only one above 1% is adjusted net profit, reflecting differing treatment of equity incentives and fair value gains on investments rather than an error.

Handling principle: all financial figures follow the company's results announcement; non-disclosed data such as share and net cash are for reference only and are not fed into the model, and both calibers are listed without averaging. Every figure marked "estimated" in this report is derived arithmetically from announced figures within this report and can be recomputed.

The only item above 1%: adjusted net profit

The company disclosed RMB 1.85 billion, while some terminals calculated RMB 1.79–1.82 billion, a difference of RMB 30–60 million. The gap reflects a difference in definition, not a calculation error. Net profit of RMB 2.071 billion exceeds adjusted net profit of RMB 1.85 billion, implying the net of adjustment items is positive: non-operating items (fair value changes on investments, disposal gains) contributed more than approximately RMB 200 million, while equity incentives are a deduction. A terminal that excludes only equity incentives and retains investment fair value gains will arrive at a higher "adjusted" figure than the company's basis; otherwise it will be lower.

Treatment: adopt the company's basis of RMB 1.85 billion, and clarify wherever "profit quality" is discussed—operating profit is only RMB 1.71 billion (estimated), and of the RMB 2.071 billion net profit, approximately RMB 360 million was not earned from operations. This point is critical: when the market says "adjusted slightly below consensus," it actually means the recovery on the operating side was milder than headline net profit suggests.

Two metrics in conflict: share and net cash

Two share figures circulate. Meituan's 2025 annual report stresses "GTV share above 60%", while Goldman Sachs puts it at about 51% on an order-volume basis. Both are right — they measure different things: Meituan held onto high-ticket orders and lost low-price ones. The industry estimate for this quarter is about 50%, 1 point below February's 51%, which sits within estimation error and does not support the claim that "share is still being lost". Hence the body of this report says "about 50%, stabilizing but not recovering" rather than citing a specific decimal.

Net cash likewise comes in two versions. Disclosed cash of RMB 104.7 billion plus short-term wealth-management products of RMB 63.6 billion, totaling RMB 168.3 billion, is a gross figure that does not deduct merchant settlement payables, rider payables or short-term borrowings; the RMB 120 billion commonly cited in the media is a net estimate. Use the net figure for valuation (more conservative) and the gross figure when discussing the balance sheet (closer to available funds). Mixing the two is the most common error in this type of report; this report keeps them strictly separate.

Conclusion

Eight checks: seven deviate by less than 0.5%, and one (adjusted net profit) by 1.6%–3.2%, which is explainable; no data needs to be overturned. Final basis: announced figures for financials, a net cash basis of RMB 120 billion for valuation, and "about 50%" as a range for share, with no decimals. Every figure this report derives is flagged in place and can be recomputed; every figure the company discloses is left unchanged.
