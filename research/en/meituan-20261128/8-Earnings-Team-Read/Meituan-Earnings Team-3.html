Earnings Deep Dive (Munger) — Meituan

First ask whether this profit can repeat

Core view

I don't care that it earned RMB 2.071 billion this quarter. What I care about is: next quarter, next year, the year after — can it still earn RMB 2.071 billion? Financial reports are the rearview mirror; I want to see the road ahead.

The sustainability of these profits: three assumptions

Profit swung from -RMB 18.632 billion to +RMB 2.071 billion, an improvement of RMB 20.7 billion. Where did that RMB 20.7 billion come from? Gross profit improved by RMB 8.7 billion, expenses saved RMB 10.8 billion, and non-operating gains contributed RMB 1.2 billion (the three roughly sum to slightly more, reflecting tax items and minority interests).

Three assumptions must hold for this profit to repeat: First, competitors stop adding subsidies; second, rider costs stop rising sharply; third, the in-store share stops being taken by Douyin. Of the three, the first is not Meituan's to decide, the second is decided by regulators, and the third is decided by Douyin. In other words, none of the three preconditions for this profit's sustainability is in Meituan's hands.

This is not to say the profit is fake — it is to say it is "rented": the lease term depends on others.

Two accounting points to watch

First, net profit of RMB 2.071 billion exceeds adjusted net profit of RMB 1.850 billion. Typically "adjusted" strips out equity-based compensation, so the figure should be higher; here it is lower, meaning the company also excluded fair value gains on investments, and excluded more of those than equity-based compensation. This is a conservative approach and deserves credit, but it also reminds us: part of net profit was not earned from operations.

Second, operating profit of RMB 1.71 billion versus net profit of RMB 2.071 billion — a gap of RMB 360 million. That RMB 360 million is investment mark-to-market gains, interest income and disposal gains, which may not exist next year, or may turn negative. To gauge this company's true earnings power, look at RMB 1.71 billion, not RMB 2.071 billion.

One more number worth noting: core local commerce operating margin of 5.6%, down from 7.9% quarter on quarter. Management calls it seasonal, and I half believe it — summer is indeed peak food-delivery season with heavy subsidies, but a drop to 5.6% also shows this business's margin is highly sensitive to seasonality. A company whose margin is sensitive to seasonality should not command a high multiple.

Competition: a ceasefire is not peace

The most misread thing this quarter is treating "competitors have stopped fighting" as "competitors have left." Share has stalled at 50%, Taobao Shangou at 43%, JD at 7% — all three are still there, they just are not marking up prices anymore.

Why has no one raised prices? Because none of them can make the math work. Gross profit per food-delivery order is RMB 1–2, and it has to cover the management cost, social insurance and dispatch systems for 6 million riders; spending RMB 10 billion to gain 2 points of share implies a payback period of over five years. Under these conditions, the rational choice is to stop.

But rationality has a shelf life. As soon as one company's board changes hands, or one company wants to pump its numbers in a given quarter, subsidies will return. That is exactly how 2025 began. So I will not write the "ceasefire" into my permanent assumptions; I will only write it as the base case for the next 4–6 quarters.

Contrarian: How could this company die?

Ask the reverse question: under what circumstances would Meituan disappear? First, regulators classify riders as employees, doubling per-order costs; second, a competitor is first to make unmanned delivery work, zeroing out the density advantage; third, the traffic gateway shifts and users stop opening the app; fourth, management makes a major mistake (such as unlimited investment in Brazil).

Of these four, the first is industry-wide (everyone is affected, and Meituan, being the largest, is best able to withstand it); the second is technological (Meituan is investing in it itself); the third is structural (the most dangerous); the fourth is controllable.

My conclusion: this company will not die within ten years, but it may barely make money for ten years. The valuation gap between these two scenarios is 2.5x — so you can only buy at the price of "not dying," not at the price of "winning." HKD 67.85 is close to the price of "not dying."

Conclusion

This profit is rented, and the lease term depends on the counterparty. Accounting is conservative (adjusted profit below net profit), but none of the three preconditions for profit sustainability are in its own hands. It will not die within a decade, but it may barely make money for a decade. Conclusion: HKD 67.85 is close to the "won't die" price—you can buy, but don't expect it to become a growth stock.
