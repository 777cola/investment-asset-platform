Core view

Taken together, the four deep dives point to three areas of consensus: the inflection point is confirmed, profit quality is unremarkable and capital allocation is conservative. Two points of disagreement remain: how long this profit can last, and whether to invest in overseas expansion now.

Consensus one: the inflection point is real

All four agree the inflection point holds. The evidence is hard: revenue +21.6%, a three-year high; net profit of RMB 2.071 billion, against -RMB 18.632 billion a year earlier; core local commerce profitable at the operating level for two consecutive quarters; and the marketing expense ratio down from 40% to 22.9%.

Duan Yongping points to users who did not leave, Buffett to gross profit up RMB 8.7 billion, Munger to expenses cut by RMB 10.8 billion, and Li Lu to category expansion. Four investors reading the same quarter from different angles—consistency on that scale is rare in earnings analysis.

Consensus 2: Mediocre profit quality

Of the RMB 2.071 billion, operating profit was RMB 1.71 billion and non-operating income RMB 360 million, an operating margin of 1.5% — a company with RMB 450 billion of annual revenue earning just RMB 1.7 billion in a quarter. All four made this point, only in different words: Duan Yongping said "the direction is right," Buffett said "thin," Munger said "rented," and Li Lu said "not important."

The shared meaning: do not annualise this quarter's profit. 2.071 × 4 = RMB 8.28 billion, implying 46x — an absurd valuation. The right approach is to look at normalised 2027 profit of about RMB 23 billion, implying 16.8x.

Disagreement one: how long can this profit last

Duan Yongping is more optimistic: users are still there, management has stopped the spending, the expense ratio still has 7 points of room, and profit will keep recovering. Munger is more cautious: none of the three premises (rivals do not raise prices, rider costs do not rise, in-store does not lose ground) are in Meituan's hands, and the truce may last only 4–6 quarters.

Our combined view: the next 4–6 quarters are a "ceasefire window" and profit will keep improving; from the second half of 2027, watch rivals' capex pace. On valuation, 19x is justifiable for 2027, but it cannot be extrapolated linearly to 2028.

Disagreement two: should we invest in overseas expansion now?

Buffett's objection: USD 1 billion deployed into buybacks at the current price would retire 2.6% of shares — a certain return. Li Lu's counter: 22 months of profitability in Saudi Arabia prove the model is replicable; if it works, the valuation framework shifts.

Our treatment: no overseas profit in the base case, contribution only in the bull case; a downgrade by one notch if annual losses in Brazil exceed RMB 5 billion. This neither denies the option's value nor pays full price for it. A "no-judgment" treatment — but in investing, no judgment is itself a judgment: we assign no valuation.

Synthesis conclusion: one quarterly report, three uses

For holders: this quarterly report confirms "no need to sell." The business is intact, cash flow has turned positive and management is pulling back on spending—no reason to sell at this price.

For those on the sidelines: this quarterly report supports "start buying." At HKD 67.85, 16.8x 2027E and 11.5x ex-net-cash, with 1:1.9 odds, it is worth a 30% position to test.

For the heavily positioned: this quarterly report does not support "going all in." The 50% share has not recovered, the marketing expense ratio remains 7 points too high, and the RMB 23 billion net profit for 2027 is an assumption. If any one of these three is disproven, the valuation must be revised down.

Conclusion

Three points of consensus — the inflection is real, quality is average, capital allocation is conservative — plus two debates (profit sustainability, overseas expansion) come down to one line: buy 30%, pay nothing extra for the overseas story, and decide in 2027 whether to add.
