Core view

Meituan reported Q3 2026 results on the evening of November 27: revenue of RMB 115.56 billion, up 21.6% YoY, and net profit of RMB 2.071 billion, versus a loss of RMB 18.632 billion a year earlier. The stock rose 8.21% the next day to close at HKD 67.85, still 36% below its 52-week high.

The inflection point is real, but not the kind of inflection point people mean.

The market read this as "the food-delivery war is over." A more accurate reading: the worst quarter has passed, but the war is not over — it has only entered a low-intensity phase.

Take share as the first piece of evidence. Meituan held about 65% of order volume in early 2025, about 51% in February 2026 and about 50% this quarter. The decline has stopped, but there is no recovery. Had the war truly ended, share should rebound; the absence of a rebound shows the rivals have not left — they have simply stopped bidding up.

The marketing expense ratio is the second. 22.9% this quarter (estimated), versus 16% in 2024. Still 7 points apart. Those 7 points are the "war premium" — it has not disappeared, it is just not being fought for now.

Why revenue can accelerate: three numbers

First number: +21.6%. That is the fastest growth since 2024, and it came in a quarter when subsidies were scaled back — only one explanation fits: subsidies were not what retained users.

Second number: delivery services at roughly RMB 30 billion, up about +20% YoY. Delivery revenue = order volume × average delivery fee per order, so a 20% increase means at least one of the two is rising.

Third number: new businesses at RMB 35.54 billion, up +30.8% YoY. That is the fastest of the three lines. Flash sales, pharmaceuticals, liquor, fresh produce — more goods sold through the same fulfillment network, at near-zero marginal cost.

Together the three numbers tell a clear story: users did not leave, order volume did not fall, and categories are expanding. On the revenue side, this is the healthiest combination available.

Profit of just RMB 2.071 billion: squeezed out by cost savings

Gross profit RMB 40.06 billion (34.7%), sales and marketing RMB 26.5 billion (22.9%), R&D RMB 8.65 billion (7.5%) and administrative expenses RMB 3.2 billion — operating profit works out to RMB 1.71 billion, a 1.5% operating margin.

That marks a RMB 19.5 billion YoY improvement, with gross profit contributing RMB 8.7 billion and expense savings RMB 10.8 billion. In other words, 56% of the improvement came from spending less and 44% from earning more. Hence our view that profit here is saved, not earned.

How much more room is there to save? The marketing expense ratio fell from 22.9% to 16% in 2024, leaving 7 points, equivalent to about RMB 32 billion a year. That is theoretical, however — if rivals start spending again, those 7 points will not hold.

Three things to watch

First, Q4 core local commerce operating profit. Q3 is peak season (summer) and Q4 is off-season. If Q4 holds at RMB 5 billion, the recovery is not seasonal; if it falls below RMB 3.5 billion, Q3 was merely a peak-season effect.

Second, merchant services revenue growth. At about RMB 44 billion this quarter, up only 8% YoY, this is the weakest line in the entire table. Merchants are recovering more slowly than users, which means the supply side has been damaged. If Q4 cannot return above 10%, the "quality" of revenue is in question.

Third, Brazil. The 5-year USD 1 billion investment commitment is the only variable where "how much we lose is up to us." New business losses widened from RMB 1.74 billion to RMB 2.16 billion, mainly because of it. If 2027 losses exceed RMB 5 billion, this investment should be held to account.

Price: is it still worth it after +8.21%?

HKD 67.85, HKD 419 billion market cap, RMB 385.5 billion (at 0.92 exchange rate). Netting out RMB 120 billion net cash, the core business is RMB 265.5 billion. On 2027 net profit of RMB 23 billion, that is 16.8x including net cash and 11.5x excluding it.

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 52 (-23%), base case HKD 98 (+44%), bull case HKD 149 (+119%). Odds of 1 : 1.9, probability-weighted at 30/50/20, imply an expected return of +39%.

Those odds are not spectacular, but the win rate is rising. The +8.21% move priced the probability of "the subsidy war is over" rising from 40% to 60%; it did not price a return to 10%+ margins in 2027. So HKD 67.85 is neither the endpoint nor the starting point — it is the midpoint.

Action: split into three tranches, lock in discipline

First tranche 30% at the current price; second tranche 30% below HKD 58 (9.5x net of cash); third tranche 40% once the Q1 2027 report confirms Q4 core local commerce operating profit ≥ RMB 5 billion.

The reduction trigger is equally hard-coded: cut unconditionally if Q4 core local commerce operating profit falls below RMB 3.5 billion or unit share drops below 48%. Either outcome would mark Q3 as a dead-cat bounce rather than an inflection point.

One honest note to close: the biggest risk to this company isn't competition, it's itself — RMB 120 billion of net cash sits on the balance sheet and buybacks are just 0.4%. If it ever announces raising buybacks to 5%, I'll fill the position outright. Until then, it's just a "cheap good business", not a "great business".

Conclusion

The inflection point is real, but it marks the end of the worst quarter, not the end of the war. Revenue +21.6% shows users are still there, profit of RMB 2.071 billion shows management can pull back, and the 1.5% operating margin shows how thin the buffer remains. At HKD 67.85, 11.5x ex-net-cash, worth buying 30%; below HKD 58 add 30%; add in full after Q4 2027 data confirms.
