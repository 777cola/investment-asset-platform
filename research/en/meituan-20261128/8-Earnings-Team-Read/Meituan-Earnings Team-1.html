Core view

My habit when reading financial reports is to look at revenue first, then expenses, and only then profit. Revenue tells us whether users are still there; expenses tell us whether management is still panicking; profit is merely the result of the two.

Revenue: users stayed, and that is what matters most

Q3 revenue came in at RMB 115.56 billion, +21.6% YoY and +10.4% QoQ, 1.9% above the consensus of 1.9%. The significance of this number is not that it beat expectations, but that revenue accelerated even as subsidies rolled off. For the past two years the market has worried about one thing: once subsidies stop, order volume falls. This quarter's answer is no.

By segment: delivery services about RMB 30 billion, up about 20% YoY; new businesses RMB 35.54 billion, +30.8% YoY; core local commerce about RMB 80.02 billion, +17.9% YoY. All three lines accelerated at once — not a single-point burst. That is what I value most. A single-point burst could be promotion; three lines accelerating together shows demand itself is recovering.

One line looks weak: merchant services about RMB 44 billion, up only 8% YoY. Merchants are the other half of this business; their slow recovery shows the 2025 battle damaged the supply side. If merchants don't make money, sooner or later the platform won't either. This line needs watching.

Expenses: management knows it is time to pull back

Sales and marketing RMB 26.5 billion, 22.9% of revenue, versus RMB 38 billion, or 40%, a year earlier. Cutting RMB 11.5 billion in a year is not penny-pinching — it is knowing when to pull back. In 2024 this figure was 16%, so there are still 7 points of room.

R&D RMB 8.65 billion, up just 11.3%, or 7.5% of revenue. In a year when every major player is scrambling for compute, that growth rate is restrained. Wang Xing says "we won't be a Token factory," and I believe him — because the numbers line up.

G&A RMB 3.2 billion, -5.3% YoY, essentially flat. Of the three expense lines, two are contracting and one is holding steady — the mark of good management. Some companies cut R&D along with marketing, which is killing the goose that lays the golden eggs.

Profit: RMB 2.071 billion, but look closely at what it is

Net profit of RMB 2.071 billion, versus a loss of RMB 18.632 billion a year earlier. Operating profit RMB 1.71 billion, a 1.5% operating margin. That 1.5% looks ugly, but it is far better than last year's -18.7%.

Two points need to be clear. First, roughly RMB 360 million of the RMB 2.071 billion did not come from operations (investment fair value, interest and the like), which is precisely why adjusted net profit of RMB 1.850 billion is lower. Second, core local commerce operating margin was 5.6%, down 2.3 points QoQ from 7.9% in the second quarter — management flagged this in August (summer marketing, heat subsidies, new work-injury insurance) and delivered as promised.

I don't like forecasting quarterly profit, because it depends on competitors. But the direction this quarter is clear: the shift from "losing money for share" to "saving money to protect profit" has already happened.

One thing I am least satisfied with: too few buybacks

Net cash of RMB 120 billion, interest-bearing debt close to zero, and a USD 2 billion buyback authorisation—just 0.4% of the HKD 419 billion market cap.A company that believes its stock is undervalued should be buying back 5%, not 0.4%.

Cash on the balance sheet does not grow. Either pay dividends, buy back stock, or invest where returns can be calculated (cold-chain for instant retail, for instance).Brazil is another matter—that is a place where I cannot calculate the returns.

So my scorecard for this quarterly report: business A, management B+, capital allocation C+.Buy? A little. Go all in? No.

Conclusion

The key takeaway from the quarter is that users stayed after subsidies ended. Second, management knows when to stop. The weakest point is capital allocation: only 0.4% of RMB 120 billion in cash went to buybacks. Conclusion: the business is worth owning and the price is acceptable, but build the position in tranches, since the timing of a return to margins above 10% is uncertain.
