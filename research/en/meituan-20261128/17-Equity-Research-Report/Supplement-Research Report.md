## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Meituan (03690.HK) is China's largest local life services platform. Wang Xing founded the company in 2010, and it listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in September 2018 (with weighted voting rights). In April 2021, it raised about USD 10 billion through a share placement and convertible bonds. Market cap at the close on November 27, 2026 was HKD 419 billion, ranking among the top ten Hong Kong-listed tech companies by market cap.

The business has two segments: Core Local Commerce (food delivery, Meituan Instashopping/instant retail, in-store dining and travel, with revenue of about RMB 80.02 billion in Q3 2026, or 69.3%); and New Initiatives (Xiaoxiang Supermarket, Kuailv, Happy Monkey community supermarkets, and Keeta overseas, with revenue of RMB 35.54 billion, or 30.7%).

Infrastructure: about 6 million active delivery riders, about 8 million active merchants, and coverage of 2,800 counties and districts. Starting July 1, 2026, the "new occupational injury" insurance for riders covers the whole country, with the platform bearing the full premium.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's local life services market is about RMB 2.5 trillion (food delivery RMB 550 billion + in-store services + instant retail), with an online penetration rate of about 30%. Food delivery penetration is about 25% (RMB 140 billion GTV), while instant retail penetration is below 5% (deliverable categories about RMB 800 billion).

2025 is an inflection point for the industry: JD.com entered in February, Alibaba upgraded "Hourly Delivery" to "Taobao Shangou" in April, and subsidy spending exceeded RMB 100 billion for the full year. The result is a 50/43/7 stalemate among the three players — no one exited, but all stopped bidding up prices.

The industry's 2026 characteristics are "ceasefire + divergence": food delivery enters low-intensity competition, in-store is continuously eroded by Douyin, and instant retail becomes the new battleground. Douyin's 2025 in-store GTV exceeded RMB 80 billion, with growth near 60%, but 2026 growth will slow to about 30%.

## Chapter 3 Business model

Meituan's model can be summed up as "using high-margin in-store profit to subsidize the low-margin fulfillment network, then using that fulfillment network to sell more categories." The three businesses play different roles:

- In-store, hotel and travel: gross margin above 80%, operating margin around 30% — the profit pool.

- Food delivery: gross profit of RMB 1–2 per order — the traffic gateway and fulfillment infrastructure, thin-margin by nature.

- Instant retail (Instashopping, Xiaoxiang): reuses the same network at low marginal cost — the growth curve.

The key variable in this model is "density": the higher the average daily order volume per site, the lower the per-order cost. The improvement in 2026Q3 came from higher density (per-order delivery loss narrowing from RMB 2.5 to within RMB 0.5) and the rollback of subsidies.

Cash flow: Q3 operating cash flow about RMB 8.5 billion, capex about RMB 2.5 billion, free cash flow about RMB 6.0 billion (estimated). Food delivery prepayments and monthly merchant settlement keep working capital negative, and cash conversion has long stayed above 1.

## Chapter 5 Management

Wang Xing (Chairman and CEO, age 47, holds about 9% and over 40% of voting power) founded Meituan in 2010, after starting Xiaonei and Fanfou. At the height of the subsidy war in 2025 he chose to "opt out of involution and defend unit economics," at the cost of a full-year net loss of RMB 23.36 billion and a share drop from 65% to 51%; by Q3 2026 this was vindicated.

Core management: Mu Rongjun (co-founder and senior vice president, oversees technology and compliance), Wang Puzhong (CEO of Core Local Commerce, appointed April 2024), Chen Shaohui (CFO, led the 2021 financing and the buybacks from 2024).

Capital allocation: a USD 2 billion buyback approved in June 2024 and reauthorized in June 2026, executed in small size within the HKD 66.5–69 range; no dividend; restrained R&D spending (no large-model "token factory"); an overseas Brazil agreement worth USD 1 billion over five years. The modest buyback scale (0.4% of market cap) is the main shortcoming.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Driver 1: Instant retail penetration. Deliverable categories total about RMB 8 trillion, with penetration below 5%; Instashopping and new initiatives revenue rose 30.8% in 2026Q3. This is the main incremental driver over the next three years.

Driver 2: In-store penetration into lower-tier cities and travel recovery. In-store services still have penetration room in lower-tier cities; travel is lifted by recovering services consumption.

Driver 3: Overseas replication. Keeta reached unit-economic profitability in Hong Kong in October 2025 and in Saudi Arabia in July 2026 (22 months), with Saudi Arabia accounting for about 25% of Keeta's daily orders; Brazil entry in 2026, USD 1 billion over 5 years.

Driver 4: Margin recovery. Marketing expense ratio at 22.9% leaves 7 points of room versus 16% in 2024, equivalent to about RMB 32 billion annualized.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Food delivery: Meituan about 50%, Taobao Shangou about 43%, JD.com about 7% (by order volume, Goldman Sachs 2026/03 and industry estimates). All three have stopped raising prices, and the landscape has been stable for three quarters.

In-store: Meituan about 60%, Douyin about 40% (by GTV, industry estimates). Douyin's in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion in 2025, with growth near 60%, slowing to about 30% in 2026. Douyin is the most dangerous rival—it is capturing in-store, where gross margin is 80%, not fulfillment, where gross margin is 10%.

Instant retail: Meituan Shangou, Taobao Shangou and JD Miaosong form a three-way contest; by order volume Meituan still ranks first but no longer holds more than half. The competitive focus is shifting from "subsidies" to "categories and fulfillment speed".

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

1. Loss of in-store share: If share falls from 60% to 50%, 2027 net profit drops from RMB 23 billion to below RMB 15 billion. 2. Subsidy restart: If the marketing expense ratio returns to 30%, full-year spending rises by RMB 32 billion and the company swings back to a loss. 3. Rider costs converted to employee costs: If classified as an employment relationship, cost per order rises from RMB 0.2 to above RMB 2, turning industry unit economics negative.

4. Brazil losses out of control: USD 1 billion over 5 years is the committed cap; annual losses above RMB 5 billion would require a downgrade. 5. Traffic entry point migration: If users shift to ordering via WeChat/Douyin/AI assistants, the valuation framework gets rebuilt. 6. Hong Kong liquidity: The Hang Seng Tech Index has fallen more than 25% in 2026, and valuation compression may persist.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

Method: sum of the parts plus net cash. 2027E net profit RMB 23 billion × 19x = RMB 437 billion + net cash RMB 120 billion = RMB 557 billion, or HKD 605.4 billion, HKD 98 per share.

Why 19x and not 25x: no pricing power (cannot raise prices), share has not recovered (50%), profit is determined by competitors. Why not 15x: strong cash flow (FCF is 3x net profit), net cash is 31% of market cap, revenue growth 21.6%.

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 52 (net profit RMB 11 billion, 16x), base case HKD 98, bull case HKD 149 (net profit RMB 33 billion, 22x). Risk/reward 1 : 1.9; probability-weighted (30/50/20) expected return +39%.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Buy, in tranches. First 30% at HKD 67.85, add to 60% below HKD 58, fill to 5% after Q4 2027 data confirms. Do not chase above HKD 74.
