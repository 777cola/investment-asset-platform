Chapter 1 Company Overview

Meituan (03690.HK) is China's largest local life services platform. Wang Xing founded the company in 2010 and it listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in September 2018 with weighted voting rights; in April 2021 it raised approximately USD 10bn through a share placement and convertible bonds. Market cap at the close on 27 November 2026 was HKD 419bn, ranking among the top ten Hong Kong-listed tech companies by market cap.

The business has two segments: Core Local Commerce (food delivery, Meituan Instashopping/instant retail, in-store dining and travel), which generated revenue of approximately RMB 80.02bn in Q3 2026, or 69.3%; and New Initiatives (Xiaoxiang Supermarket, Kuailv, Happy Monkey community supermarkets and Keeta overseas), with revenue of RMB 35.54bn, or 30.7%.

Infrastructure: approximately 6 million active delivery riders, approximately 8 million active merchants and coverage of 2,800 counties and districts. From 1 July 2026, "new occupational injury" insurance for riders covers the whole country, with the platform bearing the full premium.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's local life services market is worth roughly RMB 2.5 trillion — food delivery at RMB 550 billion, plus in-store services and instant retail — with online penetration around 30%. Food delivery penetration sits at roughly 25% (RMB 140 billion GTV), while instant retail penetration remains below 5% (deliverable categories approximately RMB 800 billion).

2025 marked an inflection point for the industry: JD.com entered in February, Alibaba upgraded "Hourly Delivery" to "Taobao Shangou" in April, and subsidy spending topped RMB 100 billion for the full year. The outcome is a 50/43/7 stalemate among the three players — no one exited, but all stopped bidding up prices.

The industry's 2026 characteristics are "ceasefire + divergence": food delivery enters low-intensity competition, in-store continues to be eroded by Douyin, and instant retail becomes the new battleground. Douyin's 2025 in-store GTV exceeded RMB 80 billion, with growth near 60%, but 2026 growth will slow to around 30%.

Chapter 3 Business model

Meituan's model boils down to "using high-margin in-store profit to subsidise the low-margin fulfilment network, then using that network to sell more categories." Each of the three businesses plays a distinct role:

  • In-store, hotel and travel: gross margin above 80%, operating margin around 30% — the profit pool.
  • Food delivery: gross profit of RMB 1–2 per order — the traffic gateway and fulfilment infrastructure, thin-margin by nature.
  • Instant retail (Instashopping, Xiaoxiang): reuses the same network at low marginal cost — the growth curve.

Density is the key variable: the higher the average daily order volume per site, the lower the per-order cost. The 2026Q3 improvement came from higher density — per-order delivery loss narrowing from RMB 2.5 to within RMB 0.5 — and from the rollback of subsidies.

Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

2026Q3: revenue RMB 115.56 billion (+21.6%), gross profit RMB 40.06 billion (34.7%, estimated), operating profit RMB 1.71 billion (1.5%, estimated), net profit RMB 2.071 billion, adjusted net profit RMB 1.850 billion. Revenue growth accelerated for a third straight quarter (+9.2% → +14.4% → +21.6%), the fastest since 2024.

The profit improvement breaks down as gross profit up RMB 8.7 billion YoY, sales and marketing down RMB 11.5 billion, R&D up RMB 880 million and operating profit up RMB 19.5 billion. 56% from cost savings, 44% from earnings.

Balance sheet: cash of RMB 104.7 billion plus short-term wealth management products of RMB 63.6 billion, interest-bearing debt near zero and net cash of about RMB 120 billion (31% of market cap). Net loss totalled RMB 2.60 billion in the first three quarters of 2026; if Q4 stays profitable, the full year is close to break-even.

Cash flow: Q3 operating cash flow of about RMB 8.5 billion, capex of about RMB 2.5 billion and free cash flow of about RMB 6.0 billion (estimated). Food delivery prepayments and monthly merchant settlement keep working capital negative, and cash conversion has long stayed above 1.

Chapter 5 Management

Wang Xing (Chairman and CEO, age 47, holds about 9% and over 40% of voting power) founded Meituan in 2010, having previously started Xiaonei and Fanfou. At the height of the 2025 subsidy war he chose to "opt out of involution and defend unit economics," accepting a full-year net loss of RMB 23.36 billion and a share-price fall from 65% to 51%; by Q3 2026 the strategy was vindicated.

Core management: Mu Rongjun (co-founder and senior vice president, oversees technology and compliance), Wang Puzhong (CEO of Core Local Commerce, appointed April 2024) and Chen Shaohui (CFO, led the 2021 financing and the buybacks from 2024).

Capital allocation: a USD 2 billion buyback approved in June 2024 and reauthorized in June 2026, executed in modest size within the HKD 66.5–69 range; no dividend; restrained R&D spending (no large-model "token factory"); an overseas Brazil agreement worth USD 1 billion over five years. The modest buyback scale (0.4% of market cap) is the main shortcoming.

Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Driver 1: Instant retail penetration. Deliverable categories total ~RMB 8 trillion at sub-5% penetration, and Instashopping and new initiatives revenue grew 30.8% in 2026Q3. This is the primary incremental driver over the next three years.

Driver 2: In-store penetration in lower-tier cities and travel recovery. In-store services retain meaningful penetration upside in lower-tier cities, while travel benefits from a recovery in services consumption.

Driver 3: Overseas replication. Keeta reached unit-economic profitability in Hong Kong (October 2025) and Saudi Arabia (July 2026, 22 months post-launch), with Saudi now ~25% of Keeta daily orders; Brazil launches in 2026, backed by USD 1 billion over 5 years.

Driver 4: Margin recovery. Marketing at 22.9% of revenue leaves 7 ppt of upside to the 16% achieved in 2024, equivalent to ~RMB 32 billion annualised.

Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Food delivery: Meituan ~50%, Taobao Shangou ~43%, JD.com ~7% (by order volume; Goldman Sachs March 2026 and industry estimates). All three players have stopped raising prices, and the competitive landscape has been stable for three quarters.

In-store: Meituan ~60%, Douyin ~40% (by GTV; industry estimates). Douyin's in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800bn in 2025, growing near 60% before slowing to ~30% in 2026. Douyin is the most dangerous rival: it is attacking in-store, where gross margin is 80%, not fulfilment, where gross margin is 10%.

Instant retail: Meituan Shangou, Taobao Shangou and JD Miaosong are locked in a three-way contest; Meituan still ranks first by order volume but no longer holds majority share. Competition is shifting from subsidies to category breadth and fulfilment speed.

Chapter 8 Risk factors

1. Loss of in-store share: A slide in share from 60% to 50% would cut 2027 net profit from RMB23bn to below RMB15bn. 2. Subsidy restart: A return of the marketing expense ratio to 30% would add RMB32bn to full-year spending and push the company back into loss. 3. Rider costs reclassified as employee costs: An employment relationship would lift cost per order from RMB0.2 to above RMB2, turning industry unit economics negative.

4. Brazil losses out of control: USD1bn over 5 years is the committed cap; annual losses above RMB5bn would trigger a downgrade. 5. Traffic entry point migration: A shift by users to ordering via WeChat/Douyin/AI assistants would force a rebuild of the valuation framework. 6. Hong Kong liquidity: The Hang Seng Tech Index has fallen more than 25% in 2026, and valuation compression may persist.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecasts

2026E: revenue RMB 429.2bn (+17.6%), operating profit RMB 2.4bn (0.6% margin), net profit attributable to shareholders -RMB 100m (roughly break-even). Q4 assumptions: revenue +2% QoQ to RMB 118.0bn and operating profit of RMB 4.5bn as subsidies taper off, though the quarter is seasonally weak.

2027E: revenue RMB 490.0bn (+14.2%), comprising RMB 345.0bn from core local commerce and RMB 145.0bn from new initiatives. Core local commerce operating margin 10% (345.0 × 10% = RMB 34.5bn), new initiatives loss RMB 2.0bn and unallocated RMB 5.0bn, giving operating profit of RMB 27.5bn; after tax and minority interests, net profit RMB 23.0bn (4.7% net margin).

Sensitivity: each 1pt shift in core local commerce operating margin moves 2027E net profit by ~RMB 2.6bn (~11%); each 1pt shift in the marketing expense ratio moves net profit by ~RMB 3.7bn. Margin, not revenue, is the key driver.

Chapter 10 Valuation

Methodology: sum of the parts plus net cash. 2027E net profit of RMB 23bn at 19x = RMB 437bn, plus net cash of RMB 120bn = RMB 557bn, or HKD 605.4bn, equal to HKD 98 per share.

Why 19x rather than 25x: no pricing power, a share price that has not recovered (50%), and profit set by competitors. Why not 15x: strong cash flow (FCF at 3x net profit), net cash at 31% of market cap, and revenue growth of 21.6%.

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 52 (net profit RMB 11bn, 16x), base case HKD 98, bull case HKD 149 (net profit RMB 33bn, 22x). Risk/reward of 1:1.9; probability-weighted (30/50/20) expected return of +39%.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Buy in tranches: 30% at HKD 67.85, scale to 60% below HKD 58, fill to 5% once 2027Q4 data confirms. Do not chase above HKD 74.

Exit triggers: 2026Q4 core local commerce operating profit <RMB 3.5 billion, unit volume share <48%, or marketing expense ratio back above 26% — any one cuts the position to below 0.5%.

Difference vs. consensus: the market reads +8.21% as "the subsidy war is over"; we see only a ceasefire. The market values Meituan on 2026 losses; we value it on 2027 normalized profit. The disagreement is how long the ceasefire holds — we assume 4–6 quarters.

Conclusion

Buy (reiterated), target price HKD 98, 44% upside. Core thesis: margin recovery from the truce (5.6%→10%), instant retail growth (+30.8%) and net cash at 31% of market cap. Key risks: in-store erosion by Douyin, subsidy restart, Brazil out of control. Triggers: Q4 core local commerce operating profit ≥ RMB 5 billion, share back to 51%, marketing expense ratio ≤22% — upgrade if two of the three are met.
