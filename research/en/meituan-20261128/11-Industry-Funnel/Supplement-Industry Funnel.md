## Screening: three filters eliminate four companies

The first filter: the business must sit in local services or instant retail—Pinduoduo has the best business quality (operating margin around 25%, valuation at 10x), but it runs far-field e-commerce and does not belong in this funnel. This filter rules it out first, not because it is weak, but because it is not comparable.

The second filter: it must be possible to pin down where profit comes from—JD.com's retail operating margin is only 3%–4%, its food-delivery share is about 7%, and its fulfillment cost is the highest of the four. Its business model is "trade logistics for experience"; that logic holds in 3C and home appliances but not in food delivery: delivery demands 30-minute turnaround, not next-day delivery, so the heavy-asset advantage of self-built logistics does not apply.

The third filter: it must be investable—Douyin is strongest in in-store services (GTV above RMB 800 billion, growth near 60%), but it is unlisted and cannot be invested in; Trip.com covers only the hotel and travel segment, and its scale is insufficient. Two remain after screening: Meituan and Alibaba.

## Final: Meituan vs Alibaba — the same contest

Both are fighting the same war, but the math differs. Alibaba's edge is traffic from 800 million users on its main site and the cash flow from e-commerce profits to fund the fight; Meituan's edge is delivery density and the high gross margin of in-store. Q3 2026: Meituan revenue +21.6%, net profit turned positive; Alibaba's instant retail remains loss-making and is subsidized by e-commerce.

On valuation, Alibaba trades at about 18x non-GAAP, Meituan at 16.8x 2027E and 11.5x after net cash. The two are roughly comparable, but Meituan's denominator is "cleaner" — its losses come from its own cash, not from e-commerce profits. More important is the elasticity: Meituan's marketing expense ratio still has 7 points of room to fall, while Alibaba's instant retail loss ratio has already narrowed to single digits, leaving less elasticity.

There is also an incomparable factor: the HKD 80 billion Alibaba allocated in 2026 went into AI infrastructure, not food delivery. That says two things — Alibaba believes AI offers a higher return than food delivery, and it does not intend to invest without limit in food delivery. For Meituan, this is a more important signal than any financial figure.

## Why Meituan: three points

First: it is the only company that holds both in-store (80% gross margin) and fulfillment (number one in density). Douyin has in-store but no fulfillment; JD has fulfillment but no in-store; Alibaba has both but is not first in either. This combination lets it reprice between the two ends, and gives it one more life than rivals.

Second: it is the only company to deliver both revenue acceleration and a swing to profit in 2026Q3. Revenue +21.6% (a three-year high) and net profit RMB 2.071 billion (a loss of RMB 18.632 billion in the year-earlier period). Both happened as subsidies tapered, meaning order volume held and costs came in — the two hardest things to achieve at the same time.

Third: it is the only company the market has pushed to 11.5x ex-net-cash. Down 36% from its 52-week high, market cap HKD 419 billion; after deducting RMB 120 billion of net cash, the core business is valued at RMB 265.5 billion. This price is not pricing in "winning"; it is pricing in "surviving." And Q3 proves it is not just surviving — it is recovering.

## The Funnel in Reverse: When the Wrong Choice Gets Picked

This funnel has an obvious hole: it mistakes "number one in share" for "eventual winner." If three players end up splitting the market and industry margins stay below 10% forever, then Meituan's current price is merely "fair," not "cheap" — which corresponds to the bear case of HKD 52.

The second hole is Douyin. Douyin is not investable, but it can determine Meituan's valuation. If in-store GTV growth holds above 30% for three years, Meituan's in-store profit gets halved, 2027 net profit falls below RMB 15 billion, and the base case of HKD 98 becomes HKD 65. This risk is not in Meituan's financials; it is in its rival's financials.

So the final pick is Meituan, but the position cannot be heavy — what the funnel screens out is "the least bad choice," not "the best choice." In this industry, the best choice may not exist at all.
