Core view

Screen six Chinese local-services and e-commerce names on one table, crudely on "business quality × valuation": PDD has the best business but no local-services exposure; Alibaba has the largest scale but food delivery is a war of attrition; JD.com carries the highest fulfillment cost; Douyin has the strongest in-store offering but is not investable; Trip.com is travel-only.

That leaves two investable names—Meituan and Alibaba. We go with Meituan: it is the only company that holds both "high-margin in-store" and "high-density fulfillment," and the only one to deliver both revenue acceleration and a swing to profit in Q3 2026.

Screening: three filters eliminate four companies

First filter: the business must sit in local services or instant retail—Pinduoduo has the best business quality (operating margin around 25%, valuation at 10x), but it runs far-field e-commerce and does not belong in this funnel. It is ruled out first not because it is weak, but because it is not comparable.

Second filter: it must be possible to pin down where profit comes from—JD.com's retail operating margin is only 3%–4%, its food-delivery share is about 7%, and its fulfillment cost is the highest of the four. Its model trades logistics for experience; that logic holds in 3C and home appliances but not in food delivery, which demands 30-minute turnaround rather than next-day delivery, so the heavy-asset advantage of self-built logistics does not apply.

Third filter: it must be investable—Douyin is strongest in in-store services (GTV above RMB 800 billion, growth near 60%), but it is unlisted and cannot be invested in; Trip.com covers only the hotel and travel segment, and its scale is insufficient. Two remain after screening: Meituan and Alibaba.

Final: Meituan vs Alibaba — the same contest

Both are fighting the same war, but the math differs. Alibaba's edge is traffic from 800 million users on its main site and the cash flow from e-commerce profits to fund the fight; Meituan's edge is delivery density and the high gross margin of in-store. In Q3 2026, Meituan's revenue rose 21.6% and net profit turned positive, while Alibaba's instant retail remains loss-making and is subsidised by e-commerce.

On valuation, Alibaba trades at about 18x non-GAAP and Meituan at 16.8x 2027E, or 11.5x after net cash. The two are roughly comparable, but Meituan's denominator is "cleaner" — its losses come from its own cash, not from e-commerce profits. More important is the elasticity: Meituan's marketing expense ratio still has 7 points of room to fall, whereas Alibaba's instant retail loss ratio has already narrowed to single digits, leaving less elasticity.

There is also an incomparable factor: the HKD 80 billion Alibaba allocated in 2026 went into AI infrastructure, not food delivery. That says two things — Alibaba believes AI offers a higher return than food delivery, and it does not intend to invest without limit in food delivery. For Meituan, this is a more important signal than any financial figure.

Why Meituan: three points

First, it is the only company with both in-store (80% gross margin) and fulfilment (number one in density). Douyin has in-store but no fulfilment; JD has fulfilment but no in-store; Alibaba has both but leads in neither. That combination lets it reprice between the two ends and gives it one more life than rivals.

Second, it is the only company to post both revenue acceleration and a swing to profit in 2026Q3. Revenue rose 21.6% (a three-year high) and net profit reached RMB 2.071 billion, against a loss of RMB 18.632 billion a year earlier. Both came as subsidies tapered — order volume held and costs came in, the two hardest things to achieve at once.

Third, it is the only company the market has pushed to 11.5x ex-net-cash. The stock is down 36% from its 52-week high, with market cap at HKD 419 billion; after deducting RMB 120 billion of net cash, the core business is valued at RMB 265.5 billion. This price does not price in "winning"; it prices in "surviving." And Q3 proves it is not merely surviving — it is recovering.

The Funnel in Reverse: When the Wrong Choice Gets Picked

This funnel has an obvious hole: it mistakes "number one in share" for "eventual winner." If three players end up splitting the market and industry margins stay below 10% in perpetuity, Meituan's current price is merely "fair," not "cheap" — the bear case of HKD 52.

The second hole is Douyin. Douyin is not investable, but it can determine Meituan's valuation. If in-store GTV growth holds above 30% for three years, Meituan's in-store profit gets halved, 2027 net profit falls below RMB 15 billion, and the base case of HKD 98 becomes HKD 65. This risk sits not in Meituan's financials but in its rival's.

So the final pick is Meituan, but the position cannot be heavy — what the funnel screens out is "the least bad choice," not "the best choice." In this industry, the best choice may not exist at all.

Conclusion

Six screened down to two, and Meituan is the final pick. Three sentences: the only one with both in-store and fulfillment; the only one delivering both revenue acceleration and a swing to profit; and the only one beaten down to 11.5x net cash. The other side must be stated clearly too: the funnel treats "number one by share" as "the eventual winner", and this industry may have no eventual winner at all. So this is "the least bad choice", and position size must be controlled — 30% for the first tranche, adding another 30% if it falls to HKD 58.
