Title and lead-in

Meituan's Quarterly Report Rescues Not Profit, but the Narrative

On the evening of November 27, Meituan reported its Q3 2026 results. The stock rose 8.21% the next day to close at HKD 67.85.

Many say "the food delivery war is over." I say no. What ended was "the worst quarter," not "the war." What this report truly rescued was a narrative—that Meituan can still make money.

First, the numbers. Revenue was RMB 115.56 billion, up 21.6% year on year, the fastest since 2024; up 10.4% quarter on quarter; 1.9% above the consensus of RMB 113.37 billion. Net profit was RMB 2.07 billion—versus a loss of RMB 18.63 billion in the same period last year. Adjusted net profit was RMB 1.85 billion, turning positive but slightly below consensus.

Layer one: why revenue can accelerate

This is the most counterintuitive part of the entire earnings report.

For the past two years, everyone worried about one thing: once subsidies stop, order volume falls. So when Meituan began scaling back subsidies in 2025, the market's judgment was that "revenue would stall."

The opposite happened. Q1 +9.2%, Q2 +14.4%, Q3 +21.6% — three consecutive quarters of acceleration. Revenue accelerating in quarters when subsidies were phased out leaves only one explanation — users were not retained by subsidies.

Breaking it down: delivery services about RMB 30 billion, up about +20% year on year; new businesses RMB 35.54 billion, up +30.8% year on year; core local commerce about RMB 80.02 billion, up +17.9% year on year. All three lines accelerated simultaneously — this is not a single-point breakout.

One line looks weak: merchant services about RMB 44 billion, up only 8% year on year. Merchants are recovering more slowly than users, indicating that the 2025 battle damaged the supply side. Keep watching this line.

The second layer: profit comes from cost savings

Net profit of RMB 2.071 billion looks like a turnaround. Broken down, it looks like this:

Gross profit RMB 40.06 billion, gross margin 34.7%, up RMB 8.7 billion year on year; sales and marketing RMB 26.5 billion, expense ratio 22.9%, versus RMB 38 billion and 40% a year earlier, RMB 11.5 billion less spent in a single quarter; R&D RMB 8.65 billion, up RMB 880 million.

Net it out: operating profit swung from -RMB 17.8 billion to +RMB 1.71 billion, an improvement of RMB 19.5 billion. 56% of that came from spending less, 44% from earning more.

That is why I say "profit is saved, not earned." A company with RMB 450 billion in annual revenue earning just RMB 1.7 billion a quarter, an operating margin of 1.5% — thin as paper.

One more thing to be clear about: of the RMB 2.071 billion net profit, about RMB 360 million did not come from operations but from investment fair value, interest and the like. So adjusted net profit of RMB 1.850 billion is actually lower.

Layer 3: what does +8.21% price in

The stock rose 8.21% the day after earnings, adding about HKD 31.8 billion in market cap. What did that price in?

My read: it priced the probability of "the subsidy war is over" moving from 40% to 60%, not 2027 earnings.

The evidence is share. Meituan held about 65% of order volume in early 2025, about 51% in February 2026, and about 50% this quarter. It has stopped falling, but it has not recovered. If the war were truly over, share should rebound; it hasn't, which means rivals haven't left — they've just stopped bidding up.

The other evidence is the marketing expense ratio. It was 22.9% this quarter versus 16% in 2024 — still 7 points apart. Those 7 points are the "war premium"; it hasn't disappeared, the fighting has just paused.

So don't treat +8.21% as a "reversal." It merely swapped the question of "will it keep losing money" for "how much can it earn."

Layer four: how much is it worth

HKD 67.85, HKD 419 billion market cap, equivalent to RMB 385.5 billion. Net cash on the balance sheet is about RMB 120 billion; netting that out leaves the core business at RMB 265.5 billion.

On 2027 net profit of RMB 23 billion: 16.8x including net cash, 11.5x excluding net cash.

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 52 (-23%), base case HKD 98 (+44%), bull case HKD 149 (+119%). Odds of 1 : 1.9 — unremarkable at first glance.

But the probability of success is rising. In August, the market worried about "will it keep losing money forever"; now it worries about "how much can it earn." These are two entirely different questions. The answer to the first is "it may lose money indefinitely"; the answer to the second is "it can earn at least RMB 20 billion."

Finally: the one thing I am least satisfied with

RMB 120 billion net cash on the balance sheet, interest-bearing debt near zero, a USD 2 billion buyback authorization — just 0.4% of the HKD 419 billion market cap.

A company that believes it is undervalued should be buying back 5%, not 0.4%.

Cash sitting on the balance sheet does not grow. Either pay a dividend, buy back stock, or invest where returns can be calculated. Brazil is another matter — that is a place where I cannot calculate the return. USD 1 billion over 5 years, the one battlefield where you set your own downside and your opponent sets your upside.

So my conclusion: this company is worth buying, but in tranches; three tranches — 30% at HKD 67.85, another 30% below HKD 58, and the full position after the Q1 2027 report confirms Q4 profit held above RMB 5 billion.

The exit conditions are equally fixed: if Q4 core local commerce operating profit falls below RMB 3.5 billion, or order volume share falls below 48%, cut unconditionally. Either one means Q3 was a dead-cat bounce, not an inflection point.

In one sentence: Meituan has rescued the narrative, but not yet the margin. This price is worth a bet, but not the whole stack.

Conclusion

Closing line (usable as cover copy): "It has stopped falling, but it has not recovered — those words are Meituan's entire story right now."
