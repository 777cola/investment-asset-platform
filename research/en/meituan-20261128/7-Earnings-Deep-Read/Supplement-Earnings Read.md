## Layer one: why revenue accelerated

Q3 revenue was RMB 115.56 billion, +21.6% YoY and +10.4% QoQ, 1.9% above the consensus of RMB 113.37 billion. Breaking it down, it is not one line accelerating but three at once: core local commerce about RMB 80.02 billion, +17.9% YoY; new initiatives RMB 35.54 billion, +30.8% YoY; delivery services about RMB 30 billion, about +20% YoY.

The mechanisms behind these three lines differ. Delivery services accelerating shows both order volume and average ticket size rising — order volume held up after subsidies tapered, which is the most counterintuitive point.New initiatives accelerated 30.8%, mainly instant retail (Shanbuy) and Xiaoxiang Supermarket; this is "selling more categories on delivery capacity," with very low marginal cost. Core local commerce overall rose 17.9%, nearly 8 points faster than Q2's 10.1%, showing in-store is also recovering — in-store is pure commission and advertising revenue, with high gross margin.

One line to watch is merchant services: about RMB 44 billion, about +8% YoY, far slower than delivery services.Merchant services is commission plus advertising, the direct expression of platform bargaining power; at only 8% growth, it shows merchants were hurt in the subsidy war and are recovering more slowly than users. If this figure cannot return above 10% in Q4, the "quality" of revenue is in doubt.

## Layer two: gross profit and expenses — where the money goes

Gross profit RMB 40.06 billion, gross margin 34.7% (derived), up 1.7 points year on year and 1.2 points quarter on quarter. Gross margin expansion during a subsidy phase-out is anomalous — falling subsidies usually drag down average order value and pressure gross margin. The gain here came from two sources: a higher mix of high-margin new businesses (instant retail, in-store), and lower procurement costs for self-operated goods (pharmaceuticals, liquor).

The expense side is the whole story of this report. Selling and marketing expenses RMB 26.50 billion, expense ratio 22.9%, versus RMB 38.01 billion and 40.0% a year earlier — a cut of RMB 11.5 billion in one year. R&D RMB 8.65 billion, up only 11.3% year on year, at 7.5% of revenue, below 8.2% a year earlier. G&A RMB 3.20 billion, essentially flat.

Put the three together: gross profit up RMB 8.7 billion year on year, marketing spend down RMB 11.5 billion, R&D up RMB 880 million, and operating profit swung from -RMB 17.80 billion to +RMB 1.71 billion, an improvement of RMB 19.5 billion. Every bit of the improvement came from burning less cash, not earning more — gross profit contributed RMB 8.7 billion and expense savings RMB 10.8 billion. That is why the profit in this report was saved, not earned.

## Tier 3: The quality of profit

Net profit was RMB 2.071 billion, while operating profit was only RMB 1.71 billion (derived); the RMB 360 million gap came from non-operating items—fair value changes on investments, disposal gains and interest income, net of tax and minority interests. Adjusted net profit of RMB 1.850 billion was below net profit, confirming this: the company's "adjustments" add back share-based compensation and strip out fair value gains on investments, and the deductions exceed the add-backs, hence the lower adjusted figure.

This is what the market means by "adjusted profit slightly below expectations": the positive headline net profit looks good, but the operating recovery is milder than the headline number suggests. Excluding the RMB 360 million of non-operating gains, the operating margin is only 1.5%—a company with RMB 450 billion of annual revenue earning just RMB 1.7 billion in a quarter. That is not the look of a good business; it is the look of one that has just climbed out of a hole.

One more number to watch: core local commerce operating margin of 5.6% (derived), versus 7.9% in Q2 and 20.9% in 2024. The 2.3-point sequential decline reflects the seasonality management flagged in August (summer marketing, heat-related rider subsidies, new work-injury insurance); the year-on-year swing from -20.3% to +5.6% is a genuine recovery. Both directions are true, and neither should be told alone.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet

Operating cash flow was about RMB 8.5 billion (estimated), more than 4x net profit; capex was about RMB 2.5 billion, and free cash flow was about RMB 6 billion, positive for two consecutive quarters.Food delivery is a prepaid business: users pay first, riders are settled later, merchants are settled monthly — as long as it does not burn cash, cash flow is naturally better than profit. The RMB -13.815 billion operating cash flow in 2025 was an exception, caused by how the subsidy war was booked, not by the business deteriorating.

The balance sheet is cleaner than a company that just lost RMB 23.355 billion should have: cash and equivalents of RMB 104.7 billion, short-term wealth management products of RMB 63.6 billion, interest-bearing debt at almost zero, and net cash of about RMB 120 billion.This means two things: first, it will not die from losses; second, it has RMB 120 billion it can use for buybacks, dividends or overseas expansion, and has used only a small portion so far.

In one sentence, this earnings report says:revenue proves the business is still there, gross margin proves unit economics are intact, expenses prove management can pull back, and cash flow proves it will not die. All four hold — only "earning power" has not come back. Q4 is the off-season; whether core local commerce operating profit can hold RMB 5 billion is the next test.
