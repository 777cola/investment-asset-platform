③ Moat anatomy: which one can be filled with money — Meituan

Four moats: two hard, two soft

Core view

A moat matters not because it exists, but because of the money and time a rival would need to fill it in. On that test, of Meituan's four moats, fulfillment is hard — money cannot buy time — while in-store is soft, because money can buy traffic.

Hard: fulfillment network — money cannot buy time

JD.com invested tens of billions of RMB in food delivery in 2025 and reached roughly 7% share by 2026. The problem is not a lack of money, but a lack of time. Taking a station from 200 orders a day to 2,000 requires 18–24 months of building user habits; reaching 5,000 takes more than three years.

The essence of this moat is "localized operational knowledge": which residential compounds you can enter, which office towers mean waiting for elevators, how many extra riders to add on rainy days, which restaurants are slow to prepare food. This knowledge does not live in the system; it lives in the heads of 2,800 station managers. Competitors can copy the system, but they cannot copy these 2,800 people.

This is also why subsidy wars produce no results: subsidies can buy orders, but they cannot buy "fast delivery"; and if delivery is not fast, users leave the moment subsidies stop. Revenue growth of 21.6% in Q3 2026 proves this logic — the subsidies retreated, but the users did not leave.

Soft: in-store services — a traffic business with no moat

In-store services is a pure traffic business: merchants want exposure, users want discounts, and whoever has the more engaging content wins. Douyin brings 700 million DAUs and a recommendation algorithm — a capability set inherently suited to in-store services.

The numbers are brutal: Douyin's in-store GTV will exceed RMB 800 billion in 2025, growing nearly 60%, while Meituan's in-store share has been compressed from above 80% to around 60%. In three years, 20 points have been taken out of the moat.

Meituan's counterattack — "low price plus immediacy", meaning group-buy vouchers, in-store pickup and delivery cross-selling — is defence, not offence. In in-store services, Meituan can hold 60%, not 80%. That is why the base case assigns in-store services only a 30% operating margin, versus above 35% in 2024.

Moderate: merchants and mindshare

Merchant side: 8 million active merchants are an asset, but multi-homing is the norm—a restaurant lists on Meituan, Douyin and Ele.me simultaneously at zero marginal cost. So merchants are not "Meituan's merchants" but "merchants of all platforms." Merchant services grew just 8% in Q3, which reflects exactly this logic.

User mindshare: "Order delivery on Meituan" still holds for high-ticket full-service meals—because only Meituan can guarantee 30-minute delivery. But on orders below RMB 30, user mindshare has shifted to "whichever is cheaper." These users belong to no platform; they belong to price.

Mindshare is valuable because it makes a platform the default option: a user's first reflex on picking up the phone is Meituan, and that habit is worth money. But subsidies can break habits—as they did once in 2025, at a cost of RMB 30 billion.

Moat direction: widening or narrowing

Three things are narrowing: in-store share (80% to 60%), merchant bargaining power (merchant services up only 8%), and low-price-tier mindshare (already educated by subsidies). One thing is widening: fulfilled categories (flash sales, pharmaceuticals, alcohol — more goods delivered over the same network).

The net effect: "the moat has not widened, but the cost of attack for rivals has risen." In 2026, taking 1 point of share costs RMB 20 billion; in early 2025 it cost only RMB 10 billion. Meituan's density is higher and its UE better, so rivals must hit harder to compete.

That is why this is a 7, not an 8 and not a 6: it cannot be killed (fulfillment is solid), but it cannot defend everything either (in-store is soft). Investing in it earns "profit recovery during a ceasefire," not "compounding from moat expansion."

Conclusion

Four moats: fulfilment strong (money cannot buy time), merchants medium, mindshare medium, in-store soft (20 points eroded in three years). Net effect: "not wider, but the cost of a rival attack has doubled." The investment therefore earns profit recovery during a ceasefire rather than compounding from moat expansion — which justifies a 19x rather than 25x valuation.
