⑤ Industry and competition: a stable three-way equilibrium — Meituan

50 / 43 / 7: no one can swallow the others

Core view

The 2026 local-services market splits 50 / 43 / 7: Meituan about 50%, Taobao Shangou about 43%, JD.com about 7%. That structure has held for three quarters — not because it makes sense, but because none of the three can make the numbers work.

Why it fails to deliver results: the input-output ratio is too poor

Food delivery economics: RMB 1–2 gross profit per order, RMB 10–20 billion to win 1 point of share, payback of more than five years. All three boards ran the numbers and reached the same conclusion — not worth it.

Alibaba is the clearest case: of the HKD 80 billion it allocated in 2026, every cent went to AI infrastructure and none to food delivery. The message is that AI offers a higher return than food delivery. Yet Alibaba has not exited either — its 43% share is its ticket into the instant retail race, too valuable to discard.

JD.com is clearer still: 7% share, the worst input-output ratio of the three, and it will not exit either — food delivery is a high-frequency entry point that can drive PLUS membership and supermarket sales. Hence its stance of "present but not adding."

Douyin: not in food delivery, but in in-store services

The real threat from Douyin is not the 43% or 7% battleground; it is in-store.Douyin's in-store GTV will exceed RMB 800 billion in 2025, growing nearly 60%, while Meituan's in-store share has been squeezed from above 80% to around 60%.

Why can Douyin win in-store?In-store is a "content + recommendation" business: Douyin has 700 million DAU and the strongest recommendation algorithm, whereas Meituan is "search + price comparison," and users open it only when they have a clear need.The former can create demand (see it and want to eat it); the latter can only satisfy demand.

The cost structure is more troublesome still:in-store requires no riders, no social insurance and no fulfillment, so Douyin's marginal cost is close to zero.For the same RMB 10 billion invested, the profit Douyin can earn in in-store is ten times that of food delivery.So it will keep fighting.

Three metrics to watch for in-store

1. In-store GTV share. Currently around 60%. A fall below 50% would collapse Meituan's profit structure: in-store gross profit is the only source of subsidies for food delivery.

2. In-store operating margin. Above 35% in 2024, now around 30%. Below 25% would signal it is trading price cuts for share — a defensive posture.

3. Douyin local life GMV growth. 60% in 2025, about 30% in 2026. Growth slowing from 60% to 30% is the best news for Meituan: Douyin's marginal returns are diminishing, and the in-store market is not infinitely contestable.

Of the three metrics, the second has deteriorated (35% to 30%), the first is flat (60%) and the third is improving (60% to 30%). Net effect: it held the line, but at a high cost.

What is the steady-state margin?

If the three players hold at 50/43/7 and Douyin's in-store share stalls at 40%, what is the steady-state industry margin? Core local commerce operating margin of 10%–12%. The rationale: marketing expense ratio stabilises at 18%, delivery nears break-even and in-store margin holds at 30%.

That compares with 20.9% in 2024 — an exceptional year when Meituan dominated alone and Douyin had not yet scaled, not to recur — and with the current 5.6%, still 5 points short of steady state.

How much profit do those 5 points represent? Core local commerce revenue annualises to about RMB 320 billion; 5 points = RMB 16 billion of operating profit, or roughly RMB 12 billion of net profit after tax. That is the source of the RMB 23 billion net profit in the neutral scenario: the current RMB 11 billion (annualised) plus RMB 12 billion of recovery.

Conclusion

The stable 50/43/7 split has taken hold: none of the three can make the numbers work, hence the ceasefire. But Douyin has not stopped attacking in-store (GTV RMB 800 billion, growth 30%), and that is the real threat.Steady-state margin 10%–12%, implying neutral net profit of RMB 23 billion.Conclusion: there is no winner in this industry, only "the least bad one" — Meituan.
