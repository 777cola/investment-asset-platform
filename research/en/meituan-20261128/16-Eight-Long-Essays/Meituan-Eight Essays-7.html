Which ones lose you money, and which are just scary

Core view

Risks must be ranked; an unranked list is no list at all. Ranked by probability × impact, the top concern is share loss in in-store (medium probability, high impact), followed by a competitor restarting subsidies (medium probability, high impact); the least concern is financial risk (extremely low probability).

Primary risk: loss of ground in in-store

In-store is the foundation of Meituan's profit: gross margin above 80%, and the only source of funding for food-delivery subsidies. Douyin's 2025 in-store GTV exceeds RMB 800 billion, growing nearly 60% and squeezing Meituan's share from above 80% to around 60%.

What happens if share falls to 50%? In-store operating profit drops from about RMB 30 billion a year now to RMB 20 billion, group net profit falls from RMB 23 billion to below RMB 15 billion, and the neutral target price drops from HKD 98 to HKD 80. Not a disaster, but an 18% decline.

Only one set of metrics to watch: in-store GTV share (semi-annually), in-store operating margin (quarterly).

Second risk: rivals restart subsidies

How did the 2025 battle begin? JD.com was chasing a high-frequency traffic entry point, Alibaba was defending instant retail, both scaled up simultaneously, and Meituan was forced to retaliate. RMB 23.355 billion burned in a single year.

All three have now stopped, but a ceasefire is not peace. Three triggers remain: a CEO change at one player accompanied by a push to boost results, a bad quarter forcing a drive for order volume, or a loosening of regulation (for example, permitting exclusive dealing). Any one of the three brings the subsidies back.

Impact: The marketing expense ratio rises from 22.9% back to 30%, an extra RMB 32 billion a year, and net profit swings from RMB 23 billion to a loss. That is the most direct route to crushing the valuation.

Third and fourth risks: rider costs and Brazil

Rider costs reclassified as employee costs: The model today is crowdsourcing/flexible staffing. From July 2026, new work-injury insurance goes nationwide, with platforms bearing the full cost — adding RMB 0.1–0.2 per order. If riders were deemed employees under labour relations, with full social insurance and housing fund contributions, per-order cost would rise from RMB 0.2 to over RMB 2, turning unit economics negative across the industry. Low probability — regulators know this would destroy the industry — but the impact would be enormous.

Brazil losses out of control: USD 1 billion over 5 years is the committed cap; actual spend could exceed it. Q3 new-business losses widened from RMB 1.74 billion to RMB 2.16 billion, mainly on the Brazil launch. If 2027 losses exceed RMB 5 billion, the investment should face accountability. Medium impact (RMB 3–5 billion a year) but manageable — management can hit the brakes at any time.

Fifth and sixth risks: entry-point migration and financials

Traffic gateway migration: If users stop opening the app and route orders through WeChat, Douyin or AI assistants instead, Meituan becomes a back-end fulfilment provider and its valuation shifts from platform to utility. About 30% of traffic now originates from external gateways, and that share is indefensible. Low probability on a ten-year horizon, but the impact would be enormous—the valuation framework would de-rate to 12x.

Financial risk: extremely low. Net cash of RMB 120 billion, interest-bearing debt near zero, operating cash flow positive. The company will not die from a lack of money; it will only slowly depreciate from a lack of profit.

Ranking all six, the conclusion is that the real worry is erosion of the profit base, not the company collapsing. That also dictates position management—no stop-loss line is needed; quarterly tracking is.

Conclusion

Six risks ranked by probability × impact: in-store share loss (medium/large), subsidy restart (medium/large), rider costs reclassified as employee costs (low/extreme), Brazil (medium/medium), entry-point migration (low/extreme), financial risk (very low).Common thread: none blows up tomorrow; all erode slowly.Conclusion: track quarterly rather than set stop-loss lines; size positions rather than forecast precisely.
