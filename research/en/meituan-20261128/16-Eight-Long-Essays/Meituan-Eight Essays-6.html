Core view

Wang Xing made two key choices: one proven right (prioritizing UE over order volume in 2025), one not yet proven (investing in Brazil in 2026). The first preserved his income statement; the second could burn another USD 1 billion.

Option one: no volume war in 2025

In 2025, JD.com and Alibaba both entered food delivery at the same time, with subsidy intensity at a historical peak. Wang Xing's choice: don't fight an order-volume war, defend unit economics. The cost was share falling from 65% to 51% and a full-year net loss of RMB 23.355 billion.

At the time, the market was harshly critical — "once share is lost it never comes back," "food delivery is winner-take-all." A year later, the choice looks right: Q3 revenue +21.6%, net profit RMB 2.071 billion, share stabilized at 50%. Had it chosen to defend share, the likely outcome now would be "55% share, RMB 8 billion quarterly loss."

The lesson isn't that "Wang Xing is brilliant" but that "this company's decision logic is about running the numbers, not saving face." Among internet companies, that is rare.

Option two: invest in Brazil in 2026

The second option is overseas expansion. Keeta reached unit-economics profitability in Hong Kong in October 2025 and in Saudi Arabia in July 2026, taking only 22 months; Saudi Arabia already accounts for about 25% of Keeta's daily orders. These two results prove the model can be replicated.

Then Brazil: a 5-year, USD 1 billion investment agreement. Brazil's problem is not a small market but strong rivals — local platform iFood dominates, and average order value is low while delivery distances are long. Saudi Arabia is "high order value plus low competition"; Brazil is "low order value plus strong rivals" — the two are not the same thing.

Q3 new-business losses widened from RMB 1.74 billion to RMB 2.16 billion, mainly due to the Brazil launch. If losses exceed RMB 5 billion in 2027, this choice should be held to account.

Capital allocation: scores across three dimensions

Buyback: C.The USD 2 billion authorization equals just 0.4% of the HKD 419 billion market cap; with RMB 120 billion net cash on the books, at the current pace it would take a decade to buy it all back.This is not a question of ability but of willingness.

R&D: A-.RMB 8.65 billion, 7.5% of revenue, growth of 11.3% below revenue growth.In a year when every major player is scrambling for compute, this restraint comes at a cost (narrative, valuation), but it preserves cash flow.

Overseas expansion: B-.Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia succeeded; Brazil is a bet.Looking only at the proven parts, this is an A; add Brazil and it becomes a B-.

Weighted across the three lines: B.A management team that can run the numbers but will not return cash to shareholders — common among Asian internet companies, and one source of the valuation discount.

Succession: the biggest unanswered question

Wang Xing is 47, far from retirement. But the company has no clear No. 2: Mu Rongjun runs technology and compliance, Wang Puzhong runs core local commerce (69% of revenue), and Chen Shaohui runs capital. Each oversees one piece, and none has been publicly positioned as "the next CEO."

Compare with peers: Alibaba has a partnership system and job rotation, JD.com has a succession pipeline, and Pinduoduo has Chen Lei. Meituan looks more like "founder plus business units" — efficient, but with concentrated succession risk.

This does not count against it now; it amounts to a discount ten years out. If no clear succession pipeline is visible in 2027–2028, the valuation should take a further 5%–10% haircut.

Conclusion

Two key choices: defend UE (proven right), bet on Brazil (unproven). Capital allocation on three fronts: buybacks C, R&D A-, overseas expansion B-, weighted B. Succession is the biggest unresolved question. Assessment of management: capability 8, generosity 6 — which is why this company deserves only 19x, not 25x.
