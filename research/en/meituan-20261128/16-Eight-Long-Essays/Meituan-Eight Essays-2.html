Core view

Meituan books RMB 450 billion in annual revenue but in essence sells only one thing: it sells "now" to users. A user willing to pay RMB 5 extra for 30-minute delivery is buying time; a merchant willing to pay a 15% commission is buying "being seen immediately".

The Price of Time: RMB 4.1 per Order

At a rough 80 million orders a day, or about 740 million orders a quarter, delivery revenue comes to roughly RMB 30 billion, an average delivery fee of RMB 4.1 per order. What users pay that RMB 4.1 for is "not having to go out."

On the cost side: riders earn RMB 7–8 per order (including platform subsidies). The platform collects RMB 4.1 and pays out over RMB 7, and merchant commissions and advertising cover the RMB 3 gap. This is Meituan's business model—using high-margin in-store profit to subsidize low-margin fulfillment.

Understand this, and it becomes clear why in-store is the lifeline. In-store gross margin above 80% is the fuel for the whole machine; food delivery is the engine, in-store is the fuel tank. When Douyin attacks in-store, it is not attacking one business line—it is attacking the fuel supply for the entire machine.

The economics of density: why only 300 of 2,800 counties and districts make money

The cost curve of instant delivery is "density determines cost": a site doing 200 orders a day has a per-order cost of RMB 9; at 2,000 orders a day, RMB 6; at 5,000 orders a day, RMB 5. The curve falls fastest over the first 2,000 orders, then flattens.

Of China's 2,800 counties and urban districts, fewer than 300 can support more than 2,000 orders a day. The remaining 2,500 are "covered but unprofitable" — kept for user mindshare (so the service works when users travel to smaller cities), not for profit.

This explains why JD.com cannot win share even by spending heavily: it can reach 1,000 orders a day in 300 cities, but not 5,000, because Meituan's riders have run there for a decade and order density is an order of magnitude higher. Subsidies buy users, not density.

Three-sided network: merchants, riders, users

Meituan is a three-sided network, not two-sided. More merchants → more users → more riders → faster delivery → merchants depend on it more: a virtuous cycle, but every link has friction.

On the merchant side, the friction is commission: Q3 merchant services grew only 8%, indicating merchants are pushing back on pricing or cutting spend. On the rider side, the friction is cost: nationwide rollout of the new work-injury insurance in July 2026 adds RMB 0.1–0.2 per order. On the user side, the friction is price: for orders below RMB 30, users go with whoever is cheaper.

Of the three sides, the merchant side (commission rate) is the only one where Meituan has pricing power, and that side is weakening. That is why it must expand into more categories—selling more through the same network is the only path to growth without raising prices.

Where is the ceiling for this business?

The ceiling for food delivery is clear: China's restaurant market is about RMB 5.5 trillion, delivery penetration is about 25%, implying RMB 1.4 trillion of GTV; Meituan holds half, or RMB 700 billion. At a 15% monetization rate, the delivery revenue ceiling is about RMB 100 billion a year.

Instant retail has a far higher ceiling: total retail sales are RMB 47 trillion, categories that can be delivered instantly (fresh food, pharmaceuticals, alcohol, 3C, beauty) are about RMB 8 trillion, and penetration is still below 5%. This is the opportunity for Meituan Instashopping and Xiaoxiang Supermarket, and the reason Alibaba and JD.com are fighting hard.

So the long-term story of this business is not food delivery, it is instant retail. Delivery is the cash flow and user funnel; instant retail is the growth curve. Q3 new initiatives +30.8% are evidence of that story.

Conclusion

Meituan is selling time: delivery fee of RMB 4.1 per order against a cost of over RMB 7, with the gap covered by in-store gross profit. Density determines cost; only 300 cities nationwide can be profitable; the merchant side, the only side of the three-sided network with pricing power, is weakening. The long-term story is not food delivery (revenue ceiling of RMB 100 billion) but instant retail (penetration below 5%).
