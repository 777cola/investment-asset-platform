Core view

The previous seven pieces all answered "what this is"; this one answers "how to do it". Three rules apply: buy in three tranches, trim on facts, and replace annual forecasts with quarterly verification.

Why split the position into three tranches rather than buying in one go?

The core assumption behind this investment—"margins back to 10%"—needs 15 months to verify (Q4 data in March 2027, interim results in August 2027). Until then, any full position rests on faith in that assumption.

Scaling in also pays psychological dividends: after the first tranche, a 20% drop won't panic you because you still have 70% of your ammunition; a 20% rally won't leave you with regret because you've already built 30%. This isn't mysticism—it's the basic function of position management.

The cost of scaling in: if the stock rallies straight up, you only earn on 30% of your position. But at this payoff ratio (1:1.9), forgoing part of the upside is acceptable—because the 23% downside risk is real.

Why trim on fundamentals rather than price stops

The trouble with a price stop-loss: it washes you out at the point of maximum panic. Meituan closed at HKD 62.70 on 26 November, down 41% from its high, and anyone who sold that day missed the +8.21% move the next session.

The advantage of a fundamental stop-loss: it triggers only when the fundamentals actually deteriorate. Q4 core local commerce operating profit below RMB 3.5 billion, market share below 48%, or the marketing expense ratio back at 26% — any one of these three would signal that the ceasefire is over or that peak-season demand was an illusion, and at that point selling is justified.

The one exception is an extreme case: if the stock falls below HKD 45 (6x ex-net-cash) with no fundamental news at all, the stop-loss is not the issue — the question is whether you got the math wrong.

When to sell: three scenarios

Scenario 1: Price reaches target. Neutral, HKD 98 (19x 2027E plus net cash); halve the position on arrival. Not because the outlook sours, but because the risk/reward has shifted — HKD 98 implies 0% upside and 23% downside.

Scenario 2: Thesis disproved. Share falls below 48%, marketing expense ratio returns to 26%, in-store share falls below 50% — if two of the three occur, exit in full. If the thesis is wrong, admit it; don't fall in love with the stock.

Scenario 3: A better opportunity emerges. If the portfolio holds another company that is "equally cheap but controls its own profit," switch. Meituan's flaw is that its profit is not self-determined — which is why it can only be a 5% position in the portfolio.

Finally: where does this report's bias lie?

Every report carries bias; better to state it than hide it. This report has three:

One: too optimistic on the "ceasefire". We assume no price war for the next 4–6 quarters—an assumption with no guarantee, resting only on the inference that "none of the three players can make the math work".

Two: too pessimistic on in-store. We treat 60% in-store share as the central case, but Douyin's growth has already slowed from 60% to 30%; if it keeps slowing, in-store could beat expectations.

Three: too little value assigned to overseas expansion. The base case assigns zero overseas profit; if Brazil succeeds, this valuation is clearly too low. But an option should not be paid for at full price—that is discipline, not judgment.

We lay out these three biases so you know where this report is most likely to be wrong if future data contradict it.

Conclusion

Three rules: buy in three tranches (30% at 67.85, raise to 60% at 58, complete on Q4 data confirmation); cut on facts (Q4 <RMB 3.5 billion or share <48%); halve the position at HKD 98. Three biases: too optimistic on the truce, too pessimistic on in-store, too little value ascribed to overseas expansion. Fix the rules; leave the rest to quarterly data.
