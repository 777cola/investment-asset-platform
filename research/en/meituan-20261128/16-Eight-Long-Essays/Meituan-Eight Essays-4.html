Core view

Net profit of RMB 2.071 billion looks like a turnaround; broken down, it is "RMB 8.7 billion more gross profit + RMB 10.8 billion less expense + RMB 360 million non-operating income." Of this, 56% came from cost savings and 44% from earnings.

Layer one: why gross profit can rise

Gross profit RMB 40.06 billion, gross margin 34.7%, up 1.7 points year on year. In a quarter of subsidy rollback and falling average order value, gross margin can only rise for two reasons: a higher share of high-margin business, or lower procurement costs.

Both hold. The revenue share of in-store (gross margin above 80%) rebounded; procurement scale in self-operated goods (pharmaceuticals, liquor) lowered the cost ratio. There is also a hidden reason: subsidies shifted from "direct price cuts" to "spend-and-save coupons," moving in accounting terms from a revenue offset to a marketing expense — the same money, but the gross margin looks higher. This warrants caution: part of the gross margin improvement is an accounting reclassification, not a real improvement in unit economics.

Second layer: how much cost has been saved, and how much more can be saved

Sales and marketing RMB 26.50 billion, expense ratio 22.9%, versus RMB 38.01 billion and 40.0% a year earlier. Saving RMB 11.5 billion a quarter, RMB 46 billion a year. This is the largest source of profit improvement.

How much more can be saved? The 2024 expense ratio was 16%, still 7 points away, equivalent to RMB 32 billion annualized. But that is theoretical: whether those 7 points can be saved depends on whether competitors raise prices, not on management.

R&D RMB 8.65 billion, +11.3% YoY, 7.5% of revenue. This growth rate is 10 points below revenue growth, a deliberate restraint. G&A RMB 3.20 billion, -5.3% YoY, essentially flat. Two of the three expense lines are contracting and one is stable — that is good management.

Layer 3: Unit economics — how much profit per order

Rough calculation: 80 million orders per day, delivery revenue of RMB 4.1 per order, rider cost of RMB 4.5–4.6 per order (including insurance and dispatch), a loss of RMB 0.4–0.5 per order in the delivery segment. In 2025Q3 the same figure was around RMB 2.5. The delivery-segment loss narrowed by 80%, the most substantive improvement.

Merchant services (commission plus advertising) contribute RMB 5.9 per order (RMB 44 billion ÷ 740 million orders), with very high gross margin. This is the only source of subsidy — the platform uses merchant-services gross profit to cover delivery losses.

So the truth of unit economics is: on a single food-delivery order, the user pays RMB 4.1, the platform pays the rider RMB 4.5, the merchant pays RMB 5.9, and the platform nets RMB 5.5, then deducts marketing (RMB 3.6 per order) and R&D and administrative costs (RMB 1.6 per order), netting RMB 0.3. That RMB 0.3 is the entire profit — thin as paper.

Tier 4: Cash flow and balance sheet

Operating cash flow of about RMB 8.5 billion, capex of about RMB 2.5 billion, free cash flow of about RMB 6 billion (derived). Net profit RMB 2.071 billion, free cash flow RMB 6 billion — cash flow is three times profit. Why: prepaid food-delivery orders, monthly merchant settlement, weekly rider settlement, negative working capital.

Balance sheet: cash of RMB 104.7 billion plus short-term wealth-management products of RMB 63.6 billion, interest-bearing debt near zero, net cash of about RMB 120 billion, 31% of market cap. Buy it now and one-third is cash, two-thirds is the business.

The business portion is RMB 265.5 billion; it earns RMB 23 billion in 2027, 11.5x. That is where the whole financial analysis lands: what is cheap is the cash and the multiple; what is expensive is the assumption.

Conclusion

RMB 2.071 billion = RMB 8.7 billion more gross profit + RMB 11.5 billion less expense - RMB 880 million more R&D + RMB 360 million non-operating. 56% from cost savings, 44% from earning more; unit economics net RMB 0.3 per order, razor-thin; but cash flow is three times profit and net cash is 31% of market cap. Conclusion: the recovery is real, quality is mediocre, the price is not expensive — buy, but not heavily.
