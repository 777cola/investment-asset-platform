## Three-month price trajectory: down 19%, up 8% on the final day

The stock closed at HKD 77.5 on August 28 and HKD 62.70 on November 26, a 19.1% decline, before rising 8.21% to HKD 67.85 on November 27 post-results. Of the three-month drawdown, 60% reflected broad Hong Kong equity multiple compression and 40% the expectation that "Q3 will be worse."

The Hang Seng Tech Index is down more than 25% year-to-date in 2026, with Tencent and SMIC off more than 30% and Alibaba and Xiaomi more than 20%. Meituan at -36% from its high is not the worst performer in this group, but nor is it cheap—half of its decline is self-inflicted.

The November 27 rally converted "Q3 will be worse" from a worry into a known, and "will it lose money" into "it already made money." The 8.21% move equates to HKD 31.8 billion of market-cap gain, roughly the market repricing the probability that the subsidy war is over rather than pricing in new profit.

## Two real signals: guidance delivered and turnaround achieved

The first signal came on August 28. Results that day were unremarkable (net profit RMB 2.69 billion, margin 2.6%), but on the call management volunteered that "per-order delivery cost will be higher in Q3, we will step up investment in in-store, and profit will be worse than Q2." In that context the remark amounted to a profit warning, yet the stock did not fall — the market did not believe it, assuming the peak season would not disappoint.

The second signal came on November 27. Q3 core local commerce operating profit was about RMB 4.52 billion (estimated), down RMB 1.15 billion from RMB 5.67 billion in Q2 — management's warning had come true. Net profit meanwhile turned positive at RMB 2.071 billion, with revenue up 21.6%, above consensus. Delivering on the promise matters more than the numbers themselves: it makes the next guidance credible.

Together the two signals form a complete timeline: in August it said things would get worse → in November they did get worse but stayed positive → so when it says in March 2027 that "Q4 will be better," the market will start to believe it. That is the mechanism of valuation repair.

## Four noise signals: don't reposition for them

Noise one: broad Hong Kong market pullback. The Hang Seng Tech Index is down 25% year to date, and Meituan fell with it. That is beta, not alpha. Selling on this basis substitutes a macro call for a company call.

Noise two: competitor earnings preview. In October the market worried Alibaba would step up quick commerce, and the stock swung. In the event, the HKD 80 billion Alibaba allocated in 2026 went to AI infrastructure, not food delivery. Expectations ran opposite to fact, and this is the kind of noise most likely to push investors the wrong way.

Noise three: 11.11 subsidy pulse. All three players stepped up "minute delivery" in 3C and beauty, and the stock fell to HKD 62. But 11.11 is a once-a-year pulse and does not change the annual picture — the 22.9% marketing expense ratio in the Q3 results already includes summer and promotional spending.

Noise four: broker ratings. In mid-November several brokers cut target prices to HKD 75–85. Brokers cut after the stock fell, not after the fundamentals changed — such reports lag by 2–4 weeks and carry limited reference value.

## Three things to learn from the event

First, the stock is far more sensitive to "subsidy expectations" than to "profit." Q3 net profit was RMB 2.071 billion — no large figure in absolute terms — yet the stock rose 8.21%, because the market is trading the possibility that the subsidy war is over, not the RMB 2.071 billion itself.

Second, management's guidance is starting to earn credibility. In August they said it would get worse; in November it did. Once that credibility is established, the Q4 guidance due in March 2027 will be taken seriously. For investors, "follow the guidance" is becoming a usable strategy.

Third, the 52-week low of HKD 61.45 is a meaningful level. That day multiple brokers cut target prices and sentiment was at its worst, yet three weeks later the results showed fundamentals improving. Sentiment bottoms and fundamental bottoms do not move in sync — the former tends to arrive earlier and lower.
