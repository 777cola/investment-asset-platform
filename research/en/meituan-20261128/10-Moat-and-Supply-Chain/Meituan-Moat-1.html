Core thesis

Four moats, scored item by item: fulfillment network 9 (physical assets; two years and RMB 100 billion cannot build it); merchant ecosystem 7 (8 million merchants, but commission bargaining power is falling); user mindshare 7 (no substitute for high-ticket full-service dining, though subsidies have educated the low-price tier); in-store 6 (the fattest profit pool, and the hardest hit by Douyin).

Weighted score 7. The key change: the moat has not widened, but the cost of attack for rivals has risen. Share has stalled at 50%, showing that the price of "grabbing one more point" has climbed from RMB 10 billion to RMB 20 billion.

Industry-chain position: controls the demand gateway, the fulfillment network and merchant digitalisation simultaneously

As noted earlier, this chain has three money bags, and Meituan took two. More precisely, it simultaneously holds the trio of "demand gateway (App DAU), fulfillment network (6 million riders) and merchant digitalisation (POS, supply chain, Kuailv)." The value of this trio lies not in any single piece but in the combination: merchants using Meituan's POS system are more willing to run promotions on Meituan; higher rider density means faster delivery; and faster delivery makes users willing to buy more categories.

The benefit of this position is pricing power — charging commissions to merchants, delivery fees to users and advertising fees to brands. The downside is that it must bear the full fulfillment cost itself: any rise in labour costs hits profit directly. Compare Douyin, which does "traffic distribution + in-store redemption" with no fulfillment, no riders and no social insurance — far lighter and far faster. This is why Douyin captured half the in-store market in 3 years, while Alibaba and JD spent two years to reach only 43% share in food delivery.

Moat by moat: which is the hardest, which is the softest

Fulfillment network is the strongest, at 9 points. This is not technology but assets: sites across 2,800 counties and cities, 6 million riders with orders, and a scheduling algorithm refined over a decade. JD.com invested tens of billions of yuan in food delivery in 2025, yet its 2026 share is still only about 7%—not for lack of money but for lack of density. A site handling 200 orders a day versus 2,000 orders a day differs by more than RMB 2 in per-order cost. The essence of this moat is time; money cannot buy time.

In-store is the weakest, at 6 points. In-store is a pure traffic business: merchants want exposure, users want discounts, and whoever has the more engaging content wins. Douyin has 700 million DAU and algorithmic recommendation—a playbook inherently suited to it. In 2025, Douyin's in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion, with growth of nearly 60%, pressing Meituan's in-store share from above 80% to around 60%. In-store is Meituan's fattest profit pool (operating margin around 30%) and also the one it can least defend.

The two in the middle—merchant ecosystem and user mindshare—score 7 points each. Eight million merchants are an asset, but merchant services grew only 8% in Q3, showing that bargaining power is eroding; user mindshare persists at high ticket sizes but has been spoiled by subsidies in the low-price band—these users belong to no platform; they belong to price.

The most dangerous rival: not Alibaba, but Douyin

Most people would name Taobao Shangou as the most dangerous competitor, given it has taken 43% of food delivery order volume. I disagree. In food delivery, Alibaba is trading money for share — once the money runs out, it has to stop. The HKD 80 billion it allocated in 2026 went to AI infrastructure, not food delivery, and that is the evidence. Food delivery is also a tough business: RMB 1–2 gross profit per order, 6 million riders to manage, social insurance to pay. Alibaba's return on capital in this business sits far below that of its e-commerce operations.

Douyin is different. What it is taking is in-store — 80% gross margin, no riders, no social insurance, no fulfillment. For the same RMB 10 billion of investment, Douyin can earn ten times the profit in in-store that it can in food delivery. Its traffic is also its own, so marginal cost is close to zero. In 2025, in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion, growing nearly 60%. If that pace holds for three years, Meituan's in-store profit will be cut in half.

So the most dangerous competitor is Douyin, because its business model fits the in-store segment better than Meituan's does, and in-store is the foundation of Meituan's profit. Losing share in food delivery only means earning less; losing share in in-store damages the foundation.

Conclusion

Four moats, weighted to a score of 7: fulfillment 9 (the hardest — money cannot buy time), merchants 7 (weakening), mindshare 7 (still holds in the high-price tier), in-store 6 (the softest, under attack by Douyin). The moat has not widened, but attacking it now costs rivals more: share has stalled at 50%, and the price of grabbing another 1 point has climbed from RMB 10 billion to RMB 20 billion. The most dangerous rival is Douyin, not Alibaba: it is attacking in-store, where gross margin is 80%, not fulfillment, where gross margin is 10%.
