## Title and lead

Title: Xiaomi's toughest quarter: profit halves 68%, but cars make money for the first time

Subtitle: Memory price hikes ate into handset gross margin; cars reached break-even by cutting prices 19%. This is not a bad company, it is a thin business.

Lead-in: After the close on November 24, Xiaomi reported third-quarter 2026 results. Revenue was RMB 107.2 billion, down 5.2% year on year, slightly above the market expectation of RMB 106.4 billion; but net profit was only RMB 3.876 billion, down 68.4% year on year. The stock has fallen 38% from this year's high of HKD 42.96 to HKD 26.60. This report contains a turning point — many people only saw the -68.4%.

## I. Who took the money

The conclusion first: this was not share stolen by competitors; it was taken by upstream suppliers.

Revenue fell only 5.2%, deliveries were roughly flat, and share was not lost—Xiaomi's products are not failing to sell. But gross margin dropped from 22.9% to 20.2%, and gross profit fell by RMB 4.24 billion. Where did that RMB 4.2 billion go? It became the operating profit of Samsung and SK hynix. In early 2026, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70%, NAND more than doubled, and memory accounts for 15%–25% of handset costs.

Xiaomi tried three routes: building inventory early to lock in prices, pressing suppliers for annual cost cuts, and raising prices on some models. It used all three and still lost 2.7 percentage points. Because it dares not truly raise prices—any increase would cost it share. That is the price of having no pricing power.

A side note: Transsion and Honor also saw gross margins decline over the same period. So this is not an operating problem specific to Xiaomi; the entire mid- and low-end handset industry is repaying the memory makers' debt.

## II. Automobiles turned a profit for the first time

This is the most underappreciated line in the earnings report: the smart EV segment achieved quarterly operating profitability for the first time.

The numbers: 108,800 units delivered, roughly flat year on year; revenue of about RMB 22.9 billion, -19% year on year; average selling price fell from RMB 260,000 to RMB 211,000. A 19% price cut bought breakeven.

This profit has two possible sources. One is scale effects — annual supply-chain cost-downs landed and utilization at the Phase 2 plant rose, so per-unit costs genuinely fell. The other is expense normalization — there were one-off store and capacity provisions in the second quarter that did not repeat in the third. My read is half and half, so watch two consecutive quarters: if it stays profitable in the fourth quarter, it is a business; if it swings back to a loss, it is a numbers game.

The next catalyst is Pengcheng. This extended-range SUV locked more than 70,000 orders in its first month; at the industry-standard 70% conversion, that implies about 50,000 units of real demand. It also enters Europe in 2027. The auto line has shifted from "can they build it" to "can they make money on it consistently" — the question has changed, the difficulty has not.

## III. Is 32x expensive?

HKD 26.60 and a market cap of HKD 685 billion work out to 32x annualized Q3 net profit of RMB 19.5 billion.

32x is an awkward number. It is not cheap — Apple trades at 33x over the same period, but Apple's gross margin is 46% versus Xiaomi's 20.2%. Nor is it absurdly expensive — the stock has already fallen 38%, so some of the froth has been squeezed out.

Three scenarios laid out: bear case HKD 12.2 (memory prices rise another year, autos swing back to a loss, -54%); base case HKD 23.2 (memory rolls over in H2 2027, autos sell 480,000 units a year, -13%); bull case HKD 36.2 (memory peaks early, Pengcheng ramps, Europe launch, +36%). Weighted, that gives HKD 23.7, 11% below the current price.

That is the whole reason I would not buy now: the upside requires three things to go right at once; the downside requires only one to go wrong. The risk-reward is asymmetric, and asymmetric in the wrong direction.

## IV. When to buy

Two signals; either one is enough to start building a position:

- The stock falls below HKD 23, equivalent to 21x 2027 earnings, at which point the risk-reward turns symmetric;

- Handset gross margin rises sequentially for two consecutive quarters, signalling the memory cycle has peaked — a harder signal than price.

If both appear, raise the position to 5%; if neither appears and the stock rebounds above HKD 32, exit entirely — at that point you are buying sentiment, not the company.

One last plain word. On the earnings call, Lu Weibing said, "This is the toughest quarter in my career." Executives at Chinese hardware companies rarely say this; most would frame -68% as "proactive adjustment." Willingness to admit difficulty makes next quarter's guidance more credible; but it also means the difficulty is not over.

## Conclusion

Xiaomi is not a bad company. Five years to build cars and reach 108,800 units in a single quarter, ship a million chips, and grow an ecosystem to 800 million devices—these are real capabilities.

But it sits in the middle of the industrial chain: oligopolists upstream, no premium downstream, so profit is always a "residual." In the third quarter of 2026, memory price hikes ate most of that residual.

The best attitude toward it is to respect its capabilities, then wait for a respectful price. It is not a lack of conviction—it is a refusal to buy asymmetric risk at a symmetric price.
