Core view

Xiaomi's business model can be summed up in one sentence: sell hardware at near cost, keep users inside the ecosystem, then monetise them through internet services. Of the RMB 107.24 billion in 2026Q3 revenue, smartphones × AIoT accounted for about RMB 83.6 billion and autos and AI innovation about RMB 22.9 billion, while internet services contributed a profit in the RMB 0.7 billion range.This model works in smartphones; in autos it has yet to be proven.

Who are the users, why do they buy, and why do they stay

Xiaomi's user base has reinvented itself three times in ten years: in 2014 it was "young people chasing value for money," by 2020 it was "middle-class buyers of smart home devices," and in 2026 it is "people buying cars." All three shifts follow the same logic—the cheapest option at the same spec.

What keeps them is the ecosystem. For a household with a Xiaomi phone, robot vacuum, air conditioner and car head unit, switching brands means replacing not one device but an entire set of accounts, apps and habits. This is exactly what Duan Yongping prizes most: not bound by contracts, but bound by hassle.

But "staying" is not the same as "willing to pay more." In Q3 2026 auto revenue fell 19% YoY and deliveries were roughly flat, showing that even existing users would not pay an extra RMB 5.0 million for the brand. The ecosystem can lift retention, but not willingness to pay—that is the ceiling of the Xiaomi model.

Three margin rates combined into one table

Phones are the traffic driver: single-digit gross margin (8.5% in Q2), just over RMB 100 of gross profit on a RMB 2,000 handset, and the job is to pull users into the store. IoT is the profit product: gross margin around 20%, with air conditioners, TVs and robot vacuums leading, and the money comes from scale and channel efficiency. Internet services are the real profit pool: gross margin above 70%, so one yuan of revenue there equals ten yuan of phone revenue.

The flaw in this structure is proportion. Internet services have long been about 10% of revenue and cannot carry overall profitability; once hardware gets squeezed on cost (memory in 2026), the whole model goes out of balance. The 20.2% consolidated gross margin and 3.6% net margin in Q3 2026 are what that imbalance looks like. Duan Yongping would say: this is not "a bad business model," it is "a business model too dependent on hardware cost control."

What did the automobile change

Cars cut both ways: average selling price rises from RMB 2,000 to RMB 210,000, but the cost curve turns heavy-asset. The first is good—one car sold to the same user generates the same revenue as 100 phones; the second is bad—factories, stores and after-sales service all require upfront spending.

The turning point came in Q3 2026: 108,800 vehicles delivered and the segment's first quarterly operating profit. Duan Yongping would press with one question: "Where did this profit come from?" If it comes from scale effects—per-vehicle cost falling with output—a good business is beginning; if it comes from expense normalization—a one-off charge over-accrued last quarter—it is merely a numbers game.

My judgment leans toward the former, but I would not commit: annual supply-chain price reductions landing in H2 2026 and rising utilization at the second-phase capacity are both real; store count, however, is still expanding, so expenses cannot decline over the long term. Watch two consecutive quarters.

Ten years of this business

Ten years out, Xiaomi will most likely still be around, with a business spanning four segments: phones, home appliances, cars and robotics. Revenue could reach RMB 2 trillion; whether net margin can reach 6%–8% is the whole question. Samsung offers the comparison: revenue is three times Xiaomi's and its net margin has long run 8%–12%, underpinned by vertical integration in memory and panels. Xiaomi has no vertical integration, so its net margin ceiling may sit at 6%.

Duan Yongping's conclusion would be that this is a "respectable but hard-earned-money" business — holdable, but undeserving of a high multiple. In his words, "understandable, but not good enough."

Conclusion

Business model: efficiency-driven, with the ecosystem a bonus and margins constrained by hardware costs. Smartphones and IoT are the base; internet services are the profit pool but too small; autos are the new growth engine but only just past breakeven. At best the model delivers a 22.9% gross margin, at worst (2026Q3) 20.2%. A model where a 3-point swing in gross margin can halve net profit should not command a growth-stock valuation.
