Financial analysis (Buffett's lens): where the profit went — Xiaomi Corporation

Gross profit came in RMB 4.24 billion lower, expenses RMB 2.19 billion higher and the investment line reversed by RMB 1.97 billion — a combined RMB 8.39 billion

Core view

Net profit fell from RMB 12.27 billion to RMB 3.876 billion, a decline of RMB 8.39 billion. The gap breaks down cleanly: gross profit down RMB 4.24 billion, expenses and taxes up RMB 2.19 billion, and investment items swinging from +RMB 960 million to -RMB 1.01 billion (a RMB 1.97 billion swing). The three items sum to RMB 8.40 billion; the RMB 10 million difference from RMB 8.39 billion is rounding.

Investment item contribution = net profit − adjusted net profit, a back-solved metric not separately disclosed by the company.
Gross profit comes in RMB 4.24 billion lower, and the first driver is exogenous

Gross margin fell from 22.9% to 20.2% and revenue dropped 5.2%; combined, gross profit fell from RMB 25.90 billion to RMB 21.66 billion. The cause is not weak Xiaomi sales but memory chip prices, which rose 60%–70% (DRAM) and more than 100% (NAND) in 2026, with memory accounting for 15%–25% of a phone's BOM.

The nature of this hit must be clear: it is exogenous, industry-wide and reversible. Transsion, Honor and OPPO are taking the same hit — gross margins across global low- and mid-range phones are declining in the second half of 2026. Buffett would classify this as "cyclical", not as "operational failure".

The second factor: costs came in RMB 2.19 billion higher, and that was deliberate

Gross profit came in RMB 4.24 billion lower and adjusted net profit RMB 6.43 billion lower; the RMB 2.19 billion gap is expenses and tax. Two drivers: R&D exceeded RMB 9 billion in the quarter (RMB 9.2 billion in Q2, +18.9% YoY), with both the Xuanjie chip and autonomous-driving lines burning cash, while selling expenses rose with auto store expansion as store count grew from just over 200 at the start of 2026 to more than 300.

This was management's deliberate choice: not to cut R&D when profit looks its worst. Long term that is right — cumulative Xuanjie O1 shipments exceed one million units, a rare success case for a domestic SoC; short term, it pushes the profit floor another RMB 2 billion lower. Buffett would endorse the choice, and would also say "this company needs to keep investing, so it is not See's Candies."

Third account: investment line swings from +RMB 960 million to -RMB 1.01 billion

This line item is the easiest to overlook, yet it hits net profit as hard as gross profit. In 2025Q3, net profit exceeded adjusted net profit by RMB 0.96 billion, implying gains on the investment portfolio; in 2026Q3 it fell RMB 1.01 billion short, implying losses. The swing was RMB 1.97 billion, or 23% of the net profit decline.

That is also why adjusted net profit is the more informative measure: it strips out changes in investment fair value and reflects the core business. In 2026Q3, adjusted net profit was RMB 4.884 billion, down 56.8% YoY — better than net profit's -68.4%, but both are stepping down rapidly.

Balance sheet: Xiaomi's most solid piece

Minimal interest-bearing debt, net cash on the balance sheet and interest coverage comfortably above 10x—a combination rarely seen among Chinese hardware companies in 2026. Operating cash flow from 2023 to 2025 covered capex for the car business; in 2026, pre-stocking to lock in costs absorbed part of it, but there is no sign of a need to raise financing.

Inventory is the only thing to watch. Stockpiling ahead of memory price increases was the right call, but should prices fall back in 2027, this batch of high-cost inventory will have to be written down.Rough estimate: if memory-related inventory accounts for 30% and prices fall 20%, the potential impairment is on the order of RMB 3–5 billion—equivalent to one quarter's net profit.

Return on capital: ROE 7.5%, ROIC lower

Annualising 2026 net profit of RMB 15.5 billion (38.76×4) against equity of about RMB 205 billion gives ROE of roughly 7.5%; with almost no interest paid, ROIC is close to ROE. That sits below Buffett's 15% threshold and below Xiaomi's own 10%–12% in 2024–2025.

The decline splits evenly between numerator and denominator: profit is down 68%, while equity shrinks only slowly on retained earnings and buybacks. Restoring 12% requires net profit back above RMB 25 billion — precisely the neutral 2027 assumption.

Conclusion

Financially, the three items break down as follows: one is cyclical (gross profit -RMB 4.24 billion), one is deliberate investment (expenses -RMB 2.19 billion) and one is volatility (investments -RMB 1.97 billion). Only the first can reverse; the second will not, and the third should not be priced into valuation. A clean balance sheet sets the floor, and a 7.5% ROE shows the current price offers no margin of safety. Rate it "Watch", not "Buy".
