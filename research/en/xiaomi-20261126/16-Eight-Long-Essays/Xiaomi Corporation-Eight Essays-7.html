Risk list: five risks, two fatal — Xiaomi Corporation

Ranked by probability × impact, the worst-case scenario is a 57% decline
Research date 2026/11/26  ·  Price HK$26.60 (+0.15%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 685 billion (25.741 billion shares)  ·  PE (annualized) Approximately 32x (rough calculation based on annualized Q3 adjusted net profit of RMB 19.5 billion)

Core view

Xiaomi's risks can be arranged in a matrix: prolonged memory price increases (40% probability), a second round of the EV price war (50%), Pengcheng ramp-up below expectations (35%), geopolitics and European tariffs (30%), valuation compression (30%). The first two can impair principal; the last three are chronic. In the worst case the stock trades at HKD 11.5, 57% below the current price.

Fatal risk one: the memory cycle runs longer than expected

In early 2026, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND rose more than 100%. Institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027, with HBM potentially rising another 80%–100% in 2027. Phones do not use HBM, but HBM crowds out capacity, and ordinary DRAM supply is equally tight.

Xiaomi's response has three legs: building inventory ahead of time, annual supply-chain cost reductions, and price increases on some models. All three were used, and gross margin still fell 2.7 percentage points. If prices do not fall in 2027, phone gross margin could drop below 6%, at which point the phone business would be loss-making.

Research cannot eliminate this; only price compensation can. By our estimates, each 1 percentage point decline in phone gross margin cuts net profit by about RMB 0.6 billion. A fall from 8.5% to 6% would cut net profit by a further RMB 1.5 billion—equivalent to another halving.

Fatal risk two: the second round of the auto price war

The first round (2024–2025) wiped out the gross profit of the new EV makers; the second round (2026–2027) will wipe out the combustion-vehicle profit of the traditional automakers; only the third round is the elimination stage. Xiaomi now sits between the first and second rounds, having just turned the segment profitable on cost and expense control.

The danger is that it has no fallback: the RMB 200,000–300,000 price band is the most fiercely contested, it lacks the brand strength to move upmarket (matching BBA would take a decade), and on cost it cannot beat BYD (vertical integration). "First single-quarter profit" may be the high point of this cycle, not the starting point.

If that judgment holds, 2027 segment profit expectations must be cut by 70%, the valuation de-rates from 22x to 15x, implying a share price of HKD 15.8. This is the core assumption of the bear case, and the one I worry about most.

Three chronic ailments: ramp-up, geopolitics, valuation

Ramp-up slip (35% probability): 70,000 locked orders in the first month, converted at a 70% rate to roughly 50,000 units of real demand, which spread over three months averages 16,000–17,000 units per month. If it stalls below 10,000 units, 2027 automotive revenue is RMB 20 billion lower.

Geopolitics and European tariffs (30% probability): entering Europe in 2027 is the right call, but EU tariffs can change at any time; India freezing RMB 4.8 billion of assets in 2022 already demonstrated once how "geopolitical risk actually lands." Following BYD's pace in Europe, the first two years are pure investment; budget for a loss in the RMB 10 billion range.

Valuation compression (30% probability): 32x annualized, against a backdrop of the Hang Seng Tech Index falling more than 25% in 2026. If the market reclassifies Xiaomi as "manufacturing," compressing the multiple to 20x, the stock falls 40% even with profit unchanged.

Worst-case scenario and "what puts me at ease"

Worst case: memory does not fall back in 2027 (handset gross margin 6%) + second round of the auto price war (segment swings to a loss) + Hong Kong liquidity contraction (18x). On 2027 net profit of RMB 15 billion at 18x, market cap is RMB 270 billion and the share price HKD 11.5, 57% below the current price. Probability about 10%.

But before turning bearish, acknowledge three reassuring things. First, a clean balance sheet: net cash, no interest-bearing debt; the company will not die from a cycle. Second, no share loss: global top three in handsets, number one in IoT, auto deliveries running at 3.0–4.0 million units a month; the customers are still there. Third, auto has crossed the breakeven line: even if it slips back into a loss in Q4, that is a return to the "investment phase", not "failure".

So the conclusion of the risk list is not "avoid", it is "wait for the price". A 57% decline in the worst case means you need to buy at a steep discount to make it pay; at 32x the current price offers no discount at all.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: both fatal risks are cycle-related and cannot be eliminated through research, only compensated through price. A 32x price compensates for nothing. Only one of the five risks (Pengcheng ramp-up) can be tracked monthly; the rest all require waiting for macro variables to turn—that is the entire meaning of "watch."
