Three accounts, one illusion, one hidden risk — Xiaomi Corporation

Behind the -68.4% net profit, only one item is cyclical
Research date 2026/11/26  ·  Price HK$26.60 (+0.15%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 685 billion (25.741 billion shares)  ·  PE (annualized) About 32x, on our rough calculation from annualised Q3 adjusted net profit of RMB 19.5 billion

Core view

Net profit came in RMB 8.39 billion lower, split three ways: gross profit -42.4 (cycle), expenses and tax -21.9 (choice), investment line swing -19.7 (volatility). Only the first reverses with the cycle; the second will not, and the third has no place in valuation. See this clearly and you will neither be frightened by the -68.4% nor fooled by the "beat".

First account: gross profit -RMB 4.24 billion, cycle

Gross margin fell from 22.9% to 20.2% and revenue declined a further 5.2%; together, gross profit dropped from RMB 25.90 billion to RMB 21.66 billion. One driver: memory price hikes. DRAM contract prices are up 60%–70% and NAND up 100% or more, with memory accounting for 15%–25% of handset BOM.

The hit is "exogenous and reversible." Exogenous: this is not a Xiaomi operating misstep — Transsion and Honor also saw gross margins fall over the same period. Reversible: memory is a cyclical product, two years of gains must eventually pause, and institutions expect supply-demand to turn in 2027. The hit therefore should not be valued as a "permanent downgrade."

Watch for a reflexivity trap, however: if prices fall in 2027, Xiaomi must take impairment on its high-priced inventory. Rough math: memory-related inventory at 30% of the total and prices down 20% imply potential impairment of RMB 3–5 billion — equivalent to eating part of first-half 2027 profit in advance. In the first year of a cycle reversal, reported numbers may not look good.

Second account: expenses and taxes -RMB 2.19 billion, choose

Gross profit came in RMB 4.24 billion lower and adjusted net profit RMB 6.43 billion lower; the RMB 2.19 billion gap is expenses and tax. Breaking it down: R&D exceeded RMB 9 billion in the quarter (RMB 9.2 billion in Q2, +18.9% YoY), while selling expenses rose as auto stores expanded from over 200 to more than 300.

Management chose this deliberately; it is not a loss of control. Cutting R&D at the bottom of the cycle amounts to selling off the next three years of product competitiveness for one quarter of profit; Xiaomi did not do that, and is still investing in the Xuanjie chip and autonomous driving. Long term this is right; short term it lowers the profit floor by another RMB 2 billion.

Investors must accept this choice and price it: if R&D holds at RMB 40 billion a year through 2027 and stores expand by another 100, rigid expense growth will consume half of the gross-margin improvement from falling memory prices. In other words, 2027 profit elasticity is not as large as the market thinks.

Investing cash flow came in at -RMB 1.97 billion, volatile

Q3 2025 net profit was RMB 12.27 billion and adjusted net profit RMB 11.31 billion, with the investment line at +RMB 960 million; Q3 2026 net profit was RMB 3.876 billion and adjusted net profit RMB 4.884 billion, with the investment line at -RMB 1.01 billion. That RMB 1.97 billion swing accounts for 23% of the net profit decline.

This line is the most easily misread. Headlines report "net profit -68.4%", yet nearly a quarter of that reflects fair value changes on the investment portfolio—neither a deterioration nor an improvement in the core business. To read Xiaomi's profit, start with adjusted net profit, then gross profit.

The same line produces the rare outcome of net profit falling below adjusted net profit (3.876 < 4.884). In most years the reverse holds, as investment gains push net profit above adjusted net profit. This year's reversal shows the capital markets have been unkind to Xiaomi's portfolio as well.

One Illusion and One Hidden Risk

The illusion is the "revenue beat." Q3 revenue came in at RMB 107.239 billion, 0.8% above the RMB 106.4 billion consensus, and the media called it a beat. But the estimate itself was cut 5% over the past three months, from RMB 112 billion to RMB 106.4 billion. "Above expectations" really means "didn't fall as much," not "demand is back."

The hidden risk is cash flow. In 1H26, operating cash flow coverage of net profit fell below 1x as memory stockpiling turned cash into inventory. Q3 was not disclosed on a standalone basis, but "gross margin up slightly, net profit down sharply" points to stockpiling continuing. If memory prices do not fall in 2027, this inventory becomes a rock weighing on the company.

The balance sheet floor is safe: interest-bearing debt is minimal, the company holds net cash, and interest coverage is well above 10x. So even if profit keeps declining, the company will not run into trouble—which is the basis for "watch" rather than "avoid."

The final layer is accounting quality. This report shows no obvious signs of window dressing: receivables did not grow abnormally, the inventory increase has a clear cause (memory stockpiling), R&D expenses show no capitalization to flatter profit, and minority interests are small. In other words, the RMB 3.876 billion net profit is "genuinely ugly," not "manufactured ugly"—which is, paradoxically, reassuring.

Conclusion

Financial reality: of the three accounts, one is cyclical and will recover, one is a choice and will not change, and one is volatility that should not be counted. Taken separately, Xiaomi's operations have not collapsed; what has collapsed is the margin of a thin business under a cost shock. A 32x valuation treats all three as permanent—that is a problem with the price, not with the company.
