Business essence: RMB 100 profit per phone, RMB 5,000 per car — Xiaomi Corporation

Break the four businesses into unit economics to see where the money actually comes from

Core view

Xiaomi's business can be broken down into four unit economics: over RMB 100 gross profit per phone, RMB 400 per air conditioner, RMB 4.6 ten-thousand gross profit per car, and a few dozen RMB contributed by each internet user per year. Multiply these four figures by their respective volumes and you get RMB 107.2 billion in revenue and RMB 21.66 billion in gross profit.

Smartphones: a traffic driver, not a profit driver

2026Q2 smartphone gross margin was 8.5%. At an ASP of about RMB 1,300, gross profit per handset is roughly RMB 110; with the blended gross margin declining in 2026Q3, the smartphone side is most likely lower. Selling a RMB 1,300 phone to make RMB 100 means this business runs on volume — 160 million units a year at RMB 17 billion in gross profit.

The real role of the phone is not to make money but to serve as an "entry point": people who buy the phone go on to buy bands, robot vacuums and air conditioners, and to watch Xiaomi Video and use Xiaomi Financial. Losing a little on the phone is fine as long as it pulls users into the ecosystem. But that also means the phone can never dare raise prices — it is a traffic-acquisition tool, not a profit center.

The 2026 memory price surge hit this thinnest point. DRAM rose 60%–70%, lifting the BOM of a handset by 10%–17% and cutting gross profit from RMB 110 to RMB 60. That is why the smartphone gross margin fell from 11.5% to 8.5% with no room to fight back.

IoT: the real profit driver

IoT and lifestyle consumer products (air conditioners, TVs, robot vacuums, routers) carry gross margins of around 20%, making them the most stable profit source among the four businesses. A RMB 2,500 air conditioner generates RMB 500 in gross profit, 4–5x that of a phone.

The moat here is "ecosystem chain + channels": Xiaomi has invested in hundreds of ecosystem-chain companies, selling their products under a unified brand, channel and app bundle.Its scale effect is more pronounced than in phones—selling ten products through one channel yields three times the sales per square meter of selling one.

But IoT has a ceiling: major appliances are Gree's and Midea's turf, and Xiaomi's value-for-money entry keeps gross margins capped; small appliances have low barriers, so anyone can play.IoT is therefore a "stable 20%", not an "expandable 30%".

Automotive: average selling price RMB 210,000, gross profit per vehicle RMB 46,000

2026Q3 deliveries 108,800 vehicles, revenue about RMB 22.9 billion, average selling price RMB 211,000 per vehicle. Backing out from segment gross margin of 21.5%–22%, gross profit per vehicle is about RMB 46,000; backing out from segment operating profit of about RMB 500 million, operating profit per vehicle is about RMB 5,000.

This unit economics says two things: first, gross profit per vehicle is 400x that of a phone (RMB 46,000 vs RMB 110), one car equals 400 phones; second, its operating margin is only 2.3% (5,000 ÷ 211,000), as thin as phones — because factory depreciation, stores and after-sales are all fixed costs.

Volume is the only lever. At 108,800 deliveries, per-vehicle cost absorption is RMB 41,000; at 150,000 deliveries it could fall to RMB 30,000, doubling per-vehicle profit. That is why "can Pengcheng monthly deliveries reach 20,000" is the single most important number for 2027.

Internet services: a few dozen yuan per user per year

Internet services revenue has long held at around RMB 40 billion with gross margin above 70%; based on 800 million active devices, each device contributes about RMB 50 a year. This is Xiaomi's highest-margin business and also its smallest by share.

Its growth depends on two things: the installed device base (already saturated) and ARPU per device (advertising, gaming, finance). Neither showed clear growth in 2026, and its share stalled at 10%. In other words, the ecosystem's monetization efficiency has not improved; only the user count is creeping up.

This also explains why Xiaomi is so committed to cars: the car is the only new category that can substantially raise per-user value — the service revenue one car can generate (insurance, charging, maps, entertainment) is more than 10x that of a phone. But that logic will not show up until 2028.

Conclusion

Conclusion on the nature of the business: Xiaomi runs a "thin margin, high volume + ecosystem traffic" model. Unit economics across its four segments differ by 400x, yet every segment earns a 2%–3% operating margin. Phones pull in traffic, IoT earns steady money, internet services earn high gross margin, and autos bet on the future. The ceiling on this model is scale, not pricing — so valuation should be assigned on scale, not on pricing power.
