## Why is the current price 0 rather than 3%?

A 3% observation position is typically used for names where the thesis holds but the price is expensive—position size buys tracking discipline. Xiaomi does not currently clear the "thesis holds" bar: of five assumptions, one has broken, one awaits verification and three have not started. Building a position before the thesis has taken shape is paying money for a story.

Opportunity cost is the other reason. At the same 32x, Apple's gross margin is more than double Xiaomi's; for the same auto bet, BYD trades at 22x with 18% ROE. Every dollar in the portfolio has an alternative, and Xiaomi's risk-reward does not rank in the top three right now.

## The arithmetic of building a position in three tranches

HKD 23 implies 21.8x 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 25 billion, or a market cap of HKD 592 billion. That price bakes in "memory prices decline in the second half of 2027 + annual vehicle deliveries of 480,000 units" — both neutral assumptions, leaving room for "getting half the thesis wrong."

HKD 20 implies 18.9x 2027 neutral net profit and 26.3x pessimistic net profit, or a market cap of HKD 514.8 billion. Even with a haircut to the neutral case, 19x is not expensive for a manufacturer with roughly 5% net margin; even on pessimistic earnings, 26x sits at the midpoint of its historical range. Only at this price does a margin of safety emerge.

HKD 32 implies 30.3x 2027 neutral net profit and 23x optimistic net profit — the price at which "the optimistic scenario is pulled forward." At that level, even if fundamentals deliver, annualised return is only in the single digits — the move then is to trim, not add.

## Stop-loss: not a price stop-loss, but a logic stop-loss

HKD 18 (17x 2027 neutral net profit) is not on its own a reason to sell — a cheap manufacturer can always get cheaper. The real stop-loss trigger is both assumptions breaking at once: autos swing back to a loss and handset gross margin falls below 8%. If that happens, Xiaomi has lost both the auto cost curve and the handset scale effect, and is left as just another contract manufacturer.

Add a soft stop too: if within 12 months of building the position, Pengcheng monthly deliveries fail to reach 1.0 million units, Europe shows no progress and handset gross margin does not recover — all three stalled means the "wait" thesis itself is wrong, so exit.

## Portfolio-level role

Xiaomi gives the portfolio exposure to "Chinese manufacturing going global": it holds handset share in India, Southeast Asia and Europe, and brings cars into Europe in 2027. That differs from "China domestic demand" names (Meituan, JD.com) and from "AI compute" names (NVIDIA, TSMC).

It fits best as a "satellite position" of no more than 5%, expressing the view that "Chinese hardware companies can build global brands." It does not suit a core position — margins are too thin, and any cost shock amplifies into profit volatility of more than 50%.

The most natural hedge within this track is memory stocks: buy Samsung and sell Xiaomi when memory prices rise, reverse when memory prices fall. In 2026 the pair's spread has already run two rounds, making it the clearest negative correlation between Hong Kong and Korean equities today.
