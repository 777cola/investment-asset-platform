## I. Company Overview

Xiaomi (01810.HK) was founded in April 2010 by Lei Jun and seven co-founders and listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in July 2018 under a weighted voting rights structure (10 votes per Class A share). At the close on 2026/11/26 it traded at HKD 26.60, with 25.741 billion shares outstanding and a market cap of HKD 685 billion, making it one of the weighted constituents of the Hang Seng TECH Index.

The business has four segments: smartphones, IoT and lifestyle products, internet services, and smart EV and AI innovation businesses. The 2026Q3 revenue mix was roughly: smartphones x AIoT about RMB 83.6 billion (78%), auto and AI innovation about RMB 22.9 billion (21%), and other about RMB 0.7 billion (1%). Smartphone shipments rank among the global top three, connected IoT devices exceed 800 million units, and the auto business delivered its first vehicles in March 2024 and 108,800 units in 2026Q3 alone.

On ownership, Lei Jun holds about 24% and a majority of voting rights; Lin Bin holds about 8%; the remainder is held by public and institutional shareholders. On organization, Lei Jun serves as chairman and CEO, Lu Weibing as president with oversight of the auto business, and Lin Shiwei as CFO. Headcount exceeds 60,000 (including the auto plant), with R&D staff at about 40%, and single-quarter R&D spending above RMB 9 billion in 2026. This is a company of "absolute founder control plus professional-manager execution": decisions are extremely fast, and checks and balances are relatively weak.

One fact that is easily overlooked: Xiaomi's cash and equivalents are on the order of RMB 100 billion (2026 interim basis), and with very low interest-bearing debt this means it has ample ammunition to keep investing in R&D and stores through the bottom of the cycle. For a manufacturing company in transition, this matters more than near-term profit—it determines whether the company can "tough it out" until the cycle turns.

## II. Industry analysis

The handset industry has entered a zero-sum game. Global shipments have held around 1.2 billion units for five consecutive years, and China's replacement cycle has stretched from 28 months to 40 months, so growth can only come from share gains and ASP uplift. The key variable for 2026 is memory price inflation: DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% early in the year and NAND by more than 100%, and institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027. This directly compresses gross margins on mid- and low-end handsets by 2–4 percentage points.

The auto industry stands at the inflection point of "penetration above half." China's new-energy passenger vehicle penetration exceeds 50% in 2026, and the industry shifts from penetration-driven to substitution-driven — every NEV sold takes a customer from a fuel vehicle or another brand. This phase is marked by slowing volume growth (within 15%), falling per-unit prices, and a still-growing number of players. The RMB 200,000–300,000 price band is the bloodiest battleground, with BYD, Huawei, Tesla and Li Auto all present.

What the two industries share: homogeneous products, overcapacity, and pricing set by the most aggressive player. Apart from Apple and BYD (via vertical integration), few companies in the chain can earn a net margin above 10% over the long run. This is the backdrop for understanding Xiaomi's 20.2% gross margin and 3.6% net margin, and the industry basis for why its valuation should not command a growth-stock premium.

## III. Business model

In one sentence: sell hardware near cost, keep users inside the ecosystem, then monetize them through internet services; since 2024 there is also an auto business. Gross margins across the four segments differ 7x — smartphones in the single digits, IoT around 20%, internet services above 70%, auto just over 20% — combining into a 20.2% blended gross margin.

The core capability of this model is "efficiency": the same BOM yields a lower retail price, and the same retail price delivers a higher spec, underpinned by procurement scale, channel turnover (Mi Home direct sales, same price online and offline) and product definition. It does not earn money from technological monopoly; it earns money from operating efficiency.

On pricing mechanics, Xiaomi is a price taker. Smartphones track the lower edge of Apple's and Samsung's price bands; autos track BYD's price band. That makes its margin a "residual" — the spread between upstream cost and downstream price — and in 2026 memory price increases consumed more than half of that spread. The fragility of the business model lies not on the demand side but on the cost side.

## IV. Financial analysis

2026Q3 revenue RMB 107.24 billion, -5.2% YoY; gross profit RMB 21.66 billion, -16.4%; net profit RMB 3.88 billion, -68.4%; adjusted net profit RMB 4.88 billion, -56.8%; net margin 3.6%, adjusted net margin 4.6%. The RMB 8.39 billion decline in net profit breaks into three pieces: gross profit -4.24 (memory price increases), expenses and tax -2.19 (R&D and stores), investment line swing -1.97 (from +0.96 to -1.01).

Asset quality is the bright spot: interest-bearing debt is minimal, the balance sheet holds net cash, and interest coverage is well above 10x, so there is no near-term solvency risk. Annualized ROE is about 7.5% (net profit RMB 15.5 billion ÷ equity of roughly RMB 205 billion), below the 10%–12% of 2024–2025, because the numerator fell 68% while the denominator declined slowly. To return to 12%, net profit must recover above RMB 25 billion — exactly the 2027 neutral-case assumption.

Cash flow is the one layer still unclear: in the first half of 2026, operating cash flow coverage of net profit has fallen below 1x (memory stockpiling turned cash into inventory), and the Q3 single-quarter figure was not disclosed. The concern is inventory — if memory prices fall in 2027, high-cost inventory will require write-downs, roughly RMB 3–5 billion, equivalent to one quarter of net profit. This can only be verified once the annual report's inventory breakdown is available.

The trend is clearer across the three quarters: revenue and profit were still at high levels in 2026Q1, net profit fell 20% QoQ to RMB 6.22 billion in Q2, then dropped another 21.5% to RMB 4.88 billion in Q3. Two consecutive quarters of QoQ decline show this is a trend, not a single-quarter fluctuation.The real signal of a bottom should appear in the quarter when gross margin recovers QoQ and the expense ratio falls QoQ at the same time; that has not happened yet.

## V. Management

Lei Jun holds about 24% and a majority of voting rights, is 57, and has no public succession timetable. His track record speaks: the car was announced in March 2021, first deliveries came in March 2024, and in Q3 2026 the segment delivered 10.88 million units in a single quarter and turned its first segment profit; Xuanjie O1 has shipped over one million units cumulatively, a rare success case among domestic SoCs. Covering in five years what others take ten, this execution ranks among the best in global manufacturing.

Capital allocation passes: share count grew only 3% over five years (mainly the HKD 42.5 billion placement in March 2025), HKD 20 billion of buybacks in 2026 (2.9% of market cap), and almost no interest-bearing debt. Two deductions: zero cash dividends in eight years as a listed company; and cumulative car investment and per-vehicle cost structure have never been disclosed in detail. The result is that investors can only guess per-vehicle economics from segment gross margin and deliveries, which already caused one definitional dispute in m03.

The succession line is clear but unannounced: Lu Weibing joined in 2019 to run Redmi, was promoted to president in 2022, and took on cars in 2024, making him the de facto number two. The risk is that he has never made a "zero to one" decision on his own. Near term this does not affect the investment case (Lei Jun has at least a 5-8 year window); long term it is a valuation discount factor.

## VI. Growth drivers

Driver 1: Auto volume ramp. 2026 deliveries are expected at about 420,000 units; 2027 at 480,000 units and ASP of RMB 210,000, implying revenue of RMB 100.8 billion. Pengcheng's 70,000 locked orders in its first month is the key variable — if monthly deliveries reach 20,000 units, 2027 auto revenue could reach RMB 120 billion; if they stall at 10,000, only RMB 90 billion. One product line determines the elasticity of RMB 30 billion in revenue.

Driver 2: Memory cycle rollover. Institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027; if it turns in the second half of 2027, each 1 percentage point recovery in handset gross margin adds about RMB 840 million in gross profit (on a base of RMB 83.6 billion revenue) and about RMB 600 million in net profit. A move from 8.5% back to 10% implies net profit accretion of about RMB 900 million to 1 billion per quarter. This is the single largest profit-elasticity variable for 2027.

Driver 3: Overseas expansion. Management confirmed entry into Europe in 2027. Based on BYD's pace in Europe, the first two years are pure investment (channels, certification, after-sales), with meaningful revenue only in 2028. One overlooked item: in-car internet services (music, maps, insurance) will grow with the installed base and show up in 2027–2028, but the scale is too small to change the profit structure near term.

The three drivers in one sentence: of the 2027 net profit range of RMB 21 billion to 33 billion, 80% is determined by two variables — the timing of the memory turn and Pengcheng's monthly deliveries; overseas expansion and internet services are upside, not determinants. That is also why the tracking list has only five metrics — most variables do not affect the conclusion.

## VII. Competitive landscape

Smartphones: Apple takes the high-end profit (gross margin ~46%), Samsung relies on vertical integration (~32%), and Xiaomi, Transsion and OPPO compete on efficiency in the low-to-mid end (8%—21%). Xiaomi's share is stable, but it earns the thinnest slice. Domestic models priced above RMB 3,000 hold steady at over 30% of the mix, showing premiumization has advanced only "half a step" — it has secured the mid-to-high end but not broken into the ultra-high end.

Automotive: BYD defines cost and price (gross margin ~18%, ROE ~18%), Huawei defines brand and intelligent driving (unlisted), and Li Auto is the first mover in the extended-range category (gross margin ~19%). Xiaomi's position is "value for money + ecosystem," competing head-on with these four in the RMB 200,000—300,000 band; any one of them cuts prices, and Xiaomi must follow.

The real opponent is not downstream, but upstream. The three memory giants (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) plus China's YMTC and CXMT account for all global supply. They need do nothing targeted at Xiaomi; they need only keep raising prices, and Xiaomi's profit vanishes on its own. Q3 2026 already demonstrated this once: Xiaomi did nothing wrong, and profit fell 68.4%.

On share data, Xiaomi's global smartphone share was about 13%—14% in 2026, ranking third; its China share was about 15%, ranking third to fourth.Stable share is good, but it also shows the "grab share" path has run its course — beyond this lies the territory of Apple and Huawei, which requires brand power, not value for money.

## VIII. Risk factors

- Memory price increases extend to end-2027 (40% probability): handset gross margin falls another 2 percentage points, net profit another RMB 4 billion lower. Watch: monthly DRAM contract prices, spot prices.

- Second round of the auto price war (50% probability): the segment swings back to a loss, valuation de-rates to 15x. Watch: monthly deliveries, terminal discount levels.

- Pengcheng ramp-up falls short (35% probability): 2027 auto revenue RMB 20 billion lower. Watch: monthly deliveries, conversion ratio from refundable orders to firm orders.

- European tariffs and geopolitics (30% probability): overseas expansion delayed, early investment wasted. Watch: EU tariff policy, local plant construction progress.

- Valuation compression (30% probability): Hang Seng Tech liquidity tightens, multiple compresses from 32x to 20x, share price falls 40%.

Worst case (all three occur together, ~10% probability): 2027 net profit RMB 15 billion, 18x, share price HKD 11.5, 57% below the current price. This scenario sets the position cap, not whether to buy.

## X. Valuation

Primary method uses PE. 2027E neutral RMB 25 billion × 22x = RMB 550 billion = HKD 597.8 billion = HKD 23.2 per share. The 22x is chosen because: Xiaomi's historical range is 15–40x, with the manufacturing attribute mapping to the low end and the ecosystem attribute to the high end; at the transition stage, we take the lower half of the midpoint.

Cross-check uses sum-of-the-parts: auto at 2027 revenue of RMB 100.8 billion and 1x PS = RMB 100.8 billion (referencing Li Auto's 0.8–1.2x PS); phones + IoT at 2027 net profit of RMB 15 billion and 15x = RMB 225 billion; internet services at net profit of RMB 10 billion and 20x = RMB 200 billion; total RMB 525.8 billion, equivalent to HKD 571.5 billion, or HKD 22.2 per share. Both methods point to HKD 22–24.

The current 32x (annualized) is above the neutral target of 22x, meaning the market is still paying a premium for "auto growth." That premium should narrow after 2026Q3 — because auto revenue is already -19%, and the evidence for growth is weakening. If the market reclassifies Xiaomi as "manufacturing + transition," 20x is a reasonable center, corresponding to HKD 21.

## XI. Investment recommendation

Observation. 12-month target price HKD 23.2 (-13%), range HKD 12.2-36.2. Existing positions can be held: autos turned profitable, share not lost, balance sheet healthy; new money waits for one of two signals: ① share price below HKD 23 (21x 2027); ② handset gross margin rising sequentially for two consecutive quarters.

Do not short. Rationale: downside hinges on one thing—memory prices not rolling over; upside hinges on three—but the stock has already fallen 38% and expectations have been beaten down, so the risk-reward for shorts is unattractive. Nor chase: if the stock rebounds above HKD 32 without fundamental improvement (29x 2027), trim rather than add.

Portfolio role: satellite position, cap 5%-6%. Its function in the portfolio is exposure to "Chinese manufacturing going global," naturally inverse to memory names, usable as a hedge against memory-cycle positions.
