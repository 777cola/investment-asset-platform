Core view

Munger does not read stories; he reads structure. His first question would be: how does a company that earned RMB 6.2 billion last quarter end up with only RMB 4.884 billion three months later? The answer lies in three places: upstream price increases, downstream price cuts, and middle-layer costs that do not move. When all three hit at once, a thin-margin business turns a squeeze into a disaster.

Upstream price hikes: phone makers do not control their own costs

Memory is 15%–25% of a phone's BOM, and in early 2026 DRAM rose 60%–70% while NAND more than doubled. Xiaomi has no countermeasure: in-house memory would take RMB 100 billion-scale capex, which it cannot fund, and switching suppliers does not help when there are only three major fabs globally. Munger would say: this is what "industry position" means—your costs are priced by someone else.

Downstream price cuts: automakers do not control their own pricing either

Deliveries of 108,800 units, revenue -19%, with average selling price per vehicle down from RMB 260,000 to RMB 211,000. A 19% price cut has been traded for a "first-ever quarterly profit" — but the quality of that profit is questionable.

Munger would press the point: if BYD cuts another RMB 10,000 next quarter, do you follow? Follow, and the profit is gone; don't, and deliveries fall. That is what it means to have no pricing power — "you can only pick the less bad of two bad choices."

The middle stays put: costs are rigid

R&D of over RMB 9 billion a quarter and more than 300 stores will not be cut just because revenue falls 5%. Revenue down 5.2%, gross profit down 16.4%, net profit down 68.4%—a textbook case of operating leverage working in reverse.

Munger's conclusion would be cold: this is not a company that will die, but one that will earn "hard money" for a long time. Such companies can be bought, but only at a price where the hard work has driven everyone else away. 32x clearly is not that.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: neither industry earns excess profit, and the two cycles converge in 2026. He would credit Xiaomi's execution but refuse to pay a premium for it — "Execution keeps you alive; pricing power is what makes you money."
