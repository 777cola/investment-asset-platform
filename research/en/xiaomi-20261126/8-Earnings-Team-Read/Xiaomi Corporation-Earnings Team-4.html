Core view

Li Lu's framework starts with "where this sits in the arc of civilization," then asks "whether the price leaves room for error." Xiaomi's industrial significance holds up: it proved Chinese companies can integrate complex supply chains into consumer products and drive costs to the lowest in the world. But the price left no room.

Industry implication: an efficiency-driven consolidator

Five years to build a car business with quarterly volume of 108,800 units, cumulative Xuanjie chip shipments above one million, and the world's largest IoT business—these three achievements are rare in any country. They reflect "integration capability" as a competitive advantage, not "original technology."

Li Lu would distinguish the two. Original technology (TSMC, ASML) captures gross margins above 40%; integration capability (Hon Hai, Xiaomi) typically captures 6%–20%. Both capabilities deserve respect, but the valuation gap is threefold.

Cycle position: neither bottom has arrived yet

The bottom for memory is in the second half of 2027 (new capacity comes online), and the bottom for the auto price war is in 2027–2028 (players consolidate from 30 to 10). Both bottoms arrive six to twelve months later than the market expects.

That means first-half 2027 earnings will most likely still look ugly. Li Lu's approach is to wait — he has time on his side: "The hardest part of value investing isn't getting the math right, it's being able to afford to wait."

Price: HKD 21 is the midpoint

Based on 2027 adjusted net profit of RMB 25 billion and a 20x multiple, market cap is RMB 500 billion and the share price around HKD 21, 21% below the current price. That price leaves room to be half wrong without losing money.

He will not buy just because it has fallen 38%. A 38% drop only turns "very expensive" into "expensive"; it is still some way from "cheap".

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: the industrial significance holds, but the price leaves no margin. He would put Xiaomi on a "five-year watchlist" and wait for it to fall below HKD 21 before spending two years building a position. "Good company + bad price = wait" — this is what he does most often.
