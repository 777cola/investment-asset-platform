Core view

After the close on November 24, Xiaomi reported its Q3 2026 results. Revenue was RMB 107.24 billion, down 5.2% year on year and slightly above consensus; adjusted net profit was RMB 4.88 billion, down 56.8% year on year. The stock has fallen 38% from its 52-week high of HKD 42.96 to HKD 26.60. These results contain a turning point: for the first time, the auto business is supporting itself, and for the first time, the phone business is repaying its upstream suppliers' debt.

I. How the profit disappeared

Numbers first. Revenue RMB 107.239 billion, down 5.2% YoY; gross margin 20.2% versus 22.9% a year earlier; net profit RMB 3.876 billion, down 68.4% YoY; adjusted net profit RMB 4.884 billion, down 56.8% YoY and 21.5% QoQ.

Net profit fell RMB 8.39 billion, and the gap breaks down cleanly: gross profit down RMB 4.24 billion, expenses up RMB 2.19 billion, and the investment line swinging from +RMB 960 million to -RMB 1.01 billion (a RMB 1.97 billion swing). The three sum to RMB 8.40 billion; the difference is rounding.

None of the three is "product not selling." Revenue fell only 5.2%, deliveries were roughly flat, and share was not lost. That is why I call this report ugly but not broken.

2. Who took the money

Memory. In early 2026, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%, with memory accounting for 15%–25% of a phone's BOM. Xiaomi chose a three-pronged approach: pre-building inventory to lock in prices, annual supply-chain cost reductions, and price increases on some models. It used all three and still lost 2.7 percentage points of gross margin.

Transsion and Honor also saw gross margins decline over the same period, so this is not a Xiaomi-specific operating problem. But it exposes a structural fact: a company with a 20% gross margin has almost no buffer against a cost shock. Revenue fell 5.2%, net profit fell 68.4% — that magnification is the math of a thin business.

One detail is easy to overlook: Q3 gross margin of 20.2% was 0.4 percentage points higher than Q2's 19.8%. The reason is that the automotive segment's gross margin recovered sequentially, offsetting the continued decline in phones. Phones have not bottomed yet; the automotive improvement is real.

III. Autos have delivered results, but only half the story

Delivered 108,800 vehicles, roughly flat year on year; revenue of about RMB 22.9 billion, down 19% year on year; average selling price fell from RMB 260,000 to RMB 211,000. A 19% price cut bought the company its first quarterly operating profit.

Where did that profit come from? Two possibilities: scale effects (annual supply-chain cost reductions landing, higher capacity utilization), or expenses normalizing (the one-off accrual in Q2 did not repeat in Q3). My read is that it is half and half, so watch two quarters — if Q4 stays profitable, it is a business; if Q4 swings back to a loss, it is a numbers game.

The next catalyst is already here: the extended-range SUV Pengcheng locked in more than 70,000 orders in its first month and enters Europe in 2027. Locked orders typically convert to deliveries at about 70%, so whether monthly deliveries can hold above 20,000 will decide whether 2027 automotive revenue is RMB 90 billion or RMB 120 billion.

IV. Why You Cannot Buy Now

At HKD 26.60 and a market cap of HKD 685 billion, annualizing Q3 adjusted net profit of RMB 19.5 billion gives 32x. That multiple is neither high nor low, but it sits on a denominator that is still being revised down.

Three scenarios laid out: bear case HKD 12.2 (-54%), base case HKD 23.2 (-13%), bull case HKD 36.2 (+36%), weighted expected value HKD 23.7, 11% below the current price. Upside requires three things to hold at once; downside requires only one.

At the results call, Lu Weibing said "this is the toughest quarter of my career." That deserves respect—executives at Chinese hardware companies rarely say this. Admitting it is hard makes next quarter's guidance more credible; but it also means the difficulty is not over yet.

V. When to buy

Two signals; either one is enough to start building a position: the share price below HKD 23 (21x 2027 earnings, making the risk-reward symmetric), or handset gross margin rising quarter on quarter for two consecutive quarters (evidence that the memory cycle has peaked).

If both appear, raise the position to 5%; if neither appears and the stock rebounds above HKD 32, exit entirely. Until then, the best action is to watch—not because we are bearish on the company, but because we do not want to pay a symmetric price for asymmetric risk.

Conclusion

Xiaomi's Q3 2026 is the textbook posture of a good company in a bad cycle: revenue holds, share is not lost, profit takes the hit, R&D keeps spending, and autos turn profitable. It proves the company's capability, and it proves the limits of this business. At 32x current price, the buyer is paying for three things to happen at once: memory peaks in 2027 + autos stay profitable + Hong Kong liquidity does not contract. The probability is below half, yet the price is full — that is the entire reason to watch.
