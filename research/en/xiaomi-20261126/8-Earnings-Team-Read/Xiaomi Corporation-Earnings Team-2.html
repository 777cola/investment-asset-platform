Buffett reads financial reports: a company with a 20.2% gross margin deserves only 15x — Xiaomi Corporation

Three numbers decide everything: gross margin, net margin, ROE
Research date 2026/11/26  ·  Price HK$26.60 (+0.15%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 685 billion (25.741 billion shares)  ·  PE (annualized) Approximately 32x (rough calculation based on annualized Q3 adjusted net profit of RMB 19.5 billion)

Core view

Buffett looks at three numbers first in manufacturing: gross margin, net margin, ROE. Xiaomi in 2026Q3 posted 20.2%, 3.6% and an annualized 7.5% — not one of the three clears the bar for a "great company." Add zero dividend, and in his eyes this earnings report is worth only 12–15x.

Gross margin: can it raise prices? One table tells the story

20.2%, 2.7 percentage points lower than the same period last year. Over the same period, Apple's gross margin was around 46%, Samsung's around 30%. This gap is not an efficiency problem; it is a pricing power problem. With memory prices rising in 2026, Apple can raise iPhone prices by USD 100, while Xiaomi can only absorb it — that is the difference between 46% and 20.2%.

The trend is more troubling. Xiaomi's gross margin has ranged between 17% and 22.9% over five years, and 20.2% now sits below the midpoint. If memory prices do not fall back in 2027, it will drop below 18%, at which point the net margin could approach zero. A company with a 20% gross margin has essentially no cyclical resilience.

Net margin and ROE: the leverage of a thin business

Net margin 3.6% (10.8% last year), adjusted net margin 4.6%. Annualized ROE 7.5% (net profit RMB 15.5 billion ÷ equity of about RMB 205 billion), below the 15% he demands and below Xiaomi's own 10%–12% in 2024–2025.

Note the leverage effect: revenue fell only 5.2%, net profit fell 68.4%. This is not management incompetence but the mathematics of the business model—for a company with a 20% gross margin, 2.7 points less gross profit equals more than half of operating profit gone. Buffett spent his whole life avoiding this kind of business.

Capital allocation: buybacks a plus, zero dividend a minus

HKD 20 billion of buybacks in 2026 (about 2.9% of market cap), share count up only 3% over five years, and almost no interest-bearing debt — he would score all three highly. But eight years listed with zero cash dividends costs it points: "If a company cannot return cash to shareholders, it had better prove that every dollar it invests earns back more." Xiaomi's car-making investment has not yet reached the point of proving that.

So the valuation can only be 12–15x. On 2026 adjusted net profit of RMB 20 billion, that is a market cap of RMB 240–300 billion and a share price of HKD 10.3–12.9. More than half below the current price. He would not buy, but would say it is a good company.

Conclusion

Buffett-style verdict: good company, thin business, expensive price. Gross margin 20.2%, net margin 3.6%, ROE 7.5% — these three numbers dictate a 12–15x multiple, while the market gives it 32x. He would file this report in the "worth tracking" drawer and wait for a price where "even if I'm wrong, I don't lose money."
