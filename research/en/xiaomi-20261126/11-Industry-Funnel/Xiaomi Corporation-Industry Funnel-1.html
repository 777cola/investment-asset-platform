Core thesis

Line up the six "consumer electronics + autos" names in one table and Xiaomi screens poorly: revenue growth of -5.2% ranks last, gross margin of 20.2% ranks second-to-last, yet its P/E of 32x is third-highest. Only Li Auto looks worse on growth (-10%), but Li Auto trades on just 15x.

The rough screen asks which name spans all three lines — phones, IoT and autos. Among the six, only Xiaomi does, so it advances to the final round by default — but "only" is not "excellent"; the final verdict is "worth tracking," not "worth buying."

Step 1: Narrow 30 companies down to 6

The screen starts with roughly 30 Chinese and global leaders across two chains: consumer electronics and new energy vehicles. The first filter — "own brand, own channel, own R&D" — eliminates pure contract manufacturers (Hon Hai, Luxshare, Wingtech) and pure component makers (Sunny Optical, BOE, Inovance), leaving 14.

The second filter, "consistently profitable over the last four quarters", eliminates loss-making NIO, XPeng and Leapmotor, plus tail-end automakers reliant on subsidies, leaving 9. The third, "market cap above the equivalent of RMB 50 billion", eliminates small consumer-electronics companies, leaving 6.

Of these 6, only Xiaomi spans all three lines: phones, IoT and cars. The "final pick is Xiaomi" conclusion therefore carries no intellectual content — it is the inevitable output of the filters, not a research finding. The real question for research is a different one: do these three lines, taken together, add or subtract value.

Step two: why the other five were eliminated

Apple was screened out not because it is inferior but because it cannot be compared. A 46% gross margin and 140% ROE are the financial hallmarks of a "standard setter"; Xiaomi's 20.2% gross margin is the hallmark of a "standard executor." At the same 33x, buying Apple is buying certainty; buying Xiaomi is buying possibility.

Samsung was screened out because it sits on the opposite side of Xiaomi. Memory price increases are a positive for Samsung and a negative for Xiaomi—holding this pair in the same portfolio is essentially a hedge, not stock selection.

BYD and Li Auto were screened out for single-category exposure. BYD's cost advantage comes from vertical integration, which Xiaomi cannot replicate; Li Auto's range-extender SUV is under direct attack from Pengcheng, and its share will most likely fall in 2027. Transsion was screened out because it has only mid- and low-end phones, with no ecosystem and no car. Each of the five has its own rationale, but none can replace Xiaomi's "three-line" combination.

Step three: why this one, and why not buy it now

Three answers to "why this one." First, it is the only Chinese company to have built a closed loop across phones, IoT and cars, and that combination delivers real user value — one account manages every device. Second, execution has a track record: five years to build cars, 108,800 units in a single quarter, and Xuanjie chip shipments above one million units. Third, the stock is down 38% from its high, so part of the bubble has been squeezed out.

Equally, three answers to "why not buy now." First, its growth is the worst of the six (-5.2%) and its gross margin ranks second to last — neither growth nor earnings quality. Second, its 32x ranks third highest of the six, yet ROE is just 7.5%, the lowest of the six. Third, the three lines above are currently a negative, not a positive: cars drag on revenue, IoT is squeezed by memory, and internet services cannot support the margin.

Conclusion: it is the name most worth tracking in this sector, but not the one most worth buying now. If money must go into this sector, BYD's 22x and 18% ROE offer better value than Xiaomi's 32x and 7.5% ROE — though its ceiling is also lower.

One more note on what "ranking" means here. The business models of these six companies differ too much for a hard P/E comparison to mean anything — Apple's 33x buys a 46% gross margin and 140% ROE, while Xiaomi's 32x buys a 20.2% gross margin and 7.5% ROE. The same multiple on completely different asset quality is why "relative valuation" often deceives in cross-industry comparisons. The truly comparable name is BYD (also manufacturing, also a price taker), whose 22x and 18% ROE are clearly better value.

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: Xiaomi wins the final selection among the six, but the rating is "Watch." It wins because it is "the only one with a closed loop across all three lines"; it is on Watch because of "lowest growth, lowest gross margin, valuation not low." The investment implication: it belongs on the watchlist, not in the current portfolio. Re-rank when gross margin or price changes.
