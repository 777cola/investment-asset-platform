## Step 1: Narrow 30 companies down to 6

The scope starts with about 30 Chinese and global leaders across two chains: consumer electronics and new energy vehicles. The first filter is **"own brand, own channel, own R&D"** — this cut eliminates pure contract manufacturers (Hon Hai, Luxshare, Wingtech) and pure component makers (Sunny Optical, BOE, Inovance), leaving 14.

The second filter is **"consistently profitable over the last four quarters"** — this cut eliminates loss-making NIO, XPeng and Leapmotor, plus tail-end automakers reliant on subsidies, leaving 9. The third filter is **"market cap above the equivalent of RMB 50 billion"**, which eliminates small consumer-electronics companies, leaving 6.

Of these 6, only Xiaomi spans all three lines: phones, IoT and cars. **So the "final pick is Xiaomi" conclusion carries no intellectual content — it is the inevitable output of the filters, not a research finding.** The real question research must answer is a different one: do these three lines, taken together, add or subtract value.

## Step two: why the other five were eliminated

Apple was screened out not because it is inferior, but because it **cannot be compared**. A 46% gross margin and 140% ROE are the financial hallmarks of a "standard setter"; Xiaomi's 20.2% gross margin is the hallmark of a "standard executor." At the same 33x, buying Apple is buying certainty; buying Xiaomi is buying possibility.

Samsung was screened out because it sits on the opposite side of Xiaomi. Memory price increases are a positive for Samsung and a negative for Xiaomi—**holding this pair in the same portfolio is essentially a hedge, not stock selection.**

BYD and Li Auto were screened out because of single-category exposure. BYD's cost advantage comes from vertical integration, which Xiaomi cannot replicate; Li Auto's range-extender SUV is under direct attack from Pengcheng, and its share will most likely fall in 2027. Transsion was screened out because it has only mid- and low-end phones, with no ecosystem and no car.**Each of the five has its own rationale, but none can replace Xiaomi's "three-line" combination.**

## Step three: why this one, and why not buy it now

There are three answers to "why this one." First, **it is the only Chinese company that has built a closed loop across phones, IoT and cars**, and the user value of that combination is real (one account manages every device). Second, execution has a track record: five years to build cars and it reached 108,800 units in a single quarter, with Xuanjie chip shipments exceeding one million units. Third, the valuation has fallen 38% from its high, so part of the bubble has been squeezed out.

There are also three answers to "why not buy now." First, **its growth is the worst among the six** (-5.2%), while its gross margin ranks second to last, meaning it has neither growth nor earnings quality. Second, its 32x ranks third highest among the six, but its ROE is only 7.5%, the lowest of the six. Third, **the three lines above are currently a negative, not a positive** — cars drag on revenue, IoT is squeezed by memory, and internet services cannot support the margin.

**Conclusion: it is the one most worth tracking in this sector, but not the one most worth buying now.** If money must be put into this sector, BYD's 22x and 18% ROE are better value than Xiaomi's 32x and 7.5% ROE — though its ceiling is also lower.

One more note on the meaning of "ranking." The business models of these six companies differ too much for a hard PE comparison to mean anything — Apple's 33x buys a 46% gross margin and 140% ROE, while Xiaomi's 32x buys a 20.2% gross margin and 7.5% ROE. **The same multiple with completely different asset quality is why "relative valuation" often deceives in cross-industry comparisons.** The truly comparable name is BYD (also manufacturing, also a price taker), whose 22x and 18% ROE are clearly better value.
