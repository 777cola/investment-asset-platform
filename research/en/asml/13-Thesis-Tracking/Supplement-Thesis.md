## Four assumptions are already in place and need no revisiting

EUV supply remains tight, installation services are accelerating, value-based pricing is fully negotiated and gross margin is stepping up—the 2026Q3 results evidence all four, so no guesswork is required. What they underpin is 2027 revenue of EUR 52 billion and a gross margin of around 57%.

That said, "holding true" is not the same as "will continue." EUV supply falling short of demand is a current state, not a permanent one, and the +50% in installation services reflects both expansion of the installed base and high customer capacity utilisation—the latter will move with the cycle.

## Two to verify: the curve that decides the post-2027 trajectory

The first is AI capex — the only exogenous variable that can kill the valuation. The verification window is January–April 2027, when the four major North American cloud providers issue 2027 capex guidance and TSMC gives annual capex. If any one of them cuts by more than 15%, 2027 revenue has to be recalculated.

The second is High-NA. At EUR 350 million per unit, customers buy it because it "can do below 2nm," not because it is cheap. If TSMC, Samsung and Intel push back adoption by 1 year, the 2028 growth curve flattens—fatal for a 31x P/E.

## Falsification conditions: three; if any one occurs, rewrite the thesis

① Gross margin has fallen below 55% for two consecutive quarters. Either value-based pricing has failed to take hold or the product mix has deteriorated — both carry significant valuation consequences.② Orders are down YoY even as revenue still grows. The company is drawing down its backlog, the classic signal of a cyclical peak.③ Mainland China's share has dropped below 10%. Export controls have effectively tightened, directly removing a chunk of revenue.

Beyond these three, watch one soft indicator: management's language on capacity expansion. When "studying further expansion" becomes "adjusting as needed," customers are delaying — and this leads orders by a quarter.

## Watch calendar

Late January 2027: Q4 and full-year results — watch whether Q4 gross margin holds at 57%, along with full-year order commentary and 2027 guidance. April 2027: Q1 results — the first full quarter of value-based pricing. July 2027: Q2 results — first comparable High-NA shipment count. October 2027: Q3 results — validate the 2028 capacity plan.

At each checkpoint, watch only three things: gross margin, orders and China share. Everything else — share price, analyst ratings, target prices — stays out of the thesis.

## The payoff structure of the thesis

If all six assumptions hold, 2027 EPS is EUR 46; at 32x, the target is USD 1,708, 4% above the current price. If only the first four hold and the last two fail (AI capex cut, High-NA delayed), EPS is about EUR 38; at 24x, the target is USD 1,058, 36% below the current price.

That is the asymmetry of the payoff: if the thesis is fully right, you make 4%; if it is half wrong, you lose 36%. The thesis is not wrong; the price treats "fully right" as the base case. Only two paths can change this structure—the price falls, or earnings rise above expectations. The former can be waited for; the latter is uncontrollable.

## Three sentences to read in the next earnings report

In the gross margin section, the first sentence should state the actual Q4 gross margin and whether the 2027 guidance gives a range; a print above 57% would confirm value-based pricing. In the orders section, the second sentence should say whether net bookings are disclosed and whether they are up or down YoY — the leading indicator for 2027–2028 revenue and more important than revenue itself. In the China section, the third sentence should say whether full-year mix guidance has changed and whether management's language on licensing has shifted from "uncertain" to "constrained".

Those three sentences are enough to decide in ten minutes whether the thesis needs revising. Everything else — EPS a few points above consensus, analysts raising target prices — does not enter the decision.
