Core call: this is no "pass" — every metric clears the bar by a wide margin

ASML clears all seven negative-screening criteria, and not by a hair: 10-year average ROE of about 30% (threshold 8%), long-term gross margin of 45%–57% (threshold 15%), operating cash flow/net profit close to 1 (threshold 0.7), and a share count that has fallen rather than risen over five years. These criteria are designed to "rule out bad companies" and offer no discrimination for a name like ASML—they prove the floor, not the entry point. The real issue is the composition of the seventh criterion: whether buybacks can sustainably cover equity incentives.

Which items are most worth watching?

Of the seven points, the first and seventh carry real information. A ten-year average ROE of 30% shows the company did not ride a single boom year to the top: through the 2019 semiconductor equipment downturn and the 2022 order cliff, ROE stayed above 20% in both years. That is not cyclical luck but structural capital efficiency — the capex of an EUV line is spread over decades, and once capacity is built, the marginal capital required for incremental revenue is extremely low.

The seventh point is more interesting: the share count is shrinking, from about 417 million shares in 2021 to about 384 million in 2026, an 8% reduction over five years. Over the same period the company issued substantial equity incentives, implying buybacks far exceeded dilution. Q3 single-quarter buybacks of EUR 4.1 billion equate, at the current price, to retiring roughly 2.5% of shares annually — genuine per-share value accretion.

For gross margin, what matters is volatility, not the level

A 57.5% gross margin is an outlier for an equipment maker (Applied Materials around 47%, Lam Research around 48%, Tokyo Electron around 45%). The threshold, however, is only 15%, so this test is not about judging "good or bad" — it is about judging whether pricing power exists. ASML's gross margin trough was 44.7% in 2019, a year when industry capex fell and EUV was still ramping; it has never fallen below 40% in any year.

The comparison is telling: through the same cycle, memory makers' gross margins can fall from 50% to negative, and foundries from 50% to 15%. ASML's gross margin floor is locked near 45%, a direct result of its order-based model plus sole-source supply.

Any borderline cases? Only interest coverage raises a question mark

ASML carries outstanding bonds (mostly euro-denominated) and interest-bearing debt in the low billions of euros, against cash and short-term investments of EUR 12.735 billion, leaving it in a net cash position. EBIT exceeds EUR 4 billion in a single quarter and interest expense runs in the tens of millions of euros, so an interest coverage ratio above 50x is not in question. The only caveat is that it is not a zero-debt company: if rates stay high for long, refinancing costs will rise, but at this scale of cash flow that poses no risk.

The other six criteria do not come close to the line. The screen yields "nothing to exclude," which is uncommon in our report set: most companies trip up on gross margin or share dilution.

What passing all means, and what it does not

What it means: this is not a company where you need to worry about accounting fraud, inflated profits or dilution by the controlling shareholder — you can focus entirely on the business and the price. The financial statements are genuinely clean: no large goodwill, no complex off-balance-sheet structures, no revenue propped up by receivables; advance receipts instead put cash flow ahead of profit.

What it does not mean: that you can buy. None of the seven criteria touches valuation; passing all of them only shows it is a first-class company, not a first-class investment.

The buying mistake with ASML was never the wrong company — it was the wrong timing. Anyone who bought at the November 2021 peak had to wait until 2024 to break even, sitting through a drawdown of more than 50% in between, even though the company passed every single hard metric over those three years.A good company + a bad price = a bad investment, and these seven criteria cannot detect that.

Conclusion

All seven tests pass, six of them by wide margins; only interest coverage lands in the "has debt but extremely safe" bucket. The negative screen therefore does not exclude ASML, and the conclusion is that it can proceed to deep research, with valuation addressed at stage four. The next step is not to hunt for flaws but to work out 2027 earnings and what multiple to assign.
