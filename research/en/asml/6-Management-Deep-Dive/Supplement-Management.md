## Power structure: a smooth but telling handover

In April 2024, Peter Wennink retired and Christophe Fouquet took over as CEO, while Martin van den Brink stepped down as President/CTO. The two leaving together marked the end of ASML's decade-long "dual-leadership system" — one running the business, one running the technology. Fouquet is French, joined from Applied Materials in 2008, and headed the EUV business; he is the type who "understands technology but operates more like a manager."

The meaning of this handover: the company shifts from "engineer-led" to "manager-led." For a company that makes its living at the limits of physics, this is a change worth watching — not a bad thing, but part of the moat (technical intuition) is no longer guarded by founder-grade engineers.

One more detail on the power structure deserves attention: under Dutch-style governance, ASML's CEO holds considerable power — major technology roadmaps are proposed by management and approved by the supervisory board, with almost no day-to-day interference. This structure worked well in the Wennink era because it was paired with a strong CTO. Now the CTO seat is empty and decision-making power is concentrated in the CEO alone — more efficient, but slower to correct course.

## Capital allocation: no major M&A; all cash returned to shareholders

The record is clean. All four historical acquisitions (Cymer, HMI, Berliner Glas, Zeiss stake) were vertical integration; not one was "buying revenue." M&A essentially stopped after 2020, with cash redirected to buybacks and dividends.

In Q3 2026, single-quarter buybacks totaled EUR 4.1 billion, alongside an interim dividend of EUR 1.88 per share, while cash still rose by EUR 5.15 billion — a state of "more money than it can spend." Buffett would approve of this allocation: absent high-return targets, buybacks are the only honest move.

The one question mark is the September 2025 Mistral AI deal: EUR 1.3 billion for 11%, with no direct link to the lithography core business. Management says it is about "understanding AI customer needs," but it looks more like a financial investment. The stake already contributed EUR 500 million in mark-to-market gains in Q3 — good-looking, but unsustainable, and it should not count toward operating capability.

There is a defensible read from another angle: ASML's customers are treating AI as the core of the next demand cycle, and management wants to "understand what customers are buying." EUR 1.3 billion is not large for a company with EUR 12.7 billion in cash, and is acceptable as an information fee. But if the investment keeps expanding, the question becomes: is this a lithography company, or an investment company.

## Integrity and governance: no blemishes, but one historical controversy

ASML's governance record ranks in the top tier among European equities: a Dutch two-tier board, an independent supervisory board, a 4% shareholding disclosure threshold, and fully staffed audit and remuneration committees. The past decade brought no financial restatements, no related-party scandals, no insider-trading penalties against management.

The one item worth noting is the 2024 "Dutch business climate" episode: the company publicly complained that Eindhoven's power grid, housing and talent visas could not keep pace with its expansion, and it even discussed expanding abroad. The Dutch government then launched the roughly EUR 2.5 billion "Project Beethoven" to upgrade infrastructure and keep the headquarters in the country. **The lesson: a company's capacity plans can be held hostage by a country's housing policy.**

## Succession: the biggest uncertainty of the next five years

Christophe Fouquet took over in 2024 and, by Dutch company convention, his term runs to around 2028. The real succession question is not the CEO but the technical layer — after Martin van den Brink retires, who signs off on the EUV and High-NA technology roadmap is unclear from the outside. ASML's technical decision-making has always been "one person decides + long-termism," an efficient but fragile structure.

The metrics to watch are specific: if High-NA customer adoption goes smoothly in 2027, the technical team has handed over successfully; if it slips repeatedly, question whether the technical judgment has gone wrong. This matters more than any financial metric because it determines the moat after 2030.

Also watch management's stance on "value-based pricing." This is the most important commercial decision of this management team and the only judgment verifiable from the outside — the 2027 gross margin is the acceptance test.

## An easily overlooked governance detail: no customer representative on the supervisory board

Under the Dutch two-tier system, the supervisory board is accountable only to shareholders; neither customers nor suppliers sit on the board. This matters greatly for ASML — are its customers also its shareholders? No. TSMC, Samsung and Intel are pure buyers, and none of them holds a stake. That means company decisions cannot be held hostage to customer interests, and value-based pricing can be pushed through.
