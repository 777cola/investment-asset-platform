Core takeaway: this is a "structural improvement" earnings report, not just "bigger numbers"

Net sales of EUR 11.662 billion came in at the top of the EUR 11–12 billion guidance range, up 55.2% YoY and 25.0% QoQ. Three points stand out: gross margin of 57.5%, 0.5 percentage points above the top end of guidance; installed-base management revenue of EUR 3.289 billion, ahead of guidance of about EUR 2.9 billion; and a EUR 5.15 billion increase in cash for the quarter, achieved alongside EUR 4.1 billion of buybacks. Net profit of EUR 4.960 billion includes roughly EUR 500 million of one-off gains; excluding these, it still beat consensus by 7%.

Layer one: revenue — all three segments accelerate at once

Revenue of EUR 11.662 billion, +55.2% YoY and +25.0% QoQ, with growth across all three segments: EUV systems c.EUR 4.1 billion, +94% YoY (17 units at an average price of c.EUR 241 million); non-EUV systems c.EUR 4.27 billion (96 units, c.EUR 44.5 million per unit, derived); and installed base management EUR 3.289 billion, well above guidance of c.EUR 2.9 billion.

The third segment matters most. Equipment sales are one-off; installed base management is an annuity that tracks the installed base. Its beat signals two things: customers are running high utilisation and are willing to spend on upgrades, and the installed base is growing. Both say more about 2027 than the number of incremental units sold this quarter.

Layer two: gross margin — the source of the 3.5pp

57.5%, 3.5pp above Q2's 54.0% and above the top end of the 55%–57% guidance. Three drivers, in order of importance: a higher share of installation services (structural), a richer EUV/immersion DUV mix (cyclical), and shipment volume diluting fixed costs (cyclical).

Whether that 3.5pp holds hinges on Q4 guidance, which the company puts at about 57%. Sustaining above 57% in 2027 would confirm value-based pricing has genuinely taken hold; a slide back to 54%–55% would mark this as product-mix luck. This is the single number to watch most closely in the entire report.

Tier 3: Costs and profit — net margin deserves a discount

Net profit of EUR 4.96 billion, up 133.4% YoY, for a net margin of 42.5%. About EUR 500 million of that reflects a revaluation of the Mistral AI stake and generates no cash; excluding it, net profit is EUR 4.46 billion, still up roughly 110% YoY and about 7% above the EUR 4.165 billion consensus.

R&D expense is not disclosed separately in this quarter's press release, but at historical levels (around 15% of revenue) it comes to roughly EUR 1.7 billion. That spending intensity is the maintenance fee on the moat—every generation of EUV and High-NA is the product of a decade of R&D, and skimping on it would be suicide. Operating margin is roughly 36%–38% (our calculation).

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the hardest section

Cash and short-term investments rose from EUR 7.582 billion to EUR 12.735 billion, an increase of EUR 5.15 billion in the quarter, alongside EUR 4.1 billion of buybacks. Together these imply quarterly operating cash flow above EUR 8 billion, exceeding net profit. The source is customer prepayments: under an order-based model customers pay first and the company delivers later, so working capital is negative.

This is the best balance-sheet shape in the equipment industry—net cash, low-interest debt, negative working capital. It gives the company two capabilities: surviving a downturn without raising capital (proven in 2019 and 2022), and buying back heavily when the share price is depressed.

The other side: prepayments are "customer commitments to future delivery," and if orders are delayed that money becomes delivery pressure or even refunds. Looking at cash means looking beyond the increase to the maturity structure of contract liabilities—not disclosed in this quarter's press release, so we must wait for the full quarterly report.

Layer five: guidance — the bridge to RMB 44–45 billion

Full-year guidance was raised from RMB 43–45 billion to RMB 44–45 billion, with only the low end lifted. The arithmetic: the first three quarters total roughly RMB 29.8 billion (Q1 about RMB 8.8 billion, Q2 about RMB 9.33 billion, Q3 RMB 11.66 billion; Q1/Q2 derived), plus Q4 guidance of RMB 14.2–15.2 billion, giving RMB 44–45 billion, midpoint RMB 44.5 billion, up about +36% YoY.

Q4 guidance of RMB 14.2–15.2 billion and gross margin of about 57% implies a further 22%–30% QoQ increase. Delivering that growth in the fourth quarter shows delivery is accelerating—about 65 EUV units for the full year, up more than +50% YoY, with capacity the only constraint. Full-year gross margin guidance is about 56%, a clear step up from 2025.

Three easily misread points

First, the 133.4% net profit growth does not reflect operating improvement alone. Strip out the EUR 500 million revaluation gain and growth is about 110% — still strong, but not a doubling. Second, China's 23% share does not signal a demand recovery. The 14% in Q2 reflected delayed licence approvals, and Q3 captured those delayed orders; full-year guidance remains 20%. Third, 17 of the 112 new machines are EUV. EUV is only 15% of units but 35% of value — the average EUV tool price is about EUR 241 million, more than five times that of non-EUV tools (derived).

Layer six: what happened between Q2 and Q3

Q2 revenue was about RMB 9.33 billion (back-solved from Q3 +25.0% QoQ), gross margin 54.0%, 86 new tools and China at 14%. One quarter later, revenue is up RMB 2.3 billion, gross margin up 3.5 percentage points, 27 more new tools, and China's share has jumped to 23%.

Of the four numbers, gross margin deserves the most attention. Revenue scaled up while gross margin still rose 3.5 percentage points — evidence the company is not chasing volume with price cuts, which would have pushed gross margin down. This is "volume and price rising together," which in the equipment industry only occurs when demand outstrips supply.

The other is unit count: 86 to 113 tools, +31% QoQ, faster than revenue growth (+25%). Deliveries of non-EUV mid- and low-end models are accelerating, driven mainly by follow-on orders from Chinese customers.

Cash flow quality check: three questions

First, did the cash increase come from operations or financing? Operations — the company issued no large-scale debt or additional equity during the period; it was buying back stock instead. Second, could advance receipts be pulling forward next year's revenue? Possibly, but customers are balance-sheet powerhouses like TSMC and Samsung, so default risk is low. Third, is the buyback propping up the share price? EUR 4.1 billion equals 0.8% of market cap — not trivial, but the company has bought back stock for the past five years, so this is routine practice, not an emergency rescue.

With these three questions cleared, the cash flow is credible. It captures the company's true earning power in 2026 better than net profit does.

57.5% and where it sits historically

Plot gross margin as a single line: 44.7% in 2019 (cycle trough, EUV ramp), 52.7% in 2021 (chip shortage, EUV volume ramp), 51.3% in 2024 (product mix skewed to DUV), roughly 52%–54% in 2025 and 57.5% in Q3 2026 — about 13 percentage points over seven years, with only one marked pullback along the way.

The slope steepens after 2025, and the driver is product mix rather than industry conditions: EUV's share of system revenue rises from 30% to nearly 50%, and EUV gross margin runs more than ten points above DUV. Add services, whose share rises from 24% to 28%. Neither structural shift can reverse within a year.

So 57.5% is most likely not an isolated peak but the floor of a new plateau. The real worry is the day the plateau becomes a peak — the test being two consecutive quarters below 55%.

Conclusion

Having worked through all four layers, the conclusion is one of structural improvement rather than bigger numbers: gross margin has stepped up, services mix has risen and cash flow exceeds profit. Only net margin and net profit growth warrant a discount. Watch three things in the next earnings report (January 2027): whether Q4 gross margin holds at 57%, whether installation management can keep growing 50%, and how full-year orders are framed.
