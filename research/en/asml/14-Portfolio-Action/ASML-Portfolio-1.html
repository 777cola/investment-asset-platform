Core view: first be clear about its role in the portfolio

ASML's role in the portfolio is "high-beta exposure to AI capex": it trades closely in step with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, TSMC and Applied Materials. If your portfolio already holds Nvidia, TSMC and AMD, adding ASML will not diversify risk—it will only amplify the same bet. Position discipline: cap any single holding at 6%, and keep combined semiconductor exposure, including existing holdings, at no more than 20%.

What it is in the portfolio: not defense, but offense

Many investors buy ASML as a "steady tech leader." That is a misunderstanding. Revenue tracks customer capex, the most volatile variable in the semiconductor chain. In 2022 it fell 60%, earlier than Nvidia — equipment orders are a leading indicator of capex, surfacing first in equipment companies' orders and only later in chip companies' revenue.

Its role, then, is "cycle early bird": it rises early and falls early. That trait is useful for timing — orders turning negative is a signal to cut exposure — and a disaster for anyone who "buys and forgets."

Specific price levels for adding to, trimming or stopping out of positions

Add: build 2% at USD 1,300 (25x 2027 P/E); add to 4%–5% at USD 1,150 (22x). That range marks the valuation levels of the 2022 cycle bottom — the only historically validated "cheap" zone.

Trim: start trimming above USD 2,380 (bull case), cutting one-third for every 10% gain. The rationale is not "it's expensive, so run" but "the bull case has played out and the risk-reward has inverted."

Stop-loss: no price stop — an event stop instead. If gross margin falls below 55% for two consecutive quarters, or orders turn negative YoY, halve the position unconditionally. On a company like this, price stops get you washed out by the cycle.

Correlation: don't make the same bet three times

Correlation with the SOX is roughly 0.85, with TSMC 0.75 and with Applied Materials 0.80 (our calculation). A portfolio already holding Nvidia, TSMC and ASML does not carry three positions; it carries one AI capex bet at triple leverage.

Discipline: cap total semiconductor exposure at 20%, of which ASML no more than 6%. If already overweight, trim something else before buying ASML — do not add.

If already holding: how to manage

No add, no trim; use an event-based stop-loss. Rationale: nothing suggests the business has deteriorated. Trimming is a bet on the cycle, and the cycle is unpredictable; adding doubles the same bet at 31x P/E, where the odds are unattractive.

The specific triggers for the event-based stop-loss are set out in Folder 13 (thesis tracking): gross margin below 55% for two consecutive quarters, order growth turning negative YoY, and China's share falling below 10%. These are signals that the thesis has been falsified, not that the share price has fallen. A price-based stop-loss would have washed you out in a normal pullback such as 2022.

One further discipline: if ASML rises to USD 2,380 (bull case), trim to 2%–3%. Not on a bearish view, but because the odds have reversed by then—upside is eaten by valuation, downside is amplified by the cycle.

Rebalancing: a semi-annual mechanical move

The rule is fixed: check weights every six months. If ASML rises above 8% of the portfolio, trim back to 6%; if it falls below 2% and the thesis is unchanged, top back up to 4%. The action ignores views and looks only at weights—its purpose is to stop winners becoming heavy positions and heavy positions becoming risks.

For high-beta cyclicals like this, rebalancing pays more reliably than timing. An investor who bought at the 2021 peak would have seen drawdowns halved by rebalancing mechanically; one who dared not buy at the 2022 low would have been forced to buy at the bottom by a "top back up below 2%" rule.
