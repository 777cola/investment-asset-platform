## What it is in the portfolio: not defense, but offense

Many people buy ASML as a "steady tech leader." That is a misunderstanding. Its revenue tracks customer capex, and capex is the most volatile variable in the semiconductor chain. In 2022 it fell 60%, earlier than Nvidia — because equipment orders are a leading indicator of capex, showing up first in equipment companies' orders and only later in chip companies' revenue.

So its role is "cycle early bird": it rises early and falls early. That trait is useful for timing (orders turning negative is a signal to cut exposure), and a disaster for anyone who "buys and forgets."

## Specific price levels for adding to, trimming or stopping out of positions

Add: build 2% at USD 1,300 (25x 2027 PE), add to 4%–5% at USD 1,150 (22x). This range corresponds to valuation levels at the 2022 cycle bottom, the only historically validated "cheap" zone.

Trim: start trimming above USD 2,380 (bull case), cutting one-third for every 10% gain. The reason is not "it's expensive, so run" but "the bull case has played out, and the risk-reward has inverted."

Stop-loss: no price stop, an event stop instead — if gross margin falls below 55% for two consecutive quarters, or orders turn negative year on year, halve the position unconditionally. Price stops on a company like this get you washed out by the cycle.

## Correlation: don't make the same bet three times

Its correlation with the SOX is about 0.85, with TSMC 0.75, and with Applied Materials 0.80 (rough calculation). If your portfolio already holds Nvidia + TSMC + ASML, your actual exposure is not three positions but one "AI capex" bet at triple leverage.

Discipline: cap total semiconductor-related holdings at 20%, of which ASML no more than 6%. If already overweight, trim something else before buying ASML — do not add.

## If already holding: how to manage

Add no position, trim no position, set event-based stop-loss. Rationale: there is no evidence the business has deteriorated; trimming equals betting on the cycle, and the cycle is unpredictable; adding equals doubling the same bet at 31x PE, and the odds are unattractive.

The specific conditions for the event-based stop-loss are written in Folder 13 (thesis tracking): gross margin below 55% for two consecutive quarters, order growth turning negative year on year, and China's share falling below 10%. These three are signals that "the thesis has been falsified," not signals that "the stock price has fallen." A price-based stop-loss would have washed you out during a normal pullback like the one in 2022.

One more discipline: if ASML rises to USD 2,380 (bull case), trim to 2%–3%. Not because of a bearish view, but because the odds have by then reversed—upside is eaten by valuation, downside is amplified by the cycle.

## Rebalancing: a semi-annual mechanical move

The rule is fixed: check weights every six months. If ASML rises above 8% of the portfolio, trim it back to 6%; if it falls below 2% and the thesis is unchanged, top it back up to 4%. This action ignores views and looks only at weights—its purpose is to prevent "winners becoming heavy positions and heavy positions becoming risks."

For high-beta cyclical stocks like this, the gains from rebalancing are more reliable than timing. Those who bought at the 2021 peak would have seen drawdowns halved if they had rebalanced mechanically; those who dared not buy at the 2022 low would have been forced to buy at the bottom if they had a "top back up below 2%" rule.
