Core call: there is only one piece of genuinely new information; the rest is noise

Of the seven ASML-related developments in the past three months (mid-July to mid-October), only one truly changes the fundamental view: the October 13 earnings call statement that "capacity will still be insufficient in 2027–2028 and further expansion is under study." The rest—the Q2 results, license rumors, the Mistral revaluation, the buyback—either confirm what was already known or are book entries that do not affect cash flow. And the share price reaction sent a different signal: the best earnings came out, and the stock fell more than 2%.

The only new information: capacity is insufficient, and further expansion is needed

This statement is new. ASML's previous language was "2026 capacity is fully booked"; this time it escalated to "we cannot meet demand in 2027 and 2028, and we are studying further expansion beyond +30%." The difference matters: visibility extends from one year to three years, and the company is willing to invest capex against demand it cannot yet see.

Customers are already discussing orders for 2029–2030 — if true, this means the current equipment cycle will run longer than any in history. It also underpins the base case (EUR 52 billion revenue in 2027).

Why the best earnings report fails to lift the stock

Three reasons. First, expectations were already maxed out: the market expected Q3 revenue of RMB 11–12 billion and a gross margin of 55%–57%, and the actual results landed at the top of that range — above the consensus but within the frame. Second, part of the net profit beat came from a EUR 500 million revaluation gain, which institutions saw through immediately. Third, AI trades became more volatile in October, and equipment stocks were sold off along with them as "shadows of AI capex."

A more fundamental layer: the share price gains from 2025 through the first half of 2026 had already priced in "supply falling short of demand through 2028." The earnings report only stated this more clearly; it added no new present value.

Which are noise

Three things can be classified as noise. First, the DUV license rumor—no formal document, and the company's stance is against further restrictions, making it a "policy under discussion" that does not affect current-period revenue. Second, the EUR 500 million Mistral revaluation—a book gain that does not enter cash flow and should be excluded from valuation. Third, the single-quarter China share jumping from 14% to 23%—delivery timing, not a demand shift.

Noise is characterized by this: a month later no one remembers it, but on the day it is published it gets written into headlines. The way to separate noise from signal is simple—ask "does this change 2027 cash flow?"

The ratio of signal to noise determines what to do

Of the seven items, one is a signal (capacity remains insufficient through 2028) and six are noise or already known. That ratio says: there is no "surprise" to dig out of this company; its value shifts slowly and predictably. For a company like this, trading returns do not come from "guessing news" but from "waiting on price."

So the near-term share-price decline (-0.96%, -17.5% from the high) is no reason to buy. A 17.5% drawdown on a stock at 41x PE only squeezes out a bit of the overbought froth; it is still more than 20% away from "cheap."

The three market moves on October 13

The results were released pre-market. Act one: the stock spiked at the open, as revenue of RMB 11.662 billion and a 57.5% gross margin both beat guidance; act two: it faded once the call began, as management's 2027 commentary, though upbeat, contained no upside numbers, and analysts pressed on the EUR 500 million revaluation embedded in net profit; act three: it weakened into the close and the next day, falling more than 2% cumulatively over two sessions.

This pattern matches the post-earnings reactions in 2024 and 2025 — ASML's share price has become desensitized to "beats" and responds only to the size of guidance raises. This time guidance was raised only at the low end (EUR 43 billion to EUR 44 billion), a "modest in-line raise" not enough to move the stock.

The further 0.96% decline on October 16 continued broad AI-sector volatility and had nothing to do with the company. Reading it as "the market is bearish" is a misread; reading it as "now it's cheap" is also a misread.

Noise identification checklist

Four criteria: anything meeting two or more is noise. First, it does not affect current cash flow (e.g., equity revaluation); second, there is no formal document (e.g., licensing rumors); third, it reflects delivery timing rather than a change in demand (e.g., quarterly swings in the China share); fourth, it will not be mentioned a month from now (e.g., a target-price change by some investment bank).

Filtering the past three months of information through these four criteria leaves only one signal out of seven items: capacity will remain insufficient through 2028, and further expansion is under study. The rest can be ignored. This is also the benefit of holding this kind of company — little to read, long to wait.

Conclusion

Attribution conclusion: the earnings beat but the stock fell, because expectations were already full + one-off gains were seen through + AI trade volatility. The only genuinely new information is one line (capacity will remain insufficient through 2028), and it supports 2027, not today. Trading implication: do not chase the decline, and do not assume the stock is cheap just because it fell 17.5%.
