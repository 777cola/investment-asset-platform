Four Masters — ASML

ASML (ASML) | Four Masters
Research date 2026/10/16  ·  Price $1,641.52 (-0.96%)  ·  Market cap About USD 624 billion (about EUR 538 billion)  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 41x

Core call: the four will split on the same question — "worth waiting for" or "worth chasing."

All four agree on the business: it is one of the hardest monopolies in global manufacturing, capacity is fully booked for 2027–2028, and value-based pricing starts to monetise from 2027. The disagreement is on price. Duan Yongping would say "a good company also needs a good price"; Buffett would say "buy cyclicals when sentiment is pessimistic"; Munger would ask "what could halve it"; Li Lu would say "the direction is right, but there is no need to chase it today." The four votes in unison: watch, do not chase. On odds, 36% downside versus 45% upside — but for an asset of this quality, the market rarely hands you a -36% opportunity.

Duan Yongping: This business's differentiation is physical

Duan Yongping defines differentiation bluntly: "Users need it, but others can't provide it." ASML is the extreme case—if TSMC wants to keep advancing at 2nm, there is no second option besides ASML; a single EUV machine costs over EUR 200 million, and customers still have to queue two years in advance and pay upfront.

He would acknowledge this is a good business he can understand: a simple revenue model (selling machines plus service fees), clear visibility on who the customers are, and a clear answer on whether advanced process nodes will still exist in ten years. But he would immediately ask about price. At 31x 2027 P/E, even if the company delivers on every promise, you only earn from earnings growth—not from a valuation re-rating.

Duan Yongping's approach is "good company + reasonable price + long-term holding." The first two are satisfied; whether the third is "reasonable" or "on the expensive side" depends on how you calculate future cash flows. His habit is not to calculate too finely, but to ask one question: If it fell 40% tomorrow, would I dare to add? For ASML, the answer is yes—which means now is not his entry point.

There is another layer: Duan Yongping repeatedly says "don't borrow money," "don't short," "don't do what you don't understand." The only thing that might make him hesitate on ASML is that it sits at the technology frontier, and the pace of change at the frontier is not controlled by the company. He would most likely say: I understand 90% of this business; the remaining 10% is physics and geopolitics, which is exactly the part I should concede—so buy only a little, not as a heavy position. For position sizing, this point matters more than price.

Buffett: a perfect moat, but this is a cyclical stock

Buffett would like the structure of this moat: a technological monopoly (sole supplier of EUV/High-NA), co-development with customers (tuning yield parameters for advanced nodes together) and an exclusive supply chain (24.9% stake in Zeiss, exclusive optics) — three layers stacked, and no one has breached it in twenty years. More unusual still, customers prepay for its R&D: this is "customers paying to help you build your moat," rare in business history.

But he would immediately caution that this is a cyclical stock. Semiconductor equipment saw order cliffs in 2019 and 2022, and ASML's share price halved in both rounds. The fate of equipment makers is that revenue follows customers' capex, and capex is the thing everyone is optimistic about at the same time and pessimistic about at the same time.

His logic, in his own words, is "be fearful when others are greedy." The current setup: orders booked out to 2028, the best financials in history, analysts collectively raising target prices — this is the standard configuration of the greedy side. The moat answers "can you hold it for ten years"; the price answers "how much you make over those ten years." Right now only the first has been answered.

Put another way, he would be more direct: in this chain, the shovel sellers are steadier than the gold diggers. TSMC has to bet on 2nm yields, Samsung has to bet on memory prices, Intel has to bet on whether its foundry can turn around — only ASML doesn't bet; whoever wins, it sells the machines. This is the "toll booth" structure he likes. The toll booth has only one problem: traffic volume. So what really needs tracking is not ASML's technology but its customers' capex tables.

Munger: invert the question — what could make it halve?

Munger would invert the question: what would it take for ASML to fall 50%? Only three things.

  • AI capex rolls over. Capex at the four major North American cloud players peaks at a historic high in 2026. If two of them cut by 20%, memory and logic foundry capacity expansion brakes immediately, and equipment orders shift from "queuing" to "postponed" within two quarters. This is the only variable capable of driving a decline of more than 40%.
  • Export controls escalate. Mainland China accounted for 23% of Q3 net system sales. The company has publicly stated it does not agree with banning DUV sales to China, but this is not its decision to make. For a monopolist, the biggest risk is never competition; it is politics.
  • High-NA economics prove false. If TSMC and Samsung push back adoption timelines, the growth curve beyond 2028 flattens and the valuation multiple de-rates first.

Munger would also note that the "lollapalooza effect" cuts both ways here: technology + customer lock-in + installed base + value-based pricing stack fourfold, powerful on the way up; conversely, once capex turns, these four forces reverse simultaneously. Great companies can still lose you money at the wrong price.

Li Lu: civilisation-scale infrastructure, though a stable position does not guarantee high returns

Li Lu would file ASML under "civilisation-level infrastructure": computing power is the foundational energy of this generation, lithography is its physical gateway, and without ASML there is no leading-edge process. On a ten-year view that position is exceptionally secure, and it only strengthens as the process node advances—below 3nm, EUV is the only solution; below 2nm, High-NA is the only solution.

He would also mark the boundary: a secure position does not equal high returns. Over the past decade ASML's annualised return came mainly from earnings growth, with valuation expansion contributing little; anyone who bought the November 2021 peak had to wait until 2024 to break even, sitting through a drawdown of more than 50% along the way—even as the company's fundamentals improved throughout those three years.

The lesson is not "don't buy expensive" but "monopolies too can be beaten by the cycle." ASML's moat blocked every competitor; it did not block its customers' capex cycle. A moat protects share, not valuation.

Li Lu's patience is measured in years, but he buys at prices set when the market has lost interest—2018, 2022. Now is clearly not such a time.He would say: this is an asset you can hold for ten years, except today's price has already prepaid part of those ten years.

36% downside, 45% upside — asymmetric at both ends

The arithmetic across the three scenarios is simple: bear case USD 1,058 (-36%), base case USD 1,708 (+4%), bull case USD 2,380 (+45%). At first glance the odds look decent—upside exceeds downside.

The problem is the probability distribution. The base case assumes "2027 revenue of EUR 52 billion and a 32% net margin"—already a fairly benign set of assumptions—yet it delivers only a 4% return. The bull case requires "full value-based pricing rollout + 38x PE," and the probability of both holding at once is not high. In other words, you are taking on 36% downside risk in exchange for 4%–45% upside, with a central case of just 4%.

The more realistic approach is to wait for a less extreme entry point: 2027 PE back to 25x, implying a share price of around USD 1,300. At that level the bear case is -19% and the bull case is +83%, and only then do the odds genuinely work. If that level never comes, accept the miss and buy something with better odds.

One final counter-boundary: if by 2027 the company really does expand capacity by another 30% or more and customers take it all, then 2028 earnings will exceed every current model, and 31x becomes 20x. That possibility exists, but it belongs to "the bull case arriving early," not to "there is a margin of safety today." The two must not be conflated.

Conclusion

All four would agree: a perfect business at a failing price. Duan Yongping would put it on the watchlist, Buffett would wait for cyclical pessimism, Munger would fixate on AI capex, and Li Lu would say the direction is right but there is no need to chase it today. In practice it comes down to one line: start building the position below USD 1,300, fill it out below USD 1,150; at the current price, only watch. If Q1 2027 results show value-based pricing increases above consensus, the buy point can be raised to USD 1,400.
