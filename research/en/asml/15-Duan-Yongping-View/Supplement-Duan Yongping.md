# ASML（ASML）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/16
- Price：1641.52 USD
- Market cap：About USD 624 billion (about EUR 538 billion)

## Notes

This module is written from the perspective of Duan Yongping's publicly stated methodology (circle of competence, differentiation, discounted future cash flow, don't do what you don't understand); these are not the subject's own remarks. Figures tie to the underlying workpapers: 2026Q3 net sales EUR 11.662 billion, gross margin 57.5%, installed-base management EUR 3.289 billion, China at 23%.

## Question 1: Is this a good business?

The good news: customers have no bargaining power. A single EUV tool costs over EUR 200 million, customers queue two years in advance and must pay upfront. This kind of business is nearly extinct in manufacturing — most manufacturers beg customers to buy; this one has customers begging it to sell.

And it gets paid twice: once for the tool, and then for the life of the installation in services. In Q3 2026, installed-base management revenue was EUR 3.289 billion, up about +50% YoY. This is an annuity — customers will not stop maintenance just because they are not expanding capacity for a year.One-off revenue plus annuity revenue — one of the best revenue structures there is.

## Question 2: Will it still be around in ten years?

Yes. The reason is not "its technology is strong" but "there is no second path." EUV at a 13.5nm wavelength is the only solution capable of volume production below 7nm, and High-NA the only one below 2nm. Alternative routes such as nanoimprint and e-beam direct write lag by a generation in throughput and yield, with no turnaround in sight within a decade.

If chips are still made with lithography a decade from now, it is an unavoidable link in the chain. The only thing that could make it disappear is "chips no longer needing more advanced processes"—a question of Moore's Law failing, not a company problem.

## Question three: Can you understand it?

Eighty percent is understandable. The revenue model (selling machines plus service fees), the customers (five or six), the cost structure (R&D plus precision manufacturing) can all be explained clearly. What is not understandable is physics and geopolitics: the technical details of the light source, the mirrors, the dual-stage wafer table, and whether the Dutch and US governments will tighten licensing again next year.

Duan Yongping said that "knowing what you don't know" matters more than "knowing what you know." These two areas you don't understand are exactly the parts he would admit defeat on—so even if you buy this business, you shouldn't take a heavy position. No position for what you don't understand—that is discipline.

## Question 4: At what price to buy?

Current price USD 1,641.52, 41x P/E (TTM), 31x 2027 P/E. Our base case (2027 revenue of USD 52 billion, 32% net margin, 32x) yields USD 1,708, just 4% above the current price—in other words, even if the company gets everything right, the one-year return is in the single digits.

His standard is "discounted future cash flow," not "buy good companies." On that standard, buying a cyclical above 30x means prepaying the seller for the next three years of growth. A reasonable entry point is 25x 2027 P/E, roughly USD 1,300—at that level, even if the bear case plays out, you only return to where you started rather than losing money.

## Question 5: What worries you most?

Three risks. First, AI capex inflects: the four major North American cloud players are at a historic peak in 2026 capex, and if two of them cut 20%, orders shift from "queuing" to "deferred" — precisely how the stock fell 60% in 2022. Second, export controls escalate: China accounts for 23% of Q3 net system sales, and the company has no say in this. Third, the economics of High-NA are disproven; customers push adoption back and the curve flattens after 2028.

Of the three, the first is the most lethal, the second the least controllable and the third the easiest to observe (watch customers' 2nm roadmaps).

## Question 6: What now?

If you already own it, stay long. Nothing has deteriorated — Q3 gross margin 57.5%, installation services +50%, capacity tight through 2028 — so there is no reason to sell. The risk of stepping out of a monopoly is greater than the risk of a 20% drawdown.

If you do not own it, wait. Start building a position at USD 1,300 (25x), add to 5% at USD 1,150, cap at 6%. If it never gets there, accept that you missed it — the market never lacks opportunities; what it lacks is people who are not in a hurry.

One last note to myself: the gap between a "good company" and a "good investment" is price, and that gap never disappears just because the company gets better.

## Connect the six questions

The six questions really split into two halves: the first three (Is the business good? Will it still be here in ten years? Can I understand it?) test asset quality; the last three (What price? What do I fear most? What do I do now?) test the odds on the trade. A perfect asset-quality score does not make the trade worth it.

Many people stumble here because they collapse two steps into one: good company → buy → hold long term. Duan Yongping's method inserts a price step in between. That step is no formality; it determines whether your annualised return over the next five years is 5% or 15%.

ASML today: asset quality 10, trade odds 4. Waiting for price is not a lack of conviction; it is calculating the two things separately.

## One question left for next year

If the January 2027 results confirm two things—Q4 gross margin holds at 57% and 2027 guidance comes in above EUR 55 billion—then today's 31x is not expensive and the EUR 46 base-case target needs to move up, with the entry point moving up alongside it.

Conversely, if gross margin falls back to 54%, showing that "value-based pricing" is just a story, then 2027 EPS must be cut to below EUR 40, and 25x implies USD 1,160, not 1,300.

The entry point is therefore not a fixed number but a function of gross margin × multiple. The current 1,300 reflects that we only know gross margin has reached 57.5%, not whether it can hold.
