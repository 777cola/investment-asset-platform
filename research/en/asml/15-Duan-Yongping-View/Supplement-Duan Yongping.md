# ASML（ASML）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/16
- Price：1641.52 USD
- Market cap：About USD 624 billion (about EUR 538 billion)

## Notes

This module is written from a perspective using Duan Yongping's publicly stated methodology (circle of competence, differentiation, discounted future cash flow, don't do what you don't understand); these are not the subject's own remarks. Figures match the underlying workpapers: Q3 2026 net sales EUR 11.662 billion, gross margin 57.5%, installed-base management EUR 3.289 billion, China at 23%.

## Question 1: Is this a good business?

Good — the good part is that customers have no bargaining power. One EUV tool costs over EUR 200 million, customers queue two years in advance, and they have to pay upfront. This kind of business is nearly extinct in manufacturing — most manufacturers beg customers to buy; this one has customers begging it to sell.

And it collects money in two parts: once for selling the machine, and for a lifetime on installation services. In Q3 2026, installed-base management revenue was EUR 3.289 billion, up about +50% year on year. This is an annuity — customers will not stop maintenance just because they are not expanding capacity for a year.**One-off revenue plus annuity revenue — this is one of the best revenue structures.**

## Question 2: Will it still be around in ten years?

Yes. The reason is not "its technology is strong" but "there is no second path." EUV at a 13.5nm wavelength is the only solution capable of volume production below 7nm, and High-NA is the only solution below 2nm. Alternative routes such as nanoimprint and e-beam direct write lag by a generation in throughput and yield, with no turnaround in sight within a decade.

If chips are still made with lithography a decade from now, it is an unavoidable link in the chain. The only thing that could make it disappear is "chips no longer needing more advanced processes"—that is a question of Moore's Law failing, not a company problem.

## Question three: Can you understand it?

Eighty percent is understandable. The revenue model (selling machines plus service fees), the customers (five or six), the cost structure (R&D plus precision manufacturing) can all be explained clearly. What is not understandable is physics and geopolitics: the technical details of the light source, the mirrors, the dual-stage wafer table, and whether the Dutch and US governments will tighten licensing again next year.

Duan Yongping said that "knowing what you don't know" matters more than "knowing what you know." These two areas you don't understand are exactly the parts he would admit defeat on—**so even if you buy this business, you shouldn't take a heavy position.** No position for what you don't understand—that is discipline.

## Question 4: At what price to buy?

Current price USD 1,641.52, 41x TTM PE, 31x 2027 PE. The base case (2027 revenue of USD 52 billion, 32% net margin, 32x) yields USD 1,708, only 4% above the current price. In other words, if the company gets everything right, your one-year return is in the single digits.

His standard is "discounted future cash flow," not "buy good companies." By that standard, buying a cyclical at above 30x means prepaying the seller for the next three years of growth. **A reasonable entry point is 25x 2027 PE, roughly USD 1,300**—at that level, even if the bear case plays out, you only return to where you started rather than losing money.

## Question 5: What worries you most?

Three things. First, AI capex inflects. The four major North American cloud players are at a historic peak in 2026 capex; if two of them cut 20%, orders go from "queuing" to "deferred" — that is exactly how the stock fell 60% in 2022. Second, export controls escalate. China accounts for 23% of Q3 net system sales, and the company has no say in this. Third, the economics of High-NA are disproven; customers push adoption back, and the curve flattens after 2028.

Of the three, the first is the most lethal, the second the least controllable, and the third the easiest to observe (watch customers' 2nm roadmaps).

## Question 6: What now?

If you already hold it, hold. The business has not deteriorated — Q3 gross margin 57.5%, installation services +50%, capacity insufficient through 2028 — there is no reason to sell. The risk of selling out of a monopoly company is greater than the risk of a 20% drawdown.

If you do not hold it, wait. Wait until USD 1,300 (25x) to start building a position, add to 5% at USD 1,150, cap at 6%. If it never gets there, admit you missed it — the market never lacks opportunities; what it lacks is people who are not in a hurry.

One last note to myself: **the gap between a "good company" and a "good investment" is price, and that gap never disappears just because the company gets better.**

## Connect the six questions

The six questions are really two sides of one thing: the first three (Is the business good? Will it still be here in ten years? Can I understand it?) test "asset quality"; the last three (What price? What do I fear most? What do I do now?) test "the odds on this trade." A perfect asset-quality score does not mean the trade is worth it.

Many people stumble here because they merge two things into one: good company → buy → hold long term. In Duan Yongping's method, a price step must be inserted in between. That step is not a formality; it determines whether your annualized return over the next five years is 5% or 15%.

ASML's current state: asset quality 10, trade odds 4. Waiting for price is not a lack of conviction; it is calculating the two things separately.

## One question left for next year

If the January 2027 results confirm two things—Q4 gross margin holds at 57% and 2027 guidance comes in above EUR 55 billion—then today's 31x is not expensive, and the EUR 46 base-case target needs to move up. The entry point should move up with it.

Conversely, if gross margin falls back to 54%, showing that "value-based pricing" is just a story, then 2027 EPS must be cut to below EUR 40, and 25x implies USD 1,160, not 1,300.

So the entry point is not a fixed number; it is a function of gross margin × multiple. The current 1,300 reflects that we only know gross margin has reached 57.5%, not whether it can hold.
