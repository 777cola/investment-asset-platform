Core view

Most investors know ASML through two labels: cyclical equipment stock and AI concept stock. Neither is wrong, but neither explains these 2026 results. The real change is that its revenue curve has shifted from moving with demand to moving with capacity ramp-up — two fundamentally different businesses.

Old map one: this is a cyclical stock, sell at the peak

That judgment held in 2019 and again in 2022. In both cycles ASML's share price fell 30% and 60% from its peak for the same reason: customer capex turned at the same time. Equipment makers' revenue is a shadow of capex, and the shadow always swings more than the thing itself.

But 2026 is different — not because "there is no cycle this time", but because the cycle's driver has shifted from demand volatility to capacity constraints. Management put it plainly: on current capacity plans it cannot meet customer demand in 2027 or 2028, and is studying further expansion. Customers are already discussing orders for 2029–2030.

Revenue over the next two years is therefore capped by how many units can be built, not by how much customers want to buy. And capacity is visible: fabs, Zeiss's mirror capacity and suppliers' expansion plans can all be seen two years ahead. The cycle remains, but it has become predictable.

Old map 2: these are AI concept stocks

ASML does not sell AI, nor does it sell compute. What it sells is the precondition for making advanced chips. That distinction matters: when the AI boom fades, Nvidia's revenue could fall 30%, whereas ASML's orders may merely be deferred—customers' technology roadmaps do not get rewritten over a single quarter of demand fluctuation.

Yet it would be wrong to call ASML unrelated to AI. In 2026 demand, advanced logic (AI chips) and HBM (memory) are the main drivers, and both stem directly from AI capex. It is indeed a shadow of AI capex—just one whose transmission chain is longer, slower and duller.

The correct map is this: ASML is not an AI company, but the last gate of AI capex. The gate opens in sequence: cloud providers place orders → chip design → foundry capacity expansion → equipment purchases. Hence it rises late and falls late.

Old map 3: Monopoly equals high returns

This is the costliest misunderstanding in the market. In November 2021 ASML traded near USD 900, at over 50x P/E. Everyone knew it was a monopolist; everyone knew EUV had no substitute. The result? The stock fell to USD 360, down 60%, and took three years just to break even.

A monopoly guarantees share and gross margin, not that the price you pay is reasonable. A moat answers "can the company win"; valuation answers "can you make money". In 2021 the two were conflated into one, at the cost of three years of opportunity cost.

Three coordinates of the new map

First, capacity: roughly 65 EUV units for the full year, with 30% growth in 2027–2028, still insufficient. This is the revenue ceiling and the most predictable variable. Second, customer capex: the four major North American cloud providers are at a historical peak in 2026; this is the only exogenous variable that can kill the valuation. Third, political boundaries: mainland China accounts for 23% of Q3 net system sales, EUV has long been banned from sale, and DUV requires licences.

With the three coordinates set, the position is clear: the capacity line is trending up, the capex line is at its peak, and the political line is tightening. Read together, the three lines say "the business keeps getting better, the odds keep getting worse." That is the entire conclusion of this report; the rest is argument.

Fourth misconception: monopolists face no substitution risk, so their position is permanently secure

"Safe" and "profitable" are not the same thing. Kodak monopolised film and Nokia monopolised the feature-phone brand; both looked "safe" until the very last day, until substitutes appeared. ASML's risk is not replacement—it is that lithography itself becomes less important.

If future chip performance gains come mainly from 3D stacking, advanced packaging and optical interconnect rather than from shrinking line widths, lithography's "switch" status will decline. That shift will not happen within ten years, but it is the only path that can invalidate the moat. So beyond orders, watch one very distant indicator: the share of performance gains contributed by advanced packaging.

A complete cycle lesson: USD 900 to USD 360

In November 2021 ASML traded near USD 900, at over 50x P/E. The narrative then was "EUV monopoly, chip shortage, forever." By October 2022 the stock was at USD 360, down 60%. What happened in between? No technology change — memory makers simply cut capex.

More interesting: from 2023 to 2025 the stock went from 360 back above 1,000, a threefold gain, while revenue growth over those three years was only just over 50%. In other words, half of that threefold gain was multiple re-rating, not earnings. For cyclicals, half the return comes from earnings and half from the multiple you buy at. That sentence is the starting point for every valuation discussion in this report.

Conclusion

Where the cognitive reset lands: retire the three old labels of "cyclical stock", "AI concept stock" and "monopoly stock" and ask a new question instead—given three conditions of visible capacity, peak capex and tightening politics, how many turns of P/E are you willing to pay for this certainty? The answer decides everything.
