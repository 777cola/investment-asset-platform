Risk list: five risks, one real — ASML

Core view

The risks, in full: an AI capex inflection point, export controls, High-NA delays, customer concentration and valuation mean reversion. Of the five, only the first can drive a decline of more than 40%; the next three either compress the multiple or cut revenue; the fifth is an outcome, not a cause.

Risk 1: AI capex inflection point (medium probability, very high impact)

Trigger path: two cloud providers cut capex by 20%, Nvidia/Broadcom cut wafer demand, TSMC delays 2nm capacity expansion, and ASML orders shift from "backlog" to "pushout" — the entire chain plays out over two quarters.

Impact: 2027 revenue cut 15%–25%, P/E de-rates from 31x to 20x, share price -36% (bear case USD 1,058). Indicators to watch: quarterly capex guidance from North America's four largest cloud providers, TSMC's annual capital expenditure, Nvidia's data center revenue growth.

Risk 2: Export controls (medium probability, medium impact)

Mainland China accounted for 23% of Q3 net system sales. If controls broaden from a "model list" to an "end-user licensing regime," the hit is a direct 15%–20% cut to revenue, not a deferral.

What to watch: Dutch government licensing rules, US BIS entity list updates, and any change in the company's language on full-year China revenue share guidance.

Risk 3: High-NA delay (medium-low probability, medium impact)

High-NA costs EUR 350 million per unit, and customers are buying it for sub-2nm nodes. If throughput disappoints, customers will stick with standard EUV for multi-patterning for several more years. The growth curve flattens after 2028, and multiples will de-rate first.

Metrics to watch: TSMC, Samsung and Intel 2nm roadmaps and High-NA unit adoption.

Risks 4 and 5: customer concentration, valuation reversion

Customer concentration is structural: five or six customers, with the top three accounting for more than half of revenue. Not fatal, since customers will not disappear all at once, but it amplifies single-quarter volatility.

Valuation mean reversion is not an independent risk; it is the outcome of the previous four. At 41x TTM / 31x 2027E, if growth expectations fall from 25% to 15%, the multiple reverts to 25x — the stock drops 20% with no bad news, merely on "not as good." This is the most realistic risk today: not a crash, but a slow grind lower.

Two overlooked small risks: exchange rates and tax rates

More than half of ASML's revenue is USD-denominated, with customers in Asia and the US, while costs are mainly in EUR. A weaker dollar therefore directly squeezes gross margin—a hidden driver behind quarterly gross margin swings, typically one to two percentage points. A relatively strong dollar in 2026 is one of the tailwinds behind the gross margin beat versus consensus.

Tax rates are also shifting: Dutch tax treaties and the US global minimum tax (15%) could push the effective tax rate from just above 15% to 17%–18%. That is a 2–3 percentage point hit to net profit—not fatal, but enough to make net profit growth look less impressive.

Prioritizing risks

Ranked by "probability × impact": AI capex inflection (medium probability, very high impact) first; export controls (medium probability, medium impact) second; High-NA delay (medium-low probability, medium impact) third; valuation reversion (high probability, medium impact) fourth; customer concentration (high probability, low impact) fifth; FX and tax rate (high probability, low impact) sixth.

That ranking dictates how tracking resources are allocated: 80% of effort on the first item (cloud capex), 15% on the second (policy documents) and 5% on the rest. Other news, including the size of any earnings beat versus consensus, is not worth the time.

Tail risk: what if 2022 repeats

Suppose two cloud vendors cut capex by 20% in 1H27 and TSMC pushes back its 2nm capacity expansion by two quarters. Within two quarters ASML's orders would shift from backlog to pushout, 2027 revenue would be cut from EUR 52 billion to EUR 44 billion, EPS would fall from 46 to 36 and the P/E would compress from 31x to 22x. Share price: 36 × 22 × 1.16 = USD 918, 44% below the current price.

This is not alarmism; it is the 2022 script. The stock fell from 900 to 360, a 60% decline, while EPS dropped from EUR 14 to EUR 12 — 70% of the decline came from valuation, 30% from earnings. The danger with cyclicals never lies in earnings; it lies in the multiple.

Conclusion

Five risks: one real (AI capex), one uncontrollable (export controls), one observable (High-NA), one structural (customer concentration), one an outcome (valuation reversion). The answer is not forecasting but position sizing: a 6% cap, entry at USD 1,300.
