Core view

Gross margin climbed from 44.7% in 2019 to 57.5% in 2026Q3, up 13 percentage points over seven years. This is not cyclical luck but the stacking of three slow variables: a rising EUV mix, a rising services mix, and rising average selling prices. All three are still trending up.

Breaking it down: how much does one machine sell for

2026Q3 delivered 113 new systems, including 17 EUV. EUV system revenue was about EUR 4.1 billion, implying an average price of about EUR 241 million per unit; non-EUV was 96 units and about EUR 4.27 billion, or about EUR 44.5 million per unit (derived). The average price per EUV unit is more than five times that of non-EUV.

High-NA is more expensive, above EUR 350 million. So the impact of "product mix" on gross margin is an order of magnitude: for every 10 percentage point increase in the EUV share, blended gross margin rises roughly 1.5–2 percentage points (rough calculation).

Why do services have higher gross margins?

Installation management covers upgrades, refurbishment, spare parts and field service. Its costs are mainly engineer hours and spare parts, with no heavy-asset manufacturing of complete machines; pricing is based on "downtime loss", so customers are insensitive to price. Industry-wide service gross margins exceed 60%, higher than for complete machines.

Service revenue share rose from just over 20% in 2020 to 28% in Q3 2026. This is structural and one-directional—the installed base only keeps growing.

Cash flow: negative working capital is a free money-printing machine

Customers pay in advance and the company delivers later. That means negative working capital: the more it sells, the more of other people's money it holds. Cash rose EUR 5.15 billion in 2026Q3 while the company bought back EUR 4.1 billion over the same period — both are outcomes of this structure.

The other side of this structure is risk: if orders are delayed, advance receipts turn into delivery pressure. So cash should not be judged by the balance alone but by the maturity profile of contract liabilities — an item not disclosed this quarter.

The Math of Buybacks

Q3 buybacks totaled EUR 4.1 billion against a market cap of about EUR 538 billion, or 0.76%; annualized, roughly 3%. Share count fell from 417 million in 2021 to 384 million, a 8% reduction over five years. Equity incentives are still being issued at the same time, for a net reduction of about 1.6% a year (rough calculation).

The accretion to per-share value is real: if net profit is unchanged and the share count falls 1.6% a year, EPS rises 1.6% a year. This is the "grow without growth" component that many people miss when calculating valuation.

The net margin trap: 42.5% is not the norm

Net profit of RMB 4.96 billion and a net margin of 42.5% make it look like a software company. But there are three layers of distortion here: a EUR 500 million equity revaluation, a possibly low tax rate for the quarter, and FX gains. The net margin was 28.3% in Q3 2025 and will likely be 32%–34% for full-year 2026 (rough estimate).

Extrapolating from 42.5% would overstate 2027 net profit by more than 25% and lead to a wrong valuation. The right approach is to forecast off gross margin (57.5%) and operating margin (36%–38%, rough estimate), excluding one-off items and tax-rate swings.

R&D spending: 15% of revenue, the maintenance cost of the moat

ASML invests roughly 15% of revenue in R&D each year, about EUR 6.5–7 billion in 2026 (rough estimate). This money is not an "expense" but a "moat maintenance fee" — every EUV generation upgrade, the High-NA optical system, and preliminary research on the next-generation Hyper-NA all depend on it.

For comparison: Applied Materials' R&D intensity is about 12%, KLA about 15%, Tokyo Electron about 12%. ASML's intensity is not the highest, but its R&D is "single-point breakthrough" type (optics, light source), unlike Applied Materials, which must cover etch, deposition and implant across multiple directions, so the same money cuts deeper.

Three Rivals of Gross Margin

To sustain 57.5%, three battles must be won. The first is product mix: the EUV share cannot fall, or gross margin drops back to 54%. The second is FX: USD-denominated revenue, EUR-denominated costs — a one-point depreciation in the dollar cuts gross margin by roughly 0.3 percentage points (rough estimate). The third is customer bargaining power: in annual price negotiations, procurement teams from TSMC and Samsung push for lower prices, and whether we can hold the line depends on "how badly they need the machines."

Of the three, the first is within our control, the second is up to the market, and the third depends on supply and demand. Gross margin is therefore fundamentally "a function of supply and demand" — which is also why it peaks earlier than revenue.

Conclusion

Financial truth: the gross margin uplift comes from three slow variables (EUV mix, service mix, average selling price), all of which are sustainable; cash flow is amplified by negative working capital; buybacks add about 1.6% to EPS each year. This is a financial history of "structural improvement," not cyclical fluctuation.
