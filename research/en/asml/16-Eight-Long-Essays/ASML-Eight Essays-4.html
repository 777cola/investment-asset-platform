How the 57.5% gross margin was calculated — ASML

Core view

Gross margin rose from 44.7% in 2019 to 57.5% in 2026Q3, up 13 percentage points over seven years. That is not cyclical luck but the stacking of three slow-moving variables: a rising EUV mix, a rising services mix and rising average selling prices. All three are still trending up.

Breaking it down: how much does one machine sell for

2026Q3 shipments totalled 113 new systems, 17 of them EUV. EUV system revenue of about EUR 4.1 billion implies an average price of roughly EUR 241 million per unit; non-EUV was 96 units and about EUR 4.27 billion, or about EUR 44.5 million per unit (derived). The average EUV price is more than five times the non-EUV average.

High-NA is dearer still, above EUR 350 million. Product mix therefore moves gross margin by an order of magnitude: every 10 percentage point increase in the EUV share lifts blended gross margin by roughly 1.5–2 percentage points (rough calculation).

Why do services have higher gross margins?

Installation management spans upgrades, refurbishment, spare parts and field service. Costs are mainly engineer hours and spare parts, with no heavy-asset manufacturing of complete machines; pricing is anchored to "downtime loss", leaving customers insensitive to price. Industry-wide service gross margins exceed 60%, above those for complete machines.

Service revenue share rose from just over 20% in 2020 to 28% in 2026Q3. This is structural and one-directional—the installed base only keeps growing.

Cash flow: negative working capital is a free money-printing machine

Customers pay in advance and the company delivers later, which produces negative working capital: the more it sells, the more of other people's money it holds. Cash rose EUR 5.15 billion in 2026Q3 while the company bought back EUR 4.1 billion over the same period — both are outcomes of this structure.

The flip side is risk: if orders are delayed, advance receipts turn into delivery pressure. Cash should therefore be judged not by the balance alone but by the maturity profile of contract liabilities — an item not disclosed this quarter.

The Math of Buybacks

Q3 buybacks totalled EUR 4.1 billion against a market cap of about EUR 538 billion, or 0.76%; annualised, roughly 3%. The share count fell from 417 million in 2021 to 384 million, an 8% reduction over five years. Equity incentives are still being issued over the same period, leaving a net reduction of about 1.6% a year (rough calculation).

The accretion to per-share value is real: with net profit unchanged and the share count down 1.6% a year, EPS rises 1.6% a year. That is the "grow without growth" component many investors miss when calculating valuation.

The net margin trap: 42.5% is not the norm

Net profit of RMB 4.96 billion and a 42.5% net margin make it look like a software company. Three layers of distortion sit behind that number: a EUR 500 million equity revaluation, a possibly low tax rate for the quarter, and FX gains. Net margin was 28.3% in Q3 2025 and will likely land at 32%–34% for full-year 2026 (rough estimate).

Extrapolating from 42.5% would overstate 2027 net profit by more than 25% and lead to a wrong valuation. The right approach is to forecast off gross margin (57.5%) and operating margin (36%–38%, rough estimate), excluding one-off items and tax-rate swings.

R&D at 15% of revenue is the maintenance cost of the moat

ASML spends roughly 15% of revenue on R&D each year — about EUR 6.5–7 billion in 2026 (rough estimate). This is less an "expense" than a "moat maintenance fee": every EUV generation upgrade, the High-NA optical system and preliminary work on the next-generation Hyper-NA depends on it.

By comparison, R&D intensity is about 12% at Applied Materials, about 15% at KLA and about 12% at Tokyo Electron. ASML's is not the highest, but its R&D is "single-point breakthrough" work (optics, light source), whereas Applied Materials must cover etch, deposition and implant across multiple directions — so the same money cuts deeper at ASML.

Three Rivals of Gross Margin

Sustaining 57.5% hinges on three battles. The first is product mix: if the EUV share slips, gross margin falls back to 54%. The second is FX: USD-denominated revenue against EUR-denominated costs means a one-point depreciation in the dollar cuts gross margin by roughly 0.3pp (rough estimate). The third is customer bargaining power: in annual price negotiations, procurement teams at TSMC and Samsung push for lower prices, and holding the line depends on how badly they need the machines.

Of the three, the first is within our control, the second is up to the market and the third depends on supply and demand. Gross margin is therefore fundamentally a function of supply and demand — which is also why it peaks earlier than revenue.

Conclusion

Financial truth: the gross margin uplift rests on three slow-moving variables — EUV mix, service mix and average selling price — all of them sustainable. Negative working capital amplifies cash flow, and buybacks add roughly 1.6% to EPS a year. This is a record of structural improvement, not cyclical fluctuation.
