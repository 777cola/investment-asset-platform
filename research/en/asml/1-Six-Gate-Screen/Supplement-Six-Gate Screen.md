# ASML（ASML）— Six-Gate Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/16
- Price：1641.52 USD
- Market cap：About USD 624 billion (about EUR 538 billion)

## Notes

Scoring follows the six-gate investment-checklist framework. P/E is based on the October 16, 2026 close of USD 1,641.52, ~380 million shares and a market cap of ~USD 624 billion (~EUR 538 billion at EUR 1 = USD 1.16). The 2027 EPS of EUR 46 is an estimate and assumes our neutral scenario: 2027 revenue of EUR 52 billion and a 32% net margin. The ex-one-off P/E of 43x is a rough calculation: TTM net profit of ~USD 15.2 billion less EUR 500 million of revaluation gains (~USD 580 million).

## Gate one: circle of competence — 9/10

ASML makes money in two ways: selling lithography tools, and upgrading and servicing the installed base. It has only five or six customers — TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK hynix, Micron and SMIC. A standard EUV tool costs just over EUR 200 million; a High-NA tool more than EUR 350 million. What buyers purchase is not equipment but the entry ticket to "can we make nodes below 3nm".

The revenue model can be stated in one sentence: systems (one-off) plus installed-base management (annuity). Easy to understand. The one point withheld is the economics of High-NA — whether it saves customers money or burns it will only be known from customer volume data in 2027. That is not a failure to understand; it is a question without an answer yet.

## Stage two: good business — 9/10

A rare economic profile for a manufacturer. Q3 gross margin 57.5%, 3.5pp above Q2's 54.0%; net margin 42.5% (including roughly EUR 500 million of one-off gains), still around 38% ex-that. Made-to-order with prepayments — customers pay first, then queue — so working capital is negative. This is not "selling equipment"; it is "selling capacity allocation."

Installed-base management revenue of EUR 3.289 billion came in clearly above guidance of about EUR 2.9 billion; this is recurring revenue at a higher gross margin than whole systems. Equipment is a cyclical business, service an annuity business; stacked together, they halve the volatility. The de-rating reflects only its status as an equipment maker — revenue ultimately tracks customers' capex, and that cannot be changed.

## Gate 3: Moat — 10/10

Only one company can build EUV, and only one can build High-NA. All three core subsystems are locked down: the light source (Cymer, acquired in 2013 for about USD 2.5 billion), the optics (Zeiss SMT, 24.9% owned by ASML and an exclusive supplier) and the dual-stage wafer table (developed in-house). This is not a patent wall but a composite barrier of physics, process and two decades of trial and error — Zeiss controls mirror surface roughness at the picometer level, and no second company worldwide can do it.

The cost of switching suppliers is not money but time: yield parameters for advanced nodes are tuned together with ASML's machines, so changing equipment means redoing the entire production line, starting at two years. This is the only full-score item in the entire report, and the only one I believe will not be eroded within ten years.

## Stage 4: Management — 8/10

Christophe Fouquet succeeded Peter Wennink as CEO in April 2024, with CFO Roger Dassen staying on; the transition was smooth and brought no strategic drift. The team has got three things right: locking in EUV capacity against customers' long-term agreements; pushing "value-based pricing", which starts to show in 2027 and is fully reflected in 2028; and using buybacks to shrink the share count from 393 million shares to about 384 million shares.

Q3 buybacks totalled EUR 4.1 billion, while cash rose from EUR 7.582 billion to EUR 12.735 billion — heavy buybacks alongside a sharp cash build points to very strong operating cash flow and advance payments. The blemish is the lack of transparency on value-based pricing execution: the company says only that it has "finished talks with major customers", without disclosing specific price increases; the 2027 gross margin is the real test.

## Gate 5: Margin of safety — 4/10 (the only one failed)

The 41x P/E (TTM) includes roughly EUR 500 million of revaluation gains on the Mistral AI stake; excluding that, it is about 43x. On 2027 EPS of EUR 46, the current price is about 31x. The base case assigns 32x, for a target price of USD 1,708, only 4% above the current price. In other words, if the company executes on everything, the expected one-year return is in the single digits.

Margin of safety means "price below value." The current price equals value and has even priced in part of 2028. For a monopoly growing 25%–30% a year, 31x is not outrageous—but "not outrageous" is not a margin of safety. There is no gray area here. Score: 4.

## Gate 6: Discipline — 6/10

The buy case boils down to three verifiable metrics: full-year EUV deliveries (about 65 units), installed-base service growth (about +50% for the year) and 2027 gross margin, the acceptance test for value-based pricing. All three can be checked in the quarterly reports. This is not a story, and on this count "verifiability" passes.

But buying at the current price embeds two assumptions: that AI capex does not stall in 2027–2028, and that value-based pricing lands smoothly. If both hold, you make 4%; if one fails, valuation and earnings take a double hit. Measured against a discipline standard, this is paying full price for "everything goes right." 6 points.

## Veto items and items to be verified

Deal-breaker: price. The company is not the problem; the valuation leaves no margin for error. Q3 was its best earnings report ever, yet the stock fell more than 2% after the October 13 release and another 0.96% on October 16 to USD 1,641.52, down 17.5% from its 52-week high of USD 1,989.44. The market did not misread the results — it had already priced in the good news.

Three items to verify: ① Net order intake: not disclosed separately in this quarter's press release; 2025Q3 was EUR 5.4 billion (including EUR 3.6 billion from EUV), a leading indicator of 2027–2028 revenue; ② Mainland China share: rebounded to 23% in Q3 (19% in Q1, 14% in Q2), with full-year guidance around 20%; any escalation of export controls would directly cut a slice of revenue; ③ The actual scale of value-based price increases and customer acceptance.
