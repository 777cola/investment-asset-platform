Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — ASML

Semiconductor equipment: in a USD 130 billion market, one company has seized pricing power

Core view

The global wafer fab equipment (WFE) market is roughly USD 130 billion in 2026 (rough estimate), of which lithography accounts for 20%–25%. No other segment of the industry is as concentrated: EUV is 100% sole-source, while DUV is split among three players—ASML, Canon and Nikon. Concentration does not guarantee profitability, however: equipment makers' revenue tracks their customers' capex, and 2026 marks the third year of an upcycle.

Concentration: the company is the sole player in the most profitable segment

Equipment is a business of segmented monopolies: etching is split between three players, metrology is dominated by KLA and EUV lithography is ASML's alone. Value distribution tracks that concentration—gross margins run 60% at KLA, 57% at ASML, 47% at Applied Materials and 45% at Tokyo Electron. The greater the exclusivity, the higher the gross margin—a rule with almost no exceptions in the equipment industry.

What sets ASML apart is that it monopolises both the most advanced segment and the direction of technological evolution within it. High-NA extends EUV rather than opening a separate route, so ASML also holds the ticket to the next generation.

Demand side: three buyers determine a year's orders

Equipment demand comes from three segments: advanced logic (TSMC, Samsung, Intel), memory (SK hynix, Samsung, Micron), and mature nodes plus China (SMIC and others). The 2026 pull comes mainly from the first two — AI compute requires advanced logic, HBM requires DRAM capacity, and both are expanding simultaneously.

This is the most dangerous feature of cyclicals: the capex decisions of three buyers are highly synchronised because they all read the same signal — AI demand. When the signal is aligned, orders explode; when it turns, orders vanish together. That is how 2022 played out: within two quarters, orders went from "fully booked" to "pushed out," and the stocks halved.

Cycle position: revenue mid-cycle, gross margin at peak

View it along two dimensions. Revenue is mid-cycle: it bottomed in 2024, is recovering in 2025 and accelerates in 2026, with one to two more years to run on the historical pattern. Management is blunter still — 30% capacity growth in 2027–2028 is not enough, and it is studying a further expansion in 2028.

Gross margin is at a cyclical peak: 57.5% is an all-time high, with limited room to rise. Historically, equipment makers' gross margins peak two to four quarters ahead of revenue. The real thing to watch is therefore gross margin, not orders. If gross margin falls below 55% in 2027, value-based pricing has failed to take hold and the cycle may already be turning.

Policy: the sector's least controllable variable

Export controls are political, not market, behaviour. Mainland China accounts for 23% of Q3 net system sales, mainly DUV and mature-node equipment; EUV has long been banned from sale to China. The company has said publicly that it does not agree with further restrictions on DUV, but the decision rests with the Dutch and US governments.

Munger would call this a risk that is "unpredictable but must be priced." Its defining feature is asymmetry: easing controls will not send the stock sharply higher, because expectations never included it, while tightening controls will send the stock sharply lower. Holders must leave a discount for this.

Conclusion

Industry view: the world's best equipment industry, and also its most cyclical. ASML sits in the most concentrated segment with the highest technical barriers — an impeccable position. On the cycle, revenue has another one to two years to run, while gross margin has already peaked; together these imply earnings elasticity in 2027 will be far smaller than in 2026.
