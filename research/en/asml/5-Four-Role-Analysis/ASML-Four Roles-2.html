Core view

Three numbers tell the story. The 57.5% gross margin marks a structural step-up and is credible. The 42.5% net margin includes a EUR 0.5 billion one-off gain and is not sustainable. Cash rose EUR 5.15 billion in a single quarter even as the company bought back EUR 4.1 billion — that is the hardest part of this company. Read ASML on gross margin and cash flow, not net margin.

Income statement: gross margin is the most valuable part of this report

Gross margin of 57.5% came in 3.5pp above Q2's 54.0% and above the top end of the 55%–57% guidance range, on three drivers: a higher share of installation services, a richer EUV and immersion DUV mix, and fixed-cost dilution on shipment volume. The first is structural; the latter two are cyclical.

Telling them apart is simple: watch next quarter. Q4 guidance calls for a gross margin of about 57%, and holding that level would confirm 57% as a new plateau rather than a peak. A step-up in gross margin matters far more than a record high in revenue — revenue is given by customers' capex, whereas gross margin is earned through pricing power.

Net margin: 42.5% — don't take this figure at face value

Net profit of EUR 4.96 billion, up 133.4% YoY, sounds alarming. Break it down: about EUR 500 million is a paper gain from revaluing the Mistral AI stake; excluding that, profit is EUR 4.46 billion, still up about 110% YoY—very strong, but not quite "doubling."

A 42.5% net margin cannot be sustained long term. It was 28.3% in Q3 2025, and full-year 2026 will likely land at 32%–34% (rough math: full-year net profit of about EUR 14.5 billion ÷ EUR 44.5 billion revenue). For a company like this, forecasting off net margin will always overstate—it is too heavily affected by tax rates, FX and equity revaluations.

Cash flow and balance sheet: this is where Buffett would look

Q3 cash and short-term investments rose from EUR 7.582 billion to EUR 12.735 billion, an increase of EUR 5.15 billion in the quarter, while EUR 4.1 billion went on buybacks over the same period. Combined, this implies quarterly operating cash flow above EUR 8 billion (derived), exceeding quarterly net profit and sourced from customer prepayments — the advantage of an order-based model is clearest here: customers pay first, the company delivers later.

The balance sheet sits in a net cash position, with interest-bearing debt mainly low-coupon euro bonds and interest coverage above 50x. That structure is rare in the equipment industry: Applied Materials, Lam Research and Tokyo Electron cannot match such a clean balance sheet.

Capital allocation: buybacks are the smartest move this management team has made

Q3 single-quarter buybacks totalled EUR 4.1 billion, cutting the share count from about 417 million in 2021 to about 384 million—an 8% reduction over five years. Equity incentives were still being issued over the same period, so buybacks ran well ahead of dilution. Interim dividend EUR 1.88 per share, with the full-year dividend estimated at around EUR 7—a yield of less than 0.5% at the current price, negligible.

This is the classic "return cash to shareholders without pretending to be a high-dividend stock" approach. Buffett would approve: when there are no M&A targets with sufficiently high returns, buybacks are the only honest form of capital allocation. The only question is the buyback price—whether buying back above USD 1,600 amounts to cheering on its own stock price won't be known until 2027.

Conclusion

Financial quality: A. Structurally rising gross margin, cash flow ahead of net profit, net cash position, shrinking share count. The only discounts required are net margin and net profit growth, both flattered by one-off gains. Valuing off a 2026 net margin of 32%–34% is far more reliable than using 42.5%.
