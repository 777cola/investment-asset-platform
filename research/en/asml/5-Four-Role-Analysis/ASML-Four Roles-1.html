Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — ASML

ASML is not selling machines; it is selling capacity quotas
Research date 2026/10/16  ·  Price $1,641.52 (-0.96%)  ·  Market cap About USD 624 billion (about EUR 538 billion)  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 41x

Core view

The business in one sentence: customers pay upfront, wait two years, take delivery of a EUR 200 million machine, then pay a maintenance and upgrade fee every year. This is not equipment sales; it is the right to allocate capacity quotas. One test matters: will advanced process nodes still need it ten years from now? If yes, the business holds.

The essence of the business: selling "can't-buy-it" to the few

ASML's customer list is improbably short for a company with a USD 600 billion market cap: TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK hynix, Micron and SMIC. It does not make chips and does not design them; it does one thing — enable customers to print patterns at 3nm and 2nm. Without it, the advanced-node road ends.

That position has a direct commercial consequence: customers have no bargaining power. A standard EUV tool costs just over EUR 200 million; High-NA more than EUR 350 million — and customers must order two years ahead and prepay part of the price. Q3 delivered 113 new machines (17 of them EUV) plus 8 used units — capacity is everything; how much it can sell depends on how much it can build.

Duan Yongping's standard is "users need it, but others cannot supply it." ASML is the extreme case of that definition: not "I am slightly better than rivals," but "there is no substitute."

Revenue structure: one-time + annuity

Q3 revenue of EUR 11.662 billion splits three ways: EUV systems c.EUR 4.1 billion (+94% YoY), non-EUV systems c.EUR 4.27 billion (derived as total systems revenue of EUR 8.373 billion less EUV of EUR 4.1 billion), and installed base management EUR 3.289 billion. Installed base management — upgrades, refurbishment, spare parts and services on the installed tool base — grew c.+50% YoY at gross margins above those on whole systems.

The quality of the business is hidden in this structure. Whole systems are one-off and swing wildly with customer capex; installed base management is an annuity that grows steadily with the global installed base of several thousand tools already sold. The larger the installed base, the thicker the annuity, and cyclical swings are cut in half. That is also why Q3 gross margin jumped from 54.0% to 57.5%: a higher share of high-margin services.

Sources of bargaining power, and its limits

Pricing power rests on uniqueness, but uniqueness has limits: ASML has only five or six customers, and any single capex decision by one of them can swing a quarter's revenue; if customers' leading-edge roadmaps slip, ASML can only wait. Put differently, it has pricing power over price, not over volume.

"Value-based pricing" is how it monetises that power: rather than quoting cost-plus, it prices on "how much more the customer can earn with this machine." Terms are already agreed with its main customers, will begin to show in 2027 and be fully reflected in 2028. This is the only lever for further improvement in business quality, and the acceptance test for 2027 gross margin.

Will it still be around in ten years?

Yes. The reason is not "strong technology" but physics: EUV at a 13.5nm wavelength is currently the only way to mass-produce nodes below 7nm, and High-NA the only way below 2nm. Alternative routes (nanoimprint, direct-write e-beam) trail by a generation on mass-production yield and throughput, with no prospect of closing the gap within a decade.

The real risk is not technology substitution but demand: if the economics of Moore's Law ever break down—if each process generation no longer delivers its cost-performance step-up—customers will stop expanding capacity. That will not happen within a decade, but it is the ceiling on this business.

Conclusion

A 10-out-of-10 business, no argument. Charge a one-time machine fee, then service fees for a lifetime, and customers still line up to pay. Duan Yongping would file it under "good businesses I understand" and move straight to the price — that is where the disagreement lies.
