Team Lead summary — ASML

Four perspectives, four disagreements, four verdicts
Research date 2026/10/16  ·  Price $1,641.52 (-0.96%)  ·  Market cap About USD 624 billion (about EUR 538 billion)  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 41x

Core view

All four reviewers rate the business identically (10 points), but split on valuation: the business and industry perspectives call the "monopoly premium reasonable," while the financial and risk perspectives say there is "no discount." After adjudicating each of the four disagreements point by point, the conclusion converges on "watch, wait for a pullback"—not a lack of conviction, but because the expected return from buying at this price is only in the single digits.

The consensus view: the business itself is not in dispute

All four agree on three judgments. First, this is one of the hardest monopolies in global manufacturing: EUV and High-NA are both exclusive, and no one has broken through in twenty years. Second, capacity for 2027–2028 is already fully booked; a 30% capacity increase is still not enough, and demand is not the issue. Third, installation service is an annuity business that halves cyclical swings — Q3 installation management revenue of RMB 3.289 billion, up about 50% year on year, is the evidence.

So the debate in this report is not whether to buy the company, but at what price.

Disagreement one: is 31x expensive or not

The business-case argument is a "scarcity premium": only one company in the world can make EUV, and such an asset deserves a premium in any portfolio; 35x is not high. The financial-case argument is that "cash flow does not support it": on a 2027 free cash flow yield basis, 31x implies a yield of about 3%, barely above the ten-year US Treasury.

Verdict: expensive, but not absurdly expensive. Set out the benchmarks — KLA at about 30x, Applied Materials at about 20x, Tokyo Electron at about 22x. ASML's 31x carries a premium to peers, and that premium can be explained by monopoly and process-node leadership. The problem is that "carries a premium" and "offers a margin of safety" are two different things: premiums are for good companies, margins of safety are for buyers.

Disagreement two: is China exposure noise or risk

The industry view holds that the 14% in Q2 was a delivery-timing issue caused by delayed licence approvals, and that the return to 23% in Q3 proves it — noise. The risk view holds that policy is uncontrollable, that the 23% exposure is real risk, and that any escalation of controls would mean a permanent loss.

Verdict: both are right, on different time scales. Quarter-to-quarter share swings are noise — 19% in Q1, 14% in Q2, 23% in Q3 is simply delivery timing. But over a three-year horizon, the downward trend in China's share from its 2023 peak is real, and that is risk. The way to judge: look at orders, not revenue — and order value was not disclosed this quarter, which is a data gap.

Divergence 3: Where are we in the cycle?

The industry view says the revenue cycle has one to two years left — bottoming in 2024, recovering in 2025, accelerating in 2026 — and historically equipment upcycles run three to four years. The financial view says the 57.5% gross margin is already a historical high, and equipment makers' gross margin peaks typically lead revenue peaks by two to four quarters.

Verdict: both judgments can hold at once, and they point to the same operational implication — earnings elasticity in 2027 will be smaller than in 2026. Revenue can still rise, but gross margin is unlikely to step up again, and the room for net margin expansion is compressed. That is why the base case gives only 4% upside: not because revenue won't grow, but because the second derivative of margin has turned negative.

Disagreement 4: Is High-NA the next growth driver or the next trap?

The commercial view holds that this is a natural extension of pricing power—customers want sub-2nm, only High-NA can deliver it, and ASML sets the price. The risk view holds that customer ROI is unproven—at EUR 350 million per unit, if throughput efficiency falls short, customers would rather run standard EUV for a few more years and use multi-patterning.

Verdict: treat it as neutral and exclude High-NA's excess returns from the valuation. The adoption progress of the three customers in 2027 will provide the answer. If High-NA goes smoothly, the optimistic scenario (EPS EUR 54) holds; if delayed, the neutral scenario's EUR 46 must also be revised down.

Final recommendation

After the argument, the four landed on a narrow conclusion: this is the company that must sit at the top of the watchlist, but it is not a stock to buy now. Those who already hold it should keep holding — the business has not deteriorated, and the risk of selling too early outweighs the risk of a drawdown. Those who do not hold it should wait for one of two conditions: the price falls back to 25x 2027 PE (about USD 1,300), or AI capex sees a verifiable downward revision (at which point both price and expectations come down together).

Position sizing: open at 2%, add to 4%–5% if it falls to USD 1,150, cap any single position at 6%. The reason for not going higher is that export controls, an exogenous variable, cannot be priced.

Conclusion

Four lenses converge on the same verdict: business 10 points, price 4 points, late-mid cycle, uncontrollable risks at 20% of revenue. The action conclusion is "watch, wait for a pullback," with a buy point of USD 1,300, an add point of USD 1,150, and a position cap of 6%.
