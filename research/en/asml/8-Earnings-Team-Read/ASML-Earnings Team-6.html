Core view

On October 13, ASML delivered its strongest quarterly report ever. Three days later, the stock traded at USD 1,641.52, 17.5% below its 52-week high. One thing stands between that report and that share price: expectations.

I. How good the numbers are

Net sales of EUR 11.662 billion, +55.2% YoY and +25.0% QoQ, at the high end of the EUR 11–12 billion guidance range. Gross margin 57.5%, 3.5 percentage points above last quarter's 54.0% and above the 55%–57% guidance ceiling. Net profit EUR 4.960 billion, +133.4% YoY.

Full-year guidance raised from EUR 43–45 billion to EUR 44–45 billion; Q4 guidance EUR 14.2–15.2 billion with gross margin of about 57%. EUV shipments of about 65 units for the full year, +50% or more YoY. Installed Base Management revenue EUR 3.289 billion, above the guidance of about EUR 2.9 billion.

On these numbers alone, this is a flawless set of results.

II. Three overlooked details

First detail: about EUR 500 million of net profit came from a revaluation of the Mistral AI stake — a book gain that generates no cash. Stripping it out leaves EUR 4.46 billion, up about 110% year on year — still very strong, but not the dramatic "doubling" it appears to be.

Second detail: mainland China accounted for 23% of net system sales, rebounding sharply from 14% in Q2. This is not a sudden improvement in demand — license approvals slipped in Q2, pushing orders into third-quarter deliveries. Full-year guidance remains 20%.

Third detail: cash and short-term investments rose from EUR 7.582 billion to EUR 12.735 billion, an increase of EUR 5.15 billion in the quarter, alongside EUR 4.1 billion of buybacks in the same period. Adding the two together implies quarterly operating cash flow above EUR 8 billion. This is the hardest number in the entire report.

III. That remark on the earnings call

Management said: under the current capacity plan, neither 2027 nor 2028 will be able to meet customer demand, and the company is studying further expansion beyond "capacity +30%". Customers are already discussing orders for 2029–2030.

This statement carries more weight than all the financial figures. It means: revenue over the next two years depends not on demand but on capacity; prices have room to rise because of scarcity; and "value-based pricing" is not an empty phrase — it is backed by customers lining up.

That is also why many people's first reaction after the call was "buy" — when the business is this good, what could possibly go wrong?

IV. The Problem Lies in Price

The problem is the price. At 41x TTM PE (about 43x excluding one-offs), and roughly 31x on 2027 EPS of EUR 46. In the base case — 2027 revenue of EUR 52 billion, a 32% net margin, and a 32x multiple — the target price is USD 1,708, just 4% above the current price.

In other words, if the company does everything right, your expected return over the next year is in the single digits. The bear case is USD 1,058, 36% downside. You are taking on 36% downside to capture 4% of central-case upside.

That is why the stock fell rather than rose after earnings. Not because the numbers were bad, but because they were too good to surprise — the market had already priced in "supply short of demand through 2028."

V. When to buy

This company deserves the top spot on the watchlist. Business 10 out of 10, price 4 out of 10 — the only thing left to wait for is the price.

The entry points are fixed: when the 2027 PE returns to 25x, corresponding to a share price of around USD 1,300, start building the position; at 22x, around USD 1,150, add to the target position. Position cap 6%, because the export-control variable cannot be priced.

And if it never gets there? Then accept the miss. The market will not give you a discount just because you are in a hurry.

Conclusion

A flawless quarterly report, at a price that is not flawless. ASML's problem was never "is it good" but "this price is not worth buying." Waiting for USD 1,300.
