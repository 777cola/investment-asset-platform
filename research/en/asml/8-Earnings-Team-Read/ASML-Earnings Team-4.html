How much is "still short of capacity in 2028" worth?

Core view

Li Lu reads quarterly reports less for the single-quarter numbers than for "what structure this statement reveals." The most important line in this report is not in the tables but on the earnings call: on current capacity plans, neither 2027 nor 2028 will meet customer demand, and the company is studying further capacity expansion.

The weight of this statement

A equipment company saying "not enough" even with orders booked two years out has happened only twice in semiconductor history: once with optical communications equipment in 2000, once with memory equipment in 2021. Both ended in overcapacity. This time is different: the capacity expansion cycle is 18–24 months, while customers' technology roadmaps are fixed—2nm and 1.4nm are already on the roadmap.

Li Lu would press one level deeper: why are customers certain? Because the demand curve for AI compute shows no sign of peaking, and the physical vehicle for compute is advanced process nodes. On a ten-year horizon, this chain holds.

Long-term supply and demand: capacity is the only constraint

ASML will ship about 65 EUV units in 2026, up more than 50% YoY. By the company's own admission, even a 30% capacity increase in 2027–2028 will not close the gap. The implications are twofold: revenue is capped by capacity rather than demand, and scarce supply leaves room for price increases.

For long-term investors this is the most comfortable setup—you do not need to forecast demand, only to believe that capacity will be bought out. That claim has been validated twice in the past two years.

Long-term financial implications

Gross margin rose from just over 50% in 2025 to 56% in 2026 and 57.5% in Q3, driven by two slow-moving variables: product mix (rising EUV share) and service mix (installed base). Neither reverses within a year.

The 42.5% net margin includes one-offs; longer term, 32%–35% is a reasonable midpoint (rough calculation). On that midpoint, 2027 EPS of EUR 46 is credible — a neutral assumption, not an optimistic one.

His qualified opinion

Li Lu would point to one thing: this quarterly report does not answer "what about China." 23% of revenue comes from a region where policy can change at any time, and the company has no control over that. Long-term investors can tolerate cycles; what they find hard to tolerate is "unpredictable permanent loss."

His approach has always been to price this risk into position sizing, not into valuation: "you can buy, but don't buy to the full position."

Conclusion

Structure: supply-demand tightness persists through 2028, capacity is the only constraint, and the slow variable of gross margin is still trending up. Li Lu's conclusion is that "this is an asset you can hold for ten years," but half the return from buying today is eaten by the price — right direction, wrong price.
