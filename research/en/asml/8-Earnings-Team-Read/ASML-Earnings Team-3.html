Core view

Munger asks only one question of any set of financial statements: what would happen if this number reversed? Gross margin of 57.5% is the highest on record, and his concern is not why it is so high but under what circumstances it would fall.

Three sources of gross margin, two of which will reverse

57.5%, up 3.5 percentage points on Q2, driven by three factors: a higher share of installation services (structural), a richer EUV and immersion DUV mix (cyclical), and shipment volume diluting fixed costs (cyclical).

The latter two will both reverse. Product mix depends on what customers buy this year—if memory makers slow and logic makers accelerate next year, the mix shifts; fixed-cost dilution depends on output—if capacity expands in 2027 and delivery pace slows, the dilution disappears. Only the service share is one-way: the installed base can only grow.

Judging the cycle position: revenue has not peaked, margins have

Munger would treat the two cycles separately. Revenue cycle: bottomed in 2024, recovering in 2025, accelerating in 2026. The equipment upcycle has historically run three to four years and we are now in year three, so there is room left. Gross margin cycle: 57.5% is the all-time high, leaving little room to rise.

The implication of this combination is very specific — revenue can still grow in 2027, but the second derivative of margin will turn negative. EPS can still rise, but not as fast as this year. The market values growth stocks on the second derivative, so by this time next year the valuation multiple will most likely be heading lower.

His favorite part: advance receipts

Customers pay upfront, wait two years in the queue and even fund R&D through the High-NA co-investment. Munger would call this an extreme form of structural advantage — not pricing power, but customers with no choice but to accept the terms.

He also knows how fragile that advantage is: once demand turns, prepayments become pressure to delay delivery and customers start renegotiating terms. Bargaining power is granted by the cycle, not eternal.

Think in reverse: what would turn this quarterly report into a top

Three signals, any one of which warrants caution: first, gross margin falls below 55% for two consecutive quarters; second, orders turn negative YoY while revenue is still growing, implying the company is drawing down its backlog; third, customers begin asking to delay delivery or renegotiate prepayment terms.

None of the three appears in the current quarterly report. Munger's judgment, then: this quarterly report is not the top, but it is closer to the top than to the bottom. The right move at this point is not to sell, but not to add.

Conclusion

Gross margin has peaked while revenue still has room to run, and advance receipts are both a strength and a vulnerability. The Munger-style conclusion: don't sell, don't add, watch gross margin. If gross margin holds at 57% in 2027, I was wrong, and we can talk about adding then.
