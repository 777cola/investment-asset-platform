## The world's most expensive machine is sold out through 2028

One company sells a single machine for more than EUR 200 million; customers queue two years in advance and must pay upfront. Its more expensive model, at EUR 350 million a unit, can be built by no one else in the world.

On 13 October it delivered the strongest quarterly report in its history: quarterly net sales of EUR 11.662 billion, up 55.2% year on year; gross margin of 57.5%, up 3.5 percentage points from the prior quarter; net profit of EUR 4.960 billion, up 133.4% year on year. Full-year guidance was raised to EUR 44–45 billion.

On the earnings call, management said something more important than any of the numbers: under current capacity plans, it cannot meet customer demand in either 2027 or 2028, and the company is studying further capacity expansion. Customers are already discussing orders for 2029 to 2030.

The company is ASML. Every sub-7nm chip in the world passes through its machines.

## Then the stock price fell

After the results were released, the stock did not rise but fell, dropping more than 2% over two days. By the close on October 16, it stood at USD 1,641.52, 17.5% below its 52-week high.

Many people's first reaction is: the market must be wrong. Such strong results, such a hard monopoly—how could the stock fall?

The market is not wrong. What may be wrong is your method.

## Start with three overlooked details

First, the 133.4% net profit figure is inflated. Of the EUR 4.960 billion, about EUR 500 million comes from a revaluation of the Mistral AI stake — a paper gain with no cash behind it. Strip that out and the figure is EUR 4.46 billion, up about 110% year on year — still extremely strong, but not the exaggerated "doubling."

Second, China's share rebounding to 23% does not reflect better demand. Last quarter it was only 14% because license approvals were delayed, pushing orders into this quarter for delivery. Full-year guidance remains 20%. This is delivery timing, not a change in demand.

Third, and the hardest point: cash and short-term investments rose from EUR 7.582 billion to EUR 12.735 billion, up EUR 5.15 billion in a single quarter, while the company also spent EUR 4.1 billion on share buybacks over the same period. Add the two together and single-quarter operating cash flow exceeds EUR 8 billion. That is the company's true earning power.

## Let's do another calculation: how much can you make if you buy at the current price?

Current price USD 1,641.52, corresponding to 41x TTM PE (about 43x excluding one-offs), and roughly 31x on 2027 EPS of EUR 46.

Assume a neutral case: 2027 revenue of EUR 52 billion, net margin of 32%, and a 32x PE. Target price USD 1,708, 4% above the current price.

In other words, if the company gets everything right—sells out capacity, lands value-based pricing, and lifts gross margin above 57%—your return over the next year is in the single digits.

What if AI capex rolls over? Bear case: 2027 EPS of EUR 38, a 24x multiple, target price USD 1,058, down 36%.

You take on 36% downside to capture 4% upside at the midpoint. That is not investing; that is buying insurance and paying the premium yourself.

## Why the market already knew

The "supply shortage through 2028" story did not originate with this earnings report. The share-price gains since 2025 had already priced it in. The report only stated it more clearly; it added not a single dollar of present value.

This is the familiar predicament of a good company: everyone knows it is good, so its goodness is already in the price. What you can still earn is only the "better than everyone expects" portion—and this quarter, there was none.

There is also a simpler explanation: the stock rose sharply from 2025 through the first half of 2026, and half of that gain was valuation expansion, not earnings. Valuation expansion rests on the premise that "everyone believes supply will be short through 2028." The company has now confirmed that premise itself—confirmation is realization, and realization is the sell signal.

Capital markets have an unflattering name for this: good news fully priced. The company has not deteriorated; the reason to buy it has simply been fully bought.

## So when do we buy?

This company deserves the top spot on the watchlist. The business scores 10, the price scores 4 — we are simply waiting for the price.

The entry points are fixed: when the 2027 PE returns to 25x, corresponding to a share price of around USD 1,300, start building the position; at 22x, around USD 1,150, add to the target position. Position cap 6%, because nobody can predict the export-control variable — mainland China accounts for 23% of its system sales, and that is not up to the company.

Those who already hold it should hold. The business has not deteriorated — Q3 gross margin 57.5%, installation services up 50%, capacity insufficient through 2028 — there is no reason to sell.

Those who do not hold it should wait. If the wait never ends, admit the miss — the market never lacks opportunities; what it lacks is people who are not in a hurry.

## The last sentence

A company can be both great and expensive. Great answers "will it still be here in ten years"; expensive answers "how much can you make over those ten years."

ASML gets both right, but the answers point in different directions.

Its current position resembles a house with a sea view: the house is fine, the view is fine, but the seller has already priced the next ten years of sea view into the asking price.

Your job is not to judge whether the house is any good, but to wait for an afternoon when the seller is anxious.
