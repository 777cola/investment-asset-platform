Release note

WeChat public account version, plain text and ready to copy. Core narrative: the world's most expensive machine is sold out through 2028 and the company has just posted its strongest quarter ever — yet the stock fell. The answer lies not in the company but in the price.

The world's most expensive machine is sold out through 2028

One company sells a single machine for more than EUR 200 million; customers queue two years in advance and must pay upfront. Its pricier model, at EUR 350 million a unit, no one else in the world can build.

On 13 October it delivered the strongest quarterly report in its history: net sales of EUR 11.662 billion, up 55.2% YoY; gross margin of 57.5%, up 3.5 percentage points QoQ; net profit of EUR 4.960 billion, up 133.4% YoY. Full-year guidance was raised to EUR 44–45 billion.

On the earnings call, management said something more important than any of the numbers: under current capacity plans, it cannot meet customer demand in either 2027 or 2028, and the company is studying further capacity expansion. Customers are already discussing orders for 2029 to 2030.

The company is ASML. Every sub-7nm chip in the world passes through its machines.

Then the stock price fell

The stock fell rather than rose after the results, dropping more than 2% over two days to USD 1,641.52 by the October 16 close, 17.5% below its 52-week high.

The first reaction for many is that the market must be wrong: how could a stock fall on such strong results and such a hard monopoly?

The market is not wrong. Your method may be.

Start with three overlooked details

First, the 133.4% net profit figure is inflated. Of the EUR 4.960 billion, roughly EUR 500 million is a revaluation gain on the Mistral AI stake — paper only, with no cash behind it. Strip that out and net profit is EUR 4.46 billion, up about 110% YoY — still extremely strong, but short of the exaggerated "doubling."

Second, China's share rebounding to 23% does not signal better demand. It was just 14% last quarter because delayed licence approvals pushed orders into this quarter for delivery. Full-year guidance remains 20%. This is delivery timing, not a change in demand.

Third, and most telling: cash and short-term investments rose from EUR 7.582 billion to EUR 12.735 billion, up EUR 5.15 billion in a single quarter, while the company also spent EUR 4.1 billion on buybacks over the same period. Add the two together and single-quarter operating cash flow exceeds EUR 8 billion — that is the company's true earning power.

Run another calculation: what is the return if you buy at the current price?

Current price USD 1,641.52, or 41x P/E (TTM) — about 43x excluding one-offs — and roughly 31x 2027E EPS of EUR 46.

Base case: 2027 revenue of EUR 52 billion, a 32% net margin and a 32x P/E. That gives a target price of USD 1,708, 4% above the current price.

In other words, even if the company gets everything right — sells out capacity, achieves value-based pricing and lifts gross margin above 57% — the return over the next twelve months is in the single digits.

And if AI capex rolls over? Bear case: 2027E EPS of EUR 38, a 24x multiple and a target price of USD 1,058, 36% below the current price.

You are taking on 36% downside to capture 4% upside at the midpoint. That is not investing; that is buying insurance and paying the premium yourself.

Why the market already knew

The "supply shortage through 2028" narrative did not originate with this earnings report. Share-price gains since 2025 had already priced it in; the report merely stated it more clearly, adding not a single dollar of present value.

This is the familiar predicament of a good company: everyone knows it is good, so its goodness is already in the price. What remains to be earned is only the "better than everyone expects" portion—and this quarter, there was none.

A simpler explanation also applies: the stock rose sharply from 2025 through the first half of 2026, and half of that gain was valuation expansion, not earnings. Valuation expansion rests on the premise that "everyone believes supply will be short through 2028." The company has now confirmed that premise itself—confirmation is realisation, and realisation is the sell signal.

Capital markets have an unflattering name for this: good news fully priced. The company has not deteriorated; the reason to buy it has simply been fully bought.

So when do we buy?

This company deserves the top spot on the watchlist. The business scores 10, the price scores 4 — we are simply waiting for the price.

The entry points are fixed: start building the position when the 2027 P/E returns to 25x, equivalent to a share price of around USD 1,300; add to the target position at 22x, around USD 1,150. Position cap 6%, because nobody can predict the export-control variable — mainland China accounts for 23% of its system sales, and that is not up to the company.

Those who already hold it should hold. The business has not deteriorated — Q3 gross margin 57.5%, installation services up 50%, capacity insufficient through 2028 — there is no reason to sell.

Those who do not hold it should wait. If the wait never ends, admit the miss — the market never lacks opportunities; what it lacks is people who are not in a hurry.

The last sentence

A company can be both great and expensive. "Great" answers whether it will still be here in ten years; "expensive" answers how much you can make over those ten years.

ASML gets both right, but the answers point in different directions.

Its current position resembles a house with a sea view: the house is fine, the view is fine, but the seller has already priced the next ten years of sea view into the asking price.

Your job is not to judge whether the house is any good, but to wait for an afternoon when the seller is anxious.
