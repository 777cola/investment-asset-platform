Core call: the numbers hold up; the risk sits in the definitions, not in their veracity

We cross-checked eight key figures against two sources. Net sales, gross margin, installed-base management revenue, market cap and P/E all match, with deviations within 0.1%. Two items require handling on definitional grounds. First, net profit of EUR 4.96 billion includes roughly EUR 500 million from revaluation of the Mistral AI stake; some media report 4.46 billion, which is not wrong but excludes that gain. Second, the 23% mainland China share versus "about 20% for the full year" is not a contradiction: one is the single-quarter actual, the other the full-year guidance. Conclusion: this earnings report is usable, but any citation must carry the definitional basis.

First discrepancy: EUR 500 million in net profit

Of the EUR 4.96 billion, roughly EUR 500 million came from the revaluation of the Mistral AI stake. ASML invested EUR 1.3 billion in the French model company in September 2025 for a stake of about 11%, and after the company's valuation was marked up in the third quarter of 2026, ASML recognised the gain under the equity method. It generates not a single euro of cash and should be excluded from operating profit.

Excluding it, net profit is about EUR 4.46 billion — still above the market expectation of EUR 4.165 billion and about 7% above consensus. So both things hold at once: the results are genuinely good, but part of the 133.4% net profit growth is "valuation revaluation," not "money earned selling machines." Every reference to net profit in this report carries this caveat.

Second measurement gap: China 23% or 20%

The 23% is mainland China's share of net system sales in 2026Q3; the 20% is the company's full-year 2026 guidance. The two do not conflict, but read together they are easily misread as "the share is set to decline." The actual path is Q1 19%, Q2 14%, Q3 23% — down first, then up, landing near 20% for the full year.

Why Q2 was only 14%: after export controls tightened, licence approvals for some Chinese customers were delayed, pushing deliveries into Q3. Why Q3 rebounded to 23%: the delayed DUV orders were delivered in a cluster. This is delivery timing, not a change in demand. Judging Chinese demand requires looking at orders rather than single-quarter revenue, and order value was not disclosed separately this quarter — a data gap that must be acknowledged.

Third, spell out how market cap and P/E are calculated

The USD 624 billion market cap is derived as USD 1,641.52 × about 380 million shares, with the share count on a post-buyback basis (about 417 million shares in 2021). Cross-checked in EUR: EUR 538 billion × 1.16 = USD 624.1 billion, a 0.05% difference between the two routes, so each corroborates the other.

Watch the P/E denominator. TTM net profit is about USD 15.2 billion (backed out from 6,240 ÷ 41), including a EUR 500 million one-off gain in Q3. Excluding it, TTM net profit is about USD 14.6 billion and P/E is about 43x. Industry convention uses the figure excluding one-offs, but market quotes use reported net profit—so the report cites 41x as the primary basis (consistent with market data software), while the analysis uses 43x (more conservative).

Final adopted basis

One easily overlooked cross-check is EPS. The report shows EUR 12.93; backing out the share count from net profit of 4,960 ÷ 12.93 gives roughly 384 million shares, consistent with the post-buyback share base and implying EPS is calculated on the current-period weighted average rather than the year-end count. This matters for valuation: 380 million shares slightly understates market cap, whereas 384 million is closer to reality.

Revenue, gross margin, installed-base management revenue and EPS (EUR 12.93) all use company-reported figures. Net profit uses the reported 4,960, with one-off items flagged at each occurrence. EUV segment revenue of EUR 4.1 billion is single-source (media) and used only in mix discussion, not as a valuation input. Mainland China share is shown on both bases: 23% (Q3 actual) and 20% (full-year guidance).

Valuation inputs: 2026 EPS of about EUR 38, 2027 EPS of EUR 46 (base case), 384 million shares, exchange rate 1.16. The 2026 EPS is a derived figure (current price EUR 1,415 ÷ 37x) and is flagged as such. All derived figures are labelled "rough calculation/derived" in the text and are kept separate from disclosed values.

Conclusion

Eight numbers, zero data errors, two definitions that need clarifying (net profit includes a EUR 500 million revaluation; China's 23% is a single-quarter figure). This is a high-quality earnings report and can be used with confidence as a valuation basis. One genuine data gap remains: net order intake was not disclosed, and that is the leading indicator for 2027–2028 revenue.
