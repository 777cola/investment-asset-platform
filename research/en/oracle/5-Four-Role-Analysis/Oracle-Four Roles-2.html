Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Oracle

Read the three statements together: where the money comes from and where it goes

Core view

The income statement looks good, the cash flow statement looks bad, and the balance sheet is deteriorating. When the three statements tell different stories, the cash flow statement is the one to trust. GAAP net income of RMB 4.76 billion, operating cash flow of RMB 23.1 billion, capex of RMB 28.5 billion and free cash flow of -RMB 5.396 billion this quarter — read together, these four numbers describe a machine that turns cash into assets.

Income statement: the numbers are real, but quality deserves a discount

Revenue was RMB 19.35 billion, up 30% year on year — a rare growth rate for a 40-year-old software company, and the source is clear: OCI brought in RMB 7.39 billion, up 121% year on year, accounting for almost all of the incremental revenue. Net profit was RMB 4.76 billion, up 62.6% year on year, with EPS of USD 1.56; profit grew faster than revenue, showing that operating leverage is at work.

But two discounts apply. First, net profit includes one-off items (asset disposals and tax matters, for which the company did not break out details), so sustainable net profit is below RMB 4.76 billion. Second, this profit has not been charged enough depreciation: capital expenditure this quarter was RMB 28.5 billion, and depreciated over six years, that adds about RMB 1.2 billion of depreciation per quarter going forward, whereas current depreciation is only around RMB 1.9 billion per quarter. The income statement has "borrowed" part of its cost from capital expenditure, and that will have to be repaid sooner or later.

Cash flow statement: this is the real truth about the company

Operating cash flow of RMB 23.1 billion looks strong at 4.9x net profit. But break it down and the figure is inflated by customer prepayments and deferred revenue from RPO — AI customers pay upfront on signing, which is booked as contract liabilities and flows into operating cash flow. That is not a bad thing, but it means the quality of operating cash flow depends on "whether new contracts keep coming," not on "whether the legacy business collects rent well."

Subtract RMB 28.5 billion of capex and free cash flow is negative RMB 5.396 billion. It has been negative for several consecutive quarters, and FY26 full-year is negative RMB 23.7 billion. When Buffett looks at a company, the first thing he looks at is not growth but "whether it can feed itself." This company currently cannot; it survives on three tubes: bond issuance, equity placement and customer prepayments. Of the three, only the third — "customers willing to pay early" — is a good signal; the first two shift future burdens into today.

Balance sheet: leverage is the pricing benchmark for this business

Total debt of RMB 129.5 billion, debt/EBITDA of 4.3x, S&P BBB-. These three numbers must be read together: 4.3x leverage sits at the upper end of investment grade, and BBB- is only one notch above junk. One more downgrade would push issuance rates up roughly 150–250 basis points; on RMB 129.5 billion of debt, that means USD 2–3 billion more in annual interest — exactly 40%–60% of this quarter's net profit.

Meanwhile, the company is still plugging the gap with a USD 20 billion equity offering. This is the most glaring line: a company with quarterly net profit of USD 4.76 billion is selling stock to build data centers.Buybacks and equity issuance are pointing in opposite directions, which says management believes "current returns on assets exceed the cost of capital." That judgment may be right, but it puts shareholders last.

ROIC is the only metric that matters

A rough calculation: invested capital of about USD 200 billion (net debt 100 billion + equity 20 billion + other), NOPAT at EBIT 30 billion × 0.79 ≈ 23.7 billion, ROIC about 11.8%. Cost of capital? BBB- bond issuance at about 5.5%–6%, cost of equity at 9%, weighted cost of capital about 7%. 11.8% versus 7% leaves a 4.8-point spread—the business creates value today, but the safety cushion is only 4.8 points.

The problem is that this 11.8% is calculated on "today's assets," while the 90 billion of assets under construction today will earn returns that depend on 2028 rents. If rents fall 20%, ROIC drops below 8% and the spread goes to zero. That is why we say it is "trading balance sheet for growth"—the growth it buys has a return that has not yet been tested through a full cycle.

Conclusion

Conclusion: good asset, bad timing, wrong price. The database business is an asset he would want to buy; OCI is an asset he would demand a steeper discount on. Combined, USD 144.79 corresponds to 13.5x EV/EBITDA — for a company with ROIC of just 11.8%, negative free cash flow and a BBB- rating, that price offers no margin of safety. His move is to put it in the "too hard" pile and wait for the price or the cash flow to prove itself.
