Team Lead summary — Oracle

Divergence and verdict across four perspectives
Research date 2026/09/14  ·  Price $144.79 (-3.65%)  ·  Market cap About USD 440 billion  ·  PE(GAAP TTM) Approximately 23.5x

Core view

Four independent analyses agree on one thing: demand is real, the model is deteriorating, and the price is not cheap. The real disagreement lies in the details—whether OCI growth can persist, whether the depreciation assumptions hold, whether RPO should be discounted, and whether to use PE or EV/EBITDA. The verdicts below give conclusions and the evidence behind them directly.

The verdict was rendered by the Team Lead based on four independent analyses and the working-paper rating, which remains "Avoid".
Divergence 1: OCI growth — capability or a window of opportunity

The business lens stresses +121% as proof of product strength and delivery capability; the industry lens cautions that the base is only RMB 7.388 billion and that the incremental growth is highly concentrated in a handful of AI customers. Both are right.

Verdict: OCI growth will most likely slow to 40%–60% in 2027 — a base effect, not a decline in competitiveness. The key is not the growth number but the gross margin — if growth slows while gross margin rises, it is picking customers; if growth and gross margin fall together, it is buying revenue. Metric to watch: whether OCI gross margin can move from around 30% currently to above 35%.

Divergence 2: Depreciation, the most easily overlooked line item

The financial view holds that six-year depreciation aligns with peers, and a 97.9% utilization rate shows assets are not idle; the risk view holds that the GPU's economic life is four years, and six years is accounting optimism.

Verdict: Six-year depreciation is "optimism by industry convention," not fraud, but it pushes costs beyond 2029. Estimated sensitivity: shortening the depreciation life from six years to five cuts FY28 net profit by about 15%; shortening it to four cuts it by about 30%. That means the current 17.9x PE becomes more than 25x when recalculated on four-year depreciation. Valuation must apply this discount.

Divergence 3: Should the RMB 664 billion RPO be taken at face value?

Bulls argue RPO is a legally binding contractual obligation and cancellation triggers penalties; bears argue AI customer contracts commonly contain termination clauses if capacity targets are missed, and half of it is concentrated in OpenAI.

Verdict: RPO is real, but it is a "conditional futures contract," not accounts receivable. Apply a 7.0 discount in valuation (about RMB 465 billion), and convert only the 12% (about RMB 76.6 billion) deliverable within 12 months into cash flow. Treating the RMB 664 billion as "future revenue" and multiplying it directly by margins is the most common error.

Divergence 4: PE or EV/EBITDA

The commercial view favors 17.9x FY27 PE, as it looks cheap against the historical 20–25x; the financial view insists PE is meaningless when free cash flow is negative.

Verdict: use EV/EBITDA 13.5x as the primary metric. Reasons: ① capital structure is the core variable for this company, and PE cannot see debt; ② depreciation policy is adjustable, while EBITDA is unaffected; ③ peers (CoreWeave, neocloud) all trade on EV/EBITDA, giving better comparability. At 13.5x, for an asset with negative free cash flow and a BBB- rating, the "AI premium is fully priced in."

Team Lead final ruling

Across four perspectives, consensus outweighs disagreement. The consensus: demand is real, execution is strong, the model is asset-heavy, leverage is high, and the price offers no margin of safety. The disagreements are all technical details, and none of them changes the direction of the conclusion.

Final verdict: maintain "Avoid," target price USD 162 (base case), no buy price, only observation conditions. Two observation conditions: ① free cash flow turns positive in a single quarter and stays positive for two consecutive quarters; ② the stock falls below USD 100 (EV/EBITDA of about 9.5x).Recommended position 0%; to express a view on AI compute, use Nvidia or the power/equipment side instead, because pricing power in that chain sits with the sellers.

Conclusion

Bottom line: avoid. All four independent analyses point the same way; differences affect only valuation precision, not the conclusion. The company is using a BBB- balance sheet to carry a USD 664 billion order book, and it bridges the gap with bond issuance, equity raises and customer prepayments. The demand may be real, but shareholders are not the highest-priority party in this trade.
