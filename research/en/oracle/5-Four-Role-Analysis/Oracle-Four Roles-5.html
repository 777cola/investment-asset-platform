Team Lead summary — Oracle

Divergence and verdict across four perspectives

Core view

Four independent analyses converge on one point: demand is real, the model is deteriorating, and the price is not cheap. The real disagreement lies in the details—whether OCI growth can persist, whether the depreciation assumptions hold, whether RPO should be discounted, and whether to use P/E or EV/EBITDA. The verdicts below set out the conclusions and the evidence behind them.

The Team Lead's verdict draws on four independent analyses and the working-paper rating, which stays at Avoid.
Divergence 1: OCI growth — capability or a window of opportunity

The business lens reads +121% as proof of product strength and delivery capability; the industry lens cautions that the base is only RMB 7.388 billion and that incremental growth is highly concentrated in a handful of AI customers. Both are right.

Verdict: OCI growth most likely slows to 40%–60% in 2027 — a base effect, not a loss of competitiveness. The number that matters is not growth but gross margin: if growth slows while gross margin rises, the company is picking customers; if both fall together, it is buying revenue. Metric to watch: whether OCI gross margin can move from around 30% currently to above 35%.

Divergence 2: Depreciation, the most easily overlooked line item

The financial view: six-year depreciation lives align with peers, and a 97.9% utilisation rate confirms assets are not sitting idle. The risk view: the GPU's economic life is four years, so six years is accounting optimism.

Verdict: Six-year depreciation reflects industry-convention optimism rather than fraud, but it pushes costs beyond 2029. Sensitivity estimates: shortening the depreciation life from six years to five cuts FY28 net profit by about 15%; shortening it to four cuts it by about 30%. On that basis, the current 17.9x P/E becomes more than 25x when recalculated on four-year depreciation. Valuation must apply this discount.

Divergence 3: Is the RMB 664 billion RPO to be taken at face value?

Bulls argue RPO is a legally binding contractual obligation and that cancellation triggers penalties; bears counter that AI customer contracts commonly carry termination clauses if capacity targets are missed, and that half the balance is concentrated in OpenAI.

Verdict: RPO is real, but it is a "conditional futures contract," not accounts receivable. Apply a 7.0 discount in valuation (about RMB 465 billion) and convert only the 12% (about RMB 76.6 billion) deliverable within 12 months into cash flow. The most common error is to treat the RMB 664 billion as "future revenue" and multiply it directly by margins.

Divergence 4: PE or EV/EBITDA

The commercial view favours 17.9x FY27 P/E, which looks cheap against the historical 20–25x range; the financial view holds that P/E is meaningless when free cash flow is negative.

Verdict: use EV/EBITDA of 13.5x as the primary metric. Reasons: ① capital structure is the core variable for this company, and P/E cannot see debt; ② depreciation policy is adjustable, whereas EBITDA is unaffected; ③ peers (CoreWeave, neocloud) all trade on EV/EBITDA, giving better comparability. At 13.5x, for an asset with negative free cash flow and a BBB- rating, the "AI premium is fully priced in."

Team Lead final ruling

Across all four perspectives, consensus outweighs disagreement: demand is real, execution is strong, the model is asset-heavy, leverage is high, and the price offers no margin of safety. The remaining disagreements are technical details, none of which changes the direction of the conclusion.

Final verdict: maintain "Avoid," target price USD 162 (base case), no buy price, observation conditions only. Two observation conditions: ① free cash flow turns positive in a single quarter and stays positive for two consecutive quarters; ② the stock falls below USD 100 (EV/EBITDA of about 9.5x).Recommended position 0%; to express a view on AI compute, use Nvidia or the power/equipment side instead, because pricing power in that chain sits with the sellers.

Conclusion

Bottom line: avoid. All four independent analyses point the same way; the differences affect only valuation precision, not the conclusion. The company is carrying a USD 664 billion order book on a BBB- balance sheet, and bridges the gap with bond issuance, equity raises and customer prepayments. Demand may be real, but shareholders are not the highest-priority party in this trade.
