## Gate one: you can read the software, but not the project financing

Score it 7; the three points deducted reflect that the center of gravity of this business has shifted. Oracle twenty years ago was three sentences: sell database licenses, collect annual maintenance fees, and any customer wanting to switch would have to spend years rewriting systems. With gross margins above 80% and capex near zero, this was a business that could expand with paper and discs.

Today the structure is: cloud services RMB 11.61 billion (60%), of which OCI RMB 7.39 billion is renting racks, renting GPUs, renting power; software licenses and support down to just RMB 5.55 billion, -3% year on year.Looking at it through a software-company framework systematically understates capital intensity and depreciation risk. OCI's unit economics are "how much invested per megawatt, payback in how many years, what power price, GPU depreciation over how many years" — this has to be viewed through the lens of data-center REITs and project finance; it is a different craft from looking at SaaS. I can model both, but mixing them into one income statement for valuation makes it very easy to get wrong.

## Stage two: for every USD 1 earned, USD 1.47 must be invested

This is the core disqualifier. Revenue this quarter was RMB 19.345 billion, +30% YoY, and capex was RMB 28.499 billion, +235% YoY. Capex is growing 8x faster than revenue; this growth is not squeezed out, it is bought. FY26 free cash flow is already negative RMB 23.7 billion; the FY27 capex guidance is RMB 90–95 billion with a net cash capex ceiling of RMB 70 billion, while full-year operating cash flow at the current pace is roughly just over RMB 90 billion — leaving a gap in between.

A good business has a rough but useful test: does growth require shareholders to keep putting in money? Coca-Cola expands capacity through bottlers, Microsoft expands cloud through operating cash flow, and Oracle expands OCI through bond issuance, equity offerings and customer prepayments. None of these three kinds of money belongs to existing shareholders. The larger the scale, the larger the gap; this is not a curve where "it will be fine once scale arrives" — it is a J-curve that worsens first and improves later, and no one can guarantee which year the inflection point falls in.

## Stage three: a moat protects the old business, not the new one

Score 6, because the two businesses must be looked at separately. The database side is real: the legacy systems of major global banks, telcos and governments run on Oracle, and migrating takes years. Database-related business grew +353% year on year this quarter, and even AWS and Azure have been forced to connect to Oracle Database—a competitor's cloud having to connect to your database is the hardest evidence of a moat.

OCI is not. Customers compare price per GPU hour, speed to power and network convergence ratio; the supplier is Nvidia, and the rivals are three cloud players with positive free cash flow. Oracle's edge is "delivering a few months earlier than others"—that is a time window, not a structural barrier. The moat is enough to defend RMB 5.5 billion in software license revenue, but not enough to support delivery of RMB 664 billion in orders—whether those orders can be delivered depends on whether it can keep borrowing to build out data centers.

## Stage 4: Execution A, capital allocation C+

Execution is not in doubt. The Abilene campus delivered 131,000 GPUs in a single quarter and completed acceptance in 24 hours—no second company worldwide can match that speed. New AI contracts signed this quarter exceeded USD 30 billion, showing it ranks among the most demanding customers' choices. Management completed its transition in September 2025, with Clay Magouyrk and Mike Sicilia as co-CEOs and Safra Catz moving to vice chairman; no strategic wavering occurred during the handover.

The deduction is for capital allocation. In the same quarter, the company completed a USD 20 billion ATM equity offering, piled debt up to USD 129.5 billion, and partly staked funding for coming quarters on customer prepayments. This is not "seizing the opportunity"—it is opening every financing channel to maximum at once. The old playbook of buying revenue with cash flow—NetSuite in 2016, Cerner in 2022—has now reversed: buying revenue with the balance sheet. A score of 5, with all deductions here.

## The fifth hurdle: 17.9x PE is an accounting construct

USD 144.79 corresponds to 17.9x FY27 non-GAAP EPS (guidance USD 8.10), and also to 23.5x GAAP TTM PE. The gap comes from stock-based compensation, restructuring charges, and most critically — the non-GAAP measure does not reflect depreciation from USD 90 billion of capex. Depreciation this quarter is already about USD 1.9 billion/quarter (rough calculation, based on FY26 depreciation of USD 7.6 billion plus annualized new assets), and full-year FY27 depreciation will most likely reach USD 11–13 billion.

Another way to calculate: market cap of USD 440 billion + net debt of about USD 100 billion = enterprise value of USD 540 billion; FY27 EBITDA roughly USD 40 billion, EV/EBITDA about 13.5x. A company with negative free cash flow and a BBB- rating at 13.5x EV/EBITDA means the AI story is fully priced in ahead of time. There is no margin of safety at this price, and there is no ambiguity on this point.

## Stage six: three preconditions, none of them in its own hands

The complete logic for buying now is: ① OpenAI and a handful of AI labs pay in accordance with contractual obligations; ② financing costs do not rise over the next two years, and BBB- can continue to issue debt; ③ GPUs do not depreciate quickly, so the depreciation assumptions hold and rents do not collapse. If any one of these three premises breaks, the valuation switches from "AI growth stock" back to "heavy-asset operator."

None of these three premises is controlled by Oracle itself. The first depends on customer financing, the second on interest rates and ratings, the third on Nvidia's iteration cadence. The discipline test is not "how high is the probability" but "would being wrong cause structural damage"—with negative free cash flow and 4.3x leverage, one mistake means the equity is cut in half. Score: 3.

## Veto items and items to be verified

Deal-breaker: cash flow and financing structure. FY26 free cash flow was -RMB 23.7 billion, and FY27Q1 alone was -RMB 5.396 billion; total debt is about RMB 129.5 billion, debt/EBITDA 4.3x; S&P downgraded to BBB- in July 2026, one notch above junk.The company is using an investment-grade-edge balance sheet to carry the construction risk of hyperscale data centers.

Items to verify (in order of importance):

- RPO conversion rate — management says about 12% of RMB 664 billion (about RMB 76.6 billion) converts to revenue within 12 months; actual revenue recognition in Q2 and Q3 is the only validation;

- Whether the capex/revenue ratio can fall from 1.47 to below 0.6 — the only hard indicator that expansion has entered the harvest phase;

- Customer concentration — the market estimates more than half of RPO is tied to OpenAI (analyst estimate, not company disclosure); a cut in orders from a single customer would collapse the order story;

- GPU depreciation life assumptions, and whether the 20% renewal premium on older cards and 97.9% utilization can hold;

- The actual bond issuance rate under the BBB- rating — a direct deduction from project returns.
