Funnel conclusion

Run the 6 major players in enterprise software and cloud computing through a funnel: the first round cuts CoreWeave and Oracle on "positive free cash flow," the second round keeps Microsoft, Amazon and Google on "AI revenue share and growth," and the third round selects Microsoft on "valuation and moat." Oracle is eliminated in the first round—not because its growth is too slow, but precisely because it bought the fastest growth with negative cash flow. That is the point of a funnel: screen for quality first, talk story later.

Round one: positive cash flow — cut two

This is the crudest and most effective filter of the round: a company with negative free cash flow has no valuation anchor. Oracle printed -RMB 5.396 billion for the quarter and CoreWeave -RMB 7.466 billion; both were cut. The remaining five, including Amazon on equally enormous capex, still generate positive free cash flow — the distinction is not how much they invest but whether the money they invest is money they earned.

This filter cuts the sexiest names, and that is precisely its value. The two fastest-growing companies in AI infrastructure happen to be the two with the worst cash flow — no coincidence, but structure: trading financing for growth.

Round two: AI revenue share and growth — three remain

Microsoft's AI revenue is already embedded in reported financials, Azure is growing around 30% and Copilot is delivering genuine seat growth; AWS is growing around 20% and its in-house Trainium chip is already in its third generation; Google Cloud is growing around 28%, TPU is proprietary and Gemini has its own distribution channels. All three run a closed loop of "compute + models + distribution."

Salesforce and SAP are left behind by this cycle: AI revenue share is low, growth is around 10%, and they are "AI beneficiaries" rather than "AI suppliers." That is not to say they are bad companies, only that they do not offer the exposure this theme is looking for.

Round Three: Valuation and Moat — Why Microsoft

Microsoft at 33x P/E is not cheap, but free cash flow covers capex, the balance sheet is rated AAA, customer switching costs are extremely high (Office + Entra + Azure form a single stack), and AI revenue can already be tracked separately. It is the only company that both sells the shovels and uses them to make money. For capital seeking AI compute exposure without leverage risk, it is the default answer.

Google at 26x P/E is cheaper and the cost advantage of its in-house TPU is more pronounced, but its problem is distribution: Gemini has the technology but not the channel, and its cloud market share ranks third. If you can only pick one, Microsoft; if you can pick two, add Google.

Why Oracle drops out of the funnel first

Its growth (+30%) ranks second among the seven, OCI growth (+121%) ranks first and RPO (RMB 664 billion) ranks first. On growth alone, it should make the final cut. But the first filter in the funnel is quality, not growth.

The more fundamental problem: its growth was bought with the balance sheet, and its moat does not extend to the side that is being built. The database's customer dependence does nothing for OCI's pricing power, and OCI's scale does nothing for the database's contraction (-3%). A company that leads on neither side does not survive the second round of the funnel. That is the complete answer to "why not it."

If forced to pick one "turnaround candidate" from this group

This is not Oracle — it is Amazon. Free cash flow, though compressed by capex, remains positive; AWS growth of 20% trails OCI's 121%, but its base is ten times larger, its customers are diversified and its in-house chips are already in mass production; and a 35x P/E already reflects the drag from retail, leaving the AI exposure as a free option.

An Oracle turnaround requires three conditions to hold at once — cash flow turning positive, rating stability and customer diversification. Amazon needs only one: AWS growth holding steady. Both are waiting for a turnaround, but waiting on one condition is far more cost-effective than waiting on three.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the funnel selects Microsoft; Oracle is eliminated in the first round. Not because Oracle's story is weak—the USD 664 billion order backlog and +121% OCI growth are real—but because screening exists to find the name with the right quality at the right price, not the one growing fastest. If Oracle's free cash flow turns positive in future, it re-enters the funnel; until then, it is out at the first screen.
