## Layer one: revenue mix — all growth comes from selling compute

Of the RMB 19.345 billion, OCI accounted for RMB 7.388 billion, up 121% YoY, contributing USD 4 billion of incremental revenue on its own — the entire source of growth. Cloud services totaled RMB 11.607 billion, or 60%, surpassing software licenses and support (RMB 5.550 billion, -3%) for the first time in history. SaaS grew just 10%, hardware +15%, services +5%.

**The weight of this structural shift matters more than the growth rate itself.** Software licenses and support is a 90%-gross-margin, zero-capex business; OCI is a 30%-gross-margin business that ties up USD 1.5 of assets per dollar of revenue. For every percentage point revenue mix shifts toward OCI, the company's overall return on capital steps down.**The market is cheering the +121%, but nobody asks: if OCI grew 20% and software +5%, what would this company be worth?**

## Second layer: gross profit and expenses — operating leverage remains, but is nearing its limit

Gross margin is about 65%, down 15 points from 80% three years ago — the inevitable result of a rising OCI mix. But the operating margin holds at 42%, a smaller decline than gross margin — because cost control is extremely aggressive: sales, R&D and G&A together account for only 23.2% of revenue, up just 22% year on year, slower than revenue growth.

**This is a classic case of offsetting gross-margin erosion with expense discipline.** It can hold for a year or two, not for five: depreciation is climbing from RMB 7.6 billion a year toward RMB 13 billion, and every additional RMB 1 billion of depreciation shaves 0.5 percentage points off the operating margin. Nor can R&D spending keep growing at just 20% indefinitely — OCI has to compete with Nvidia and Microsoft on the software stack, so R&D investment will have to catch up sooner or later.**The 42% operating margin may be the peak of this cycle.**

## Layer three: orders — RMB 664 billion is real, but it is not cash

RPO USD 664 billion, up 46% year on year, with USD 26 billion added in the quarter, above the consensus of USD 630.6 billion. Management gave the conversion schedule: about 12% (USD 76.6 billion) converts to revenue within 12 months, and 34% (USD 225.8 billion) within 36 months. New AI contracts signed in the quarter exceeded USD 30 billion.

Three things need to be clear. **First, RPO is a contractual obligation, not a receivable**; customers pay damages on default, but AI customer contracts generally include clauses allowing termination if capacity targets are missed. **Second, concentration** — the market estimates more than half relates to OpenAI, meaning the quality of the order book equals the credit of one loss-making private company. **Third, the conversion schedule determines cash flow**: USD 76.6 billion converts within 12 months, and at a 65% gross margin that is about USD 50 billion of gross profit, just enough to cover half of one year's capex.

## Tier four: cash flow and balance sheet — the ugliest section in this entire report

Operating cash flow of about RMB 23.1 billion (estimated), capex of RMB 28.499 billion, free cash flow of -RMB 5.396 billion. To fill the gap, the company completed a USD 20 billion ATM equity offering this quarter. **A company with quarterly net income of USD 4.76 billion is selling stock to build data centers—that sentence alone is a verdict on the valuation.**

Balance sheet: total debt of about RMB 129.5 billion, debt/EBITDA of 4.3x, S&P BBB- (downgraded July 2026). FY27 capex guidance of RMB 90–95 billion and a net cash capex ceiling of RMB 70 billion imply another RMB 30–40 billion of financing for the full year. **A rough calculation: if financing costs rise 100 basis points in 2027, full-year interest expense increases by RMB 1.3 billion; if the rating falls one notch to BB+, the increase is RMB 2.5–3.5 billion. Both figures exceed 25% of this quarter's net income.**

## Layer five: asset quality — what the RMB 90 billion of capex is buying

Capex only means something once it is broken down into "what is being bought." The RMB 90–95 billion for FY27 splits roughly into three buckets: GPUs and servers (about 60%), data halls and power facilities (about 30%), networking and software (about 10%). **These three buckets have completely different depreciation lives: GPUs four to six years, data halls twenty years and up, networking equipment five to seven years.** The company depreciates everything over six years as a whole, which amounts to lumping the shortest-lived, most expensive assets together with the longest-lived assets in one basket.

The consequence: early depreciation pressure is understated and then erupts in a cluster later. By 2029, if cumulative investment reaches USD 30 billion and is amortized over six years on average, annual depreciation would reach USD 5 billion — three times full-year net profit in 2026. **The income statement then depends on 2029 rents, not on 2026 orders.** That is why the key things to read in this report are the depreciation policy and the scale of construction in progress, not this quarter's net profit.
