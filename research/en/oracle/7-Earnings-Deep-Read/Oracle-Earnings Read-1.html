Earnings read-through

FY27Q1 was a tale of two halves: a pretty top line, an ugly bottom line. Revenue came in at RMB 19.35 billion, +30% YoY, with OCI +121%, RPO of RMB 664.0 billion, GAAP net income of RMB 4.76 billion and EPS of USD 1.56 — all ahead of consensus. But capex was RMB 28.50 billion, +235% YoY, free cash flow was -RMB 5.40 billion, and the company simultaneously completed a USD 20 billion equity offering. Put simply, the income statement tells a growth story while the cash flow statement tells a financing story.

Layer one: revenue mix — growth is entirely compute-driven

Of the RMB 19.345 billion total, OCI contributed RMB 7.388 billion, up 121% YoY and adding USD 4 billion of incremental revenue on its own — the entire source of growth. Cloud services reached RMB 11.607 billion, or 60% of the total, overtaking software licenses and support (RMB 5.550 billion, -3%) for the first time. SaaS grew just 10%, hardware +15% and services +5%.

The structural shift matters more than the growth rate itself. Software licenses and support is a 90%-gross-margin, zero-capex business; OCI carries a 30% gross margin and ties up USD 1.5 of assets per dollar of revenue. Every percentage point of mix that shifts toward OCI steps down the company's overall return on capital.The market is cheering the +121%, but nobody asks: if OCI grew 20% and software +5%, what would this company be worth?

Second layer: gross profit and expenses — operating leverage persists, but is close to its limit

Gross margin is around 65%, down 15 points from 80% three years ago — the inevitable result of a rising OCI mix. Operating margin, however, holds at 42%, a smaller decline than gross margin, because cost control is extremely aggressive: sales, R&D and G&A together account for only 23.2% of revenue and grew just 22% YoY, slower than revenue.

This is a classic case of offsetting gross-margin erosion with expense discipline. That can hold for a year or two, not for five: depreciation is climbing from RMB 7.6 billion a year toward RMB 13 billion, and every additional RMB 1 billion of depreciation shaves 0.5 percentage points off the operating margin. Nor can R&D spending keep growing at just 20% indefinitely — OCI has to compete with Nvidia and Microsoft on the software stack, so R&D investment will have to catch up sooner or later. The 42% operating margin may be the peak of this cycle.

Layer three: orders — RMB 664 billion is real, but it is not cash

RPO of USD 664 billion, up 46% YoY, with USD 26 billion added in the quarter, ahead of the USD 630.6 billion consensus. Management laid out the conversion schedule: roughly 12% (USD 76.6 billion) converts to revenue within 12 months and 34% (USD 225.8 billion) within 36 months. New AI contracts signed in the quarter exceeded USD 30 billion.

Three points stand out. First, RPO is a contractual obligation, not a receivable; customers pay damages on default, but AI customer contracts generally carry clauses allowing termination if capacity targets are missed. Second, concentration — the market estimates more than half relates to OpenAI, so the quality of the order book equates to the credit of a single loss-making private company. Third, the conversion schedule drives cash flow: USD 76.6 billion converts within 12 months, which at a 65% gross margin is about USD 50 billion of gross profit — just enough to cover half of one year's capex.

Tier four: cash flow and balance sheet — the ugliest section in this entire report

Operating cash flow of about RMB 23.1 billion (estimated), against capex of RMB 28.499 billion, leaves free cash flow of -RMB 5.396 billion. To bridge the gap, the company completed a USD 20 billion ATM equity offering this quarter. A company earning USD 4.76 billion of net income a quarter is selling stock to build data centers—that sentence alone is a verdict on the valuation.

Balance sheet: total debt of about RMB 129.5 billion, debt/EBITDA of 4.3x, rated BBB- by S&P (downgraded July 2026). FY27 capex guidance of RMB 90–95 billion and a net cash capex ceiling of RMB 70 billion imply a further RMB 30–40 billion of financing for the full year. A rough calculation: a 100 basis point rise in financing costs in 2027 adds RMB 1.3 billion to full-year interest expense; a one-notch downgrade to BB+ adds RMB 2.5–3.5 billion. Either figure exceeds 25% of this quarter's net income.

Layer five: asset quality — what the RMB 90 billion of capex buys

Capex only means something once it is broken down into what is actually being bought. The RMB 90–95 billion for FY27 splits roughly into three buckets: GPUs and servers (about 60%), data halls and power facilities (about 30%), networking and software (about 10%). Depreciation lives differ sharply across the three: four to six years for GPUs, twenty years and up for data halls, five to seven years for networking equipment. The company depreciates everything over six years as a whole, lumping the shortest-lived, most expensive assets together with the longest-lived in one basket.

The consequence: early depreciation pressure is understated, then erupts in a cluster later. By 2029, if cumulative investment reaches USD 30 billion and is amortised over six years on average, annual depreciation would reach USD 5 billion — three times full-year net profit in 2026. The income statement then depends on 2029 rents, not on 2026 orders. That is why the key things to read in this report are the depreciation policy and the scale of construction in progress, not this quarter's net profit.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the results prove two things—demand is real, and the model has deteriorated. Revenue, orders and profit all beat consensus, confirming Oracle has secured a seat in AI compute; capex, free cash flow and debt deteriorated in tandem, confirming that seat is being bought with shareholders' money. The next earnings report hinges on just three numbers: OCI gross margin, capex/revenue ratio, and the 12-month conversion rate of RPO. If those three do not improve, even faster revenue growth only converts more cash into more depreciation.
