## Why the three that passed do not count

ROE is the first trap. Over ten years this company spent most of its free cash flow on buybacks, compressing shareholders' equity to just a few tens of billions of dollars. A small denominator mechanically produces a high ROE. **But that logic reversed in 2026: it stopped buying back stock and began issuing shares.** This quarter's USD 20 billion ATM offering equals 4.5% of market cap, inflating the equity denominator; next year ROE will fall on its own. Judging Oracle today by ROE is driving with the rearview mirror.

Gross and net margins carry the same lag. Depreciation shadows capex: today's USD 90 billion of capex will feed through the income statement gradually over the next five to six years. **Today's 65% gross margin reflects the cheap assets of three years ago; the gross margin three years out depends on how fast today's expensive assets depreciate.** The metric clears the bar, but the direction has reversed.

## However, the four conditions are hard constraints

Free cash flow is the first and hardest test. A single-quarter figure of -RMB 5.396 billion, with a wider full-year guidance gap, means the company must keep raising external capital. Interest coverage of 4.6x looks acceptable, but it is calculated on "EBIT of RMB 30 billion," and that EBIT already deducts current-period depreciation of roughly RMB 7.6 billion; if depreciation is annualized at RMB 12 billion on the new assets, coverage falls below 4x.

Net debt/EBITDA of 4.3x, paired with a BBB- rating, is the most critical of the four.**The rating determines bond issuance rates, rates determine project returns, and project returns determine whether orders convert into profit.** In this chain, Oracle controls only the middle link. Equity dilution of 4.5% is an outcome, not a cause: cash flow falls short, so shares are sold; shares are sold, so per-share value is diluted. The four failed metrics are in fact four sides of the same problem.

## Whether it is fatal, however, depends on two scenarios

**If AI compute demand does not turn into a glut before 2028, these four points are merely "temporary ugliness."** Once the data centers are built, capex falls away; depreciation is high but revenue is more durable, free cash flow turns positive in some quarter, and leverage comes down on its own. Microsoft went through a similar phase in 2015–2018, and the market likewise accused it of "becoming asset-heavy."

**If demand turns into a glut in 2027, these four points are simply fatal.** A glut means rents fall, RPO conversion slows, customers demand price renegotiations — and debt is rigid: the RMB 129.5 billion will not be repaid a cent less because rents fell. At that point the equity is a leveraged call on a put option.**Negative screening does not judge which scenario is more likely; it only tells you this is a company whose outcome depends entirely on external conditions, and not worth betting on with a heavy position.**
