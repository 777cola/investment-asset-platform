Eliminating the weak: conclusion

Three of seven hard metrics pass, which does not look too bad, but all three that pass are illusions. ROE is high only because a decade of buybacks has compressed shareholder equity to the low tens of USD billions; gross and net margins are high only because depreciation has not yet caught up with capex. What really determines the quality of this company is the four that fail: free cash flow of -RMB 5.396 billion, EBIT/interest of about 4.6x, net debt/EBITDA of 4.3x, and single-quarter share dilution of 4.5%. That is the point of negative screening: with these four failing, there is no need to look at valuation.

The thresholds map to AI Berkshire's seven hard metrics. ROE, gross margin, EBIT and annual interest are rough estimates: ROE derives from TTM net profit of about RMB 18.7 billion ÷ equity of about RMB 20 billion; gross margin is weighted by segment mix; annual interest is estimated as RMB 129.5 billion of debt × 4.7%. The company does not disclose these three items separately in its quarterly report, hence the rough-estimate label.
Why the three that passed do not count

ROE is the first trap. Over the past decade the company has funnelled most of its free cash flow into buybacks, compressing shareholders' equity to just a few tens of billions of dollars — a small denominator that mechanically inflates ROE. That logic reversed in 2026, when it stopped repurchasing stock and began issuing shares. This quarter's USD 20 billion ATM offering equals 4.5% of market cap and inflates the equity denominator; ROE will fall of its own accord next year. Judging Oracle on ROE today is driving with the rearview mirror.

Gross and net margins carry the same lag. Depreciation shadows capex: today's USD 90 billion of capex will feed through the income statement gradually over the next five to six years. Today's 65% gross margin reflects the cheap assets of three years ago; the gross margin three years out depends on how fast today's expensive assets depreciate. The metric clears the bar, but the direction has reversed.

However, the four conditions are hard constraints

Free cash flow is the first and hardest test. A single-quarter figure of -RMB 5.396 billion, against a wider full-year guidance gap, means the company must keep raising external capital. Interest coverage of 4.6x looks acceptable, but it is calculated on "EBIT of RMB 30 billion" that already deducts current-period depreciation of roughly RMB 7.6 billion; annualise depreciation at RMB 12 billion on the new assets and coverage falls below 4x.

Net debt/EBITDA of 4.3x, paired with a BBB- rating, is the most critical of the four.The rating determines bond issuance rates, rates determine project returns, and project returns determine whether orders convert into profit. Oracle controls only the middle link of that chain. Equity dilution of 4.5% is an outcome, not a cause: cash flow falls short, so shares are sold; shares are sold, so per-share value is diluted. The four failed metrics are in fact four sides of the same problem.

Whether it is fatal, however, depends on two scenarios

If AI compute demand does not turn into a glut before 2028, these four points are merely "temporary ugliness." Once the data centres are built, capex falls away; depreciation is high but revenue is more durable, free cash flow turns positive in some quarter and leverage comes down on its own. Microsoft went through a similar phase in 2015–2018, when the market likewise accused it of "becoming asset-heavy."

If demand turns into a glut in 2027, these four points are simply fatal. A glut means rents fall, RPO conversion slows and customers demand price renegotiations — and debt is rigid: the RMB 129.5 billion will not be repaid a cent less because rents fell. At that point the equity is a leveraged call on a put option.Negative screening does not judge which scenario is more likely; it only tells you this is a company whose outcome depends entirely on external conditions, and not worth betting on with a heavy position.

Conclusion

Conclusion: fails the quality screen. Three of the seven criteria pass, but all three rest on distorted denominators and cannot be relied on. The four failures point to the same problem: growth funded by external financing. A company like this should be screened out at the filter stage, with no need to move on to valuation. The only reason to keep it on the watchlist: if FY28 free cash flow turns positive and debt/EBITDA falls below 3x, it becomes a company that can be valued again. Until then, with four of the seven criteria still unaddressed, any P/E is an illusion.
