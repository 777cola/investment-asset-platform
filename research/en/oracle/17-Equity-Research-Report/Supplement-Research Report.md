## I. Company Overview

Oracle (ORCL, NYSE) was founded in 1977 by Larry Ellison and two others and is the world's largest enterprise database vendor. FY2026 revenue is roughly USD 70 billion (approximate), split across four segments: cloud infrastructure OCI, cloud applications SaaS (Fusion ERP/HCM, NetSuite), software licenses and support, and hardware and services. It employs about 160,000 people worldwide and has a market cap of about USD 440 billion.

A management transition completed in September 2025: Clay Magouyrk and Mike Sicilia became co-CEOs, Safra Catz moved to vice chairman, and Ellison retained the roles of executive chairman and CTO. The company has historically expanded through acquisitions (PeopleSoft, Sun, NetSuite, Cerner); in 2026 it is shifting to self-built expansion centered on AI compute.

## II. Industry analysis

The global cloud computing market will be worth about USD 800 billion in 2026, growing around 20%; AI compute leasing is the fastest-growing sub-sector, with growth above 100%. The four major cloud providers will spend a combined RMB 700 billion (USD 700 billion) in capex in 2026, and the industry is entering an asset-heavy phase.

On competitive landscape, AWS, Azure and Google Cloud together hold about 65% of the IaaS market; Oracle ranks in the fourth tier with OCI but leads in growth. The industry's key variable has shifted from "whose software is better" to "who can secure power, secure chips and energize on schedule." The essence of this round of competition is capital and engineering, not product.

## III. Business model

The company runs two models side by side: legacy rent collection (database and support, 90% gross margin, zero capex) and incremental contract work (OCI, 30% gross margin, USD 1.5 of assets per USD 1 of revenue). In fiscal 2026, contract work exceeded 60% of revenue for the first time, effectively turning the company into "a compute infrastructure operator with a software business."

On revenue recognition, OCI is recognized by usage/contract term, and RPO is the contracted backlog for future revenue. Customers are mainly AI labs (OpenAI and others) and traditional enterprises; the former grow fast but are highly concentrated, the latter are stable but slow-growing.

## IV. Financial analysis

FY27Q1: revenue RMB 19.345 billion (+30%), GAAP net income RMB 4.760 billion (+62.6%), EPS USD 1.56. Gross margin about 65%, operating margin about 42%, strict cost control (three expense items at 23.2% of revenue).

Cash flow is the weak spot: operating cash flow about RMB 23.1 billion, capex RMB 28.499 billion, free cash flow negative RMB 5.396 billion. Debt about RMB 129.5 billion, debt/EBITDA 4.3x, S&P BBB-. Completed a USD 20 billion follow-on offering this quarter. ROIC roughly 11.8%, cost of capital about 7%, spread 4.8 points.

## V. Management

Execution ranks first in the industry (131,000 GPUs delivered in a single quarter at Abilene, accepted within 24 hours), while capital allocation leans aggressive (bond issuance + share issuance + a bet on a single customer). Ellison holds about 40% and, at 82, still drives strategy — the biggest asset and the biggest single point of risk. On governance, the co-CEO structure has settled day-to-day management but not strategic succession.

## Six, growth drivers

Three drivers: 1) AI compute demand — RPO of RMB 664 billion, about RMB 76.6 billion converting within 12 months; 2) database migration to cloud — database-related business +353% this quarter, AWS/Azure connect to Oracle Database; 3) industry cloud — healthcare (Cerner), financial and government customers migrating to Fusion suite.

Of the three drivers, the first contributed almost all the incremental growth, and almost all the capex. The quality of growth depends on whether the first can persist, and the first is not within the company's control.

## VII. Competitive landscape

At the IaaS layer, Microsoft is the most dangerous rival: OpenAI's largest shareholder, capable of cross-subsidization, and a head-on collision at the enterprise software layer. AWS and Google Cloud are stronger on cost and in-house chips. CoreWeave is a peer competitor and poses no structural threat. At the database layer, the real competitor is "customer self-build plus open-source substitution," and this quarter's -3% software support revenue is the evidence.

## VIII. Risk factors

① Customer concentration: more than half of RPO likely comes from OpenAI; counterparty credit is the core risk; ② Financing cost: BBB- rating, a 100bp rise in rates affects annual interest by RMB 1.3 billion; ③ Technology iteration: GPU depreciation assumptions may be too optimistic; ④ Overcapacity: capacity concentrated coming online in 2027H2–2028H1; ⑤ Regulation: export controls and antitrust; ⑥ Equity dilution: 4.5% diluted this quarter, likely to continue.

## IX. Earnings forecast

FY27E revenue of about USD 90 billion (+29%), non-GAAP EPS of USD 8.10 (company guidance), capex of USD 90–95 billion, free cash flow of about -USD 15 billion. FY28E revenue of about USD 110 billion, EPS of USD 9.50, capex of about USD 95 billion, free cash flow of about -USD 3 billion (close to turning positive). The biggest uncertainties in our forecasts lie on the revenue side (RPO conversion) and the depreciation side (useful-life assumptions), not on the expense side.

## X. Valuation

Cross-checking three methods: PE method — FY28 EPS USD 9.5 × 17x = USD 162; EV/EBITDA method — FY27 EBITDA of about RMB 40 billion × 12x − net debt of RMB 100 billion = market cap of RMB 380 billion, corresponding to USD 125; sum-of-the-parts method — software business RMB 300–350 billion + OCI RMB 150 billion − net debt of RMB 100 billion = RMB 350–400 billion, corresponding to USD 115–132. Taking the median of the three methods, the USD 162 target price is on the optimistic side, while USD 125 is closer to the conservative value.

## XI. Investment recommendation

Rating: Avoid. Target price USD 162 (base case), conservative valuation USD 125.Rationale: demand is priced in, risk is not priced in, cash flow is negative, and leverage sits near the lower bound for investment grade. Watch two triggers: free cash flow turning positive in a single quarter, or the share price falling below USD 100.Alternatives: Microsoft (positive cash flow + full-stack AI), Nvidia (seller pricing power).

## II (continued). Industry: Timing of Concentrated Supply Startups

Lay out the 2026 industry data: capex at North America's four major cloud providers totals around USD 700 billion, up 45% year on year; global data center new installed capacity is about 12GW, three times that of 2023. Given an 18–24 month construction cycle, the concentrated commissioning window falls between the second half of 2027 and the first half of 2028.

Demand-side figures are equally strong: global AI training compute demand grows more than 80% year on year in 2026, with inference demand growing faster still (as applications begin to land). But demand growth is slowing — 150% in 2024, 110% in 2025, 80% in 2026. Supply growth is rising while demand growth is falling; the odds are not low that the two lines cross in 2027. This is the core basis for our cautious view on the industry cycle.

## IV (continued). Financials: debt structure and maturity profile

Of the USD 129.5 billion in debt, roughly three buckets: about USD 90 billion in long-term notes (8-year weighted average maturity, 4.5%–5% average coupon), about USD 15 billion in operating lease liabilities, and about USD 24.5 billion in construction project financing and other (rough estimate, based on publicly disclosed line items). Near-term maturity pressure is modest — about USD 10 billion matures over the next 12 months against roughly USD 29.5 billion of cash on hand, a coverage ratio of 3x.

The real issue is not "can't repay" but "refinancing cost." New bonds issued in 2026 carry coupons of about 5.5%–6%, versus just 2.5%–3.5% on bonds issued in 2020–2021. Rolling maturing debt into new bonds at a 250 basis point spread adds USD 250 million of annual interest per USD 10 billion of principal. About USD 35 billion of old debt matures over the next three years; refinancing it all adds USD 800–900 million of annual interest — equivalent to 18% of this quarter's net profit.

## Seven (continued). Competitive landscape: peer comparison

Put the five in one table and the gaps are obvious: Microsoft at 33x PE with strongly positive free cash flow and a AAA rating; Amazon at 35x PE with AWS growing 20% but a diversified customer base; Google at 26x PE with in-house TPU development; Oracle at 23.5x PE, the fastest grower but with negative cash flow; CoreWeave with no PE given and the highest leverage. Oracle is the only one of the five combining "first in growth, last in cash flow."

That combination gets chased in a bull market (for the growth) and abandoned in a choppy market (for the cash flow). September 2026 is the latter — the AI trade shifting from narrative to cash flow, which is exactly why it fell from 248.15 to 144.79.

## Ten (continued). Valuation: DCF and sensitivity

Cross-check with a simple two-stage DCF: assuming FY28–FY32 free cash flow improves from -RMB 3 billion to +RMB 25 billion (capex falling to RMB 60 billion and operating cash flow rising to RMB 85 billion each year), a 3% perpetuity growth rate and 8.5% WACC, enterprise value discounted to today is about USD 500 billion; less net debt of RMB 100 billion, equity value is RMB 400 billion, or about USD 132 per share. That is 19% below the USD 162 derived from the PE method.

Sensitivity: each 100 bp rise in WACC cuts valuation 12%; each 1 pp fall in perpetuity growth cuts valuation 15%; each RMB 5 billion fall in FY32 free cash flow cuts valuation 8%. Of the three variables, WACC is the least controllable—it is set by ratings and market rates, not by the company. That is why we set a USD 162 target price but stress a "conservative value of USD 125."

## Eight (continued). Risks: three sensitivity tests

Test 1: Depreciation life shortened from six years to five. FY28 depreciation rises about 15%, net income falls about 15%, EPS drops from 9.5 to 8.1, and at 17x the target price falls to USD 138 (-5%).

Test 2: OCI growth slows from 60% to 30%. FY28 revenue falls about RMB 12 billion, EBITDA falls about RMB 4 billion, EV/EBITDA rises from 13.5x to 15x (as the denominator shrinks), valuation falls about 20%, and the target price is USD 130.

Test 3: Rating cut to BB+. Financing costs rise 250 basis points, annual interest rises RMB 3.2 billion, FY28 EPS falls below USD 7, and with the valuation multiple compressed to 12x, the target price is about USD 84 (-42%). Of the three tests, the third is the most lethal, and it is precisely the one least within the company's control.

## I (continued). Shareholding structure and shareholder returns

Simple share structure: a single class of common stock, no dual-class shares, about 3.04 billion shares outstanding (including this quarter's issuance). Larry Ellison holds about 40%, an absolute controlling shareholder; the top twenty institutional shareholders hold about 30% combined, mostly passive index funds (Vanguard, BlackRock). The implication of this structure: retail and institutions together cannot change any decision — the company is effectively a public company controlled by one person.

On shareholder returns: a quarterly dividend of USD 0.50, USD 2.00 annualized, a yield of about 1.4%, paid with debt while free cash flow is negative; buybacks have essentially stalled since fiscal 2025 and turn into net issuance in fiscal 2026. The past decade's history of shrinking the share count through buybacks has formally ended in this cycle.

## III (continued). Customer structure and contract terms

Customers fall into two groups. The first is AI labs (OpenAI, xAI, Meta and others): contracts run 4–6 years, carry large amounts, include capacity and acceptance clauses, and involve prepayment; these customers contribute most of the incremental RPO and account for the demand behind almost all new capital expenditure. The second is traditional enterprises (banks, telecoms, government, healthcare): annual subscriptions, highly sticky, single-digit growth, the ballast of cash flow.

Contract terms are the key variable. AI customer contracts typically include clauses allowing termination for late delivery and price adjustments if compute utilization falls short, which means RPO carries weaker legal enforceability than traditional IT contracts. This is the direct reason we apply a 30% haircut to RPO in our valuation.

## VI (continued). Quantifying the three growth paths

Path one: OCI grows from RMB 7.388 billion/quarter to 15 billion/quarter by FY29, or 60 billion in annualized revenue; at a 30% gross margin that contributes 18 billion in gross profit, covering 20% of 90 billion in capex — OCI's gross profit alone cannot cover its capex; this is the structural gap in the business.

Path two: database migration to the cloud. This quarter's +353% growth comes from a cloud database off a tiny base; even sustaining 100% growth for three years, it reaches only 6 billion/quarter by FY29, a limited contribution. Path three: industry cloud (healthcare, financial, government), growing around 10%, a stable item rather than a growth item. Together the three paths put FY29 revenue at about RMB 130 billion, and whether free cash flow turns positive still depends on capex holding within 70 billion.

## Nine (continued). Segment profit forecast

FY28E segment forecast (rough): OCI revenue about RMB 42.0 billion (+55%), gross margin 32%; SaaS about RMB 19.0 billion (+10%), gross margin 70%; software licenses and support about RMB 21.5 billion (-2%), gross margin 90%; hardware and services about RMB 9.0 billion (+8%), gross margin 25%. Total about RMB 91.5 billion, weighted gross margin about 63%, down another 2 percentage points from FY27.

Cost side: sales plus R&D together account for about 22% of revenue, with absolute amounts growing 15% alongside revenue; depreciation rises from RMB 7.6 billion to RMB 13.0 billion, the biggest drag on profit. Conclusion: FY28 gross margin continues to decline, depreciation rises, operating margin falls back from 42% to around 40%, and net profit growth relies mainly on revenue scale rather than margin improvement.
