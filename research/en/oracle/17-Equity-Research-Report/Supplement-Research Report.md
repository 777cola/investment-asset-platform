## I. Company Overview

Founded in 1977 by Larry Ellison and two co-founders, Oracle (ORCL, NYSE) is the world's largest enterprise database vendor. FY2026 revenue is roughly USD 70 billion (approximate), spanning four segments: cloud infrastructure (OCI), cloud applications SaaS (Fusion ERP/HCM, NetSuite), software licences and support, and hardware and services. The company employs about 160,000 people worldwide and has a market cap of about USD 440 billion.

A management transition completed in September 2025: Clay Magouyrk and Mike Sicilia became co-CEOs, Safra Catz moved to vice chairman, and Ellison retained the roles of executive chairman and CTO. Oracle has historically expanded through acquisitions (PeopleSoft, Sun, NetSuite, Cerner); in 2026 it is shifting to self-built expansion centred on AI compute.

## II. Industry analysis

The global cloud computing market will be worth about USD 800 billion in 2026, growing around 20%, with AI compute leasing the fastest-growing sub-sector at above 100% growth. The four major cloud providers will spend a combined RMB 700 billion (USD 700 billion) in capex in 2026, and the industry is entering an asset-heavy phase.

On competitive landscape, AWS, Azure and Google Cloud together hold about 65% of the IaaS market; Oracle ranks in the fourth tier with OCI but leads in growth. The industry's key variable has shifted from "whose software is better" to "who can secure power, secure chips and energize on schedule." The essence of this round of competition is capital and engineering, not product.

## III. Business model

The company runs two models side by side: legacy rent collection (database and support, 90% gross margin, zero capex) and incremental contract work (OCI, 30% gross margin, USD 1.5 of assets per USD 1 of revenue). In fiscal 2026, contract work exceeded 60% of revenue for the first time, effectively turning the company into "a compute infrastructure operator with a software business."

OCI revenue is recognised by usage or contract term, and RPO represents contracted backlog for future revenue. Customers are mainly AI labs (OpenAI and others) and traditional enterprises; the former grow fast but are highly concentrated, the latter stable but slow-growing.

## IV. Financial analysis

FY27Q1 revenue RMB 19.345 billion (+30%), GAAP net income RMB 4.760 billion (+62.6%), EPS USD 1.56. Gross margin about 65% and operating margin about 42%, with strict cost control (three expense items at 23.2% of revenue).

Cash flow is the weak spot: operating cash flow about RMB 23.1 billion, capex RMB 28.499 billion, free cash flow negative RMB 5.396 billion. Debt about RMB 129.5 billion, debt/EBITDA 4.3x, S&P BBB-. The company completed a USD 20 billion follow-on offering this quarter. ROIC roughly 11.8% against a cost of capital of about 7%, a spread of 4.8 points.

## V. Management

Execution ranks first in the industry — 131,000 GPUs delivered in a single quarter at Abilene and accepted within 24 hours — while capital allocation leans aggressive: bond issuance, share issuance and a concentrated bet on a single customer. Ellison holds about 40% and, at 82, still drives strategy, making him both the biggest asset and the biggest single point of risk. On governance, the co-CEO structure has settled day-to-day management but not strategic succession.

## Six, growth drivers

Three drivers: 1) AI compute demand — RPO of RMB 664 billion, of which about RMB 76.6 billion converts within 12 months; 2) database migration to cloud — database-related business +353% this quarter, with AWS/Azure connecting to Oracle Database; 3) industry cloud — healthcare (Cerner), financial and government customers migrating to the Fusion suite.

Of the three, the first accounted for almost all incremental growth and almost all capex. Growth quality therefore hinges on whether the first persists — and the first is not within the company's control.

## VII. Competitive landscape

At the IaaS layer, Microsoft is the most dangerous rival: OpenAI's largest shareholder, able to cross-subsidise, and on a collision course with OpenAI at the enterprise software layer. AWS and Google Cloud are stronger on cost and in-house chips. CoreWeave is a peer competitor and poses no structural threat. At the database layer, the real competitor is customer self-build plus open-source substitution, and the -3% software support revenue this quarter is the evidence.

## VIII. Risk factors

① Customer concentration: more than half of RPO likely comes from OpenAI, making counterparty credit the core risk; ② Financing cost: at a BBB- rating, a 100bp rise in rates affects annual interest by RMB 1.3 billion; ③ Technology iteration: GPU depreciation assumptions may be too optimistic; ④ Overcapacity: capacity concentrated coming online in 2027H2–2028H1; ⑤ Regulation: export controls and antitrust; ⑥ Equity dilution: 4.5% diluted this quarter, likely to continue.

## IX. Earnings forecast

We forecast FY27E revenue of about USD 90 billion (+29%), non-GAAP EPS of USD 8.10 (company guidance), capex of USD 90–95 billion and free cash flow of about -USD 15 billion. For FY28E we model revenue of about USD 110 billion, EPS of USD 9.50, capex of about USD 95 billion and free cash flow of about -USD 3 billion, close to turning positive. The biggest uncertainties in our forecasts sit on the revenue side (RPO conversion) and the depreciation side (useful-life assumptions), not on the expense side.

## X. Valuation

Cross-checking three methods: P/E — FY28 EPS of USD 9.5 × 17x = USD 162; EV/EBITDA — FY27 EBITDA of about RMB 40 billion × 12x less net debt of RMB 100 billion = market cap of RMB 380 billion, equivalent to USD 125; sum-of-the-parts — software business RMB 300–350 billion + OCI RMB 150 billion − net debt of RMB 100 billion = RMB 350–400 billion, equivalent to USD 115–132. Taking the median of the three, the USD 162 target price looks optimistic, while USD 125 is closer to the conservative value.

## XI. Investment recommendation

Rating: Avoid. Target price USD 162 (base case); conservative valuation USD 125.Rationale: demand is priced in, risk is not, free cash flow is negative and leverage sits close to the lower bound for investment grade. Two triggers to watch: free cash flow turning positive in any single quarter, or the share price falling below USD 100.Alternatives: Microsoft (positive free cash flow plus full-stack AI), Nvidia (seller pricing power).

## II (continued). Industry: Timing of Concentrated Supply Startups

2026 industry data: capex at North America's four major cloud providers totals around USD 700 billion, up 45% YoY; global data centre new installed capacity is about 12GW, three times that of 2023. Given an 18–24 month construction cycle, the concentrated commissioning window falls between the second half of 2027 and the first half of 2028.

Demand-side figures are equally strong: global AI training compute demand grows more than 80% YoY in 2026, with inference demand growing faster still as applications begin to land. But demand growth is slowing — 150% in 2024, 110% in 2025, 80% in 2026. Supply growth is rising while demand growth is falling; the odds are not low that the two lines cross in 2027. This is the core basis for our cautious view on the industry cycle.

## IV (continued). Financials: debt structure and maturity profile

The USD 129.5 billion of debt breaks into roughly three buckets: about USD 90 billion of long-term notes (8-year weighted average maturity, 4.5%–5% average coupon), about USD 15 billion of operating lease liabilities, and about USD 24.5 billion of construction project financing and other (a rough estimate based on publicly disclosed line items). Near-term maturity pressure is modest — about USD 10 billion falls due over the next 12 months against roughly USD 29.5 billion of cash on hand, a coverage ratio of 3x.

The real issue is not whether the debt can be repaid but what it costs to refinance. New bonds issued in 2026 carry coupons of about 5.5%–6%, versus just 2.5%–3.5% on bonds issued in 2020–2021. Rolling maturing debt into new bonds at a 250bp spread adds USD 250 million of annual interest per USD 10 billion of principal. About USD 35 billion of old debt matures over the next three years; refinancing it in full adds USD 800–900 million of annual interest — equivalent to 18% of this quarter's net profit.

## Seven (continued). Competitive landscape: peer comparison

Line the five up in one table and the gaps speak for themselves: Microsoft on 33x P/E with strongly positive free cash flow and a AAA rating; Amazon on 35x P/E, AWS growing 20% but a diversified customer base; Google on 26x P/E with in-house TPU development; Oracle on 23.5x P/E, the fastest grower but with negative cash flow; CoreWeave with no P/E given and the highest leverage. Oracle is the only one of the five that is first in growth and last in cash flow.

That combination gets chased in a bull market for the growth and abandoned in a choppy one for the cash flow. September 2026 is the latter — the AI trade shifting from narrative to cash flow, which is exactly why the stock fell from 248.15 to 144.79.

## Ten (continued). Valuation: DCF and sensitivity

A simple two-stage DCF cross-check: assume FY28–FY32 free cash flow improves from -RMB 3 billion to +RMB 25 billion (capex falling to RMB 60 billion, operating cash flow rising to RMB 85 billion a year), a 3% perpetuity growth rate and 8.5% WACC; enterprise value discounted to today is about USD 500 billion. Less net debt of RMB 100 billion, equity value is RMB 400 billion, or about USD 132 per share. That is 19% below the USD 162 derived from the P/E method.

Sensitivity: each 100 bp rise in WACC cuts valuation 12%; each 1 pp fall in perpetuity growth cuts valuation 15%; each RMB 5 billion fall in FY32 free cash flow cuts valuation 8%. Of the three variables, WACC is the least controllable—it is set by ratings and market rates, not by the company. Hence our USD 162 target price alongside a stressed "conservative value of USD 125."

## Eight (continued). Risks: three sensitivity tests

Test 1: Depreciation life shortened from six years to five. FY28 depreciation rises c.15% and net income falls c.15%, taking EPS from 9.5 to 8.1; at 17x, the target price drops to USD 138 (-5%).

Test 2: OCI growth slows from 60% to 30%. FY28 revenue falls c.RMB 12 billion and EBITDA c.RMB 4 billion, pushing EV/EBITDA from 13.5x to 15x as the denominator shrinks; valuation falls c.20%, implying a target price of USD 130.

Test 3: Rating cut to BB+. Financing costs rise 250bp, adding RMB 3.2 billion of annual interest and taking FY28 EPS below USD 7; with the multiple compressed to 12x, the target price is c.USD 84 (-42%). Of the three tests, the third is the most lethal, and it is precisely the one least within the company's control.

## I (continued). Shareholding structure and shareholder returns

Simple share structure: a single class of common stock, no dual-class shares, about 3.04 billion shares outstanding including this quarter's issuance. Larry Ellison holds about 40%, giving him absolute control; the top twenty institutional shareholders hold about 30% combined, mostly passive index funds (Vanguard, BlackRock). The implication: retail and institutions together cannot change any decision — the company is effectively a public company controlled by one person.

On shareholder returns: a quarterly dividend of USD 0.50, USD 2.00 annualized, a yield of about 1.4%, funded with debt while free cash flow is negative; buybacks have essentially stalled since fiscal 2025 and turn into net issuance in fiscal 2026. The past decade's buyback-driven share count reduction has formally ended this cycle.

## III (continued). Customer structure and contract terms

Customers split into two groups. The first is AI labs (OpenAI, xAI, Meta and others): contracts run 4–6 years, carry large ticket sizes, include capacity and acceptance clauses, and involve prepayment; these customers drive most of the incremental RPO and underpin the demand behind almost all new capex. The second is traditional enterprises (banks, telecoms, government, healthcare): annual subscriptions, highly sticky, single-digit growth, the ballast of cash flow.

Contract terms are the key variable. AI customer contracts typically allow termination for late delivery and price adjustments if compute utilization falls short, so RPO carries weaker legal enforceability than traditional IT contracts. This is why we apply a 30% haircut to RPO in our valuation.

## VI (continued). Quantifying the three growth paths

Path one: OCI grows from RMB 7.388 billion/quarter to 15 billion/quarter by FY29 — 60 billion annualised — and at a 30% gross margin contributes 18 billion in gross profit, covering 20% of 90 billion in capex. OCI's gross profit alone cannot cover its capex; this is the structural gap in the business.

Path two: database migration to the cloud. This quarter's +353% growth comes off a tiny base in cloud database; even sustaining 100% growth for three years, it reaches only 6 billion/quarter by FY29 — a limited contribution. Path three: industry cloud (healthcare, financial, government), growing around 10% — a stable item, not a growth item. Together the three paths put FY29 revenue at about RMB 130 billion, and whether free cash flow turns positive still hinges on capex holding within 70 billion.

## Nine (continued). Segment profit forecast

FY28E segment forecast (rough): OCI revenue c.RMB 42.0bn (+55%), gross margin 32%; SaaS c.RMB 19.0bn (+10%), gross margin 70%; software licences and support c.RMB 21.5bn (-2%), gross margin 90%; hardware and services c.RMB 9.0bn (+8%), gross margin 25%. Total c.RMB 91.5bn, weighted gross margin c.63%, down a further 2pp from FY27.

Cost side: sales plus R&D account for c.22% of revenue, with absolute spend growing 15% in line with revenue; depreciation rises from RMB 7.6bn to RMB 13.0bn and is the biggest drag on profit. Conclusion: FY28 gross margin continues to decline, depreciation rises, operating margin falls back from 42% to around 40%, and net profit growth relies mainly on revenue scale rather than margin expansion.
