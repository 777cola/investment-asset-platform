## Two claims already falsified — what does that tell us

The first assumption to fall was that "the rating can hold." S&P's downgrade to BBB- in July 2026 was no surprise but the inevitable consequence of the capex cadence: RMB 90 billion of annual investment cannot be sustained by RMB 32 billion of operating cash flow, so leverage had to rise—and at 4.3x, a downgrade followed naturally. Falsifying this assumption also negates the implicit premise of "stable financing costs."

The second assumption to fall was that "no equity financing is needed." A company that bought back hundreds of billions over the past decade conducted its first large-scale secondary offering in twenty years in August 2026. Management has acknowledged the cash flow gap through action, which is more credible than any guidance. Together, the two falsifications show the company has entered a state in which it "must continuously raise money externally," and that state reinforces itself: the more cash it lacks, the more it must finance; the more it finances, the worse the rating; the worse the rating, the more expensive the financing.

## Three claims to verify — which warrants the closest watch?

RPO conversion is the first and most observable test: management has committed to converting RMB 76.6 billion within 12 months, and incremental cloud revenue in Q2 and Q3 will show directly whether it delivers. If both quarters fall short by more than 10%, the RMB 664 billion must be discounted again.

Capex peaking is the second and most critical. FY27 guidance is RMB 90–95 billion; if FY28 guidance no longer rises, expansion has plateaued; if it keeps climbing, the gap is still widening. OCI gross margin is the third, but it ranks last—the company does not disclose segment gross margin, leaving only management commentary, the least verifiable.

## Falsification conditions: when to abandon the thesis entirely

Three red lines, any one of which removes the stock from the watchlist: ① a rating cut to BB+ or below (funding costs jump and project returns turn negative); ② free cash flow below -RMB 8 billion for two consecutive quarters (the gap widens and dilution accelerates); ③ disclosure that a single major customer contract has been terminated or sharply reduced (the order book's foundation collapses).

Conversely, the thesis holds only if all three turn green: single-quarter free cash flow turns positive, the rating outlook returns to stable or above, and the 12-month conversion rate of RPO meets the commitment. None of the three is in place today.

## Watch calendar for next steps

Mid-December 2026: FY27Q2 results — watch whether RPO keeps growing, whether capex stays on the RMB 90–95 billion track, and whether the company raises capital again. January 2027: annual rating agency reviews — whether Moody's and Fitch follow S&P. March 2027: FY27Q3 results — the 12-month conversion window is halfway through; incremental cloud revenue is the hard metric. June 2027: FY28 capex guidance — the only official signal for judging whether the expansion has peaked.

Four points in time, each capable of changing the thesis. But before the first one arrives, there is nothing to do — and that, too, is part of discipline.
