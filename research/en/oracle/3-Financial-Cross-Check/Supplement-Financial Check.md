## Why this step is essential

One feature defines Oracle's numbers this quarter: the same earnings report can support two completely different conclusions. On non-GAAP net income of USD 5.02 billion and capex excluding finance leases, free cash flow is -USD 4.85 billion; on GAAP net income of USD 4.760 billion and capex including leases, it is -USD 5.396 billion. The first reads as "minor bleeding," the second as "hemorrhaging." A USD 540 million gap, 2.8% of quarterly revenue, is enough to make both a bullish and a bearish report look reasonable.

Cross-validation is not about finding errors; it is about confirming which basis you stand on and staying there. Every judgment on leverage, cash flow and valuation later in this report rests on the USD 129.5 billion and -USD 5.396 billion figures that include leases; switching basis would require recalculating all conclusions.

## Three deviations above 1%, explained one by one

First discrepancy: total debt RMB 129.5 billion vs RMB 118 billion (9.7% gap). The RMB 11.5 billion difference is mainly operating lease liabilities and project financing added this quarter. Media habitually count only long-term notes payable because that line sits in the most prominent position on the balance sheet; but for a company leasing land, power and data centers at scale, lease liabilities are as rigid as notes — miss the rent and the data center goes dark. This report therefore uses RMB 129.5 billion and sets debt/EBITDA at 4.3x.

Second discrepancy: free cash flow -RMB 5.396 billion vs -RMB 4.85 billion (10% gap). Company-defined capex includes equipment acquired via finance leases (GPUs can also be obtained through leases); stripping this out improves quarterly cash flow by RMB 540 million. But leased equipment still has to be paid for, just in later years. Moving leases out of capex shifts the spend from "now" to "later" without changing the total.

Third discrepancy: net profit RMB 4.760 billion vs RMB 5.02 billion (5.5% gap). Non-GAAP adds back share-based compensation (roughly RMB 340 million this quarter) and restructuring costs. For a company aggressively expanding data center operations and sales headcount, share-based compensation is a real cost, merely non-cash in accounting terms. This report relies primarily on GAAP and uses non-GAAP only when discussing management guidance (FY27 EPS USD 8.10), with explicit labeling.

## The final adopted basis versus the figures not used

This report applies consistent definitions throughout: revenue and profit on a GAAP basis; capex including finance leases; debt including operating leases; free cash flow = operating cash flow − capex; valuation showing both GAAP P/E (TTM) and non-GAAP FY27 P/E, with EV/EBITDA given priority. Market cap is based on 3.04 billion diluted shares (post-offering), consistent with the USD 440 billion market cap.

One number this report deliberately avoids: the widely circulated "OpenAI contract totalling USD 300 billion over five years." That figure appears in none of Oracle's announcements or filings; it recurs only in media and sell-side reports, with durations and definitions that vary by source. It may be close to fact, but it is not verifiable data. This report relies only on the company-disclosed RPO of USD 664 billion and on the explicitly uncertain statement that "market estimates attribute more than half to OpenAI." Building on unverifiable numbers is the most common way this kind of research goes wrong.
