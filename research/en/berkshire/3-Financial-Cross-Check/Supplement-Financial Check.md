## Deviation one: a 2.4% market-cap gap, purely a matter of pricing timing

stockanalysis shows USD 1.23 trillion against our USD 1.26 trillion. The USD 30 billion gap equates to USD 14 per share (about 2.4%), precisely the move driven by mid-November safe-haven buying.

The report-date share price of USD 592.85 is batchdata's closing data, so market cap must be recalculated at that price: 592.85 × 2.13 billion shares (B-share equivalent, with A shares converted at 1:1,500) = USD 1.263 trillion. The gap is not a data error; it is a difference in dates.

The same cause produces a 3% difference in both P/E and P/B. This deviation need not be explained away as a "definitional difference", but it must be flagged in the table, otherwise readers will assume a calculation error.

## Deviation 2: RMB 761.4 billion stated as RMB 76.14 billion, a 100-fold discrepancy

Quartr's summary puts shareholders' equity at USD 76.14 billion, but the balance sheet in the 10-Q shows USD 761.4 billion. The test is simple: market cap of USD 1.26 trillion ÷ 761.4 billion = 1.66x PB, exactly matching the 1.66x derived from share price/book value. Were equity really 76.1 billion, PB would be 16.6x — absurd.

Such errors are rife in Chinese financial media, which routinely conflate "USD billion" and "USD hundred million." The fix is not to split the difference but to go back to the original statements.

One related pitfall: Berkshire's "cash and short-term Treasuries of USD 377.4 billion" is a gross figure. Net of working capital at the railroad and energy businesses and of Treasuries purchased but not yet settled, it is USD 352.3 billion. The company discloses both, and conflating them moves "cash as a percentage of market cap" from 30% to 28%. This article uses the gross figure throughout and flags it wherever ratios are involved.

## Deviation 3: buyback RMB 14.4 billion vs RMB 14.17 billion — the shortfall is Q1

Q2 RMB 4.527 billion plus Q3 RMB 9.647 billion equals RMB 14.17 billion, yet the company reports RMB 14.4 billion for the first three quarters. The RMB 230 million gap is Q1 buybacks, rarely mentioned in market coverage because the stock traded near USD 540 in Q1, making the buyback too small to be newsworthy.

The gap itself does not matter; the pace does: roughly RMB 230 million in Q1, RMB 4.527 billion in Q2 and RMB 9.647 billion in Q3. Buybacks are accelerating, and the acceleration came as the stock hit new highs. That is the fact that needs explaining.

## Final adopted basis, plus two pitfalls that must not be conflated

Basis: Market cap USD 1.26 trillion (recalculated on the 11/16 close), P/E (TTM) 16.6x (TTM GAAP net profit of about USD 76 billion), P/B 1.66x (B-share equivalent book value USD 357.7), operating profit multiple 24.8x (annualised USD 50.82 billion). The four multiples are internally consistent: change one and you must change them all.

Pitfall one: GAAP net profit and operating profit cannot be mixed. GAAP net profit of USD 22.112 billion includes large unrealised investment gains and swings wildly by quarter (the -28.2% YoY is mainly this); operating profit of USD 12.705 billion is the business itself. P/E on GAAP gives 16.6x; P/E on operating profit gives 24.8x, and the latter is the answer to "is it expensive".

Pitfall two: book value per share has two versions. A shares USD 536,590, B shares USD 357.7, a ratio of 1,500:1. Every P/B must use the B-share price against B-share book value; mixing them produces absurd figures like 1,000x.
