## Deviation one: a 2.4% market-cap gap, purely a matter of pricing timing

stockanalysis shows USD 1.23 trillion; our figure is USD 1.26 trillion. The USD 30 billion gap equates to a USD 14 per-share difference (about 2.4%), exactly the move driven by the mid-November safe-haven buying.

The report-date share price of USD 592.85 is batchdata's closing data, so market cap must be recalculated at that price: 592.85 × 2.13 billion shares (B-share equivalent, with A shares converted at 1:1,500) = USD 1.263 trillion. **The gap is not a data error; it is a difference in dates.**

The same cause produces a 3% difference in both PE and PB. This kind of deviation does not need to be explained as a "definitional difference", but it must be flagged in the table, otherwise readers will assume someone made a calculation error.

## Deviation 2: RMB 761.4 billion written as RMB 76.14 billion, a 100-fold difference

Quartr's summary says "shareholders' equity USD 76.14 billion," but the balance sheet in the 10-Q shows USD 761.4 billion. The basis for judgment is simple: **market cap of USD 1.26 trillion ÷ 761.4 billion = 1.66x PB, which matches exactly the 1.66x calculated from share price/book value**; if equity really were 76.1 billion, PB would become 16.6x, which is absurd.

This kind of error is common in Chinese financial media—mixing up the units "USD billion" and "USD hundred million." The fix is not to "take the average" but to go back to the original statements.

A related pitfall worth mentioning: Berkshire's "cash and short-term Treasuries of USD 377.4 billion" is a **gross** figure; after deducting working capital at the railroad and energy businesses and Treasuries purchased but not yet settled, it is USD 352.3 billion. Both figures have been disclosed by the company, and mixing them would change "cash as a percentage of market cap" from 30% to 28%. This article uses the gross figure throughout and notes it wherever ratios are involved.

## Deviation 3: buyback RMB 14.4 billion vs RMB 14.17 billion — the shortfall is Q1

Q2 RMB 4.527 billion + Q3 RMB 9.647 billion = RMB 14.17 billion, but the company says RMB 14.4 billion for the first three quarters. The RMB 230 million gap is Q1 buybacks, rarely mentioned in market reports, because the stock traded near USD 540 in Q1, making the buyback small and not newsworthy.

The gap itself does not matter; what matters is the **pace**: about RMB 230 million in Q1, RMB 4.527 billion in Q2, RMB 9.647 billion in Q3. Buybacks are accelerating, and the acceleration came as the stock hit new highs. That is the fact that needs explaining.

## Final adopted basis and two pitfalls that must not be conflated

**Basis used:** Market cap USD 1.26 trillion (recalculated on the 11/16 close), PE(TTM) 16.6x (TTM GAAP net profit of about USD 76 billion), PB 1.66x (B-share equivalent book value USD 357.7), operating profit multiple 24.8x (annualized USD 50.82 billion). The four multiples are internally consistent; change one and you must change them all.

**Pitfall one: GAAP net profit and operating profit cannot be mixed.** GAAP net profit of USD 22.112 billion includes large unrealized investment gains and swings wildly by quarter (the -28.2% year-on-year is mainly this); operating profit of USD 12.705 billion is the business itself. PE on GAAP gives 16.6x; PE on operating profit gives 24.8x, **and the latter is the answer to "is it expensive"**.

**Pitfall two: book value per share has two versions.** A shares USD 536,590, B shares USD 357.7, a ratio of 1,500:1. Every P/B must use the B-share price against B-share book value; mixing them produces absurd figures like 1,000x.
