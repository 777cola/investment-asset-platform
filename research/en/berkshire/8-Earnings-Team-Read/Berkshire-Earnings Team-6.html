Berkshire Q3: A Steady but Unspectacular Scorecard, and USD 377.4 Billion of Silence
Research date 2026/11/16  ·  Price $592.85 (-0.02%)  ·  Market cap About USD 1.26 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 16.6x

Core view

Underwriting profit fell 43%, yet the stock trades at an all-time high. That contradiction is the entry point to understanding Berkshire in 2026.

Numbers: what fell fell, and what rose rose

Berkshire's November 7 filing was the first full quarterly report of the Abel era. Operating profit came in at USD 12.705 billion, -5.8% YoY, and GAAP net income at USD 22.112 billion, -28.2%. Insurance underwriting fell 43% to USD 1.352 billion, of which GEICO contributed USD 982 million, -45%.

The same statements, however, show BNSF Railway at USD 1.729 billion, +19.3%, Berkshire Hathaway Energy at USD 1.612 billion, +8.3%, and manufacturing, services and retail at USD 4.042 billion, +11.8%. Together the three physical businesses generated USD 7.383 billion, +12.6%, filling half the hole on the insurance side.

That leaves the headline at -5.8% — neither ugly nor pretty. A combined ratio of 91.4% means every dollar of premium still earns 8.6 cents; it simply lacks last year's outsized windfall.

Why the stock keeps hitting new highs: it has become a safe haven from an "AI collapse"

Global tech stocks swung violently in mid-November: CoreWeave fell more than 70% from its high, and single-day moves in tech names routinely hit double digits. Over the same stretch Berkshire rose 13.2% in a month, beating the S&P 500 by 19.2 percentage points—the largest rolling monthly relative gain since 2010 and the biggest in 30 years outside 2008.

The reason is simple: USD 377.4 billion in cash and short-term Treasuries, two-thirds of profit from railroads and power, and no heavy position in any AI theme. When the market starts to doubt "circular financing" and "compute glut," money needs an asset that tells no story. Berkshire is exactly that asset.

That is also the problem: once everyone spots a safe haven, it stops being one and becomes a crowded trade. At 1.66 times book and 24.8 times annualized operating earnings, that is the price of the crowding.

Abel's first report card: a pass

Took over as CEO on January 1, handed the chairman role to Buffett on September 18, and delivered this quarterly report on November 7. It is the first quarterly report in sixty years without Buffett's signature.

In substance, he did three things: he did not dress up bad news, he raised buybacks from 4.5 billion to 9.6 billion, and he kept doing small-scale M&A (Taylor Morrison at 6.8 billion). All three are reasonable, but none proves "he dares to act aggressively in a crisis."

This report earns only 7 out of 10. The remaining 3 points must wait for the next panic.

Conclusion: a good company, but not a good price

Three scenarios: bear case USD 488 (-18%, AI trade rebounds and the premium unwinds), base case USD 626 (+6%, book value compounds at 8% a year), bull case USD 734 (+24%, risk-off persists plus one large M&A deal). Upside 6%, downside 18% — the risk/reward does not hold.

The entry point is 1.4x book, around USD 500. Otherwise, wait for one of three signals: a single M&A deal above USD 30 billion, underwriting profit back to USD 2 billion/quarter, or the stock below 500.

This company is worth waiting for. It is not worth chasing.

Conclusion

Watch; wait for a pullback. Target entry point USD 500 (1.4x book); neutral target USD 626.
