Close reading (Buffett) — Berkshire

Where retained earnings went matters more than what was earned this quarter
Research date 2026/11/16  ·  Price $592.85 (-0.02%)  ·  Market cap About USD 1.26 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 16.6x

Core view

A single quarter of operating profit proves little. The test I apply is whether every dollar of retained earnings has translated into a dollar of market value. Berkshire's record is positive, but it has deteriorated over the past five years.

Retained earnings test: book value +10.5% is the hard metric

Book value per A-share of USD 536,590, up 10.5% YoY. That figure says more than operating profit does—it captures investment gains, FX and the share-count reduction from buybacks all at once. Roughly 9%–11% annualised over ten years—that is the true speed of this machine.

For comparison, the same money in the S&P 500 over the same decade would have earned roughly 12%–13% annualised, dividends included. Berkshire underperformed the index for ten years. That is not a scandal; it is the cost of size—a USD 1.26 trillion market cap leaves no good opportunity large enough.

Anyone buying it must therefore understand: you are not buying excess returns, you are buying "losing less when others lose money."

Segments: the three physical businesses are the real "moat"

Insurance profit is a by-product of float; the equity portfolio's gains belong to the market. The true "operations" are BNSF, BHE and manufacturing, services and retailing — together these three earned RMB 7.383 billion pre-tax this quarter, +12.6%, the most solid part of this report.

Railroad RMB 1.729 billion, +19.3%: volumes are back and costs are contained. Energy RMB 1.612 billion, +8.3%: the rate base is expanding, with data-center power demand the new variable. Manufacturing RMB 4.042 billion, +11.8%: Precision Castparts' aerospace parts recover with flight hours.Together the three annualize to roughly USD 29.5 billion pre-tax — the valuation floor.

Take USD 29.5 billion pre-tax, tax at 21% for about USD 23.3 billion after tax, and apply 15x for USD 350 billion — less than 30% of market cap. The remaining 70% is insurance, the equity portfolio and cash.

Conclusion

I would call this quarter "in line". Operating profit fell 5.8% while book value rose 10.5% — taken together, the message is that the business is compounding at a low rate, not deteriorating.

At 1.66x book, though, I cannot bring myself to buy. In my entire life I have paid such a price only once, and I regretted it afterwards.
