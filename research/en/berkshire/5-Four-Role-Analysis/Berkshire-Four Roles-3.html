Where the conglomerate stands in 2026: insurance hard cycle peaking, railway flat, power tight

Core view

Berkshire spans no single industry, and each of its sub-industries runs its own cycle. As of November 2026: the insurance hard market has peaked, rail freight is recovering modestly, power demand is tight on data centres, and manufacturing tracks the industrial cycle.

Insurance: hard market over, soft market not yet underway

2023–2025 was a hard market for global P&C insurance: auto and property rates rose for consecutive years and industry-wide underwriting profit hit records. The turning point came in 2026, when auto claims costs caught up and commercial property rates fell as reinsurance capital flooded in. Berkshire's -43% underwriting profit is the reading on that turning point.

This is not a collapse, however. The combined ratio was 91.4% against an industry average of roughly 96%–98%, keeping Berkshire in the top 20%. Its edge lies in large-ticket liability and reinsurance — business that requires a AAA balance sheet, which only a handful of firms globally can offer.

Industry position: moving from "cycle peak" toward "cycle midpoint," with 2–3 more quarters of decline.

Railways: freight volumes are back, but pricing is not

BNSF's pre-tax profit was USD 1.729 billion, +19.3%, driven by the recovery in intermodal (containers from port to inland). US rail freight volumes bottomed in 2025 and are recovering in 2026 as manufacturing restocking picks up.

Rail pricing power, however, is constrained on two fronts: truck rates and regulation. In 2026, excess truck capacity and weak rates kept railroads from pushing through sharp price increases, while the Surface Transportation Board has grown stricter in ruling on rate disputes. So rail is "volume up, price flat," with profit growth coming from efficiency rather than price hikes.

Its only rivals are Union Pacific and CSX (east), making the western network a duopoly. That structure won't change for a decade, and it is where the value lies.

AI data centres turn utilities from bond proxies into growth stocks

This is the most underappreciated change of 2026. BHE's pre-tax profit of RMB 1.612 billion, +8.3%, looks unremarkable, but the order book is shifting: data-center power demand has lifted US electricity demand growth from 0.5% to 2%–3%, giving utilities a volume-growth story for the first time.

Berkshire's edge is capital—building power plants and transmission grids takes tens of billions of dollars, and only a handful of companies can fund it. The drawback is regulation: wildfire liability, rate approvals, state-level politics. BHE's wildfire litigation across several western states is the longest tail on this balance sheet.

Industry position: demand has only just entered an upcycle, but regulatory risk is rising in tandem. This is a "good business + bad politics" combination.

Conclusion

Three sub-sectors, three cycles: insurance past its peak and heading down, railways neutral, power on the up. Net, a "mild downturn" — the sector-level explanation for the -5.8% decline in operating profit.

The mix buys resilience to single-point risk at the cost of any one engine driving group acceleration. Double-digit growth requires M&A.
