Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Berkshire

Breaking down the three statements: quality of profit, destination of cash, true value of assets
Research date 2026/11/16  ·  Price $592.85 (-0.02%)  ·  Market cap About USD 1.26 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 16.6x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Read the quarterly in layers: operating profit of RMB 12.705 billion (the business itself), GAAP net income of RMB 22.112 billion (including investment gains and losses), and a cash increase of more than RMB 30 billion (including unsettled Treasury securities). Taken together, these three layers point to completely opposite conclusions.

Insurance earnings fell 26%, while the physical business grew 12.6%

Break the group into its five segments and the story reduces to one line: insurance totaled RMB 4.086 billion, down 26.4% YoY, while the physical side (BNSF + BHE + manufacturing) came in at RMB 7.383 billion, up 12.6%. Net the two and operating earnings fall 5.8%.

The insurance split matters more: underwriting RMB 1.352 billion (-43%), investments RMB 2.734 billion (-14.1%). Underwriting gave back the money of the auto-insurance cycle; investments lost short-term reinvestment yield, sliding from a 5.25% peak to 4.4%–4.6%. Both are "earning less," not "losing money."

GAAP net income was RMB 22.112 billion, -28.2%, with the entire gap coming from quarterly swings in investment gains and losses. On that number the P/E is 16.6x, which looks cheap; on operating earnings it is 24.8x, and that is the real price.

Cash: RMB 377.4 billion is not idle money but money that hasn't found its price

Cash of RMB 40.7 billion plus short-term Treasuries of RMB 336.7 billion gives RMB 377.4 billion gross; net of working capital tied up in the railroad and energy businesses and of Treasuries purchased but not yet settled, roughly RMB 352.3 billion. Either way, that is 28%–30% of market cap.

The pile generates about USD 17 billion of pre-tax income a year (rough math: RMB 377.4 billion × 4.5%) and is the main source of this quarter's RMB 2.734 billion of investment income.Far from a drag, it is a call option yielding 4.5% annualized. The catch: the option's strike price is "cheap assets appear," and that condition has not appeared in three years.

The capital-allocation math matters too: buybacks of RMB 14.4 billion and M&A spend of RMB 16.2 billion (including RMB 6.8 billion for Taylor Morrison) in the first three quarters, RMB 30.6 billion combined, against operating cash flow over the same period that far exceeded it. Not being able to spend the money is the real problem.

Balance sheet: of RMB 761.4bn in equity, how much is "real money"

Total assets of RMB 1.304 trillion, equity of RMB 761.4 billion and leverage of 1.7x — absurdly low for an insurer. The two largest asset blocks are short-term Treasuries of RMB 336.7 billion and the equity portfolio (roughly RMB 300 billion on a 13F basis), with the remainder in fixed assets and goodwill across rail, energy and manufacturing.

One key point: book value understates true value. Holdings such as Apple, American Express and Coca-Cola are carried at cost or fair value, leaving part of decades of unrealized gains off the books; BNSF and BHE are likewise carried far below replacement cost. At 1.66x book, the true premium may therefore be only 1.3–1.4x.

That explains why management dares to buy back at this price: they are looking at intrinsic value, not book value. You can disagree with the estimate, but you cannot call it illogical.

Conclusion

No hard flaws in the financials: the profit is real (operating basis), the cash is real (short-term debt), and leverage is very low. The only problem is "too much money"—RMB 377.4 billion sits in assets yielding 4.5%, dragging ROE down to 10.4% and making the 24.8x operating profit multiple look richer.

Deploy that cash within three years and the valuation immediately looks reasonable; leave it sitting there and 25x is paying for "waiting."
