## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.B, NYSE) is a diversified holding company funded chiefly by insurance float, with a market cap of about USD 1.26 trillion. It operates across five segments: insurance underwriting, insurance investment, BNSF railroad, Berkshire Hathaway Energy (BHE), and manufacturing, services and retailing.

2026 marks the first time in the company's history that both the CEO and the chairman changed hands at once. Greg Abel took over as CEO on January 1, and Warren Buffett stepped down as chairman on September 18, succeeded by Howard Buffett; Buffett became chairman emeritus.

Shareholders' equity stood at USD 761.4 billion, total assets at USD 1.304 trillion, insurance float at USD 178.5 billion, and cash and short-term Treasury bills at USD 377.4 billion (gross).

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

The company spans four industries, each on a different cycle. Insurance: the hard market ended in 2023–2025 and normalises in 2026; the industry combined ratio is around 96%–98%, and the company's 91.4% remains significantly better than the industry. Rail: US freight volumes bottomed in 2025 and recover in 2026 on restocking; the western rail network is a duopoly of BNSF and Union Pacific. Power: AI data centres push US electricity demand growth from 0.5% to 2%–3%, turning utilities from a "bond substitute" into an asset with volume. Manufacturing: expands modestly with the industrial cycle.

Overall: the four sub-industries are out of sync, with a mild overall downturn, but no systemic risk in any single segment.

## Chapter 3 Business model

The core is "negative-cost liabilities + positive-return assets + the freedom not to act." Float of USD 178.5 billion, a combined ratio of 91.4% and a negative cost of funds. The asset side splits three ways: wholly owned subsidiaries (stable cash flow), large minority stakes (the top five account for 68.5% of the 13F), and short-term Treasuries (USD 336.7 billion, 4.4%–4.6% annualised).

The moat lies not in products but in the balance sheet and reputation: buyers of huge liability insurance policies are paying for the certainty that "you will still be here in twenty years," and that trust cannot be replicated with money.

Three cross-checks: ① cash plus short-term debt USD 377.4 billion ÷ market cap USD 1.26 trillion = 30%, consistent with the "roughly 30% of market cap" statement; ② first three quarters net income USD 57.89 billion ÷ average equity USD 73.95 billion × 4/3 = annualised ROE 10.4%; ③ underwriting profit USD 1.352 billion ÷ combined ratio implies premiums of USD 15.7 billion; against underwriting profit of USD 2.369 billion in the same period of 2025, implied premium scale is broadly flat — a margin decline, not a shrinking business.

## Chapter 5 Management

Abel joined BHE in 1992, took charge of energy in 2008, was promoted to vice chairman in 2018 and succeeded to CEO in January 2026. Jain continues to head insurance, while Howard Buffett serves as non-executive chairman. The compensation system carries no stock options; bonuses are tied to business capital returns.

Three observations stand out from the first standalone quarterly report: bad news was not glossed over; buybacks rose from USD 4.5 billion to USD 9.647 billion; and M&A remains confined to small deals below USD 10 billion. Operating capability is proven; capital allocation and crisis response have no track record, which is why management scores only 7/10.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three points: ① Organic growth in float. Historically up 2%–5% a year; on a base of RMB 178.5 billion, that means USD 4–9 billion of new investable capital annually. ② Expansion of the rate base at regulated assets. BHE is accelerating capex on data-center power demand, with allowed returns booked at a fixed percentage. ③ M&A. With USD 377.4 billion of cash, any USD 30 billion deal would lift operating earnings per share by more than 10%.

Of the three, the first two are slow variables (3%–6% annualized); the third is a step variable. The market now pays 1.66x book, implying it is pricing in the third as well.

The three drivers differ widely in return on capital: M&A has historically ranged from -30% (PCP) to +200% (Japanese trading houses), an enormous variance; rate-base returns are locked at 9%–11%, essentially risk-free; float growth is "free leverage," and its return depends on the price at which it is deployed. On valuation, therefore, the first two should be assigned separate multiples — the market currently blends all three into a single 1.6x book price, a crude simplification.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

By segment, auto insurance competes with Progressive (a leader in UBI pricing) and Allstate; commercial insurance and reinsurance with Chubb, AIG and Munich Re; rail with Union Pacific and CSX; utilities with NextEra and Duke; manufacturing faces fragmented competition.

Only two threats are real: Progressive's cost advantage in auto insurance and 4.5% US short-term Treasuries. The latter is the common rival of every equity asset, since the risk-free rate raises the opportunity cost. Berkshire itself holds USD 377.4 billion in short-term Treasuries — effectively conceding that this rival exists.

Share data tells the story: in the USD 300 billion auto insurance market, Progressive's share rose from about 13% to over 16% over the past five years, while GEICO's fell from about 12% to around 11% (rough calculation, on a premium basis). Two points of share equate to roughly USD 6 billion of premiums and USD 500 million of underwriting profit. That is the dividing line between "cycle" and "structure" — the cycle comes back; share does not come back on its own.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

① Underwriting cycle deterioration: The combined ratio stands at 91.4%; a move above 100% in 2027 would turn float from negative-cost to positive-cost and force a rewrite of the valuation model. ② Safe-haven inflows receding: Outperformance of 19.2 percentage points versus the S&P over the past month, the widest since 2010; historically, such extreme divergence has retraced within 1–3 months. ③ Wildfire litigation: BHE's liability across multiple western states could reach the USD 10 billion magnitude. ④ Large acquisition risk: The roughly USD 9.8 billion write-down on PCP in 2016 sets a precedent. ⑤ Succession risk: The new management team has not been tested in a crisis. ⑥ Curse of size: At a market cap of USD 1.26 trillion, long-term returns converge toward the broad market.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

Assumptions (our rough calculation, not company guidance): 2027 underwriting profit steady at USD 1.5–1.7 billion per quarter (combined ratio 92%–94%); insurance investment income down a further 5% on lower short-term bond yields; BNSF and BHE each up 6%–8%; manufacturing, services and retail up 6%. This implies 2027 operating profit of about USD 53 billion (2026 annualised USD 50.8 billion × 1.04).

Book value: end-2027 Class B equivalent of about USD 386–390 (up 8%–9% annually), or about USD 580,000 per Class A share. At 1.6x P/B, the share price is about USD 620–625; at 1.4x, about USD 545.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

Anchor on book value per share. The current USD 592.85 is 1.66x the Class B equivalent book value of USD 357.7, putting it in the 70th percentile of the historical 1.2–1.9x range (midpoint 1.4x).

Three scenarios: bear case USD 488 (1.30x one-year-forward book, risk-off retreat plus underwriting decline), base case USD 626 (1.62x) and bull case USD 734 (1.85x, sustained risk-off plus a large acquisition). Probabilities 30/50/20; expected return +2.4% (rough calculation).

Why not use P/E for valuation: GAAP net income includes large unrealised investment gains and is highly volatile. The operating earnings multiple (24.8x) works as a cross-check but is unsuitable as a target-price anchor, being overly sensitive to the underwriting cycle.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Rating: Watch; wait for a pullback. 12-month target price USD 626. At the current USD 592.85, the stock offers 6% upside and 18% downside. Risk/reward does not work, and we would not advise building a position at this level.

We recommend a three-tier entry: USD 500 (1.4x book) to build 3%, USD 460 to build to 5%, and USD 430 to build to 8%. Cap at 8%, positioned as defensive ballast.

Stop-loss triggers are thesis-based, not price-based: a combined ratio above 100% for two consecutive quarters, float declining YoY, a large acquisition at more than 2x book, or continued large buybacks above 1.8x book. Any trigger cuts the rating to "Avoid".
