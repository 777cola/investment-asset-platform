## First-pass screen: scale and structure

First, cut the ones with the wrong structure. Markel is a "mini-Berkshire" with an identical model—insurance float plus long-term equity holdings—but its market cap is only USD 25 billion and its float is less than 1/20 of Berkshire's. The model is right, but the scale is insufficient to absorb large allocations.

Loews is family-controlled, spanning insurance, energy and hotels, with a market cap of USD 20 billion, poor liquidity, and none of Berkshire's negative-cost funding advantage. Eliminated.

Progressive and Chubb are pure insurers with no physical assets and no capital allocation business; they are essentially single-industry beta and do not constitute comparable "integrated holding" candidates, but are retained for reference.

## Second screening cut: cash flow and balance sheet

Compare Berkshire, Chubb and Union Pacific on cash flow quality. Union Pacific runs a 38% operating margin and 35% ROE, but that ROE is manufactured through buybacks and leverage, capex consumes most of cash flow, and the business is single-industry with no diversification. Chubb has a 10% underwriting margin and trades at 12x PE — cheap, but its investment income depends on a bond portfolio, so profits shrink when rates fall.

Berkshire: negative-cost float, no net debt at the parent, RMB 377.4 billion in cash, and two-thirds of profit from operating businesses. Of the three, it has the highest cash flow quality and also the highest valuation.

## Final pick: why it is still the one, but not now

If you could buy only one company and had to hold it for ten years, the answer is Berkshire — not because it has the highest return, but because it is the least likely to force you to sell within those ten years. No debt maturities, no customer concentration, no technological substitution, no single-industry collapse can kill it.

But if the question is "which one to buy now," the answer may not be it. Chubb trades at 12x PE; Progressive at 18x PE but with 25% ROE — both offer similar cash flow at lower valuations. The extra 4x PE you pay for Berkshire buys "optionality in a crisis."

So the funnel's conclusion takes two sentences: on a ten-year horizon, it is the only answer; on a one-year horizon, it is not the best answer. That distinction is the entire meaning of "watch, wait for a pullback."
