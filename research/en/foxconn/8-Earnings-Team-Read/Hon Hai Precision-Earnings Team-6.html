The company just broke the trillion-revenue mark — so why is the stock falling?
Research date 2026/10/19  ·  Price NT$240 (-1.84%)  ·  Market cap About TWD 3.37 trillion  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 12.6x
← Back to the hub

Core view

September revenue hit TWD 1 trillion 158.6 billion, making it the first Taiwanese company to top TWD 1 trillion in a single month. That same week, the share price pulled back 22.5% from its high. The two facts are not contradictory; they describe the same thing.

First, the good news

Hon Hai's Q3 2026 revenue reached TWD 3 trillion 26.9 billion, up 47.1% YoY, with first three quarters totalling TWD 7 trillion 669.8 billion, up 39.5% YoY. Q2 operating profit was TWD 94.8 billion, up 68% YoY—27 percentage points ahead of revenue growth. With capacity utilisation maxed out, depreciation and administrative expenses are diluted: this is genuine operating leverage.

The company holds more than 40% of the AI server market and is the only manufacturer able to deliver simultaneously from Mexico, Vietnam, India and the US. In April this year, its single-month AI rack shipments led Quanta by about 2,100 racks and Wistron by about 1,300–1,400 racks.In this round of AI infrastructure buildout, it has the strongest execution capability, bar none.

Then there is that ugly number.

Gross margin 6.12%, down 0.06pp QoQ and 0.21pp YoY. Revenue rose 41% while gross margin fell.

The reason is simple: a single NVL72 rack costs USD 3 million, with GPU, HBM and storage accounting for the bulk, all customer-specified and booked at market prices. The contract manufacturer charges for assembly, testing, integration and delivery at USD 20,000–30,000 per rack. Materials inflate revenue; profit does not follow. In one sentence: revenue doubles, profit rises only 35%.

Meanwhile, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%. The pricing gains stayed upstream; the scale went to the contract manufacturer.

Cash flow is the third signal

AI racks carry high unit prices and an 8–12 week material lead time, so every extra dollar of revenue ties up components, receivables and inventory before it is collected. Free cash flow was negative in the first half. For a company running a 2.37% net margin, that matters: growth has to be funded with borrowed money.

Profit can be generated through turnover; cash depends on customers paying on time. Hon Hai has delivered the former; the latter rests with its customers.

So how should it be priced?

At RMB 239.5, the stock trades on a TWD 3.37 trillion market cap, 12.6x 2026E P/E and 10.6x 2027E. The absolute multiple is not expensive, but the denominator is peak-cycle EPS: if cloud capex decelerates in 2027 and EPS falls back to RMB 15, the current price implies 16x.

Our three scenarios put the bear case at RMB 160, the base case at RMB 280 and the bull case at RMB 380. Weighted 40/45/15, that gives an expected value of RMB 247, 3% above the current price. Taking on 33% downside for a 3% expected return—those odds do not justify a position.

Only two paths change this: the stock falls below RMB 180, or gross margin holds above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters. The first depends on the market; the second on the earnings report. Until either signal appears, breaking the trillion-dollar revenue mark again is no reason to buy.

A final note on customer mix: North American top-five CSPs accounted for about 70% of full-rack procurement in 2025, and that share is expected to fall to 60% in 2026. Neoclouds and sovereign clouds fill the gap—the former buying GPUs on debt financing, the latter on fiscal budgets. Customers are more diversified, but credit quality has deteriorated—the worst possible shift for a company with a 2.37% net margin.

Conclusion

Conclusion: A good company, a bad business, a fair price, the wrong odds. Watch and wait for the gross margin inflection point, or for a price below RMB 180.
