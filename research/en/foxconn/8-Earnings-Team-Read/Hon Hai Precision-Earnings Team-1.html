Core view

The key line in this report is not revenue but the 2.37% net margin. Revenue came to RMB 2 trillion 525.9 billion, of which RMB 59.97 billion reached shareholders. Duan Yongping's test for reading financial statements: does the business earn real money, must the money earned be ploughed straight back in, and does reinvesting it still earn the same return.

Real money? It earns processing fees, not profit

Gross margin 6.12%, operating margin 3.75%, net margin 2.37% — each profit layer is thinner than the one above it, so something erodes profit at every stage: materials absorb 94%, expenses 2.4%, and taxes plus minority interests a further 1.4%. As Duan Yongping would put it, this is not "earning little" but "there was never much to earn."

The essence of the processing-fee business is how much customers will pay for "peace of mind." AI racks carry high integration complexity, and the premium customers will pay looks higher than for phone assembly — a 6.12% gross margin is indeed a notch above the 4%–5% of peers. But a 6% gross margin corresponds to a 2.4% net margin: any 1.0-percentage-point decline in yield and profit goes to zero.

Reinvest immediately: yes, and double down

Revenue rose 39%, working capital rose with it, and capex must expand into four countries. Free cash flow was negative in the first half, so the money earned cannot be distributed and more must be borrowed. What Duan Yongping dislikes most is exactly this kind of "the more it grows, the more cash it lacks" business—it stakes shareholder returns on "after growth stops."

His test is simple: if this company stopped growing tomorrow, how much cash could it pull out? The answer is positive, and not small—but by then revenue would fall and the stock price would fall first. That is why such companies can only be bought at extremely low prices.

Return: ROE 9%–12%, driven by turnover rather than margin

DuPont in three parts: net margin 2.37% × asset turnover 1.4x × equity multiplier 2.4x ≈ 8%. ROE is propped up by fast asset turnover, not by earning much on each sale. That structure looks great when demand rises; when demand falls and turnover slows, ROE drops faster than anyone else's.

The Duan Yongping-style conclusion: this is a business you can understand and calculate clearly, but one not worth waiting for. He would consider it below 8x P/E, and only as a cyclical. At 12.6x and RMB 239.5, it is outside his range.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping's takeaway: the financials prove this is a real business — and also that it is not a good one. The profit is real, the cash is not, and returns depend on turnover. Do not buy at RMB 239.5; around RMB 160 is negotiable.
