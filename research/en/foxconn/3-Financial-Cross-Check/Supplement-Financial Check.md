## Start with the six that line up: no dispute at the revenue level

The strongest verification is the internal cross-check: Q3 revenue of RMB 302.69 billion exactly equals July's RMB 94.65 billion + August's RMB 92.18 billion + September's RMB 115.86 billion. The monthly announcements are statutory disclosures and the quarterly figure is their sum; the two reconcile precisely, leaving no room for window dressing at this level. Gross margin of 6.12% also ties out both ways: the figure reported at the earnings call matches gross profit divided by revenue.

The two foreign brokers' EPS estimates differ by RMB 0.02 (19.06 vs 19.04; 22.52 vs 22.49), a 0.1% deviation — model minutiae. That consistency is itself informative: sell-side profit forecasts for Hon Hai diverge very little, because revenue is determined by customer orders, not by management's intentions. Low dispersion is a good thing, but it also means expectations are already fairly full.

## Deviation one: AI server revenue — a 30% gap is not a calculation error

Management guides to "multiple growth for the full year, exceeding a trillion," while sell-side models sit at about 1.4 trillion — a 30% gap. Revenue recognition explains it: in rack-level business, booking high-priced components such as GPUs and HBM gross on a buy-and-sell basis inflates revenue several-fold, whereas under consignment, where the customer supplies the materials, only processing fees are recognised. The same shipment can differ by a factor of two between the two methods. Management did not specify on the earnings call which basis its AI server revenue uses, so the figure is not comparable across companies.

The fix is simple: write only "multiple growth" and "cloud and networking products account for more than 40% of revenue (rough calculation)" throughout, and cite no absolute AI server revenue figure. Where a sense of scale is needed, use verifiable proxies — total quarterly revenue of 3.03 trillion and single-month September revenue of 1.16 trillion, breaching the trillion mark. Both are statutory disclosures and cannot be argued away.

## Deviation 2: the 10.4% EPS gap reflects seasonality, not optimism

2026E EPS of RMB 19.06 less the RMB 7.83 already delivered in H1 (3.56 + 4.27) leaves RMB 11.23 for H2, 43% above H1. That looks aggressive, but Q3 revenue already grew 20.4% QoQ and broke the trillion mark in September alone; at a rough 2.37% net margin, Q3 EPS is about RMB 5.1. Q4 then needs only RMB 6.1 to hit the number, and Q4 is the traditional peak for AI rack deliveries.

The same calculation yields a checkpoint: H2 EPS of RMB 11.23 requires an H2 net margin of no less than 2.45%. If gross margin falls another 0.2pp, operating margin cannot hold and the RMB 19.06 fails — sell-side models in fact embed an assumption that gross margin stops deteriorating, and that assumption has already been broken for two consecutive quarters in 2026.

## Final adopted basis

Revenue, gross margin, operating margin and EPS are company-disclosed; 2026E/2027E EPS are taken from foreign-brokerage models and labelled "sell-side estimates, not company guidance". Market cap is RMB 239.5 × about 14.06 billion shares = TWD 3.37 trillion, consistent with the working papers. PB is roughly 1.5x on 2026Q2 equity attributable to the parent.EV/EBITDA is not used — Foxconn's depreciation lives and lease treatment are opaque, and the EV-based error exceeds the P/E-based error.

Two further disciplines apply. First, no absolute figures are given other than "AI server revenue share". Second, all cash-flow statements give direction only ("negative", "under pressure") rather than amounts, because the earnings call did not break them out and any specific number would be fabricated.
