Core call: revenue and margins credible, AI server revenue unusable

Eight core figures were cross-checked against two sources one by one. Six deviations are within 0.1%, and two exceed 1%: first, full-year 2026 AI server revenue, where the company's framing of "multiple growth, above TWD 1 trillion" differs from the sell-side model's TWD 1.4 trillion by about 30%; second, 2026E EPS of 19.06 yuan differs from the Q2 single-quarter annualized 17.08 yuan by 10.4%. The first is a revenue-recognition difference (gross buy-and-sell vs net), and the second is seasonality—AI rack deliveries are concentrated in Q3 and Q4. Approach: use company-disclosed values for revenue, gross margin and EPS; for AI server revenue, state only the direction and "accounting for more than 40% of cloud and networking" without giving an absolute value; calculate market cap independently by multiplying the closing price by share count, consistent with the working papers.

Start with the six that line up: no dispute at the revenue level

The strongest verification is the internal cross-check: Q3 revenue of RMB 302.69 billion exactly equals July's RMB 94.65 billion + August's RMB 92.18 billion + September's RMB 115.86 billion. The monthly announcements are statutory disclosures and the quarterly figure is their sum; the two reconcile precisely, indicating no room for window dressing at this level. The 6.12% gross margin also ties out both ways: the figure reported at the earnings call matches the number derived by dividing gross profit by revenue.

The two foreign brokers' EPS estimates differ by RMB 0.02 (19.06 vs 19.04; 22.52 vs 22.49), a 0.1% deviation — model minutiae. That consistency is itself informative: sell-side profit forecasts for Hon Hai diverge very little, because its revenue is determined by customer orders, not by management's intentions. Low dispersion is a good thing, but it also means expectations are already fairly full.

Deviation one: AI server revenue — a 30% gap is not a calculation error

The company says "multiple growth for the full year, exceeding a trillion," while sell-side models put it at about 1.4 trillion — a 30% gap. The reason lies in revenue recognition: in rack-level business, if high-priced components such as GPUs and HBM are booked gross on a buy-and-sell basis, revenue is inflated several-fold; if the customer supplies the materials (consignment), only processing fees are recognized. The same shipment can differ by a factor of two under the two methods. The company did not specify at the earnings call which basis its AI server revenue uses, so this figure cannot be compared across companies.

The fix is simple: throughout, write only "multiple growth" and "cloud and networking products account for more than 40% of revenue (rough calculation)," and cite no absolute AI server revenue figure. Where a sense of scale is needed, use verifiable proxies — total quarterly revenue of 3.03 trillion and single-month September revenue of 1.16 trillion, breaching the trillion mark. Both are statutory disclosures and cannot be argued away.

Deviation 2: the 10.4% EPS gap reflects seasonality, not optimism

2026E EPS of RMB 19.06, less the RMB 7.83 already delivered in H1 (3.56 + 4.27), leaves RMB 11.23 for H2, 43% above H1. That looks aggressive, but Q3 revenue already grew 20.4% QoQ and broke the trillion mark in September alone; at a rough 2.37% net margin, Q3 EPS is about RMB 5.1. Q4 then needs only RMB 6.1 to hit the number, and Q4 is the traditional peak for AI rack deliveries.

This calculation also yields a checkpoint: H2 EPS of RMB 11.23 requires an H2 net margin of no less than 2.45%. If gross margin falls another 0.2pp, operating margin cannot hold and the RMB 19.06 fails — sell-side models in fact embed an assumption that gross margin stops deteriorating, and that assumption has already been broken for two consecutive quarters in 2026.

Final adopted basis

Revenue, gross margin, operating margin and EPS use company-disclosed figures; 2026E/2027E EPS use foreign-brokerage models and are labelled "sell-side estimates, not company guidance"; market cap is calculated as RMB 239.5 × about 14.06 billion shares = TWD 3.37 trillion, consistent with the working papers; PB is roughly 1.5x based on 2026Q2 equity attributable to the parent.EV/EBITDA is not used — Foxconn's depreciation lives and lease treatment are opaque, and the EV-based error is larger than the PE-based error.

Two further disciplines: first, no absolute figures are given other than "AI server revenue share"; second, all statements involving cash flow give direction only ("negative", "under pressure"), not amounts, because the earnings call did not break them out and any specific number would be fabricated.

Conclusion

The data-availability tiers are clear: revenue and margins are hard data, consistent across two sources; EPS is a sell-side estimate, usable but subject to a seasonality discount; AI server revenue is soft data and can only be treated qualitatively. The conclusion does not change on verification — the denominator of 12.6x 2026E PE rests on an H2 net margin of no less than 2.45%, and two consecutive quarters of gross-margin decline have already undermined that premise. Watch, and wait for a gross-margin inflection.
