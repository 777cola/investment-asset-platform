Core call: the growth assumption holds, the profit assumption does not

We break the bull case for Hon Hai into five verifiable assumptions. Shipment multiple growth: holds. Scale driving operating margin expansion: holds, but near the ceiling. Gross margin rising with product mix: does not hold, down two consecutive quarters. Free cash flow turning positive: does not hold, negative in the first half. Customer base diversified with no deterioration in collections: pending verification. Two of the five hold, two do not and one is pending verification — the quantitative basis for our "Watch" rating. At the November Q3 earnings call, watch only gross margin and working capital tied up.

Evidence: 2026Q2 results and earnings call (2026/08/12); September 2026 revenue announcement (2026/10/05). The 6.5% gross margin threshold is the buy condition set out in the working papers. Free cash flow was not separately quantified on the call, so the status assessment relies on directional commentary and the gap between revenue and net income growth.
Two conditions must be met for establishment: shipments and expense leverage

The first point is largely uncontested. Q3 revenue was 3 trillion 269 billion, up 47.1% YoY, with September alone at 1 trillion 1,586 billion — the first time it has broken the trillion mark; the monthly figures reconcile exactly with the quarterly total. The AI rack ramp is fact, not expectation. The 2027 handover to VR200 rests only on industry research inference (TrendForce expects NVL72 output value to rise 214%), with no order evidence, so the "2026 portion" of this assumption holds while the "2027 portion" remains unverified.

The second point also holds: operating margin was 3.75%, up 0.6pct YoY, with operating expenses up only 6% while revenue rose 41%. But the expense ratio has already fallen to 2.36% against a theoretical floor of 2.1%–2.2%; squeezing out another 0.2pct would add 5 billion in pre-tax profit, or 8% of net income. This assumption can still deliver one small surprise, but it cannot support a re-rating — it has been downgraded from growth engine to defensive item.

Two claims that do not hold: gross margin and cash flow

Gross margin is the core issue. 2026Q1 came in at 6.18% and Q2 at 6.12%, down for two consecutive quarters and -0.21pct YoY. Management has now said twice that "the product mix will improve in the second half" and has delivered neither time. On discipline grounds we downgrade this assumption from "pending verification" to "partially falsified" — not overturned by the data, but drained of credibility by repeated statements.

Cash flow does not hold up either: free cash flow was negative in the first half, driven by receivables and inventory swelling as revenue doubled, plus capex from capacity expansion at four locations. Only two paths turn it positive — revenue growth slows (working capital stops expanding), or customer payment terms shorten (pricing power improves). The first would hit the share price; the second requires gross margin to improve in tandem.

One point still to verify: the "quality" of the customer mix

The share of full-rack purchases by North America's five largest CSPs falls from about 70% in 2025 to about 60% in 2026 — an improving mix. But the gap is filled by Neoclouds and sovereign clouds: the former buy GPUs on debt financing, the latter on fiscal budgets. More customers and lower bargaining power per customer is good; worse credit quality among new customers and collection that depends more on financing progress is bad.

Days sales outstanding is the way to verify this: if revenue grows 40% while receivables grow more than 50%, customers are using their own payment terms to tie up Hon Hai's capital. This metric can be calculated from the interim report and is more reliable than what is said on the earnings call.

Falsification conditions: when to walk away outright

Three hard triggers; any one removes the stock from the watchlist: ① gross margin below 5.8% for two consecutive quarters, signalling structural dilution from the full-cabinet model beyond expectations; ② quarterly revenue falling QoQ with no guidance cut and no explanation on the earnings call, signalling a break in order visibility; ③ receivables turnover days up more than 15 days YoY, signalling deteriorating customer credit.

Conversely, any one of three improvements warrants an upgrade: gross margin above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters, free cash flow turning positive, or the share price falling below RMB 180 (a valuation trigger, not a fundamental one). Why fix these conditions in writing: no need to agonise anew each quarter—just watch the metrics.

Watch calendar

  • 2026/12/05: November revenue. Watch whether YoY growth holds above 30%.
  • 2027/01/05: December and full-year revenue. Watch whether Q4 grows QoQ.
  • 2027/03: Q4 results and annual report. Watch full-year free cash flow, receivables days and the depreciation breakdown.
  • 2027/05: Q1 results. First full quarter after the VR200 transition; gross margin direction is the key read.

Conclusion

The five assumptions reconcile to one result: growth holds, profit does not. This is not a vague "wait and see" stance; it is a clear "the denominator is unproven." The rating stays at "watch, await the gross margin inflection," with falsification conditions and a watch calendar locked in. Only one thing really needs doing: on the November earnings call, look at gross margin first, everything else second.
