Six-Gate Screen — Hon Hai Precision

Hon Hai Precision (2317.TW) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/10/19  ·  Price NT$240 (-1.84%)  ·  Market cap About TWD 3.37 trillion  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 12.6x
← Back to the hub

Core call: good company, reasonable price, but earnings quality does not justify a Buy

Six gates cleared, 32 points, two hard flaws. Gate two, good business: fail — a 2.37% net margin means every RMB 100 of revenue leaves RMB 2.4 in shareholders' pockets. Gate six, discipline: fail — the buy case rests essentially on a single macro assumption, that North American cloud capex keeps growing in 2027, which Hon Hai's own financials cannot falsify. The other four gates pass: the business is understandable; the moat is the widest of the six names (6.12% gross margin versus peers at 4.2%–5.7%); Liu Young-way's six years at the helm give a track record that can be checked; and 12.6x 2026E P/E is neutral-to-low for contract manufacturing. But that P/E is on cycle-peak EPS. Conclusion matches the working paper: watch, wait for a gross-margin inflection, do not chase at 239.5.

Gate one: circle of competence—you understand the business, but you cannot predict the timing

Hon Hai's model has not changed in thirty years: buy materials for brand owners, assemble, test, deliver, and collect a processing fee. In 2026 the content of that fee has changed—from phones and notebooks to rack-scale AI servers, where the ASP of a single NVL72 rack is more than ten times that of a general-purpose server, lifting revenue to the scale of TWD 3 trillion a quarter. Who pays, why they pay, and how costs move can be explained in three sentences; this should not cost points.

The 2 points are deducted on the demand side. Cloud and networking products were already Hon Hai's largest segment in Q2 2026, at more than 40% of revenue (roughly estimated on the earnings-call basis), with orders coming from the capex of five North American CSPs. This variable is not in Hon Hai's financials; it shifts quarter to quarter only in customers' guidance. Understanding the business does not mean accurately sizing how much of it will remain next year.

Stage two: a good business — RMB 2.4 of profit per RMB 100 of revenue

2026Q2 breakdown: revenue TWD 2,525.9 billion, gross profit TWD 154.5 billion (6.12%), operating expenses TWD 59.7 billion, operating profit TWD 94.8 billion (3.75%), net profit attributable to parent TWD 59.97 billion (2.37%), EPS TWD 4.27. Every TWD 1 of revenue growth leaves just 2.4 cents behind. This is not operational mismanagement: the most expensive GPUs, HBM and high-capacity storage in the racks are customer-specified and booked at market prices, and the foundry merely handles them without ever owning them.

The longer view is clearer still. Full-year 2025 EPS was TWD 12.09 and foreign brokers estimate TWD 19.06 for 2026; the +58% growth looks impressive, yet net margin moved from 2.3% in 2025Q2 to 2.37% in 2026Q2 — less than 0.1 percentage point in a year. All profit growth comes from scale; not a cent comes from pricing. This is a business that generates revenue with its balance sheet: in good years it earns hard money, and in bad years it cannot even hold on to that.

Gate three: moat — the widest of the six brothers, yet the thinnest

Digitimes tallied 2026Q2 gross margins across the six Taiwanese peers: Hon Hai 6.12%, Wistron 5.66%, Quanta 5.02%, Compal 4.6%, Pegatron 4.43% and Inventec 4.23%. Hon Hai ranks first and is the only one with a full lineup spanning components, mechanical parts, PCB, power, thermal and rack integration. The benefit of that gap: when customers negotiate consignment ratios, Hon Hai holds the cards.

The nature of the moat, however, needs to be clear: it blocks new entrants, not customers. In April 2026 single-month AI rack shipments, Hon Hai led Quanta by about 2,100 racks and Wistron by about 1,300–1,400 racks, on the strength of capacity and yield; for a customer, switching plants costs three months of certification, not a ten-year contract. Each time Nvidia changes platform generation, the supplier list is reshuffled. The moat is therefore wide but not deep — score it 6.

Gate 4: Management — six-year track record verifiable, capital allocation skews toward expansion

Liu Young-way succeeded Terry Gou in July 2019, replacing one-man decision-making with a nine-member management committee. His hardest task in office has been rebuilding the company from an "Apple assembler" into an "AI infrastructure supplier." In 2025 he set a target of "AI server revenue exceeding phone assembly," and on first-half 2026 segment revenue that target has been met. His words match the numbers—rare in the contract manufacturing industry.

Three points deducted for how the money is spent. The payout ratio is about 55%, and at 239.5 the dividend yield is under 3% (rough calculation). Meanwhile the company is expanding capacity simultaneously in the US, Mexico, Vietnam and India, with 2026 capex rising significantly, so shareholders bear all the risk of capacity going in ahead of demand. The dividend is not generous and the expansion is not restrained—neither is a mistake, but together they mean this stock can only make money on price spread, not be held for the dividend.

Gate 5: Margin of safety — paying 12.6x for peak-cycle earnings

RMB 239.5 × about 14.06 billion shares ≈ TWD 3.37 trillion market cap. 2026E EPS of RMB 19.06 puts the stock on 12.6x, 2027E EPS of RMB 22.5 on 10.6x, with PB around 1.5x. Foxconn's P/E has ranged roughly 8–15x over the past decade; at 12.6x it sits below the midpoint, so on an absolute basis it is not expensive — which is why it clears the fifth gate.

But "cheap" can be computed two ways. On normalized EPS: if cloud capex slows in 2027 and EPS falls back to RMB 15, RMB 239.5 implies 16x. On assets: 1.5x PB is only at the historical midpoint, not the bottom. In other words, the current price already discounts half of the "AI rack volumes keep ramping" story, leaving only half a margin of safety. Score 5, a marginal pass.

Gate 6: Discipline — the buy thesis is not in your hands

The question here is not whether the company is good, but whether you can hold the position. Buying Hon Hai now means buying one judgment: that North American cloud providers' capex keeps growing in 2027. That judgment cannot be verified from Hon Hai's quarterly reports—record revenue only proves orders were there last quarter, not next year. Its verification indicators sit in other companies' earnings calls, and their guidance changes every quarter.

Score: 3. The actionable alternative is to hard-code the discipline: either the price falls below TWD 180 (the pessimistic scenario already priced in), or gross margin holds above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters (denominator revised up). Until then, no number of fresh trillion-dollar revenue records constitutes a reason to buy.

Veto items and items to be verified

Disqualifier: good business. A 2.37% net margin leaves shareholders no buffer — any upstream price increase, FX swing or yield decline can wipe out a quarter's profit. Until this is resolved, full marks on the other five tests are irrelevant.

  • To verify 1: gross margin direction. With the VR200 rack transition in 2027 and ASP doubling again, a gross margin still stuck just above 6% would mean the contract manufacturer's position in the value chain is permanently pinned down. Watch: gross margin and product mix commentary on each quarterly earnings call.
  • To verify 2: free cash flow. Negative in the first half, with working capital absorption expanding alongside revenue. Watch: cash flow statement in the interim report, receivables and inventory turnover days.
  • To verify 3: customer structure. The five North American CSPs' share of full-rack purchases falls from about 70% in 2025 to about 60% in 2026, with Neoclouds and sovereign clouds filling the gap — greater customer diversification is positive, but collection depends more on their financing progress.
  • To verify 4: US capacity utilization. Fixed costs from the Texas and Wisconsin expansions arrive ahead of orders; if utilization drops below 70%, the 3.75% operating margin will not hold.

Conclusion

32/60, four hurdles cleared. Hon Hai is the contract manufacturer with the strongest execution and the least replaceable position in this AI infrastructure cycle, but it earns a processing fee, so gross margin, not revenue, sets its valuation ceiling. Action: add to the watchlist, no position. Two triggers — the share price falls below TWD 180, or gross margin holds above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters. Revisit a buy only if either occurs; if revenue keeps hitting new highs in 2027 while gross margin falls below 5.8%, remove it from the watchlist outright.
