## Why 0, and not "buy a little first"

"Buy a little and watch" makes sense in two cases: you want to track the company and need a position to force your attention, or the risk-reward is roughly symmetric and a small position lets you test the waters. Neither holds for Hon Hai. It is one of the top three companies in Taiwan by market cap, so tracking it requires no position, and its risk-reward is 33% downside against 17% upside — too asymmetric to justify a trial.

More practically, the stock's volatility comes from AI capex expectations, and those expectations reset every quarter. At a 0% position you can wait for the November earnings call to decide; at 1% you will be thinking about averaging down on the way down. When the risk-reward is wrong, the best position is no position.

## Three add-on price levels, each tied to a distinct thesis

RMB 180 marks the cycle-bottom valuation: 10x 2027E EPS of RMB 18, a 20% discount to the neutral RMB 22.5, implying about 1.15x PB. Hon Hai's two prior lows, in 2019 and 2022, both came at 8–10x P/E and 1.1–1.2x PB. At this level the -33% pessimistic scenario has already played out; a further decline needs fresh bad news.

RMB 160 marks the asset floor: 8x 2027E, PB of about 1.0x, close to liquidation value plus capacity replacement cost. A contract manufacturer's PB rarely breaks below 1, because plants and equipment are real. Add to 3%–4% here, but be prepared to hold for two years.

The fundamental trigger (6.5% gross margin) is the only reason to add that does not depend on price—it changes the denominator, not sentiment. When it appears, build 1% first and wait for a second quarter of confirmation before adding.

## Stop-loss and trimming: look at structure, not price

Price-based stop-losses on cyclicals tend to shake you out; structural stop-losses are more reliable. Three structural signals: ① gross margin below 5.8% for two consecutive quarters; ② single-quarter revenue turns negative QoQ and the company does not cut guidance (indicating a break in order visibility); ③ receivables days outstanding up more than 15 days YoY. If any one appears, it is not "it fell, so sell" — it is "the thesis has been falsified, so sell."

Use a price stop-loss in only one case: a break below RMB 140 (PB below 0.9x). At that point the issue is not fundamentals but the market pricing a more extreme scenario (for example, cloud capex halved), which calls for reassessment rather than stubbornly holding on.

## Correlation with other holdings

Hon Hai's correlation with TSMC, Quanta, Wistron and Wiwynn exceeds 0.8 and approaches 0.9 against AI-themed ETFs—it is not a diversification tool but another expression of the same risk factor. If the portfolio already holds TSMC or Quanta, adding Hon Hai is equivalent to levering up long AI capex.

True diversification can only come from "different positions along the same trend": upstream (TSMC, SK hynix) earns on price increases, midstream (Hon Hai) on scale and downstream (cloud providers) on applications. All three rise and fall together, but the order of drawdowns differs when the cycle turns—upstream holds up best, while midstream falls first and rebounds first.If midstream exposure is a must, Hon Hai is the first choice; but having bought it, one should not also buy Quanta.
