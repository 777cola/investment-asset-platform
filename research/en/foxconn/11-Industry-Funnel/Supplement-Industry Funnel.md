## Screening: three companies eliminated on a single word

Compal: AI servers now account for 70% of server revenue, which reads like a thorough transformation—but at a 4.6% gross margin and 1.2% operating margin, even 10x P/E is not cheap. It earns on scale, not profit, and gross margin fell 1.3pct YoY through the transition. Inventec: revenue +44.6% with profit at a record high, yet gross margin of 4.23% and operating margin of 1.76%, both down YoY—a textbook case of revenue growth without profit growth. Pegatron: revenue up only 10%, AI server share too low, essentially still a phone assembler.

The common problem across all three: in the same price-hike cycle, their gross margins run 1.5–2 percentage points below Hon Hai's, leaving no buffer. When the cycle turns, they are the first to lose money.

## Final three: elasticity, model, scale

Quanta offers the most upside: the highest AI server revenue share, revenue growth of +55% and ROE of about 25%. But its gross margin is 5.02%, down 2.03pct YoY and the most diluted of the six, and a P/E of 18x already prices in that upside.Buying Quanta means buying "higher beta", not "a better business".

Wiwynn's gross margin is 9.3%, the only double-digit figure among the six, with ROE of about 45%. But it runs a white-box direct-supply model—selling straight to CSPs, bypassing brand and channel, which naturally lifts gross margin; the cost is more concentrated customers and smaller scale (market cap HKD 55 billion), with a P/E of 20x.Its high gross margin comes from customer mix, not pricing power, and a single customer order shift would break its revenue.

Hon Hai: largest scale (HKD 337 billion), highest gross margin (6.12%), lowest P/E (12.6x), and the only one with global capacity.Its advantage is not that any one item is the strongest, but that all four are solid.

## Why it stands out: clients want "certainty"

The core of the AI rack business is lead time and yield, not price. When a USD 3 million rack slips a quarter, the customer loses the entire commissioning schedule for its data center. Customers therefore rank suppliers on four criteria: can you deliver on schedule, can you ramp yield, can you back up across multiple sites, and finally price. Price ranks fourth, which explains why Hon Hai's gross margin runs one percentage point above peers and why it cannot easily be replaced.

The same logic caps what it can earn: with price ranking fourth, the premium customers will pay has a ceiling. "Why it" and "why not buy it" have the same answer — it is the most reliable executor of this cycle, so there is no bargain to be had in its price.

## After the Final Selection: Why Still Monitor

The funnel screens for "the company most worth researching," not "the stock to buy right now." Of the six, Hon Hai trades on the lowest P/E, but that multiple reflects the thinnest margins and the strongest cyclicality in the group—a justified discount, not a mispricing. Setting its 12.6x P/E against Quanta's 18x is a comparison of business models, not of cheapness.

Turning the "final pick" into a "buy" requires one further condition: an empirical inflection in gross margin. That has to come from its own financial reports, not from peer comparison. The funnel's endpoint is therefore not a conclusion but the top of the watchlist. In other words, what this funnel screens for is "the one most worth watching," not "the one most worth buying."
