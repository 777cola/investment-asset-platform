## Screening: three companies eliminated on a single word

Compal: AI servers account for 70% of server revenue, which sounds like a thorough transformation, but with a 4.6% gross margin and 1.2% operating margin, even a PE of 10x is not cheap—it earns on scale, not profit, and gross margin fell 1.3pct YoY during the transition. Inventec: revenue +44.6% and profit at a record high, but gross margin of 4.23% and operating margin of 1.76%, both ratios down YoY—a classic case of "revenue growth without profit growth." Pegatron: revenue up only 10%, AI server share too low, essentially still a phone assembler.

The common problem across all three: in the same price-hike cycle, their gross margins run 1.5–2 percentage points below Hon Hai's, leaving no buffer. When the cycle turns, they are the first to lose money.

## Final three: elasticity, model, scale

Quanta has the most upside: highest AI server revenue share, revenue growth of +55%, ROE of about 25%. But its gross margin is 5.02%, down 2.03pct YoY, the most diluted of the six, and a PE of 18x has already priced in that upside.Buying Quanta means buying "higher beta", not "a better business".

Wiwynn's gross margin is 9.3%, the only double-digit figure among the six, with ROE of about 45%. But it runs a white-box direct-supply model—selling straight to CSPs, bypassing brand and channel, which naturally lifts gross margin; the cost is more concentrated customers and smaller scale (market cap HKD 55 billion), with a PE of 20x.Its high gross margin comes from customer mix, not pricing power, and a single customer order shift would break its revenue.

Hon Hai: largest scale (HKD 337 billion), highest gross margin (6.12%), lowest PE (12.6x), and the only one with global capacity.Its advantage is not that any one item is the strongest, but that all four are solid.

## Why it stands out: clients want "certainty"

The core of the AI rack business is not price but lead time and yield. When a USD 3 million rack slips a quarter, the customer loses the entire commissioning schedule of its data center. So customers rank suppliers by: can you deliver on schedule → can you ramp yield → can you back up across multiple sites → price. Price ranks fourth, which explains why Hon Hai's gross margin runs one percentage point above peers, and why it cannot easily be replaced.

But the same logic explains why it cannot earn more: price ranking fourth means the premium customers will pay has a ceiling. "Why it" and "why not buy it" have the same answer — it is the most reliable executor of this cycle, so there is no bargain to be had in its price.

## After the Final Selection: Why Still Monitor

The funnel screens out "the company most worth researching," not "the stock to buy right now." Among the six, Hon Hai has the lowest PE, but its PE is low because its margins are the thinnest and its cyclicality the strongest—the market's discount is justified, not a mispricing. Comparing its 12.6x PE with Quanta's 18x is not a comparison of cheapness but of business models.

Turning the "final pick" into a "buy" requires one more condition: an empirical inflection in gross margin. That condition does not come from peer comparison, only from its own financial reports. So the funnel's endpoint is not a conclusion but the top of the watchlist. In other words, what this funnel screens out is "the one most worth watching," not "the one most worth buying."
