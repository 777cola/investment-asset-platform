① Cognitive reset: you are not buying an Apple contract manufacturer — Hon Hai Precision

Three old assumptions, and one new trap
Research date 2026/10/19  ·  Price NT$240 (-1.84%)  ·  Market cap About TWD 3.37 trillion  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 12.6x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Most people's impression of Hon Hai stops in 2015: assembling phones for Apple, a 4% gross margin, a stock that goes nowhere. Hon Hai in 2026 is no longer that company—cloud and networking products account for more than 40% of revenue, its AI server share exceeds 40%, and single-month revenue in September broke the trillion mark. But once that perception updates, a new trap opens up: mistaking "successful transformation" for "margin improvement."

Old view 1: low gross margin means no growth

Data in 2026 overturned half of this perception. Revenue: Q3 3 trillion 269 billion, +47.1% YoY; first three quarters 7 trillion 6,698 billion, +39.5% YoY. Profit: Q2 net profit 599.7 billion, +35% YoY; EPS 4.27 yuan, +34% YoY. The growth is real, and faster than in any year of the past decade.

But the "low gross margin" half was not overturned: gross margin 6.12%, -0.21pct YoY. Revenue rose 41% while gross margin kept falling. So the correct update is: it has become a high-growth company, but not a high-profit company. The two must be recorded separately, otherwise 12.6x PE gets misjudged as a "cheap high-growth" stock.

Old perception 2: 70% of revenue comes from Apple

That was true before 2023. The approximate structure in 2026Q2: cloud networking about 45%, consumer intelligence about 28%, computer terminals about 12%, components and others about 15% (rough estimate). Apple-related phone assembly has fallen from "lifeline" to "base business."

The significance of this shift lies not in diversifying risk but in a change in the valuation logic: phone assembly is stable low growth, AI racks are high growth with high volatility. The market is willing to assign a higher multiple to the latter, so Hon Hai's PE has risen from 8–10x to 12–13x. The re-rating comes from the business mix, not from profitability — keep this clearly in mind.

Old perception 3: pure assembly, no technology

That is also half right. Full-rack assembly itself is becoming more technically demanding: liquid-cooling plumbing, power architecture, rack-level testing, yield control — these are not "turning screws." Hon Hai's gross margin is 1.1 percentage points higher than Quanta's and 1.5 percentage points higher than Compal's, and the gap comes from these in-house components and integration capabilities. But "having technology" is not the same as "having pricing power" — these are two different things.

Technically, it can build full racks that others cannot; commercially, it can only charge the processing fee customers are willing to pay. TSMC also has technology, but TSMC's customers have no second option; Hon Hai's customers always have a second option. Technical barriers determine "whether you can do it"; market structure determines "how much you can charge."

The new trap: mistaking a successful transition for improved profitability

The most misread statement about 2026 is that "Hon Hai's AI transformation succeeded." That statement is correct, but it answers "where growth comes from," not "where profit comes from." The mark of a successful transformation is a changed revenue mix; the mark of improved profitability is a changed gross margin—the former has happened, the latter has not.

More dangerous is the valuation: the successful transformation leads the market to pay 12.6x, and the denominator of that 12.6x is peak-cycle EPS. If capex decelerates in 2027 and EPS falls back to 15, the same 12.6x becomes 16x normalized PE. The correct endpoint of the cognitive reset is not "this is a good company," but "this is a good company, but the price of a good company has already been paid."

Comparison: Hon Hai in 2020 vs. Hon Hai in 2026

2020: revenue about NTD 5.3 trillion, gross margin about 5.6%, cloud and networking under 30% of sales, PE about 10x, share price around NTD 80. 2026: first three quarters revenue NTD 7.67 trillion, gross margin 6.12%, cloud and networking above 40%, PE 12.6x, share price NTD 239.5. Over six years, revenue +45%, gross margin +0.5pct, valuation +26%, and the share price tripled.

Breaking down the rally: half came from EPS growth (NTD 12.09 vs just over NTD 7 in 2020), half from re-rating (10x to 12.6x). The re-rating is what the market paid for the new identity as an AI server supplier. Whether that premium holds depends on whether the new identity lifts gross margin — so far it has not.

One more number to calibrate: single-month revenue in September 2026 was NTD 1,158.6 billion, the first company in Taiwan to break NTD 1 trillion in a month. That record is about scale, not profit — in the same month, gross margin was heading down. Put the two together and you have the company's entire 2026 story.

Conclusion

The conclusion of this piece: of Hon Hai's three old labels, two should be torn off (Apple dependence, pure assembly), and one should stay (low gross margin). The transformation is real; the profit improvement is not. Read on with this in mind, and the 47% revenue growth will not lead you astray. Reading the full series reveals that this insight is the foundation for the next seven pieces: without tearing off the old labels, you cannot see the real problems; tear off the labels without forming new judgments, and you will mistake it for a growth stock at 239.
