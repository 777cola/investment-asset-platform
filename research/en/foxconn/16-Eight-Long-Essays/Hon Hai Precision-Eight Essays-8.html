⑧ Decision framework: three prices, two signals, one veto — Hon Hai Precision

Turn judgment into executable rules

Core view

The previous seven pieces covered "what this is"; this one covers "how to do it". There are only three rules: three prices determine position size, two signals determine adding, and one veto determines exit. I am writing them down so I don't have to agonise over it again next time—the market manufactures new stories every day, and rules are the only thing that can stand up to stories.

Three prices

RMB 239.5 (current price): 0 position. 12.6x 2026E P/E, 33% downside, 17% upside, expected return +3%. At this price you are buying "certainty", not "cheapness".

RMB 180: 1%–2%. This equates to 10x 2027E EPS of RMB 18 (a 20% haircut to the neutral case of 22.5) and PB of about 1.15x, close to the two cycle lows of 2019 and 2022. RMB 160: 3%–4%. This equates to 8x and PB of about 1.0x, close to capacity replacement cost. The difference between these two levels is not "cheaper", it is "downside shrinking from -11% to -5%".

Two signals

Signal one: gross margin at or above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters. This is the only reason to build a position without looking at the price — it changes the denominator (EPS revised up to RMB 26), not sentiment. Why two consecutive quarters? Management has twice guided to "improvement in the second half" without delivering, and a single quarter may reflect a one-off mix shift.

Signal two: free cash flow turns positive. That would prove growth no longer consumes cash — evidence the business can feed itself. If both signals appear together, the position cap can be raised to 5%; if only one appears, the cap is 2%.

One veto

Veto condition: gross margin below 5.8% for two consecutive quarters. That would signal structural dilution from the full-container model running ahead of expectations, forcing a cut in the valuation centre from 12.5x to 10x — on 2027E EPS of RMB 20, fair value is RMB 200, so the current price is expensive. A trigger means a full exit, no watchlist, wait for the next cycle.

Secondary vetoes: quarterly revenue turns negative QoQ without a guidance cut (order visibility breaks), or receivables days rise more than 15 days YoY (customer credit deteriorates). Any one of the three triggers, and rules take precedence over judgment.

Execution discipline: three things we do not do

First, don't trade the revenue headline. The stock fell 1.8% on the very day it crossed the trillion mark in September — the clearest lesson that revenue reflects orders, not profit. Trading on revenue news is trading on your customers' purchase orders.

Second, don't hold it alongside TSMC and Quanta. With correlation above 0.8, that is the same wager twice. If the portfolio already holds AI upstream, Hon Hai can only be a small position for "midstream elasticity," not a diversification tool.

Third, don't build a position the day before the earnings call to bet on gross margin. Trading at an information disadvantage offers a small edge and poor odds.

The limits of this framework

The framework assumes gross margin is the only variable. Should a new variable emerge in 2027—Hon Hai winning an exclusive full-rack design for a customer, say, or setting the liquid-cooling standard—the framework must change. Rules exist to handle the normal case, not to reject exceptions; when an exception appears, change the rules first, then the position.

Time is another boundary: the framework yields a 12-month target price, not a ten-year one. Over ten years Hon Hai will remain the largest contract manufacturer, but gross margin still sets its valuation ceiling. Short term, watch price; long term, watch gross margin—the two do not conflict.

When should this framework be revisited?

Three points in time warrant a re-read: ① after every earnings call (gross margin, capacity utilisation, capex guidance); ② after every cloud vendor earnings season (capex guidance underpins every assumption); ③ when a stock moves more than 7% in a single day (first separate sentiment from information).

A re-read is not a re-judgment but a reconciliation: set the assumptions made then alongside the evidence now, and see which held up and which were overturned. The hardest part of investing is not judging, but remembering why you judged as you did. A written framework exists to fight forgetting.

Conclusion

Decision framework: 0 position at the current price; 1%–2% at RMB 180; 3%–4% at RMB 160. Build 1% without waiting on price if gross margin holds at 6.5% for two quarters or FCF turns positive; exit fully if gross margin stays below 5.8% for two quarters. Fix the rules and leave the rest to the earnings reports. One last reminder: rules are set for yourself, not for the market. The market will not change course because of your rules, but you can make fewer mistakes because of them.
