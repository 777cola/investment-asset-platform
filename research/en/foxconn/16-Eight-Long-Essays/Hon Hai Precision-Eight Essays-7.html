⑦ Risk list: five tail risks, ranked by probability and severity — Hon Hai Precision

Which will come first

Core view

Rank risks on "probability × damage," and five tails at Hon Hai are worth writing down: cloud capex deceleration (high probability, high damage), upstream price increases (already happening, medium damage), idle US capacity (medium probability, high damage), customers disintermediating and self-sourcing (low probability, extreme damage), and FX and tariffs (high probability, low damage). Of the five, the first two are already playing out; the last three are 2027 matters.

Risk 1: Hyperscaler capex decelerates (high probability)

This is the biggest risk. Hon Hai's orders are driven by capex from five North American CSPs, and 2026 marks the fourth and most aggressive year of that spend. If any one of them cuts guidance, Hon Hai's revenue growth falls from +47% to +10%. The bigger problem is EPS: a 6% gross margin leaves no buffer, and a 30-percentage-point drop in revenue growth could cut EPS by 40%.

This risk cannot be controlled or predicted; it can only be hedged through price. Buying at 12.6x offers no defense; buying at 8x means the defense is already priced in.

Risk 2: Upstream price increases (already underway)

2026 DRAM contract prices are up 60%–70% and NAND 100%, with the HBM undersupply expected to persist into 2027. These costs pass straight through the foundries: revenue grows, gross margin shrinks. Gross margin fell from 6.33% to 6.12%, and half the reason sits here.

What defines this risk is that it does not kill you in one blow; it grinds you down continuously. It will not push Hon Hai into a loss — it merely leaves its margin permanently 0.2pp short, and a 0.2pp valuation gap is 20% of the share price.

Risk 3: US capacity sits idle (medium probability)

Capacity expansion in Texas and Wisconsin is asset-heavy — plants, equipment, labour — and fixed costs land first. If orders fail to follow and utilisation drops below 70%, the 3.75% operating margin will not hold. Labour costs at the US plants run several times those in Vietnam, so assembly fees for the same rack cabinets must be higher in the US; whether customers will pay that premium will be answered in 2027.

Track utilisation commentary on the earnings call and the ramp schedule at the US plants. Any mention of "delayed production" should be treated as a signal to trim the position.

Risk 4: Customers bypass distributors and buy direct (low probability, severe damage)

If cloud providers standardise rack-level integration—sourcing components and assembling racks in-house—ODMs would be demoted from "system integrators" back to "assemblers," and even a 6% gross margin would not survive. That risk still looks distant, since rack-level yield and delivery complexity still require specialist manufacturers, but the direction is clear.

There is only one hedge: keep deepening in-house component production and integration capability until "disassembling the rack" no longer pays. Foxconn is doing this, but not quickly.

Risk 5: FX and tariffs (high probability, low impact)

Every 1 yuan appreciation in the New Taiwan dollar shaves 0.1–0.2pct off gross margin on our rough calculation, and a stronger New Taiwan dollar in 2026 is part of the reason for the gross margin decline. Tariffs cut both ways: customers demand multi-site backup, which benefits Hon Hai, but Hon Hai bears the fixed costs of dispersed capacity.

This risk alone is not fatal, but it compounds with the other four. On a 2.37% net margin, any 0.2pct swing is 8% of profit.

Probability-weighted: combined value of the five tails

Rough probability-and-damage weighting of the five tail risks: capex slowdown (40% probability, EPS -30%), sustained upstream price increases (70%, EPS -8%), idle US capacity (25%, EPS -15%), cabinet teardown and self-sourcing (10%, EPS -40%), FX and tariffs (60%, EPS -5%). The weighted EPS impact is about -17%, taking the fair value from RMB 280 to RMB 232.

That is slightly below the current price of RMB 239.5. In other words, with all five tails priced in, the current price is not "a bit expensive" — it is "slightly expensive." This is the quantitative basis for "watch" rather than "avoid" — the gap is small, but the direction is wrong.

One more item not on the list: mechanical selling by index and passive funds. Weight adjustments for Taiwan equity heavyweights and rebalancing by foreign passive funds can cause 3%–5% swings when fundamentals have not changed. Such swings are noise, but they can push the price to levels where the rules trigger — good news for anyone waiting for RMB 180.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: Of the five tail risks, two are already unfolding (expectations of capex deceleration, upstream price increases) and three are 2027 events. None is a black swan; all are gray rhinos—visible, avoidable, but requiring price protection. That is why we would rather wait for RMB 180 than "buy a little" at RMB 239. None of the five tails needs a black swan to trigger it; a normal cyclical downturn is enough. Price protection therefore matters more than judgment—judgment can be wrong, price cannot.
