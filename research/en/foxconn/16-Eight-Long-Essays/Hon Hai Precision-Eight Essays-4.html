④ Financial reality: the profit is real, the cash is not — Hon Hai Precision

Three contradictions buried in the three financial statements

Core view

The report's authenticity is not in doubt: monthly revenue ties exactly to the quarterly total, gross margin reconciles in both directions, and the two foreign brokers' EPS estimates differ by just RMB 0.02. The issue is thickness, not authenticity. Read the three statements together and three contradictions emerge, each pointing to the same conclusion.

Contradiction one: revenue accelerates while gross margin declines

Q3 revenue rose 47.1% YoY, ahead of the 41% delivered in Q2, yet gross margin slipped from 6.33% in Q2 2025 to 6.12%. Growth and margin moving in opposite directions signals deteriorating quality of growth — each additional dollar of revenue leaves a smaller share behind.

The driver is a richer mix of full-rack systems: on a USD 3 million rack the assembly fee is USD 20,000–30,000, and once fully recognised, materials dilute gross margin. The larger the scale, the heavier the dilution. This is not a short-term fluctuation; it is the mathematics of the product mix.

Contradiction 2: operating profit +68% versus net profit up just +35%

Operating profit came in at RMB 94.8 billion, up 68% YoY, while net profit was RMB 59.97 billion, up just 35%. Where did the gap come from? First, interest expense: both advance-funded procurement and capacity expansion across four sites require borrowing. Second, minority interests: subsidiaries such as Foxconn Industrial Internet are not wholly owned, so profit must be shared out. Funding costs and minority shareholders absorbed part of the benefit created by scale leverage.

That gap will widen: the heavier the capex, the higher the interest expense; the more the AI business concentrates in Foxconn Industrial Internet, the more minority interests take. Net profit attributable to shareholders will keep growing more slowly than operating profit — this is structural.

Contradiction 3: profit growth with negative cash flow

Free cash flow was negative in the first half. The income statement cheers (net profit +35%); the cash flow statement sounds the alarm, with working capital and capex both expanding. For a company running a 2.37% net margin, this is no small matter—it means growth has to be funded with borrowed money.

Buffett has a test: operating cash flow / net profit persistently below 1 means receivables and inventory are embedded in profit. Hon Hai in the first half of 2026 most likely sits between 0.6 and 0.8 (rough estimate). If this ratio does not improve, "profit growth" means nothing to shareholders.

An easily overlooked line item: depreciation

Equipment from the 2026 capacity expansions at the four sites will be transferred to fixed assets in 2027–2029 and amortised over 5–8 years. If order growth slows in 2027 while depreciation runs on schedule, gross margin must cede a further 0.2–0.3pct on top of 6.12%. This year's capex is the fixed cost of three years from now.

Read this earnings report with that lag in mind: 2026 is "the best-looking report of the expansion phase"; the bill only comes due in 2027. Whether management's talk of "product mix improvement" is real cannot be verified until Q1 and Q2 of 2027.

Side by side: the difference between a 50% gross margin and a 6% gross margin

TSMC's 2026 gross margin is around 50%+, versus 6.12% at Hon Hai. Both are capital-intensive, both depend on capacity, both serve global customers; the only difference is whether customers have a second choice. Those 44 percentage points between 50% and 6% are the price of pricing power.

The gap shows up in three places in the financials: TSMC funds capex from its own cash, Hon Hai has to borrow; TSMC's depreciation runs at 15% of revenue yet its gross margin holds, while Hon Hai's depreciation at 3% of revenue is already enough to drag its gross margin down; TSMC's 8% R&D expense ratio is a moat, Hon Hai's 1.5% is a cost.Same income statement, but different gross margins determine what each line means.

One further accounting discipline: every figure in this report at the "RMB billion" level can be traced to filings or reports, except that cash flow statement amounts were not broken out separately at the earnings call. So wherever this series touches cash flow, it gives direction only, not amounts—better to be one number short than to make one up.

Conclusion

Financial truth: the profit is real, the cash is not, and depreciation is deferred. What 12.6x 2026E P/E buys is a business "swapping balance sheet for revenue." The next earnings report hinges on just two numbers: gross margin and working capital tied up. Behind the three contradictions lies one thing: customers set revenue, upstream sets profit, and Hon Hai merely turns over inventory in between. Turnover can produce an income statement, but not free cash flow.
