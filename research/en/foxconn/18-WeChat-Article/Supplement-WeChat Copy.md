## Taiwan's first company to top NT$1trn in a single month, yet the stock is falling

Hon Hai reported September revenue on October 5 of TWD 1 trillion 158.6 billion, up 38.4% YoY — the first Taiwanese company to break the trillion mark in a single month. The stock fell 1.8% that same day.

The broader trend is just as strong: Q3 revenue of TWD 3 trillion 26.9 billion, up 47.1% YoY, and TWD 7 trillion 669.8 billion for the first three quarters, up 39.5% YoY. Each number outdoes the last. Yet the share price has fallen all the way from its July high of TWD 309 to TWD 239.5, a 22.5% drawdown.

When good news and bad news land together, the market is usually looking elsewhere. Here, that something is gross margin.

## 6.12%: the entire secret of this business

Hon Hai reported 2026Q2 revenue of NT$2 trillion 525.9 billion, gross profit of NT$154.5 billion and a gross margin of 6.12%. Operating profit came to NT$94.8 billion after opex, and net profit attributable to the parent reached NT$59.97 billion after tax and minorities, a net margin of 2.37%.

In plain terms: for every 100 dollars of business done, 94 goes to buying materials, and 2.4 ends up in shareholders' pockets.

This is not for lack of effort. An NVL72 rack sells for USD 3 million, and its most expensive components — Nvidia's GPUs, SK hynix and Samsung's HBM, and Micron's memory — are all customer-specified and priced at market. Hon Hai's job is to assemble them into a working system and charge a processing fee of roughly USD 20,000 to 30,000 per rack.

Revenue is therefore inflated by components while profit is not. Revenue rose 41%, yet gross margin fell 0.21 percentage points. In 2026, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%, and every increase passed straight through Hon Hai's income statement, leaving the gains upstream.

One more comparison: on the same rack, Nvidia's chip gross margin is 70% against Hon Hai's 6% assembly gross margin. A gap of more than ten times comes not from who is smarter, but from who has a choice.

## An overlooked piece of good news: costs are coming to the rescue

Look beyond the negatives. Revenue rose 41% on operating expense growth of just 6%, lifting operating margin from 3.16% to 3.75% and operating profit 68% YoY — 27 percentage points ahead of revenue.

That reflects depreciation and administrative costs spread thinner as utilisation reached full capacity, and it is genuine execution. But the lever is nearly exhausted: the expense ratio is already down to 2.36%, and further savings alone cannot drive a re-rating.

The verdict on this report: scale buys profit, expenses rescue gross margin. The revenue is real, the efficiency is real, and so is the thin profit.

## 12.6x PE — is that cheap?

At RMB 239.5, market cap is RMB 3.37 trillion, or 12.6x 2026E EPS of RMB 19.06 and 10.6x 2027E EPS of RMB 22.5 — cheaper than Quanta at 18x and Wiwynn at 20x.

But cheapness depends on the denominator. This EPS is peak-cycle EPS. If cloud capex decelerates in 2027 and EPS falls back to RMB 15, RMB 239.5 is 16x — not cheap at all.

Three scenarios: bear case RMB 160 (EPS RMB 15 × 10x), base case RMB 280 (RMB 22.5 × 12.5x), bull case RMB 380 (RMB 25.3 × 15x). Weighted at 40%, 45% and 15%, the expected value is RMB 247, 3% above the current price.

Take on 33% downside for 3% expected return. Those odds are not worth acting on.

## Who is buying, who is selling

Foreign investors are Hon Hai's largest marginal buyer, holding about 40%. In the first half of 2026, foreign investors kept adding, driving the stock to a July high of 309. After the August earnings call they turned net sellers, offloading over 100,000 lots in two months. This is not a bearish bet on the company; it is a bearish bet on the combination of a 6% gross margin and a 12x PE.

Domestic investment trusts and retail investors absorbed a good deal in the 220–240 range. Margin balances are rising, a sign some are betting on a rebound. Foreign selling into domestic buying typically means the stock needs time to digest, not a single bullish candle to reverse.

## One more thing: cash

AI racks carry high unit prices and require 8 to 12 weeks of material preparation, so every extra dollar of revenue means fronting materials, receivables and inventory first. Free cash flow was negative in the first half.

For a company running a 2.37% net margin, that is no small matter: growth has to be funded by borrowing. Profit can be manufactured through turnover; cash depends on customers paying on time. Hon Hai has delivered the former. The latter rests with its customers—and on whether they have the money and the willingness to pay on schedule.

## So, when to buy

All that unpleasantness said, I owe you an actionable answer. Two conditions, and I would revisit only if either is met:

- The stock falls below RMB 180. That is 10x 2027 EPS of RMB 18 and about 1.15x PB, close to the two cycle lows of 2019 and 2022. By then the bear case is largely priced in.

- Gross margin holds above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters. That would show the product mix has genuinely improved and the denominator is revised up, making even RMB 239 cheap.

Until either condition holds, revenue breaking the trillion mark a few more times is no reason to buy. Because gross margin, not revenue, sets the valuation ceiling on this business.

One fair point in closing: Hon Hai executes better than any company in this AI buildout, with over 40% global AI server share, and it is the only one that can deliver simultaneously from Mexico, Vietnam, India and the US. It deserves respect; it just does not deserve to be chased at RMB 239.

Good company, bad business, fair price, wrong odds. That is all.
