## Taiwan's first company to top a trillion in a single month, yet its stock is falling

On October 5, Hon Hai reported September revenue: TWD 1 trillion 158.6 billion, up 38.4% year on year. This is the first Taiwanese company to break the trillion mark in single-month revenue. That same day, the stock fell 1.8%.

Looking further back, Q3 revenue was TWD 3 trillion 26.9 billion, up 47.1% year on year, and revenue for the first three quarters was TWD 7 trillion 669.8 billion, up 39.5% year on year. Each number is prettier than the last. Yet the share price fell all the way from its July high of TWD 309 to TWD 239.5, a drawdown of 22.5%.

Good news and bad news arriving at the same time usually means the market is looking at something else. That something is gross margin.

## 6.12%: the entire secret of this business

In the second quarter of 2026, Hon Hai's revenue was NT$2 trillion 525.9 billion, gross profit NT$154.5 billion, gross margin 6.12%. After operating expenses, operating profit was NT$94.8 billion; after tax and minority interests, net profit attributable to the parent was NT$59.97 billion, net margin 2.37%.

In plain terms: for every 100 dollars of business done, 94 goes to buying materials, and 2.4 ends up in shareholders' pockets.

This is not for lack of effort. An NVL72 rack sells for USD 3 million, and the most expensive parts inside are Nvidia's GPUs, SK hynix and Samsung's HBM, and Micron's memory — all customer-specified and priced at market. Hon Hai's job is to assemble them into a working system and charge a processing fee, roughly USD 20,000 to 30,000 per rack.

So revenue is inflated by components while profit is not. Revenue rose 41%, yet gross margin fell 0.21 percentage points. In 2026, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%, and all of those increases passed straight through Hon Hai's income statement, leaving the gains upstream.

One more comparison: for the same rack, Nvidia's chip gross margin is 70%, while Hon Hai's assembly gross margin is 6%. A gap of more than ten times comes not from who is smarter, but from who has a choice.

## An overlooked piece of good news: costs are coming to the rescue

Don't look only at the bad side. Revenue rose 41% while operating expenses rose just 6%, the operating margin climbed from 3.16% to 3.75%, and operating profit grew 68% year on year — 27 percentage points faster than revenue.

This is the result of depreciation and administrative expenses being spread thinner once capacity utilization hit full, and it is real skill. But it is nearly exhausted: the expense ratio is already down to 2.36%, and no further savings can deliver a re-rating.

So the verdict on this report: scale buys profit, expenses rescue gross margin. The revenue is real, the efficiency is real, and so is the thin profit.

## 12.6x PE — is that cheap?

RMB 239.5, market cap RMB 3.37 trillion, 2026E EPS RMB 19.06, PE 12.6x; 2027E RMB 22.5, 10.6x. Within the foundry sector, that is cheaper than Quanta's 18x and Wiwynn's 20x.

But cheapness depends on what the denominator is. This EPS is peak-cycle EPS. If cloud capex decelerates in 2027 and EPS falls back to RMB 15, then RMB 239.5 is 16x — not cheap at all.

Three scenarios: bear case RMB 160 (EPS RMB 15 × 10x), base case RMB 280 (RMB 22.5 × 12.5x), bull case RMB 380 (RMB 25.3 × 15x). Weighted at 40%, 45% and 15%, the expected value is RMB 247, 3% above the current price.

Take on 33% downside for 3% expected return. Those odds are not worth acting on.

## Who is buying, who is selling

Foreign investors are Hon Hai's largest marginal buyer, holding about 40%. In the first half of 2026, foreign investors kept adding, driving the stock to a July high of 309. After the August earnings call they turned net sellers, offloading over 100,000 lots in two months. This is not a bearish bet on the company; it is a bearish bet on the combination of a 6% gross margin and a 12x PE.

Domestic investment trusts and retail investors absorbed a good deal in the 220–240 range. Margin balances are rising, a sign some are betting on a rebound. Foreign selling into domestic buying typically means the stock needs time to digest, not a single bullish candle to reverse.

## One more thing: cash

AI racks carry high unit prices and require 8 to 12 weeks of material preparation, so every extra dollar of revenue means fronting materials, receivables and inventory first. Free cash flow was negative in the first half.

For a company with a 2.37% net margin, this is no small matter—it means growth has to be funded by borrowing. Profit can be manufactured through turnover; cash depends on customers paying on time. Hon Hai has delivered the former; the latter depends on customers—and on whether those customers have the money and the willingness to pay on schedule.

## So, when to buy

Having said all that unpleasantness, I owe you an actionable answer. Two conditions; revisit only if either is met:

- The stock falls below RMB 180. That corresponds to 10x 2027 EPS of RMB 18 and about 1.15x PB, close to the two cycle lows of 2019 and 2022. By then the bear case is largely priced in.

- Gross margin holds above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters. That would show the product mix has genuinely improved and the denominator is revised up, making even RMB 239 cheap.

Until either condition holds, revenue breaking the trillion mark a few more times is no reason to buy. Because the valuation ceiling on this business is set by gross margin, not by revenue.

One fair point in closing: Hon Hai has the strongest execution of any company in this AI buildout, with over 40% global AI server share, and it is the only one that can deliver simultaneously from Mexico, Vietnam, India and the US. It deserves respect; it just does not deserve to be chased at RMB 239.

Good company, bad business, fair price, wrong odds. That is all.
