Core call: the market is pricing gross margin; everything else is noise

Seven notable events hit Hon Hai between July and October. Three carried signal: Q2 earnings call gross margin of 6.12% missed expectations (-4.5% that day); the US capacity plan announcement triggered capex concerns (-2.0%); and September revenue broke the trillion mark yet the stock fell (-1.8%, good news fully priced). Four were noise: TrendForce raised its 2027 rack output value forecast (+2.8%), Apple's new product launch (+0.5%), June revenue met target (-1.5%), and foreign brokerage reports made minor adjustments. Classify the 7 events by whether they change gross margin or cash flow, and the answer is immediately clear: the market has spent these three months doing just one thing—repricing a 6% gross margin.

First, split the seven items into two categories

Only one classification criterion matters: whether an event changes expectations for "gross margin" or "free cash flow." What changes them is signal; what does not is noise. By this standard, only 3 of the 7 items are signal, and all three point in the same direction—margins are deteriorating.

The four noise items are noise because they change "scale" without changing "unit profit": revenue meeting targets, the upward revision to industry output value, the new Apple model, and minor foreign broker target-price tweaks. Scale contributes almost nothing to Hon Hai's valuation, because its valuation ceiling is set by gross margin (see Part 4). The market responded to the upward revision in industry output value with a 2.8% gain, and to the 0.21 percentage point decline in gross margin with a 4.5% drop—this asymmetry is itself information.

Signal one: that 4.5% drop on the earnings call

Revenue, operating profit and EPS all beat consensus on the August 12 earnings call, yet the stock fell 4.5%. The market is looking past Q2, which is already history, and focusing on three implicit assumptions: ① whether Q3 gross margin can stop falling; ② what will fill the gap to full-year EPS of 19; ③ how much further capex will rise. Of these three questions, the first determines the other two.

Management spoke of "product mix improvement in the second half" but gave no gross margin guidance. The selloff was the market's way of expressing distrust of the word "improvement" — after all, the same thing was said in Q1 2026, and Q2 gross margin still fell 0.06 percentage points. This is a textbook information-deficit decline, not a collapse in fundamentals.

Signal two: capex is being recalculated

The US Texas capacity plan, announced on September 22, sent the stock down 2.0%. The logic is straightforward: spreading expansion across four locations lifts depreciation from 2027, with no sign of gross margin improvement. The market is starting to reclassify "expansion" from a growth story to a cost story.

That shift matters. In 2024–2025 every Hon Hai expansion announcement lifted the stock; from the second half of 2026, the same news sends it lower. The dividing line was the Q2 results — once gross margin was shown not to improve with scale, capex turned from "investment" into "burden".

Signal three: the -1.8% move on the day it broke the trillion mark

September revenue, reported on October 5, came in at NT$1 trillion 158.6 billion — the first single month above NT$1 trillion for any Taiwanese company — and the stock fell 1.8%. That day deserves recording, because it put "all good news priced in" right on the face of it. Breaking NT$1 trillion was the brightest number in the company's history, and the market's response was to sell.

The reason is not complicated: the trillion mark was already flagged in the Q3 outlook at the August earnings call ("double-digit quarter-on-quarter growth"), and October 5 merely confirmed it. Meanwhile, news of DRAM and NAND price increases kept coming, and the market's worry was "the bigger the revenue, the lower the gross margin." When revenue becomes a negative signal, it shows pricing power has shifted entirely to the gross margin line.

Why noise is noise: three counterexamples

TrendForce raised its 2027 rack output value forecast by +214%, and the stock rose 2.8%. For Hon Hai that number cuts both ways: orders are still there, but so is tighter component supply and steeper price increases—negative for gross margin. The market only read the first half of the sentence.

Apple unveiled the iPhone 18, with Hon Hai as the largest assembler, and the stock rose 0.5%. Handsets will account for less than 30% of revenue in 2026 and carry a lower gross margin than cloud networking; the segment is no longer a stock-price driver. This is a classic "news-relevance illusion."

June revenue hitting target and foreign brokers nudging target prices are both repricing of known information, not a change in the long-term thesis. There is only one kind of real signal: the few numbers that can change 2027 EPS—gross margin, capex and customer mix.

Conclusion

7 events in 3 months, and the market is really saying just one thing: a 6.12% gross margin does not justify a 12.6x PE. All three signals point to margin; all four noise items are about scale. The implication for investors: do not trade on revenue headlines—watch the gross margin at the November Q3 earnings call. That is the answer to all the declines of the past three months, and the precondition for all the gains to come.
