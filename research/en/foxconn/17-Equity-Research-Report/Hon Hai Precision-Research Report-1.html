Investment highlights

Hon Hai is the most direct beneficiary of global AI rack ramp-up: 2026Q3 revenue reached TWD 3.03 trillion, up 47.1% YoY, with September alone surpassing TWD 1 trillion for the first time, and AI server market share above 40%. But its profit does not scale in step with revenue — gross margin 6.12%, net margin 2.37%; revenue doubled while profit rose only 35%. The current TWD 239.5 corresponds to 12.6x 2026E PE and 10.6x 2027E PE — the valuation is not expensive, but the denominator is cycle-peak EPS. Three-scenario target prices of TWD 160/280/380, probability-weighted TWD 247, just +3% versus the current price. Initiate at "Watch," awaiting a gross-margin inflection or a price below TWD 180.

Chapter 1 Company Overview

Hon Hai Precision (2317.TW) was founded by Terry Gou in 1974 and listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange in 1991. It is the world's largest electronics manufacturing services (EMS) provider. Market capitalization was about TWD 3.37 trillion in 2026, ranking among the top three weighted stocks on the Taiwan market. The business spans four segments: cloud and networking (AI servers, racks, networking), consumer intelligence (phones, wearables), computer terminals, and components and others (connectors, PCB, mechanical parts, optics).

2026Q2 revenue was TWD 2 trillion 5,259 billion, net profit after tax TWD 599.7 billion, and EPS TWD 4.27. The company employs hundreds of thousands of people, with capacity in Mexico, Vietnam, India, the United States, Taiwan and mainland China. In July 2019, Young Liu succeeded as chairman, and decision-making shifted to a nine-member management committee.

Ownership and governance: the company has no controlling shareholder. Terry Gou is the largest individual shareholder (about 10%), and foreign investors hold about 40%, one of the highest foreign ownership ratios among large-cap weighted stocks in Taiwan. The board has functional committees including audit and compensation, while day-to-day operations are run by the nine-member management committee and the general managers of each business group. 2025 revenue was about TWD 7.6 trillion (back-calculated from the year-on-year growth rate for the first three quarters of 2026); the first three quarters of 2026 already reached TWD 7.67 trillion, and full-year revenue will exceed TWD 10 trillion for the first time.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

The AI server market entered an upcycle in capex from 2023, and 2026 is the fourth and most aggressive year. The four major North American cloud providers have sharply raised capex, and rack-scale solutions (NVL72 and successor platforms) have become mainstream, with per-rack ASP of about USD 3 million, more than ten times that of general-purpose servers. TrendForce expects NVL72 rack revenue to grow another 214% in 2027, driven by a doubling of ASP and rising prices for upstream wafers and HBM.

The competitive structure pits a buyer oligopoly against a seller oligopoly: five downstream CSPs account for about 70% of rack-scale procurement (falling to about 60% in 2026), while upstream GPU and HBM supply is highly concentrated. Contract manufacturing involves Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Wiwynn, Inventec, and overseas players Celestica and Jabil, with gross margins in the 4%–6% range.Industry growth is certain, but profit allocation disadvantages contract manufacturers—this is the core judgment of this chapter.

On demand structure, 2026 incremental demand comes mainly from two categories: training clusters and inference clusters. Training clusters use rack-scale systems (NVL72 and similar solutions), while inference clusters use general-purpose servers plus a small number of racks. The rising rack-scale share is the fundamental reason for the doubling of ASP and for contract manufacturers' revenue growth far outpacing shipment growth.After the switch to the VR200 platform in 2027, per-rack ASP is expected to step up again, but this is an industry projection with no verifiable order data yet.

Taking a longer view: AI servers accounted for less than 10% of total server shipments in 2023 and have exceeded 40% in 2026 (by value). The pace of penetration gains is the hardest support for this cycle and the variable most in need of reassessment after 2027—further penetration gains require application deployment, not capex rhetoric.

Chapter 3 Business model

Hon Hai's model is buy-and-sell: it procures components for customers, assembles, tests and delivers, charging a processing fee per cabinet. The boundary between risk and reward is clear — component prices are negotiated between the customer and upstream suppliers, while Hon Hai bears the procurement funding and inventory risk, earning a fixed processing fee plus the margin on self-made parts.

Vertical integration is its differentiation: connectors, mechanical parts, PCB, power, thermal and cabinets are all done in-house, with self-made parts carrying a 15%–25% gross margin and lifting overall gross margin by about 1 percentage point. This is what makes its gross margin higher than peers, but it also defines the ceiling — self-made parts account for less than 10% of a cabinet's value, so the weighted contribution is limited. The essence of this business is "trading balance sheet for revenue"; growth inevitably consumes working capital and capex.

There are three quoting models, which directly determine the gross margin level: buy-and-sell (full revenue recognition, high revenue, low gross margin), consignment (customer supplies materials, only the processing fee is recognized, low revenue, high gross margin) and pure contract manufacturing (process steps only). Hon Hai is mainly buy-and-sell, so its 6.12% is not directly comparable with Wiwynn's 9.3% under the white-box model. The company does not disclose the revenue mix across the three models, which is a blind spot in financial statement analysis.

The cash flow characteristics also need to be spelled out: under buy-and-sell, Hon Hai pays for components first and collects payment later, a 60–90 day gap. When revenue doubles, this funding outlay doubles too — the direct reason free cash flow was negative in the first half, and a dead knot this type of model cannot untie by "improving turnover".

Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

2026Q2: revenue 2,525.9 billion, gross profit 154.5 billion (6.12%), operating profit 94.8 billion (3.75%), net profit 60.0 billion (2.37%), EPS 4.27 yuan. Gross margin -0.21pct YoY, down for two consecutive quarters; operating margin +0.6pct YoY as expense leverage emerges; net margin broadly flat.

Cash flow is the weak spot: free cash flow was negative in the first half as both working capital and capex expanded. The balance-sheet structure remains stable (net debt ratio about 15%, interest coverage about 10x, rough calculation), but assets are getting heavier, and depreciation will weigh on gross margin from 2027.ROE is about 9%–12%, driven by turnover rather than margin, with asset turnover of 1.4x and equity multiplier of 2.4x (rough calculation).

The quarterly trend warrants attention: revenue rose from 2,119.5 billion in 2026Q1 to 2,525.9 billion in Q2 and 3,026.9 billion in Q3, accelerating each quarter; gross margin went from 6.18% to 6.12%, declining each quarter.The two curves move in opposite directions, showing that scale expansion has not brought margin improvement.In addition, the growth gap between operating profit and net profit (+68% vs +35%) stems mainly from interest expense and minority interests, both of which will widen as capex and the AI business take a larger share.

Chapter 5 Management

Liu Young-way took over in 2019 and completed three things in six years: building cloud networking into the largest division, turning Foxconn Industrial Internet into an independent AI platform, and using a four-region capacity footprint to hedge tariffs. Execution is top-tier for an EMS provider, and his transformation call led the industry by two years.

Capital allocation is the weak spot: the 2016 Sharp acquisition (JPY 388.8 billion) delivered mediocre financial returns, and the 2022 Lordstown plant (about USD 230 million) became a failure case. Dividend payout is about 55%, yield under 3%, with expansion taking priority over shareholder returns. On governance, the nine-member committee is real progress; Terry Gou remains the largest individual shareholder (about 10%). The succession mechanism is in place but the successor is undecided — a medium-term uncertainty.

On incentives, Taiwan-listed companies disclose director and supervisor compensation only by bracket, so individual pay is unavailable; employee bonuses are tied to performance, but the company has not disclosed terms linked to return on invested capital (ROIC). This means management incentives naturally favor "growing revenue" and are not fully aligned with shareholders' preference for "thickening profit." This is a common problem in EMS, not unique to Hon Hai, but it affects the priority given to capital allocation.

Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three drivers, in decreasing order of strength. First, AI rack shipments: multiple growth in 2026, with VR200 taking over in 2027 and order visibility extending to 2027 — this is the hardest driver and the sole reason for +47% revenue in 2026.

Second, a rising in-house component ratio: extending from connectors and mechanical parts into power modules and liquid-cooling CDUs. Each 1 percentage point increase in the in-house ratio lifts gross margin by roughly 0.05–0.08pct (rough estimate), and it will take three to five years to accumulate 0.4pct. Third, a more diversified customer mix: CSP share falls from 70% to 60%, with Neocloud and sovereign cloud filling the gap. The second driver sets the valuation ceiling, the first only sets revenue, and the third sets risk.

A rough sensitivity calculation: for every 10% increase in revenue, net profit rises about 10% (linear) at a 2.37% net margin; for every 0.1pct change in gross margin, net profit changes about 4% (rough estimate on a 6.12% base and a 20% tax rate). In other words, a 0.25pct change in gross margin equates to a 10% change in revenue. This explains why the market is far more sensitive to gross margin than to revenue.

Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

2026Q2 gross margins of the six Taiwanese peers: Hon Hai 6.12%, Wistron 5.66%, Quanta 5.02%, Compal 4.6%, Pegatron 4.43%, Inventec 4.23%. Hon Hai is the largest (over 40% share of the AI server market), the most vertically integrated and the only one with global capacity; in April 2026 its monthly shipments led Quanta by about 2,100 racks and Wistron by about 1,300–1,400 racks.

But the six are caught in a prisoner's dilemma: whoever raises prices first loses orders first, so they all grind it out in the 4%–6% range. Wiwynn, selling white-box directly, reached 9.3%, proving that business model differences matter more than management efficiency in determining gross margin.Hon Hai's advantage is that it "won't be replaced"; its disadvantage is that it "won't get a price increase either."

The customer allocation mechanism is key to understanding the landscape: CSPs typically split orders 60/25/15 across three suppliers and renegotiate prices every year. Hon Hai takes the largest share but is never the sole supplier.This mechanism sets the ceiling on industry gross margins and ensures that no player can gain pricing power through share expansion.Wistron's 2026Q2 gross margin rose 1.22pct YoY to 5.66%, making it the only competitor closing the gap and worth continued tracking.

Chapter 8 Risk factors

① Cloud providers slow capex (high probability, high damage): revenue growth could fall from +47% to single digits, with a larger EPS decline. ② Upstream price hikes (already happening): DRAM +60%–70%, NAND +100%, directly squeezing gross margin. ③ US capacity sits idle (medium probability): fixed costs arrive before orders; if utilization falls below 70%, operating margin breaks.

④ Customers pull racks and self-source (low probability, extreme damage): once racks are standardized, the foundry degrades to an assembler. ⑤ FX and tariffs (high probability, low damage): each 1 yuan appreciation of the New Taiwan dollar cuts gross margin by 0.1–0.2pct (rough estimate). All five are gray rhinos, not black swans; all can be hedged by price protection, none can be eliminated by judgment.

Sensitivity analysis: if 2027 revenue growth falls from 30% to 5%, at a 6.1% gross margin and 3.9% operating margin, EPS drops from about 22.5 yuan to 17 yuan (rough estimate), 14x the current price; if gross margin simultaneously falls to 5.8%, EPS is about 15 yuan, 16x. This is the real risk of "peak-cycle EPS times 12x" — it is not that the valuation is high, it is that the denominator shrinks.

The risks also amplify each other: slower cloud capex drags utilization down, lower utilization leaves fixed costs underabsorbed, and depreciation lands concentrated in 2027 — combined, operating margin could fall from 3.75% to below 2.8%, and the EPS decline would exceed 35%. This is the scenario the report sees as most in need of defense, and the basis for the bear-case valuation of 160 yuan.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026: revenue of about 10.8 trillion (first three quarters 7.67 trillion + Q4 about 3.1 trillion, rough calculation), EPS 19.06 yuan (foreign brokerage model). This implies H2 EPS of 11.23 yuan, 43% more than H1's 7.83 yuan, requiring an H2 net margin of no less than 2.45%—this assumption has already been undermined by two consecutive quarters of gross margin decline and is the most fragile link in the forecast.

2027: EPS 22.5 yuan (foreign brokerage model), assuming VR200 takes over smoothly and gross margin holds at 6.1%. If gross margin falls to 5.8%, EPS is about 20 yuan; if it rises to 6.5%, EPS is about 25 yuan.The elasticity of EPS to gross margin is about 1:4—for every 0.1pct change in gross margin, EPS changes by about 0.4 yuan.

Quarterly breakdown: Q3 revenue of 3,026.9 billion has been announced; at a 2.37% net margin, net profit is roughly 71.8 billion and EPS about 5.1 yuan. Q4 is the traditional delivery peak; if revenue rises 5% QoQ and the net margin holds, EPS is about 5.4 yuan. H2 totals 10.5 yuan, 0.7 yuan short of the foreign brokerage model's 11.23 yuan—that gap is the price of the assumption that gross margin stops deteriorating.

Chapter 10 Valuation

Three scenarios: bear case RMB 160 (EPS RMB 15 × 10x), base case RMB 280 (RMB 22.5 × 12.5x), bull case RMB 380 (RMB 25.3 × 15x). Probabilities 40%/45%/15%, weighted RMB 247, +3.1% versus the current price of RMB 239.5.

Cross-sectional view: 12.6x 2026E PE is below Quanta's 18x, Wiwynn's 20x and Wistron's 15x, but above Compal's 10x. The discount reflects thin margins and high cyclicality, not a market error. 1.5x PB sits in the middle of the historical range (1.2–1.8x), not at the bottom. Conclusion: valuation is fair and offers no margin of safety.

PB view: the current price implies about 1.5x PB against a historical range of 1.2–1.8x and a median of about 1.45x, so the stock trades slightly above the median. Dividend view: payout ratio is about 55%, and RMB 239.5 implies a yield of less than 3%, below the 4%–5% on Taiwan high-dividend names. Both views point to the same conclusion: valuation is fair, neither cheap nor expensive, with no margin of safety.

The historical comparison needs a reference point: before the AI rally began in 2021, Hon Hai's PE held at 9–11x for a long stretch; after AI servers became the main theme in 2024, it re-rated to 12–14x. The current 12.6x sits at the midpoint of this post-re-rating range, meaning the "AI premium" is already priced in and further multiple expansion requires gross margin, not revenue.

Valuation in one line: what is cheap is the current multiple; what is expensive is the cycle position.

For buyers, the more practical approach is to watch PB: below 1.15x, asset protection starts to appear; above 1.5x, only cyclical upside remains.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Initiate at "Watch", 12-month target price of RMB 280 (base case), +17% versus the current price, but downside risk of -33%; the risk/reward is unattractive. We do not recommend building a position at RMB 239.5.

Two buy triggers: ① share price ≤ RMB 180 (10x 2027E EPS of RMB 18, PB about 1.15x); ② gross margin ≥6.5% for two consecutive quarters (denominator revised up, base-case EPS rises to RMB 26). Reduce/avoid triggers: gross margin <5.8% for two consecutive quarters, or single-quarter revenue turns negative sequentially and the company does not cut guidance. At the portfolio level, do not hold alongside TSMC and Quanta — correlation above 0.8 means it is the same bet.

Position sizing: 0% at the current price, 1%–2% at RMB 180, 3%–4% at RMB 160; if gross margin reaches 6.5% for two consecutive quarters, build 1% first without waiting for the price. On correlation, Hon Hai's daily return correlation with TSMC, Quanta and Wistron is above 0.8; it should not be held simultaneously with these names, otherwise you are leveraging the same risk factor. Stop-loss is structural, not price-based: clear the position if gross margin is <5.8% for two quarters.

Conclusion

Hon Hai is the most irreplaceable executor of this AI infrastructure cycle, and the least priceable link in the value chain. 12.6x 2026E PE against a market cap of TWD 3.37 trillion leaves the valuation fair but offers no margin of safety; probability-weighted expected return of +3% means the risk/reward is unfavorable. Maintain "Watch, wait for the gross margin inflection": a buy requires the stock below TWD 180 or gross margin above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters; a gross margin drop below 5.8% removes it from the watch list.
