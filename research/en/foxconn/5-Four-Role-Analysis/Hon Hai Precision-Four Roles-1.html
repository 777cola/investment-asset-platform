Business Analysis (Duan Yongping's Perspective) — Hon Hai Precision

What does this business sell, and do the unit economics add up
Research date 2026/10/19  ·  Price NT$240 (-1.84%)  ·  Market cap About TWD 3.37 trillion  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 12.6x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Hon Hai does not sell servers; it sells the ability to deliver complex products on time and in volume. That ability has value, but the buyer sets the price. Q2 2026 revenue was TWD 2,525.9 billion and net profit TWD 59.97 billion, a net margin of 2.37% — TWD 2.4 retained per TWD 100 of revenue. That is the entire answer to this business.

Unit economics: how much profit per cabinet

The BOM of one NVL72 rack is about USD 3 million, of which GPU, HBM, CPU and switch chips account for the bulk, all customer-specified and booked at market price. Hon Hai charges assembly, test, integration and delivery fees at the rack level; sell-side estimates put pure assembly fees at USD 20,000–30,000 per rack. Recognized on a full buy-and-sell basis, that USD 20,000–30,000 spread over USD 3 million of revenue translates into a gross margin contribution of around 1%.

Company-wide gross margin reaches 6.12% because self-made components — parts, mechanicals, PCB, connectors — support it. The higher the share of self-made content, the higher the gross margin — which is why Hon Hai sits one percentage point above Quanta (5.02%) and Compal (4.6%). In other words, its margin comes not from the rack but from the parts inside it.

Is scale a moat?

Yes, but it only keeps new entrants out. In April 2026 monthly shipments, Foxconn led Quanta by about 2,100 racks and Wistron by about 1,300–1,400 racks; its global AI server share exceeds 40%. To stand up a new rack production line across three continents within three months, there is no second company that can do it. But scale buys "order priority," not "pricing power."

The Duan Yongping-style test is simple: if Nvidia demanded a 3% price cut tomorrow, could Foxconn say no? It could not. A 3% price cut would eat half its net profit, while turning down the order would only mean forgoing one deal. In this game, the bigger the scale, the harder it is to say no.

There is another cost that is often overlooked: customer-specified components mean Foxconn must fund procurement upfront. A single rack carries millions of dollars in materials, with an 8–12 week preparation period, and the contract manufacturer pays first while the customer pays later. The bigger the scale, the more upfront funding required — so this is both a capacity business and a financing business, and part of the processing fee it earns is really a charge for tied-up capital. When revenue doubles, this upfront funding doubles with it — that is the mechanism behind negative free cash flow.

Ceiling: revenue can double, profit cannot

Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 reached RMB 766.98 billion, up 39.5% year on year, with Q3 alone at RMB 302.69 billion, up 47.1% year on year. At that pace, breaking RMB 1.2 trillion in revenue by 2027 is not fantasy. But the net margin is locked at 2.3%–2.4%, so doubling revenue means net profit at most doubles, and only after funding twice the working capital and a hefty capex bill.

That is the ceiling of the "selling capacity" business: growth is driven by the balance sheet, not by pricing. Duan Yongping would call this a business you "can understand, can model, but isn't worth waiting for"—unless the price falls to a level where no modeling is needed.

Conclusion

Commercial desk conclusion: Unit economics work but are too thin; scale buys order priority, not pricing power; the revenue ceiling is far off, the profit ceiling is close. Rate "Watch", no position. If the stock falls below RMB 180 (8x 2027E PE), it can be bought as one tranche of a cyclical, not held as a growth stock.
