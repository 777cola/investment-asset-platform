Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — Hon Hai Precision

Oligopoly structure, cutthroat profits: who collects the rent

Core view

EMS is an oligopoly, but not a good industry. Taiwan's Big Six plus Celestica, Jabil and Flex — anyone can build, and no one dares raise prices. In 2026Q2 the Big Six's gross margins ranged from 4.23% to 6.12%, with Hon Hai highest at just 6.12% — that is the pricing the industry structure dictates, not a failure of effort by any one company.

Competitive structure: five players dividing the cake, and none of them holds the knife

The barrier to entry for AI racks is higher than for phone assembly: long certification cycles, difficult yield ramps, and the need for global delivery. So the field has narrowed from twenty players to five — Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Wiwynn and Inventec, plus Celestica and Jabil in the US. Concentration has risen, but prices have not, because the buyer side is even more concentrated: the five North American CSPs account for 70% of full-rack procurement.

Munger would call this "buyer oligopoly versus seller oligopoly," with the buyers holding the alternatives. In 2026 the customer mix dilutes from 70% to 60%, with Neoclouds and sovereign clouds filling the gap — which sounds like good news, but in practice means more buyers and less bargaining power for any single one, while the new buyers have weaker financing capacity and higher collection risk. Structural improvement and rising risk are happening at the same time.

Cycle position: which year of this capex cycle

Counting from 2023, 2026 is the fourth year of AI infrastructure capex and the most aggressive one: Foxconn's Q3 revenue rose 47% year on year, with a single-month record in September. TrendForce expects the NVL72 rack market to grow another 214% in 2027, driven by a doubling of ASP and upstream price increases. On order visibility, demand is not lacking in 2027; on the slope of growth, the year-on-year base for 2027 will look very ugly.

In Munger's framework, the most dangerous moment for a cyclical stock is not when demand declines, but when "everyone knows it will keep growing" — because valuation has already priced in the growth. Foxconn falling from 309 to 239 means the market did the math one step ahead.

Who collects the tolls: four toll booths along the value chain

The first toll booth is Nvidia: GPUs account for over 60% of rack BOM, with gross margins of 70%+. The second is the memory trio: 2026 DRAM contract prices up 60%–70%, NAND up over 100%. The third is power and data centers: Nvidia provides residual-value guarantees for OpenAI's campus, Google commits USD 44 billion to Anthropic's leases. The fourth is advanced packaging and substrates. Only after these four toll booths collect does foundry and assembly get its share.

What foundries do in this chain is "assemble other people's high-margin parts into a system that runs," collecting a fixed processing fee. Munger would put it this way: "They're not producing profit; they're producing revenue."

Rank gross margins across each link: Nvidia about 70%, the HBM trio 40%–50%, power and thermal players Delta and Lite-On 25%–30%, Hon Hai 6.12%, pure assembly plants 4%–5%. The closer to the chip, the more pricing power; the closer to the rack, the more it depends on turnover. This is not a difference in capability but a difference in position—all Hon Hai can do is be the best within its own segment; it cannot change the thickness of that segment itself.

Conclusion

Industry desk conclusion: Oligopoly, but buyers cap profits; the cycle is in its fourth year, and the 2027 base will look ugly; all four toll booths along the value chain sit above foundry. In this industry, "knowing where you are in the cycle" matters more than "knowing how good the company is." Rating: Watch, until the industry desk sees a gross-margin inflection.
