Risk assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Hon Hai Precision

Three tail risks: customer concentration, geopolitics, capex cliff
Research date 2026/10/19  ·  Price NT$240 (-1.84%)  ·  Market cap About TWD 3.37 trillion  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 12.6x

Core view

Hon Hai's risk is not operational, it is structural. The top five customers account for a high share of revenue (the five North American CSPs take about 70% of full-rack purchases, falling to about 60% in 2026), capacity is spread across four countries, each exposed to policy, and demand hinges on a single variable: cloud capex. If any one of three tail risks triggers, a 6% gross margin leaves no buffer.

Customer concentration: 70% is history, 60% is not safe

The share of AI rack procurement accounted for by the five major North American CSPs was about 70% in 2025 and is expected to fall to 60% in 2026. The numbers are improving, but note who is filling the gap: Neoclouds (CoreWeave and the like) and sovereign clouds — the former buying GPUs on debt financing, the latter on fiscal budgets. The customer base is more diversified, but credit quality has deteriorated.

For a company with a net margin of 2.37%, a single bad debt can wipe out a quarter's profit. The historical experience with the Apple business is stable collections; the new AI customers have no such track record. This is a risk invisible in the financial statements, surfacing only in accounts-receivable days.

Geography: a four-site footprint is both an advantage and four fixed-cost bases

Simultaneous delivery across Mexico, Vietnam, India and the US is the hard capability that won Foxconn the orders. But each site requires building plants, hiring workers and stocking materials, and utilization below 70% means a loss. A shift in tariff policy turns a capacity footprint from an advantage into a burden — tariff adjustments since 2025 have led customers to demand "multi-site backups"; Foxconn is a beneficiary, and also the party bearing the idle costs.

A longer-term point: labor costs at the US plant are several times those in Vietnam, so the processing fee for assembling the same rack in the US must be higher. Whether customers will pay that premium will have an answer in 2027.

The fourth hidden risk is FX. Foxconn's revenue is denominated in US dollars while costs mix New Taiwan dollars, RMB, Mexican pesos and Vietnamese dong; every 1 dollar of NT dollar appreciation shaves 0.1–0.2 percentage points off gross margin (rough calculation, based on the USD revenue share and the natural hedge ratio). The NT dollar strengthened in 2026, which is part of the reason gross margin fell for two consecutive quarters, and is the line most easily overlooked in the financials.

Tail risk: capex cliff and "unbundling cabinets for self-procurement"

The most extreme risk is not a 10% demand decline; it is cloud providers standardizing full-rack integration and then sourcing and assembling it themselves. Once a rack becomes a "standard part," the contract manufacturer retreats from system integrator to assembler and cannot even hold a 6% gross margin.That risk still looks distant—rack-level yield and delivery complexity still require specialist manufacturers—but the direction is clear.

Li Lu's test is "will this business still exist in ten years, and if so, who makes the money." The answer: it will exist, but the money is made upstream.With the trend right and the position lowest in the chain, he would only buy such a target when it is extremely cheap, not at 12.6x PE.

Conclusion

Risk seat conclusion: deteriorating customer credit quality, idle-cost drag from the four production sites, and the self-sourcing technology route for container-unstuffing — none of these three tail risks is priced in at the current level. This seat votes "avoid," not because the company is expected to run into trouble, but because RMB 239.5 does not pay for these three tails. Below RMB 160, the stance could shift to watch.
