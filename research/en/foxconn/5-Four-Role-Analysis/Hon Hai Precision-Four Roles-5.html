Team Lead summary — Hon Hai Precision

Four seats' points of divergence and verdicts
Research date 2026/10/19  ·  Price NT$240 (-1.84%)  ·  Market cap About TWD 3.37 trillion  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 12.6x

Core view

Four independent reports landed, and their conclusions converged with striking consistency: three Hold, one Avoid, no Buy.The disagreement is not over whether to buy, but over why we cannot buy yet and what evidence would change that conclusion.This section does three things only: lay out the disagreements, deliver a verdict, and set the monitoring indicators.

Votes and common ground

Commercial seat (Duan Yongping lens): unit economics are too thin; scale is not pricing power. Financial seat (Buffett lens): profits are real, cash is not, ROE is low. Industry seat (Munger lens): oligopoly, but buyers have it locked down—fourth year of the cycle. Risk seat (Li Lu lens), avoid: three tails are unpriced. All four seats agree on two things: Hon Hai is the most irreplaceable executor of this AI infrastructure cycle, and its margin is not set by itself.

They also share one timing call: the Q3 and Q4 2026 earnings reports are the watershed. Q3 revenue of TWD 3.03 trillion is already out; gross margin awaits the November earnings call; Q4 is the traditional delivery peak and the last window to verify whether growth carries profit.

The division of labour among the four seats also defines what each cannot see: the commercial seat cannot see report details, the financial seat cannot see the technology roadmap, the industry seat cannot see customer contracts, and the risk seat tends to overestimate tail probabilities. The summary is therefore not a vote; it fills blind spots. Once that layer is filled in, the four seats' consensus on "don't buy now" only hardens—each reaches the same conclusion from the side it can see.

Disagreement 1: Can gross margin rise with VR200?

The commercial desk disagrees: cabinet processing fees are charged per cabinet, so a doubling of ASP leaves the absolute processing fee unchanged; it merely raises the materials share, and gross margin could fall. The industry desk disagrees in the other direction: VR200 raises integration complexity, contract manufacturers are bringing liquid cooling, power and mechanical parts in-house, and gross margin has room to expand 0.3–0.4pp. Both have a point; the swing factor is in-house content share.

Verdict: do not pre-judge; verify against the financials. The metric to watch is gross margin over two consecutive quarters, with a 6.5% threshold. If gross margin holds above 6.5% in both 2027Q1 and Q2, adopt the industry desk's view; if it keeps hovering near 6.1%, adopt the commercial desk's view and cut the valuation centre from 12.5x to 10x.

Divergence 2: The probability of idle US capacity

The risk desk sees this as the most underpriced tail: fixed costs from the four-site expansion land before orders, utilization drops below 70%, and the 3.75% operating margin cannot hold. The finance desk assigns it low probability: the customers' "multi-site backup" requirement is itself an order, and capacity at the US fab is order-driven rather than a bet. The disagreement ultimately turns on the credibility of customer commitments.

Ruling: monitor per the risk desk's framework, size the position per the finance desk's judgment. Watch for capacity-utilization language on the earnings call and progress at the US fab; cap the position at 2% and build only below 180.

Disagreement 3: Is the current price expensive or not

The financial desk calls 12.6x 2026E P/E "not expensive," the commercial desk calls 10.6x 2027E P/E "also not expensive," and the risk desk calls a probability-weighted expected return of +3% "very expensive." The three differ not in the math but in what goes into the denominator: current-period EPS, normalized EPS, or probability weighting.

Ruling: probability weighting prevails. A 12.6x P/E on peak-cycle EPS equates to 16x normalized P/E—for a company with ROE of 9%–12% and negative free cash flow, that is not cheap. The conclusion matches the working paper: observe, wait for the gross margin inflection point; buy trigger below RMB 180 or gross margin above 6.5% for two consecutive quarters.

Three things we explicitly will not do

First, don't chase the rally on September revenue breaking the trillion mark. Revenue reflects orders, not profit; crossing a trillion adds almost nothing at the margin to valuation. It proves customers are placing orders, not that Hon Hai is making money.

Second, don't size positions on a single-point 2027 EPS estimate. The 22.5 is a sell-side model, and it embeds the assumption that gross margin stops deteriorating — an assumption broken for two consecutive quarters. Use the RMB 15–25 range instead: take the 18 midpoint, which corresponds to 13x P/E, and the conclusion shifts immediately from cheap to fair.

Third, don't build a position the day before the Q3 earnings call to bet on gross margin. This is a textbook information-disadvantage trade: gross margin is the first question asked on the call and the easiest for the buy side to learn in advance. Waiting for the numbers costs you a 5% gain but spares you a 10% drop.

Conclusion

Team Lead ruling: maintain "watch, wait for the gross margin inflection," do not build a position. All four seats voted against buying, so the issue is not "a slightly lower price would do" but that "the earnings structure has not been proven." Watch calendar: November 2026 Q3 earnings call (gross margin, capacity utilization), January 2027 full-year revenue, March 2027 Q4 results (free cash flow), May 2027 Q1 (first full quarter after the VR200 switch). If any of these four checkpoints delivers a 6.5% gross margin, reconvene the four seats.
