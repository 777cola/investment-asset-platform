## Layer one: revenue mix — Cloud & Network is already the largest segment

On our rough earnings-call-based estimate, the 2026Q2 revenue mix is approximately: cloud and networking products (AI servers, racks, networking) about 45%, consumer smart products (phones, wearables) about 28%, PC terminals about 12%, and components and others (connectors, PCB, mechanical parts, optics) about 15%. Three years ago this mix was inverted—consumer smart products accounted for over 40% and cloud and networking for less than 30%.

The mix shift explains how revenue can grow 47%: an NVL72 rack carries an ASP more than ten times that of a general-purpose server, so the same capacity generates entirely different revenue. It also explains why gross margin is falling—a full rack has the highest component share and the lowest assembly-fee share. Mix upgrading and margin-structure downgrading are two sides of the same coin.

One level further: within cloud and networking, the increment comes almost entirely from AI racks, with networking (switches, routers) the existing base; within consumer smart products, phone assembly share is stable but unit prices are not rising; components and others is the highest-gross-margin segment and Hon Hai's real profit buffer. The growth ranking across the four segments is cloud and networking > components and others > PC terminals > consumer smart products, but the gross-margin ranking is exactly the reverse. That is why mix upgrading inevitably comes with gross-margin decline.

## Layer two: gross margin. Not a single basis point of the 6.12% came from price increases

Gross margin of 6.12% came in 0.06pct below 6.18% in 2026Q1 and 0.21pct below 6.33% in 2025Q2. Two consecutive quarters of decline—and both fell in the two quarters with the fastest revenue growth. That is not coincidence but mechanism: the faster revenue grows, the higher the share of high-priced components, and the more gross margin is diluted.

Three specific drivers: ① GPUs and HBM are booked at market prices; 2026 DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%, and those increases pass straight through the income statement; ② the mix shifts toward full-rack systems, where buy-and-sell full recognition enlarges the denominator; ③ a stronger New Taiwan dollar compresses gross profit once USD revenue is converted. None of the three is within Hon Hai's control—that is the concrete meaning of "gross margin is not set by the company itself."

## Layer 3: Costs and profit — the 0.6 percentage point saved

Operating expenses were RMB 59.7 billion, up just 6% and equal to 2.36% of revenue (3.13% a year earlier). Revenue grew 41% against 6% expense growth — the inevitable outcome of running utilisation at full tilt, which diluted depreciation, G&A and R&D. Operating profit reached RMB 94.8 billion, +68% YoY, 27 percentage points ahead of revenue growth of +41%, and this was the sole driver.

That leverage, however, is nearly exhausted. The expense ratio has already fallen to 2.36%; a further 0.3pct of compression would add only RMB 7.6 billion pre-tax — equivalent to 12% of net profit. In other words, the expense line can still spring one small surprise, but it cannot underpin a re-rating. Gross margin remains the real variable.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — growth is eating cash

Free cash flow was negative in the first half. The mechanism is simple: revenue rose 39%, receivables and inventory rose with it, and AI racks carry high unit prices and an 8–12 week component lead time, so cash goes out first; capacity is also being expanded across four locations, with capex well above last year. A company with a 2.37% net margin has no internal accumulation to pay this bill and can only rely on rolling operating cash flow and external financing.

The balance sheet remains reasonably stable: net debt ratio of about 15%, interest coverage of about 10x (rough calculation), no solvency risk. But assets are getting heavier — plant, equipment and inventory are all low-turnover assets, and from 2027 depreciation will begin to weigh on gross margin. The one sentence to remember from this report: profit can be generated through turnover, but cash depends on customers paying on time.

Looking further out, 2026 capex turns into depreciation in 2027–2029. Amortised over 5–8 years, if order growth slows in 2027 while depreciation is still charged as normal, gross margin will have to give up another 0.2–0.3 percentage points on top of the 6.12% base. In other words, next year's gross margin is not a question of "whether it can improve" but of "whether it can hold 6%". This is the most easily overlooked point in this report, and the one that most deserves to be priced in early.

## Link the four layers: three contradictions, one explanation

Three contradictions sit on the face of this report: revenue is accelerating, gross margin is sliding, and operating margin is rising. All three are facets of one thing—a richer mix of full-cabinet solutions scales revenue, drags on gross margin and dilutes the expense ratio. Grasp that, and you will not misread "operating margin improvement" as "profitability improvement."

The second contradiction is subtler: operating profit +68%, net profit only +35%. Where did the gap go? First, interest expense—fronting procurement and expanding capacity across four sites both require borrowing; second, minority interests—subsidiaries such as Foxconn Industrial Internet are not wholly owned, so a slice of profit leaves the group. Funding costs and minority shareholders absorbed part of what scale leverage created; net profit attributable to the parent captured only a third.

The third contradiction is a timing gap: capex lands this year, depreciation starts pressing on gross margin next year. The 2026 report is "the best-looking one of the expansion phase"; the bill for this money only comes due in 2027.

## Three things to watch most closely

- Gross margin trajectory (highest weight).This is the first question on the 2026Q3 earnings call. A print below 6.0% immediately invalidates the base-case EPS of 22.5; a print above 6.3% would signal that the mix improvement is starting to bite. The 6.5% threshold is the buy trigger in our model.

- Free cash flow.Focus on the cash flow statements in the interim and annual reports. The absolute number matters less than the ratio of operating cash flow to net profit; below 0.8 means profit is not converting into cash.

- Receivables and inventory turnover days.Both will inevitably rise as revenue doubles; the question is whether they rise faster than revenue. If turnover days increase by more than 15 days YoY, customers are using their own payment terms to tie up Hon Hai's capital.
