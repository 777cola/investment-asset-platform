## Layer one: revenue mix — Cloud & Network is already the largest segment

Roughly estimating on the earnings-call basis, the 2026Q2 revenue mix is approximately: cloud and networking products (AI servers, racks, networking) about 45%, consumer smart products (phones, wearables) about 28%, PC terminals about 12%, and components and others (connectors, PCB, mechanical parts, optics) about 15%. **Three years ago this mix was inverted—consumer smart products accounted for over 40% and cloud and networking for less than 30%.**

The mix shift explains why revenue can grow 47%: the ASP of an NVL72 rack is more than ten times that of a general-purpose server, so the same capacity generates entirely different revenue. But it also explains why gross margin is falling—a full rack has the highest component share and the lowest assembly-fee share. **Revenue mix upgrading and margin structure downgrading are two sides of the same coin.**

Breaking it down one level further: within cloud and networking, the increment comes almost entirely from AI racks, while networking (switches, routers) is the existing base; within consumer smart products, phone assembly share is stable but unit prices are not rising; components and others is the highest-gross-margin segment and Hon Hai's real profit buffer. **The growth ranking across the four segments is: cloud and networking &gt; components and others &gt; PC terminals &gt; consumer smart products, but the gross-margin ranking is exactly the reverse.** That is why mix upgrading inevitably comes with gross-margin decline.

## Layer two: gross margin — not a single cent of the 6.12% came from price increases

Gross margin 6.12%, 0.06pct below 6.18% in 2026Q1 and 0.21pct below 6.33% in 2025Q2. Two consecutive quarters of decline, and the decline came in the two quarters with the fastest revenue growth—not a coincidence but a mechanism: **the faster revenue grows, the higher the share of high-priced components, and the more gross margin is diluted.**

Three specific reasons: ① GPUs and HBM are booked at market prices; 2026 DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%, and the price increases pass through the income statement; ② the share of full-rack systems rises, and buy-and-sell full recognition enlarges the denominator; ③ a stronger New Taiwan dollar compresses gross profit after converting USD revenue. **None of the three is within Hon Hai's control—that is the concrete meaning of "gross margin is not set by the company itself."**

## Layer 3: Costs and profit — the 0.6 percentage point saved

Operating expenses were RMB 59.7 billion, up only 6%, or 2.36% of revenue (3.13% in the year-ago period). Revenue rose 41% while expenses rose 6% — the inevitable result of running capacity utilization at full tilt: depreciation, G&A and R&D were diluted. **Operating profit was RMB 94.8 billion, +68% YoY, 27 percentage points faster than revenue growth of +41%, and all of it came from here.**

But this leverage is nearly exhausted. The expense ratio has already fallen to 2.36%; squeezing out another 0.3pct would add only RMB 7.6 billion pre-tax — equivalent to 12% of net profit. **In other words, the expense side can still deliver one small surprise, but it cannot support a re-rating.** The real variable remains the gross margin line.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — growth is eating cash

Free cash flow was negative in the first half. The mechanism is not complicated: revenue rose 39%, receivables and inventory rose with it, AI racks carry high unit prices and an 8–12 week component lead time, so cash has to go out first; at the same time, capacity is being expanded in four locations, with capex significantly above last year. **A company with a 2.37% net margin has no internal accumulation to pay this bill and can only rely on rolling operating cash flow and external financing.**

The balance sheet is still reasonably stable: net debt ratio of about 15%, interest coverage of about 10x (rough calculation), no solvency risk. But assets are getting heavier — plant, equipment and inventory are all low-turnover assets, and from 2027 depreciation will begin to weigh on gross margin. **The one sentence to remember from this report: profit can be generated through turnover, but cash depends on customers paying on time.**

One step further out: 2026 capex will turn into depreciation in 2027–2029. Amortized over 5–8 years, if order growth slows in 2027 while depreciation is still charged as normal, gross margin will have to give up another 0.2–0.3 percentage points on top of the 6.12% base. **In other words, next year's gross margin is not a question of "whether it can improve" but of "whether it can hold 6%".** This is the most easily overlooked point in this report, and the one that most deserves to be priced in early.

## Link the four layers: three contradictions, one explanation

Three contradictions sit on the face of this report: revenue is accelerating, gross margin is sliding, and operating margin is rising. These are three facets of one thing—**a higher mix of full-cabinet solutions scales up revenue, drags down gross margin, and dilutes the expense ratio.** Grasp that, and you will not misread "operating margin improvement" as "profitability improvement."

The second contradiction is subtler: operating profit +68%, net profit only +35%. Where did the gap go? First, interest expense—fronting procurement and expanding capacity across four sites both require borrowing; second, minority interests—subsidiaries such as Foxconn Industrial Internet are not wholly owned, so a slice of profit goes out the door. **Part of what scale leverage created was taken by funding costs and minority shareholders; net profit attributable to the parent captured only a third.**

The third contradiction is a timing gap: capex lands this year, depreciation starts pressing on gross margin next year. **The 2026 report is "the best-looking one of the expansion phase"; the bill for this money only comes due in 2027.**

## Three things to watch most closely

- **Gross margin trajectory (highest weight).**The first question at the 2026Q3 earnings call. If it falls below 6.0%, the EPS of 22.5 under the base case is immediately invalidated; if it holds above 6.3%, it shows the product mix improvement is starting to take effect. The 6.5% threshold is the buy condition set in our model.
- **Free cash flow.**Look at the cash flow statements in the interim and annual reports; the key is not the absolute value but the ratio of "operating cash flow / net profit." Below 0.8 means profit is not turning into cash.
- **Receivables and inventory turnover days.**Both will certainly rise when revenue doubles; the key is whether the increase outpaces revenue growth. If turnover days rise more than 15 days year on year, it means customers are using their own payment terms to tie up Hon Hai's capital.
