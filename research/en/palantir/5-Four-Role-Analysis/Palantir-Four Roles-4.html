Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Palantir

Five risks — which one would actually kill this investment
Research date 2026/10/05  ·  Price $189.40 (+0.34%)  ·  Market cap About USD 456 billion  ·  PS(TTM) Approximately 74x
← Back to the hub

Core thesis

Risks must be layered: the ones that make the company worse and the ones that lose money on the stock are not the same set. Palantir's company risk is small; its stock risk is enormous—because the price already treats the low-probability company risk as zero.

Ranked by probability of "killing this investment": valuation 60%, government budgets 20%, competition 12%, dilution 5%, governance and founder 3%. This ranking runs counter to many people's intuition, but it starts from the current price of USD 189.40, not from whether the company is good or bad.

Risk one: valuation — not whether it falls, but by how much

A 74x P/S multiple implies the annualized return the market expects, having already discounted all growth through 2032. Base case USD 120, 37% downside; bear case USD 70, 63% downside. Neither number requires the company to stumble — only for the multiple to mean-revert.

Historical reference: in November 2021 it reached 45x P/S; by December 2022 it had fallen to 6x, an 85% decline, while revenue was still growing 24% over the same period. The fundamentals did not change, the multiple did, and the stock fell 85%. This is the company's own history, not a hypothetical.

Risk 2: Government budgets and concentration

The US government accounts for 42% of revenue, and adding international governments takes it past half (rough calculation). This is not simply "customer concentration" — renewal cycles, budget line items and priorities all follow politics. A single government shutdown, a single procurement rule reform, a single move to standardize AI procurement into framework agreements can shift bargaining power back to the buyer.

Specific triggers: the funding cadence for AI programs in the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA), whether the General Services Administration (GSA) folds these tools into a unified procurement catalog, and post-election priority shifts at the Department of Defense. None of these are within the company's control.

Risk 3: Competition — not being defeated, but being "absorbed"

The most dangerous form of competition is not a rival building a better product, but cloud vendors turning this capability into a default feature. Microsoft Fabric, AWS's Bedrock + SageMaker and Google's Vertex are all moving along the "data + model + decision" chain. Once customers feel that "what comes with the cloud is good enough", Palantir shifts from "must sign" to "can be cut".

Open source is closing in too. The ontology layer is not a technology that cannot be replicated; it is an engineering paradigm. The odds are not low that within three years an open-source ontology framework capable of running in the customer's own data center will appear.

Risk 4 and 5: Dilution and founder dependence

Equity compensation runs at 13.7% of revenue and share count grows 2% a year — a value leakage that occurs with certainty every year. Against a pricing that assumes a 30% expected return, 2% leakage cuts the expected return to 27.4%.

Founder dependence is a soft risk but a real one. Karp's public profile, political stance and moral defense of the government business are themselves part of the company brand. If he leaves or is forced out, the company will not collapse, but the story of "why Palantir" loses a piece. Add the Class F super-voting rights, which leave outside shareholders unable to change direction by vote — that is a governance discount, not a governance premium.

In Li Lu's framework, four of these five are "price problems" and one is a "business problem." And there is only one solution to a price problem: wait. He often says investment returns come from the gap between "the price you pay" and "the value you get," not from how bullish you are on the industry.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: Company risk low, stock risk high. Four of the five risks can be avoided by "not buying"; one (government concentration) is a long-term structural feature that cannot be avoided, only priced. The Li Lu-style conclusion: the trend is right, the company is strong, the position is good, but the price has already priced all three, plus an extra leg. Conclusion: avoid; observation conditions consistent with m01.
