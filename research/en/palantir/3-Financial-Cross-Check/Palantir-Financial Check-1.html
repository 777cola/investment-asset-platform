Cross-validation: the numbers are hard, the definitions are soft

We cross-checked eight key metrics against two sources; seven deviations were within 1%. The only one above 1% was free cash flow (−2.7%), as the data provider calculates it as "operating cash flow − capex", whereas the company's definition also deducts finance leases and one construction-in-progress investment. This is not a misstatement but a difference in definition.

The real caution flag is not the numbers but three definitional traps: treating "adjusted operating profit" as GAAP, treating net profit that includes interest income as operating profit, and treating TCV as revenue. The definitions we ultimately adopt are set out at the end: revenue on a GAAP basis, margins on GAAP operating profit, and valuation on price-to-sales rather than price-to-earnings.

Free cash flow was the biggest miss, coming in USD 33 million below consensus

The company reports RMB 1.22 billion; the data vendor shows RMB 1.187 billion — a RMB 33.4 million gap. The reconciliation is straightforward: the vendor uses "operating cash flow − purchases of fixed assets," while the company deducts finance-lease principal and a data-center construction outlay from the same base. Both numbers are right; they just answer different questions.

We use the company's figure because it is consistent with management's "free cash flow margin of 63%" guidance and comparable across quarters. Readers should note, however: on the stricter definition (deducting only capex), free cash flow margin is 61.3%, not 63.1%. The 1.8-percentage-point difference does not change the conclusion; what it changes is how solid the "63%" figure is.

A side note: for a software company with very low capex, free cash flow and operating cash flow should be roughly equal. The wider the gap between the two, the more "adjustments" the definition contains. Palantir's gap is 1.6% — clean.

Three framing traps more common than numerical misstatement

Type 1: Adjusted vs GAAP. The company reports both GAAP operating profit of 912 million (47.1%) and adjusted operating profit of 1.194 billion (61.7%); the 283 million gap is mainly 265 million of stock-based compensation. Many sell-side reports cite only the 62% figure, which makes the margin look a notch higher than it actually is. Stock-based compensation is a real cost here — 2% dilution a year is the evidence — so this report uses GAAP throughout.

Type 2: Net income includes interest. Q2 net income of 1.062 billion exceeds operating profit of 912 million; the extra 150 million comes from interest and investment income on USD 9.2 billion of cash. At a rough 4% short-end rate, that implies annual interest income of about USD 370 million. If rates fall, this profit disappears.

Type 3: TCV treated as revenue. Q2 total contract value was 3.373 billion, up 49% YoY, while revenue was 1.935 billion, up 93% YoY. The growth rates differ by 44 percentage points, and comparing the two directly yields completely opposite conclusions. TCV is signed contract value, recognised over multiple years; revenue is the amount recognised in the period. Read their relationship through the trend in the TCV / revenue ratio, not the absolute value in a single quarter.

Final basis adopted: three decisions

Revenue is recognised on a GAAP basis; TTM sums four quarters. TTM revenue of RMB 6.162 billion = FY2025 RMB 4.480 billion − H1 2025 RMB 1.886 billion + H1 2026 RMB 3.569 billion. The two 2025 half-year figures are back-solved from company-disclosed YoY growth rates (Q1 +85%, Q2 +93%) rather than reconciled line by line to the original statements; the error is within 0.5% and does not affect the multiple assessment.

Margin is based on GAAP operating profit, not adjusted profit or net income. Rationale: stock-based compensation is a real cost, and interest income is not sustainable. The two adjustments pull in opposite directions, so using GAAP operating profit avoids both at once.

Valuation uses P/S, not P/E. GAAP net income is distorted at both ends by interest income and stock-based compensation, so the 140x P/E carries too much variance. P/S is clean in both numerator and denominator, and Palantir's margin structure is stable, making P/S more comparable. The trade-off is that it ignores margin changes—so the scenario valuation sets out both revenue assumptions and net margin assumptions.

Conclusion

Eight metrics, two sources, seven deviations at or below 0.7%. The only one above 1% is free cash flow at 2.7%, traced to a definitional difference (finance leases and construction-in-progress investment) rather than a misstatement. The data is usable and the conclusions are unaffected. Three caveats to retain: ① use GAAP, not adjusted; ② net income includes about USD 370 million of annualized interest income; ③ TCV growth and revenue growth are not the same thing, and in Q2 they differed by 44 percentage points.
