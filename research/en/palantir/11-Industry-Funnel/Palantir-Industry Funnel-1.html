Industry funnel: why this one, and why not buy now

Screen the data software comparables — Microsoft, Databricks, Snowflake, Palantir, Booz Allen and C3.ai — against three hard lines: growth >25%, gross margin >60% and already profitable. Three names drop out, leaving Microsoft, Databricks and Palantir.

Round two asks who captures the excess profit from AI deployment. First-round screen: three hard cutoffs eliminate half

Three criteria: revenue growth >25%, gross margin >60%, and GAAP profitability. These are not onerous—they simply separate out companies that are "not the same kind of business."

Booz Allen is the first to drop out. Gross margin 22%, growth 8%—a consulting firm that charges by the person-day, with no software leverage. It competes with Palantir for government budgets, but it is not the same asset class, and its valuation logic is entirely different (15x P/E versus 74x price-to-sales).

C3.ai is the second to drop out. Growth 20%, gross margin 60%, persistently loss-making, and only one-hundredth of Palantir's scale. It tells the same story but cannot produce the same numbers—which shows that Palantir's moat is not conferred by the "AI application" category, but by the qualifications and customer relationships it built up over fifteen years.

Snowflake is the third to drop out. Growth of 25% just clears the bar, gross margin of 68% clears the bar, but adjusted operating margin is only 5%, and it remains GAAP loss-making. It is a good company, but its position (data cloud) is too far from decision-making to capture the excess profits of AI deployment.

Second-round picks: three companies competing for "who captures AI deployment spending"

That leaves Microsoft, Databricks and Palantir. All three clear the quality bar; the difference lies in "who captures the excess share of enterprise AI budgets."

Microsoft has the strongest distribution, but AI is "a little added on top of a USD 3.5 trillion market cap" — however fast Azure AI grows, it cannot move the overall valuation. Buying Microsoft is buying a good company, not buying AI leverage.

Databricks has the best position (the data entry point) and 50% growth, but it is unlisted and ordinary investors cannot buy it; its private valuation has already reached the USD 100 billion level, and its post-IPO pricing will most likely not be cheap either.

Palantir is the only name that is "listed + high growth + high profit + embedded at the decision layer." Its 93% growth is six times Microsoft's, its 47% operating margin is nine times Snowflake's, and its position sits closer to the money than Databricks. Purely from the angle of "why this one," the answer is clear.

Round three: price turns the final pick into a watch

The funnel has screened down to this point, and the usual call would be "Buy." This report, however, stops at step three: a 74x price-to-sales ratio.

Peer valuations: Microsoft at 12x, Snowflake at 13x, C3.ai at 3x. Palantir trades at 6 to 25 times those levels. The premium the market grants it is equivalent to the assumption that "it must remain the only winner."

Is the premium justified? Yes. Its growth rate is three to four times that of peers, its margins are an order of magnitude higher, and it sits closer to the decision layer. But does the premium have a ceiling? Also yes. When a company's price-to-sales ratio is six times that of its peers, its share price is no longer driven by fundamentals but by whether market sentiment can continue to support that multiple.

So the funnel's conclusion must be stated in full: It is the best company in this space, and it is the most expensive stock in this space. The two statements are not contradictory; they simply answer different questions—"what to buy" and "at what price."

Conclusion

Six names screened, one survivor: Palantir is the only listed data-software name that combines high growth, high margins and a position at the decision-making layer. But at 74x price-to-sales, six times the peer average, the valuation turns "final pick" into "watchlist." Conclusion: top of the watchlist; the buy trigger is price-to-sales back below 30x (around USD 80).
