Four readers, one financial report—what did each take away?

Core thesis

Four close readings came in, all pointing to the same conclusion: nobody disputes the quality of these results; the entire dispute is over what these profits are worth. Four people, four methods, one value range: USD 70–195, median USD 120, 37% below the current price.

Synthesising them, one number stands out: shareholder earnings after deducting stock-based compensation of USD 955 million per quarter, which annualized from Q2 implies a P/E of 119x. All four acknowledge this number; they just phrase it differently.

What do the four people agree on?

First, revenue is unquestionably real. Operating cash flow at 117% of net profit, RDV +124% and customers adding orders are three cross-checks that corroborate each other. This is not a company growing through accounting tricks.

Second, margins are real, but two items need stripping out: stock-based compensation at 13.7% and interest income at roughly 8% of net profit. Adjusted, GAAP operating margin is 47% and shareholder earnings margin is 49% — still top-tier.

Third, the moat splits in two: extremely wide on the government side, moderate on the commercial side. The four of us disagree on how wide it is after weighting, landing in a 7–9 range, but agree that none of us reaches 10.

Fourth, the price is too expensive. The four of us apply different discounts (37%/50%/60%), but the direction is the same.

Where do the four disagree?

Duan Yongping asks whether customers will still buy next year, answers "yes," and reduces the question to price. Buffett works from owner earnings, calculates a 119x P/E (annualized Q2), and concludes: don't buy. Munger asks which number is lying, identifies three misreadings, and concludes: stay out. Li Lu looks at the structure ten years out, calculates a best-case annualized return of 7.7%, and concludes: the odds aren't good enough.

The four don't actually disagree about the company; they disagree about the standard for "how much return justifies taking risk."Duan Yongping demands at least a double, Buffett demands 15% annualized, Munger demands don't lose money, Li Lu demands threefold upside. Four standards, one conclusion.

The three numbers to remember after synthesis

119x. P/E after deducting stock-based compensation (Q2 annualised). This is the one number all four of us calculated, and the least susceptible to accounting manipulation.

49%. Shareholder earnings margin. The business itself is fine — this figure ranks in the top 5% of the software industry. That it coexists with 119x is the complete definition of "good business, bad price."

1.74x. TCV / revenue. The only leading indicator still in healthy territory. A fall below 1.2 would be the first signal of fundamental trouble — earlier than a decline in revenue growth, and more reliable than a share price drop.

Conclusion

Synthesis: a good business with real profits and a wide moat (on the government side), but priced with no margin of safety. The median valuation across the four is USD 120, 37% below the current price. Three numbers to remember: 119x P/E, a 49% shareholder earnings margin, 1.74x TCV/revenue. Conclusion: avoid; watch TCV/revenue and SBC as a share of revenue.
