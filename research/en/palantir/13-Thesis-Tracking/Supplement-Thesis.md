## Assumption 1: AIP drives US commercial growth of over 40% annually

This is the foundation of the entire thesis. If it does not hold, Palantir is a government IT contractor and should be valued at 8–12x price-to-sales.

Strength of evidence: high. Q2 US commercial USD 764 million, +149% year over year; TCV USD 2.132 billion, +153%; RDV USD 6.238 billion, +124%. The three figures corroborate one another — more signed (TCV), more retained (RDV), more recognized (revenue). **This is not a one-quarter fluke but the result of four consecutive quarters of acceleration.**

The base is the risk. The 149% growth rests on a base of USD 307 million last year (derived). By 2027 the base will exceed USD 1.5 billion and growth will naturally fall to 40%–50%. **So the test for this assumption is not "sustain 149%" but "no lower than 40%."** Below 30%, and the foundation shifts.

## Assumption 2: government business growth stays above 20%

This claim is harder to verify than the first. **US government revenue rose 90% in Q2, which looks extremely strong, but total TCV grew only 49% — indicating no large government contracts were signed this quarter and revenue is being recognized from contracts signed earlier.**

Government business is characterized by "step functions": sign one large contract, revenue jumps a level, then stays flat for several years. The +90% in Q2 most likely comes from execution of Maven-related contracts signed in 2025. Without new strategic-level large contracts in the second half of 2026, growth will fall below 20% in 2027.

What to watch: **AI program appropriations in the NDAA (National Defense Authorization Act), and the procurement pace of the Department of Defense's Next Generation Command and Control (NGC2).** These two are the most direct leading indicators on the government side, running two to three quarters ahead of earnings reports.

## Assumption 3 and 4: margins and orders, one good and one bad

The margin thesis holds. A 47.1% GAAP operating margin, four consecutive quarters of expansion, and the expense ratio compressed from 46% to 33%. **The operating leverage is real, not a one-off cost cut.** Test: quarterly margin ≥45%; as long as it stays above that line, the business model is fine.

Orders are flashing red. TCV +49% vs revenue +93%, a 44-percentage-point gap. As I analyzed in m07, this is structural (no large government deals, slow international), not an accounting issue. **The test is not "TCV growth catching up to revenue" — that is impossible; it is "TCV/revenue ≥1.5x."** It is currently 1.74x, still healthy, but trending down.

If this ratio falls below 1.2, it means "new contracts cover only 1.2 years of revenue" — at that point, even if revenue is still growing, it is living off the backlog.

## Assumptions 5 and 6: dilution and multiples, two soft constraints

SBC has not improved. A 13.7% share of revenue and 2% annual dilution — three years of the same story, nothing has changed. **This is not a case of management being unable to do it; they do not want to** — Silicon Valley's logic of retaining talent with stock is deeply entrenched, and Karp's own USD 1.1 billion pay package is also stock.

The multiple is the most fragile leg. It currently sits at 74x TTM and 39x 2027 revenue. For this investment to pay off, the multiple must hold or expand. **And the multiple is the only one of these six assumptions the company does not control.** It is driven by rates, risk appetite and the AI narrative cycle.

So the structure of the whole thesis is this: **five legs the company can deliver on its own, one leg that depends on the market's goodwill.** That structure looks solid in a bull market and is most fragile at a turning point.

## Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar

Four falsification conditions are fixed; if any one triggers, this thesis is void and coverage stops:

- TCV/revenue below 1.2x for two consecutive quarters;
- US commercial revenue growth below 40% for two consecutive quarters;
- GAAP operating margin falls below 40% (signaling a price war or runaway costs);
- US government revenue posts negative growth for two consecutive quarters (signaling a change in budget or procurement rules).

Watch calendar: **early November** Q3 results (watch TCV and government growth); **December** NDAA finalized (watch AI appropriations); **February 2027** Q4 results + 2027 guidance (the most important one); **May 2027** Q1 results (test the credibility of full-year guidance).

Price watching needs no calendar, only a condition: revalue when PS(TTM) ≤30x (about USD 80). Until then, twenty minutes a quarter on these three numbers is enough.
