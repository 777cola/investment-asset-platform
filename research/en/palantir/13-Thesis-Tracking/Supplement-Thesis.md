## Assumption 1: AIP drives US commercial growth above 40% a year

This is the foundation of the entire thesis. If it fails, Palantir is a government IT contractor and should trade at 8–12x price-to-sales.

Strength of evidence: high. Q2 US commercial revenue USD 764 million, +149% YoY; TCV USD 2.132 billion, +153%; RDV USD 6.238 billion, +124%. The three figures corroborate one another — more signed (TCV), more retained (RDV), more recognised (revenue). This is no one-quarter fluke but the product of four consecutive quarters of acceleration.

The base is the risk. The 149% growth rests on a base of USD 307 million last year (derived). By 2027 the base will exceed USD 1.5 billion and growth will naturally fall to 40%–50%. The test for this assumption is therefore not "sustain 149%" but "no lower than 40%." Below 30%, and the foundation shifts.

## Assumption 2: government business growth stays above 20%

This claim is harder to verify than the first. US government revenue rose 90% in Q2, which looks extremely strong, but total TCV grew only 49% — implying no large government contracts were signed this quarter and that revenue is being recognised from contracts signed earlier.

Government business runs on "step functions": sign one large contract, revenue jumps a level, then stays flat for several years. The +90% in Q2 most likely reflects execution of Maven-related contracts signed in 2025. Absent new strategic-level large contracts in 2H26, growth will fall below 20% in 2027.

What to watch: AI programme appropriations in the NDAA (National Defense Authorization Act), and the procurement pace of the Department of Defense's Next Generation Command and Control (NGC2). These are the most direct leading indicators on the government side, running two to three quarters ahead of reported earnings.

## Assumption 3 and 4: margins and orders, one good and one bad

The margin thesis holds: a 47.1% GAAP operating margin, four consecutive quarters of expansion, and the expense ratio compressed from 46% to 33%. The operating leverage is real, not a one-off cost cut. Test: quarterly margin ≥45%; as long as it stays above that line, the business model is fine.

Orders are flashing red: TCV +49% vs revenue +93%, a 44-percentage-point gap. As analysed in m07, this is structural (no large government deals, slow international), not an accounting issue. The test is not "TCV growth catching up to revenue" — that is impossible; it is "TCV/revenue ≥1.5x." It is currently 1.74x, still healthy, but trending down.

If this ratio falls below 1.2, it means "new contracts cover only 1.2 years of revenue" — at that point, even if revenue is still growing, it is living off the backlog.

## Assumptions 5 and 6: dilution and multiples, two soft constraints

SBC has not improved: 13.7% of revenue and 2% annual dilution — three years of the same story, nothing has changed. This is not a case of management being unable to do it; they do not want to — Silicon Valley's stock-based retention logic is deeply entrenched, and Karp's own USD 1.1 billion pay package is also stock.

The multiple is the most fragile leg. It currently sits at 74x TTM and 39x 2027 revenue. For this investment to pay off, the multiple must hold or expand. And the multiple is the only one of these six assumptions the company does not control. It is driven by rates, risk appetite and the AI narrative cycle.

So the structure of the whole thesis is this: five legs the company can deliver on its own, one leg that depends on the market's goodwill. That structure looks solid in a bull market and is most fragile at a turning point.

## Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar

Four falsification conditions are fixed; if any one triggers, the thesis is void and coverage stops:

- TCV/revenue below 1.2x for two consecutive quarters;

- US commercial revenue growth below 40% for two consecutive quarters;

- GAAP operating margin below 40% (signaling a price war or runaway costs);

- US government revenue declining for two consecutive quarters (signaling a change in budget or procurement rules).

Calendar to watch: early November Q3 results (TCV and government growth); December NDAA finalized (AI appropriations); February 2027 Q4 results + 2027 guidance (the most important); May 2027 Q1 results (test the credibility of full-year guidance).

Price watching needs no calendar, only a condition: revalue when P/S (TTM) ≤30x (about USD 80). Until then, twenty minutes a quarter on these three numbers is enough.
