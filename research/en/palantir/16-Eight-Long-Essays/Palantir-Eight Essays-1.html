01 Cognitive reset: you think you know Palantir, but you only know its share price — Palantir

Move past two misconceptions: "government contractor" and "AI concept stock"
Research date 2026/10/05  ·  Price $189.40 (+0.34%)  ·  Market cap About USD 456 billion  ·  PS(TTM) Approximately 74x

Core view

The two most common takes on Palantir are both wrong. The first: it is an IT contractor that lives off government relationships. The second: it is a stock inflated by the AI narrative. The first is wrong because US commercial revenue alone is USD 764 million, 39.5% of total revenue, up 149% year on year; the second is wrong because its profits are real.

This piece dismantles both misconceptions and then rebuilds the question: if it is neither a contractor nor a concept stock, then what exactly is it?

Misconception one: it is a government contractor

That impression comes from the revenue mix at its 2020 listing — government revenue was then over half of the total, and the client list was all Department of Defense, CIA and FBI. Today, US government revenue is USD 809 million, or 41.8%, and US commercial is USD 764 million, or 39.5% — the two now run nearly neck and neck.

More important is where growth comes from. In Q2, US commercial revenue rose 149% year on year and US government 90%. Commercial growth is 1.65x government growth, and it has led for four consecutive quarters. If this were a government contractor, that number could not appear — a contractor's growth is set by the budget and does not double on its own.

But it is not a pure enterprise software company either. It sells through on-site staffing, customization and process reengineering, and its customer count grows only 24% a year. The accurate description: it is a company that sells software the way a consultancy does. That hybrid shows up in the financials as — an 80% gross margin (a software trait), and a low sales-expense ratio but high delivery cost (a consulting trait).

Misconception 2: It is an AI concept stock

After AIP launched in 2023, its share price ran from USD 8 to USD 190, a 23-fold gain. That move led many to file it under "AI concept stock" — meaning a good story with no profit.

But look at the numbers: GAAP operating margin 47.1%, free cash flow margin 63.1%, net cash USD 9.2 billion, no interest-bearing debt. This is not what a concept stock looks like. This is what a money printer looks like. Most AI concept stocks are still loss-making, burning cash and surviving on financing.

The correct read, then: it is a highly profitable software company pushed by the AI narrative to a price that does not belong to a software company. The two must be kept separate — the former is fundamentals, the latter is valuation. Confuse them and you equate "good company" with "buyable stock", when a price sits between the two.

So what exactly is it

Three words: ontology, subscription, forward deployment.

Ontology is the product — it translates a customer's business world into a map a machine can compute on. Subscription is the revenue model — annual billing, high renewal rates. Forward deployment is the sales method — engineers go on-site and rewrite the workflow.

This combination is rare. Pure software companies (Snowflake) have no forward deployment; they sell tools. Pure consultancies (Accenture) have no product; they sell headcount. Pure government contractors (Booz Allen) have neither; they sell compliance. Palantir has all three, so its gross margin looks like software, its growth looks like SaaS, and its customer relationships look like consulting.

Only by understanding this can you understand why its customer count grew just 24% while revenue grew 93% — because its growth does not come from acquiring new customers but from going deep with each one. And only then can you understand why its valuation is so expensive — the market prices it as SaaS (multiples tied to growth), but its growth model is actually closer to consulting (project-driven, people-driven, with a ceiling set by customer count). This mismatch is the core of its valuation risk.

Conclusion

The conclusion of the cognitive reset: it is not a government contractor, nor an AI concept stock; it is a company that sells software through a consulting model, with excellent margins, extremely fast growth, but a very narrow customer base. This definition is more useful than any label—it simultaneously explains its high margins (software) and its growth ceiling (consulting).
