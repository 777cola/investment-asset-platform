08 Decision framework: turn "at what price to buy" into a rule — Palantir

Discipline, not judgment

Core thesis

This series runs to eight pieces; the first seven describe what this company is. This one addresses a more practical question: when you face a good company at a bad price, what should you actually do?

The answer is not "wait" — it is "write down the conditions for waiting, then stop watching the tape."

Step 1: Separate "company view" from "price view"

These two things are often conflated. The conclusion of the company judgment is "a good company"; the conclusion of the price judgment is "too expensive." Once separated, the action becomes clear: a good company goes on the watchlist; a price that is too expensive means you don't buy.

The confusion happens at the second step: buying because it is "a good company," then explaining away "short-term losses" with "long-term bullishness." This is the most common path to losing money. A good company can lose you money as long as you pay a high enough price—the Coca-Cola example from 1998 is right there.

Step two: turn the buy criteria into verifiable numbers

"Wait for it to get cheaper" is not a condition; "PS 30x" is. Three conditions, in priority order:

  • Price: PS (TTM) ≤30x, roughly USD 80. This is a hard condition; sentiment does not factor in.
  • Fundamentals: TCV/revenue ≥1.5x (currently 1.74), US commercial growth ≥40%.
  • Quality: SBC/revenue ≤10% (currently 13.7%), operating margin ≥45%.

Buy only when the price threshold is met and fundamentals are intact. If the price threshold is not met, do not buy no matter how good the fundamentals. If the price threshold is met but fundamentals have deteriorated, also do not buy—that is a value trap.

Step 3: Position sizing and stop-loss, set in advance

First tranche of 3% at USD 80, add to 5% at USD 55 (cap). Why not buy the full position at once? Because you have no absolute conviction on how long its growth rate can hold; scaling in is how you price uncertainty.

Set stops on the thesis, not on price.Four thesis stops: TCV/revenue below 1.2 for two consecutive quarters, US commercial growth below 40% for two consecutive quarters, operating margin falling below 40%, US government revenue contracting for two consecutive quarters.

Price only decides whether to buy, not whether to sell.When buying, price is your friend; when selling, fundamentals are your friend.

Step 4: Update frequency — twenty minutes per quarter

No need to watch the market every day. When quarterly earnings come out, spend twenty minutes on four numbers: revenue growth, TCV/revenue, operating margin, SBC as a share of revenue. If all four are within range, do nothing.

If the thesis stop-loss triggers, act. If the buy condition triggers, redo the valuation.The rest of the time, don't look.

The value of this discipline isn't in "getting it right" — it's in "not being carried away by volatility."Palantir fell more than 5% in a single day on three days within three months, with no announcement — if you watch the market every day, you will certainly do something, and those things are most likely wrong.

Step 5: Accept that it may never come

This is actually the hardest rule. A great company may never return to 30x price-to-sales — if it keeps growing above 50%, the market will keep giving it a high multiple.

So what do you do then? Nothing. You miss it. Your portfolio does not need to own every great company, only the great companies whose price is below value.

Duan Yongping said something similar: investing is not an exam, you do not have to answer every question. You can answer only the ones you are sure of and skip the rest. Skipping Palantir will not drop your annualized return from 15% to 14%, but buying it at 74x price-to-sales could turn one of your years into −50%.

Conclusion

The conclusion of the decision framework: turn judgment into rules, turn rules into numbers, then execute. Buy conditions: PS 30x (about USD 80) + TCV/revenue ≥1.5 + US commercial growth ≥40%. Position: start at 3%, cap at 5%. Stop-loss: the four thesis points, not price.Current status: wait, twenty minutes per quarter. It may never come; accept that.
