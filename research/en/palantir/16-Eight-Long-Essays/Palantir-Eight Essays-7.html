07 Risk checklist: six items, ranked by probability of killing the investment — Palantir

Valuation 60%, government budget 20%, competition 12%, dilution 5%, governance 3%

Core thesis

Risk lists are easy to write as "worry about everything at once." This one is not: it is ranked by "starting from today's USD 189.40, which one is most likely to lose you money."

The conclusion is somewhat counterintuitive: the biggest risk is not that the company goes wrong, but that the company is fine and the price mean-reverts.

Risk 1 (60%): valuation reversion

74x P/S: USD 120 base case, 37% downside; USD 70 bear case, 63% downside. Neither number requires anything to go wrong at the company — only multiple reversion.

The historical reference sits with the company itself: 45x P/S in November 2021, 6x in December 2022 — an 85% decline — while revenue grew 24% over the same period. Fundamentals unchanged, multiple changed, stock fell 85%. This is not a hypothesis; it happened within four years.

Triggers: growth falling from 93% to below 50%; or rising rates and falling risk appetite; or a broad unwind of the AI narrative. Any one of the three is enough.

Risk 2 (20%): government budget and concentration

The U.S. government accounts for 41.8% of revenue; add international governments and the figure exceeds half (our rough calculation). Renewals, budget line items and priorities all follow politics.

Specific triggers: the pace of AI program funding in the NDAA, whether the GSA folds these tools into a unified procurement catalog, and post-election priority shifts at the Department of Defense. None of these are within the company's control.

Historical reference: during the 2025 DOGE federal contract cuts, Palantir's stock price volatility widened markedly — the market is extremely sensitive to the words "government spending."

Risk 3 (12%): included rather than defeated

The most dangerous competition is not a rival building a better product; it is a cloud vendor making this capability a default feature. Microsoft Fabric, AWS Bedrock and Google Vertex are all moving in that direction. Once customers decide that "what comes with the cloud is good enough," the question shifts from "must sign" to "can cut."

Open source is closing in too. The ontology layer is not irreproducible technology; it is an engineering paradigm. The odds are not low that within three years an open-source ontology framework capable of running in the customer's own data center will appear.

Risk 4 (5%) and Risk 5 (3%): dilution and governance

Equity incentives run at 13.7% and the share count grows 2% a year—value leakage that is certain to recur annually. On a valuation assuming a 30% expected return, 2% leakage cuts that return to 27.4%.

Governance: Class F shares lock up voting control, leaving outside shareholders unable to change direction. Karp's personal style and political stance are themselves part of the company's brand; his departure would trigger a narrative discount.

Risk 6 (no probability assigned, but essential to flag): where this report could be wrong

The one place I could be wrong is the discount rate. If the risk-free rate settles below 3% for the long term and the market's discount rate for growth stocks falls systematically, a 24x P/S could become 36x, and the neutral scenario would then be USD 180.

That is the only thing that could make "avoid" wrong. But it is a macro assumption, not a company judgment — I will not buy a stock at 74x P/S for the sake of a macro assumption. If it happens, what I miss is a stretch of upside; if it does not, what I avoid is a stretch of downside.

The asymmetry is this: a missed opportunity can come again, but a loss takes time to climb back from. That is why, at these odds, I choose not to participate.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: four of the six risks are price problems and two are business problems. Price problems are solved by waiting; business problems are solved by tracking. The largest (valuation reversion, 60%) needs nothing to go wrong at the company—only time. Conclusion: avoid; monitoring indicators: TCV/revenue, US commercial growth, SBC as a share of revenue, PS multiple.
