05 Industry and competition: rivals are on a different track — Palantir

No competitors at its own layer, giants upstream, and a savvier customer base downstream

Core view

The most common mistake in reading a competitive landscape is to look only at your own layer. At its layer — ontology and decision-making — Palantir has almost no rival: C3.ai is an order of magnitude smaller, Databricks sits a layer below, Snowflake a layer below that.

The real threat comes from two directions: cloud vendors upstream pushing down (distribution advantage), and consultancies downstream climbing up (delivery advantage). Both are happening at once in 2026.

Same layer: why no rival

The ontology and decision layer demand three things: a product, an on-site team and customer trust. Globally, only Palantir has all three. C3.ai has a product but no team, Databricks has technology but no business semantics, and Booz Allen has customers but no product.

This "three-in-one" structure is hard to replicate because the challenge is organisational, not technological: you must sustain an engineering force of several thousand people and a world-class software team at the same time, and the cultures of the two conflict. Palantir took fifteen years to make them coexist.

Competition at the same layer therefore will not be intense within three years. But that does not mean safety—because what kills you is often not a rival at your layer, but the layer that renders you "unnecessary."

Microsoft's distribution is the most dangerous weapon upstream

Microsoft has three things Palantir does not: enterprise distribution (default entry points in Office, Teams and Azure), the CFO's trust (existing contracts, completed security reviews) and procurement convenience (no new vendor onboarding). Fabric bundles data integration, governance and BI into Azure, so customers "already have it".

This is not a product contest; it is a contest of purchasing habits. A CFO is far more willing to approve "add a module to an existing cloud contract" than "sign a new vendor and go through six months of security review".

Where is Microsoft weak? In "understanding the business". Fabric is a general-purpose tool; it does not know how a customer schedules production lines, adjudicates policies or deploys troops. That knowledge comes from on-site accumulation, and Microsoft is not in that business. So the battlefield lies between "general" and "specialized" — Palantir wins on depth, Microsoft wins on breadth.

Downstream: consulting firms are getting smarter

Accenture, Deloitte and Booz Allen are all building their own AI delivery capabilities. They win on deep client relationships, cheaper pricing and no need for clients to change processes; they lose on product, so every engagement starts from scratch.

They are not taking Palantir's core customers, but the segment that "cannot afford Palantir." That segment is precisely Palantir's future growth space—meaning its downmarket is being intercepted.

Anduril is a newer threat. It bundles hardware (drones, sentry towers) with software (Lattice), and its influence at the Department of Defense is rising fast. If DoD procurement logic shifts from "buying software" to "buying capability," Anduril is the biggest beneficiary.

Cycle: both engines rising together — the best of times

Revenue rides two cycles: government budgets (defense AI procurement) and enterprise AI budgets. Both trend up in 2026—the macro backdrop for 93% growth.

Both cycles moving up in tandem means the risk of them moving down in tandem is underappreciated. If defense budget growth slows in 2027 and enterprise AI budgets shift from "must do" to "wait and see," both engines decelerate at once. A 74x price-to-sales ratio leaves no room for that scenario.

Conclusion

Competitive conclusion: unrivalled at its layer, giants upstream, customers downstream getting smarter. Microsoft's distribution is the most dangerous threat: it does not compete on product, it competes on procurement convenience. On the cycle, both engines are pointing up at once — the best of times, and also the most expensive.
