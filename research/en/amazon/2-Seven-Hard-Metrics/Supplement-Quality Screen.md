## Of the six criteria met, one was discounted

ROE of about 30%, ROIC of about 15%, gross margin of 52.4%, operating cash flow/net profit of 1.24x, interest coverage of 16.6x—these five metrics are solid and would rank as first-class in any industry. The 4th point deserves separate mention: the 19.4% net margin is a book figure; other income of USD 68,359 million in the first nine months accounted for 46% of pre-tax profit, mainly from a large appreciation in the Anthropic investment. Strip that out and the net margin is 10.4%, just barely at the threshold.

This adjustment is not nitpicking; it is about judging the nature of the profit. Operating profit (operating income of RMB 79.39 billion, up 44% year on year) is what this business actually earns. Investment gains are an accounting result of asset appreciation—realizable, but not a measure of business capability. Buying Amazon means buying the former; earning money from the latter is just luck.

## The only line that missed: RMB -42.4 billion — but that is not the same as the business deteriorating

TTM free cash flow was -RMB 42.4 billion versus +RMB 14.8 billion a year earlier, a 386% YoY swing. At the second-quarter report it was still -RMB 7.6 billion, implying roughly -RMB 34.8 billion for the quarter (backed out). The reason is singular: purchases of property and equipment reached RMB 77.26 billion in the quarter, +120% YoY, taking cumulative capex for the first three quarters to nearly RMB 165 billion against full-year guidance of RMB 220 billion.

What does failing this test mean? It means the company cannot be valued on discounted cash flow, only on earnings. It also means external financing is required: long-term debt rose from RMB 65.6 billion to RMB 173.1 billion, and interest expense climbed from RMB 538 million to RMB 1.691 billion, +214% YoY. This is the accounting imprint of a switch from "cash-rich" to "capital-intensive."

Is it fatal? That depends on the nature of the capex. Amazon's spending is almost entirely new capacity (data centres, chips, power) rather than maintenance, and that capacity is already booked by customers through 2027–2028 (order backlog of RMB 578 billion). Deferrable spending is not rigid spending—the most fundamental difference between this and companies that "burn cash for growth." If the order book is real, this RMB 220 billion is an asset; if it is fake, it is an impairment.

## Three things this indicator set cannot see

First, depreciation elasticity. Servers are depreciated over 5–6 years, and brokers estimate that new computing assets in 2026 will carry roughly USD 40 billion of annual incremental depreciation. Extending useful life by one year inflates current-period profit substantially, with no effect on cash flow. This is the easiest lever for such companies to "engineer" profit, so useful-life assumptions must be checked in the annual report notes.

Second, the denominator effect of capex. ROIC's denominator is invested capital: RMB 220 billion of spending enters the denominator now, while the return enters the numerator two to three years later. Today's 15% ROIC has therefore been diluted by new capacity, and the denominator is still expanding.

Third, profit concentration in advertising and AWS. AWS alone contributes RMB 17.78 billion of operating profit, 63% of the group total; advertising generates RMB 19.6 billion of revenue at very high gross margin but does not disclose profit separately. Group margins are therefore highly sensitive to AWS pricing and utilization—a 5-point drop in AWS operating margin cuts group margin by 2.8 points.
