Core thesis

Six of seven hard metrics pass. The one that fails is the most critical: TTM free cash flow of -USD 42.4 billion, swinging from +USD 14.8 billion the prior year and negative for two consecutive quarters. The other six clear their thresholds, but one needs a discount—the 19.4% net margin includes USD 68.3 billion in unrealized gains on the Anthropic investment; strip that out and it is only 10.4%, just above the 10% line.

This metric set is designed to screen out "sub-tier-one companies." Amazon passing six shows it is not a mediocre business; the one it fails shows it is currently not a cash-printing machine but a machine under construction. Application: do not reject on a single failure, but cut the initial position to one-third because of this one.

Of the six criteria met, one was discounted

ROE of about 30%, ROIC of about 15%, gross margin of 52.4%, operating cash flow/net profit of 1.24x, interest coverage of 16.6x—these five metrics are solid and would rank as first-class in any industry. The 4th point deserves separate mention: the 19.4% net margin is a book figure; other income of USD 68,359 million in the first nine months accounted for 46% of pre-tax profit, mainly from a large appreciation in the Anthropic investment. Strip that out and the net margin is 10.4%, just barely at the threshold.

This adjustment is not nitpicking; it is about judging the nature of the profit. Operating profit (operating income of RMB 79.39 billion, up 44% year on year) is what this business actually earns. Investment gains are an accounting result of asset appreciation—realizable, but not a measure of business capability. Buying Amazon means buying the former; earning money from the latter is just luck.

The only line that missed: RMB -42.4 billion, but that is not the same as the business deteriorating

TTM free cash flow was -RMB 42.4 billion, versus +RMB 14.8 billion in the prior-year period, a 386% year-on-year swing; at the second-quarter report it was still -RMB 7.6 billion, meaning roughly -RMB 34.8 billion for the quarter (backed out). There is only one reason: purchases of property and equipment were RMB 77.26 billion for the quarter, +120% year on year, and cumulative capital expenditure for the first three quarters has reached nearly RMB 165 billion, against full-year guidance of RMB 220 billion.

What does failing this test mean? It means you cannot value the company on discounted cash flow, only on earnings; it also means it needs external financing—long-term debt rose from RMB 65.6 billion to RMB 173.1 billion, and interest expense rose from RMB 538 million to RMB 1.691 billion, +214% year on year. This is the accounting imprint of a switch from "cash-rich" to "capital-intensive."

Is it fatal? That depends on the nature of the capital expenditure. Amazon's spending is almost entirely new capacity (data centers, chips, power), not maintenance repairs, and that capacity is already booked by customers through 2027–2028 (order backlog of RMB 578 billion). Deferrable spending is not rigid spending—this is the most fundamental difference between it and companies that "burn cash for growth." If the order book is real, this RMB 220 billion is an asset; if the order book is fake, it is an impairment.

Three things this indicator set cannot see

First, depreciation elasticity. Servers are depreciated over 5–6 years, and brokers estimate that new computing assets in 2026 will correspond to roughly USD 40 billion in annual incremental depreciation. Extending the useful life by one year inflates current-period profit substantially, with no effect on cash flow. This is the easiest profit for such companies to "engineer," so the useful-life assumptions must be checked in the annual report notes.

Second, the denominator effect of capex. The denominator of ROIC is invested capital; RMB 220 billion of spending enters the denominator now, while the return enters the numerator two to three years later. Today's 15% ROIC has therefore been diluted by new capacity, and the denominator is still expanding.

Third, profit concentration in advertising and AWS. AWS alone contributes RMB 17.78 billion of operating profit, 63% of the group total; advertising has RMB 19.6 billion of revenue and very high gross margin but does not disclose profit separately. This means group margins are highly sensitive to AWS pricing and utilization—a 5-point drop in AWS operating margin cuts group margin by 2.8 points.

Conclusion

Six of seven criteria met—qualifies for the portfolio, not for a heavy position. The one failure (free cash flow) is the only metric in the entire set that cannot be explained by "industry characteristics": RMB 90 billion in profit against negative RMB 42.4 billion in cash flow, with the entire gap in capital expenditure, and the pace of capex still accelerating. The approach is simple—maintain the Buy rating, cap the initial position at one-third, and add after depreciation and capex data for Q1 2027 are released.
