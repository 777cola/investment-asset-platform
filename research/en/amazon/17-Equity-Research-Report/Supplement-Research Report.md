## I. Company Overview

Amazon (AMZN, NASDAQ) is the world's largest e-commerce platform and largest cloud computing provider. The stock closed at USD 259.30 on November 6, 2026, implying a market cap of about USD 2.80 trillion (259.30 × 10.81 billion shares) and a TTM P/E of 20.8x. The business spans four segments: North America retail (revenue USD 112.85 billion/quarter), international retail (USD 43.11 billion), AWS (USD 45.98 billion), and advertising and subscriptions (USD 19.64 billion + USD 14.01 billion). Headcount stands at 1.638 million, up 4% YoY.

Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was USD 584.07 billion, up 16% YoY, with operating profit of USD 79.39 billion, up 44.4% YoY, and net profit of USD 113.59 billion (including USD 68.36 billion of non-operating investment gains). Management guides fourth-quarter revenue to USD 240–247 billion and operating profit to USD 28.5–33.5 billion.

## II. Industry analysis

Two tracks. Cloud: in 2026Q3 AWS posted USD 46 billion, Azure grew 48% and Google Cloud reached USD 30.7 billion/+103%; all three are accelerating, with the industry growing above 40%. The competitive axis has shifted from "enterprises moving to the cloud" to "AI training and inference", and model partnerships have become the new deciding factor (Microsoft tied to OpenAI through April 2026, Amazon aligned with both Anthropic and OpenAI).

E-commerce: nearly 80% of global retail still happens offline (Jassy's own words), and rising online penetration is a long-term trend. Amazon adds roughly twice the quarterly sales of its second-largest competitor in the US. Advertising is the third track: Amazon uses "transaction intent" to capture search ad budgets, with Q3 revenue of USD 19.64 billion, +12%.

## II (supplementary) Industry size: cloud and e-commerce penetration both remain early

The ceiling on each market sets the ceiling on valuation. Cloud: cloud infrastructure's share of global enterprise IT spending is still climbing, and AWS's USD 184 billion annualized run rate captures only a sliver of global IT spend; AI inference has only just begun moving into production, and Jassy describes enterprise-scale adoption as "still at a very early stage." E-commerce: nearly 80% of global retail still happens offline, and every 1 percentage point of online penetration gain represents tens of billions of dollars of incremental GMV opportunity for Amazon.

The penetration story, however, is deceptive. "Early" does not mean "fast": US online penetration has already passed its steepest stretch, and 2% growth in online first-party sales in 2026 is the evidence. The real high growth lies not in "more people buying online" but in "the same people buying more categories" (grocery, pharmacy) and in "seller and advertising monetization."

This report therefore does not invoke "penetration gains" to justify a valuation premium; it uses them only to gauge how long the business can run. The answer is a long time — but the driver is not penetration, rather category expansion and cloud unit compute demand.

## III. Business model

The flywheel runs through four stages: retail scale → logistics efficiency → member retention → traffic monetisation (advertising) → cash flow funding AWS capex → cloud scale lowering company-wide technology costs. Each stage has a verifiable metric: retail scale via paid unit sales (+12%), logistics efficiency via the gap between shipping cost growth and unit sales growth (-400bp), membership via subscription revenue (+11%), advertising via revenue growth (+12%), and AWS via operating margin (38.7%).

By profit structure, AWS contributes 63.3% of operating profit, North America retail 31.1% and international 5.6%. Valuation therefore has to be sum-of-the-parts — a cloud company plus a retail company — not a single consolidated multiple.Valuing AWS on retail multiples would severely undervalue it; valuing retail on cloud multiples would severely overvalue it.

## IV. Financial analysis

The income statement delivered the strongest quarter on record: operating profit of RMB 28.08 billion, +61%, and an operating margin of 13.9%. Gross margin was 53.2% (50.8% prior year), fulfillment expenses rose 8.4% (below volume growth), technology and infrastructure +20.2%, sales and marketing -0.3% and general and administrative -6.1%.

The cash flow statement delivered the weakest quarter: purchases of property and equipment of RMB 77.26 billion, +120%, and TTM free cash flow of -RMB 42.4 billion (prior year +RMB 14.8 billion), still -RMB 7.6 billion at the second-quarter report. Long-term debt rose from RMB 65.6 billion to RMB 173.1 billion; interest expense was RMB 1.69 billion in the quarter, +214%, with interest coverage of 16.6x. Operating cash flow TTM was RMB 168.3 billion, +28.7%.

Profit quality caveat: other income of RMB 68.36 billion over the first three quarters accounted for 46% of pre-tax profit. Excluding it, net margin was 10.4% (19.4% reported) and the TTM P/E rose from 20.8x to about 34x.

## V. Management and governance

CEO Andy Jassy took office in July 2021, having joined in 1997, founded AWS in 2006 and served as AWS CEO from 2016. CFO Brian Olsavsky has held the role since 2015, while Matt Garman has led AWS since June 2024 and Doug Herrington heads Worldwide Stores.

Ownership: Bezos holds 884 million shares (8.2%) and serves as Executive Chairman; Jassy holds 2.3 million shares (0.02%) plus 1.049 million unvested RSUs; institutions hold about 76% (Vanguard 7.8%, BlackRock 6.9%). The governance record is clean, with no accounting restatements and no SEC enforcement, though the FTC antitrust lawsuit is still advancing. Capital allocation is aggressive: 2026 capex of RMB 220 billion, RMB 107.5 billion in debt raised, and no dividend or buyback.

## Six, growth drivers

Four drivers, ranked by certainty. First, AWS capacity release: a RMB 578 billion backlog locked through 2027–2028, with management guiding growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. Second, retail margins: North America at 7.7% versus 4.3% a year earlier, with robotic warehouses and regionalised fulfilment still ramping and per-order fulfilment cost down four consecutive years. Third, advertising: Q4 guidance back to high-teens, with Bedrock and the enterprise customer data loop providing long-term support. Fourth, in-house chips: Graviton/Trainium revenue commitments up 20x YoY, external sales by end-2027, and customers saving over USD 2 billion this quarter.

Option-like businesses excluded from earnings forecasts: Amazon Leo (Globalstar spectrum for USD 11.5 billion, initial operations by end-2026), Zoox, pharmacy (customers +60%).

## VII. Competitive landscape

Cloud: AWS ranks first in scale (USD 46 billion) but third in growth (39.3%, versus Azure at 48% and GCP at 103%). Its moat is migration cost and in-house capacity; its weaknesses are enterprise relationships weaker than Microsoft's and model capability weaker than Google's. Retail: Walmart defends groceries and Temu/Shein attack the low-price segment, but neither can dislodge the "fast + broad + membership" combination. Advertising: competes with Google and Meta for budgets, with the advantage of transaction-intent data.

The most dangerous rival is Microsoft: as AI spending shifts from "innovation projects" to "IT infrastructure," decision rights return to the CIO, whose default choice is Microsoft. Next is Google Cloud's price anchor—if TPU unit costs are lower, AWS's pricing power comes under pressure.

## VIII. Risk factors

① Capex out of control: at RMB 330 billion in 2027 (Deutsche Bank), free cash flow stays negative; UBS calls it "close to zero or negative." ② Depreciation pressure: new computing assets added in 2026 carry roughly USD 40 billion of average annual incremental depreciation, released in a concentrated wave in 2027–2028; operating margin could fall back from 13.9% to 11%–12%. ③ Competitive price cuts: every 5% cut by AWS costs the group about -2.8pct of margin. ④ Demand cadence: a single year of slower AI training demand growth immediately pressures capacity utilization. ⑤ Debt cost: long-term debt +RMB 107.5 billion in a year; rising rates amplify interest expense. ⑥ Regulation: FTC antitrust case, AI regulation (concerns ease after the November 2026 midterms).

## VIII (Addendum) Stress Test: Share-Price Implications Under Three Bad Scenarios

Scenario 1: AWS growth falls to 25%. 2027 EPS of about USD 12 (depreciation weighs on profit, investment gains no longer contribute), market applies 18x, implying USD 216, -17%. This is the bear case in this report.

Scenario 2: AWS growth falls to 15% and capex does not come down. Depreciation is rigid, revenue stalls, operating margin drops below 10%, 2027 EPS of about USD 9, market applies 15x, implying USD 135, -48%. This is the real "thesis is broken" scenario; low probability but the downside must be known.

Scenario 3: AI trade cools systemically (multiple compression, no earnings hit). EPS still USD 12–15, but the multiple compresses from 20x to 15x, implying USD 187–225, -13% to -28%. This is the most likely one, and it already played out once in November 2026 (NVIDIA -14% in a single day).

What the three scenarios share: none bankrupts the company, all make the stock look ugly. That is why the position must be small enough that a 48% drop does not affect judgment.

## 9. Earnings forecast

2026E: revenue of about USD 828.0 billion (584.1 in the first three quarters plus a Q4 guidance midpoint of 243.5), up about 15.5% YoY; operating profit of about USD 110.4 billion (79.4 in the first three quarters plus a Q4 midpoint of 31.0), an operating margin of 13.3%. Book net income is distorted by investment gains and we do not forecast it at a single point.

2027E: our base case assumes revenue of USD 927.0 billion (+12%) and an operating margin of 13.5%, as rising depreciation is offset by AWS scale, giving operating profit of USD 125.1 billion; book net income of USD 145.0–160.0 billion (including continued investment revaluation, equivalent to EPS of USD 13.3–14.7); excluding investment gains, net income of about USD 90.0–95.0 billion, or EPS of USD 8.3–8.7. Our bear case assumes AWS growth slows to 25% and an operating margin of 11.5%; our bull case assumes AWS growth of 45%+ and an operating margin of 14.5%.

## X. Valuation

Three scenarios on 2027 EPS at 18/22/25x P/E: bear USD 12.0 × 18 = USD 216 (-17%); base USD 15.0 × 22 = USD 330 (+27%); bull USD 17.5 × 25 = USD 438 (+69%). Odds ratio 1.63 to 1.

Important note on valuation basis: The EPS above is "book EPS," which includes ongoing revaluation of investments such as Anthropic. On operating EPS excluding investment gains (2027E of about USD 8.3–8.7), the base-case target price of USD 330 implies roughly 38x — the biggest divergence between this report and the underlying work.The underlying work applies 18–25x to book EPS; this report holds that the market will eventually shift to an operating basis, and therefore maintains the target price while cutting the initial position to one-third.

## 10 (Supplementary) Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) Cross-Check

Cross-check using a completely different method: value the three segments separately. AWS: annualised revenue of RMB 184 billion; on 2027 revenue of RMB 260 billion and a 38% operating margin, operating profit of about RMB 98.8 billion; at 25x (the typical range for cloud companies), about USD 2.47 trillion. Retail (North America + International): annualised revenue of about RMB 620 billion, operating profit of about RMB 40 billion; at 15x, about RMB 600 billion. Advertising: annualised RMB 80 billion; at a 45% margin, operating profit of RMB 36 billion; at 20x, about RMB 720 billion. The three sum to about USD 3.79 trillion, or about USD 350 per share.

That is 6% above the neutral-case USD 330, so the direction is consistent. But all three assumptions are loose: 25x for AWS is not low, the 45% advertising margin is a rough estimate, and 15x for retail already prices in an efficiency-improvement premium. Tighten each assumption one notch (AWS 22x, advertising 18x, retail 12x) and the result is USD 2.9 trillion, or USD 268 per share—almost exactly the current price.

The SOTP conclusion matches the P/E method: the current price is roughly fair, with upside requiring AWS growth to deliver and downside cushioned by retail and advertising. It is not cheap; it is a buy with acceptable odds.

## XI. Investment recommendation

Buy; build the position in tranches; 12-month target price USD 330 (base case). Execution: open one-third at the current price of USD 259.30 (3%–5% position); after the Q1 2027 earnings report, add to 5%–6% if AWS growth is ≥35%, North America retail margin is ≥7%, and depreciation growth does not exceed operating profit growth; deploy the final tranche if the stock falls below USD 216 or free cash flow turns positive. Trim line USD 330. Stop-loss trigger: AWS backlog declines QoQ for two consecutive quarters.

Three reasons not to buy the full position: first, free cash flow is negative, leaving no defence when the market panics; second, the 34x operating P/E embeds no margin of safety; third, the stock is highly correlated with Microsoft, Alphabet and Nvidia, so total AI-factor exposure should be capped. Three reasons to buy: AWS acceleration is verifiable, retail margin is improving structurally, and the 20.8x book valuation sits below Microsoft's 25.8x.

## XI (Addendum) Comparison of Sell-Side Views

A week after earnings, sell-side views diverged sharply. Cuts: Oppenheimer 320 to 285 and Morgan Stanley 350 to 335, both citing capex above expectations and deteriorating free cash flow. Hikes: UBS 318 to 348 (pre-earnings), arguing "the market is underestimating 2027 AWS margins"; StoneX 400 to 380, maintaining Buy while flagging TTM levered free cash flow of -RMB 46.9 billion.

The split is fundamentally about time horizon: those looking 12 months out focus on free cash flow, those looking 24 months out on AWS margins. This report takes the middle path, a 12-month price target of USD 330 (close to the lowered sell-side median), but with position size cut to one-third, awaiting depreciation data in Q1 2027. This is not fence-sitting; it is translating "can't see clearly" into "buy less."

## Appendix 2: Three counterarguments to our conclusions

To avoid being persuaded by my own conclusion, let me list the strongest counterarguments. One, the depreciation timing mismatch. The 2026 margin was earned in a window before depreciation ramped up, so deriving 2027 EPS from an unsustainable margin is inherently optimistic. Two, AWS is no longer the fastest-growing cloud. Azure +48%, Google Cloud +103%. If incremental customers are captured by model-bound platforms, AWS becomes "the largest legacy cloud" and its valuation should gravitate toward a utility. Three, the financing method for capex is deteriorating. Long-term debt +RMB 107.5 billion in a year, interest expense +214%. That path works for two or three years; five years will force an equity issuance.

None of these three is enough to overturn "Buy," but all are enough to overturn "heavy position." This report maintains the Buy rating and caps the initial position at one-third precisely to price in these three arguments.

## Appendix 3: Sensitivity of Key Assumptions (Base Case)

Flex each of the two core base-case assumptions by one notch to gauge the impact on target price. AWS growth: 40%→35% gives 2027 EPS of about USD 14; at 22x that implies USD 308; →45% gives EPS of about USD 16.2, implying USD 356. Operating margin: 13.5%→12.5% (heavier depreciation) gives EPS of about USD 13.6, implying USD 299; →14.5% gives EPS of about USD 16.4, implying USD 361.

Conclusion: the target price range is USD 299–361, midpoint USD 330. In other words, the base case carries a ±10% margin of error—a range estimate, not a precise forecast. Any report claiming a "target price of USD 330.0" is offering false precision.
