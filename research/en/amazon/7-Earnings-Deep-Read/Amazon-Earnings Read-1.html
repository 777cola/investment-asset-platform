Core thesis

One set of results, two statements, two very different stories. The income statement shows operating profit of RMB 28.08 billion, up 61% YoY, and a record-high operating margin of 13.9%, with AWS contributing RMB 17.78 billion in the quarter. The cash flow statement shows purchases of property and equipment of RMB 77.26 billion, up 120% YoY, and TTM free cash flow of negative RMB 42.4 billion.

The split is clean: the profit improvement rests on three genuine efficiency gains (fulfillment costs, ad monetization, AWS scale), while the cash deterioration reflects one deliberate choice (racing for compute capacity). These are not results getting worse — they are results getting heavier.

Layer one: revenue mix — services revenue becomes the sole growth driver for the first time

Net sales reached RMB 201.94 billion, split between product sales of RMB 75.29 billion, up just 1.7%, and service sales of RMB 126.65 billion, up 19.4%. This marks a structural shift: Amazon's growth has decoupled from selling goods. The first nine months tell the same story, with products up 8.7% and services up 21.1%, the latter now 61.6% of total revenue.

The segment breakdown is starker still. Online stores rose only 2% to RMB 68.47 billion, against nearly 10% growth a year earlier, while third-party seller services grew 9% to RMB 46.49 billion; seller goods now account for 61% of global paid units, so Amazon increasingly resembles a platform collecting rent rather than a store earning the spread. Advertising grew 12% to RMB 19.64 billion and subscriptions 11% to RMB 14.01 billion, the two highest-margin businesses. In short: first-party is decelerating while platform, advertising and cloud accelerate, and revenue quality is improving.

One markdown to flag: management attributes advertising's mere 11%–12% growth to Prime Day shifting from Q3 to Q2, an impact of about 850 basis points, yet paid units still grew 12% over the same period. The explanation is credible but does not account for everything; Q4 guidance of a return to high-teens growth is a promise still to be verified.

Layer two: gross profit and expenses — three expense lines in conflict

Gross profit: revenue of RMB 201.94 billion less cost of revenue of RMB 94.57 billion gives gross profit of RMB 107.37 billion and a gross margin of 53.2% (52.4% for the first three quarters), up 2.4 percentage points from 50.8% a year earlier. The margin expansion was not price-driven; it came from the revenue mix shifting from goods to services.

The three expense lines moved in completely different directions: fulfillment expenses of RMB 29.997 billion, +8.4%, below the 12% volume growth — operating leverage at work; technology and infrastructure of RMB 34.814 billion, +20.2% (+23.3% for the first three quarters), reflecting AWS depreciation plus technical headcount, the fastest-growing line; sales and marketing of RMB 11.649 billion, -0.3%, and general and administrative of RMB 2.701 billion, -6.1% — the organizational contraction after cutting 30,000 jobs shows up directly in these two lines.

Taken together, the operating expense ratio fell from 90.3% to 86.1%, and the operating margin was 13.9%. The quality of this improvement is good: it was not bought by cutting long-term investment (technology and infrastructure is still surging), but came from fulfillment efficiency and organizational slimming.

Layer three: profit — operating profit is real, net profit is not

Operating profit came in at RMB 28.076 billion, up 61.2% YoY, for a 13.9% operating margin — the highest quarter in the company's history. AWS contributed 17.773 billion (63.3%), North America 8.723 billion (31.1%) and International 1.580 billion (5.6%). The margin gap across the three segments shows that this business earns almost all its profit from "renting out assets", not from "selling goods".

Net profit, by contrast, was 20.685 billion, down 2.4% YoY, and EPS was USD 1.89, down 3.1%. The cause is not weaker operations but the USD 9.5 billion pre-tax gain on Anthropic in the year-earlier period, which raised the base. Other income this quarter was in fact -703 million. In other words: operating profit rose 10.85 billion YoY while net profit fell 502 million — the entire gap comes from non-operating items.

The first three quarters need more careful reading: other income totaled 68.359 billion, 46% of pre-tax profit, and net profit rose 101% YoY. If you describe this company as "net profit doubled", you are talking about Anthropic's valuation, not Amazon's business.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — where the money went. The answer is a single line

Operating cash flow TTM RMB 168.3 billion, +28.7% YoY — healthy. Capex on property and equipment reached RMB 77.26 billion in the quarter, +120% YoY, or roughly RMB 165 billion cumulative over the first three quarters on a quarterly run-rate basis, against full-year guidance of RMB 220 billion. Free cash flow TTM was negative RMB 42.4 billion versus negative RMB 7.6 billion at the second-quarter report — implying the third quarter alone burned about RMB 34.8 billion.

Two balance-sheet shifts: long-term debt rose from RMB 65.6 billion to RMB 173.1 billion, up RMB 107.5 billion in a year, while quarterly interest expense climbed from RMB 538 million to RMB 1.691 billion. The company is funding its AI buildout with debt — the marker of a transition from cash-rich to capital-intensive. Interest coverage still stands at 16.6x; the problem is that multiple is falling fast.

An easily missed signal: sales and marketing and G&A expense lines are declining, while technology and infrastructure grows +20%. The company is cutting 30,000 corporate roles while installing one AI accelerator every 14 seconds — its current capital allocation stance in a nutshell: swap labour cost for compute capital, betting the latter earns the higher return.

A frequently overlooked accounting detail: the sign on other income

"Other income (net)" came in at -USD 703 million this quarter, versus +USD 10.186 billion in the prior-year period. The line swung from a large positive contribution to a modest drag—a USD 10.89 billion swing—which on its own accounts for the entire -2.4% YoY decline in net profit and has nothing to do with operations.

When reading quarterly reports, the sign change on this line warrants separate flagging: it sits outside operating control, yet it can turn "profit growth" into "profit decline." By the same token, the cumulative +RMB 68.359 billion over the first three quarters lifted net profit to +101% YoY. One line item, two directions, two narratives—readers of financial statements must decide for themselves which one to believe.

Conclusion

Read this earnings report by treating the income statement as proof of capability and the cash flow statement as the bill. The capability is real: a 13.9% operating margin is no one-off, with fulfilment efficiency, ad monetisation and AWS scale all improving. The bill is real too: RMB 220 billion of capex becomes tens of billions of depreciation a year in 2027–2028. One test matters: whether depreciation growth outpaces operating profit growth. Q4 guidance (revenue of RMB 240–247 billion, operating profit of RMB 28.5–33.5 billion) is the first checkpoint.
