Core view

A "buyable but not a heavy position — must be scaled in tranches" name. Its role is the portfolio's AI infrastructure core holding: target position 6%–8%, first tranche 3%–5%, with the remaining two tranches awaiting data. Rationale: 1.63 to 1 is adequate, but negative free cash flow means it offers no defense when the market panics.

On correlation, it must be counted alongside Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia and TSMC — all share the same factor (AI capex). Combined exposure no more than 25%, otherwise the portfolio becomes a single option on AI capex.

Price discipline: three price levels, three actions

USD 216 is the level to add, not the stop-loss. That is 18x 2027 EPS of USD 12, or 17.3x TTM EPS of USD 12.46 — clearly undervalued for a company with AWS growing 39%. USD 330 is the level to trim, 22x 2027 EPS of USD 15, where the risk-reward has already inverted. Set the stop on facts, not price: AWS backlog declining QoQ for two consecutive quarters.

Why anchor the stop to facts rather than price? Because price volatility is 15.7% (three-month range 240.35–278.09), and a price stop would be knocked out by noise. Backlog is the hardest metric to fake and the most leading — once it declines, customers are cancelling or deferring orders, reflecting demand two quarters ahead of the share price.

Correlation: it is the same bet as your other AI holdings

Amazon trades in lockstep with Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia and TSMC because they share a single revenue driver: hyperscaler capex. Amazon at USD 220 billion, Microsoft at USD 170 billion, Alphabet at USD 195–205 billion — combined, the four will spend over USD 200 billion per quarter in 2026. All four stocks rose 5%–6% on the same day, November 4, 2026 — direct proof of that correlation.

Manage exposure by factor, not by single stock. Recommendation: cap AI infrastructure (cloud + chips + optical modules + power equipment) at 25% of the portfolio combined, with Amazon at no more than 8%. If you already hold Nvidia and TSMC, cut Amazon to 3%–4%, because they will fall together.

Negatively or low-correlated hedges: Berkshire (cash + insurance float), consumer staples, and cash itself. An 8% position in a USD 2.8 trillion market-cap company is effectively leverage on the portfolio — so a hedge is mandatory.

Rebalancing discipline: look only four times a year

This is a name to review four times a year on the financials, not to monitor on the tape daily. The rebalancing point is fixed at one week after earnings: watch three numbers — AWS growth, AWS segment margin and TTM free cash flow. If all three stay within assumptions, do nothing; if any one breaks out, act per the table above.

Set one hard rule besides: when the unrealised gain on a single position exceeds 40%, trim it back to the target position cap. The bull case for Amazon is USD 438 (+69%); at that level the odds no longer justify holding an overweight. The value of this rule lies not in earning more, but in avoiding turning one successful call into one failed position.

Conclusion

Action: open a first tranche of 3%–5% at the current price; wait for Q1 2027 data before the second tranche; deploy the third tranche only below USD 216 or once free cash flow turns positive. Trim line at USD 216... no — trim line at USD 330; frame the exit condition around backlog, not price. Cap total AI-related exposure at 25% — the most important line in this recommendation, because Amazon will not fall alone.
