Six-Gate Screen — Amazon

Amazon (AMZN) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/11/06  ·  Price $259.30 (-0.35%)  ·  Market cap About USD 2.80 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 20.8x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Amazon cleared five of six gates; the only one it failed to score highly on is margin of safety. Profit is accelerating; the valuation has not moved: Q3 2026 operating income was USD 28.08 billion, up 61% year on year, and the 13.9% operating margin is the highest in the company's thirty-year history; the P/E ratio over the same period is still 20.8x. AWS grew 39.3%, accelerating for a sixth consecutive quarter, with a backlog of USD 578 billion, up triple digits year on year — no cracks visible on the demand side.

But one data point holds this conclusion in check: quarterly capex of USD 77.26 billion, up 120% year on year, pushed TTM free cash flow to negative USD 42.4 billion. Buying it means accepting one premise — that Jassy's claim of "AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028" will materialize. That premise can be reconciled against quarterly data, so it passes; but do not buy to full weight in one go.

Stage one to stage three: this flywheel has run for twenty years, and only this year did it show up on the income statement

Amazon's business breaks into three layers, each of which can be priced separately. The first is retail: in 2026Q3, online stores USD 68.47 billion, up just 2%; third-party seller services USD 46.49 billion, up 9%; physical stores USD 5.71 billion, up 2%. The second is AWS: USD 45.98 billion, up 39.3%, an annualized run rate of USD 184 billion. The third is advertising and subscriptions: advertising USD 19.64 billion, up 11%; subscriptions USD 14.01 billion, up 11%. The three layers grow at vastly different rates, yet almost all profit comes from the latter two.

The real change is in retail margins. North America segment revenue grew only 6%, yet operating income rose from USD 4.79 billion to USD 8.72 billion, margin 4.3%→7.7%; the international segment reached 3.7%. CFO Olsavsky cites three mechanisms: optimized inventory placement, shorter shipping distances, and package consolidation. Shipping costs +11% year on year against unit volume +12% — shipping costs growing slower than volume, the first time in two decades this has become the norm. Excluding fuel and line-haul inflation, shipping costs rose only 8%, roughly 400 basis points below volume.

The moat is not in dispute, but it is worth spelling out why it is a chain: retail scale funds logistics, logistics efficiency lowers per-unit cost, low cost buys membership retention (subscription revenue USD 14 billion), member traffic sells ad inventory at premium prices, and cash flow from advertising and retail ultimately funds AWS capex. Break one link and the other three loosen. That is why I do not give it a 10: it depends too heavily on every link performing flawlessly.

Stage four: Jassy treats "compute scarcity" as a permanent condition

Management scores 7. The credit is clear: Jassy put three numbers together on the call — one AI accelerator installed every 14 seconds, customers consuming compute almost as fast as it is installed, and inference already accounting for more than half of AWS AI revenue. Together these three statements form a verifiable supply-side story, not a vision. He also said revenue commitments for Graviton and Trainium each grew more than 20x year over year, and that Trainium would be sold outside AWS by the end of 2027.

The deductions are equally clear: he gave very aggressive guidance — AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028, and USD 1 trillion in revenue in five to ten years. This is not an "outlook"; it is betting the company on a very long demand cycle. If AI training demand growth slows in 2027, USD 220 billion in annual capex turns from "investment" into "fixed cost." Depreciation will not wait; revenue will.

There is a finer issue: the sequencing of capital allocation. Amazon chose to build its own data centers, buy its own chips and sign long-term power contracts, rather than follow Microsoft in cutting its 2026 capex guidance from USD 175 billion to USD 170 billion and relying more on third-party compute. Heavy assets are a moat when supply is tight and a burden when demand weakens. Jassy is betting the former lasts longer. That judgment has been right so far, but it is a judgment, not a fact.

Gate 5: Buying 61% profit growth at 20.8x, with cash verification the missing piece

This is the watershed. USD 259.30 corresponds to a market cap of USD 2.80 trillion, TTM EPS of USD 12.46, and a P/E of 20.8x; on the neutral-scenario 2027 EPS of USD 15 in this report, about 17x. A company with 61% operating profit growth, 39% AWS growth and USD 578 billion of backlog should not trade at just 20x. Why has the market not awarded a premium? Because free cash flow is negative USD 42.4 billion.

But two things must be separated: whether negative free cash flow is an active choice or a passive outcome. Amazon is the former—of its USD 168.3 billion in operating cash flow, it spent USD 210 billion on land, buildings and chips, and the remainder was not enough. The difference lies in whether cash flow can turn positive immediately once capex stops. Amazon can: almost all of its capex is deferrable capacity build-out, not maintenance spending.

So this gate scores 6 rather than lower. A caveat on the valuation basis: the 20.8x includes USD 68.3 billion of non-operating gains in the first three quarters of 2026 (mark-to-market gains on the Anthropic investment); excluding these, the TTM P/E is about 34x (rough calculation). Paying 34x for 40% cloud growth is not cheap, but it is hardly a bubble. That is the real meaning of "barely passes"—not a discount, but "expensive for good reason".

Stage 6: The buy case must be reconcilable quarter by quarter

I do not accept the premise of being "long-term bullish on AI." This discipline requires writing assumptions as numbers and checking them every quarter. What is written down now is three items: AWS growth of no less than 35%; North America retail operating margin of no less than 7%; TTM free cash flow back above zero within 2027. The first two have already been validated once; the third has not.

The benefit of these three is that all can be falsified. If AWS growth falls below 30%, retail margin drops back to 5%, or free cash flow is still at -RMB 30 billion in 2027, any one of these occurring breaks the buy thesis, and there is no need to discuss the narrative further.Conversely, if all three hold, the 20.8x valuation will be caught up by earnings, rather than making money through multiple expansion.

Conclusion

46/60, pass, buyable, but only in tranches.This is a company whose income statement is accelerating while its cash flow statement is overdrawn; the two statements are two sides of the same story—it is trading today's RMB 77.2 billion of cash for 2028 compute capacity. The entry point is not cheap valuation (20.8x TTM, roughly 34x after stripping out unrealized gains), but earnings growth outrunning the share price. Build one-third of the position first; decide whether to fill it out after Q1 2027 capex guidance and depreciation data are released.
