Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Amazon

Under what circumstances would this investment lose money, and how much would it lose?

Core view

Li Lu's approach splits risk into "risk of going to zero" and "risk of volatility." Amazon has no risk of going to zero — it has operating cash flow of RMB 16.83 billion, an order book of RMB 57.80 billion, and three cash-generating businesses. Its risk is "valuation cut in half" and "profit eaten by depreciation," not "the company disappearing." The two types of risk are handled in completely different ways: the former through position sizing, the latter through time.

The magnitude is a rough estimate in this report: a 13.7 percentage point decline in AWS margin implies operating profit lower by about USD 6.3 billion per quarter (on revenue of USD 45.98 billion), or 22% of the group's USD 28.08 billion. The depreciation scenario assumes 2027 capex of USD 300 billion and straight-line depreciation over 5–6 years.
Risk one: this is not a demand risk, it is a timing risk

The worst-case scenario is not "nobody uses AI" but "AI adoption runs a year slower than expected." Amazon has already built the capacity, bought the chips and signed the power contracts, and these costs are amortized on a straight-line basis over 5–6 years, regardless of demand swings. Jassy said AWS growth would exceed 40% in both 2027 and 2028, a statement that ties the company to a very steep curve. If actual growth is 25% rather than 40%, the depreciation ratio spirals out of control.

Put numbers on it: 2026 capex of RMB 220 billion, depreciated over 6 years, is RMB 36.7 billion a year; if 2027 capex is RMB 300 billion, add another RMB 50 billion. The two years combined add roughly RMB 87 billion a year in depreciation, equal to 1.5x 2025 group operating profit. That bill can only be paid with revenue growth; there is no other way.

Risk 2: Valuation offers no margin of safety

At USD 259.30, the stock sits just 8.7% below its 52-week high of USD 284.02 and 30% above its low of USD 198.79. That positioning shows the market is not panicking, which means there are no bargains. If the AI trade sees a systemic cooldown in 2027 (a preview played out in November 2026: Nvidia fell 14% in a single day and CoreWeave drew down more than 70% from its high), Amazon, as "the largest spender on AI capex," could see its valuation compressed to 15x TTM, implying about USD 187.

This is the "price determines risk" point Li Lu would stress. The same company offers 1.6-to-1 odds when bought at USD 260 and 4-to-1 odds at USD 200. The focus of risk management is not being right or wrong; it is spreading the purchase price across different scenarios.

Two easily overlooked risks: FX and tariffs

The market fixates on capex and misses two slower-moving variables. The first is FX: the international segment grew 5% as reported and 8% in constant currency, meaning FX shaved 3 percentage points. Management guides that FX will drag revenue growth by another 60 basis points in the fourth quarter. For a company with 77% of revenue in retail and 21% international, FX is no small matter.

The second is tariffs and trade policy. The company itself listed "tariffs and trade policy" among the risks in its earnings call. Retail gross margins are thin (7.7% in North America), and a tariff increase cannot be fully passed on to consumers, so part of it must be absorbed. This is the kind of risk that never makes headlines but eats two points of margin.

Li Lu would classify this kind of risk as "unpredictable but bearable" — it does not change the investment thesis, it changes the required margin of safety. So the buy point should be within 25x operating P/E, not 34x.

Conclusion

The risk desk's verdict: buy, but size the position for the worst case. No zeroing out, volatility ahead, and depreciation likely to bite for two years — together these point to a base position plus staggered buying. What really needs a stop-loss is not the price but the order book: if AWS backlog falls quarter on quarter for two consecutive quarters, demand-side cracks have appeared, and at that point exit regardless of price.
