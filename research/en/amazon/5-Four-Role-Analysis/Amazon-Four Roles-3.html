Core view

Amazon is fighting on two fronts at once: e-commerce, where scale determines cost, and cloud computing, where scale determines gross margin. Both point to the same conclusion—the money in these industries ultimately flows to whoever owns the physical assets and runs them at the highest utilisation. In 2026, Amazon ranks first by scale in both, but first by growth in neither.

Layer one: cloud is winner-takes-all, but the winners are becoming three

Munger liked to ask, "What will this industry look like in ten years?" For cloud, the answer is that concentration keeps rising — but into three players, not one. The current numbers tell the story: AWS USD 46 billion, growth 39.3%; Azure growth 48%; Google Cloud USD 30.7 billion, growth 103%. AWS remains the largest by scale, but its growth now ranks last — the most important industry fact of this cycle.

The reason lies in a changed demand structure. Cloud growth in 2023–2025 came from "enterprises moving to the cloud," which played to AWS's strength (a large base of internet-native customers). Growth in 2026 comes from "AI training and inference," where the deciding factor is the model partner — Microsoft is tied to OpenAI (its exclusivity period ended in April 2026), while Google has its own Gemini and TPU. Amazon's response is to embrace both sides at once: it is Anthropic's largest outside shareholder, and in 2026 it also struck a USD 50 billion-scale partnership with OpenAI, with GPT-6 Astra and Bedrock Managed Agents both already live.

Layer two: the real bottleneck is power, not chips

Munger would spot something easily missed: the binding constraint on this expansion round is power and land, not capital. By building its own data centers, signing long-term power contracts and designing its own chips, Amazon is effectively seizing the scarce resource — powered-up machine rooms. When Jassy says "most of our data center capacity is owned or long-term leased," what he means is Amazon is locking up supply, not locking in prices.

That also explains the runaway capex: when the bottleneck is grid interconnection time — queues of three to five years in parts of the US — the only option is to buy early, build early and sign early. The cost is front-loaded depreciation and back-loaded cash flow. The money in this industry ultimately flows to the companies that can turn power into compute before 2027 — and this is the part of Amazon Munger would endorse: on this front it is more ruthless than rivals.

The third front: advertising's true scale

Advertising is the most underappreciated part of this company: USD 19.64 billion a quarter, +12% YoY, annualising to nearly USD 80 billion — a scale that already exceeds many standalone ad agencies, yet the company never discloses its profit separately. The non-disclosure is itself information: it is too profitable, and disclosing it would make retail look even worse.

Its moat is "transactional intent": people come to Amazon ready to buy, an intent that Google and Meta must infer. Amazon advertising therefore converts better, and brands pay a premium. Q3 2026 growth was only 12%, which management attributed to the Prime Day shift (about 850 basis points of impact) and the prior-year comparison base from search engine marketing programs, and guided to a return to high-teens in Q4.

How would Munger assess it? He would call it "a business attached to someone else's" — advertising scale is set by retail traffic, and retail traffic is set by the logistics experience. It is not an independent moat; it is a toll booth on the moat.

Conclusion

Sell-side verdict: favourable structure, favourable position, risky timing. Cloud and e-commerce are both scale businesses, and Amazon is number one in each—the hardest part to replicate. The risk is "bottom-of-the-pack growth": if Microsoft and Google capture incremental AI demand through model lock-in, AWS becomes "the largest legacy cloud" rather than "the cloud of incremental growth." Two metrics can track this call: whether AWS growth holds at 35%, and whether Bedrock token volume is still accelerating.
