TeamLead summary — Amazon

Where the four perspectives diverge, and how to adjudicate

Core view

Three seats vote "pass," the finance seat votes "conditional pass," and the risk seat wants "positions sized to the worst case." None of the four perspectives is fundamentally opposed; the disagreement centres on a single number: 2027 depreciation. This page sets out that disagreement, then delivers the verdict and execution plan.

Disagreement one: is 20.8x cheap or expensive

The commercial and sector seats call it cheap: operating profit growth of 61%, an AWS backlog of RMB 578 billion, and 20x means "profit is running faster than the share price." The finance seat cautions that the 20.8x includes RMB 68.3 billion of unrealized gains; strip that out and it is about 34x, which is not cheap for a capital-intensive company.

Verdict: both are right, because the bases differ. On "operating profit," 34x for 40% cloud growth is reasonably expensive; on "book profit available to shareholders," 20.8x is the market consensus. Operationally, adopt the conservative basis: require a margin of safety at 34x, meaning the entry point should be within 25x 2027 operating EPS, corresponding to a more comfortable level below USD 230. The current price of USD 259.30 is slightly above that range, so build only one-third of the position.

Disagreement two: is AWS's lagging growth a crisis or noise?

The industry seat flagged a hard fact: at 39.3%, AWS is the slowest of the three major clouds (Azure +48%, Google Cloud +103%). The risk seat reads this as the most alarming signal, since it implies incremental demand is being captured by others.

Verdict: a warning, not a crisis. Three reasons. First, the bases differ — AWS did 46 billion in the quarter versus Google Cloud's 30.7 billion, so growth rates are only partly comparable. Second, AWS's backlog grew triple digits YoY, indicating orders are not being lost. Third, AWS's operating margin is 38.7%, and it is the only one of the three that discloses cloud profit and sustains it at a high level (Microsoft and Google do not break out cloud profit separately).But this goes on the watchlist: if AWS growth falls below 30% in the first half of 2027 while Azure remains above 40%, that is share loss, not a base effect.

Verdict and execution: one-third core position, three add conditions

Across all four seats the conclusion is Buy, build the position in tranches, consistent with the working papers. Execution plan:

  • First tranche, 1/3 of position: establish near the current price of USD 259.30. Earnings are compounding far faster than the share price, so the cost of waiting exceeds the cost of paying 5% too much.
  • Second tranche, 1/3: after Q1 2027 results, conditional on AWS growth ≥35%, North America retail operating margin ≥7%, and depreciation growth no faster than operating profit growth.
  • Third tranche, 1/3: when trailing-twelve-month free cash flow turns positive, or the share price falls back to USD 216 (bear case). Add on either trigger.

The stop-loss trigger is not price but facts: AWS backlog declining quarter-on-quarter for two consecutive quarters, or the company extending server useful lives while cutting capex guidance. The first signals a crack in demand, the second accounting concealment; if both appear, exit without waiting for price.

If you could give only one piece of advice

The disagreement across the four seats could be debated indefinitely, but it comes down to one operational rule: leave the "right to add" to the depreciation data, not to the share price.

The logic is simple: the company's 2026 income statement was earned in a window before depreciation had climbed, and the 13.9% operating margin is not sustainable into the second half of 2027. What really determines the success of this investment is whether AWS can still grow above 35% once depreciation rises in 2027, and whether the group can still hold an operating margin above 12%. That answer will only become clear in Q2 2027.

So the first one-third is "trusting the current evidence," and the next two tranches are "waiting for new evidence." This is not hesitation; it is pricing uncertainty. In a USD 2.8 trillion company, the only certain advantage is that you have time to scale in.

Conclusion

One line across the four lenses: the business is right (8/10); the profit is real but flattered by unrealised gains (apply a 30% haircut); its industry position is being shaken (growth at the bottom of the pack); and the price offers no margin of safety (8.7% below the high). Put the four together and the only answer is "buy a little, wait for the data." That is not fence-sitting; on a USD 2.8 trillion company, it is the only approach that preserves both odds and discipline at once.
