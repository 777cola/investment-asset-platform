Core view

The Amazon buy thesis rests on six assumptions, each tied to a metric that can be tracked. Today two are validated (AWS acceleration, retail margin expansion), two are being validated (AWS margin, advertising growth) and two are contrary evidence (capex peaking, depreciation lives unchanged).

The value of this checklist is that you need not watch the share price every day, only these six metrics. If one is disproven, trim the position; if two or more are disproven, exit.

Two verified: this is the foundation of the thesis

The AWS acceleration is real. Growth rose YoY for six straight quarters from 2025Q2 to 2026Q3, hitting 39.3% in the latest quarter — the fastest in nearly five years — on an annualised run rate of USD 184 billion. The harder evidence is orders: backlog of USD 578 billion, up triple digits YoY, with management saying customers have locked up 2027–2028 capacity. There is no crack on the demand side; this point needs no further debate.

The retail margin expansion is real. North America revenue grew just 6%, yet operating income rose from USD 4.79 billion to USD 8.72 billion, lifting the margin from 4.3% to 7.7%. The mechanism is verifiable: shipping costs +11% and units +12%; excluding fuel and line-haul inflation, shipping costs rose 8%, 400 basis points below unit growth, and cost per unit shipped fell for a fourth consecutive year. But this counts as only one verification, because the quarter got a assist from Prime Day shifting from Q3 to Q2; the Q4 holiday season will be the clean read.

Two pieces of counter-evidence: the weakest parts of the paper

Capex shows no sign of peaking. 2026 capex of RMB 220 billion is already a record high, and management has guided that 2027 will "increase significantly" — Deutsche Bank sits at RMB 330 billion, UBS at over RMB 300 billion. Free cash flow therefore most likely stays negative or close to zero in 2027 (in UBS's words, "free cash flow expected to be near zero or negative"). The assumption that "free cash flow turns positive in 2028" rests on no company-level evidence; it is pure extrapolation.

Depreciation life is an unexploded accounting variable. Servers are depreciated over 5–6 years, and 2026's incremental compute assets imply roughly USD 40 billion of incremental annual depreciation (broker estimates). The company made no change this quarter, which is reassuring; but any change would immediately flatter current-period profit while leaving cash flow unchanged. This item belongs on the checklist not to forecast, but to avoid being fooled.

Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar

Falsification conditions (trigger = trim position):

  • AWS growth falls below 30% YoY for two consecutive quarters while Azure holds above 40% over the same period;
  • AWS backlog declines QoQ for two consecutive quarters (currently growing triple digits YoY; any sequential decline is a new signal);
  • North America retail operating margin drops back below 5%;
  • TTM free cash flow negative for four consecutive quarters, with the absolute value widening;
  • Company extends server depreciation lives while cutting capex guidance (combined signal only; either one alone does not count).

Watch calendar: Early December 2026, AWS re:Invent (new products and Trainium roadmap); February 2027, Q4 results + annual report (depreciation policy, capex guidance, Q4 advertising growth); April 2027, Q1 results (first quarterly read on 2027 capex); July 2027, Q2 (first test against the high base); October 2027, Q3 (whether AWS growth holds at 35%).

Conclusion

Thesis status: intact, but two legs rest on extrapolation. AWS acceleration and retail efficiency gains are hard evidence and sufficient to support a "Buy"; capex peaking and free cash flow turning positive are extrapolations, sufficient only to support a "one-third position". The next key validation point is the annual report in February 2027 — depreciation policy and 2027 capex guidance will land together, and that is the moment that decides whether to fill the position.
