Core view

Amazon has carried three labels for thirty years, and all three have expired: low-margin retailer, slowing growth, capex as a burden. All three were true in 2021 and all three are wrong in 2026. Until the perception resets, the valuation will never be right.

Impression one: it is not a retailer but "a tech company that acquires customers through retail"

Look at the source of profit and it is clear: AWS earns USD 45.98 billion in annual revenue and USD 17.78 billion in operating profit, 63.3% of the group total. North America retail generates USD 112.85 billion in revenue but contributes only USD 8.72 billion in profit (7.7%), while international contributes just USD 1.58 billion on USD 43.11 billion (3.7%). Retail is the gateway; the profit sits in technology and advertising.

This matters because it dictates the valuation method: you cannot value Amazon at Walmart's 25x, nor at retail's 10x—63% of its profit comes from a business with a 38.7% gross margin. Conversely, you cannot apply a cloud company's 40x either, because 77% of revenue is still retail.

Impression 2 and 3: growth is back, but this time it was bought with spending

In 2022, Amazon's revenue growth briefly fell to single digits, and the market's conclusion at the time was that "an elephant cannot dance." In the first three quarters of 2026, revenue grew 16% and AWS grew 39.3%—growth is back. But the way it came back is different this time: the last recovery relied on pandemic tailwinds; this one relies on USD 220 billion of annual capital expenditure.

So the third impression must also be reset—capex is no longer a "mild burden"; it is now the company's most important operating variable. Quarterly capex of USD 77.26 billion, up 120% year over year, pushed TTM free cash flow to negative USD 42.4 billion; long-term debt rose from USD 65.6 billion to USD 173.1 billion. In other words: its growth has a price, and the price is written on the cash flow statement.

The takeaway from this cognitive reset: view it as a company "transitioning from asset-light to asset-heavy." For an asset-light company, watch free cash flow; for an asset-heavy company, watch return on capital and capacity utilization. Use the wrong yardstick, and you will buy at the wrong price.

The overlooked fourth figure: the international segment

Everyone is watching AWS; no one is watching the International segment. USD 43.11 billion in revenue, USD 1.58 billion in operating income, a 3.7% margin. Five years ago that number was negative. International was once Amazon's profit black hole (prolonged losses in Europe, Japan and India); now it contributes steady positive profit, albeit thin.

Its significance lies not in profit but in headroom. Jassy says nearly 80% of global retail still happens in physical stores, and the main battleground for rising online penetration is Europe, Japan, India and Brazil. A 3.7% margin means two things: first, there is ample room for improvement (North America went from 4.3% to 7.7% over several years); second, any swing in oil prices or exchange rates will push it back into loss.

So when looking at Amazon's retail, you cannot look only at North America. North America is a factory already retooled; International is a factory just breaking ground—the former supplies profit, the latter supplies optionality.

Remember the new profile with three numbers

13.9% (operating margin, a record high), 39.3% (AWS growth, the fastest in five years), -RMB 42.4 billion (free cash flow, the worst in a decade). The first number shows it can make money, the second shows it can still grow, the third shows it is spending. Only when all three hold at once is it today's Amazon. Any one of them on its own is a misreading.

Why the market misremembers: anchored to 2021

One psychological reason: most people formed their impression of Amazon in 2021–2022—when its operating margin was only 2%–5%, free cash flow was positive but small, and growth relied on pandemic tailwinds. The stock fell 50% during that period, and the impression got pinned to "low-quality growth."

The 2026 financials have a different skeleton: operating margin of 13.9%, AWS contributing 63% of profit, retail efficiency improved from 4.3% to 7.7%.But the market's valuation anchor hasn't shifted—20.8x, a multiple for a "retailer," not for "cloud + advertising."

This is why opportunity and risk coexist: if the market re-rates it to a "cloud company" multiple, the stock revalues; if the market sticks to a "retailer" multiple, profit growth can only be realized through EPS, at just over 20% a year.Neither scenario loses money, but the gain differs by a factor of two.

Conclusion

The reset picture: a high-margin, fast-growing company that is getting heavier. Its valuation anchor is no longer "retail PS", nor merely "cloud PS", but "segment profit × segment multiple − the depreciation cost of capex". Three lines to remember: retail is the gateway, cloud is the profit, capex is the price.
