④ Financial reality: RMB 90 billion profit, negative RMB 42.4 billion cash — Amazon

Both numbers are right; the question is which one you use to price the company

Core view

In the first three quarters of 2026, Amazon earned net profit of RMB 113.59 billion; over the trailing twelve months, free cash flow was -RMB 42.4 billion. Both figures are real; the difference is that one is accounting and the other is cash. Who prices the company determines whether you see 20x or 34x.

Fact one: investment gains inflated profit by 46%

Other income for the first three quarters was USD 68.359 billion, 46% of pre-tax profit of USD 147.56 billion, mainly from a large appreciation in the Anthropic equity stake. This is not fabrication—accounting standards require fair-value measurement—but it renders the "net profit doubled" headline meaningless. Stripping out the after-tax impact, net margin falls from 19.4% to 10.4%, and the TTM P/E rises from 20.8x to about 34x.

How much does this correction matter? It shifts the conclusion from "cheap" to "reasonably expensive."Buying a company with 61% profit growth at 20.8x is clearly cheap; buying a company with a 13.9% operating margin that may fall back to 11%–12% next year at 34x is "expensive for good reason, but with no discount."

Truth two: capex has consumed all the cash, and that spending can be deferred

TTM operating cash flow was RMB 168.3 billion, capex about RMB 210.7 billion, a gap of -RMB 42.4 billion. Single-quarter purchases of property and equipment were RMB 77.26 billion, +120% YoY; the first three quarters totaled about RMB 165 billion, with full-year guidance of RMB 220 billion. Not enough money, so borrow: long-term debt rose from RMB 65.6 billion to RMB 173.1 billion, and single-quarter interest expense was RMB 1.69 billion, +214% YoY.

The nature of this spending determines the size of the risk. If it is maintenance capex (skip it and things break), it is a black hole; if it is expansionary capex (can be stopped), it is investment. Three pieces of evidence point to the latter: an order book of RMB 578 billion locked through 2027–2028, retail-side capex growth far below cloud-side, and Jassy saying the company will "keep shortening the time to breakeven." Deferrable spending is not rigid spending — that is the most fundamental difference between this and "burning cash for growth."

But concede this too: deferrable does not mean it will be deferred. Management explicitly said capex will "increase significantly" again in 2027, and Deutsche Bank forecasts RMB 330 billion. Until capex peaks, free cash flow will not turn positive — a risk that can be marked on a calendar.

The depreciation knife: spent in 2026, lands in 2027

Capex is not an expense in the year it is incurred; it first becomes an asset, then amortizes over its useful life. Servers depreciate on a 5–6 year straight-line basis, data centers over longer periods. So the RMB 220 billion spent in 2026 will gradually turn into depreciation across 2027–2031, with 2027–2028 the peak years.

Run the numbers: RMB 220 billion over 6 years is about RMB 36.7 billion a year; if 2027 sees RMB 300 billion, add another RMB 50 billion; if 2028 also sees RMB 300 billion, add another RMB 50 billion. Three years out, incremental annual depreciation approaches RMB 137 billion—1.8x the group's full-year 2025 operating profit.

Whether this bill can be paid comes down to one ratio: AWS revenue growth vs. depreciation growth. AWS runs at an annualized RMB 184 billion; at 40% growth it adds RMB 73.6 billion of revenue a year; at a 45% incremental operating margin, that is RMB 33.1 billion of operating profit. As long as AWS holds 40%, depreciation is covered; at 25%, incremental profit is only RMB 20.7 billion and a gap opens.

That is why "AWS growth of 35%" is the threshold that recurs throughout this report—it is not arbitrary; it is the break-even line for covering depreciation.

The two-number test

Two numbers are all you need to assess this company: operating profit growth (Q3 +61%) and capex growth (Q3 +120%). When the former exceeds the latter, investment is efficient; when the latter outpaces the former over time, the company is building capacity for demand that does not exist. Right now the latter leads, and that is the source of all the risk.

Conclusion

Financial reality: this is a report of "real profit, negative cash, real investment." Pricing requires two yardsticks at once: 34x to judge whether it is expensive, and RMB -42.4 billion to judge whether to take a heavy position. The answer is that it is expensive for good reason, so I would only buy a third.
