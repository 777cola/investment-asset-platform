Framework one: why scale in tranches rather than wait for a cheaper price

The question we hear most often is "why not wait until 216 to buy." The answer: you don't know whether it will get there. The stock traded at 278 on August 10, 240 on October 30 and 266 on November 4 — a 15.7% swing within three months, on a single change in fundamentals. In that kind of volatility, waiting for a specific price carries roughly the same odds as missing out.

The point of scaling in is not to boost returns but to reduce the cost of getting it wrong in one go. Buying a one-third position at 259, even if the stock falls to 187 (-28%), costs the portfolio -1.4% (assuming a 5% position). A loss of that size won't push you into an emotional decision.

Framework 2: Why sell on price, stop out on facts

Sell on price, because valuation is rational: USD 330 equates to 22x 2027 EPS of USD 15, and the risk-reward no longer supports holding. Use facts for stop-loss, because price is emotional—within a 15.7% amplitude, a price stop would be knocked out three times by noise.

Which "fact"? The one hardest to fake and most leading: AWS backlog. It leads revenue by one to two quarters, is harder to dress up than profit, and is more honest than the share price. If this figure of RMB 578 billion declines QoQ for two consecutive quarters, customers are delaying or cancelling orders—and you still have time to exit.

One last rule: do not add to the position out of "long-term bullishness on AI." That sentence is not a judgment; it is emotion. The part you can understand (AWS acceleration, retail efficiency gains) justifies one-third of the position; the part you cannot understand (2027 depreciation, when capex peaks) should not be filled in with faith.

When should this framework be overturned?

Every framework has conditions under which it fails; writing them down prevents ad hoc rationalisation later. Three conditions would invalidate this decision framework:

One, AWS growth falls below 30% for two consecutive quarters. At that point the "trade depreciation for growth" math no longer holds, the valuation should de-rate below 15x, and the USD 330 target price is void.

Two, the company extends server depreciation lives. The bad news is not the act itself but what it signals: management is building a cushion for a quarter that will miss expectations. All historical margin data would then need to be recalculated, and P/E would be distorted.

Three, AI capex financing shifts from debt to equity. A share issuance would dilute, and it would signal management acknowledging that cash flow cannot sustain the spending. There is no sign of this now — it chose to issue debt, indicating confidence that returns exceed the cost of debt.

If none of the three conditions appears, execute by the original rules: one-third, add at 216, trim at 330, follow the backlog.

Looking back at this page six months later

If the February 2027 annual report shows AWS still growing >35%, depreciation growing slower than operating profit and free cash flow narrowing, then today's one-third position was placed in the right spot and a second tranche can be added. If two of the three conditions fail, do not change the framework — change the position. The framework exists to see facts clearly, not to find justifications for oneself.

A concrete example: if you buy 100 shares today

Buy 100 shares at USD 259.30 for USD 25,930. Scenario 1 (base case): USD 330 after 12 months, market value USD 33,000, unrealized gain USD 7,070, +27%. Scenario 2 (bear case): USD 216, market value USD 21,600, unrealized loss USD 4,330, -17%; add the second tranche per the rules here, averaging cost down to about USD 237. Scenario 3 (thesis broken): USD 135, unrealized loss USD 12,430, -48%.

Probability-weighting the three scenarios (this report assumes 50%/35%/15%) gives an expected return of about +14%. That number is not high, but it comes not from "getting the direction right" but from "still being able to add in the worst case."

What really determines the return, then, is not the entry point but "whether there is cash to add at USD 216." That is the point of scaling in — it makes you a buyer on the way down, not a seller.

Conclusion

Final call: Buy in tranches, one third for the first tranche, target price USD 330. Add on three quarters of data, trim on price, exit on the order book. These rules do not guarantee a profit, but they do ensure you hold on when you are right and get out when you are wrong.
