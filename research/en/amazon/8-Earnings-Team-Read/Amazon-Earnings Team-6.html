Amazon 2026Q3: best income statement in a decade, worst cash flow statement in a decade

Core view

Amazon reported third-quarter 2026 results after the close on October 29. Operating income was USD 28.08 billion, up 61% YoY, and operating margin reached 13.9%, the highest in the company's thirty-year history; AWS grew 39.3%, its sixth consecutive quarter of acceleration. The same report contained another number: trailing-twelve-month free cash flow of -USD 42.4 billion.

First, the good part: this is not financial engineering but real capability

Breaking the three segments apart shows clearly where the profit comes from: AWS revenue of USD 45.98 billion, operating income of USD 17.78 billion and a 38.7% margin, contributing more than 60% of group operating income; North America retail revenue of USD 112.85 billion, up only 6%, yet operating income rose from USD 4.79 billion to USD 8.72 billion, with margin expanding from 4.3% to 7.7%; international segment 3.7%.

The most valuable line is buried in the details: global paid units +12% YoY, shipping costs only +11%; excluding fuel and line-haul inflation, shipping costs rose just 8%, 400 basis points below unit growth. CFO Olsavsky attributes this to optimized inventory placement, shorter transport distances and package consolidation. Translated: goods sit closer, and the same money moves more orders. Per-unit fulfillment cost fell for a fourth consecutive year — one of the hardest things to achieve in a twenty-year retail business.

Demand shows no cracks either. AWS backlog stands at USD 578 billion, up triple digits YoY, with capacity locked by customers through 2027–2028. Jassy offered a vivid line on the call: "Customers are snapping up capacity almost as fast as we deploy it." He also said the company installs roughly one AI accelerator every 14 seconds.

Now the cost: RMB 77.2 billion spent in a single quarter

Capex on property and equipment reached USD 77.26 billion, up 120% YoY and 30% above consensus. First three quarters totalled roughly USD 165 billion; full-year guidance holds at USD 220 billion, with a "significant increase" again in 2027. Ahead of the results, UBS raised its 2027 capex forecast from USD 250 billion to over USD 300 billion, and Deutsche Bank puts it at USD 330 billion.

The consequence is free cash flow: TTM -USD 42.4 billion, versus +USD 14.8 billion a year earlier. At the second-quarter report it was still -USD 7.6 billion, implying about USD 34.8 billion burned in a single quarter. Short of cash, the company is issuing debt — long-term debt rose from USD 65.6 billion to USD 173.1 billion, up USD 107.5 billion in a year, while quarterly interest expense climbed from USD 538 million to USD 1.691 billion, up 214% YoY.

One distinction matters: negative free cash flow is not the same as a deteriorating business. Amazon's operating cash flow was USD 168.3 billion TTM, up 28.7% YoY — the business is generating cash; capex is consuming it. And these outlays are almost entirely expansionary capacity (data centres, chips, power) rather than maintenance, with capacity already booked by customers through 2028. Deferrable spending is not rigid spending.

Two pitfalls to flag

The first trap is net income. This quarter's net income was RMB 20.685 billion, down 2.4% YoY, with EPS of USD 1.89 versus consensus of USD 1.96. The miss reflects not deteriorating operations but the USD 9.5 billion pre-tax gain from Anthropic in the year-ago period. Look instead at the first three quarters: other income totalled RMB 68.359 billion, 46% of pre-tax profit, and net income rose 101% YoY. The headline "net income doubled" is about Anthropic's valuation, not Amazon's business. Strip that out and the net margin for the first three quarters is 10.4%, not the reported 19.4%; the TTM P/E is 34x, not 20.8x.

The second trap is depreciation. RMB 220 billion of capex depreciated over 5–6 years adds RMB 37–44 billion of depreciation annually from 2027; brokers estimate the new compute assets in 2026 will carry roughly USD 40 billion of average annual incremental depreciation. Technology and infrastructure expenses are already up 20.2% YoY, the fastest-growing line in the expense table. This quarter's 13.9% margin was earned in a window before depreciation has ramped up.

How should this price be set?

Closed at USD 259.30 on November 6 with a market cap of USD 2.80 trillion and a TTM P/E of 20.8x, down 8.7% from the 52-week high of USD 284.02 and up 30% from the low of USD 198.79. What this positioning means: the market is not in panic, so there are no bargains.

On 2027 EPS of USD 12/15/17.5 at 18/22/25x, I derive three valuation tiers: bear case USD 216 (-17%), base case USD 330 (+27%) and bull case USD 438 (+69%), a reward-to-risk ratio of 1.63 to 1. For a USD 2.8 trillion company that ratio is not spectacular, but it is adequate.

Operationally, I therefore recommend "Buy, build the position in tranches": open one-third at the current price; once Q1 2027 capex guidance and depreciation data are out, add another third if AWS growth is still ≥35% and North America retail operating margin is ≥7%; leave the final third for "free cash flow turns positive" or "the stock returns to USD 216". The stop-loss condition is not a price but a fact: AWS backlog declines quarter on quarter for two consecutive quarters.

Conclusion

One-line summary: Amazon is spending USD 77.2 billion of today's cash to buy 2028's compute. If it is right, earnings will grow into the 20x P/E. If it is wrong, the USD 220 billion turns into depreciation pressing on the income statement. You do not need to judge whether it is right; you need to judge which outcome you can bear—then decide how much to buy.
