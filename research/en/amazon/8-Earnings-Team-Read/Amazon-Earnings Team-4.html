Core view

Li Lu's habit when reading financial reports is to place the company back into the "flow of capital." The most striking item in this report is not profit but RMB 220 billion in capex plus RMB 107.5 billion in new debt—the company is doing something akin to a country building high-speed rail: spend first to build the network, then collect the tolls.

Where we are in this cycle: early-to-mid stage

Infrastructure investment cycles typically run through three phases: capacity grab (capex leads, free cash flow turns negative), capacity release (revenue accelerates, margins recover), then harvest (capex declines, free cash flow surges). Amazon now sits between the first and second phases: capacity is still being built, but orders are already locked through 2027–2028, and AWS growth has accelerated for six consecutive quarters.

Historically this is the hardest phase to call. Railroad companies in the 1870s also had "order books full and share prices rising first," then collapsed once capacity became excessive. The difference lies in the nature of demand: rail capacity is rigid and substitutable, whereas cloud compute can be shut down on demand and customers' data stays on it. Cloud "overcapacity" therefore means falling prices, not asset write-offs.

Three details that must be verified

First, capacity utilization disclosure. The company states only that "compute sells out as soon as it is installed" and gives no utilization figure. Watch whether growth in "technology and infrastructure" expense in the next 10-Q continues to outpace revenue growth — if it does, capacity is being deployed faster than it is monetized.

Second, the true scale of external Trainium sales. External sales begin at the end of 2027 — a key step from internal use to cut costs to selling chips externally — but the company gave no revenue target. If it works, Amazon becomes both a cloud provider and a chipmaker.

Third, the timing of Leo capitalization. The company says it will begin capitalizing part of production and launch costs in the first quarter of 2027, meaning those costs are still being expensed in the fourth quarter of 2026. This is an accounting arrangement that front-loads the pain and back-loads the benefit; expect a margin jump in the first quarter of 2027 — do not misread it as operating improvement.

Conclusion

Close-read conclusion: this is a textbook "infrastructure-first" cycle, in its early-to-middle stage. The revenue-side evidence (order book, accelerating growth) supports that read, and the cash-side cost (-RMB 42.4 billion) fits the hallmarks of this stage. The real watershed comes in 2027: if capex peaks and AWS growth holds at 35%, the cycle enters its harvest phase; if capex is still rising while growth falls to 25%, that marks the onset of capacity oversupply.
