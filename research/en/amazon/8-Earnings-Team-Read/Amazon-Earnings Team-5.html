Core view

All four close readings point to the same conclusion: a good business, real profits, negative cash flow and a shifting position. The only disagreement is what that "good" is worth. Duan Yongping works off unit economics and calls the price not cheap but acceptable; Buffett cannot get past cash flow; Munger argues the financial statements have been engineered and should be read on a worst-case basis; Li Lu believes the cycle position justifies paying a premium.

Synthesis 1: Four deep reads converge on the same "window period" conclusion

Duan Yongping's improving unit costs, Buffett's 15% ROIC, Munger's "the depreciation clock has just started ticking" and Li Lu's "early-to-mid cycle" all describe the same thing: this is the point of maximum efficiency and minimum cost pressure for the company, and it will pass.

The implication for investors is direct: do not value the company on the assumption that today's record margin is the norm. Treat the 13.9% operating margin as perpetual and 20x earnings looks absurdly cheap; assume it reverts to 11%–12% by 2027 and 20x is fair. At 20x, the market is already pricing the latter. That is also why the stock fell in after-hours trading rather than rallying after earnings.

Synthesis II: The only real point of contention is whether cash flow should be discounted

Buffett's argument is that negative free cash flow must be discounted: a company that cannot be discounted can only be valued on a profit-margin multiple, not a cash-flow multiple. Li Lu frames this as a cyclical feature—the first phase of an infrastructure cycle inherently generates no free cash flow, and railways, power grids and fibre optics all followed the same path.

Verdict: both are right; it depends on the time scale. On a three-year horizon Li Lu is right: as depreciation rises and capex peaks in 2027–2028, free cash flow turns positive. On a five-year horizon Buffett is right: if "investing RMB 300 billion every year" becomes the norm, this is permanently a negative-cash-flow company and the valuation should compress below 15x.

This disagreement cannot be settled by analysis, only by observation. The conclusion therefore translates into action: buy, but in three tranches, with the trigger conditions for the latter two written as "free cash flow turns positive" or "capex peaks." That is not procrastination; it turns the disagreement into discipline.

Conclusion

Synthesis: one earnings report, four readings, one action — buy, but not in full. None of the four deep dives says "avoid," and none says "go heavy." That highly convergent disagreement is itself a signal: this is not a question of right or wrong, it is a question of position size.
