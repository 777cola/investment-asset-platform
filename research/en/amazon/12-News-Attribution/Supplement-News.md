## Attribution: the three-month decline came entirely from valuation, not earnings

Split the three months into two phases. Phase one, 8/10–10/21: the stock fell from 278 to 262, down 5.8%, with no earnings information in the period — purely a cooling of the AI trade. Phase two, 10/22–11/06: from 262 to 259, roughly flat, but this span contained one earnings release (-5.6%) and one rebound (+6.1%). Combined, the two phases were -6.8%, while operating profit rose 61% year on year — the denominator (profit) rose and the numerator (price) fell, so the multiple compressed.

By how much? Before the release, the 10/28 close of 254.60 on TTM EPS of 12.46 implied 20.4x; after the release, the 10/30 close of 240.35 implied 19.3x; by 11/06 it was back to 20.8x. In other words, the market pushed the multiple down to 19x after the release, then bought back half of it. This is not "bearish" — it is "no idea what multiple to assign."

## Which two are signals

Signal 1: Capex guidance. Q3 alone came in at RMB 77.26 billion, up 120% YoY and 30% above the consensus, with full-year capex at RMB 220 billion and a "significant increase" in 2027. This alone pushed free cash flow to negative RMB 42.4 billion, and it is the only variable the market is pricing. Note one detail: the stock had already fallen 4.25% on the earnings day (10/29), then fell another 7%–12% after the results in after-hours trading, showing that the market had long expected "good growth, poor cash" — it just did not anticipate the scale of capex.

Signal 2: How sell-side is pricing 2027 capex. Deutsche Bank on 10/20 raised its 2027 capex forecast to about RMB 330 billion while leaving its target price and earnings forecasts essentially unchanged, and the stock moved just 0.1% that day. UBS on 10/26 raised its target price to USD 348 and stated explicitly that "the market has already priced in a 2027 capex increase from RMB 250 billion to above RMB 300 billion, with free cash flow expectations near zero or negative." Put the two together: high capex is already absorbed in the price; the real variable is when capex peaks.

## Which parts are noise, and why the noise is worth mentioning

Each of the five noise items has its own character. Model launches (GPT-6 Astra, Bedrock Managed Agents) are a long-term positive, but their near-term revenue contribution cannot be quantified — AI accounts for only part of AWS's RMB 46.1 billion, and listing a model does not mean revenue jumps immediately. Layoffs number only a few hundred, a rounding error for a company with 1.638 million employees, but they prove the organization is still slimming down; sales and marketing expenses at -0.3% and G&A at -6.1% are its traces.

Globalstar spectrum is an option, not profit: USD 11.5 billion for 25 MHz of spectrum, with Leo commercialization pushed to initial operations in late 2026 and partial cost capitalization only beginning in Q1 2027. The midterm elections are pure macro: Democrats sweep both chambers, AI regulatory fears ease, and cloud players rally across the board (Amazon +6.1%, Microsoft +6% to a record high) — the money gained in one day roughly equals the money lost over the two days of earnings — which in itself shows short-term prices are driven by sentiment.

The noise is worth mentioning because it explains the range: 240.35 to 278.09, a 15.7% amplitude, while fundamentals changed only once in these three months — "one earnings report." Whoever trades the swings within this range is earning sentiment money, not company money.
