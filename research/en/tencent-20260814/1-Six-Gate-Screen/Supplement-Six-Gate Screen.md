# Tencent Holdings（00700）— Six-Gate Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/08/14
- Price：440.0 HKD
- Market cap：Approximately HKD 4.01 trillion

## Notes

The six-gate score follows the AI Berkshire framework, with 10 points per gate. Data is taken from Tencent's Q2 2026 results announcement (2026/08/12): revenue RMB 204.79 billion, gross margin 57.8%, net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 56.02 billion, Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 68.42 billion, marketing services RMB 43.57 billion, value-added services RMB 98.41 billion, fintech and business services RMB 60.29 billion, capital expenditure RMB 52.78 billion, operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion, free cash flow -RMB 13.8 billion, net cash RMB 58.19 billion, WeChat MAU 1.44 billion. The 2026 Non-IFRS net profit of RMB 280 billion is a rough estimate annualized from the first-half figure of RMB 136.32 billion.

## Six-gate screen (2026/08/14, share price HKD 440.00)

| Checkpoint | Determination | Rating | Based on |
|---|---|---|---|

| Consensus | Through | 48/60 | Buy, scale in; add on a break below HKD 400 |

> Scores use a 10-point subjective scale, following the pre-purchase checklist framework used by Buffett. Share prices and 52-week ranges are taken from the closing quotes on 2026/08/14.

## Gate 1: Circle of competence (9/10, pass)

Tencent's revenue splits into four blocks, each with a one-sentence driver: VAS at RMB 98.414 billion tracks game billings and paying-user rates; marketing services at RMB 43.565 billion tracks traffic × ad load × eCPM; fintech and business services at RMB 60.286 billion tracks payment volume and cloud usage. This is the most comfortable kind of company within my circle of competence—**no need to forecast technology roadmaps, only to judge whether people will keep using WeChat and whether advertisers will keep raising budgets**.

The one point deducted is for capex. RMB 52.784 billion goes out, and what comes back is compute, depreciation, and an AI revenue line not yet broken out in the financials. I cannot work out this "input-return" math, so I give 9 points, not 10.

## Gate Two: Good Business (9/10, Pass)

Gross margin was 57.8%, up 1 percentage point year on year, and that came alongside general and administrative expenses up 22% and R&D spending of RMB 27.28 billion, up 35%. **This shows growth came from mix, not from cost-cutting**: the share of marketing services (segment gross margin 57%) and games (high margin) rose, while lower-margin livestreaming tips fell.

Advertising is the quintessential low-marginal-cost business: load one more ad and the cost is near zero, so the revenue is pure increment. Games are different — content costs are front-loaded and there is a product cycle, so gross margin runs a notch below advertising. With the two businesses combined, 57.8% is already a very high blended score.

## Gate 3: Moat (9/10, pass)

WeChat and WeChat combined MAU 1.439 billion, +2% YoY; Video Accounts usage time +20% YoY. **These two numbers together are the moat**: users have not left, and time spent inside WeChat is still rising — in the short-video battle, Tencent has gone from "being hit" to "holding the line."

A moat is not "many users," it is "cannot be replaced." Contacts, groups, payments, mini programs, official accounts and WeCom stack layer upon layer on the same relationship chain, so any single-point substitute will fail. There is only one test: can it raise prices. Tencent's ad load has long trailed peers, showing it still has unused pricing power.

## Gate 4: Management (8/10, pass)

The capital allocation record is clean: HKD 80 billion of buybacks in 2025, a final dividend of HKD 5.30 per share, a further 50.03 million shares repurchased for HKD 24.4 billion in the first half of 2026, and continuous buybacks in August (21 consecutive days before the results, roughly HKD 4.1 billion in total). **Large-scale buybacks on one hand and an explicit refusal of equity financing on the other** is the most direct statement to shareholders.

The deduction is the 2026 step-up: RMB 52.784 billion of capex in a single quarter, potentially close to RMB 200 billion for the full year, which management frames as "a one-off startup investment in new AI-native businesses." The framing holds, but it needs 2027 financials to verify — calling several tens of billions a year of spending "one-off" is itself a claim that must be tested.

## Gate 5: Margin of safety (6/10, barely passes)

Valuation is not the problem: 15x TTM, 13.2x 2026E Non-IFRS, and Citi's late-June one-year forward figures of 12.3x and 11.8x ex-cash all sit in the historical bottom range. The asset side is solid too: net cash of RMB 58.19 billion at end-June, plus a listed investment portfolio of about USD 72 billion that can be monetized.

What really costs points is the direction of cash flow. Operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion (RMB 74.38 billion a year earlier, RMB 101.35 billion last quarter), capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion and 2025 dividends of RMB 41.6 billion left free cash flow at negative RMB 13.8 billion, **the first negative quarterly free cash flow for Tencent in years**. The margin of safety is therefore no longer "it can't fall further" but "earnings must not be revised down." The certainty of these two things is entirely different, hence a score of 6.

## Level 6: Discipline (7/10, pass)

The buy thesis must be verifiable through quarterly reports, and Tencent is friendly on this front: three metrics suffice: **marketing services growth** (up 22% this quarter; a drop below 15% would signal the foundation is weakening), **whether capex has peaked** (RMB 52.784 billion this quarter, up 65% QoQ; watch whether it falls back in Q3), and **cloud revenue growth** (management's framing accelerated from "high teens" in Q1 to "low twenties" in Q2).

Two of the three metrics can be counted next quarter; the third must wait. The 7 score reflects that I cannot set a benchmark for the pace of AI revenue realization—this is a gap in discipline that can only be filled through position sizing.

## Veto items and items to be verified

**No veto items.** None of the six gates is a "fail"; the fifth gate passes narrowly rather than being skirted. That is rare for a company down 35% — most stocks down 30% have at least one broken gate.

**Two preconditions:** first, marketing services growth must not fall below 15%; it is the only segment that accelerated this quarter, and once it decelerates, the RMB 52.784 billion of capex turns from "investment" into "pure cost". Second, capex cannot stay above RMB 50 billion per quarter for long, otherwise buyback capacity erodes and the shareholder-return line breaks.

**Three things to verify:** ① Q3 marketing services growth and WeChat Channels ad load; ② whether full-year capex exceeds RMB 200 billion and whether Q3 capex falls sequentially; ③ whether enterprise services (cloud) growth holds above 20%. **If two of the three deteriorate, the fifth gate's score of 6 must be revised.**

## Align three scope points with the working papers

Three definitional discrepancies between the six-gate analysis and the working paper (condensed version) are noted here for reference: **①Gross margin** — the working paper shows 57.8%, some media report 58%; this note uses 57.8% throughout; **②Non-IFRS net profit growth** — the working paper shows +9%, the company's exact figure is +8.51%; this note uses +9% (rounded); **③Market cap** — the working paper shows HKD 4.01 trillion, calculated as 440.00 × 9.103 billion shares = HKD 4.005 trillion, consistent.

None of the three affects the conclusion. They are noted here because when the same figure appears in two forms in one report, readers will doubt the other figures. **Consistency of definition matters more than precision.**
