Six-Gate Screen — Tencent Holdings

Core view

Six gates 48/60, all passed — but this is a "good company + reasonable price" paper, not "good company + cheap price." HKD 440 equates to 15x P/E (TTM) and just over 13x 2026 Non-IFRS P/E, pressing against the lower edge of Tencent's valuation range over the past decade. Over the same period revenue of RMB 204.79 billion grew 11%, gross margin reached 57.8% and marketing services rose 22% — no part of the core business is deteriorating.

The disagreement sits solely at the fifth gate: quarterly capex of RMB 52.78 billion, +176% YoY, and free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion. The fulcrum of margin of safety has shifted from "assets on the books" to "earnings no longer being cut" — hence a score of 6, not a full position.

Gate 1: Circle of competence (9/10, pass)

Tencent's revenue splits into four blocks, each with a one-line driver: VAS at RMB 98.414 billion tracks game billings and paying-user rates; marketing services at RMB 43.565 billion tracks traffic × ad load × eCPM; fintech and business services at RMB 60.286 billion tracks payment volume and cloud usage. This is the most comfortable kind of company within my circle of competence—no need to forecast technology roadmaps, only to judge whether people will keep using WeChat and whether advertisers will keep raising budgets.

The one point deducted is for capex. RMB 52.784 billion goes out, and what comes back is compute, depreciation, and an AI revenue line not yet broken out in the financials. I cannot work out this "input-return" math, so I give 9 points, not 10.

Gate Two: Good Business (9/10, Pass)

Gross margin reached 57.8%, up 1 percentage point YoY, even as general and administrative expenses rose 22% and R&D spending climbed 35% to RMB 27.28 billion. Growth came from mix, not cost-cutting: the revenue share of marketing services (segment gross margin 57%) and high-margin games increased, while lower-margin livestreaming tips declined.

Advertising is the quintessential low-marginal-cost business: one more ad carries near-zero cost, so the revenue is pure increment. Games differ — content costs are front-loaded and the product cycle bites, leaving gross margin a notch below advertising. Blending the two, 57.8% is already a very high score.

Gate 3: Moat (9/10, pass)

WeChat and Weixin combined MAU reached 1.439 billion, +2% YoY, with Video Accounts time spent +20% YoY. Together these two numbers are the moat: users have not left, and time spent inside WeChat is still rising — in short video, Tencent has gone from being hit to holding the line.

A moat is not "many users" but "cannot be replaced." Contacts, groups, payments, mini programs, official accounts and WeCom stack layer upon layer on the same relationship chain, so any single-point substitute fails. There is only one test: can it raise prices. Tencent's ad load has long trailed peers, showing unused pricing power remains.

Gate 4: Management (8/10, pass)

The capital allocation record is clean: HKD 80 billion of buybacks in 2025, a final dividend of HKD 5.30 per share, a further 50.03 million shares repurchased for HKD 24.4 billion in 1H26, and continuous buybacks in August (21 consecutive days before the results, roughly HKD 4.1 billion in total). Large-scale buybacks on one hand and an explicit refusal of equity financing on the other is the most direct statement to shareholders.

The deduction is the 2026 step-up: RMB 52.784 billion of capex in a single quarter, potentially close to RMB 200 billion for the full year, which management frames as "a one-off startup investment in new AI-native businesses." The framing holds, but it needs 2027 financials to verify — calling several tens of billions a year of spending "one-off" is itself a claim that must be tested.

Gate 5: Margin of safety (6/10, barely passes)

Valuation is not the issue: 15x TTM, 13.2x 2026E Non-IFRS and Citi's late-June one-year forward multiples of 12.3x and 11.8x ex-cash all sit in the historical bottom range. The asset side is equally solid, with net cash of RMB 58.19 billion at end-June and a listed investment portfolio of about USD 72 billion that can be monetised.

What really costs points is the direction of cash flow. Operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion (versus RMB 74.38 billion a year earlier and RMB 101.35 billion last quarter), capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion and 2025 dividends of RMB 41.6 billion left free cash flow at negative RMB 13.8 billion — the first negative quarterly free cash flow for Tencent in years. The margin of safety is therefore no longer "it can't fall further" but "earnings must not be revised down." The certainty of these two propositions is entirely different, hence a score of 6.

Level 6: Discipline (7/10, pass)

The buy thesis has to be verifiable through quarterly reports, and Tencent is accommodating on this front: three metrics suffice. Marketing services growth rose 22% this quarter; a drop below 15% would signal the foundation is weakening. Whether capex has peaked: RMB 52.784 billion this quarter, up 65% QoQ, so watch whether it falls back in Q3. Cloud revenue growth: management's framing accelerated from "high teens" in Q1 to "low twenties" in Q2.

Two of the three can be counted next quarter; the third must wait. The 7 score reflects that I cannot set a benchmark for the pace of AI revenue realisation—a gap in discipline that only position sizing can fill.

Veto items and items to be verified

No veto items. None of the six gates fails outright; the fifth passes narrowly rather than being skirted. That is rare for a stock down 35% — most names down 30% have at least one broken gate.

Two preconditions. First, marketing services growth must not fall below 15%: it is the only segment that accelerated this quarter, and once it decelerates the RMB 52.784 billion of capex shifts from "investment" to "pure cost". Second, capex cannot stay above RMB 50 billion per quarter for long, or buyback capacity erodes and the shareholder-return line breaks.

Three things to verify: ① Q3 marketing services growth and WeChat Channels ad load; ② whether full-year capex exceeds RMB 200 billion and whether Q3 capex falls sequentially; ③ whether enterprise services (cloud) growth holds above 20%. If two of the three deteriorate, the fifth gate's score of 6 must be revised.

Align three scope points with the working papers

Three definitional discrepancies between the six-gate analysis and the working paper (condensed version) are noted for reference: ①Gross margin — the working paper shows 57.8%, some media report 58%; this note uses 57.8% throughout; ②Non-IFRS net profit growth — the working paper shows +9%, the company's exact figure is +8.51%; this note uses +9% (rounded); ③Market cap — the working paper shows HKD 4.01 trillion, calculated as 440.00 × 9.103 billion shares = HKD 4.005 trillion, consistent.

None of the three affects the conclusion. They are flagged because a figure appearing in two forms within one report invites doubt about the other figures. Consistency of definition matters more than precision.

Conclusion

The six gates score 48/60: good business, wide moat, credible management, price at the low end of its historical range. This is not "a cheap bad company" but "a good company hitting a turn in the direction of its cash flow." The turn costs RMB 52.784 billion in quarterly capex, and it is happening because the AI entry point cannot be outsourced to others.

Action: open the first position at HKD 440 (half the target position) and fill out the rest below HKD 400. Two hard-coded conditions would change the thesis — marketing services growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters, or capex above RMB 50 billion for three consecutive quarters with cloud growth falling below 15%. If either occurs, exit.
