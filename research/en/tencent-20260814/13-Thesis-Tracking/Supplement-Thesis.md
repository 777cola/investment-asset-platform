## How the assumptions were documented: begin by stating "where I am wrong"

The order in which assumptions are written matters. Most people reach a conclusion first and then hunt for evidence; the right approach is to ask first "if this judgment is wrong, where would it break," then write that down as a verifiable sentence.

For this Tencent position, the three "could be wrong" points are: ① I assumed advertising could grow 20% indefinitely, but the load rate has a ceiling; ② I assumed capex was one-off, but it is in fact the norm; ③ I assumed WeChat would not be bypassed, but AI entry points will rewrite habits. These map to the three hard assumptions at the top of this report.

The benefit of writing it this way: after each quarterly report there is no need to rethink the logic from scratch—just check whether the verification metrics for the three assumptions have deteriorated. The value of discipline lies not in "never being wrong," but in "knowing immediately when wrong."

## Four hold up, with varying degrees of substance

The strongest is the first: MAU of 1.439 billion, +2%, still edging up in a Chinese internet market where penetration has peaked — evidence the social graph remains intact. The third is the next strongest: gross margin of 57.8%, +1pct, and lifting margin in a year of rising costs is something only pricing power can achieve.

The second (advertising) and fourth (cloud) are "valid but need to persist": advertising +22% had a one-off factor from 618, and cloud's low-twenties growth had a price-increase factor from the AI compute shortage.If these two pull back next quarter, it does not mean the thesis is wrong, only that the pace has slowed.

## The one still to be verified is the most important one.

"Capex is a one-time startup investment" — that is management's line, not mine. It settles two things: if it holds, 2026 is the investment year and 2027 the harvest year, and 13x P/E is clearly undervalued; if it does not, asset-heavy operations become the norm and fair value is only 10–11x.

The verification windows are 2026Q3 and 2027Q1: watch whether quarterly capex falls from RMB 52.784 billion back below RMB 40 billion, and whether the company begins separately disclosing AI revenue or utilization rates on its compute assets. If neither metric appears, treat it as "does not hold."

## The one wavering item: 3% shareholder return

HKD 24.4 billion of buybacks and RMB 41.6 billion of dividends in H1 2026 annualise to roughly 2.3%, barely clearing the threshold. But that threshold rests on free cash flow, and free cash flow was negative RMB 13.8 billion this quarter. If Q3 and Q4 stay negative, the buyback must shrink.

Wavering is not rupture. Tencent still holds RMB 58.191 billion of net cash and a USD 72 billion investment portfolio to fund buybacks—enough to last two to three years. But it means "buybacks put a floor under the share price" cannot be the main thesis this year—one reason I opened a 4% initial position rather than 8%.

## Falsification conditions: when to abandon the thesis entirely

Four hard conditions, any one of which halves the position and any two of which trigger a full exit: 1) Marketing services growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters (the advertising foundation is collapsing); 2) Capex above RMB 50 billion for three consecutive quarters while cloud growth falls below 15% (spending without returns); 3) Non-IFRS net profit posts negative YoY growth (the core business starts to shrink); 4) WeChat MAU posts negative YoY growth for two consecutive quarters (the relationship chain is loosening).

One soft condition also applies: MAU of Doubao-style AI assistants exceeds 300 million while Xiaowei has yet to scale — the entry point is being bypassed and the moat is downgraded. The soft condition does not directly trigger a sale, but cuts the target position from 8% to 4%.

## Watch calendar for next steps

November 2026 (Q3 results): whether capex falls back below RMB 40 billion, whether operating cash flow returns above RMB 70 billion, and whether marketing services growth holds at 18%. March 2027 (annual report): the full-year 2026 capex landing point (RMB 180 billion or RMB 220 billion), whether depreciation policy changes, and whether AI revenue is broken out separately for the first time.

May 2027 (Q1): Spring Festival game grossings, cloud growth, and whether buybacks resume. August 2027 (Q2): the tough base for domestic games (RMB 47.3 billion in Q2 2026), whether Hy4 launches commercially, and mini-program user scale. March 2027 matters most of these four dates—it decides whether the stock trades at 13x or 17x.

## One-line status

Six assumptions: four green, one yellow, one grey. The green ones are the reason to hold; the grey one is the reason not to go all in. This portfolio does not need weekly reviews, only a quarterly check — because its risk is not sudden deterioration, but slow deterioration.
