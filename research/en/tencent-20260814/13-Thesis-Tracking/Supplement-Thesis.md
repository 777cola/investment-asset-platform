## How the assumptions were written down: start by stating "where I am wrong"

The order in which assumptions are written matters. Usually people reach a conclusion first, then look for evidence; the right approach is to first ask "if this judgment is wrong, where would it go wrong," then write it as a verifiable sentence.

The three "could be wrong" points for this Tencent position are: ① I assumed advertising could grow 20% indefinitely, but the load rate has a ceiling; ② I assumed capex was one-off, but it is in fact the norm; ③ I assumed WeChat would not be bypassed, but AI entry points will rewrite habits. These three correspond to the three hard assumptions at the top of this report.

The benefit of writing it this way: after each quarterly report, there is no need to rethink the logic from scratch—just check whether the verification metrics for the three assumptions have deteriorated. The value of discipline lies not in "never being wrong," but in "knowing immediately when wrong."

## Four hold up, with varying degrees of substance

The strongest is the first: MAU of 1.439 billion, +2%, still edging up in China's internet market where penetration has peaked, showing the social graph remains intact. The second strongest is the third: gross margin of 57.8%, +1pct, lifting margin in a year of rising costs is something only pricing power can achieve.

The second (advertising) and fourth (cloud) are "valid but need to persist": advertising +22% had a one-off factor from 618, and cloud's low-twenties growth had a price-increase factor from the AI compute shortage.If these two pull back next quarter, it does not mean the thesis is wrong, only that the pace has slowed.

## The one still to be verified is the most important one.

"Capex is a one-time startup investment" — that line came from management, not from me. It settles two things: if it holds, 2026 is the investment year and 2027 the harvest year, and 13x PE is clearly undervalued; if it does not, this is asset-heavy operations becoming the norm, and fair value is only 10–11x.

The verification windows are Q3 2026 and Q1 2027: watch whether quarterly capex falls from RMB 52.784 billion back below RMB 40 billion, and whether the company begins separately disclosing AI revenue or utilization rates of its compute assets. If neither metric appears, treat it as "does not hold."

## The one wavering item: 3% shareholder return

Buybacks of HKD 24.4 billion and dividend payments of RMB 41.6 billion in H1 2026 annualize to roughly 2.3%, barely clearing the threshold. But that threshold depends on free cash flow, and free cash flow this quarter was negative RMB 13.8 billion. If Q3 and Q4 stay negative, the buyback scale must shrink.

Wavering is not rupture. Tencent still has RMB 58.191 billion in net cash and a USD 72 billion investment portfolio to support buybacks, enough to last two to three years. But it means "buybacks put a floor under the share price" cannot be the main thesis this year—one reason I opened a 4% initial position rather than 8%.

## Falsification conditions: when to abandon the thesis entirely

Four hard conditions: any one triggers a halving of the position, any two trigger a full exit: 1) Marketing services growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters (the advertising foundation is collapsing); 2) Capex above RMB 50 billion for three consecutive quarters while cloud growth falls below 15% (spending without returns); 3) Non-IFRS net profit posts negative year-on-year growth (the core business starts to shrink); 4) WeChat MAU posts negative year-on-year growth for two consecutive quarters (the relationship chain is loosening).

There is also one soft condition: MAU of Doubao-style AI assistants exceeds 300 million while Xiaowei has yet to scale — meaning the entry point is being bypassed and the moat is downgraded. The soft condition does not directly trigger a sale, but cuts the target position from 8% to 4%.

## Watch calendar for next steps

November 2026 (Q3 results): whether capex falls back below RMB 40 billion, whether operating cash flow returns above RMB 70 billion, and whether marketing services growth holds at 18%. March 2027 (annual report): the full-year 2026 capex landing point (RMB 180 billion or RMB 220 billion), whether depreciation policy changes, and whether AI revenue is broken out separately for the first time.

May 2027 (Q1): Spring Festival game grossings, cloud growth, and whether buybacks resume. August 2027 (Q2): the tough base for domestic games (RMB 47.3 billion in Q2 2026), whether Hy4 launches commercially, and mini-program user scale. Of these four dates, March 2027 matters most—that is the day that decides whether this stock trades at 13x or 17x.

## One-line status

Six assumptions: four green, one yellow, one grey. The green ones are the reason to hold; the grey one is the reason not to go all in. This portfolio does not need weekly reviews, only a quarterly check — because its risk is not sudden deterioration, but slow deterioration.
