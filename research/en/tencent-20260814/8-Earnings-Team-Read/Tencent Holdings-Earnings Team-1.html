Master Class Close Read (Duan Yongping) — Tencent Holdings

Focus on just two things: where the incremental growth comes from, and whether users are still worth the price.

Core view

Nearly all of this quarter's incremental growth came from advertising: marketing services RMB 43.57 billion, +22%, +14% QoQ, while social networks grew just 0.8% and international games fell 0.8%. A company whose incremental growth concentrates in the business with the most pricing power — that is the best combination.

The Duan Yongping-style question: was this incremental growth "taken" or "lifted by a rising industry tide"? The answer is taken — industry advertising growth was only single-digit to low-double-digit.

Incremental attribution: of the RMB 22.2 billion increase, advertising contributed RMB 7.8 billion

By segment: marketing services up about RMB 7.8 billion year on year (435.65 vs. about 357), domestic games up about RMB 6.9 billion (473 vs. 404), fintech and business services up about RMB 5.0 billion (602.86 vs. 553), social networks up about RMB 260 million (325 vs. 322.5), international games down RMB 150 million.The four together add up to about RMB 19.8 billion; the remaining increment comes from "others."

Advertising contributed one-third of the increment and is the only one of the four that is accelerating. Duan Yongping would like this structure: the increment comes from a business where "loading one more ad costs almost nothing extra," not from a business that "requires more R&D and more chips."

The quality of revenue: one-off or recurring

How much of the +22% in marketing services was a one-off pull from 618? Look at the quarter-on-quarter figure: +14%. If it were all 618, QoQ would be a large positive and YoY would be flat; both are high now, which means three things are stacking: inventory + efficiency + the major promotion.

The +17% in gaming is lower quality: it rests on four products online at once, the top of a product cycle. Duan Yongping's view of this kind of revenue is "good, but not something you get every year." So in his valuation, the RMB 47.3 billion from gaming should be discounted as a "cyclical peak," while the RMB 43.565 billion from advertising can be given a multiple on "steady growth." This is where sum-of-the-parts valuation is more accurate than a blended PE.

Not-to-do list: a decade of restraint on load rate — that is culture

Tencent's ad load on Video Accounts has long trailed peers; this is public fact. The forgone revenue runs into the tens of billions, but what it buys is user experience that holds up and time spent still rising (+20% this quarter). The "benfen" that Duan Yongping values most shows up concretely in this choice not to "earn the last copper coin."

Conversely, this is also a measurable "hidden asset": ad load is not yet maxed out, meaning ad growth over the next two to three years has an organic source and does not need a macro recovery. Such an asset is invisible on the financial statements but valuable in valuation.

Is RMB 142 per user per quarter expensive?

RMB 204.79 billion ÷ 1.439 billion = RMB 142 per quarter. Broken down: advertising RMB 30, gaming about RMB 46, payments and cloud about RMB 42, social networks RMB 23. Against China's per-capita quarterly disposable income (about RMB 11,000), Tencent earns 1.3% from each user — a low ratio, implying room to grow.

Duan Yongping would frame it more simply: do users think the price is worth it? The switching cost of the communications, payments, content and mini-programs WeChat provides is far above RMB 142 per quarter. As long as that inequality holds, there is room to raise prices (ad load, membership, cloud).

Conclusion

A Duan Yongping-perspective read: the increment comes from the best business segment, user value still has room, and culturally he sees nothing he dislikes. The one reservation is capex — a company that "doesn't take the last copper coin" suddenly spending RMB 52.784 billion in a single quarter needs explaining within his framework.

His explanation would probably be: if this is defense (invest or be knocked out), then it should be invested; if this is offense (grabbing the AI entry point), then it depends on the odds. Tencent is now both, so he buys, but not fully invested.
