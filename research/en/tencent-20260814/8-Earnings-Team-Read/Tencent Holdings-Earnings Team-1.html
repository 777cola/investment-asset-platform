Master Class Close Read (Duan Yongping) — Tencent Holdings

Focus on just two things: where the incremental growth comes from, and whether users are still worth the price.

Core view

Advertising drove nearly all of the quarter's incremental growth: marketing services reached RMB 43.57 billion, +22% and +14% QoQ, while social networks grew just 0.8% and international games fell 0.8%. Incremental growth concentrated in the business with the most pricing power — the best possible combination.

The Duan Yongping-style question is whether that incremental growth was "taken" or "lifted by a rising industry tide". It was taken: industry advertising growth ran only single-digit to low-double-digit.

Incremental attribution: of the RMB 22.2 billion increase, advertising contributed RMB 7.8 billion

By segment: marketing services +RMB 7.8bn YoY (435.65 vs. c.357), domestic games +RMB 6.9bn (473 vs. 404), fintech and business services +RMB 5.0bn (602.86 vs. 553), social networks +RMB 260m (325 vs. 322.5), international games -RMB 150m.Together the four add c.RMB 19.8bn; the balance of the increment comes from "others."

Advertising supplied one-third of the increment and is the only one of the four accelerating. Duan Yongping would like this mix: the increment comes from a business where "loading one more ad costs almost nothing extra," not one that "requires more R&D and more chips."

The quality of revenue: one-off or recurring

How much of the +22% in marketing services was a one-off pull from 618? The QoQ figure answers it: +14%. If 618 explained the whole move, QoQ would be a large positive and YoY flat; both are now high, which means three things are stacking: inventory, efficiency and the major promotion.

The +17% in gaming is lower quality: it rests on four products live at once, the top of a product cycle. Duan Yongping's view of this kind of revenue is "good, but not something you get every year." In his valuation, the RMB 47.3 billion from gaming should therefore be discounted as a "cyclical peak," while the RMB 43.565 billion from advertising can carry a multiple on "steady growth." This is where sum-of-the-parts valuation beats a blended P/E.

A decade of load-rate discipline — that is culture

Tencent's ad load on Video Accounts has long trailed peers — a matter of public record. The forgone revenue runs into the tens of billions, but what it buys is a user experience that holds up and time spent still rising (+20% this quarter). The "benfen" that Duan Yongping values most shows up concretely in this choice not to "earn the last copper coin."

Conversely, this is also a measurable "hidden asset": ad load is not yet maxed out, so ad growth over the next two to three years has an organic source and does not need a macro recovery. Such an asset is invisible on the financial statements but valuable in valuation.

Is RMB 142 per user per quarter expensive?

RMB 204.79 billion ÷ 1.439 billion = RMB 142 per quarter. By segment: advertising RMB 30, gaming c.RMB 46, payments and cloud c.RMB 42, social networks RMB 23. Against China's per-capita quarterly disposable income of about RMB 11,000, Tencent captures 1.3% from each user — a low ratio that implies headroom to grow.

Duan Yongping would put it more simply: do users think the price is worth it? The switching costs embedded in the communications, payments, content and mini-programs WeChat provides sit far above RMB 142 per quarter. So long as that inequality holds, there is room to raise prices — via ad load, membership and cloud.

Conclusion

A Duan Yongping-perspective read: the increment comes from the best business segment, user value still has room, and culturally he sees nothing he dislikes. The one reservation is capex — a company that "doesn't take the last copper coin" suddenly spending RMB 52.784 billion in a single quarter needs explaining within his framework.

His explanation would probably run: if this is defense (invest or be knocked out), then it should be invested; if it is offense (grabbing the AI entry point), it depends on the odds. Tencent is now both, so he buys, but not fully invested.
