The market only got half of Tencent's earnings report right
Research date 2026/08/14  ·  Price HK$440.00 (-0.23%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 4.01 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 15.0x

Core view

Free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion and capex up 176% were enough to take Tencent from HKD 677.5 to HKD 440. Those numbers are real, but the conclusion is only half right.

The other half: revenue of RMB 204.785 billion, +11%, gross margin 57.8%, +1 point, advertising +22%, domestic games +17%. The core business is improving even as cash thins — both at once, and the market has priced only the second.

I. A financial report that has been misread

Tencent reported second-quarter 2026 results after the close on August 12. Revenue came in at RMB 204.785 billion, up 11% YoY and 1% above the Bloomberg consensus of RMB 202.8 billion, with gross margin at 57.8%, up 1 percentage point YoY.

H-shares fell 4.46% to HKD 441 the next day and drifted a little lower still, to HKD 440, the day after. Measured from HKD 677.5 last October, the stock is down 35%.

The case for the selloff looks solid: quarterly capex of RMB 52.784 billion, up 176% YoY, and free cash flow of negative RMB 13.8 billion — the company's first quarterly net cash outflow in years. This is plainly a trade of present cash for a future ticket.

II. The overlooked half: advertising is accelerating

Marketing services revenue in the same report was RMB 43.565 billion, up 22% YoY and 14% QoQ. The QoQ number is the more telling of the two: YoY carries a base effect, whereas QoQ points to genuine acceleration. Industry advertising growth is running only in the single digits to low double digits, so Tencent is expanding at close to twice the pace.

Management sets out the drivers plainly: rollout of the AIM+ delivery system, recommendation models lifting conversion, WeChat Stores and short dramas adding new inventory, and the 618 shopping festival. Three of the four sit within Tencent's own control.

Domestic games generated RMB 47.3 billion, up 17%, with four titles live at once: Delta Force, Valorant, Roco Kingdom: World and Peacekeeper Elite. This marks the peak of the product cycle, but it also shows Tencent's operating capability remains intact.

III. Two measures of profit, a RMB 10.5 billion gap

Non-IFRS operating profit was RMB 75.64 billion, +9%. On an alternative basis that excludes the revenue, cost and expenses of new AI products, it was RMB 86.1 billion, +19%. The RMB 10.46 billion gap is the net drag from new AI products this quarter.

The number matters because it separates two things: core business profit is accelerating (+19%), while new AI products are burning cash (annualised over RMB 40 billion). "Tencent's profit has stalled" is wrong, and so is "Tencent's profit is strong"—it depends on which half you mean.

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 56.022 billion, up only 0.7% and RMB 12.4 billion below Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders; the difference is fair value changes on the investment portfolio. This item is unrelated to operations but will keep distorting the statements.

4. Half of the -RMB 13.8 billion is actually timing

Free cash flow = operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion − capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion − other ≈ -RMB 13.8 billion. The quarter also absorbed the 2025 annual dividend of RMB 41.6 billion. The dividend is paid once a year; capex is the recurring item.

Management offered an adjusted basis: excluding prepayments for computing power procurement, free cash flow is +RMB 37.6 billion. That figure cannot be taken as a conclusion in itself, but it shows one thing—operating cash generation has not broken; cash was drained by two large outlays: "prepayments + dividend."

The real concern is that operating cash flow itself fell 29% (RMB 52.71 billion vs RMB 74.376 billion). If this line does not recover in Q3, the nature of the story changes.

Five, what are you buying at HKD 440?

Market cap HKD 4.01 trillion, 2026 Non-IFRS net profit of about RMB 280 billion, 13.2x. That sits at the low end of Tencent's decade-long valuation range and 10% below the level at which management itself bought back stock (average repurchase price HKD 487.7 in the first half).

Base case: 2027 Non-IFRS RMB 320 billion × 14x = HKD 535, +22%. Bear case: RMB 280 billion × 11x = HKD 370, -16%. Bull case: RMB 350 billion × 17x = HKD 700, +59%.

Payoff ratio 1.4:1. I assign 50% probability to the base case, 30% to bear and 20% to bull, for an expected return of +18%. This setup suits scaling in tranches, not a full position. Buy half at HKD 440, add the rest below 400, stop adding above 480.

One honest note to close: this is not an investment that turns your life around. It is an investment in "the best business at a fair price" — 12%–15% annualized, driven by 9% profit growth plus 2.3% shareholder return, plus a little valuation repair.

Conclusion

Conclusion: The market has priced in half the story—the swing to negative cash flow—and forgotten the other half: accelerating advertising and margin expansion. At HKD 440 you are buying the second half; the risk sits in the first.
