## Position in the supply chain (1): who depends on whom

Upstream, Tencent is a buyer: of chips, power and content. It has no bargaining power over chipmakers (compute is scarce), but it does over content providers (it can build its own studios). Downstream, Tencent is a seller: advertisers need its traffic, developers need access to its platform, merchants need its payments.

This "weak upstream, strong downstream" structure hurts most when compute is scarce: costs rise, but prices cannot rise in step (ad prices are set by auction). So Tencent's 1pt of gross margin expansion in 2026 is no small feat—it offset rising costs through revenue-mix optimisation (a higher ad share).

## Position in the industry chain (II): what is the platform position worth

Tencent's on-chain value in one sentence: it wholesales the attention of 1.439 billion people, retails it to advertisers and gamers, and earns a 57.8% gross margin in between. The lower the wholesale price (content, bandwidth, compute) and the higher the retail price (advertising eCPM, game spending), the more it earns.

The 2026 problem is that the wholesale price has risen (compute). If the increase is permanent, Tencent's gross margin ceiling gets pressed to around 55%; if it is cyclical (compute oversupply in 2027), gross margin can return to 58%–60%. That is why the "compute cycle" is the most important external variable for this company, more important than the advertising cycle.

## Social: the only category that earns a 10

The reason is not "many users" but "migration is impossible." Replacing WeChat means replacing contacts, group chat history, payment bindings, loyalty cards in mini programs and clients in WeCom. That is five things at once—something no single-point product can do.

The data confirms it: MAU of 1.439 billion, up 2% YoY, still edging higher even with penetration at its ceiling; time spent on Channels up 20%, showing it is still taking time from other apps. A product considered "old" is still gaining engagement—evidence that its moat is still deepening.

## Cloud and AI: the case for a 6

Cloud is a second-tier player in China: Alibaba Cloud leads, while Huawei Cloud and Tencent Cloud contest second place. Tencent Cloud differentiates on audio-video, gaming and its social ecosystem rather than on general-purpose compute. That lets it retain a set of customers, but not win the largest ones.

AI is thinner still. Hunyuan Hy3 is a cost-efficiency-first model: 295B total parameters, 21B activated, 256K context, API priced at RMB 0.25 per million tokens—a price-war play, not a capability-leadership play. Hy4 does not arrive until year-end, and whether it reaches the first tier is unknown.

Tencent, however, has something no one else has: a scenario base of 1.439 billion people. Models not leading, scenarios leading—this is the only card it can play. WorkBuddy ranking first in traffic among domestic office AI validates that logic.

## The most dangerous rival: ByteDance, and Doubao behind it

ByteDance poses a two-layered threat. The first is time spent: Douyin is still competing with WeChat Channels for the same pool of user time, and WeChat Channels is holding the line rather than winning users back. The second is the entry point: Doubao is positioning itself as "the AI assistant as entry point." If users grow accustomed to asking an assistant questions instead of opening an app, WeChat's value as an entry point gets bypassed.

The second layer is the truly dangerous one. WeChat's moat rests on "you must open it." Once "you can get things done without opening it" becomes possible, the moat degrades from a social graph to a contact list. Tencent's response is Xiaowei — embedding AI inside WeChat so that the entry point remains WeChat. The product is still in gray-box testing; success or failure will be decided in 2027.

## Specific figures for switching costs

"High switching costs" is a claim that needs numbers. The average WeChat user has roughly 200 contacts, a dozen or so group chats, linked bank cards, memberships across a handful of mini-programs and work relationships on WeCom. Moving to another app means rebuilding all five, a cost measured in months with zero offsetting benefit.

Contrast that with switching short-video apps, which costs "retraining the recommendation algorithm" — about two weeks — or switching e-commerce apps, which costs "re-entering your address" — about ten minutes. Hence Douyin can capture time spent but not WeChat: it captures the replaceable part, not the irreplaceable part.

## The flip side of a moat: what can breach it

Three forces: 1) A shift in the entry point — if AI assistants become the primary entry point, users no longer need to open the app, and WeChat is demoted from "entry point" to "contact list"; 2) Regulator-mandated interoperability — mandated interconnection would erode the exclusivity of the relationship chain; 3) Generational migration — if young users migrate en masse to new forms of social networking, the relationship chain breaks across generations.

The first is already happening (Doubao, Yuanbao), the second is a long-term policy direction, and the third has no supporting evidence so far (WeChat is still growing slightly). The first therefore warrants the closest watching, and Tencent's response is WeChat mini-programs — putting AI inside WeChat.

## If you could keep only one moat

Choose the relationship chain. Not because it scores highest (10 points), but because it is the most resistant to technological change. Technology will change, entry points will change, devices will change, but "the person you are looking for is in this network" will not.

Conversely, if the relationship chain loosens, the other four fail together: games lose distribution, advertising loses traffic, payments lose scenarios, cloud loses customers. That is why I made "WeChat MAU negative growth for two consecutive quarters" one of the hard falsification conditions.

Defending this one costs the least (no need to spend RMB 20 billion a year); defending cloud and AI costs the most. That is the difference between a "moat" and an "arms race."
