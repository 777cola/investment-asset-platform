04 Financial Reality: Five Numbers, One Story — Tencent Holdings

Only one item in the report is genuinely new.

Core view

Five numbers stand out: revenue of RMB 204.785 billion (+11%), gross margin of 57.8% (+1pct), Non-IFRS operating profit of RMB 75.64 billion (+9%; RMB 86.1 billion, +19% excluding AI), operating cash flow of RMB 52.710 billion (-29%) and free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion, negative for the first time.

The first three extend existing trends; the last two are new information. The market prices only new information, hence the 4.46% drop.

All incremental revenue came from advertising and gaming

Revenue rose RMB 20.3 billion (2,047.85 vs. 1,845), with marketing services adding about RMB 7.8 billion, domestic games about RMB 6.9 billion, FinTech and business services about RMB 5.0 billion and social networks about RMB 0.3 billion, partly offset by a RMB 0.15 billion decline in international games. Advertising and games contributed 72% of the increment.

The mix points to decent growth quality: advertising is the highest-gross-margin segment (57%), followed by games. Growth came from high-margin businesses, which is why gross margin rose 1 point. Had the increment come from low-margin livestreaming or hardware, gross margin would have fallen.

It also reveals high concentration: the moment advertising decelerates, overall growth drops straight to single digits. This is the most fragile part of the report.

Truth two: the RMB 10.5 billion AI loss sits between two accounting bases

The company reported two Non-IFRS operating profit figures: RMB 75.64 billion (+9%) and RMB 86.1 billion (+19%), the latter excluding "revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products." The RMB 10.46 billion gap is the net drag from new businesses—Hunyuan, Yuanbao, WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy and Xiaowei—in the quarter.

Track this number separately: it is the only public data point for gauging AI investment intensity. Annualised at over RMB 40 billion, it approaches 14% of 2026 Non-IFRS net profit.

It also serves as a validation yardstick. If the gap narrows to within RMB 5 billion by 2027, the AI business is nearing break-even; if it widens to RMB 15 billion, investment is still accelerating. When reading the financials, look for this gap first—it is more direct than capex.

Truth three: three cash-flow lines bleed simultaneously

Operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion (RMB 74.38 billion in the year-ago period, RMB 101.35 billion last quarter), capex payments RMB 59.3 billion, dividend payments RMB 41.6 billion. All three lines moved outward together, leaving free cash flow at negative RMB 13.8 billion.

Assigning responsibility: capex is "active" (building AI infrastructure), dividends are "passive" (once a year, concentrated at a single point), and the decline in operating cash flow is what "needs explaining." Of the three, only the third is a genuine warning signal.

Management's adjusted framing (excluding compute prepayments of +RMB 37.6 billion) clarifies how the second item relates to the first: prepayments are money paid in advance, not money spent. But prepayments eventually become depreciation, so they only defer the pain, not eliminate it.

Truth four: net cash fell RMB 88.7 billion in a single quarter

Net cash stood at RMB 58.19 billion at end-June versus RMB 146.86 billion at end-March. The RMB 88.7 billion decline is fully accounted for by capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion plus dividends of RMB 41.6 billion, partly offset by operating cash flow. This is not a loss; it is a change in how cash was used.

RMB 58.19 billion is thin for a company with a RMB 4 trillion market cap (1.4% of market cap). But Tencent has another card to play: a listed investment portfolio of about USD 72 billion (roughly RMB 510 billion, 14% of market cap), which can be monetised and is the real reserve tank.

Management was explicit that it will not raise equity and will fund AI investment through operating cash flow plus the investment portfolio. The implication: if cash flow falls short over the next two years, we will see investment stakes sold down. That weighs on the income statement (lower investment gains) but supports cash flow.

Truth five: depreciation has yet to bite

Capex of RMB 52.784 billion, with very little depreciation booked this quarter, as the equipment is still being delivered, installed and commissioned. The real depreciation pressure hits in 2027.

Rough math: if 2026 capex is RMB 190 billion, depreciated on a 5-year weighted basis, incremental depreciation in 2027 is about RMB 38 billion. At a 25% tax rate, that drags net profit by about RMB 28.5 billion, or 8.9% of the 2027 Non-IFRS forecast of RMB 320 billion. That ratio is not small, but not fatal either.

The real risk is a change in the depreciation life. If auditors require GPUs to be depreciated over 3 years, given the pace of iteration, annual depreciation rises by another RMB 13 billion and the impact doubles to 17%. When the 2027 annual report lands, the depreciation policy note matters more than the profit number.

One-sentence summary of the three financial statements

Income statement: Revenue +11%, gross profit +13%, net profit +0.7% — gross profit is outgrowing revenue, while net profit is held back by non-operating items. Cash flow statement: Operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion, investing outflow RMB 59.3 billion, free cash flow -RMB 13.8 billion — every dollar earned was spent back out. Balance sheet: Net cash RMB 58.19 billion, investment portfolio USD 72 billion — still holding up, but thinning.

Taken together, the three statements tell a clear story: a profitable company that plowed this quarter's earnings, plus a slice of its existing cash, entirely into compute. This is not a crisis; it is a choice. A choice can be validated, and it can be overturned.

Conclusion

The financial reality: of the five numbers, three extend old trends and two are new information. The market de-rated the stock on the new information and ignored the old trends.
