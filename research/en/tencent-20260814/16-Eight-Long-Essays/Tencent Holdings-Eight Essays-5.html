05 Industry and Competition: Where the Four Tracks Really Stand — Tencent Holdings

Advertising is gaining share, gaming is at peak, cloud is raising prices, and the large-model space remains a free-for-all
Research date 2026/08/14  ·  Price HK$440.00 (-0.23%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 4.01 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 15.0x

Core view

Tencent spans four tracks, each in a very different state: advertising is taking share (+22% vs. industry single digits), gaming is at a cyclical peak (+17%), cloud is seeing both volume and price rise (low-twenties percent), and large models are in a melee (second tier).

Our view: the industry as a whole is not growing fast, but concentration is rising. That is good for the leader, not for the valuation — because the market only pays a premium for growth.

Advertising: triple-industry growth is unsustainable, but will persist for a long time

Marketing services +22% versus single-digit to low-double-digit industry growth. The near-twofold gap reflects share shift, not an industry tailwind — short-video budgets diverted from Douyin, new inventory from WeChat Stores and short dramas, and AI lifting conversion rates.

How long can it last? That hinges on remaining headroom in Video Accounts' ad load. Tencent has consistently been more restrained than peers, implying two to three more years of upside. But +22% itself is unsustainable: once ad load is maxed out, growth reverts to 12%–15%.

My valuation therefore assumes 20% advertising growth over two years, then 12% thereafter, rather than linearly extrapolating 22%. This is where sell-side estimates are most prone to overstating.

Gaming: Four simultaneous hits is luck, and also capability

Domestic RMB 47.3 billion, +17%, with four titles live at once: Delta Force (peak DAU 50 million), Valorant, Roco Kingdom: World and Peacekeeper Elite (Q1 peak DAU 90 million). Four simultaneous breakouts are a low-probability outcome; delivering them points to a mature operating system.

The 2027 base, however, will look ugly. Tencent's answer is the pipeline: Out of Control Evolution, launching in July with 40 million pre-registrations, has topped the iOS free chart, while internationally it leans on Miniclip's casual category (Arrows–PuzzleEscape is the global download champion).

On competition, NetEase is stronger in MMORPGs and anime-style games and miHoYo is stronger in open world, but neither matches Tencent's distribution network. Gaming's moat is channels plus operations, not creativity.

Cloud: first volume-and-price rise in three years

Cloud revenue growth accelerated from "high teens" in Q1 to "low twenties" in Q2, with public pricing raised and discounts cut in May. This marks the first "volume and price both rising" in three years.

Drivers: surging AI inference demand has turned compute into a seller's market; international expansion; recovering usage of general-purpose cloud. The first matters most—it pulls cloud from a "price war" back to "supply and demand."

On share, however, Tencent remains in the second tier. Alibaba Cloud leads with a clear share advantage, Huawei Cloud is strong in government and enterprise, and Tencent Cloud's differentiation lies in audio-video, gaming and social ecosystems. That supports a base of high-stickiness customers, but it cannot win the largest general-purpose compute orders.

Large models: Hunyuan's approach is "good enough + cheap"

Hy3: 295B total parameters, 21B activated, 256K context, API RMB 0.25 per million tokens. Read across: flagship-class capability on fewer activated parameters, winning call volume on price.

The rivals on this path are DeepSeek, also competing on cost-effectiveness, and Doubao, riding ByteDance's traffic. Tencent's edge is not the model but the scenarios: task resolution rate rose from 72% to 90% after WorkBuddy integration, and June PC visits reached 20.97 million, ranking first in China.

Hy4 is in training; management guides to launch by year-end and targets the top tier. This is the last event in 2026 that can change the narrative. Success re-rates the valuation; failure leaves 13x a fair price.

The most dangerous rival: ByteDance's two blades

The first blade is time spent: Douyin and WeChat Channels are competing for the same user hours. WeChat Channels' time spent +20% shows it has held its ground, but this is a defensive battle — high cost, low return.

The second blade is the entry point: Doubao is positioning itself as "the AI assistant as the entry point." If users grow accustomed to asking an assistant questions rather than opening an app, WeChat's value as an entry point gets bypassed. That is a structural threat, far more serious than the fight over time spent.

Tencent's answer is Xiaowei — embedding AI inside WeChat so that WeChat remains the entry point. The product is in gray-box testing, and management says users must confirm manually for safety reasons. Its success or failure will determine whether Tencent is "the entry point of the AI era" or "the address book of the AI era."

Endgame scenarios: three possibilities

1) Platform stays strong (45% probability): AI merely leverages the existing business, ad load maxes out, cloud grows steadily and Tencent holds a 15–18x valuation. 2) Piped (25% probability): AI assistants bypass the entry point, WeChat degrades into a messaging tool, ad growth drops to single digits and the valuation falls to 10–12x.

3) Second growth curve (30% probability): Mini programs prove out, WorkBuddy charges fees, cloud takes off on AI, Tencent becomes a growth stock again and the valuation exceeds 20x. The second is the only loss-making scenario, at 25% probability — which is why we bought only half.

Conclusion

Industry and competition: of the four tracks, three favour Tencent; large models are its weak spot. That weakness is not fatal, however, because Tencent's moat lies not in models but in scenarios.
