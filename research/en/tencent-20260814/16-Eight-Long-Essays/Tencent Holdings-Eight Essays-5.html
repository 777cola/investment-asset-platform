05 Industry and Competition: The Real Position of Four Tracks — Tencent Holdings

Advertising is taking share, gaming is at the peak, cloud is raising prices, and the large-model space is a free-for-all
Research date 2026/08/14  ·  Price HK$440.00 (-0.23%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 4.01 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 15.0x

Core view

Tencent spans four tracks, each in a completely different state: advertising is taking share (+22% vs. industry single digits), gaming is at a cyclical peak (+17%), cloud is seeing both volume and price rise (low-twenties percent), and large models are in a melee (second tier).

Judgement: the industry as a whole is not growing fast, but concentration is rising. Good for the leader, not for the valuation — because the market only pays a premium for growth.

Advertising: triple industry growth is unsustainable, but will stay that way for a long time

Marketing services +22%, versus industry growth of single digits to low double digits. The near-twofold gap comes from share shift, not industry tailwind. Sources of the shift: short-video budgets diverted from Douyin, WeChat Stores and short dramas providing new inventory, and AI lifting conversion rates.

How long can it last? It depends on how much headroom remains in Video Accounts' ad load. Tencent has consistently been more restrained than peers, which implies two to three more years of upside. But the +22% figure itself is unsustainable—once ad load is maxed out, growth will revert to 12%–15%.

So in my valuation I assume 20% two-year growth for advertising, then 12% thereafter, rather than linearly extrapolating 22%. This is where sell-side estimates are most prone to overstating.

Gaming: Four simultaneous hits is luck, and also capability

Domestic RMB 47.3 billion, +17%, with four titles live simultaneously: Delta Force (peak DAU 50 million), Valorant, Roco Kingdom: World, and Peacekeeper Elite (Q1 peak DAU 90 million). The odds of four titles breaking out at once are low; pulling it off shows a mature operating system.

But the 2027 base will look ugly. Tencent's answer is the pipeline: Out of Control Evolution, launching in July with 40 million pre-registrations, has topped the iOS free chart; internationally it leans on Miniclip's casual category (Arrows–PuzzleEscape is the global download champion).

On competition, NetEase is stronger in MMORPGs and anime-style games, and miHoYo is stronger in open world, but no one has Tencent's distribution network. Gaming's moat is channels plus operations, not creativity.

Cloud: first volume-and-price rise in three years

Cloud revenue growth accelerated from "high teens" in Q1 to "low twenties" in Q2, with public pricing raised and discounts cut in May. This is the first "volume and price both rising" in three years.

Drivers: AI inference demand surging, turning compute into a seller's market; international business expansion; recovering usage of general-purpose cloud. The first of the three matters most—it pulls cloud from a "price war" back to "supply and demand."

But on share, Tencent remains in the second tier. Alibaba Cloud is first (with a clear share lead), Huawei Cloud is strong in government and enterprise, and Tencent Cloud's differentiation lies in audio-video, gaming and social ecosystems. This means it can hold a base of high-stickiness customers, but cannot win the largest general-purpose compute orders.

Large models: Hunyuan's approach is "good enough + cheap"

Hy3: 295B total parameters, 21B activated, 256K context, API RMB 0.25 per million tokens. What these specs mean: flagship-class capability with fewer activated parameters, winning call volume on low price.

The rivals on this path are DeepSeek (also competing on cost-effectiveness) and Doubao (riding ByteDance's traffic). Tencent's edge is not the model but the scenarios: after WorkBuddy integration, task resolution rate rose from 72% to 90%, and June PC visits reached 20.97 million, ranking first in China.

Hy4 is in training; management says launch by year-end, targeting the top tier. This is the last event in 2026 that can change the narrative. If it succeeds, the valuation re-rates; if not, 13x is a fair price.

The most dangerous rival: ByteDance's two blades

The first blade is time spent: Douyin and WeChat Channels are fighting for the same users' time. WeChat Channels' time spent +20% shows it has held its ground, but this is a defensive battle — high cost, low return.

The second blade is the entry point: Doubao is building "the AI assistant as the entry point." If users grow accustomed to asking an assistant questions rather than opening an app, WeChat's value as an entry point will be bypassed. This is a structural threat, far more serious than the fight over time spent.

Tencent's response is Xiaowei — embedding AI inside WeChat so that WeChat remains the entry point. It is currently in gray-box testing, and management says users must confirm manually for safety reasons. The success or failure of this product will determine whether Tencent is "the entry point of the AI era" or "the address book of the AI era."

Endgame scenarios: three possibilities

1) Platform stays strong (45% probability): AI merely leverages the existing business, ad load maxes out, cloud grows steadily, and Tencent holds a 15–18x valuation. 2) Piped (25% probability): AI assistants bypass the entry point, WeChat degrades into a messaging tool, ad growth drops to single digits, and the valuation falls to 10–12x.

3) Second growth curve (30% probability): Mini programs prove out, WorkBuddy charges fees, cloud takes off on AI, Tencent becomes a growth stock again, and the valuation exceeds 20x. Of the three, the second is the only loss-making scenario, at 25% probability — which is why we bought only half.

Conclusion

Industry and competition conclusions: Of the four tracks, three favor Tencent; one (large models) is its weak spot. But the weak spot is not fatal—because its moat lies not in models but in scenarios.
