02 Business essence: four segments, four economic profiles — Tencent Holdings

Why revenue of the same size can differ threefold in quality
Research date 2026/08/14  ·  Price HK$440.00 (-0.23%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 4.01 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 15.0x

Core view

Tencent reports four segments, but economically these are four distinct business models: advertising is an "auction house" (near-zero marginal cost), gaming a "content factory" (front-loaded costs, cyclical), payments a "toll booth" (strong scale effects, heavy regulation) and cloud a "landlord" (asset-heavy, a utilisation game).

Returns on capital differ threefold across the four. Grasp that and it becomes clear why advertising growth is worth more than gaming growth, and why a recovery in cloud growth cannot re-rate the whole valuation.

Advertising: the right way to use a money printer

Marketing services RMB 43.57 billion, +22% YoY, segment gross margin 57%. This is essentially an auction business: every impression goes to the highest bidder, and the platform's only job is matching people with ads. The marginal cost of serving one more ad is close to zero.

Almost all ad revenue growth therefore drops through to gross profit. Assume a higher ad load delivers RMB 10 billion of incremental ad revenue; at a 57% gross margin and 25% tax rate, net profit rises about RMB 4.3 billion — the most efficient "revenue-to-profit" converter among the four businesses.

That is why it deserves the highest valuation: costs (bandwidth, servers) are fixed on one side, auction-based demand sits on the other, and algorithm efficiency — which improves every year — sets the spread between them.

Gaming: a good business, but not a stable one

Total game revenue RMB 65.9 billion, +11% YoY, with domestic up 17% to RMB 47.3 billion. This is at heart a content factory: you pay for the product up front with no idea whether it will work; if it does, you earn for three years, and if it doesn't, the entire cost is expensed.

Four products running simultaneously this quarter is a rare combination: Delta Force (peak DAU 50 million, already on the evergreen list), Valorant, Roco Kingdom: World (top of both DAU and grossing among new games in the first half) and Peacekeeper Elite (Q1 peak DAU 90 million). Four hits at once diversifies product-cycle risk, but it also raises the 2027 base.

Game revenue should therefore be treated as a cyclical peak. That doesn't change the fact that it's a good business (high gross margin, strong cash flow), but it does affect the valuation multiple — 15x for gaming and 25x for advertising is more reasonable than 20x for the whole company.

Payments: a true toll road, but the ceiling is clear

Fintech and business services: RMB 60.286 billion, +9%, segment gross margin 52%. Payments is the most stable piece: a small cut of every transaction, the bigger the scale the more it earns, and it needs almost no new investment.

Its ceiling, however, is clear: it tracks total retail sales of consumer goods. China's consumption growth is running at around 5% and payment fee rates are constrained by regulation, so long-term growth here is "single digit plus minor share shifts."

The value is stability, not growth. This cash flow is the base that lets Tencent invest for the long term—advertising and gaming fluctuate, payments do not. Only with RMB 60 billion a quarter of stable revenue in the portfolio does it dare to spend RMB 52.784 billion in a single quarter.

Cloud and AI: a landlord business where utilisation is what matters

Cloud is at heart a landlord business: capex upfront on data centres and cards, then rent out by usage. Gross profit is rental income less depreciation and electricity. The key metric is utilisation — machines depreciate whether or not they are busy.

Tencent Cloud's growth this quarter slowed from "high teens" to "low twenties", and in May the company raised public pricing and cut discounts. Two signals: demand is picking up (AI inference) and supply is tight (compute). For the landlord, this is the best moment — rents rising, occupancy high.

The ceiling sits in the same place. Once compute supply overshoots — domestic cards ramp, capacity releases — rents fall while depreciation does not. Cloud revenue growth can therefore be viewed optimistically, but cloud capex cannot be extrapolated. This is the most counterintuitive part of the business.

Unit economics: where the RMB 142 comes from

RMB 204.785 billion ÷ RMB 1.439 billion = RMB 142 per user per quarter. By segment: advertising RMB 30, gaming c.RMB 46, payments and cloud c.RMB 42, social networks RMB 23. The four sum to RMB 141, reconciling with the total.

For context, China's quarterly per capita disposable income is roughly RMB 11,000 (RMB 1.1 ten-thousand), of which Tencent captures 1.3% per user. Meta's quarterly revenue per user is more than three times Tencent's; the gap reflects ad pricing and payment habits, not user count.

That gap is the runway. If the load rate rises to half the peer level, ad revenue per user climbs from RMB 30 to RMB 45, adding RMB 86 billion of revenue and RMB 37 billion of net profit a year — equivalent to 13% of 2026 Non-IFRS net profit. This is Tencent's most certain growth path.

What the four businesses are worth combined

Rough sum-of-the-parts (2026E): advertising 435.65×4 ≈ RMB 174.0 billion revenue at a 57% gross margin and 25x, or about RMB 1.5 trillion; gaming 659×4 ≈ RMB 264.0 billion at 15x, or about RMB 1.6 trillion; fintech and business services 602.86×4 ≈ RMB 241.0 billion at 12x, or about RMB 1.2 trillion. The three sum to about RMB 4.3 trillion, or roughly HKD 520 per share.

The point of this rough math is cross-validation rather than precision: HKD 535 derived from 13x P/E on the whole and HKD 520 from the parts are of the same order of magnitude. That shows the neutral target of 535 is not plucked from thin air.

Cloud and AI get no separate multiple in this method — they are folded into the low multiple on fintech and business services. That is what "assigning no AI valuation" looks like in practice.

Conclusion

Business mix: Tencent is four businesses in one—advertising carries the most value, gaming the fattest margins, payments the most stability and cloud the heaviest capital load. Each must be valued separately; a sum-of-the-parts shortcut that lumps them together overvalues cloud and undervalues advertising at the same time.
