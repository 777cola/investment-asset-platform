Quality Screen — Tencent Holdings

Core thesis

Five of seven hard metrics pass. The two that fail are "profit growth" and "operating cash flow/net profit", and both point to the same place: the company earns plenty, but keeps less of it. This is not the business deteriorating; it is cash conversion being clipped by capex in 2026.

Of the five that pass, gross margin and ROE reflect real capability, net cash and shareholder returns reflect the balance-sheet foundation, and revenue growth reflects the present. None of the seven is a "structural failure", so this company should not be screened out by the deterioration filter — its problem is valuation and timing, not quality.

Of the five criteria met, not all carry equal weight

A 57.8% gross margin and ROE of about 23% underpin this business; only two or three companies in Chinese internet satisfy both at once. Gross margin also rose 1 point YoY, showing pricing power remains intact. High ROE stems from an asset-light model plus large buybacks shrinking the share count — both shareholder-friendly structures.

Net cash of RMB 58.19 billion is merely "passable", not "very good": it stood at RMB 146.86 billion a quarter earlier, down RMB 88.7 billion, mainly on RMB 59.3 billion of capex payments and RMB 41.6 billion of dividend payments. The base is still there, but watch the direction of the decline. Revenue +11% is the most solid number this quarter — Bloomberg consensus was RMB 202.8 billion against actual RMB 204.79 billion, a 1% beat.

The two that fall short are really the same thing.

Operating cash flow/net profit of 0.94 just misses; net profit attributable to shareholders +0.7% falls far short. Taken together, the picture is clear: the income statement was flattened by non-operating items, and cash flow was cut off by capex. Net profit attributable to shareholders rose only 0.7% against 9% growth in Non-IFRS, and the RMB 12.4 billion gap came from fair value changes and impairments on the investment portfolio. Operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion fell below net profit because part of revenue had not yet converted into cash.

Viewed separately, the two would point to "earnings quality has deteriorated"; viewed together, the conclusion should be revised to "earnings are not broken, cash was actively invested out the door." The difference matters: the former is a capability problem, the latter a choice problem. Choices can change; capabilities cannot.

But is it fatal? Not fatal, but it warrants a discount.

Not fatal. Three criteria. First, free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion turns to +RMB 37.6 billion excluding compute procurement prepayments — a positive number means operating cash generation is intact. Second, non-IFRS operating profit of RMB 75.64 billion, up 9% YoY, becomes RMB 86.1 billion, up 19%, after stripping out revenue and expenses from new AI products, so core-business profit is growing twice as fast as new-business losses are widening. Third, the company did not borrow to invest in compute, and net cash of RMB 58.191 billion, though thin, remains positive.

A discount is warranted — not from withholding a P/E multiple but from withholding the "cash-cow premium." For the same company, when FCF runs at RMB 100 billion year after year the market will pay 20x; when FCF turns negative it will pay only 13x. That is the entire content of the move from 677.5 to 440. The de-rating has already happened, so failing these two of the seven criteria is not a reason to sell.

One metric beyond the seven is often overlooked: capex/operating cash flow

Capex payments this quarter were RMB 59.3 billion against operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion, a ratio of 1.12 — capex has now exceeded operating cash flow. That metric is not one of the seven, but it explains the share-price decline better than any of them.

Historically the ratio held at 0.15–0.25 over the long run, and for full-year 2025 it was around 0.3. A jump to 1.12 means the company must draw on existing cash — net cash fell from RMB 146.86 billion to RMB 58.19 billion — or sell investments. Management chose the former plus "no sale of core assets," which keeps the balance sheet respectable but turns "how long can buybacks continue" into a real question.

Will those two that fell short spread?

Yes. The chain runs like this: free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion → reduced buyback capacity → the shareholder return criterion falls from 2.3% to below 1.5% → one more of the seven criteria fails. So these two are not isolated; they are two readings of the same variable.

The reverse chain also holds: if Q3 capex falls back to RMB 35 billion and operating cash flow returns above RMB 70 billion, free cash flow turns positive immediately, one of the two criteria repairs itself, and the shareholder return criterion stabilizes along with it. That is why I classify this as a "choice problem" rather than a "capability problem" — it will fix itself, as long as management is willing.

What remains after the bad is stripped out

After seven screens, the picture on Tencent: a 57.8% gross margin, ROE of about 23%, revenue still growing 11%, and nearly RMB 200 billion of annual cash flowing into a business that cannot yet be measured separately. Not cheap enough to buy with eyes closed, but not bad enough to be screened out.

This is negative screening used correctly: it answers only "should I keep looking," not "should I buy." The answer is to keep looking, because none of the seven screens flags structural deterioration — which looks like gross margin falling three years running, ROE propped up by leverage, revenue padded by consolidation. Tencent has none of these.

Conclusion

Five of the seven criteria pass, and the two failures share a root cause: profit flattened by non-operating items, cash cut off by capex. The negative screen concludes: do not eliminate — gross margin, ROE, net cash and revenue growth, the four hard tests, all hold, which points to a first-rate company facing a cyclical problem rather than a second-rate company facing a valuation problem.

But "do not eliminate" does not equal "buy now." The 1.12 ratio beyond the seven criteria is a reminder: this company is using existing cash to buy an AI ticket. Anyone buying it must accept one thing — over the next four quarters, free cash flow will most likely be negative.
