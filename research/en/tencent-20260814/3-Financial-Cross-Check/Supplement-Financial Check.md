## The only major deviation: the two denominators for operating profit

Non-IFRS operating profit was RMB 75.64 billion, up 9% YoY; excluding revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products, it was RMB 86.1 billion, up 19% YoY. The RMB 10.46 billion gap between the two is the net drag from this quarter's new AI businesses (Hunyuan, Yuanbao, WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Xiaowei and others).

That gap is the most informative number in the entire report. It shows two things: core business profit growth of 19% is twice the headline 9%; and the AI business lost roughly RMB 10.5 billion in the quarter, an annualized pace above RMB 40 billion. Any approach that uses RMB 86.1 billion to calculate PE is wrong (that is the figure after stripping out losses), and any claim that "Tencent's profit has stalled" is also wrong (the stall occurs only in the new businesses).

## Free cash flow: what explains the gap between -138 and +376

Reported basis: operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion less capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion less other items (including leases) of roughly RMB 13.8 billion, giving about -RMB 13.8 billion. Management's adjusted basis adds back prepayments for computing power purchases to arrive at +RMB 37.6 billion. The RMB 51.4 billion gap is the portion that is "paid for but not yet received": prepayments for GPUs and servers show up as investing cash outflows in the cash flow statement, but economically they behave like an inventory expense.

Our treatment: use -RMB 13.8 billion throughout the body and tables, since that is the cash investors can actually access, while acknowledging +RMB 37.6 billion in the judgment, since it shows cash-generation capacity remains intact. Taking -RMB 13.8 billion as the norm would severely undervalue the company; taking +RMB 37.6 billion as the norm would overstate its financial flexibility. Both must be stated to avoid misleading readers.

## Market cap and share capital: reconciling the 9.103 billion shares

HKD 440.00 × 9.103 billion shares = HKD 4.005 trillion, consistent with both Hong Kong media figures of "HKD 4 trillion" and "about RMB 3.4 trillion" (RMB 3.68 trillion at a 0.92 conversion). The 9.103 billion share count is net of treasury shares held from buybacks: 50.03 million shares were repurchased in 1H26 and HKD 80 billion worth over full-year 2025, so the count keeps shrinking.

One easy mistake: calculating P/E on the share count at the start of the year overstates it by about 1%. Tencent also has ADRs, but they represent the same total share count, so no A/H conversion is needed — a relatively hassle-free structure among Hong Kong stocks.

## Three things the company no longer discloses

AI revenue: Discussed only qualitatively on the earnings call ("cloud revenue growth accelerated from high teens to low twenties") and not broken out in the financials, so AI spending is visible (RMB 52.784 billion) while the output is not. Channels advertising revenue: No longer disclosed separately after 2023; can only be inferred from marketing services revenue of RMB 43.565 billion. Cloud segment profit: Reported together with fintech and business services at a 52% gross margin, yet cloud and payments carry vastly different margins, so bundling them obscures whether cloud is profitable.

All three undisclosed items are choices that work against the bulls: the market cannot verify AI returns, so it treats the spend as an expense. This is not accounting fraud but a disclosure strategy, and the valuation impact is real—less information means a larger discount.

## A sensitivity test I ran: depreciation

On a full-year 2026 capex assumption of RMB 190 billion (RMB 84.72 billion in 1H, with 2H slightly higher), servers depreciated over 4 years and IDC over 10 years, the weighted depreciation life is roughly 5 years → annual depreciation rises by about RMB 38 billion, half of which hits in 2027. At a 25% tax rate, the hit to 2027 net profit is about RMB 14 billion, or 4.4% of our 2027 Non-IFRS net profit forecast of RMB 320 billion.

That is manageable, but only on one precondition: the depreciation life must hold. Should auditors require 3-year depreciation (given rapid GPU iteration), annual depreciation rises by about RMB 13 billion and the impact doubles to 9%. On the 2027 financials, therefore, changes in depreciation policy warrant closer attention than the absolute level of capex.

## Why I don't use sell-side forecasts directly

Sell-side forecasts for Tencent's 2026 Non-IFRS net profit span RMB 275 billion to RMB 295 billion, a RMB 20 billion spread. The disagreement sits almost entirely in depreciation assumptions and the treatment of AI losses, not on revenue—revenue estimates cluster at +10% to +12%.

I use my own annualised figure of RMB 280 billion: first-half RMB 136.32 billion × 2, marked up 3% for a slightly stronger second half (peak game season, Double 11 advertising). That puts me at the midpoint of the sell-side range; the advantage is that I can re-check every step myself, the disadvantage is no alpha. On a judgment with 1.4:1 odds, a 5% difference in the denominator does not change the conclusion, so extra precision is not worth the time.
