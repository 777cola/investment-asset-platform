Financial Check — Tencent Holdings

Core view

Eight key data points were cross-checked against two sources: seven deviate by less than 0.3%, and only one is off by 12.2% — Non-IFRS operating profit of RMB 75.64 billion vs the "operating profit of RMB 86.1 billion" cited by media. This is not a calculation error by either side; it is two different scopes: the RMB 86.1 billion figure excludes revenue, costs and expenses from new AI products.

What genuinely requires independent work is free cash flow: the disclosed figure is -RMB 13.8 billion, while management's adjusted figure is +RMB 37.6 billion; the RMB 51.4 billion gap is prepayments for computing power procurement. Reports can only use -RMB 13.8 billion, but any judgment must account for the existence of +RMB 37.6 billion.

The only major deviation: the two denominators for operating profit

RMB 75.64 billion is Non-IFRS operating profit, up 9% YoY; RMB 86.1 billion is Non-IFRS operating profit "after excluding revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products," up 19% YoY. The RMB 10.46 billion gap between the two is the net drag from this quarter's new AI businesses (Hunyuan, Yuanbao, WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Xiaowei and others).

This gap is the most informative number in the entire report. It shows two things: core business profit growth of 19% is twice the headline 9%; and the AI business lost roughly RMB 10.5 billion in the quarter, an annualized pace above RMB 40 billion. Any approach that uses RMB 86.1 billion to calculate PE is wrong (that is the figure after stripping out losses), and any claim that "Tencent's profit has stalled" is also wrong (the stall occurs only in the new businesses).

Free cash flow: what explains the gap between -138 and +376

Reported basis: operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion − capital expenditure payments RMB 59.3 billion − other (including leases) ≈ -RMB 13.8 billion. Management's adjusted basis: add back prepayments for computing power purchases to get +RMB 37.6 billion. The RMB 51.4 billion gap is the portion that is "paid for but not yet received"—prepayments for GPUs and servers appear as investing cash outflows in the cash flow statement, but economically they are an inventory-like expense.

My treatment: use -RMB 13.8 billion throughout the body and tables, because that is the cash investors can actually access; acknowledge +RMB 37.6 billion in the judgment, because it shows the "cash-generation capacity" is intact. Treating -RMB 13.8 billion as the norm would severely undervalue this company; treating +RMB 37.6 billion as the norm would overstate its financial flexibility. Both must be stated to avoid misleading.

Market cap and share capital: how the 9.103 billion shares reconcile

HKD 440.00 × 9.103 billion shares = HKD 4.005 trillion, which matches both the Hong Kong media figures of "HKD 4 trillion" and "about RMB 3.4 trillion" (RMB 3.68 trillion at a 0.92 conversion). The 9.103 billion shares figure is net of treasury shares held from buybacks — 50.03 million shares were repurchased in the first half of 2026, and HKD 80 billion worth were repurchased in full-year 2025, so the share count keeps shrinking.

One easy mistake here: calculating PE on the share count at the start of the year overstates it by about 1%. Also, Tencent has ADRs but the total share count is the same set, so no A/H conversion is needed — a relatively hassle-free structure among Hong Kong stocks.

Three things the company no longer discloses

AI revenue: Described only qualitatively on the earnings call ("cloud revenue growth accelerated from high teens to low twenties"), not broken out in the financials. This means AI spending is visible (RMB 52.784 billion) while the output is not. Channels advertising revenue: No longer disclosed separately after 2023; can only be inferred from marketing services revenue of RMB 43.565 billion. Cloud segment profit: Reported together with fintech and business services at a 52% gross margin, but cloud and payments have vastly different margins, so bundling them obscures whether cloud is profitable.

All three undisclosed items are choices that work against the bulls—the market cannot verify AI returns, so it treats the spend as an expense. This is not accounting fraud but a disclosure strategy; the valuation impact is real: less information means a larger discount.

A sensitivity test I ran: depreciation

Assuming full-year 2026 capex of RMB 190 billion (RMB 84.72 billion in the first half, with the second half estimated slightly higher), servers depreciated over 4 years and IDC over 10 years, the weighted depreciation life is about 5 years → annual depreciation rises by about RMB 38 billion, half of which falls in 2027. At a 25% tax rate, the impact on 2027 net profit is about RMB 14 billion, or 4.4% of the 2027 Non-IFRS net profit forecast of RMB 320 billion.

That magnitude is manageable, but with one precondition: the depreciation life cannot change. If auditors require 3-year depreciation (rapid GPU iteration), annual depreciation rises by about RMB 13 billion, doubling the impact to 9%. So when looking at the 2027 financials, changes in depreciation policy deserve closer attention than the absolute level of capex.

Why I don't use sell-side forecasts directly

Market forecasts for Tencent's 2026 Non-IFRS net profit range from RMB 275 billion to RMB 295 billion, a spread of RMB 20 billion. The disagreement lies almost entirely in depreciation assumptions and the treatment of AI losses, not on the revenue side—revenue estimates are broadly similar (+10% to +12%).

I use my own annualized figure of RMB 280 billion: first-half RMB 136.32 billion × 2, then marked up 3% for a slightly stronger second half (peak game season, Double 11 advertising). This sits at the midpoint of the sell-side range; the advantage is that I can re-check every step myself, the disadvantage is no alpha. For a judgment with 1.4:1 odds, a 5% difference in the denominator does not change the conclusion, so it is not worth spending extra time on precision.

Conclusion

Cross-check conclusion: this report has no conflicting numbers, only a choice of basis. Seven of the eight items agree; the sole 12.2% discrepancy (RMB 75.64 billion vs. RMB 86.1 billion) is the company's own "ex-AI" perspective — information if used correctly, a trap if not.

Final basis adopted: revenue/gross profit/net profit use reported figures; operating profit uses RMB 75.64 billion with a note on the RMB 86.1 billion ex-AI basis; free cash flow uses -RMB 13.8 billion with a note on the restated +RMB 37.6 billion; market cap HKD 4.01 trillion, share count 9.103 billion shares.
