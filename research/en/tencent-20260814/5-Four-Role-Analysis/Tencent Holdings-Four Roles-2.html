Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Tencent Holdings

Look at gross margin, cash flow and balance sheet, one by one

Core view

Start with gross profit: RMB 118.43 billion, a 57.8% gross margin, up 1 point YoY. Expanding gross margin in a year when capex doubled is pricing power. Now cash: operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion, capex payments RMB 59.3 billion, free cash flow negative RMB 13.8 billion. The income statement looks good and the cash flow statement looks bad, but the two are two sides of the same coin.

Judgement: financial quality has not deteriorated; the priority for allocating cash has changed — from returning it to shareholders to investing it in the future.

Gross profit is expanding, but so are expenses, leaving net profit caught in between

Gross profit of RMB 118.43 billion rose 13%, ahead of revenue growth of 11%, lifting gross margin from 56.8% to 57.8%. That is the clearest evidence of the quality of the business: advertising and mini-game revenue sharing have displaced low-margin livestream tipping, and the mix is shifting upward on its own.

Two large gaps open up on the expense side: G&A of RMB 38.81 billion, +22%, which management attributes entirely to large-model iteration and an AI overhaul across the product line; and R&D of RMB 27.28 billion, +35%. Together the two grew more than twice as fast as revenue, absorbing most of the gross profit gain.

The upshot: Non-IFRS operating profit of RMB 75.64 billion, +9%, and Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 68.42 billion, +8.5%. Net profit attributable to shareholders rose just 0.7% to RMB 56.02 billion, with the RMB 12.4 billion gap stemming from fair value changes and impairments on the investment portfolio—an accounting rather than an operating issue.

Cash flow: one foot on the gas, one on the brake

Operating cash flow came in at RMB 52.71 billion, RMB 21.6 billion below the RMB 74.376 billion reported in the same period last year and RMB 48.6 billion below the RMB 101.351 billion of the prior quarter. A 30% drop in single-quarter operating cash flow is more alarming than capex—it could reflect collections, advance receipts or earnings quality.

Capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion exceeded operating cash flow. Add the 2025 dividend of RMB 41.6 billion and free cash flow was negative RMB 13.8 billion. Management offers a reconciled basis: excluding prepayments for computing power procurement, free cash flow is positive RMB 37.6 billion. Both numbers are true; it depends on how you define "free."

Buffett's view would be: shareholder free cash flow is negative RMB 13.8 billion—that is a fact; management's positive RMB 37.6 billion is a "what if," not a fact. Buyers of the stock get the former.

Balance sheet: net cash of RMB 58.19 billion, down RMB 88.7 billion in a single quarter

Net cash stood at RMB 58.19 billion at end-June, down from RMB 146.86 billion at end-March — a RMB 88.7 billion decline in a single quarter. Management gives the reason plainly: RMB 59.3 billion of capex payments plus RMB 41.6 billion of dividends, which reconcile exactly. Tencent did not borrow to invest in compute; it drew down its own cash pile.

A second large asset sits off the balance sheet: a listed investment portfolio worth about USD 72 billion (roughly RMB 510 billion at 7.2), equal to 14% of market cap — a sellable reserve tank. Management was explicit that it will not raise equity and will fund AI investment from operating cash flow plus this portfolio. The weight of that statement is that it turns asset sales from an option into a commitment.

Capital returns: average buyback price of HKD 487.7 — buying on its own conviction

In 1H26 it repurchased 50.03 million shares for HKD 24.4 billion at an average price of HKD 487.7; for full-year 2025 it repurchased HKD 80 billion; and in the 21 consecutive trading days before the results announcement it bought back about HKD 4.1 billion in total. Management kept buying its own stock in the HKD 440–490 range—the most honest statement on valuation.

Do the math: on 2026E Non-IFRS of RMB 280 billion, annualised buybacks of HKD 46 billion equate to roughly a 1.5% reduction in share count; add dividends of RMB 41.6 billion and total shareholder return is about 3.4%. A 3.4% return at 13x P/E is itself a decent starting point for compounding—provided buybacks can continue, which depends on when free cash flow turns positive.

Conclusion

Financial perspective: the income statement is improving, the cash flow statement is deteriorating, and the balance sheet is still holding up. Taken together, the picture is of "a profitable company deliberately spending money," not "an unprofitable company barely hanging on."

The key question is why operating cash flow fell by 30%. If Q3 recovers to above RMB 70 billion, this quarter's -RMB 13.8 billion was a one-off timing issue; if it stays around RMB 50 billion for two consecutive quarters, collections or advance receipts are impaired, and the nature of the situation is entirely different.
