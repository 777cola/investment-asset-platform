Core view

Tencent's business model in one sentence: use WeChat to capture user time, then monetise that time through advertising and gaming. Of the RMB 204.785 billion in 2026Q2 revenue, marketing services at RMB 43.565 billion (+22%) is the healthiest block, gaming at RMB 65.9 billion (+11%) is the thickest cash-flow block, and fintech and business services at RMB 60.286 billion (+9%) offers the highest ceiling but carries the heaviest weight.

Verdict: a good business, but one shifting from "pure rent collection" to "rent collection plus heavy-asset investment." Duan Yongping would buy it, but he would watch for the point at which capex stops.

User value: 1.439 billion people come every day — for what?

Combined WeChat and Weixin MAU reached 1.439 billion, up 2% YoY. Growth has stalled, but that is not the point: time spent on Channels rose 20% YoY. Users are not leaving, and they are spending more time inside the same app — for a product already at saturation, that is worth far more than MAU growth.

Users come to WeChat for three things: finding people (contacts, group chats), paying (WeChat Pay, red packets) and getting things done (mini programs, official accounts, WeCom). The three reinforce each other: payment makes the app indispensable, and that indispensability lets mini programs build an ecosystem. This is a self-reinforcing chain, not a pile of features.

Monetisation mix: advertising accelerating, gaming recovering, payments underpinning

Marketing services RMB 43.565 billion, +22% YoY and +14% QoQ — the only one of the four segments to accelerate. The drivers are clear: full rollout of the AIM+ delivery system, recommendation models lifting conversion, new inventory from WeChat Stores, short dramas and mini games, and the 618 shopping festival.The key to this line is not demand but supply — ad load on Video Accounts has long trailed peers, and Tencent has been raising it with restraint.

Domestic games RMB 47.3 billion, +17%, with four titles contributing at once: Delta Force, Valorant, Roco Kingdom: World, plus the evergreen Game for Peace base. International games RMB 18.6 billion, -0.8% YoY but +4% in constant currency, as Miniclip's casual titles filled the gap. Social networks RMB 32.5 billion, up just 0.8% — a normal state for a mature business.

FinTech and Business Services RMB 60.286 billion, +9%, with cloud revenue growth accelerating from "high teens" in Q1 to "low twenties" in Q2, alongside a public price increase and reduced discounting in May.Cloud is showing the first signs of rising volumes and prices together — the second most important thing in this earnings report.

Unit economics: each WeChat user is worth RMB 142 per quarter

RMB 204.785 billion ÷ 1.439 billion = RMB 142 per user per quarter, or about RMB 570 annualised. Marketing services contribute RMB 30 per quarter (435.65 ÷ 14.39) and gaming about RMB 46. Against comparable global platforms, this is not high: Meta's quarterly revenue per user is more than three times that, a gap driven mainly by lower ad unit prices and payment habits in China.

Not high means room to grow. If WeChat Channels' ad load rose to half the peer level, marketing services revenue per user per quarter could climb from RMB 30 to RMB 45, adding about RMB 86 billion in annual revenue and, at a 57% gross margin and 25% tax rate, roughly RMB 37 billion in net profit. That is why ad growth is worth more than gaming growth: gaming depends on new products, whereas advertising only needs a looser hand.

Is AI one and the same business: partly yes, partly no

The "yes" half: AI recommendations lift ad conversion, AI tools cut game R&D costs (Delta Force uses Hy3D to build a unified PC-mobile pipeline), and AI customer service and agents make WeCom easier to sell. These add leverage to the existing business, and the returns show up directly in gross margin.

The "no" half: new AI products such as WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Yuanbao and Xiaowei build a separate revenue line, each requiring its own customer acquisition, its own pricing and its own compute depreciation. This quarter they dragged down operating profit by about RMB 10.5 billion (the gap between 756.4 and 861), or over RMB 40 billion annualized.

A Duan Yongping-style judgment: the part that adds leverage to the existing business is worth paying for; the part that builds a separate revenue line is only an option, not a valuation base. So I value Tencent at 13x core business earnings rather than treating AI as a second growth curve.

Conclusion

The business is easy to understand: a gateway monopoly, tiered monetisation and unit economics that still have room to run. The only thing that has deteriorated is capital intensity—the company has gone from an asset-light rent collector to spending nearly RMB 200 billion a year on compute. It is still a good business, but the degree of "good" is now discounted.

Commercially, the conclusion is this: advertising is the core variable, gaming the ballast, cloud the surprise upside and new AI products the option. If just one of the four—advertising—is accelerating, that is enough to support a 13x P/E.
