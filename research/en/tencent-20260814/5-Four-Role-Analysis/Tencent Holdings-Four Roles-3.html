China Internet: Advertising, Gaming, Cloud and AI — Four Tracks

Core view

Tencent sits at the intersection of four tracks: advertising (a zero-sum fight for existing demand, with share concentrating into Channels), gaming (existing demand plus overseas expansion), cloud (a second growth phase driven by AI) and large models (an investment phase, with the landscape still unsettled). The first two are cash cows; the latter two burn cash.

Our view: the industry is not growing fast, but concentration is rising—good for the leader. The real variable is AI compute supply and demand, which determines both cloud revenue and Tencent's costs.

Advertising: share shift in a mature market

China's online advertising sector has moved past its high-growth phase, with overall growth now running between single digits and low double digits. Tencent's marketing services, by contrast, grew 22%, three times the industry rate—evidence that it is taking share rather than riding industry tailwinds. The source of that share gain is clear: some short-video ad budgets are shifting from Douyin to Video Accounts, while WeChat Stores and short dramas add new inventory.

Munger would ask how long this share shift can last. The answer hinges on two things: how much more advertising Video Accounts can hold (ad load), and whether the WeChat ecosystem's transaction loop can truly get running (WeChat Store GMV). The former sits within Tencent's control; the latter depends on whether merchants are willing to treat private-domain traffic as their main battlefield.

Gaming: a mature domestic market makes overseas expansion the growth driver

Domestic games RMB 47.3 billion, +17%, driven by four titles live at once: Delta Force (peak DAU above 50 million, already on the evergreen list), Valorant, Roco Kingdom: World (top of both DAU and grossing among new games in the first half) and Peacekeeper Elite (peak DAU of 90 million in Q1). Four titles contributing at once is good news, but it also sets a very high base for 2027.

International games RMB 18.6 billion, +4% at constant currency, with Miniclip's Arrows–PuzzleEscape taking the global download crown. Overseas is the only market where Chinese game companies still post double-digit growth; Tencent's weak spot is modest acceptance of self-developed hardcore titles in Europe and the US, and its strength is the investment-plus-publishing combination.

A Munger-style reminder: content is always a cyclical business. Today's +17% sits near the top of the product cycle, not the norm. Extrapolating it into 2027 would be a mistake.

Cloud and computing power: the real variable this cycle

Cloud revenue growth accelerated from "high teens" in Q1 to "low twenties" in Q2, helped by public cloud price increases and discount cuts in May — the first "volume and price rising together" in three years. The reason is not complicated: AI inference demand has taken off, and compute has become a seller's market.

But the same coin has two sides. Tight compute lets cloud raise prices; it also forces Tencent itself to spend RMB 52.784 billion on cards and data centers. The industry shares a common worry: domestic compute capacity keeps coming online, and domestic hardware is being pushed to market at an accelerating pace. Once supply overtops demand, price wars will return, and today's price increases will have to be given back tomorrow.

Munger would like this judgment: compute is a bottleneck now, and may become a commodity in two years. So cloud revenue growth is worth celebrating; cloud capex is not worth extrapolating.

Cycle positioning: three cycles, different directions

Advertising cycle: neutral to slightly positive. 618 promotions lift volume and drive WeChat Channels inventory releases, but weak macro consumption keeps advertisers cautious on budgets, so this cycle will not be especially strong. Capex cycle: clearly upward. RMB 84.72 billion in the first half, +82% YoY, with Q2 alone at RMB 52.784 billion; no peak in sight yet.

Valuation cycle: clearly downward. The Hang Seng Tech Index has fallen more than 25% year to date in 2026, and Tencent has dropped from 677.5 to 440, -35%, as the market's main thread shifts from the "AI narrative" to "cash flow and shareholder returns." Tencent sits exactly at the intersection of these two lines: cash flow is deteriorating, and the narrative is receding.

Of the three cycles, two work against the share price and one works for the business. That is why the stock has fallen sharply despite decent results—the market is trading the cycle, not this quarter's profit.

Conclusion

Sector view: Across the four tracks, advertising is gaining share, gaming sits at a cyclical peak, cloud is seeing both volume and price rise, and large models remain a free-for-all. That mix is not bad for the leaders, but it cannot support a valuation above 20x.

Cycle call: capex up plus valuation down is the most painful combination. It will not reverse immediately; the only option is to wait for capex to peak — that is the starting point for a re-rating.
