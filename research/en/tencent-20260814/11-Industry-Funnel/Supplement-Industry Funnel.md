## First-round screen: can it be bought at all?

The first screen asks whether the business is understandable and whether it makes money. Five of the six pass: Tencent, Alibaba, NetEase, Kuaishou and Baidu are all profitable. Meituan's margins were squeezed thin in the local-services subsidy war, distorting its P/E, so it is screened out first — not because the company is bad, but because at this price it cannot be valued on P/E.

The five that remain share one trait: they own their traffic. That matters in 2026, with traffic costs rising and companies lacking proprietary traffic — pure agencies, pure e-commerce operators — being squeezed hardest.

## Round two: scoring on growth, gross margin and valuation

Tencent: +11% growth, 57.8% gross margin, 12.3x — clears all three. Alibaba: single-digit to low-double-digit growth, 40% gross margin, 12.5x — fails on growth and gross margin. NetEase: single-digit growth, 60% gross margin, 12x — fails on growth. Kuaishou: +1.4%, 51.6%, 10–13x — fails badly on growth. Baidu: single-digit growth, 50% gross margin, 13.3x — fails on growth; at 2.8x ex-cash, the market is pricing in disbelief in the core business.

Of the five, only Tencent delivers double-digit growth, and only Tencent posts a gross margin above 55%. That is no coincidence: the advertising + gaming + payments mix is the most recession-resistant revenue structure in Hong Kong-listed internet.

## Initial screen exclusions, explained one by one

Alibaba: As cheap as Tencent on valuation, but its growth engine is cloud, and cloud capex came earlier and heavier than at Tencent. Buying Alibaba means buying a "cloud re-rating" — higher payoff, lower certainty. NetEase: 60% gross margin is good, but revenue is growing in the single digits and the product line is thin (one old game contributes the bulk), making it a "high-dividend value stock," not a "growth-recovery stock."

Meituan: The subsidy war in local services is not over, and profit is unpredictable. Kuaishou: Revenue +1.4%, and the AI business (Kling) is too small a share to support the valuation. Baidu: A 2.8x P/E after deducting cash shows the market believes the core business is shrinking; it carries the greatest transformation uncertainty.

## Final pick: Why Tencent

Three reasons, in order of importance: 1) Growth does not hinge on a single product — advertising +22%, domestic games +17%, cloud up by a low-twenties percentage: three legs to stand on; 2) Gross margin is expanding — 57.8%, +1pct, evidence that pricing power holds; 3) Valuation sits at the low end of its historical range — 12.3x forward, 11.8x ex-cash, richer than NetEase (8x ex-cash), but with two more growth curves than NetEase.

Why not Alibaba? Because Alibaba's problem is "how long before cloud can support the valuation" — a question that takes 3 years to verify; Tencent's is "when does capex peak" — a question answered within 4 quarters. At the same odds, pick the one with the shorter verification period.

## A note beyond the funnel: why not buy the cheaper one

NetEase trades at 8x ex-cash and Baidu at 2.8x ex-cash, both cheaper than Tencent. But they are cheap because they have "no growth" — the 108x cheapness reflects the market pricing in "no future growth", not a mispricing.

Tencent's 13x cheapness reflects the market pricing in "future growth but negative cash this year". The two kinds of cheapness differ in nature: the former is the classic shape of a value trap, the latter the classic shape of a cyclical mismatch. This is the final layer of the funnel — what you buy is not the cheapest, but the one whose "reason for being cheap will disappear".

## One overlooked dimension: who delivers the highest shareholder returns

Tencent: HKD 80 billion of buybacks in 2025 plus RMB 41.6 billion of dividends, about RMB 115 billion in total, or 3.1% of market cap. Alibaba: buybacks are also large, but it is simultaneously committing heavy capital to cloud. NetEase: high payout ratio, but low growth. Meituan and Kuaishou: limited buybacks, cash reserved for competitive battles. Baidu: buybacks support valuation, but the core business is shrinking.

Tencent stands out by doing three things at once — buybacks, dividends and large-scale capex — the only one of the six to do so. That speaks to cash generation still stronger than peers: even with negative FCF this quarter, the full-year picture (Non-IFRS RMB 136.32 billion in the first half) still covers it.

## Boundaries of the funnel method

The funnel misses two types of company: 1) those not yet profitable but operating in an excellent sector (e.g., pure AI application companies), which have no P/E comparables; 2) those at the bottom of a cycle with temporarily distorted profits (e.g., Meituan today), where neither P/E nor gross margin reflects the norm.

The funnel's conclusion therefore answers only "which one to buy at this price level," not "which company is best." Tencent winning here does not make it best on every dimension—it trails ByteDance in AI model capability and Alibaba in cloud. It wins because all three metrics qualify and the reason it is cheap will disappear.
