Core thesis

Screen the six Hong Kong-listed internet leaders on one table: double-digit revenue growth, gross margin above 50%, forward PE within 15x — only Tencent meets all three at once. Alibaba is cheap but growth rests on cloud, NetEase has high gross margin but its terminal business is shrinking, Meituan is at war, Kuaishou is growing 1.4%, Baidu is transforming.

Judgement: this is not a conclusion that "Tencent is best", but that "at 13x, Tencent is the only company that does not need a story".

First-round screen: can it be bought at all?

The first screen is "is the business understandable + does it make money". Five of the six pass: Tencent, Alibaba, NetEase, Kuaishou and Baidu are all profitable; Meituan's margins were squeezed thin in the local-services subsidy war, distorting its PE, so it is screened out first — not because the company is bad, but because at this price it cannot be valued on PE.

The five that remain share one trait: they own their traffic. That matters in 2026: traffic costs are rising, and companies without proprietary traffic (pure agencies, pure e-commerce operators) are being squeezed hardest.

Round two: scoring on growth, gross margin and valuation

Tencent: +11%, 57.8%, 12.3x — passes all three. Alibaba: growth in the single digits to low double digits, gross margin 40%, 12.5x — fails on growth and gross margin. NetEase: single-digit growth, 60% gross margin, 12x — fails on growth. Kuaishou: +1.4%, 51.6%, 10–13x — fails badly on growth. Baidu: single-digit growth, 50% gross margin, 13.3x — fails on growth; at 2.8x ex-cash, the market is pricing in disbelief in the core business.

Of the five, only Tencent has double-digit growth, and only Tencent has a gross margin above 55%. That is no coincidence: the advertising + gaming + payments mix is the most recession-resistant revenue structure in Hong Kong-listed internet.

The reasons for elimination in the initial screen, explained one by one

Alibaba: As cheap as Tencent on valuation, but its growth engine is cloud, and cloud capex came earlier and heavier than Tencent's. Buying Alibaba means buying a "cloud re-rating" — higher payoff, lower certainty. NetEase: 60% gross margin is good, but revenue is growing in the single digits and the product line is thin (one old game contributes the bulk), making it a "high-dividend value stock," not a "growth-recovery stock."

Meituan: The subsidy war in local services is not over, and profit is unpredictable. Kuaishou: Revenue +1.4%, and the AI business (Kling) is too small a share to support the valuation. Baidu: A 2.8x PE after deducting cash shows the market believes the core business is shrinking; it carries the greatest transformation uncertainty.

Final pick: Why Tencent

Three reasons, in order of importance: 1) Its growth does not depend on a single product — advertising +22%, domestic games +17%, cloud up by a low-twenties percentage, walking on three legs; 2) Its gross margin is rising — 57.8%, +1pct, showing pricing power remains; 3) Its valuation sits at the low end of its historical range — 12.3x forward, 11.8x ex-cash, more expensive than NetEase (8x ex-cash), but with two more growth curves than NetEase.

Why not Alibaba? Because Alibaba's problem is "how long before cloud can support the valuation" — a question that takes 3 years to verify; Tencent's problem is "when does capex peak" — a question whose answer is visible within 4 quarters. At the same odds, pick the one with the shorter verification period.

A note beyond the funnel: why not buy the cheaper one

NetEase trades at 8x ex-cash and Baidu at 2.8x ex-cash, both cheaper than Tencent. But they are cheap because they have "no growth" — the 108x cheapness is the market pricing in "no future growth", not a mispricing.

Tencent's 13x cheapness is the market pricing in "future growth but negative cash this year". The two kinds of cheapness differ in nature: the former is the classic shape of a value trap, the latter the classic shape of a cyclical mismatch. This is the final layer of the funnel — what you buy is not the cheapest, but the one whose "reason for being cheap will disappear".

One overlooked dimension: who delivers the highest shareholder returns

Tencent: HKD 80 billion of buybacks in 2025 plus RMB 41.6 billion of dividends, about RMB 115 billion in total, or 3.1% of market cap. Alibaba: buybacks are also large, but it is simultaneously committing heavy capital to cloud. NetEase: high payout ratio, but low growth. Meituan and Kuaishou: limited buybacks, cash reserved for competitive battles. Baidu: buybacks support valuation, but the core business is shrinking.

Tencent's distinguishing feature is doing three things at once: buybacks, dividends and large-scale capex, the only one of the six to do so. It shows the company's cash generation remains stronger than peers — even with negative FCF this quarter, the full-year picture (Non-IFRS RMB 136.32 billion in the first half) still covers it.

Boundaries of the funnel method

This funnel misses two types of companies: 1) those not yet profitable but in an excellent sector (e.g., pure AI application companies), which have no PE comparables; 2) those at the bottom of a cycle with temporarily distorted profits (e.g., Meituan now), where neither PE nor gross margin reflects the norm.

So the funnel's conclusion can only answer "which one to buy at this price level," not "which company is best." Tencent winning in this funnel does not mean it is best on every dimension—it trails ByteDance in AI model capability and Alibaba in cloud. It wins on "all three metrics qualify, and the reason it is cheap will disappear."

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: six screened down to one. Tencent is the only company that clears all three tests: double-digit growth, gross margin above 55%, and forward PE within 15x, and the reason it is cheap (peak capex) has a clear end date.

Actionable takeaway: for Hong Kong-listed internet exposure, Tencent is the first choice; for "AI upside," Kuaishou and Baidu offer better odds but lower certainty; for "high dividend," NetEase is the better fit.
