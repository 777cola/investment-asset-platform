Core thesis

Three lines sum up the quarter: revenue RMB 204.785 billion, +11%, gross margin 57.8%, up 1pt; net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 56.022 billion, up just 0.7%, Non-IFRS RMB 68.415 billion, +9%; operating cash flow RMB 52.710 billion against capex of RMB 52.784 billion, leaving free cash flow at -RMB 13.8 billion.

Verdict: the earnings engine is intact; the spending intensity is what has changed. Free cash flow turning negative is the only genuinely new datapoint — everything else extends existing trends.

Layer one: revenue mix — three speeds, one throughline

Total revenue rose 11%, splitting into three tiers: marketing services +22% and domestic games +17% at the top; fintech and business services +9% and value-added services +8% in the middle; social networks +0.8% and international games -0.8% at the bottom. The first tier drove almost all the incremental growth, the second held the base, and the third is a mature business.

The main thread deserves clarity: Tencent's growth has switched from game-driven to advertising-driven. Before 2021, games were the growth engine; in 2026, advertising is. That is no bad thing — advertising carries a lower marginal cost than games and is more predictable — but it ties Tencent's results more tightly to macro consumption.

Second layer: gross profit — mix improvement offsets cost inflation

Gross profit RMB 118.43 billion, +13%; gross margin 57.8%, +1.0pct. With R&D up 35%, G&A up 22% and depreciation only starting to climb, a higher gross margin can only be explained by an improving revenue mix: a rising share from marketing services (segment gross margin 57%) and domestic games (high margin), and a falling share from low-margin live-streaming tips and music licensing.

Gross margin is the hardest metric to fake. Accounting policy can move it (depreciation lives, revenue recognition), but four consecutive quarters of expansion on a consistent basis points to real pricing power. Tencent has consistently been more restrained than peers on ad load, which means there is a second leg to the gross margin story.

Layer three: costs and profit — the RMB 10.5 billion consumed by AI

Non-IFRS operating profit of RMB 75.64 billion, +9%; excluding the revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products, RMB 86.1 billion, +19%. The RMB 10.46 billion gap is the net drag from new AI products this quarter, or over RMB 40 billion annualised. One report, two measures, two stories: the core business is accelerating while new products burn cash.

Further down: net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 56.022 billion, +0.7%, RMB 12.4 billion below Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 68.415 billion; the difference is fair value changes and impairments on the investment portfolio. This line swings every quarter; valuing Tencent's profit requires looking past it.

One detail: basic earnings per share of RMB 6.207 implies net profit of about RMB 56.5 billion on 9.103 billion shares, slightly above the RMB 56.022 billion reported. The gap reflects weighted average versus period-end share count—buybacks are shrinking the share base, so the weighted figure runs below the period-end figure.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — three lines bleeding at once

Operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion (RMB 74.38 billion a year ago, RMB 101.35 billion last quarter), capex payments RMB 59.3 billion, dividend payments RMB 41.6 billion. All three lines draining at once, free cash flow -RMB 13.8 billion. Management's adjusted view: +RMB 37.6 billion excluding prepayments for compute procurement.

The balance sheet still holds up: net cash RMB 58.19 billion (RMB 146.86 billion at the end of March), plus a listed investment portfolio of about USD 72 billion. No new debt to fund compute — that is where discipline shows.

But one thing needs to be clear: if Q3 capex stays at RMB 50 billion and dividends are already paid, Q3 free cash flow lands somewhere between 0 and -RMB 5 billion. Two consecutive negative quarters, and the market's pricing of this "cash cow" changes for good.

Three easily overlooked details

1. Advertising +14% QoQ matters more than +22% YoY. The YoY figure carries a base effect; QoQ shows real acceleration. Q2 had 618, Q3 has summer and back-to-school. If QoQ growth stays positive, this line is solid.

2. Cloud's revenue growth is buried inside "FinTech and Business Services." The segment grew +9% overall, but cloud grew "low-twenties" while payments grew single digits—so payments dragged down cloud's actual growth. Treating segment growth as cloud growth understates cloud.

3. Capex of RMB 52.784 billion is an accrual; cash payment was RMB 59.3 billion. Accruals hit the income statement (starting point for depreciation); payments hit the cash flow statement. The RMB 6.5 billion gap is timing between payables and prepayments. Use 59.3 for cash flow, 52.784 for depreciation.

Quarterly and 1H results: the trend matters more than any single data point

1H26: revenue RMB 401.24 billion, +10%; gross profit RMB 229.70 billion, +12%; gross margin up from 57% to 58%; Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 136.32 billion, +10%; capex RMB 84.72 billion, +82%. The half-year gross margin sits above the single-quarter figure (58% vs 57.8%), pointing to a better mix in Q1 and a slight drag in Q2 from low-margin categories during 618.

Taken together, the two quarters tell a clear story: revenue growth accelerated from +9% in Q1 to +11% in Q2, Non-IFRS net profit growth decelerated from +11% to +8.5%, and capex jumped from RMB 31.94 billion to RMB 52.78 billion. One acceleration, one deceleration, one jump — read together, these three lines are the textbook shape of trading profit for the future.

Gross margin by segment: what explains the 57% versus 52% gap

Marketing services gross margin 57%, fintech and business services 52%, overall 57.8%. The overall figure exceeds both segments, implying games and social networks carry gross margins above 60% — the true source of Tencent's profit.

Run the numbers: marketing services RMB 43.565 billion × 57% = RMB 24.8 billion gross profit; fintech and business services RMB 60.286 billion × 52% = RMB 31.4 billion; combined RMB 56.2 billion. Against total gross profit of RMB 118.433 billion, that leaves RMB 62.2 billion from value-added services of RMB 98.414 billion, implying a 63% gross margin. Games were the highest-margin segment this quarter, which is why domestic games +17% drove profit far more than their revenue share would suggest.

What happens if Q3 replicates this quarter?

Assume Q3 revenue of RMB 210 billion (+9%), gross margin of 57.5%, capex of RMB 50 billion, operating cash flow of RMB 65 billion and no large dividend payment. Free cash flow = 650 − 560 (payments) ≈ +RMB 9 billion, turning positive.

The scenario makes one point: half of the -RMB 13.8 billion reflects dividend timing (RMB 41.6 billion paid in Q2) rather than capex alone. A second consecutive negative quarter is therefore unlikely—absent a large Q3 dividend, FCF should return to around 0. Any market pricing of "permanently negative FCF" would be a mispricing.

Accounting quality score: four good, two to watch

Four positives: revenue was not recognised early (operating cash flow trails net profit, indicating no channel stuffing); receivables did not balloon abnormally; non-IFRS adjustments were disclosed in full; and segment data reconcile to total revenue (984.14 + 435.65 + 602.86 = 2,022.65, a RMB 2.52 billion gap versus 2,047.85 that sits in "other").

Two items to watch: first, depreciation lives — computing-power assets are rising fast as a share of the total, so any change in depreciation policy moves profit; second, the RMB 6.5 billion gap between capital expenditure "accrued" and "paid" — prepayments are up, and future quarters' cash flow will be locked in by earlier periods. Neither is a problem, but both warrant checking every quarter.

Reverse inference: where the RMB 12.4 billion non-operating loss sits

Net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 56.02 billion versus Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 68.42 billion — a gap of RMB 12.4 billion. Three components drive it: fair value changes on the investment portfolio, impairment provisions and share-based compensation expense. None of the three enters Non-IFRS; all three enter IFRS.

That explains the divergence between 0.7% growth in net profit attributable to shareholders and 9% growth in Non-IFRS: these three items were likely a positive contribution last year (investment gains) and a negative one this year. Investment-related volatility will persist, so Tencent's profit trend must be read on a Non-IFRS basis.

IFRS still cannot be ignored entirely: if non-operating items stay negative for four consecutive quarters, the investment portfolio is shrinking systematically — a real loss, not accounting noise.

Conclusion

Takeaway from a close reading of the results: core business ahead of expectations, cash flow turning negative. Revenue beat consensus by 1%, gross margin rose 1pt and advertising accelerated to 22% — all three point to intact fundamentals; free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion and net cash down RMB 88.7 billion in a single quarter point to thinning cash.

Three things to watch most closely: whether marketing services growth holds at 20% in Q3, whether capex falls back from RMB 52.784 billion in Q3, and whether operating cash flow returns above RMB 70 billion. These three determine whether 2027 is an investment year or an expense year.
