## Layer one: revenue mix — three speeds, one throughline

Total revenue +11%, which breaks into three tiers: marketing services +22% and domestic games +17% in the first tier; fintech and business services +9% and value-added services +8% in the second; social networks +0.8% and international games -0.8% in the third. The first tier contributed almost all the incremental growth, the second tier held the base, and the third tier is mature business.

This main thread deserves clarity: Tencent's growth has switched from "game-driven" to "advertising-driven." Before 2021, games were the growth engine; in 2026, advertising is. That is not a bad thing — advertising has lower marginal cost than games and higher predictability than games — but it ties Tencent's results more tightly to macro consumption.

## Second layer: gross profit — mix improvement offsets cost inflation

Gross profit RMB 118.43 billion, +13%; gross margin 57.8%, +1.0pct. In a year of R&D +35%, G&A +22% and depreciation beginning to climb, the only explanation for a higher gross margin is an improving revenue mix: a rising share from marketing services (segment gross margin 57%) and domestic games (high margin), and a falling share from low-margin live-streaming tips and music licensing.

Gross margin is the hardest metric to fake. Accounting policy can affect it (depreciation lives, revenue recognition), but four consecutive quarters of expansion on a consistent basis shows real pricing power. Tencent has consistently been more restrained than peers on ad load, which means there is a second leg to the gross margin story.

## Layer three: costs and profit — the RMB 10.5 billion eaten by AI

Non-IFRS operating profit RMB 75.64 billion, +9%; excluding revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products, it is RMB 86.1 billion, +19%. The RMB 10.46 billion gap is the net drag from new AI products this quarter, annualizing to over RMB 40 billion. One report, two measures, two stories: the core business is accelerating, new products are burning cash.

Further down: net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 56.022 billion, +0.7%, RMB 12.4 billion less than Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 68.415 billion; the difference is fair value changes and impairments on the investment portfolio. This line swings every quarter; valuing Tencent's profit requires looking past it.

One detail: basic earnings per share RMB 6.207, implying net profit of about RMB 56.5 billion on 9.103 billion shares, slightly above the RMB 56.022 billion reported. The difference stems from weighted average share count versus period-end share count—buybacks are shrinking the share base, so the weighted figure runs below the period-end figure.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — three lines bleeding simultaneously

Operating cash flow RMB 52.71 billion (RMB 74.38 billion in the year-ago period, RMB 101.35 billion last quarter), capex payments RMB 59.3 billion, dividend payments RMB 41.6 billion. All three lines draining at once, free cash flow -RMB 13.8 billion. Management's adjusted view: +RMB 37.6 billion excluding prepayments for compute procurement.

The balance sheet still holds up: net cash RMB 58.19 billion (RMB 146.86 billion at the end of March), plus a listed investment portfolio of about USD 72 billion. No new debt to fund compute — that is where discipline shows.

But one thing needs to be clear: if Q3 capex stays at RMB 50 billion and dividends are already paid, Q3 free cash flow lands somewhere between 0 and -RMB 5 billion. Two consecutive negative quarters, and the market's pricing of this "cash cow" changes for good.

## Three easily overlooked details

1. Advertising's +14% QoQ matters more than +22% YoY. The YoY figure carries a base effect; QoQ shows real acceleration. Q2 had 618, Q3 has summer and back-to-school. If QoQ growth stays positive, this line is solid.

2. Cloud's revenue growth is buried inside "FinTech and Business Services." The segment overall grew +9%, but cloud grew "low-twenties" and payments grew single digits—meaning payments dragged down cloud's actual growth. Treating segment growth as cloud growth understates cloud.

3. Capex of RMB 52.784 billion is an accrual; cash payment was RMB 59.3 billion. Accruals hit the income statement (starting point for depreciation); payments hit the cash flow statement. The RMB 6.5 billion gap is timing between payables and prepayments. Use 59.3 for cash flow, 52.784 for depreciation.

## Quarterly and first-half results: the trend matters more than any single data point

First half: revenue RMB 401.24 billion +10%, gross profit RMB 229.70 billion +12%, gross margin up from 57% to 58%, Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 136.32 billion +10%, capex RMB 84.72 billion +82%. The half-year gross margin is higher than the single-quarter figure (58% vs 57.8%), indicating Q1 had a better mix and Q2 was dragged slightly by low-margin categories during 618.

Looking at the two quarters together, the trend is clear: revenue growth accelerated from +9% in Q1 to +11% in Q2, Non-IFRS net profit growth decelerated from +11% to +8.5%, and capex jumped from RMB 31.94 billion to RMB 52.78 billion. One acceleration, one deceleration, one jump — the combination of these three lines is the textbook shape of "trading profit for the future".

## Breakdown of gross margin by segment: what explains the gap between 57% and 52%

Marketing services gross margin 57%, fintech and business services 52%, overall 57.8%. The overall figure exceeds both segments, implying games and social networks carry gross margins above 60% — the true source of Tencent's profit.

Do the math: marketing services RMB 43.565 billion × 57% = RMB 24.8 billion gross profit; fintech and business services RMB 60.286 billion × 52% = RMB 31.4 billion; combined RMB 56.2 billion. Total gross profit RMB 118.433 billion, leaving RMB 62.2 billion from value-added services of RMB 98.414 billion, implying a 63% gross margin. Games were the highest-margin segment this quarter, which is why domestic games +17% drove profit far more than their revenue share would suggest.

## What happens if Q3 replicates this quarter?

Assume Q3 revenue of RMB 210 billion (+9%), gross margin of 57.5%, capex of RMB 50 billion, operating cash flow of RMB 65 billion, and no large dividend payment. Free cash flow = 650 − 560 (payments) ≈ +RMB 9 billion, turning positive.

This scenario shows one thing: half of the -RMB 13.8 billion stems from dividend timing (RMB 41.6 billion paid in Q2), not entirely from capex. So the probability of "negative for two consecutive quarters" is not high—as long as Q3 does not pay a large dividend, FCF should return to around 0. If the market prices in "permanently negative FCF," that is a mispricing.

## Accounting quality score: four good, two to watch

Four positives: revenue was not recognized early (operating cash flow below net profit, indicating no channel stuffing), receivables did not balloon abnormally, non-IFRS adjustments were disclosed in full, and segment data reconcile to total revenue (984.14 + 435.65 + 602.86 = 2,022.65, a RMB 2.52 billion gap versus 2,047.85 that sits in "other").

Two items to watch: first, depreciation lives — computing-power assets are rising fast as a share of the total, so any change in depreciation policy moves profit; second, the RMB 6.5 billion gap between capital expenditure "accrued" and "paid" — prepayments are up, and future quarters' cash flow will be locked in by earlier periods. Neither is a problem, but both warrant checking every quarter.

## Reverse inference: where the RMB 12.4 billion non-operating loss sits

Net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 56.02 billion vs Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 68.42 billion, a gap of RMB 12.4 billion. Three main components: fair value changes on the investment portfolio, impairment provisions, and share-based compensation expense. None of the three enter Non-IFRS, but all enter IFRS.

This explains why net profit attributable to shareholders grew only 0.7% while Non-IFRS grew 9%: these three items were likely a positive contribution last year (investment gains) and a negative contribution this year. Investment-related volatility will persist, so Tencent's profit trend must be read on a Non-IFRS basis.

But IFRS cannot be ignored entirely: if non-operating items are negative for four consecutive quarters, the investment portfolio is shrinking systematically — that is a real loss, not accounting noise.
