Core thesis

Between 18 May and 14 August, Tencent fell from 449.2 to 440.0, trading between a low of 411.8 and a high of 492.2.Only three of the eight events were signals: the Hy3 launch (7/6), mutual fund selling (7/21) and the Q2 earnings report (8/12).The remaining five were sentiment, sector moves and sell-side views.

Assessment: over the three months, flows rather than fundamentals drove the share price direction, even as fundamentals improved (advertising accelerated, gross margin rose).

Path: three declines, two failed rebounds

First decline: 449.2 in mid-May to 427.2 at end-May, down 5%, triggered by market concern over capex after the Q1 results (RMB 31.936 billion already spent in Q1). First rebound: 10 consecutive days of buybacks plus a sector recovery, including a 10.5% gain on 6/2 alone to close at 481.6.

Second decline: mid-June to 6/26, from 481.6 to 411.8, down 14.5%, on a global AI stock selloff compounded by southbound capital outflows, with no company-specific bad news. Second rebound: the Hy3 launch on 7/6 drove the stock back to 484.0 by mid-July.

Third decline: public fund reduction disclosures on 7/21, with a 7.0% drop on 7/22 alone, followed by a further 4.46% fall on 8/13 after the 8/12 results. This time it is a fundamental event, not sentiment.

Real signal one: Hy3 launch, capabilities externally validated

On July 6, Tencent released and open-sourced the official version of its Hunyuan Hy3 model: 295B total parameters, 21B activated, 256K context, API priced at RMB 0.25 per million tokens, with adaptation for a domestic compute card (Moore Threads MTT S5000) completed the same day. This is Yao Shunyu's first deliverable since joining Tencent, and the first time the market can assess Tencent's model capabilities using third-party data.

Why it matters: before this, "Tencent is behind in AI" was a judgment that could not be verified; after this, it has benchmarks, pricing and third-party adaptation. The stock rose 4.8% that day, but more importantly it lays the narrative groundwork for the August earnings report — advertising's AIM+ and gaming's Hy3D pipeline are both built on this model.

Real signal two: public funds cut holdings by RMB 18.7 billion as pricing power shifts

Public mutual funds completed their Q2 disclosures on July 21, and Tencent topped the table for market-value reduction, with RMB 18.737 billion sold. The stock fell 7.0% the next day to close at 440.6. The move reflected not fundamentals but who was pricing the stock.

With domestic mutual funds selling, marginal pricing power shifted to foreign investors and southbound retail — and foreign investors were underweight China internet in 2026. That explains the fall despite decent results: no one was bearish; there was simply no buyer. For judging near-term moves, this signal is more useful than any financial statement figure.

Real signal 3: earnings have shifted the pricing anchor to cash flow

August 12 earnings: revenue RMB 204.785 billion, +11% and above consensus, gross margin 57.8% (+1pct), advertising +22%, domestic games +17% — all good news. But free cash flow came in at -RMB 13.8 billion, Tencent's first negative quarter in years. The stock fell 4.46% the next day.

The market's reaction was blunt: no more talk of growth, only cash flow. That means the valuation anchor has shifted from P/E to FCF. In a quarter of negative FCF, any P/E is illusory — which is why buying at 13x P/E did not produce a rally.

The opportunity lies on the other side: once FCF turns positive, the anchor shifts back, and the re-rating will happen quickly.

Five noise items, not signals

① The +10.5% move on 6/2: buyback plus sector rebound plus short covering, not a change in fundamentals; the stock subsequently round-tripped. ② Goldman Sachs' HKD 700 target price: the stock fell a further 10% after the 6/11 note; sell-side views are not buy signals. ③ The 411.8 print on 6/26: contagion from the global AI selloff; Tencent made no announcement that day.

④ The six-day, +10.91% rally from 7/31 to 8/5: pre-earnings positioning, a pattern seen many times before, with the gains given back after results. ⑤ Market cap overtaken by ChangXin Memory Technologies on 8/13: a ranking headline only, with no read-across to cash flow, profit or the competitive landscape. Reading it as a signal confuses the "memory cycle" with "Tencent's decline."

Attribution: in a 35% decline, how much is self-inflicted

From 677.5 to 440, down 35%. Decomposing the move: the multiple compressed from about 23x to 15x, accounting for the entire decline, while Non-IFRS net profit grew over the same period (RMB 224.84 billion for full-year 2025, +16%; RMB 136.32 billion for 2026H1, +10%).

The answer, then: 100% of the decline came from valuation, 0% from earnings. Three factors drove the de-rating: AI capex turning free cash flow negative (about half), the broad de-rating of China ADRs (about 30%), and the loss of domestic pricing power (about 20%). Only the first is the company's own problem, and it is reversible.

That is the entire basis for our "Buy": the problem sits on the valuation side, it is reversible, and the stock has already fallen to the bottom of its historical range.

The line between signal and noise

All three signals share one trait: they either change cash flow (FCF in the financials) or change pricing power (public fund selling, the capability re-rating driven by Hy3). The five noise items share the opposite: they change neither.

On that standard, the +10.5% move on 6/2, though the largest and most eye-catching, changed neither cash flow nor pricing power, so it was noise. The -4.46% move on 8/13, by contrast, was modest in size but shifted the market's judgment on whether Tencent is still a cash cow, so it was a signal.Magnitude does not equal information content — the most common mistake when reading unusual moves.

Conclusion

Attribution: eight events in three months — three signals (Hy3, mutual fund selling, earnings) and five noise items. Noise shares one trait: it does not change cash flow. Signals share one trait: they change cash flow or pricing power.

Trading implication: do not judge this company on single-day moves. Judge whether capex falls and operating cash flow recovers in the Q3 report — that is the next real share-price driver.
