## I. Company Overview

Tencent Holdings (00700.HK) is China's largest integrated internet services provider. The stock closed at HKD 440.00 on 2026/08/14, implying a market cap of about HKD 4.01 trillion (roughly RMB 3.69 trillion) on 9.103 billion shares outstanding. The business spans four segments: value-added services (gaming and social networks), marketing services, fintech and business services, and others.

2026Q2 revenue came in at RMB 204.785 billion (+11%), with a gross margin of 57.8%, Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 68.415 billion (+9%) and net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 56.022 billion (+0.7%). Weixin and WeChat remain the core asset, with combined MAU of 1.439 billion, up 2% YoY, and time spent on Video Accounts up 20% YoY.

On the shareholding structure, Ma Huateng holds 8.82% (805 million shares) and Prosus/MIH holds 22.8%. Over the past year the stock has fallen from HKD 677.5 to HKD 440, a decline of 35%, driven mainly by valuation compression as free cash flow turned negative on AI capex.

## II. Industry analysis

China's internet sector has entered a stock-market phase. Online advertising is growing in the single digits to low double digits, domestic gaming in the single digits, overseas gaming in the double digits and public cloud in the double digits (accelerating on AI), while large models remain in early commercialisation. There is no high growth overall, but concentration keeps rising and leaders benefit.

The biggest variable for the industry in 2026 is compute. AI training and inference demand has exploded, GPUs and HBM are tight across the board, chipmakers' gross margins have risen to around 70%, and cloud providers have become queuers in a seller's market. Tencent's first-half capex of RMB 84.72 billion, up 82% YoY, is a direct result of this variable.

Liquidity is the other variable. The Hang Seng Tech Index has fallen more than 25% year to date in 2026, and the market's main thread has shifted from the "AI narrative" to "cash flow and shareholder returns." That explains why results beat expectations while the share price fell—the pricing anchor has switched from P/E to FCF.

## III. Business model

Tencent's business model boils down to "one gateway, three monetisation layers": WeChat supplies the attention and social graph of 1.439 billion people; gaming sells content, advertising sells traffic, and payments and cloud sell infrastructure.

Each business has a different economic character: advertising carries near-zero marginal cost (57% gross margin), gaming is cost-front-loaded (gross margin above 60%), payments is a toll booth on consumption, and cloud is a landlord — asset-heavy, a utilisation game. So advertising growth is worth more than gaming growth, and cloud growth alone cannot drive a re-rating.

What changes in 2026 is the attempt at a "fourth monetisation layer": AI products (WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Yuanbao, Xiaowei) start charging. Together they currently drag on operating profit by about RMB 10.5 billion per quarter, but if they work, the company upgrades from "selling traffic" to "selling productivity." That is the source of the valuation upside — and of the current disagreement.

## IV. Financial analysis

G&A expenses rose 22% to RMB 38.808 billion and R&D 35% to RMB 27.28 billion, with the entire increment directed to AI. Non-IFRS operating profit RMB 75.64 billion (+9%), or RMB 86.1 billion (+19%) excluding new AI products.

Cash flow: Operating cash flow RMB 52.710 billion (RMB 74.376 billion a year ago, RMB 101.351 billion last quarter), capex payments RMB 59.3 billion and dividend payments RMB 41.6 billion, leaving free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion (+RMB 37.6 billion excluding prepayments for computing power).

Balance sheet: Net cash of RMB 58.191 billion at end-June (RMB 146.860 billion at end-March), plus a listed investment portfolio of about USD 72 billion. First-half buybacks of 50.03 million shares for HKD 24.4 billion (average HKD 487.7), and RMB 41.6 billion paid in 2025 dividends. Financial quality has not deteriorated; the use of cash has changed.

## V. Management and governance

Pony Ma (Chairman and CEO, 8.82% stake) sets strategy and proposes a three-layer AI architecture spanning intelligence, applications and infrastructure. Martin Lau (President) oversees capital allocation and frames capex as "a one-off startup investment in new AI-native businesses". Ren Yuxin (COO) oversees gaming and platforms, while Yao Shunyu (Chief AI Scientist, formerly of OpenAI) leads models, with Hy3 released on 2026/07/06.

Strong capital allocation record: distributed JD shares in 2022 and Meituan shares in 2023, bought back HKD 80 billion in 2025 and HKD 24.4 billion in 2026H1, and paid a dividend of HKD 5.30 per share. Governance risk: Prosus/MIH holds 22.8% and has been slowly reducing over the long term; three key revenue lines (AI, Weixin Channels advertising, cloud profit) are not disclosed separately.

Assessment: capital allocation A, disclosure B, governance B+. Management and shareholder interests are aligned (founder ownership plus large buybacks), but insufficient transparency will keep weighing on the valuation.

## VI. Growth drivers

Driver 1: ad load. WeChat Channels' ad load has long trailed peers, and marketing services grew 22% this quarter and 14% QoQ — the ramp has only just begun. If ad load reaches half of peers' levels, annual revenue would rise by about RMB 86 billion and net profit by about RMB 37 billion.

Driver 2: game pipeline. Four domestic titles are live simultaneously (Delta Force, Valorant, Legend of Kingdoms: World, Peacekeeper Elite), and Chaos Evolution drew 40 million pre-registrations in July; internationally, Miniclip anchors casual genres.

Driver 3: cloud and AI. Cloud growth accelerated from high teens to low twenties, helped by a May price increase; WorkBuddy ranked first in China with 20.97 million PC visits in June; Xiaowei is in grayscale testing.Of the three drivers, the first is the most certain and the third has the most upside.

## II (Appendix), Industry Size and Structure

China's online advertising market is worth roughly RMB 700 billion on a public research basis, with growth slowing to single digits to low double digits; mobile gaming is roughly RMB 300 billion, split between single-digit domestic growth and double-digit overseas growth; public cloud is growing at a double-digit rate, with AI-related demand the main increment; and the large-model API market remains early-stage, with intense price competition.

Structural change matters more than scale: advertising budgets are shifting from brand exposure to performance conversion, game revenue from user-acquisition-led to content-led, and cloud from resource leasing to model services. All three shifts favour companies with scenarios and content.

Tencent sits on the right side of all three: WeChat's closed loop captures performance advertising, evergreen games capture content-led growth, and Hunyuan + MaaS captures model services. This is the structural reason its growth outpaces the industry.

## V (Appendix). Incentives and Organization

Net headcount rose by 5,672 in 2025 and attrition fell to 15.9%; management explicitly stated there are no plans for major layoffs. In a year when AI replaces white-collar work, "no layoffs" is a cost choice and an organisational signal.

R&D spend was RMB 27.28 billion, up 35% YoY, and G&A expenses were RMB 38.808 billion, up 22%. All of the incremental spend went to large-model iteration and AI transformation across the entire product line. Resources are tilting toward the technology side, and part of the slowdown in mature businesses reflects deliberate reallocation.

The crux of incentives is "who is accountable for return on capital." Those who decide capex are currently assessed on model capability and call volume, while depreciation is borne by the whole company. This is a common failing of large companies building new businesses, not unique to Tencent, but it will affect the discipline of AI investment.

## VIII (Appendix). Stress test: three downside scenarios

Scenario 1 (capex does not fall): capex holds at RMB 50 billion a quarter in 2027, depreciation rises by RMB 38 billion a year, Non-IFRS net profit RMB 270 billion, 11x → HKD 320, -27%. Scenario 2 (advertising stalls): marketing services growth slows to 8%, net profit RMB 260 billion, 12x → HKD 340, -23%. Scenario 3 (both combined): net profit RMB 240 billion, 10x → HKD 265, -40%.

What the three bad scenarios share is a simultaneous cut to both the multiple and net profit — the most painful combination. But all of them require 2027 to be verified — over the remaining two quarters of 2026, the worst case is HKD 370 (pessimistic scenario).

The point of a stress test is not to scare yourself but to size the position: if the -40% scenario keeps you awake at night, then the 4% initial position is too heavy.

## VII. Competitive landscape

Advertising: ByteDance leads, Tencent is second, and Tencent is gaining share through Channels and the WeChat closed loop. Gaming: Tencent is global No. 1; NetEase is stronger in MMORPGs and anime-style games and miHoYo is stronger in open-world titles, but neither matches Tencent's distribution network. Cloud: Alibaba Cloud is No. 1, Huawei Cloud is strong in government and enterprise, and Tencent Cloud differentiates on audio-video and the gaming ecosystem.

Large models: Doubao (ByteDance), Qwen (Alibaba), DeepSeek and Hunyuan compete in the same arena. Hunyuan follows a "parameter efficiency + low price" path (Hy3: 295B/21B, API RMB 0.25 per million tokens) rather than a capability-leadership path.

ByteDance remains the most dangerous rival: the fight for time spent is the first layer, the AI entry point (Doubao) the second. Tencent's response is WeChat mini-programs — embed AI inside WeChat so that the entry point remains WeChat.

## VIII. Risk factors

①Capex normalization: Sustaining capex above RMB 50 billion a quarter keeps free cash flow negative over the long term and de-rates the multiple to 11–12x. ②Depreciation and impairment: About RMB 38 billion of new depreciation in 2027; amortizing over 3 years doubles the hit. ③Advertising slowdown: At +22%, growth falling below 15% would choke off incremental profit. ④Gaming high base: Domestic games lap a high base of RMB 47.3 billion in 2027.

⑤AI entry point bypassed: Should AI assistants become the primary entry point and WeChat be relegated to a contact list, the valuation could fall to 8–10x. ⑥Regulation: Game licenses, payment compliance, platform liability. ⑦Major shareholder selldown: Prosus's 22.8% stake and a prolonged selldown weigh on the valuation. ⑧FX and liquidity: Shrinking Hong Kong market liquidity amplifies volatility.

## X. Valuation

Method: Non-IFRS net profit × target P/E ÷ exchange rate ÷ share count. We use Non-IFRS rather than IFRS, which is distorted by fair value changes in the investment portfolio (a RMB 12.4 billion difference this quarter).

Target P/E of 14x rests on three points: the lower-middle of Tencent's actual 11–23x trading range since 2021; 12–15x for a mature company growing revenue 10%; and a 4–6x discount to the 18–20x historical midpoint for AI uncertainty.

Target price: 3,200 × 14 ÷ 0.92 ÷ 91.03 = HKD 535, +22% versus the current price. Bear case (2,800 × 11) = HKD 370 (-16%); bull case (3,500 × 17) = HKD 700 (+59%). Cross-check: sum-of-the-parts of about HKD 520, consistent in magnitude with 535.

## XI. Investment recommendation

Rating: Buy (staged). Open the first tranche at HKD 440 (half the target position), add below HKD 400, stop adding above HKD 480, and halve the position at HKD 535 while retaining a core holding. Logic-based stop: marketing services growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters; capex above RMB 50 billion for three consecutive quarters with cloud growth below 15%; Non-IFRS net profit turning negative YoY.

Why not "Strong Recommend": the fifth gate (margin of safety) scores only 6, and negative free cash flow means the margin of safety rests on "no earnings downgrade" rather than on assets. The shape of this investment is "hard floor below, sentiment above" — suited to trading time for upside, not to leverage.

Expected return: 9% profit growth + 2.3% shareholder return + re-rating from 13x to 14x, for 12%–15% annualized over three years. This is not a life-changing investment; it is an investment that can beat the index.

## III (Appendix). Unit economics: RMB 142 and the headroom around it

RMB 204.785 billion ÷ 1.439 billion = RMB 142 per user per quarter, or RMB 568 annualised. Breakdown: advertising RMB 30, gaming c.RMB 46, payments and cloud c.RMB 42, social networking RMB 23.

Against Meta, revenue per user per quarter is more than three times Tencent's. The gap lies not in user count but in two factors: ad unit pricing (China eCPM sits below Europe and the US) and payment habits (a low share of Chinese users pay for content). Neither shifts near term; ad load can.

Estimate: lifting marketing services revenue per user per quarter from RMB 30 to RMB 45 (ad load raised to half the peer level) adds about RMB 86 billion of annual revenue; at a 57% gross margin and 25% tax rate, net profit rises by about RMB 37 billion, equal to 13% of 2026E Non-IFRS. This is the most certain growth path in this report.

## VI (Appendix). Quantifying advertising growth

Marketing services revenue = DAU × ad impressions per user × eCPM. Of the three, eCPM is set by the auction and sits outside Tencent's control, impressions are set by load rate and are controllable, and DAU has peaked at 1.439 billion.

Ad growth therefore decomposes as follows: load rate contributes 60%, eCPM 25% and DAU 15%. Of this quarter's +22%, the main drivers were load rate and AI-driven efficiency gains, which lift conversion rates and hence eCPM; 618 was a seasonal factor.

That decomposition implies a sustainable growth rate of 15%–18%: load rate still has room but will not keep rising rapidly, and eCPM tracks the macro.I therefore model marketing services at +16% in 2027E rather than extrapolating the +22%.

## X (Appendix). Comparable company valuation

Citi 1-year forward P/E as of 2026/06/28: Tencent 12.3x (11.8x ex-cash), Alibaba 12.5x (10.4x), NetEase 12.0x (8.0x), Baidu 13.3x (2.8x), JD.com 5.3x ex-cash, Meituan 0.8x P/S.

At 12.3x, Tencent trades at the lowest multiple among the "platforms with growth": Alibaba sits at 12.5x but its growth is cloud-driven, NetEase at 12x with zero growth, and Baidu at 13.3x with a shrinking core business.On a rough PEG basis, Tencent's 12.3x / 10% growth = 1.2, versus Alibaba 12.5x / 8% = 1.6 and NetEase 12x / 3% = 4.0.

That comparison supports the conclusion that "Tencent offers the best value for money among the six," and explains why this report assigns a target P/E of 14x rather than 18x — peers' valuation centres all sit at 12–13x, so a higher multiple would be unrealistic.

## XI (Appendix). Suitability for different investors

Long-term allocation (3+ years): Suitable. Buy at 13x for 12%–15% annualised; ignore the volatility. Absolute return (1 year): Average. 22% upside versus 16% downside is not a compelling payoff—wait for below HKD 400. Trend-following: Not suitable. Earnings and capex now drive this stock, not trend.

Unsuitable investors: Those who cannot tolerate a -25% drawdown, those who need leverage, and those who cannot stomach "good results, falling share price." The last group is the largest—they will doubt their own judgment after the -4.46% on 8/13, then capitulate at HKD 400.

Suitability here hinges less on risk appetite than on whether you can hold the position. The holding experience will be poor: good earnings, falling share price, repeatedly.
