Lead-in

Tencent is down 35%, and the debate has centred on capex and free cash flow. Those concerns are real, but they are only half the story. The other half: revenue +11%, gross margin 57.8%, advertising +22%, and WeChat's 1.439 billion users are still there. This piece lays out both halves, then gives an actionable price.

I. First, the numbers

Tencent Q2 2026: revenue RMB 204.785 billion, +11% YoY and 1% above consensus. Gross margin 57.8%, up 1 percentage point from a year earlier. Advertising revenue RMB 43.565 billion, +22% YoY. Domestic games RMB 47.3 billion, +17% YoY. WeChat MAU 1.439 billion.

Three jarring lines follow: capex RMB 52.784 billion, +176% YoY; operating cash flow RMB 52.710 billion, -29% YoY; and free cash flow of -RMB 13.8 billion — Tencent's first quarterly net cash outflow in years.

The stock fell 4.46% to HKD 441 the day after the results, then HKD 440 a day later — down 35% from HKD 677.5 last October.

II. What fell was not earnings, but the multiple

That is no reassurance. Run the numbers: at HKD 677.5 this time last year the market was paying around 23x; at HKD 440 today it is 15x TTM and just over 13x 2026 Non-IFRS.

Net profit rose over the same period: RMB 224.8 billion for full-year 2025, up 16% YoY, and RMB 136.3 billion in Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders for the first half of 2026, up 10% YoY.Earnings did not fall; the multiple fell 35%. That is the whole story.

Why did the multiple fall? Because AI capex turned free cash flow negative. A company that routinely generated over RMB 100 billion in free cash flow suddenly posted a RMB 13.8 billion outflow in a single quarter, and the market's first reaction was "cash cow turns into cash-burning machine" — so it valued the company as one.

3. Half of the RMB -13.8 billion is timing-related

Free cash flow = operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion − capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion − other. This quarter, however, also absorbed the 2025 annual dividend of RMB 41.6 billion — a once-a-year outlay, not the norm.

Management offered an adjusted basis: adding back prepayments for computing power procurement gives free cash flow of +RMB 37.6 billion. That figure cannot be taken as a conclusion in its own right — prepayments will eventually convert into depreciation — but it does show one thing: the company is still making money; it has simply spent the money ahead of time.

The real cause for caution is the 29% drop in operating cash flow. If it recovers above RMB 70 billion next quarter, this was a one-off timing issue; if it stays around RMB 50 billion for two consecutive quarters, collections are the problem.

IV. The Overlooked Half: Advertising Is Accelerating

Everyone talks about capex; nobody talks about advertising. Yet advertising is the brightest spot in these results: RMB 43.57 billion, up 22% YoY and 14% QoQ, versus industry ad growth of only single digits to low double digits — Tencent is running at nearly twice the pace.

The drivers are concrete: the AIM+ ad delivery system has been rolled out, recommendation models are lifting conversion, WeChat Stores and short dramas are adding new inventory, and the 618 shopping festival provided a seasonal boost. Three of these four drivers sit within Tencent's own control.

More important, Video Accounts' ad load remains below peers, giving ad growth an organic source for the next two to three years without relying on a macro recovery — an asset hidden outside the financial statements.

V. Behind the RMB 10.5 billion: core business actually grew 19%

Non-IFRS operating profit came in at RMB 75.64 billion, up 9% YoY. On a second basis, excluding the revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products, it was RMB 86.1 billion, up 19% YoY.

The RMB 10.46 billion gap is the net drag from new AI products this quarter — an annualised RMB 40 billion-plus. It separates two stories: the core business is accelerating (+19%), while the new business is burning cash (annualised RMB 40 billion).

So "Tencent's profit has stalled" is wrong, and "Tencent's profit is great" is not entirely right either — it depends on which half you mean. And when the stock fell, the market saw only the half that is burning cash.

Six: What are you buying at HKD 440?

Market cap HKD 4.01 trillion (about RMB 3.69 trillion) on 2026 Non-IFRS net profit of about RMB 280 billion, or 13.2x — the low end of Tencent's valuation range over the past decade.

A more direct reference: Tencent itself bought back 50.03 million shares in the first half, spending HKD 24.4 billion at an average price of HKD 487.7. The company bought at 490; you buy at 440, 10% below what the company paid.

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 370 (-16%), base case HKD 535 (+22%), bull case HKD 700 (+59%). That is 1.4:1 odds; I put probabilities at 50% base, 30% bear and 20% bull, for an expected return of +18%.

This setup does not suit a full position; it suits scaling in: buy half at 440, add to full below 400, stop adding above 480.

VII. When I would admit I am wrong

Three markers to hold myself to, recorded here for future reference:

  • Ad growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters — the foundation has cracked; this is not a capex issue.
  • Capex above RMB 50 billion for three consecutive quarters while cloud growth falls below 15% — the spend is earning no return, and AI is pure cost.
  • Non-IFRS net profit turning negative YoY — the core business has begun to shrink, and even 13x would not be cheap then.

If any one holds, I halve the position; if two hold, I exit entirely — no explanations, no excuses. In investing, admit mistakes fast and hold winners slowly.

Eight, a final word of honesty

This is not an investment that will turn your fortunes around. Tencent is no longer the Tencent of 2011: revenue is growing 11% and profit 9%, making it a mature infrastructure company.

At 13x, what you get is 9% profit growth + 2.3% dividend and buyback + a bit of valuation repair — roughly 12%–15% annualised over three years.

That return is not exciting, but it comes from a company used daily by 1.4 billion people, with a 57.8% gross margin, whose management is buying back stock at HKD 487.7. In a market where everyone is chasing AI stories, this kind of "boring certainty" is scarce.

Nine: one line for each of the three types of people

If you are investing money you won't need for three years or more: 440 is a level to start buying. Ignore whether next quarter's earnings look good; watch whether capex rolls over in 2027. If you want a quick trade: don't buy. The payoff ratio is only 1.4:1 — no fast money here.

If you are already stuck above 600: don't rush to average down. Watch two quarters — if advertising is still growing above 20% and capex is rolling over, there is still hope; if both deteriorate, taking the loss is more dignified than holding on.
