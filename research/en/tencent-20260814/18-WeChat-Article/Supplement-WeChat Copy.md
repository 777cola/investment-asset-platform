## I. First, the numbers

Tencent Q2 2026: revenue RMB 204.785 billion, +11% YoY, 1% above the consensus. Gross margin 57.8%, up 1 percentage point from a year earlier. Advertising revenue RMB 43.565 billion, +22% YoY. Domestic games RMB 47.3 billion, +17% YoY. WeChat MAU 1.439 billion.

Then three jarring lines: capex RMB 52.784 billion, +176% YoY. Operating cash flow RMB 52.710 billion, -29% YoY. Free cash flow -RMB 13.8 billion — Tencent's first quarterly net cash outflow in years.

The day after the results, the stock fell 4.46% to HKD 441. A day later, HKD 440. That is down 35% from HKD 677.5 last October.

## II. What fell was not earnings, but the multiple

That line is not reassurance. Do the math: at HKD 677.5 this time last year, the market was assigning a P/E of around 23x; at HKD 440 now, it is 15x TTM and just over 13x 2026 Non-IFRS.

Net profit rose over the same period: RMB 224.8 billion for full-year 2025, up 16% year on year; RMB 136.3 billion in Non-IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders for the first half of 2026, up 10% year on year.Earnings did not fall; the multiple fell 35%. That is the whole story.

Why did the multiple fall? Because AI capex turned free cash flow negative. A company that routinely generated over RMB 100 billion in free cash flow suddenly saw an outflow of RMB 13.8 billion in a single quarter, and the market's first reaction was "cash cow turns into cash-burning machine" — so it valued the company as a cash-burning machine.

## 3. Half of the RMB -13.8 billion is timing-related

Free cash flow = operating cash flow of RMB 52.71 billion − capex payments of RMB 59.3 billion − other. But this quarter also paid the 2025 annual dividend of RMB 41.6 billion, which is a once-a-year outlay, not the norm.

Management also gave an adjusted basis: adding back prepayments for computing power procurement, free cash flow is +RMB 37.6 billion. That number cannot be used directly as a conclusion—prepayments will eventually turn into depreciation—but it shows one thing: the company is still making money, it has just spent the money ahead of time.

What really warrants caution is the 29% drop in operating cash flow. If it returns above RMB 70 billion next quarter, this was a one-off timing issue; if it stays around RMB 50 billion for two consecutive quarters, then collections are the problem.

## IV. The Overlooked Half: Advertising Is Accelerating

Everyone talks about capex; nobody talks about advertising. But advertising is the brightest spot in this earnings report: RMB 43.57 billion, up 22% year on year and 14% quarter on quarter. Industry ad growth is only in the single digits to low double digits, and Tencent is running at nearly twice that.

The reasons are concrete: the AIM+ ad delivery system has been rolled out, recommendation models are lifting conversion, WeChat Stores and short dramas are bringing in new inventory, and there was the 618 shopping festival. Three of these four drivers are within Tencent's own control.

More important is that Video Accounts' ad load remains below peers. That means ad growth has an organic source for the next two to three years, with no need to count on a macro recovery. This is an asset hidden outside the financial statements.

## V. The truth behind RMB 10.5 billion: core business actually grew 19%

Non-IFRS operating profit was RMB 75.64 billion, up 9% YoY. The company gave another figure: excluding revenue, costs and expenses of new AI products, it was RMB 86.1 billion, up 19% YoY.

The RMB 10.46 billion gap is the net drag from new AI products this quarter, an annualized RMB 40 billion-plus. The number separates two things: the core business is accelerating (+19%), the new business is burning cash (annualized RMB 40 billion).

So "Tencent's profit has stalled" is wrong, and "Tencent's profit is great" is not entirely right either. It depends on which half you mean. And when the stock fell, the market saw only the half that is burning cash.

## Six: What are you buying at HKD 440?

Market cap of HKD 4.01 trillion (about RMB 3.69 trillion), 2026 Non-IFRS net profit of about RMB 280 billion, 13.2x. This is the low end of Tencent's valuation range over the past decade.

A more direct reference: Tencent itself bought back 50.03 million shares in the first half, spending HKD 24.4 billion at an average price of HKD 487.7. The company bought at 490; you buy at 440, 10% cheaper than the company paid.

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 370 (-16%), base case HKD 535 (+22%), bull case HKD 700 (+59%). Odds of 1.4:1; I estimate probabilities at 50% base, 30% bear, 20% bull, for expected return of +18%.

This setup does not suit a full position; it suits scaling in: buy half at 440, add to full below 400, stop adding above 480.

## VII. When I would admit I am wrong

Three points, written here for future reference:

- Ad growth below 15% for two consecutive quarters — the foundation has collapsed; this is not a capex issue.

- Capex above RMB 50 billion for three consecutive quarters while cloud growth falls below 15% — spending is yielding no return, and AI is pure expense.

- Non-IFRS net profit turns negative year on year — the core business has begun to shrink, and even 13x would not be cheap then.

If any one holds, I halve the position; if two hold, I exit entirely, no explanations, no excuses. In investing, admit mistakes fast and hold slowly.

## Eight, a final word of honesty

This is not an investment that will turn your fortunes around. Tencent is not the Tencent of 2011: revenue is growing 11% and profit 9%, making it a mature infrastructure company.

Buying it at 13x, what you get is: 9% profit growth + 2.3% dividend and buyback + a bit of valuation repair. That works out to roughly 12%–15% annualized over three years.

That return is not exciting, but it comes from a company used daily by 1.4 billion people, with a 57.8% gross margin, whose management is buying back stock at HKD 487.7. In a market where everyone is chasing AI stories, this kind of "boring certainty" is scarce.

## Nine: one line for each of the three types of people

If you are investing money you won't need for three years or more: 440 is a level to start buying. Ignore whether next quarter's earnings look good; watch whether capex rolls over in 2027. If you want a quick trade: don't buy. The payoff ratio is only 1.4:1 — no fast money here.

If you are already stuck above 600: don't rush to average down. Watch two quarters — if advertising is still growing above 20% and capex is rolling over, there is still hope; if both deteriorate, taking the loss is more dignified than holding on.
