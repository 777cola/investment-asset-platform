## Layer one: revenue mix — four curves, only one rising

The RMB 30.807 billion splits into four blocks: online marketing RMB 11.9 billion (-22%), AI cloud RMB 8.2 billion (+94%), AI applications RMB 2.6 billion (flat) and AI-native marketing RMB 2.4 billion (-15%). Together they total RMB 25.1 billion, matching "general business RMB 25.0 billion"; the remaining roughly RMB 5.8 billion is consolidated businesses such as iQIYI.

Structure is more telling than the total. Advertising is 39% of revenue, down more than 30 points from over 70% three years ago, while AI cloud is 27%, versus a single-digit share three years ago.The centre of gravity in revenue has shifted from "selling intent" to "selling compute" — the single most important takeaway from this earnings report, and far more important than the -1.1% headline.

One further detail deserves attention: AI-native marketing came in at RMB 2.4 billion, -15% YoY. This is the company's own definition of "advertising in the AI era," and it is falling too — evidence that the new ad format has yet to find a scalable vehicle, and the most direct evidence against an inflection point in the advertising business.

## Second layer: gross profit and costs — gross margin down to 35.4%

Cost of sales rose 9% YoY to RMB 19.9 billion, growing against the tide in a quarter when revenue fell 1.1% and directly compressing gross margin to 35.4% (our calculation). Management is blunt on the drivers: higher cloud business costs and traffic acquisition costs.

One item warrants quantification: depreciation and amortisation of about RMB 2.8 billion (derived: adjusted EBITDA 45 less adjusted operating profit 17), equal to 9% of revenue. That RMB 2.8 billion is the bill for the past two years of capex, and it is rigid — purchased cards are depreciated over three to five years, leased machine rooms incur rent. Capex was RMB 11.4 billion in Q2 and RMB 7.3 billion in Q3; these numbers will convert into depreciation over the next 8–12 quarters.

The gross margin decline therefore is not over. Even with no further deterioration in revenue mix, incremental depreciation alone can shave another 2–3 points. Only one path reverses this: cloud scale effects outrunning depreciation growth — at a quarterly scale of RMB 8.2 billion, a tall order.

One cost line is easily overlooked: traffic acquisition costs. Cost of sales rose 9% YoY, which the company attributes to "cloud costs and traffic acquisition"; the latter shows Baidu must buy traffic externally to fill the gap left by its own entry points. A company that once monopolised Chinese search traffic has begun paying for traffic — the most direct financial evidence of eroding entry-point value, and more credible than MAU figures.

## Tier 3: Cost and profit — RMB 1.5 billion of savings to defend a 2% margin

Cost is the one thing this quarter was managed well: sales and administrative expenses came in at RMB 5.5 billion, down 17% YoY, and R&D at RMB 4.8 billion, down 7% YoY. Together the two saved roughly RMB 1.5 billion YoY, exactly offsetting the pressure from cost of sales, up 9%. The result is an operating margin still held at 2.0%.

But this profit is squeezed out, not earned. R&D down 7% is a danger signal in the AI race — peers are raising R&D, Baidu is cutting. The 17% decline in sales and administrative expenses looks more like the product of layoffs and organisational contraction. There is at most another year or two of room to shift costs around; after that, either revenue picks up or margins keep falling.

Ignore the RMB 1.395 billion net profit attributable to shareholders — last year's -RMB 11.232 billion included a RMB 16.190 billion impairment of long-term assets, a pure base effect. Look instead at non-GAAP RMB 2.395 billion, down 36%: that is the true speed of the core business — a third of the profit gone in a year.

Keep the expense ratio in mind: sales and administrative plus R&D total RMB 10.3 billion, 33% of revenue. Unless that ratio falls, every 1% of revenue growth yields only RMB 0.65 of incremental gross profit — the leverage is negative.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the safety cushion is still there, but thinning

Operating cash flow was positive at RMB 2.8 billion, so the business is still generating cash. Against capex of RMB 7.3 billion, free cash flow was -RMB 4.5 billion (rough calculation). Read the two together: the company has already cut capex from RMB 11.4 billion to RMB 7.3 billion and it still isn't enough to cover—the cloud business's cash-flow model is currently "negative" and needs to be subsidised by cash on the balance sheet.

The asset side remains the hardest part of the whole piece: cash and investments of RMB 276.6 billion against a market cap of RMB 153 billion, PB 0.52. Total shares outstanding about 2.64 billion, 1 ADS = 8 shares. What this table says is: even if the main business went to zero tomorrow, liquidation value would still exceed the current share price.

But "the safety cushion is thinning" is real. Annualising this quarter's -RMB 4.5 billion free cash flow gives RMB 18 billion consumed per year; RMB 276.6 billion would last fifteen years—not dangerous, but it shows that "the cash is always there" is an illusion. This is also why the SOTP takes net cash at only RMB 160 billion rather than RMB 276.6 billion.

## Profit bridge: how did gross profit fall from RMB 10.9 billion to RMB 630 million?

Laying the income statement out on a single line is more revealing than reading each number in isolation. Gross profit of RMB 10.907 billion, less selling and administrative expenses of RMB 5.5 billion and R&D of RMB 4.8 billion, leaves just RMB 607 million; add other items of about RMB 20 million and operating profit comes to RMB 630 million. In other words, expenses absorbed 94% of gross profit.

This bridge tells us two things. First, Baidu's cost structure is "heavy sales + heavy R&D," the two together totalling RMB 10.3 billion, or 33% of revenue. That ratio is not high for an internet company, but it sits alongside zero revenue growth—no operating leverage has emerged. Second, an operating margin of 2.0% is already close to break-even; a further 3% decline in revenue, or a 5% rebound in expenses, would push operating profit negative. The safety cushion is thin enough to absorb only one quarter of surprises.

This is also why "non-GAAP net profit of RMB 2.395 billion" warrants caution: it exceeds operating profit by RMB 1.8 billion, the gap driven mainly by add-backs of share-based compensation and by interest and investment income. Operations themselves earned only RMB 630 million—for a single quarter, at a company with hundreds of billions in revenue.
