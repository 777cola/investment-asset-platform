Key takeaways

The four layers of this earnings report get uglier one after another. Revenue layer: RMB 30.807 billion, -1.1%; AI cloud +94% offsets advertising -22%, leaving total revenue flat. Gross profit layer: cost of sales RMB 19.9 billion, +9%, gross margin compressed to 35.4%. Profit layer: operating profit RMB 630 million, margin 2.0%; the RMB 1.395 billion net profit attributable to shareholders "turnaround" rests entirely on the low base created by last year's RMB 16.190 billion impairment. Cash flow layer: operating cash flow RMB 2.8 billion, capex RMB 7.3 billion, free cash flow -RMB 4.5 billion.

Layer one: revenue mix — four curves, only one rising

RMB 30.807 billion splits into four blocks: online marketing RMB 11.9 billion (-22%), AI cloud RMB 8.2 billion (+94%), AI applications RMB 2.6 billion (flat), AI-native marketing RMB 2.4 billion (-15%). The four add up to RMB 25.1 billion, matching "general business RMB 25.0 billion"; the remaining roughly RMB 5.8 billion is consolidated businesses such as iQIYI.

Structure tells you more than the total. Advertising is 39% of total revenue, down more than 30 points from over 70% three years ago; AI cloud is 27%, a single-digit share three years ago.The center of gravity in revenue has shifted from "selling intent" to "selling compute" — the single most important takeaway from this earnings report, far more important than the -1.1% headline.

One more detail deserves attention: AI-native marketing came in at RMB 2.4 billion, -15% year on year. This is the company's own definition of "advertising in the AI era," and it is falling too — evidence that the new ad format has yet to find a scalable vehicle, and the most direct evidence against an inflection point in the advertising business.

Second layer: gross profit and costs — gross margin falls to 35.4%

Cost of sales was RMB 19.9 billion, up 9% YoY, growing against the tide in a quarter when revenue fell 1.1%, directly compressing gross margin to 35.4% (rough calculation). The company is blunt about why costs rose: higher cloud business costs and traffic acquisition costs.

One item here must be quantified: depreciation and amortization of about RMB 2.8 billion (derived: adjusted EBITDA 45 minus adjusted operating profit 17), equal to 9% of revenue. That RMB 2.8 billion is the bill for the past two years of capex, and it is rigid — bought cards are depreciated over three to five years, leased machine rooms incur rent. Capex was RMB 11.4 billion in Q2 and RMB 7.3 billion in Q3; these numbers will turn into depreciation over the next 8–12 quarters.

So the gross margin decline is not over. Even if the revenue mix deteriorates no further, the incremental depreciation alone can shave another 2–3 points. Only one path reverses this: scale effects in cloud outrunning depreciation growth — at a quarterly scale of RMB 8.2 billion, that is hard.

One cost line is easily overlooked: traffic acquisition costs. Cost of sales rose 9% YoY, which the company attributes to "cloud costs and traffic acquisition"; the latter shows Baidu must buy traffic externally to fill the gap left by its own entry points. A company that once monopolized Chinese search traffic has begun paying for traffic — the most direct financial evidence of eroding entry-point value, more credible than MAU figures.

Tier 3: Costs and profit — save RMB 1.5 billion to hold a 2% margin

Costs are the only thing done well in this report: sales and administrative expenses RMB 5.5 billion, down 17% YoY; R&D RMB 4.8 billion, down 7% YoY. Together the two saved roughly RMB 1.5 billion YoY, exactly offsetting the pressure from cost of sales +9%. The result is an operating margin still held at 2.0%.

But this profit is squeezed out, not earned. R&D -7% is a danger signal in the AI race — peers are raising R&D, Baidu is cutting. The -17% in sales and administrative expenses looks more like the result of layoffs and organizational contraction. There is at most another year or two of room to shift costs around; after that, either revenue picks up or margins keep falling.

Ignore the RMB 1.395 billion net profit attributable to shareholders — last year's -RMB 11.232 billion included a RMB 16.190 billion impairment of long-term assets, a pure base effect. Look at non-GAAP RMB 2.395 billion, -36%: that is the true speed of the core business — a third of the profit gone in a year.

Remember the expense ratio: sales and administrative plus R&D total RMB 10.3 billion, 33% of revenue. Unless that ratio falls, every 1% of revenue growth yields only RMB 0.65 of incremental gross profit — the leverage is negative.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the safety cushion is still there, but thinning

Operating cash flow was RMB 2.8 billion, positive, showing the business is still generating cash. Capex was RMB 7.3 billion, free cash flow -RMB 4.5 billion (rough calculation). Read the two together: the company has already cut capex from RMB 11.4 billion to RMB 7.3 billion and it still isn't enough to cover—showing that the cloud business's cash-flow model is currently "negative" and needs to be subsidized by cash on the balance sheet.

The asset side remains the hardest part of the whole piece: cash and investments RMB 276.6 billion, market cap RMB 153 billion, PB 0.52. Total shares outstanding about 2.64 billion, 1 ADS = 8 shares. What this table says is: even if the main business went to zero tomorrow, liquidation value would still exceed the current share price.

But "the safety cushion is thinning" is real. Annualizing this quarter's -RMB 4.5 billion free cash flow gives RMB 18 billion consumed per year; RMB 276.6 billion would last fifteen years—not dangerous, but it shows that "the cash is always there" is an illusion. This is also why the SOTP takes net cash at only RMB 160 billion rather than RMB 276.6 billion.

Profit bridge: how did RMB 10.9 billion gross profit shrink to RMB 630 million?

Stringing the income statement into a single line makes it clearer than looking at each number alone. Gross profit of RMB 10.907 billion, less selling and administrative expenses of RMB 5.5 billion and R&D expenses of RMB 4.8 billion, leaves just RMB 607 million—add other items of about RMB 20 million and you get operating profit of RMB 630 million. In other words, 94% of gross profit was eaten up by expenses.

This bridge tells us two things. First, Baidu's expense structure is "heavy sales + heavy R&D," with the two totaling RMB 10.3 billion, or 33% of revenue. That ratio is not high for an internet company, but it corresponds to zero revenue growth—no scale effect has emerged. Second, an operating margin of 2.0% is already close to the break-even line; if revenue falls another 3%, or expenses rebound 5%, operating profit turns negative. The safety cushion is thin enough for only one quarter of surprises.

This is also why "non-GAAP net profit of RMB 2.395 billion" warrants caution: it is RMB 1.8 billion higher than operating profit, with the difference mainly from share-based compensation add-backs and interest and investment income. The money actually earned from operations was only RMB 630 million—for one quarter, at a company with hundreds of billions in revenue.

Conclusion

Close-read conclusion: This is a report where the mix is shifting, profit is downhill, and the asset base is still solid. Revenue leans on cloud to offset advertising, gross margin is squeezed by costs and depreciation, profit is propped up by expense cuts, and cash flow is topped up from cash on the balance sheet. None of the four layers is improving; the sole exception is AI cloud growth. So watch just two numbers next: depreciation as a share of revenue (now 9%), and cloud segment operating profit. The former sets the floor for gross margin; the latter decides whether this story can turn into profit.
