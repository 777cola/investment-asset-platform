Core view

One earnings report can yield five profit figures: operating profit of RMB 630 million, adjusted operating profit of RMB 1.7 billion, adjusted EBITDA of RMB 4.5 billion, non-GAAP net profit of RMB 2.395 billion, and net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 1.395 billion. The five figures run from 6.3 to 45, a 7x spread. Which one you pick determines how you see this company. This report's choice: use 6.3 for operations, 23.95 for the core-business trend, 13.95 for the accounting result, and not 45.

Three profit layers: the adjustment chain from 6.3 to 45

Let us lay out the chain clearly. Layer one: operating profit of RMB 630 million — the cleanest number, revenue minus cost minus expenses, after depreciation and amortization. Margin 2.0%. Layer two: adjusted operating profit of RMB 1.7 billion — adding back non-cash items such as share-based compensation, margin 5.6%. Layer three: adjusted EBITDA of RMB 4.5 billion — adding back a further RMB 2.8 billion of depreciation and amortization.

Munger's verdict on layer three was scathing: EBITDA is for people who think depreciation is not a cost. That applies especially to Baidu — depreciation is the money spent on chips amortized over time, a real cash outflow. Viewing Baidu through EBITDA is tantamount to pretending the cloud business needs no capex. So this report does not use the 45 figure.

Layer four is non-GAAP net profit of RMB 2.395 billion, RMB 1.8 billion above operating profit, with the gap mainly from add-backs of share-based compensation plus interest income and investment gains. This number suits trend analysis (down 36% YoY) but not absolute level — because it counts interest generated by assets as operating profit. For a company holding RMB 276.6 billion of cash, interest income should never have counted as core business.

Depreciation: A bill that has not yet come due

Depreciation and amortization of RMB 2.8 billion, 9% of revenue, is the bill for the past two years of capex. This quarter's capex was RMB 7.3 billion and Q2 was RMB 11.4 billion—these numbers will turn into depreciation over the next 8–12 quarters.

A rough calculation: assuming annualized capex of RMB 29.2 billion (73×4) depreciated over 4 years, new depreciation is RMB 7.3 billion a year, or RMB 1.8 billion a quarter. That is, over the next year depreciation could rise from RMB 2.8 billion/quarter to more than RMB 4.0 billion/quarter, with its share of revenue climbing from 9% to 13%. Those 5 percentage points come straight out of gross margin.

This is the report's hardest call because it relies on no forecast—only on money already spent. For the cloud business to offset this pressure, revenue growth must outpace depreciation growth. At a scale of RMB 8.2 billion/quarter, that is difficult. Gross margin has not finished trending down.

Cash flow: 28 minus 73 equals negative 45

Operating cash flow RMB 2.8 billion, capex RMB 7.3 billion, free cash flow negative RMB 4.5 billion. The number says this: cash generated by the company's core business is not enough to cover its investments. The gap is plugged with cash on the balance sheet.

Some will argue that "capex is investing in the future, not a loss." That is half right. The premise of investing in the future is that "future returns exceed the cost of capital." Baidu is investing in AI cloud, where the return is a 20%–30% gross margin; after depreciation and operating expenses, the net return may be only single digits. If the return is below the 8% cost of capital, that investment is destroying value.

More noteworthy is the trend: capex fell from RMB 11.4 billion to RMB 7.3 billion, showing management is also pulling back. That pullback is a good signal—it means the company is starting to care about cash flow rather than expanding capacity without limit. But the other side of the pullback: cloud revenue growth will slow over the next two quarters, because compute supply is no longer expanding.

Assets: how much of RMB 276.6 billion is "real money"

The last question is the easiest to overlook: RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments is not all cash. It comprises cash, short-term investments and long-term equity investments. Cash and short-term investments are hard; long-term equity investments depend on the underlying — listed shares are marked to market, unlisted holdings are carried at cost or fair value and are illiquid.

That is why the SOTP takes net cash at only RMB 160 billion rather than RMB 276.6 billion: deduct interest-bearing debt, then apply a 50% haircut to long-term equity investments. The discount is not conservatism but a liquidity discount — if you wanted to sell today, you could not.

RMB 160 billion over 2.64 billion shares is about RMB 60.6 per share, or about HKD 66.5. In other words, the current price of HKD 63.55 is almost exactly the "net cash price" — the core business and Robotaxi come free. That is the quantified expression of "cheap," and why downside is limited — unless the cash is burned.

Conclusion

The financial truth in one sentence: this company's core business barely makes money (2.0% operating margin), the cash on its books almost equals its market cap (net cash of RMB 160 billion, discounted ≈ HKD 66.5/share), and the depreciation bill has not yet fully arrived. These three facts hold simultaneously and explain why PB is 0.52, why the share price hugs its 52-week low, and why it is "cheap but no one dares to buy." The precondition for buying is not a cheaper price, but that the depreciation bill can be covered by revenue growth.
