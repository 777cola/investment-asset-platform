Core view

Eight risks, ranked by "probability × damage to intrinsic value." The three most lethal are accelerating advertising decline, an AI cloud price war, and no shareholder returns over the long term. The first two destroy intrinsic value; the third destroys the valuation. The remaining five—cash burn, depreciation, Robotaxi failure, the China ADR structure and talent drain—are amplifiers: not lethal on their own, but they make the first three worse.

Category 1: Risks that erode intrinsic value (first two items)

Accelerating ad decline is the most lethal factor. If the current -22% proves a steady decline, ad revenue retains 48% in three years; if decline accelerates (-25%, -28%, -30%), only 34% remains. The intrinsic value gap between the two scenarios exceeds 20%. The mechanism is real: advertisers pull out → ad slot quality deteriorates → user experience worsens → more users shift to AI products → fewer ad slots — a self-reinforcing loop.

The AI cloud price war is the second factor. New capacity comes online in 2027, and Alibaba Cloud and Volcano Engine have every incentive to clear the field on price. Baidu's RMB 8.2 billion per quarter carries no cost advantage into a price war; growth falls from +94% to below 20%. Once the "high growth" story breaks, the valuation method must shift from "growth + assets" to "pure assets," and the neutral scenario drops from HKD 85 to below HKD 70.

Category 2: valuation de-rating risk (point 3)

The single most direct cause of the stock's 60% decline is the prolonged absence of shareholder returns. With RMB 276.6 billion of cash against a market cap of RMB 153 billion, a 2% dividend and a 5% buyback would be enough to lift PB above 0.7. Instead, the company has paid no dividend for five years, and its buyback during the year totalled USD 373 million — less than 2% of market cap.

What makes this risk hard to handle is that it has "no trigger point": unlike ad-price cuts, it produces no quarterly number — it is simply "nothing happening." Investors slowly lose patience while waiting, and the valuation slowly de-rates while waiting.

Our response is to quantify "inaction": if by mid-2027 (8 months after the reporting date) there is still no dividend announcement and no expansion of the buyback authorisation, we downgrade from "Watch" to "Avoid." This is not a bearish call on fundamentals but an acknowledgment that "this management's capital allocation will not change."

Category 3: Amplifiers (last five items)

The remaining five are not fatal individually, but they compound the first three.

  • Capex rebound: capex climbs from RMB 7.3 billion back above RMB 10 billion, the free cash flow gap widens to -RMB 7 billion per quarter and cash burn accelerates.
  • Depreciation squeezes gross margin: the most certain of the five (50% probability), since the bill has already been incurred. Gross margin falls below 30%, operating profit turns negative and the valuation shifts to "asset liquidation".
  • Robotaxi failure: the smallest impact, as the market currently prices it at zero. Failure merely voids the option and does not impair the assets.
  • China ADR structural risk: low probability but devastating, cannot be hedged and can only be managed through position sizing (no more than 6%).
  • Talent drain: R&D expense at -7% is already a signal. Cutting R&D in an AI race is slow suicide.
Why is "share price decline" not on the risk list?

This warrants separate treatment: a falling share price is not risk, it is price. A stock on 0.52x PB that falls 20% sits at 0.42x PB — a deeper asset discount, not deteriorating fundamentals. Listing that as a risk would drive exactly the wrong behaviour: the more it falls, the more afraid you get; the more afraid you get, the more you sell.

The real risk is declining intrinsic value, not a declining quote. Every item below therefore addresses what would make the company earn less, shrink its assets or lower returns — not one is about how far the share price will fall. This is also why there is no price-based stop-loss: a stop-loss should be triggered by falsification conditions, not by quotes.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: Three of the eight are fatal — advertising, price war, no returns to shareholders; five are amplifiers. What matters most is not the share price but three numbers: quarterly online marketing YoY, cloud segment operating profit, and the dividend announcement. If two of the three deteriorate, exit; if two improve, add. In the ambiguous middle ground, do nothing.
