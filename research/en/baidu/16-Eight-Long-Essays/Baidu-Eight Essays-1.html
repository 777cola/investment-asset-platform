Core view

Three popular consensus views on Baidu each need resetting: "Baidu is China's No. 1 AI stock," "the 60% drop is a systemic China ADR selloff," and "0.52x PB is a safety cushion." Each is half right, yet all three conclusions are wrong. After the reset, Baidu is neither a growth stock nor deep value but an "asset-backed option" — that is its true position today.

Misreading 1: Baidu is not the "No. 1 Chinese AI stock"

In 2023 the label was right. ERNIE Bot was the first domestic product benchmarked against ChatGPT, Baidu was the only company with a full-stack story of "chip + cloud + model + application," and the stock ran from HKD 100 to HKD 160.5. Three years on, the label remains; the position has shifted.

Consider three sets of numbers. AI cloud: Baidu does RMB 8.2 billion a quarter, Alibaba Cloud is several times its size, Volcano Engine has ByteDance's cash flow behind it, and the three carrier clouds bring power and government-enterprise relationships — Baidu sits in the third tier, albeit growing fastest (+94%). Models: ERNIE is squeezed on the consumer side by Doubao, Quark and Yuanbao, and on the enterprise side must be sold bundled with cloud; model API prices have already been pushed below cost. Applications: the search gateway with 604 million MAU is still there, but users' paths to information are migrating away.

"Full-stack" is a technical fact; "number one" is a market narrative. Technical facts don't lose you money; market narratives do. Value Baidu as the "number one AI stock" and you conclude it is "severely undervalued"; value it as "third-tier cloud + second-tier models + a shrinking ad business" and you conclude it is "roughly fair, at a slight discount." The latter is correct.

Misreading 2: A decline does not equal a mistaken sell-off

In 2026 the Hang Seng Tech Index fell more than 25%, Tencent more than 30%, Alibaba over 20% and Baidu 60.4%. Had the decline been entirely sector-driven, Baidu should have fallen only 25%–30%; the extra 30 points are the company's own problem.

What do those 30 points correspond to? Advertising down double digits for several consecutive quarters (-22% this quarter), non-GAAP net profit -36%, gross margin down from 50% to 35.4%, and no dividend for five years. The market is not selling it on emotion; it is re-rating it one notch at a time.

That distinction dictates the trade. If the decline is sector-driven, a sector rebound alone unwinds the loss — a beta story. If it is company-driven, only fundamental improvement can repair it — an alpha story. You cannot buy Baidu on the "China ADR rebound" thesis, because that does not explain the extra 30 points of decline.

Misreading 3: 0.52x PB is not a safety cushion — it is the pricing

The most dangerous consensus. Many investors see "market cap HKD 168 billion, cash and investments RMB 276.6 billion, P/B 0.52" and their first reaction is "free money." But a P/B of 0.52 is what the market calculated, not what the market miscalculated.

Run the numbers: net assets of about RMB 294.2 billion (market cap RMB 153 billion ÷ 0.52, back-solved), annualised net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 5.58 billion, ROE 1.9%. For a company with ROE of 1.9%, net assets should inherently trade at a discount to their value to shareholders. At an 8% cost of capital, RMB 294.2 billion of net assets should generate RMB 23.5 billion of profit a year; actual profit is only RMB 5.58 billion—just a quarter of that. A 0.52x P/B precisely reflects "assets operating at only a quarter of the efficiency."

So a low P/B is not a margin of safety; it is the market saying: "I believe these assets exist, but I don't believe they can make money." There is only one path to turn it into a margin of safety—raise the return on assets, or return the assets to shareholders. The former depends on AI cloud profits turning positive; the latter on dividends and buybacks. Neither has happened yet.

Reset coordinates: asset-based options

With three misreadings cleared up, Baidu's position becomes clear. It is not a growth stock (revenue -1.1%), nor deep value (ROE 1.9%, no dividend), but an "asset-type option": the strike price is set by the assets, the premium is paid by the share price, and the payoff depends on whether management is willing to exercise.

In options language, intrinsic value comes from RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments (the floor beneath it), time value from AI Cloud and Apollo Go (upside imagination), and theta (time decay) from the continued decline in advertising. The right way to buy an option is "small position, long horizon, wait for a catalyst," not "heavy position, chase the rebound."

This is why the rating is "Watch, wait for the inflection" rather than "Buy." An option's value lies not in being cheap now, but in the event that will one day reprice it—a dividend, cloud profit turning positive, or advertising bottoming out, one of the three. Until then, it is just a quiet contract losing a little time value every day.

Conclusion

The cognitive reset, concluded: Baidu is "a third-tier AI cloud + a second-tier model + a shrinking advertising business + a hard-asset balance sheet" — four pieces stitched into an asset-backed option. Value it on a growth framework and you overpay; value it on deep value and you buy too early. Only an options framework tells you how large a position to take and which signal to wait for. That premise underpins the seven pieces that follow.
