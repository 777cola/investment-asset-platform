## Chips are the only link in the chain with pricing power; Baidu has achieved only "in-house use"

Profit in the AI supply chain concentrates upstream, above all in chips. Global AI capex rises in 2026, HBM and advanced process nodes stay tight, Nvidia's gross margin holds above 70% long term and Huawei Ascend remains in short supply domestically.Whoever controls compute supply takes the largest slice of gross profit in the chain.

Baidu's Kunlun chip is the only in-house product from an internet company in this segment, and its strategic value lies in "no chokehold" and "lower unit cost." It is not sold externally at scale, however, and is mainly for internal use—which means it saves cost, not earns profit. Kunlun still trails Nvidia and Ascend in performance and ecosystem and cannot become a profit centre in the near term.

In servers, IDC and power, Baidu is a buyer on all three and has no pricing power. IDC is scarce in tier-1 cities and power prices are a rigid cost; both will keep rising as AI cloud expands—part of why cost of sales rose 9%.

## Midstream: cloud and models are the most crowded segment and the least profitable

Midstream is a "selling shovels" business: it packages chips, data centres and power into compute-hours sold to customers. Two problems dog the segment — homogenisation and scale wars. Alibaba Cloud, Volcano Engine, Tencent Cloud, the three telco clouds and Baidu AI Cloud are five players chasing the same customer pool, with little product differentiation and price the only variable.

Baidu sits in the "high-growth third tier": RMB 8.2 billion per quarter, +94%, growing faster than the industry but from the smallest base. Alibaba Cloud is several times its size, Volcano Engine has ByteDance's own model demand and cash flow behind it, and the telco clouds bring power and government-enterprise relationships. Baidu's only differentiation is its full stack (chips-cloud-models integrated) and the cost advantage of Kunlun chips — technically strong, but not enough to win on commercial terms.

The model layer is worse. ERNIE is squeezed on the consumer side by Doubao, Quark and Yuanbao, and on the enterprise side depends on cloud bundling. By 2026, model API prices have fallen below the cost line; the segment barely makes money and serves as a customer-acquisition tool.

## Downstream: advertising is shrinking, Robotaxi is still waiting, and there is no third leg in between

Downstream is where value capture is richest: whoever owns the user sets the price. Douyin, WeChat and Xiaohongshu earn gross margins above 70% on advertising because they are "attention monopolists." Baidu once was too, but at RMB 11.9 billion, -22%, its gross margin remains high while its scale shrinks.

Robotaxi sits at the other end of downstream: 30 cities, 34 million kilometers in a single quarter, 390 million cumulative kilometers, the widest operating footprint globally. Yet the segment has yet to achieve value capture—vehicles carry safety operators at a human-to-vehicle ratio of no less than 1:3, cost per kilometer exceeds ride-hailing, and the business model is unproven. Its current role is an "option," not a "business."

What is missing in the middle? A new form that can absorb 604 million MAU and convert traffic into revenue. AI-native marketing, at RMB 2.4 billion, -15%, was supposed to be the answer, but it has not taken off. This is the most awkward position in the value chain: upstream cannot earn, midstream cannot compete, downstream is shrinking.

## Cycle position: capex at the peak, price war mid-slope, advertising downturn at the foot

The three segments sit at completely different points in their cycles — the easiest thing to get wrong when assessing Baidu.

- Upstream chips: cycle peak. AI capex was raised sharply for 2026, HBM supply is expected to stay short of demand into 2027, and chipmakers are still enjoying the shortage windfall.

- Midstream cloud: late expansion. New capacity comes online in 2027, a price war will most likely break out in the first half of 2027, and Baidu's 94% growth will come under pressure then.

- Downstream advertising: mid-recession. Search advertising has been losing share for three years with no sign of a bottom, and AI-native marketing has yet to pick up the slack.

With the three cycles out of sync, Baidu is absorbing three pressures at once: high upstream costs, a midstream price war and shrinking downstream revenue. The result in 2026Q3: revenue -1.1%, gross margin 35.4%, operating margin 2.0%. Any improvement requires at least one of these cycles to turn — advertising is the most likely to turn first (on a lower base), and the cloud price war the least likely.
