## Upstream: chips are the only link with pricing power; Baidu only achieved "in-house use"

Profits in the AI supply chain concentrate upstream, especially in chips. Global AI capex rises in 2026, HBM and advanced process nodes stay tight, Nvidia's gross margin holds above 70% long term, and Huawei Ascend is in short supply domestically.**Whoever controls compute supply takes the largest slice of gross profit in the chain.**

Baidu's Kunlun chip is the only in-house product from an internet company in this segment; its strategic value is "no chokehold" and "lower unit cost." But it is not sold externally at scale and is mainly for internal use—**which means it saves cost, not earns profit.** Kunlun still trails Nvidia and Ascend in performance and ecosystem, and cannot become a profit center in the near term.

In servers, IDC and power, Baidu is a buyer on all three and has no pricing power. IDC is scarce in tier-1 cities and power prices are a rigid cost; both will keep rising as AI cloud expands—part of why cost of sales rose 9%.

## Midstream: cloud and models are the most crowded segment and the least profitable

The midstream is a "selling shovels" business: packaging chips, data centers and power into compute-hours sold to customers. This segment has two problems — **homogenization and scale wars**. Alibaba Cloud, Volcano Engine, Tencent Cloud, the three telco clouds and Baidu AI Cloud — five players fighting for customers in the same pool, with little product differentiation and price the only variable.

Baidu's position is "high growth in the third tier": RMB 8.2 billion per quarter, +94%, growing faster than the industry but with the smallest scale. Alibaba Cloud is several times its size, Volcano Engine has ByteDance's own model demand and cash flow, and the telco clouds have power and government-enterprise relationships. **Baidu's only differentiation is its full stack (chips-cloud-models integrated) and the cost advantage of Kunlun chips — technically strengths, but not enough to win on commercial terms.**

The model layer is worse: ERNIE is squeezed on the consumer side by Doubao, Quark and Yuanbao, and on the enterprise side depends on cloud bundling. By 2026, model API prices have fallen below the cost line; this segment barely makes money and serves as a customer-acquisition tool.

## Downstream: advertising is shrinking, Robotaxi is still waiting, and there is no third leg in between

Downstream is where value capture is richest—whoever owns the user sets the price. Douyin, WeChat and Xiaohongshu earn gross margins above 70% on advertising because they are "attention monopolists." Baidu once was too, but now at RMB 11.9 billion, -22%, its gross margin is high but its scale is shrinking.

Robotaxi is the other end of downstream: 30 cities, 34 million kilometers in a single quarter, 390 million cumulative kilometers, the widest operating footprint globally. But this segment has yet to achieve value capture—vehicles carry safety operators at a human-to-vehicle ratio of no less than 1:3, cost per kilometer exceeds ride-hailing, and the business model is unproven. **Its current role is an "option," not a "business."**

What is missing in the middle? A new form that can absorb 604 million MAU and convert traffic into revenue. AI-native marketing, at RMB 2.4 billion, -15%, was supposed to be the answer, but it has not taken off. This is the most awkward position in the value chain: upstream cannot earn, midstream cannot compete, downstream is shrinking.

## Cycle position: capex at the peak, price war mid-slope, advertising downturn at the foot

The three segments sit at completely different points in their cycles, which is the easiest thing to get wrong when assessing Baidu.

- **Upstream chips**: cycle peak. AI capex was raised sharply for 2026, HBM supply is expected to stay short of demand into 2027, and chipmakers are still enjoying the shortage windfall.
- **Midstream cloud**: late expansion. New capacity comes online in 2027, a price war will most likely break out in the first half of 2027, and Baidu's 94% growth will come under pressure then.
- **Downstream advertising**: mid-recession. Search advertising has been losing share for three years with no sign of a bottom, and AI-native marketing has yet to pick up the slack.

**The three cycles are out of sync, meaning Baidu is absorbing three pressures at once: high upstream costs, a midstream price war and shrinking downstream revenue.** The result in 2026Q3: revenue -1.1%, gross margin 35.4%, operating margin 2.0%. Any improvement requires at least one of these cycles to turn — advertising is the most likely to turn first (on a lower base), and the cloud price war the least likely.
