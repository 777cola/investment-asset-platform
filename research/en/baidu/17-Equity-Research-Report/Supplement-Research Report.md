## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Baidu (9888.HK / BIDU.US) was founded by Robin Li in January 2000, listed on Nasdaq in August 2005, and completed a secondary listing on the HKEX in March 2021, making it one of the few Chinese internet companies listed in both the US and Hong Kong. Headquartered in Beijing, its core assets include the Baidu App (604 million MAU), Baidu AI Cloud, Kunlunxin, the ERNIE large model, Apollo Go, and its consolidated subsidiary iQIYI.

According to its Q3 2026 results, the business is divided into four segments: online marketing services RMB 11.9 billion, AI cloud infrastructure RMB 8.2 billion, AI applications RMB 2.6 billion, and AI-native marketing RMB 2.4 billion, plus roughly RMB 5.8 billion from iQIYI and others, for total revenue of RMB 30.807 billion.**AI businesses combined reached RMB 13.2 billion, 53% of the RMB 25 billion in "general business," exceeding half for three consecutive quarters.**This marks a key inflection point in the company's narrative: three years ago, AI revenue share was in the single digits.

On ownership, Robin Li holds less than 20% of shares but controls more than half the voting power through Class B ordinary shares (10 votes per share). Total share capital is about 2.64 billion shares (including both Class A and B), with 1 ADS equal to 8 ordinary shares. Based on the November 23, 2026 close of HKD 63.55, market cap is approximately HKD 168 billion, or about RMB 153 billion. Total employee headcount and registered user numbers were not disclosed in this earnings summary, and this report makes no estimates.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's AI industry can be viewed in three layers. Upstream is compute: AI chips, servers, IDCs, power. This layer sits at a cyclical high in 2026, with global AI capex revised sharply upward, HBM and advanced process nodes tight, and the supply-shortfall pattern expected to persist into 2027. Midstream is models and cloud: compute packaged into hours and sold to enterprises and developers, heavily commoditized, with price the main competitive lever. Downstream is applications and entry points: search, social, short video, AI assistants, where value capture is richest.

Baidu's position spans all three layers, but it leads in none. Upstream it has Kunlun chips, mainly for internal use and not sold externally at scale; midstream its AI cloud is a third-tier player, smaller than Alibaba Cloud and Volcano Engine; downstream its search entry point is losing traffic. This "full-stack but not dominant" position determines its gross margin structure: advertising above 70%, cloud 20%–30%, which on a weighted basis leaves overall gross margin at only 35.4% (rough calculation).

On the industry cycle, one key judgment: **AI cloud's high growth has a window.** In 2026 compute is in short supply, and whoever has the cards makes money; in 2027 new capacity comes online, supply loosens, and a price war will most likely start in the first half of 2027. For a player like Baidu that lacks scale advantage, falling prices mean both growth and gross margin come under pressure. This report therefore assigns AI cloud RMB 50 billion in 2027 revenue (annualized growth of about 40%), rather than extending 94%.

On policy, autonomous driving commercialization depends on each city's road-testing and operating permits. Apollo Go currently operates in 30 cities, with a regulatory requirement of no less than a 1:3 vehicle-to-safety-driver ratio. This ratio is a key variable in Robotaxi cost structure and the regulatory metric most worth tracking over the next three years.

## Chapter 3 Business model: three curves, one team

Baidu's business model can be broken into three curves, each with a completely different return-on-capital structure.

**First, selling intent (search advertising).** Users enter keywords, and advertisers bid on intent. The appeal of this business is near-zero marginal cost, prepaid customers and no capex; the drawback is that inventory depends on "links", and AI search does not generate links. Q3 2026 revenue was RMB 11.9 billion, down 22% YoY, accounting for 39% of total revenue, versus more than 70% three years ago.

**Second, selling compute (AI cloud).** Buy chips, build data centers, sell by the hour. This business is like electricity: asset-heavy, scale-driven, power-price-driven. Q3 2026 revenue was RMB 8.2 billion, up 94% YoY, gross margin 20%–30% (rough estimate), quarterly capex RMB 7.3 billion. Its cash flow model is currently negative: operating cash flow of RMB 2.8 billion minus capex of RMB 7.3 billion leaves free cash flow of negative RMB 4.5 billion.

**Third, selling miles (Robotaxi).** Replacing driver costs with autonomous driving. In Q3 2026 it covered 30 cities, with 34 million autonomous driving kilometers in the quarter and 390 million cumulative kilometers, but a safety driver is still required in the vehicle, unit economics are unproven, and revenue is not separately disclosed in the financials.

Together, the three curves form a structure of "complementary cash flow, mutually exclusive profit": advertising generates cash but is shrinking in scale, cloud consumes cash but is growing in revenue, and Robotaxi only consumes and produces nothing. **The result is flat total revenue (-1.1%), declining gross margin (35.4%) and negative free cash flow (negative RMB 4.5 billion).** This structure can be sustained while cash remains in reserve; once advertising's cash contribution can no longer cover cloud capex, trade-offs become unavoidable.

## Chapter 4 Financial analysis: the return to profit is an accounting phenomenon

**Income statement.** 2026Q3 total revenue RMB 30.807 billion, -1.1% YoY; cost of sales RMB 19.9 billion, +9% YoY; gross profit RMB 10.907 billion (rough calculation), gross margin 35.4%; selling and administrative expenses RMB 5.5 billion (-17%), R&D expenses RMB 4.8 billion (-7%); operating profit RMB 630 million, margin 2.0%; adjusted operating profit RMB 1.7 billion, margin 5.6%; net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 1.395 billion, versus -RMB 11.232 billion in the year-ago period (including RMB 16.190 billion of long-lived asset impairment); non-GAAP net profit RMB 2.395 billion, -36% YoY.

The "turnaround" in net profit attributable to shareholders is entirely a base effect. **Last year's RMB 16.190 billion impairment was one-off and absent this year, so the YoY comparison looks dramatic.** Stripping out non-recurring items, the core business is declining at -36%, and that is the real trend. Looking at this company through its RMB 630 million operating profit is more accurate than through any non-GAAP metric.

**Costs and depreciation.** The company is blunt about why cost of sales rose 9%: higher cloud business costs and traffic acquisition costs. The latter deserves separate mention — a company that once monopolized Chinese search traffic has begun paying for traffic, the most direct financial evidence of eroding gateway value. Depreciation and amortization was about RMB 2.8 billion (derived: adjusted EBITDA 4.5 − adjusted operating profit 1.7), 9% of revenue; with annualized capex of RMB 29.2 billion, depreciation will keep rising over the next 8–12 quarters.

**Cash flow and assets.** Operating cash flow RMB 2.8 billion, capex RMB 7.3 billion, free cash flow -RMB 4.5 billion. Cash and investments RMB 276.6 billion, 1.8x the market cap (about RMB 153.0 billion); PB 0.52; ROE 1.9% (rough calculation: annualized net profit RMB 5.58 billion ÷ implied net assets RMB 294.2 billion). **The balance sheet is the only "A-grade" part of this report; the income statement is C-grade.**

## Chapter 5 Management and Governance

Robin Li was born in 1968, studied information management at Peking University and earned a master's at SUNY Buffalo, and founded Baidu in January 2000. Among Chinese internet founders he made the earliest call on AI: he set up the Institute of Deep Learning in 2013, brought in Lu Qi in 2017 to drive "All in AI", launched ERNIE Bot in 2023, and by 2026 AI accounts for 53% of general business. **On strategic judgment alone, a 9 is not excessive.**

Capital allocation is the weak spot. The USD 1.9 billion acquisition of 91 Wireless in 2013 failed to integrate and led to large subsequent impairments; the USD 3.6 billion purchase of YY Live's domestic business in 2020–2021 bought a declining asset. **The two together total USD 5.5 billion, close to a quarter of current market cap.** iQIYI burned cash for ten years before turning an annual profit in 2022, and Kunlunxin and Apollo Go are pointed in the right direction but slow to pay back.

On governance, Class B super-voting rights give the founder a majority of the vote, leaving minority shareholders unable to influence capital allocation. The result: no dividend for five years, USD 373 million of buybacks during the year (under 2% of market cap), and a first dividend that remains only "expected to be announced before year-end". Since Lu Qi's departure in 2018 the company has not appointed a COO and has no public succession plan — Wang Haifeng (CTO), Shen Dou (Cloud), He Junjie (MEG) and Wang Yunpeng (IDG) all report directly to the CEO.

**This structure is at its most efficient while the founder is in place and at its most risky when he exits.** The valuation impact: for the same asset quality, the market applies a larger discount to a "one-man company". That explains part of the 0.52x PB.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers: Three Verifiable Engines

**Engine one, AI cloud.** Q3 2026 revenue RMB 8.2 billion, +94% YoY, of which GPU cloud +191%, +43% QoQ. Drivers are compute supply shortage and secured supply of Kunlun chips. This report forecasts RMB 50 billion in 2027 (+52%); the key assumption is that the price war only begins in H2 2027. If the price war starts earlier, growth could be just 30%.

**Engine two, AI applications bundled with models.** RMB 2.6 billion, broadly flat. This segment does not pursue standalone profit; it sells to enterprises as a package of "model + cloud + private deployment," and its function is to pull through cloud revenue. The yardstick is not this segment's revenue growth but whether it can lift cloud revenue retention.

**Engine three, Robotaxi.** 30 cities, 34 million km in the quarter, 390 million km cumulative — the widest-operating Robotaxi network globally. The driver is regulatory approval to remove safety drivers. If the human-to-vehicle ratio relaxes from 1:3 to 1:1, cost per km could fall by more than one-third, and only then could the business model hold. This report gives no profit forecast for this line — timing is unforecastable, so treat it as an option.

What the three engines share is that "all of them need money." Cloud needs capex, Robotaxi needs vehicles and operating investment, AI applications need R&D. **And the source of cash (advertising) is shrinking.** This mismatch means the company's free cash flow will most likely stay negative for the next two years, and that shareholder returns will be hard to raise materially.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape: Four Battlegrounds, One Supporting Player

**The compute battlefield.** Alibaba Cloud ranks first in scale with the lowest unit cost; Volcano Engine has ByteDance's cash flow and in-house model demand, so it can fight without making money; the three telecom operators' clouds have power and government-enterprise relationships. Baidu AI Cloud, at RMB 8.2 billion per quarter, is the fastest grower in the third tier (+94%), but the scale gap is obvious. This battle turns on capex and electricity prices, not technology.

**The model battlefield.** On the consumer side, Doubao (ByteDance), Quark (Alibaba) and Yuanbao (Tencent) have captured mindshare; Ernie is the one that "launched first but is not the easiest to use." The enterprise side remains a melee, with model API prices driven below cost and sold as bundled "model + cloud + private deployment" packages—which favors Baidu, with its cloud and chips, but generates no profit.

**The application battlefield.** WeChat is the social entry point, Douyin the content entry point, Doubao the Q&A entry point; Baidu has fallen from "first entry point" to "fallback entry point." Its 604 million MAU remain, but online marketing is -22%, showing that "the people stayed, the intent left." This battle is already lost.

**The Robotaxi battlefield.** Globally the players are Waymo and Baidu; domestically, Pony.ai, WeRide and Didi. Baidu's edge is operating area and data volume; Waymo's edge is per-vehicle technology and high-ticket markets. **The deciding factor is not technology but the speed of removing safety drivers and the speed of reaching per-city break-even.** The most dangerous competitor is still ByteDance's Doubao—it does not take Baidu's customers, it makes the act of "searching" unnecessary.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

① **Advertising decline accelerates (30% probability, high impact).** If the Q4 year-on-year decline widens beyond -25%, total revenue turns negative by more. The decline is self-reinforcing: advertisers leave → ad inventory quality falls → user experience deteriorates → more users shift to AI → fewer ad slots.

② **AI cloud price war (40% probability, high impact).** New capacity comes online in 2027, giving Alibaba Cloud and Volcano Engine an incentive to clear the field on price. Baidu's RMB 8.2 billion/quarter has no cost advantage; growth could fall from 94% to below 20%, and the valuation approach would need to shift from "growth + assets" to "pure assets."

③ **Depreciation squeezes gross margin (50% probability, medium impact).** This is the most certain item because the bill has already been incurred. Depreciation as a share of revenue rises from 9% to 13%, gross margin could fall below 30%, and operating profit turns negative.

④ **Lack of shareholder returns (45% probability, high impact).** No dividend for five years and buybacks below 2% of market cap mean the discount could persist long term. This risk has no trigger point—it is "nothing happens," and is the most easily underestimated by investors.

⑤ **Governance and succession (long term).** Super-voting rights + no clear No. 2 + no succession plan. ⑥ **China ADR structural risk (low probability, severe damage).** VIE/ADR-related uncertainty cannot be hedged; only position sizing can manage it.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecasts

The forecast uses a bottom-up sum-of-the-parts method with four core assumptions: first, online marketing declines 15% year on year, to RMB 47.6 billion in 2026E and RMB 40.5 billion in 2027E; second, AI cloud reaches RMB 50 billion in 2027 (up 52% from RMB 32.8 billion in 2026E); third, AI applications +15% (RMB 12 billion) and AI-native marketing -6% (RMB 9 billion); fourth, consolidated businesses such as iQIYI are broadly flat (about RMB 23.5 billion).

Summing these gives total revenue of RMB 123.6 billion in 2026E and RMB 135 billion in 2027E, up 9.2% year on year.**All of this growth comes from AI cloud; traditional businesses remain a negative contribution.**Excluding AI cloud, the remaining businesses total RMB 85 billion in 2027E, 6% less than RMB 90.8 billion in 2026E.

On profit: assuming gross margin holds above 32% and expense ratios stay at current levels (sales and administrative plus R&D together at 33% of revenue), non-GAAP net profit is RMB 9 billion in 2026E and RMB 10 billion in 2027E, corresponding to EPS of HKD 3.74 and HKD 4.16 (roughly, on 2.64 billion shares and HKD 1 ≈ RMB 0.911).

Sensitivity:**each 1 percentage point decline in gross margin cuts 2027E net profit by about RMB 1.35 billion (-13%).**If gross margin falls below 30%, 2027E net profit would need to be cut to below RMB 7.5 billion, and the corresponding neutral valuation would drop from HKD 85 to around HKD 70. This is the assumption in this report that most needs watching.

## Chapter 10 Valuation: SOTP fair value HKD 85

Baidu is not suited to PE valuation: the 2026 PE of 19.7x is distorted by last year's RMB 16.19 billion impairment, rendering the denominator unreliable; nor is PS suitable: advertising and cloud are lumped together, and the two businesses warrant completely different multiples; EV/EBITDA is even worse—net cash is so large that EV is close to negative. The only appropriate method is SOTP.

Component one, net cash and investments. Gross RMB 276.6 billion; after deducting interest-bearing debt and applying a 50% haircut to long-term equity investments, take RMB 160 billion, corresponding to RMB 60.6 per share, or about HKD 66.5. Component two, core business. 2027E net profit of RMB 10 billion × 12x = RMB 120 billion, corresponding to RMB 45.5 per share, or about HKD 49.9 (12x is a reasonable multiple for "zero-growth internet + AI optionality"). Component three, Robotaxi optionality RMB 0–10 billion, or HKD 0–4.2 per share.

The three components sum to HKD 116–121, **then apply a 30% holding-company discount to arrive at HKD 81–85.** The rationale for the discount is concrete: the cash sits with management, no dividends are paid, and minority shareholders cannot influence allocation—this portion of value is "visible but unreachable."

Three-scenario cross-check: **bear case HKD 45** (advertising fails to stabilize, net cash after discount RMB 120 billion, core business 8x, 40% discount); **base case HKD 85** (as above); **bull case HKD 125** (core business 18x, Robotaxi RMB 10 billion, 15% discount). Weighted at 30/50/20 probabilities, the expected value is HKD 81, +27% versus the current price of HKD 63.55. Cross-checked on PB: 0.52x corresponds to net assets of RMB 294.2 billion; if the discount narrows to 0.7x, market cap would be about RMB 206 billion, or about RMB 78 per share (roughly HKD 86), consistent with the SOTP base case.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendation: Watch

Rating **"Watch, wait for inflection"**, 12-month target price HKD 85, implying 34% upside from the 2026/11/23 close of HKD 63.55. The reason for not assigning "Buy" is not that valuation is insufficiently cheap, but that catalysts are absent: advertising -22%, cloud segment profit not yet positive, dividend not yet implemented. **If any one of the three conditions improves, the rating moves up; if none improve, 0.52x PB can hold for two years.**

Trigger conditions (any one met allows building a 3% position): ① online marketing year-on-year decline narrows to within -10%, confirmed for two consecutive quarters; ② AI cloud exceeds RMB 10 billion in a single quarter and segment operating profit turns positive; ③ annual dividend yield above 2%, or buyback authorization expanded to over 5% of market cap. If two conditions are met simultaneously, the position can be increased to 5%–6%.

Falsification conditions (any one triggered means exit): advertising decline widens to beyond -30%; gross margin falls below 30%; single-quarter capex returns above RMB 10 billion with cloud growth below 40%; by mid-2027 there is still no dividend or stepped-up buyback. **No price stop-loss**—using a price stop-loss on a stock at 0.52x PB is equivalent to selling assets to the market at a discount.

Portfolio implications: this is an "asset-type option," highly correlated with Tencent and Alibaba (roughly 0.7–0.8), and on a combined basis should not exceed 15% of the portfolio. Single-name cap 6%, and only to be used after signals are confirmed.
