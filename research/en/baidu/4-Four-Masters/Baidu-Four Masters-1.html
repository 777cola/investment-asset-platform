Four Masters' Consensus

Four investors look at the same set of financials and split into two camps. Duan Yongping, Buffett and Munger all concede it is "cheap", but none of them considers it a good business: Duan Yongping would ask whether search will still be around in ten years, Buffett would work out how much profit is left after three years if advertising shrinks 20% a year, and Munger would simply map the four value-trap traits onto it. Li Lu is the only one leaning positive — he values the 390 million km of data assets behind Apollo Go, though he does not think Baidu is the only winner in the AI era either. Not one of the four says "buy now" — hence "watch". Three scenarios of HKD 45/85/125, probability-weighted HKD 81, +27%, realized over 2–3 years, an annualized 8%–12%, not enough.

Duan Yongping: when something looks cheap, first ask why it is cheap

Duan Yongping applies a practical test: if a company is cheap enough to "work out on paper," the business is usually deteriorating. Baidu fits that description exactly — a market cap of about RMB 153 billion against RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments. Anyone can do the math. But advertising revenue is -22%, which shows users' information habits have changed. The cheapness you can calculate rests on a decline you cannot.

He would concede that AI cloud is real growth: RMB 8.2 billion, +94% YoY, GPU cloud +191%, and that pace is not built on subsidies. But he would press one question: where is the moat? Selling compute is essentially selling electricity — anyone with the cards can sell it, and differentiation is minimal. Duan wants businesses "others cannot do" — Moutai's brand, Apple's ecosystem, Tencent's social graph. Baidu Cloud is not on that list.

He would also look at "integrity": is management doing the right thing? Robin Li keeps more than RMB 200 billion of cash on the balance sheet, buybacks below 2% of market cap, and a dividend that may only be announced at year-end — in Duan's framework, that is not being fully accountable to shareholders. His line would be roughly: "At this price I would take a look, but I would not buy. I cannot see what makes money ten years from now."

Buffett: a cigar butt offers one last puff, and then it's gone

Buffett would start with the balance sheet: RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments, 0.52x price-to-book, and operating cash flow still positive at RMB 2.8 billion. This is the classic "cigar butt" setup—one last puff left, but once you smoke it, it's gone. He learned that lesson the hard way with the Berkshire textile mill in his younger years, and only later worked it out: with a cheap bad business, time is on the other side.

He would run the numbers: advertising shrinks 20% a year, so RMB 11.9 billion becomes RMB 6.1 billion in three years; AI cloud, even sustaining 40% growth, reaches RMB 22.5 billion in three years, but at only 20%–30% gross margin that is RMB 4.5–6.8 billion of gross profit—not enough to offset the roughly RMB 4.1 billion of gross profit lost from advertising (RMB 5.8 billion of revenue × 70% gross margin). Total revenue may hold steady while gross profit keeps falling. The arithmetic is identical to what he faced when he shut the textile mill in 1985.

He would also offer a fair verdict: this is not a "value destroyer" but a "value staller." Operating cash flow is positive, it is not living on borrowed money, and the assets are real. "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre"—Baidu now sits squarely on that line, and which way it tips depends on whether management is willing to return the cash to shareholders.

Munger: The four traits of a value trap — all present

Munger would invert the problem: "If I were a short seller, how would I pitch this company?" He would list the four classic traits of a value trap and tick them off one by one.

  • Looks cheap: 0.52x PB, net cash at 1.8x market cap. Check.
  • Business in structural decline: advertising -22%, and not a cyclical one — users have stopped clicking links. Check.
  • Management explains everything with "transformation": AI cloud +94%, Apollo Go in 30 cities — a good story, but no profit. Check.
  • Minimal shareholder returns: USD 373 million of buybacks year-to-date, under 2% of market cap, and never paid a dividend. Check.

All four hit. In his words, that is a "lollapalooza" — multiple factors stacking in the same direction. But he would also offer a way out: if management is willing to return cash to shareholders, the value trap becomes a value stock. A first dividend is a signal; the key is whether it is a real payout or a token one. That is the single thing most worth watching over the next two quarters — more important than AI cloud growth.

Li Lu: The migration of entry points is a civilisation-level shift, and Baidu will not be the only beneficiary

Li Lu would take a longer historical view. Search was one of the best business models of the PC internet era because it monopolised "intent"—when users want to buy something, go somewhere or learn something, their first move is to open a search box. But AI has turned "intent" into "conversation," and the gateway has shifted. This is not product iteration; it is a civilisation-level change in how people interact: from "people finding information" to "information finding people."

He would pinpoint the essence of Baidu's problem: not a lack of technology, but the defence of a gateway that is being bypassed. The 604 million MAU is real, but its value depends on what users bring with them—MAU that arrives with intent is valuable; MAU that comes merely to scroll the feed is not. That distinction does not show up in the financials, but users know it.

He would also flag the undervalued part: Apollo Go across 30 cities, 34 million kilometres in a single quarter, 390 million cumulative kilometres—the world's largest real-world Robotaxi operating data asset. If autonomous driving ultimately pans out, this asset is currently priced at zero. But how long "ultimately" takes, no one knows—in Li Lu's framework, such assets should be priced as options, not via DCF. His conclusion would be: AI is real, but Baidu is not the only winner, and not even the main one.

Odds assessment: expected value +27%, though time cost absorbs half

The three scenarios carry 30%/50%/20% weights: 0.3×45 + 0.5×85 + 0.2×125 = HKD 81, 27% above HKD 63.55. That looks attractive, but it has to be discounted for time — SOTP value release needs confirmation from the financials, and at the fastest that takes 2–3 years (with AI cloud completing a full capex cycle in 2027). Spread 27% over three years, that is 8.4% annualised; over two years, 12.9%.

So the risk-reward is not bad, but not good enough. The real opportunity cost sits here: for the same HKD 63.55, Tencent offers the certainty of social cash flow, Alibaba the elasticity of a cloud re-rating, and Baidu "asset discount + transformation option." Risk-reward across the three is similar; certainty differs by a notch.

The conditions for the risk-reward to turn "good" are specific: if the advertising decline narrows to within -10%, the probability of the neutral scenario rises from 50% to 65%, expected value rises to HKD 88 (+38%), annualised above 12%; if the first dividend lands and buybacks step up, the probability of the pessimistic scenario falls from 30% to 15%, expected value reaches HKD 90. Neither variable requires AI to win, only for management to act.

Conversely, the conditions for deterioration are equally clear: if the Q4 advertising decline widens to -25%, the neutral scenario must be revalued to HKD 70, the pessimistic scenario to HKD 38, and expected value falls below HKD 60 — at that point it is not "cheap," it is "the decline is not over." The key to this stock is therefore not valuation, but the direction of the trend.

Conclusion

Four masters deliberate: three call it cheap, none says buy now. Duan Yongping cannot see what the business looks like a decade out; Buffett calculates that gross profit is still falling; Munger finds all four value-trap traits present. Li Lu alone sees option value—but options need time, and time is the enemy of a mediocre business. Three-scenario expected value HKD 81, +27%, 8%–12% annualized, trailing the opportunity cost. Rating maintained at "Watch, wait for the inflection": wait for the advertising decline to narrow to within -10%, or for a first dividend and stepped-up buybacks to land. Only when one of the two appears are the odds sufficient.
