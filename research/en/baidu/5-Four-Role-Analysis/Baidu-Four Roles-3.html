Core view

Baidu sits at the intersection of three pools, each at a completely different temperature. Search advertising is a shrinking pool — not because the industry is contracting, but because the entry point is migrating; AI cloud is an expanding but extremely crowded pool — Alibaba Cloud, Volcano Engine, Tencent Cloud and the three telecom operators are all in it; Robotaxi is a pool that has yet to take shape — the technology works, the business model does not. In none of the three pools is Baidu the strongest player.

Search advertising: the industry isn't shrinking, the entry point is shifting

Munger would first correct a common misjudgment: China's internet advertising market has not collapsed; advertisers' budgets are still growing, they have simply shifted from the search box to short video and feed. Baidu's online marketing -22% is share loss, not industry decline. The distinction matters—an industry decline can wait for the cycle to turn; share loss does not come back.

The more troublesome part is the mechanism: AI search gives answers directly, so users do not need to click links. The traditional search business model rests on "ten blue links," each one an ad slot. An answer page has no links, so it has no ad slots. That is why AI-native marketing is only RMB 2.4 billion and still -15%—the new ad format has yet to find a vehicle that can replace the old ad slots.

AI cloud: demand is real, but this is a battle of scale

There is no problem with demand for AI cloud. Industry-wide capex rises in 2026, GPU cloud supply falls short of demand, and Baidu's GPU cloud revenue of +191% and +43% QoQ is the evidence. The problem is on the supply side: cloud is a scale business; scale determines unit cost, and unit cost determines whether you can fight a price war. Alibaba Cloud's scale is an order of magnitude larger than Baidu's, Volcano Engine has ByteDance's cash flow and its own model demand, and the three carriers have power and data centers.

Baidu has only two points of differentiation: Kunlun chips (in-house accelerator cards that ensure supply security and lower unit cost) and full-stack integration (chip-cloud-model-application as one). Technically these are advantages; commercially they are not enough — customers buy cloud on price, stability and ecosystem, not on whether you are "full-stack." So the RMB 8.2 billion/quarter, +94% figure can hold in the short term, but is questionable in the long term.

Robotaxi: technology leads, business unproven

Apollo Go is Baidu's scarcest asset: operations in 30 cities, 34 million km of autonomous driving in a single quarter, 390 million km cumulative. Only Waymo globally is comparable on this data volume; in China there is no rival.

But Munger would ask a blunt question: how much do you make per kilometer? Safety drivers are still in the cars, and regulators require a human-to-vehicle ratio of no less than 1:3, meaning one person watches three cars. Safety-driver wages, remote-takeover center costs, vehicle depreciation and insurance all spread across each kilometer, and at this stage gross margin is most likely negative. Technological leadership does not equal a viable business — Waymo has run for over a decade and is still burning cash. So this asset should be priced as an option, not as a business. At the current share price, the market assigns it a value close to zero, which is both an opportunity and a sign of market rationality.

Cycle position: capex at the peak, depreciation pressure mid-slope

2026 is a big year for AI capex, with the whole industry buying chips and building data centers. Baidu's quarterly capex fell from RMB 11.4 billion in the second quarter to RMB 7.3 billion in the third quarter — a deliberate pullback. The move shows management has recognized: invest further, and depreciation arrives first; revenue may not arrive at all.

In terms of where the industry cycle sits, compute supply will most likely be tightest from late 2026 to the first half of 2027; after that, new capacity comes online and prices drift lower. For a player without scale advantages like Baidu, falling prices mean gross-margin pressure. So the time window for the "high-growth AI cloud" story is roughly 4–6 quarters.

Conclusion

Industry-view conclusion: Of the three tracks, Baidu is the weak player in two and the leader in one but has not monetized it. Search advertising's share loss is irreversible, AI cloud's scale disadvantage is hard to fix, and only Robotaxi has a genuine first-mover advantage. Its industry position caps the company's ceiling — it is not the protagonist of the AI era but a supporting player with an option. Corresponding valuation: a low multiple for the core business plus option value for Robotaxi; the sum is the SOTP neutral scenario of HKD 85.
