Core view

Baidu now runs two entirely different businesses at once. One is dying: search advertising at RMB 11.9 billion, down 22% YoY, high gross margin, asset-light, with hundreds of thousands of SMEs as customers. The other is growing: AI cloud at RMB 8.2 billion, up 94% YoY, low gross margin, asset-heavy, with enterprises and developers as customers. Together they total RMB 30.807 billion, down 1.1% YoY. The critical commercial question is not which business is better, but whether the new business earns enough to cover the old business's losses.

Revenue mix: two opposing curves that offset each other

Baidu's RMB 30.807 billion this quarter breaks into four revenue lines: online marketing RMB 11.9 billion (-22%), AI cloud infrastructure RMB 8.2 billion (+94%), AI applications RMB 2.6 billion (roughly flat) and AI-native marketing RMB 2.4 billion (-15%). Together these give AI business of RMB 13.2 billion, or 53% of general business.

Of the four, only AI cloud is genuinely growing, two are shrinking (traditional advertising and AI-native marketing) and one is stalled (AI applications). Total revenue of -1.1% is simply the net of these four lines — not "stabilised", but one riser just offsetting three non-risers. That balance is fragile: a 20-point slowdown in cloud growth would immediately push total revenue to -5%.

Unit economics: RMB 100 of ads and RMB 100 of cloud leave very different margins

Duan Yongping starts with unit economics when assessing a business. On RMB 100 of advertising revenue, more than RMB 70 of gross profit remains after traffic acquisition costs, with almost no capex required. On RMB 100 of cloud revenue, RMB 20–30 of gross profit remains after depreciation, electricity and data centre costs (rough calculation), and the cards must be bought upfront — capex of RMB 7.3 billion this quarter against RMB 8.2 billion of cloud revenue, meaning every RMB 1 of cloud revenue requires RMB 0.9 of assets invested first.

The commercial meaning of "AI cloud +94%" is therefore entirely different from that of "advertising +94%".The former trades capital for revenue; the latter trades traffic for cash. Using cloud growth to offset advertising decline keeps reported revenue flat while free cash flow is negative — negative RMB 4.5 billion this quarter (rough calculation).

Customers and pricing power: from selling intent to selling electricity

Search advertising sells "intent": users actively type in keywords and advertisers bid for that intent. It is the most expensive traffic commercially because intent is scarce, priceable and attributable. AI cloud sells "compute hours", which is essentially no different from selling electricity: commoditised, price-comparable, and customers can switch suppliers at any time.

The gap in pricing power shows up in gross margin: above 70% for advertising, 20%–30% for cloud. Baidu's commercial predicament can be summed up in one sentence: it swapped the most expensive traffic business for the cheapest compute business. The swap is also incomplete: advertising still contributes 39% of revenue and most of the profit, while cloud is still burning cash for scale. One leg is shrinking, the other is building muscle, and the walk is lopsided.

Switching costs: advertisers are leaving, and so are cloud customers

Advertisers face near-zero switching costs: budgets can move to Douyin, Xiaohongshu or WeChat within a single day. Cloud customers face higher switching costs (migration, re-architecture, data), but AI cloud in particular is low—model training jobs can run in batches across different clouds, whichever is cheaper. Customer stickiness is therefore eroding in both businesses, just at different speeds.

What does have real stickiness is the consumer-facing gateway: an app with 604 million MAU where, once user habits form, switching costs are high. But the gateway itself is depreciating—users remain, behaviour has changed. This is the state Duan Yongping least wants to see: the visible asset is still there, the invisible habit is gone.

Conclusion

Business conclusion: this is not a good business worth paying a premium for, nor a bad one that deserves liquidation. The cash flow and the assets are still there; it is the way it makes money that is heading lower. Duan Yongping's verdict would be "understandable, unappealing, worth a look if the price is low enough", and the corresponding action is to watch rather than buy. To change that judgment, AI-native marketing needs to stop declining—that would signal a new advertising format has proven out, rather than leaving cloud to prop up revenue.
