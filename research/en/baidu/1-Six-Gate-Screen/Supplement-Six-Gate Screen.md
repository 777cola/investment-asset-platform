# Baidu（9888.HK）— Six-Gate Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/11/23
- Price：63.55 HKD
- Market cap：Approximately HKD 168 billion (approximately USD 21.5 billion)

## Notes

Scoring method: each of the six gates is scored 0–10; 6 and above is a pass; "doubtful" counts as 0.5 of a gate; composite score = sum of the six gates (37/60). Data basis: segment revenue from the 2026Q3 financial report (released 2026/11/16); market cap of HKD 168 billion roughly calculated as HKD 63.55 × about 2.64 billion shares (including Class A/B, 1 ADS = 8 shares); RMB conversion at HKD 1 ≈ RMB 0.911. Gross margin of 35.4% is roughly calculated as (revenue 308.07 − cost of sales 199)/308.07. Cloud gross margin of 20%–30% is an order-of-magnitude estimate; the company does not disclose segment gross profit separately.

## Gate one: circle of competence — the business is understandable, the variables are not

All three businesses have a clear way of making money: search advertising sells traffic by click and impression, AI cloud sells compute by the hour, and Apollo Go sells transport capacity by the mile.But "understanding the business" and "understanding this company's future" are two different things.The real difficulty is answering one question: when users stop clicking links and instead read the answer the AI gives them directly, where is the ad slot, what is the unit price, and what is the conversion rate?

Baidu itself is still testing this question. AI-native marketing came in at RMB 2.4 billion for the quarter, down 15% year on year, which shows the new ad format has yet to prove itself — not slow growth, but negative growth. So I give it 8 points rather than 10:the shape of the business is understandable; the migration of the business is not.The boundary of the circle of competence lies right here, and judging beyond that line is a gamble.

## Stage two: a good business — trading high gross margin for low gross margin

Search advertising was once the best business on the Chinese internet: near-zero marginal cost, gross margins above 70%, almost no capex, hundreds of thousands of SME customers, prepayment, no receivables. Now that business is shrinking: online marketing RMB 11.9 billion, -22% YoY, falling from over 70% of total revenue a few years ago to 39%.

AI cloud is picking up the slack: RMB 8.2 billion, +94% YoY, with GPU cloud +191% and +43% QoQ. The numbers look good, but the economics of cloud are entirely different — you buy cards, rent data centers and pay for power first, then sell capacity by the hour. Cost of sales this quarter was RMB 19.9 billion, +9% YoY, compressing overall gross margin to 35.4% (rough calculation: (308.07−199)/308.07). On the same RMB 100 of revenue, advertising leaves RMB 70 of gross profit; cloud gross margin is only in the 20%–30% range (rough calculation), and requires billions in capex upfront.

So this is not "the business got worse" — it is "the business changed, and changed into a worse one." Shifting the revenue mix from advertising to cloud means giving up both high margin and asset-light economics at once. Score: 4.

## Stage three: the moat — not breached, but bypassed

Baidu still holds four things that are hard for others to replicate: an app gateway with 604 million MAU, two decades of accumulated Chinese search corpus and user intent data, compute supply security from Kunlun chips, and Apollo Go's operations across 30 cities with 390 million cumulative autonomous driving kilometers.

The problem is that a moat is defined as "something rivals cannot get around." Today, ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Quark and Tencent's Yuanbao are not attacking search head-on—they are changing user habits: people ask AI directly instead of opening a search box. The riverbed remains, but the water has changed course. The 604 million MAU is real, but MAU does not equal gateway value; gateway value depends on "users arriving with intent," and intent is flowing to the chat box.

The score is 5 rather than lower because Apollo Go is genuinely scarce: 30 cities and 34 million autonomous driving kilometers in a single quarter make it one of the broadest Robotaxi operations globally. No one can catch up with this moat in the short term—it just has not yet turned into profit.

## Stage four: management — right direction, low returns

Robin Li's AI judgment ran five years ahead of most peers: he set up the Institute of Deep Learning in 2013, built ERNIE in 2019, went all in on large models in 2023, and from 2024 pushed Kunlun chips, AI Cloud and Apollo Go on three fronts at once. Execution is no slower — AI cloud infrastructure did RMB 8.2 billion in a single quarter, +94%, a growth rate that is fast among clouds with annualized revenue above RMB 30 billion.

The deduction is for capital allocation. Buybacks of USD 373 million during the year amount to less than 2% of the USD 21.5 billion market cap; the company holds RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments, 1.8 times its market cap, yet has never paid a dividend, with the first payout still stuck at "expected to be announced before year-end" (Hong Kong Economic Journal, 2026/11/15). A company whose net cash exceeds its market cap, buying back less than 2% of itself — that is not conservatism, that is a choice. Management's choice is to keep the money on the balance sheet and keep investing in AI. That choice is understandable, but it turns "cheap" into "cheap for a long time." Score: 6.

## Gate 5: Margin of safety — the market prices the core business at close to zero

This one is the easiest to calculate. Market cap of HKD 168 billion, converted at the 63.55 HKD exchange rate to about RMB 153 billion; cash and investments of RMB 276.6 billion, PB 0.52x. Even if you halve the RMB 276.6 billion of long-term equity investments and deduct all interest-bearing debt, the remaining net amount still exceeds the current market cap—in other words, the market is pricing AI cloud, search and Apollo Go at zero, or even negative.

A PB of 0.52x is an extreme among Hong Kong internet stocks. Tencent currently trades above 3x PB, Alibaba above 1.5x (rough calculation, based on Hong Kong quotes and the latest net assets). Baidu is not "cheaply valued"—its assets are being discounted. Score: 8.

The boundary condition must be stated clearly: this margin of safety holds only on the premise that the cash is not burned. Capital expenditure this quarter was RMB 7.3 billion, operating cash flow RMB 2.8 billion, free cash flow -RMB 4.5 billion (rough calculation). If the cloud business keeps expanding, the cash turns from a "safety cushion" into "fuel".

## Gate six: discipline — the buy thesis must be verifiable

This test is about yourself, not the company. The easiest reason to slide into buying Baidu is "China ADRs have fallen for so long, they will eventually rebound" — that is a macro bet, unverifiable, and impossible to set a stop-loss on. Only two verifiable reasons exist: the year-on-year decline in advertising narrows to within -10%, or AI cloud quarterly revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion and segment operating profit turns positive. Both are hard metrics visible in quarterly earnings.

I frame these two as trigger conditions, not as "long-term bullish on AI." Score 6: the discipline framework is clear, but execution is easy to break — because the stock is too cheap, and cheapness itself is the biggest temptation.

## Veto items and items to be verified

Disqualifier: the core business is shrinking. Online marketing has fallen double digits for several consecutive quarters (-22% this quarter), and AI cloud growth only just offsets it, leaving total revenue -1.1% YoY and non-GAAP net profit -36% YoY. Cheap cannot offset decline; it can only price it in — this is the one of the six tests that can single-handedly veto a "buy".

- Ad decline: whether Q4 narrows to within -10% or deteriorates further to -25%. This is the single most important metric.

- Cloud segment profit: management says "the payback period on AI cloud investment is shortening" (earnings call, 2026/11/15); we need specific figures showing the cloud segment turning an operating profit, not the overall adjusted operating margin of 5.6%.

- Shareholder returns: whether the first dividend lands before end-2026 and whether 2027 buybacks step up to the USD 1 billion level.

- Apollo Go per-ride economics: safety drivers are still required, with the regulator mandating a human-to-vehicle ratio of no less than 1:3, and per-vehicle break-even is unproven.
