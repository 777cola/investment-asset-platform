Six-Gate Screen — Baidu

Core thesis

Six gates 37/60, rating "Watch". The gates it passes, it passes easily; the gates it fails, it fails fatally. Gate five, margin of safety 8/10: market cap of about RMB 153.0 billion against RMB 276.6 billion of cash and investments on the books, P/B 0.52x — the market is handing over AI cloud, search and Apollo Go for free. Gate one, circle of competence 8/10: all three businesses are understandable. What actually blocks it are gate two, good business (4/10), and gate three, moat (5/10) — online marketing RMB 11.9 billion, -22% YoY, and AI cloud RMB 8.2 billion, +94%, exactly offset each other, leaving total revenue at RMB 30.807 billion, -1.1% YoY. Cheap is real, and so is the shrinking business. Cheap cannot offset decline; it can only price it, which is why this is a watch, not a buy.

Gate one: circle of competence — the business is understandable, the variables are not

All three businesses have a clear way of making money: search advertising sells traffic by click and impression, AI cloud sells compute by the hour, and Apollo Go sells transport capacity by the mile.But "understanding the business" and "understanding this company's future" are two different things.The real difficulty is answering one question: when users stop clicking links and instead read the answer the AI gives them directly, where is the ad slot, what is the unit price, and what is the conversion rate?

Baidu itself is still testing this question. AI-native marketing came in at RMB 2.4 billion for the quarter, down 15% YoY — the new ad format has yet to prove itself, and this is negative growth, not slow growth. Hence 8 points rather than 10:the shape of the business is understandable; the migration of the business is not.The boundary of the circle of competence lies right here, and judging beyond that line is a gamble.

Stage two: a good business — trading high gross margin for low gross margin

Search advertising was once the best business on the Chinese internet: near-zero marginal cost, gross margins above 70%, almost no capex, hundreds of thousands of SME customers, prepayment, no receivables. That business is now shrinking: online marketing revenue came in at RMB 11.9 billion, -22% YoY, down from over 70% of total revenue a few years ago to 39%.

AI cloud is picking up the slack: RMB 8.2 billion, +94% YoY, with GPU cloud +191% and +43% QoQ. The numbers look good, but the economics of cloud are entirely different — you buy cards, rent data centers and pay for power first, then sell capacity by the hour. Cost of sales this quarter was RMB 19.9 billion, +9% YoY, compressing overall gross margin to 35.4% (rough calculation: (308.07−199)/308.07). On the same RMB 100 of revenue, advertising leaves RMB 70 of gross profit; cloud gross margin is only in the 20%–30% range (rough calculation), and requires billions in capex upfront.

So this is not "the business got worse" — it is "the business changed, and changed into a worse one." Shifting the revenue mix from advertising to cloud means giving up both high margin and asset-light economics at once. Score: 4.

Stage three: the moat — not breached, but bypassed

Baidu still owns four assets rivals would struggle to replicate: an app gateway with 604 million MAU, two decades of accumulated Chinese search corpus and user intent data, compute supply security via Kunlun chips, and Apollo Go's footprint across 30 cities with 390 million cumulative autonomous driving kilometers.

The problem is that a moat is by definition "something rivals cannot get around." Today ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Quark and Tencent's Yuanbao are not attacking search head-on—they are changing user habits: people ask AI directly instead of opening a search box. The riverbed remains, but the water has changed course. The 604 million MAU is real, but MAU does not equal gateway value; gateway value depends on "users arriving with intent," and intent is flowing to the chat box.

We score it 5 rather than lower because Apollo Go is genuinely scarce: 30 cities and 34 million autonomous driving kilometers in a single quarter make it one of the broadest Robotaxi operations globally. No one can close that gap in the near term—it simply has not yet turned into profit.

Stage four: management — right direction, low returns

Robin Li's AI calls ran five years ahead of most peers: he founded the Institute of Deep Learning in 2013, built ERNIE in 2019, went all in on large models in 2023 and, from 2024, pushed Kunlun chips, AI Cloud and Apollo Go on three fronts at once. Execution has kept pace — AI cloud infrastructure did RMB 8.2 billion in a single quarter, +94%, fast for any cloud with annualised revenue above RMB 30 billion.

The deduction is for capital allocation. Buybacks of USD 373 million during the year amount to less than 2% of the USD 21.5 billion market cap. The company holds RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments, 1.8 times its market cap, yet has never paid a dividend, and the first payout remains stuck at "expected to be announced before year-end" (Hong Kong Economic Journal, 2026/11/15). Net cash above the market cap, and a company buying back less than 2% of itself — that is not conservatism, it is a choice. Management has chosen to keep the money on the balance sheet and keep investing in AI. Understandable, but it turns "cheap" into "cheap for a long time." Score: 6.

Gate 5: Margin of safety — the market is pricing the core business at close to zero

This is the simplest of the three to calculate. A market cap of HKD 168 billion converts at 63.55 to roughly RMB 153 billion against RMB 276.6 billion of cash and investments, implying 0.52x PB. Even halving the RMB 276.6 billion of long-term equity investments and deducting all interest-bearing debt leaves a net amount above the current market cap—in other words, the market is ascribing zero, or even negative, value to AI cloud, search and Apollo Go.

A 0.52x PB is an extreme among Hong Kong internet names. Tencent trades above 3x PB and Alibaba above 1.5x (rough calculation, based on Hong Kong quotes and the latest net assets). Baidu is not "cheaply valued"—its assets are being discounted. Score: 8.

One boundary condition needs stating clearly: this margin of safety holds only if the cash is not burned. Capex this quarter was RMB 7.3 billion, operating cash flow RMB 2.8 billion and free cash flow -RMB 4.5 billion (rough calculation). If the cloud business keeps expanding, cash turns from a "safety cushion" into "fuel".

Gate six: discipline — the buy thesis must be verifiable

This test is about you, not the company. The easiest way to talk yourself into buying Baidu is the line that China ADRs have fallen for so long they must eventually rebound — a macro bet, unverifiable, and impossible to set a stop-loss against. Only two verifiable reasons exist: the YoY decline in advertising narrows to within -10%, or AI cloud quarterly revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion and segment operating profit turns positive. Both are hard metrics visible in quarterly earnings.

I frame these as trigger conditions, not as "long-term bullish on AI." Score 6: the discipline framework is clear, but execution is easy to break — because the stock is too cheap, and cheapness itself is the biggest temptation.

Veto items and items to be verified

Disqualifier: the core business is shrinking. Online marketing has now fallen double digits for several consecutive quarters (-22% this quarter), and AI cloud growth only just offsets it, leaving total revenue -1.1% YoY and non-GAAP net profit -36% YoY. A cheap multiple cannot offset decline; it can only price it in — this is the one of the six tests that can single-handedly veto a "buy".

  • Ad decline: whether Q4 narrows to within -10% or deteriorates further to -25%. This is the single most important metric.
  • Cloud segment profit: management says "the payback period on AI cloud investment is shortening" (earnings call, 2026/11/15); we need specific figures showing the cloud segment turning an operating profit, not the overall adjusted operating margin of 5.6%.
  • Shareholder returns: whether the first dividend lands before end-2026 and whether 2027 buybacks step up to the USD 1 billion level.
  • Apollo Go per-ride economics: safety drivers are still required, with the regulator mandating a human-to-vehicle ratio of no less than 1:3, and per-vehicle break-even is unproven.

Conclusion

Six gates 37/60, rating "Watch, wait for the inflection point". The gates passed are "assets are cheap + business is understandable"; the ones blocked are "the business is shrinking + the moat is being bypassed". Both sets of facts hold at once, so the only conclusion is to do nothing: HKD 63.55 is not a buy point, it is a watch point. Two trigger conditions are fixed in advance — advertising YoY decline narrows to within -10%, or AI cloud quarterly revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion with operating profit turning positive. If either holds, then discuss building a position; if neither holds, keep watching and do not chase a rebound at 0.52x PB.
