## Five red lines: not a cycle, but a structural shift

Taken together, the five failing metrics point to one conclusion: revenue is not growing, gross margin is being diluted, profit is shrinking, and capex is consuming cash. Revenue fell 1.1%; the cause is gross margin sliding from around 50% to 35.4% — AI cloud at RMB 8.2 billion, +94%, versus advertising at RMB 11.9 billion, -22%. Low-margin revenue is displacing high-margin revenue: total revenue holds flat, but gross profit is falling.

ROE of 1.9% is the most telling number. Net assets of about RMB 294.2 billion (backed out from market cap ÷ PB 0.52) against quarterly net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 1.395 billion, or RMB 5.58 billion annualised, divides to less than 2%. A company carrying over RMB 200 billion of assets on its balance sheet generates only a 2% return — below even government bonds. The assets are not worthless; they are simply not making money.

Free cash flow of -RMB 4.5 billion is the fifth. Operating cash flow of RMB 2.8 billion remains positive, but capex of RMB 7.3 billion leaves a RMB 4.5 billion gap. Capex was RMB 11.4 billion in Q2 and RMB 7.3 billion in Q3 — slowing, yet supporting RMB 8.2 billion of cloud revenue on RMB 7.3 billion of quarterly capex is still a poor input-output ratio.

## Two pass: net cash is the only hard asset

Both points concern the balance sheet rather than operations. Net cash and investments of RMB 276.6 billion are 1.8x the RMB 153 billion market cap, and operating cash flow of RMB 2.8 billion is 1.2x net profit of RMB 2.395 billion — so profit was not propped up by receivables.

Their significance: they turn a "bad business" into a convertible bond with a floor. Strip out the RMB 276.6 billion and Baidu is a declining company — advertising revenue down 20% a year, a cash-burning cloud business and ROE below 2% — deserving 0.3x PS rather than 0.52x PB. With the cash, downside is cushioned by cash flow and assets, while upside hinges on AI cloud and Apollo Go delivering.

## But is it fatal: not fatal, but it caps the upside

Failing five of the seven criteria does not on its own disqualify a "first-class company," nor does it rule out "investability." The distinction is one of time: a first-class company's failure is temporary; a declining company's failure is a trend. Baidu's advertising decline is a trend (user behaviour has shifted), and its cloud growth is a trend (AI capex is rising). The two trends offset each other, and no one can say which will reach an inflection point first.

The right way to use these seven criteria, then, is not to "draw a line and eliminate" but to "set a position cap." A company passing five or more of the seven can be given an 8%–10% position; one passing two can get a watch position of at most 2%, raised to 5% once trigger conditions are met, and never a heavy position. Negative screening filters out the "qualification for a heavy position," not the "qualification to buy."

## Division of labour with Liuguan: one asks whether it is worth watching, the other whether it deserves a heavy position

The six gates ask "is this worth a closer look"; the seven criteria ask "does it deserve a heavy position." Baidu scores 37/60 on the six gates, passing four, and clears only two of the seven. The two sets of scores do not conflict: the "good business" test within the six gates was already a fail, and the seven criteria merely quantify that judgment. The difference lands on "margin of safety" — the six gates award 8 points, and the corresponding "net cash/market cap 181%" in the seven criteria is the only attractive number. One thing, asked two ways, points to the same conclusion: this is not a first-rate company but a company whose assets are heavily discounted. The former rules out a heavy position; the latter means it is still worth watching.
