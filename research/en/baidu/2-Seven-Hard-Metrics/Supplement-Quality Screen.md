## Five red lines: not a cycle, but a structural shift

Taken together, the five failing metrics point to the same thing: **revenue is not growing, gross margin is being diluted, profit is shrinking, and cash is being consumed by capex.** Revenue -1.1% is the result; gross margin falling from around 50% to 35.4% is the cause — AI cloud RMB 8.2 billion, +94%, advertising RMB 11.9 billion, -22%. Low-margin revenue is replacing high-margin revenue: total revenue holds flat, but gross profit is falling.

ROE of 1.9% is the most telling number. Net assets of about RMB 294.2 billion (backed out from market cap ÷ PB 0.52), quarterly net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 1.395 billion, annualized RMB 5.58 billion, which divides to less than 2%. **A company carrying over RMB 200 billion of assets on its balance sheet generates only a 2% return** — a return below even government bonds. It is not that the assets are worthless; it is that the assets are not making money.

Free cash flow of -RMB 4.5 billion is the fifth. Operating cash flow of RMB 2.8 billion is still positive, but capex is RMB 7.3 billion, leaving a RMB 4.5 billion gap. Capex was RMB 11.4 billion in Q2 and RMB 7.3 billion in Q3 — slowing, but supporting RMB 8.2 billion of cloud revenue on RMB 7.3 billion of quarterly capex is still a poor input-output ratio.

## Two pass: net cash is the only hard asset

The two points just discussed both concern the balance sheet, not operations. Net cash and investments of RMB 276.6 billion are 1.8x the market cap of RMB 153 billion; operating cash flow of RMB 2.8 billion is 1.2x net profit of RMB 2.395 billion, showing that profit was not propped up by receivables.

The significance of these two points: **they turn a "bad business" into a convertible bond with a floor.** Without this RMB 276.6 billion, Baidu would be a declining company with advertising revenue down 20% a year, a cash-burning cloud business and ROE below 2%, deserving 0.3x PS rather than 0.52x PB. With this cash, downside is cushioned by cash flow and assets, while upside depends on AI cloud and Apollo Go delivering.

## But is it fatal: not fatal, but it caps the upside

Failing five of the seven criteria is not sufficient to reject a "first-class company," nor is it sufficient to reject "investability." The difference lies in time: **a first-class company's failure is temporary; a declining company's failure is a trend.** Baidu's advertising decline is a trend (user behavior has changed), and its cloud growth is a trend (AI capex is rising). The two trends offset each other, and no one can say which will reach an inflection point first.

So the right way to use these seven criteria is not "draw a line and eliminate," but "set a position cap." A company passing five or more of the seven can be given an 8%–10% position; a company passing two can get a watch position of at most 2%, raised to 5% once trigger conditions are met, and never a heavy position. **Negative screening filters out the "qualification for a heavy position," not the "qualification to buy."**

## Division of labor with Liuguan: one asks whether it is worth watching, the other asks whether it deserves a heavy position

The six gates ask "is this worth a closer look"; the seven criteria ask "does it deserve a heavy position." Baidu scores 37/60 on the six gates (passing four), and passes only two of the seven — the two sets of scores do not conflict: the "good business" test within the six gates was already a fail, and the seven criteria merely quantify that judgment into numbers. The difference lands on "margin of safety": the six gates award 8 points, and the corresponding "net cash/market cap 181%" in the seven criteria is the only attractive number. **One thing, asked two ways, points to the same conclusion: this is not a first-rate company but a company whose assets are heavily discounted.** The former rules out a heavy position; the latter means it is still worth watching.
