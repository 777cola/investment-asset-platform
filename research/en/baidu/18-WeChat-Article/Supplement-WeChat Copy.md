## Look at two numbers first.

Baidu's market cap is now HKD 168 billion.

Its cash plus investments on the books total RMB 276.6 billion.

Converted at the exchange rate, that market cap is roughly RMB 153 billion. In other words, the cash on the company's books exceeds the value of the entire company by RMB 120 billion.

Post a stock like this in any value-investing group and someone will reply "free money."

Yet it fell from HKD 160.5 to HKD 63.55, down 60.4%, over a full year, and nobody rushed in.

Why?

## Because it really is shrinking.

On November 16, Baidu reported its third-quarter results. Total revenue was RMB 30.807 billion, down 1.1% year on year.

That number is easy to overlook because it fell only 1 point. But break it open and you see two completely opposite curves:

AI cloud infrastructure, RMB 8.2 billion, up 94% year on year, of which GPU cloud rose 191%.

Online marketing, meaning search advertising, RMB 11.9 billion, down 22% year on year.

One is growing hard, one is disappearing fast, and the two exactly offset each other. So total revenue stands still — not because it has stabilized, but because one growing segment just barely offsets three flat ones.

The remaining two: AI applications, RMB 2.6 billion, flat; AI-native marketing, RMB 2.4 billion, down 15% year on year.

Note that last number. AI-native marketing is Baidu's own definition of "advertising in the AI era," and it is falling too. That shows the advertising problem is not campaign pacing but the ad slot itself disappearing — AI search delivers answers directly, users no longer click links, and with no links there are no ad slots.

Advertising's share of Baidu's revenue has fallen from over 70% three years ago to 39% now.

## "Turning a loss into a profit" is accounting magic

On earnings day, many headlines declared "Baidu turned profitable."

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 1.395 billion, versus a loss of RMB 11.232 billion a year earlier. On the face of it, a dramatic reversal.

But last year's RMB 11.2 billion loss included an RMB 16.190 billion impairment on long-lived assets. One-off, and absent this year. So the "turnaround" was not earned back — last year's hole was simply too deep.

Strip out one-off items and look at non-GAAP net profit: RMB 2.395 billion, down 36% year on year.

Now operating profit: RMB 630 million, a 2.0% margin. For a company with annual revenue on the order of RMB 120 billion, a quarter of operations yields just RMB 600 million.

Gross profit of RMB 10.9 billion was 94% consumed by RMB 5.5 billion in sales and administrative expenses and RMB 4.8 billion in R&D, leaving RMB 600 million.

And a bill is coming due: depreciation and amortization now runs at RMB 2.8 billion a quarter, 9% of revenue. Over the past two quarters the company spent RMB 11.4 billion and RMB 7.3 billion on chips and data centers. That spending will convert into depreciation over the next two to three years, weighing on gross margin.

## Three reasons it is cheap, each a hard one

First, the core business is shrinking. Advertising falls 20% a year. AI cloud is growing, but gross margin is only 20%–30%—it is a capital-intensive business of selling compute. Trading low-margin revenue for high-margin revenue leaves total revenue flat while gross profit shrinks.

Second, the assets generate no return. Net assets are about RMB 294.2 billion, annualized net profit RMB 5.58 billion, ROE 1.9%. That return is worse than government bonds. So 0.52x PB is not the market miscalculating—it is the market saying: "I believe these assets exist, but I don't believe they can make money."

Third, management is not returning cash. The company has never paid a dividend. This year it bought back USD 373 million against a market cap of USD 21.5 billion—under 2%. The first dividend remains "expected to be announced before year-end."

A company with net cash close to twice its market cap buys back under 2% and pays no dividend for five years. That is not conservatism; it is a choice. Management chooses to keep the cash on the balance sheet and keep investing in AI. The choice is understandable, but it turns "cheap" into "cheap for a long time."

## On November 23, one telling detail

On November 20, global tech stocks plunged, with Nvidia down 14% in a single day. On November 23, Nvidia rebounded 31%.

On the same day, Baidu closed at HKD 63.55, down 0.78%.

In 2023, Baidu was "China's No. 1 AI stock" — when Nvidia rose, it rose too. That linkage has now broken.

The reason is simple: the main thread of the AI trade is compute power and model capability. Baidu's AI cloud sits in the third tier, ERNIE is being squeezed on the consumer side by Doubao and Quark, and advertising is still shrinking. It is neither a compute beneficiary nor a model winner — all that remains is the identity of "an old internet company with an AI business."

That pricing is in fact correct. Conversely, it also means that once it actually delivers profit, the re-rating upside will be substantial — because at its current share price, the AI component is zero.

## So when will it be a buy?

I'll give two conditions, both hard metrics visible in the financials, no guesswork needed.

First, the year-on-year decline in online marketing narrows to within -10% for two consecutive quarters. That means total revenue can turn positive and the advertising downcycle has stopped.

Second, AI cloud quarterly revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion and the cloud segment's operating profit turns positive. Note this is the cloud segment, not overall adjusted profit—the overall number mixes in advertising and interest, so you can't see whether cloud actually makes money.

If one appears, build a 3% position. If both appear, add to 5%–6%.

Any other reason—"it's cheap," "it's fallen too much," "the great AI era," "China ADRs will rebound"—does not count as a basis for buying. These are wishes, not logic.

As for selling: if the advertising decline widens beyond -30%, or gross margin falls below 30%, exit. No price stop-loss. Using a price stop-loss on a stock at 0.52x PB is equivalent to selling assets to the market at a discount.

## The last sentence

Baidu is not "absurdly expensive" — it has simply been "cheap for too long."

Assets give you downside protection, AI cloud gives you imagination, and the advertising decline gives you reality. All three exist at once, which makes it a "look, don't touch" name.

HKD 63.55, rating: Watch, wait for the inflection point.

Wait for advertising to stop falling, or for management to start returning cash.
