Three alternative headlines

  • "Market Cap Is RMB 100 Billion Below Net Cash — Why Nobody Wants This Stock"
  • "Baidu Down 60%: for the First Time I Can't Tell Cheap from Expensive"
  • "RMB 276.6 Billion of Cash Against a RMB 168 Billion Market Cap: That's Not an Opportunity, That's a Problem"

About 1,500 words, ready to copy and publish. Every figure is taken from Baidu's Q3 2026 report and the November 23 close — not one is an estimate.

Look at two numbers first.

Baidu's market cap now stands at HKD 168 billion.

Cash and investments on the balance sheet total RMB 276.6 billion.

At the prevailing exchange rate, that market cap equates to roughly RMB 153 billion. In other words, net cash on the books exceeds the value of the entire company by RMB 120 billion.

Post a stock like this in any value-investing group and someone will call it "free money."

Yet over a full year it fell from HKD 160.5 to HKD 63.55, down 60.4%, and nobody rushed in.

Why?

Because it really is shrinking.

Baidu reported third-quarter results on November 16. Total revenue came in at RMB 30.807 billion, down 1.1% YoY.

The headline decline of just 1 point is easy to overlook, but the underlying mix reveals two sharply divergent trajectories:

AI cloud infrastructure: RMB 8.2 billion, up 94% YoY, with GPU cloud up 191%.

Online marketing — i.e. search advertising: RMB 11.9 billion, down 22% YoY.

One is growing hard, the other shrinking fast, and the two offset each other almost exactly. Total revenue is therefore flat — not because the business has stabilised, but because a single fast-growing segment just barely offsets three stagnant ones.

The remaining two: AI applications at RMB 2.6 billion, flat; AI-native marketing at RMB 2.4 billion, down 15% YoY.

Note that last figure. AI-native marketing is Baidu's own definition of "advertising in the AI era," and it is shrinking too. The advertising problem is therefore not one of campaign pacing but of the ad slot itself disappearing: AI search delivers answers directly, users no longer click through, and without links there are no ad slots.

Advertising's share of Baidu's revenue has fallen from over 70% three years ago to 39% today.

"Turning a loss into a profit" is accounting magic

On earnings day, many headlines declared "Baidu turned profitable."

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 1.395 billion, versus a loss of RMB 11.232 billion a year earlier — on the face of it, a dramatic reversal.

But last year's RMB 11.2 billion loss included an RMB 16.190 billion impairment on long-lived assets, a one-off absent this year. So the "turnaround" was not earned back — last year's hole was simply too deep.

Strip out one-off items and non-GAAP net profit was RMB 2.395 billion, down 36% YoY.

Operating profit was RMB 630 million, a 2.0% margin. For a company with annual revenue on the order of RMB 120 billion, a quarter of operations yields just RMB 600 million.

Of RMB 10.9 billion in gross profit, RMB 5.5 billion went to sales and administrative expenses and RMB 4.8 billion to R&D — 94% consumed, leaving RMB 600 million.

And a bill is coming due: depreciation and amortization now runs at RMB 2.8 billion a quarter, 9% of revenue. Over the past two quarters the company spent RMB 11.4 billion and RMB 7.3 billion on chips and data centers. That spending will convert into depreciation over the next two to three years, weighing on gross margin.

Three reasons it is cheap, each a hard one

First, the core business is shrinking. Advertising is falling 20% a year. AI cloud is growing, but at only 20%–30% gross margin—it is a capital-intensive business of selling compute. Trading high-margin revenue for low-margin revenue leaves total revenue flat while gross profit shrinks.

Second, the assets generate no return. Net assets are about RMB 294.2 billion against annualised net profit of RMB 5.58 billion—an ROE of 1.9%, worse than government bonds. So 0.52x PB is not the market miscalculating; it is the market saying: "I believe these assets exist, but I don't believe they can make money."

Third, management is not returning cash. The company has never paid a dividend. This year it bought back USD 373 million against a market cap of USD 21.5 billion—under 2%. The first dividend remains "expected to be announced before year-end."

A company with net cash close to twice its market cap buys back under 2% and pays no dividend for five years. That is not conservatism; it is a choice. Management chooses to keep the cash on the balance sheet and keep investing in AI. The choice is understandable, but it turns "cheap" into "cheap for a long time."

On November 23, one telling detail

Global tech stocks sold off on November 20, with Nvidia down 14% on the day; by November 23 it had rebounded 31%.

Baidu closed at HKD 63.55 the same day, down 0.78%.

In 2023 Baidu was "China's No. 1 AI stock" — when Nvidia rose, it rose too. That linkage has now broken.

The reason is simple: the main thread of the AI trade is compute power and model capability. Baidu's AI cloud sits in the third tier, ERNIE is being squeezed on the consumer side by Doubao and Quark, and advertising is still shrinking. It is neither a compute beneficiary nor a model winner — all that remains is the identity of "an old internet company with an AI business."

That pricing is in fact correct. Conversely, it also means that once it actually delivers profit, the re-rating upside will be substantial — because at its current share price, the AI component is zero.

So when will it be a buy?

I'll set out two conditions, both hard metrics visible in the financials, with no guesswork required.

First, the YoY decline in online marketing narrows to within -10% for two consecutive quarters. That would allow total revenue to turn positive and would confirm the advertising downcycle has ended.

Second, AI cloud quarterly revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion and the cloud segment's operating profit turns positive. Note this is the cloud segment, not overall adjusted profit—the overall number blends advertising and interest, so it does not show whether cloud actually makes money.

If one appears, build a 3% position. If both appear, add to 5%–6%.

Any other reason—"it's cheap," "it's fallen too much," "the great AI era," "China ADRs will rebound"—does not count as a basis for buying. These are wishes, not logic.

On the sell side: exit if the advertising decline widens beyond -30% or gross margin falls below 30%. No price stop-loss. A price stop-loss on a stock at 0.52x PB is tantamount to selling assets to the market at a discount.

The last sentence

Baidu is not "absurdly expensive" — it has simply been "cheap for too long."

The assets offer downside protection, AI cloud offers the imagination, and the advertising decline is the reality. All three coexist, which makes this a "look, don't touch" name.

HKD 63.55, rating: Watch, pending an inflection point.

Wait for advertising to stop falling, or for management to start returning cash.

Conclusion

Closing: Baidu is not "absurdly expensive" — it has simply been "cheap for too long." 0.52x P/B, RMB 276.6 billion in cash, advertising -22%, AI cloud +94% — with all four numbers holding at once, the only conclusion is "watch." Wait for advertising to stop falling, or for management to start returning cash. Every number in this article can be verified; the only thing that cannot is "when things will get better."
