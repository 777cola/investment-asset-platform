Attribution conclusion

Seven developments from the past three months are worth flagging, grouped by nature into three buckets: two genuine signals (Q3 results showing advertising -22%, and the Hong Kong Economic Times report that a first dividend is near), three narrative-noise items (Apollo Go mileage, management's "shorter recovery cycle" comment, sell-side target prices) and two sector disturbances (the global AI selloff on 11/20 and the rebound on 11/23). The key takeaway is not the decline itself but the 11/23 session: Nvidia rose 31% in a single day while Baidu fell 0.78% — the market no longer prices it as an AI stock.

Bad news triggers only modest declines, while good news fails to lift the stock

Plot the seven events on a timeline and a counterintuitive pattern emerges: bad news hits and the stock does not crash; good news hits and the stock does not rise. Q2 earnings showed record capex and the reaction was muted; Q3 earnings showed negative revenue growth and the stock fell slightly; the global tech selloff on 11/20 dragged it down; on 11/23 Nvidia rose 31% and Baidu fell 0.78%.

This numbness is a classic signature of the shareholder base: those who wanted to sell finished selling months ago, and those left do not intend to sell; but no new money is willing to come in either. Average daily turnover shrinks, volatility compresses, and the stock drifts sideways near its 52-week low. This is not "it cannot fall further" — it is "nobody cares." For a name that needs a catalyst to re-rate, nobody caring is worse than falling.

Three noise items: mileage, rhetoric, target price

Apollo Go's 30 cities and 390 million km is the item most readily read as good news, yet it has no matching revenue line: Robotaxi revenue is folded into "AI applications" or another category, and the filings disclose no per-ride cost, per-vehicle revenue or gross margin. Operating metrics with no financials behind them are narrative only.

"The payback period on AI cloud investment is shortening" is management's comment on the results call — a qualitative judgment with no quantified basis. What the payback period used to be, what it is now and how it is calculated all go unstated. Statements like this anchor expectations rather than convey information.

JPMorgan's HKD 130 target price implies 105% upside to the current price, but it is the view of a single sell-side report, not a fact. Its value is as an external reference: even the most bullish sell-side house goes only to HKD 130, implying a market cap of about HKD 340 billion — still below cash plus investments — which itself shows how low the market's pricing consensus on this asset is.

Two real signals: ad decline and dividend

Only two real signals matter, and both sit in the financials and filings. The first is online marketing -22% — a continuation of multi-quarter double-digit declines — and AI-native marketing at RMB 2.4 billion, -15%, shows the new format is not picking up either. This number sets the direction of total revenue and determines whether the valuation deserves 10x or 20x.

The second is the first-ever dividend. Hong Kong Economic Times reported on 2026/11/15 that the company "expects to announce its first dividend before year-end." The market underestimates this: for a company that has never paid a dividend, the first payout signals a shift in capital allocation philosophy — from "keep the cash to invest in AI" to "return the cash to shareholders." If it materializes, the re-rating from 0.52x PB has an internal driver and does not need to wait for AI to win.

The test is simple: watch the filings, not the reports. The dividend amount, the buyback authorization size and the execution pace — all three numbers must be out before it counts.

The most notable day: November 23

Global tech stocks crashed on November 20, with Nvidia down 14% in a single day before rebounding 31% on November 23. Baidu closed at HKD 63.55 the same day, down 0.78%. That contrast carries more information than any research report: the market no longer grants Baidu "AI beta."

In 2023 Baidu was the flagship of China's AI theme — it rose with Nvidia, and it rose when OpenAI launched new models. That linkage is now broken. The reason is not hard to grasp: the main line of the AI trade is compute and model capability. Baidu's AI cloud is third tier, its models second tier, and advertising is still shrinking — it is neither a compute beneficiary nor a model winner, just an old internet company with an AI business.

That pricing is correct. Conversely, it also means that once Baidu actually delivers profit — the cloud segment turning positive, a dividend materialising — the re-rating upside would be large, because AI currently accounts for zero of its valuation.

Conclusion

Attribution: only two of the seven items are signal — advertising -22% and the approaching dividend; the rest is narrative and sector noise. The stock has flatlined at HKD 63.55, a 52-week low, not because it cannot fall further but because no new money cares. On 11/23, Nvidia rose 31% while Baidu fell 0.78% — the most direct evidence that "AI is priced at zero." Action: do not pay for narrative; wait for two verifiable signals.
