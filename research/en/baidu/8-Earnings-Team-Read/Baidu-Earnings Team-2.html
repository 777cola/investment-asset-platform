Core view

When Buffett reads financial statements, he calculates three numbers first: ROE, capex/depreciation, and where retained earnings go. Baidu's three numbers are 1.9%, 2.6x, and retained on the balance sheet. Together they form a classic bad signal: the company is using more and more capital to earn less and less money, and is not returning it to shareholders.

ROE 1.9%: RMB 290 billion in assets, under RMB 6 billion in profit

Net assets of about RMB 294.2 billion (market cap of RMB 153 billion ÷ PB 0.52, back-solved), single-quarter net profit attributable to parent of RMB 1.395 billion, annualized RMB 5.58 billion, ROE 1.9%. That number is below the 10-year government bond yield, and below bank wealth-management products.

Buffett would say: this is not "cheap," this is "capital not being used effectively." If a company's ROE sits below its cost of capital for a prolonged period, its net assets are a burden to shareholders rather than an asset—because every yuan of retained profit is destroying value. That is also why a PB of 0.52 is reasonable: the market is not marking it down, it is pricing low returns.

Conversely, if management took a third of the RMB 276.6 billion and used it for buybacks, ROE would immediately jump above 3% and PB would repair. So the problem is not the assets, it is the allocation.

Capex is 2.6x depreciation: assets are expanding, returns are diluting

Capex this quarter was RMB 7.3 billion, or RMB 29.2 billion annualized; depreciation and amortization was about RMB 2.8 billion, or RMB 11.2 billion annualized. Capex is 2.6x depreciation, meaning the asset base is still expanding rapidly, while the corresponding revenue growth was only 94% (cloud) and -1.1% (total revenue).

This is the combination Buffett warns about most: when the denominator (assets) grows faster than the numerator (profit), ROE drifts down on its own. And depreciation lags—the cards bought today gradually turn into expenses over the next three to five years. So today's 1.9% is not the bottom; next year it could be lower.

He would also certainly ask: what is the return on this capex? RMB 7.3 billion invested, against RMB 8.2 billion of cloud revenue at 20%–30% gross margin, implies a payback period of more than five years on a rough calculation. If that return is below the cost of capital, the more they invest, the more value they destroy.

Where retained profits sit: idle on the balance sheet, not returned to shareholders

Buffett has an old rule: one dollar of retained earnings must create more than one dollar of market value, otherwise it should be distributed. The market value created by Baidu's retained earnings over the past five years is negative — its share price has fallen more than 60% since its Hong Kong listing in 2021, with a market cap of HKD 168 billion against RMB 276.6 billion in cash and investments on the books.

It bought back USD 373 million, less than 2% of market cap, and has never paid a dividend. This shows management's judgment is that "money is worth more in my hands than returned to shareholders." But the ROE of 1.9% refutes that judgment. The market is also voting with a 0.52x PB: we don't believe it.

So Buffett's conclusion would be direct: this company's problem is not valuation, it is the willingness to allocate capital. If it is willing to return money, it is a value stock; if not, it is a permanent discount.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: returns on capital fail the test; capital allocation is the core issue. ROE of 1.9%, capex/depreciation of 2.6x and shareholder returns below 2% of market cap — three figures that corroborate each other: this company is using shareholders' money to expand a low-return business. The precondition for buying is not a lower share price, but a directional change in ROE or shareholder returns.
