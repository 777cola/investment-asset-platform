Core view

Baidu's 2026Q3 print was framed around "AI now more than half of revenue." That number is real, and it is the least important line in the report. Three other numbers matter more: RMB 11.9 billion, RMB 8.2 billion, RMB 630 million. The first is online marketing revenue, down 22% YoY; the second is AI cloud infrastructure, up 94% YoY; the third is operating profit, a 2.0% margin.

1. The two curves offset each other exactly, so total revenue stays flat

Total revenue came in at RMB 30.81 billion, down 1.1% YoY — a further quarter of negative growth. Two opposing curves emerge from the breakdown: AI Cloud at RMB 8.2 billion, +94%, of which GPU cloud +191% and +43% QoQ; online marketing at RMB 11.9 billion, -22%. New businesses are growing fast while the old ones are shrinking fast, and the two exactly offset each other.

That "offset" is easily misread as "stabilised." It is not. Stabilisation means every segment is flat; Baidu's reality is one growing segment offsetting three stagnant ones — AI applications at RMB 2.6 billion, flat; AI-native marketing at RMB 2.4 billion, still -15%. Only one of the four curves is genuinely growing, and the balance is extremely fragile. A 20-point slowdown in cloud growth would immediately push total revenue to -5%.

II. "Turning a loss into a profit" is an accounting phenomenon, not an operating improvement

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 1.395 billion, versus a loss of RMB 11.232 billion a year earlier. Headlines will say "Baidu turns profitable," but last year's RMB 11.2 billion loss included RMB 16.190 billion of long-lived asset impairment — a one-off. Strip out that base effect and non-GAAP net profit was RMB 2.395 billion, down 36% YoY.

Operating profit is more telling: RMB 630 million, a 2.0% margin. For a company with annual revenue on the order of RMB 120 billion, a quarter of operations yields just RMB 600 million. Gross profit of RMB 10.9 billion was 94% consumed by RMB 5.5 billion of selling and administrative expenses and RMB 4.8 billion of R&D, leaving just RMB 600 million. The structure shows operating leverage has not kicked in — with revenue flat, expenses are a rigid burden.

One cost is unavoidable: depreciation and amortization of about RMB 2.8 billion, 9% of revenue. This is the bill for the GPUs bought over the past two years, and it will keep arriving over the next 8–12 quarters. Capex was RMB 11.4 billion in the second quarter and RMB 7.3 billion in the third; year by year these numbers turn into depreciation and weigh on gross margin.

III. Market cap HKD 168 billion; net cash RMB 276.6 billion

The stock trades at HKD 63.55, 60.4% below its 52-week high of HKD 160.5, for a market cap of about HKD 168 billion (roughly RMB 153 billion). Cash and investments on the balance sheet total RMB 276.6 billion; P/B is 0.52x. Halve long-term equity investments and deduct all interest-bearing debt, and the remaining net amount still exceeds the market cap — the market is pricing the core business at close to zero.

It is genuinely cheap. The question is why, and for how long. Buybacks year to date total USD 373 million, less than 2% of the USD 21.5 billion market cap; the company has never paid a dividend, and its first payout remains at "expected to be announced before year-end." For a company whose net cash is nearly twice its market cap, buying back less than 2% is not conservatism — it is a choice. Management has chosen to keep the money on the balance sheet and keep investing in AI. That is understandable, but it turns "cheap" into "cheap for a long time."

The key to this stock is therefore not valuation but catalysts. Two signals: online marketing's YoY decline narrows to within -10%; or AI cloud quarterly revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion with operating profit turning positive. If either appears, 0.52x P/B has a reason to re-rate; if neither does, cheap can get cheaper for another two years.

Conclusion

Bottom line: Baidu is not "absurdly expensive" — it has simply been "cheap for too long." The assets provide downside protection, AI cloud supplies the narrative, and the advertising decline keeps it grounded in reality. All three hold at once, so the stance is "watch, wait for the inflection" — HKD 63.55 is a watch price, not a buy point. Wait for advertising to stop falling, or for management to start returning cash.
