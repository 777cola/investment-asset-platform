Core view

Read together, the four notes show more consensus than conflict. Three points of consensus: the revenue mix has shifted, depreciation is compressing profit, and the assets are hard. One point of conflict: whether the company should be priced on its "assets." The synthesis is itself a stress test: if four different frameworks all land on "watch," then "watch" is not ambiguity—it is the conclusion.

Consensus 1: The shift in revenue mix is structural

Four people said the same thing in four languages. Duan Yongping called it "going from collecting rent to hauling bricks"; Buffett cited "insufficient growth"; Munger noted the share gain came from a shrinking denominator; Li Lu pointed to a "migration of entry points." In numbers: advertising RMB 11.9 billion, -22%; cloud RMB 8.2 billion, +94%; total revenue RMB 30.807 billion, -1.1%.

What matters about this consensus is that it rules out a "cyclical" explanation. Were it cyclical, at least one of the four would have said "wait for advertiser budgets to recover." None of the four notes does, because the evidence does not support it: AI-native marketing came in at RMB 2.4 billion, -15%, with even new-format advertising declining.

Consensus 2: Depreciation weighs on profit, and the bottom is not yet in sight

Buffett runs the numbers on capex/depreciation at 2.6x, Munger on EBITDA adjustments at 7x, Li Lu on the price war once capacity comes online in 2027, and Duan Yongping on gross margin falling from 70% to 35%. Four men, four directions, one wall: depreciation.

Depreciation and amortisation is RMB 2.8 billion, 9% of revenue, against annualised capex of RMB 29.2 billion — over the next 8–12 quarters that RMB 29.2 billion steadily converts into depreciation. Even with no further revenue deterioration, gross margin gives up another 2–3 points. This is the hardest conclusion across the four notes, because it rests on no forecast — only on capex already spent.

Conflict: should this company be priced as an "asset"?

This is where the only substantive disagreement lies. Buffett and Munger lean toward pricing on earnings power: ROE of 1.9%, profits on a downward slope, assets worth little. Li Lu leans toward pricing on assets plus options: RMB 276.6 billion in cash, 390 million km of data, and the market assigns zero value to the core business — that is the opportunity.

Synthesis: both frameworks are right; it depends on the time horizon. Within three years, Buffett is right — profits are shrinking and the assets will not monetise themselves. Beyond five years, Li Lu may be right — if Robotaxi works, the current valuation is a giveaway. Between three and five years, management holds the deciding vote: whether they are willing to return capital determines whether the "assets" can become "returns".

Conclusion

Synthesis: This is a company whose assets are priced but whose profits are depreciated. The four notes agree enough to support "don't buy"; their disagreements are not enough to support "avoid". Hence the rating is "watch, wait for the inflection" rather than "buy" or "sell". The inflection must be defined narrowly: the advertising decline narrows to within -10%, or the cloud segment's operating profit turns positive. Both are hard signals visible in the financials, not guesswork.
