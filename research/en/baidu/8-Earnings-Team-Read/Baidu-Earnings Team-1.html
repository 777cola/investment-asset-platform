Core view

Duan Yongping reads financial reports for one thing only: is the money this business earns real, and can it last. Baidu's answer: the money is still real, but the way it is earned is being downgraded—from "advertisers pay first" to "customers pay later," from "70% gross margin" to "35% gross margin." Total revenue is unchanged; the quality has changed.

First question: is the money real money

Operating cash flow of RMB 2.8 billion against net profit of RMB 2.395 billion gives a ratio of 1.2x — the test is passed, and profit has not turned into receivables. Watch the mix, though: advertising is a prepaid business, with customers topping up before they spend, so cash flow naturally leads profit; cloud runs on payment terms, with revenue recognised before cash is collected. As cloud's share rises from single digits to 27%, this ratio will drift down.

Duan Yongping's rule of thumb: when the revenue mix shifts, first check whether the cash flow ratio deteriorates. At 1.2x it is still healthy; if it falls to 0.8x two quarters out, cloud revenue is being propped up by payment terms, and the "growth" then deserves a discount.

Second question: will this business still exist in ten years?

The financials cannot tell us what the world will look like in ten years, but they offer clues. Advertising revenue came in at RMB 11.9 billion, -22%, down double digits for several consecutive quarters; AI-native marketing was RMB 2.4 billion, -15% — even the "new advertising" is shrinking, which points not to a format problem but to a disappearance of demand scenarios. Users no longer click links, so the ad slots cease to exist.

The counter-evidence is AI cloud at RMB 8.2 billion, +94%, with GPU cloud at +191%. This line will most likely still exist in ten years, because demand for computing power is long-term; but its gross margin is only 20%–30%, and it will remain at that level in ten years — cloud is a utility, not a brand business. What Duan Yongping wants is a business that is "still around in ten years and can still raise prices." Baidu satisfies neither: advertising is disappearing, and cloud cannot raise prices.

Third question: what is management doing with shareholders' money

The report puts it plainly: RMB 276.6 billion of cash and investments, USD 373 million of buybacks during the year, and RMB 7.3 billion of capex per quarter. Three numbers, one story: plenty of money, a lot invested, very little returned to shareholders.

Duan Yongping's "benfen" standard is simple: once you make money, either invest it where it earns a return or return it to shareholders — you cannot both under-invest and withhold. Baidu today has "invested a portion, returned a little, kept a pile." That is the most awkward position of all: no high-return investment opportunity justifies the retained cash, and the retained cash is not being returned to shareholders.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style verdict: the business is still there, but the quality has degraded. Revenue, cash and assets are all real, but the way it makes money has shifted from "collecting rent" to "hauling bricks." At 0.52x P/B, such a business merits a look but neither a premium nor a long-term hold. Wait until it proves "the new advertising can stand on its own."
