Core view

When Li Lu reads financial reports, he looks for "where this company sits in the arc of civilization." Baidu's position is shifting downward: from "monopolist of the information gateway" to "one of many compute suppliers." The two most telling numbers in the report are RMB 11.9 billion (advertising, -22%) and RMB 8.2 billion (AI cloud, +94%)—one exiting the stage of history, the other entering a red ocean.

Entry migration: the most expensive line in an earnings report

Online marketing revenue of RMB 11.9 billion, -22% YoY — that single line is worth more than HKD 3 billion of market cap. It is not a one-quarter budget swing: it marks the end of "search" as an information gateway, with users no longer typing keywords but asking AI directly.

In Li Lu's framework this is a "paradigm shift": the monopolist of the old paradigm is rarely beaten by peers, it is bypassed by a new one. Baidu was not beaten by ByteDance's search; it was beaten by products such as Doubao that changed user behaviour. Its 604 million MAU is still there, but the "intent density" behind that MAU is falling — invisible in the financial statements, yet palpable in revenue.

The AI cloud window: 4–6 quarters left

RMB 8.2 billion, +94%, GPU cloud +191% — the brightest numbers in this entire piece. But Li Lu would ask: is this growth a demand dividend or a capability premium? If the former, it disappears the moment the window closes; if the latter, it is sustainable.

The evidence leans toward "demand dividend": compute was tight across the industry in 2026, and whoever had chips made money; Baidu secured part of its supply through Kunlunxin. But as new capacity comes online in 2027, prices will drift down, and the large players (Alibaba Cloud, Volcano Engine) will use price to clear the field. On this battlefield, Baidu's RMB 8.2 billion/quarter is "high growth in the third tier", not a "first-tier moat."

So the +94% in the earnings report needs a parenthetical: (window of 4–6 quarters). After that it most likely falls to 30%–40%, which is why the neutral scenario assigns AI cloud revenue of RMB 50 billion in 2027.

The asset that never shows up on the financial statements: 390 million kilometres

Apollo Go: 30 cities, 34 million km in a single quarter, 390 million km cumulative. None of these three numbers maps to a revenue line in the financial statements, because they sit inside "AI applications" or are expensed outright. On an accounting basis this asset is worth close to zero; strategically, it may be the most valuable thing Baidu owns.

This is where Li Lu would take notice: the market prices the company on an accounting basis, while civilisation-level change tends to happen where accounting cannot see. Waymo's data assets are not on its statements either, yet they are valuable. The question is when that value is realised—if the Robotaxi business model works by 2028, it comes free at Baidu's current share price; if it does not, those kilometres are R&D expense.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: a structural headwind, but with one option attached. The migration of advertising entry points is irreversible, AI cloud's high growth has a window, and the only long-term asset is the Robotaxi data that does not show up on the financial statements. Framed as "asset + option", the current 0.52x PB is the asset price and the option comes free; but the option's time value depends on financing and patience, and Baidu has both — so this is not an avoid, it is a watch.
