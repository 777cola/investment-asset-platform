# Baidu（9888.HK）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/11/23
- Price：63.55 HKD
- Market cap：Approximately HKD 168 billion (approximately USD 21.5 billion)

## Notes

This article simulates a Q&A in Duan Yongping's public investment framework (business model, differentiation, capital, Stop Doing List, don't do what you don't understand); it is not a statement by him. Data basis: 2026Q3 financials (advertising RMB 11.9 billion, -22%; cloud RMB 8.2 billion, +94%; capex RMB 7.3 billion), 2026/11/23 close HKD 63.55, market cap HKD 168 billion, PB 0.52, cash and investments RMB 276.6 billion.

## Q: Is this a good business?

"Not great. It used to be a wonderful business — search advertising, high gross margin, no capital outlay, customers pay upfront. That is the kind of business I like at first glance. **Of the two things it is doing now, one is shrinking and the other does not make money.**

Advertising came in at RMB 11.9 billion, down 22% — this is not a one- or two-year issue, user habits have changed. Cloud came in at RMB 8.2 billion, up 94%, which looks impressive, but you have to understand that selling compute is much like selling electricity: anyone with the cards can sell it, gross margin is 20-30%, and you have to buy the cards first. Capital expenditure this quarter was RMB 7.3 billion, more than it earns, and free cash flow was negative RMB 4.5 billion.

**The business model has changed, from collecting rent to hauling bricks.** Hauling bricks can make money too, but it takes hard work and thrift, and anyone else can haul them as well. I generally do not much like this kind."

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

"I can't say for sure. The company will certainly still be around—it has over RMB 200 billion in cash on the books, so it won't collapse. **But 'the company still being around' and 'the business still being around' are two different things.**

Ten years ago we said search was a gateway; now young people with questions ask AI directly—they don't search anymore. I see this change, but I don't know how fast it's moving. Maybe five years, maybe ten. Baidu's app still has 600 million monthly active users—the people are still there, but what they come for, I don't know.

Cloud should still be around in ten years—compute demand is long-term. But it will become a utility, like electricity—prices regulated, margins low. I understand this kind of business, but I don't like it—**companies that earn hard money don't get high valuations.**"

## Q: Can you understand it?

"I understand the business, I understand the accounts, but I don't understand the future. **My principle is 'don't invest in what you don't understand,' but 'understand' doesn't mean understanding the product — it means understanding how it makes money ten years from now.**

I can explain all three of Baidu's businesses clearly: how ads are sold, how cloud is billed, how Apollo Go runs. But I can't explain three things: what AI search will ultimately make money from? How far will the cloud price war go? When will Apollo Go no longer need safety drivers?

If I can't answer these three questions, I don't count as understanding it. **If I don't understand it, I don't buy it — that has nothing to do with how cheap it is.** I've seen plenty of cheap things; cheap things I don't understand always turn out expensive in the end."

## Q: At what price would you buy?

"Good question, but I won't answer with price. **Cheap is not a reason to buy; 'cheap + things getting better' is.**

The price now is HKD 63.5, market cap HKD 168 billion, cash plus investments on the books RMB 276.6 billion, price-to-book 0.52. The math is easy, and it does come out cheap — the market is essentially giving away AI cloud, search and Apollo Go for free.

But I won't buy just because it's cheap. I'll wait for one of two things: **first, advertising stops declining — say, down only within 10% year on year; second, cloud starts making money — over RMB 10 billion in a single quarter with positive operating profit.** If either one shows up, I'm willing to buy a little at this price. If both show up, I can buy a bit more.

If neither shows up, I won't buy even if it falls to HKD 45. Because what I'm buying isn't cheapness, it's 'the business has stabilized.'"

## Q: What do you fear most?

"What I fear most isn't the fall — it's 'cheap with no end in sight.'

I've seen companies like this. Plenty of cash on the books, the business slowly shrinking, management says 'we're transforming' every year, and the stock price goes lower year after year. Every time you think it has bottomed, it drops another 20%. **That isn't value investing — it's a race against time, and time is on the other side.**

The second fear is that the money gets spent. RMB 276.6 billion looks like a lot, but if annual capex runs at RMB 29.2 billion, it's gone in ten years. And what gets spent goes into buying chips and building data centers — assets that depreciate in three to five years. If the return on that investment is only 2% or 3%, then that money is slowly disappearing for shareholders.

The third fear is 'sudden improvement' — it actually makes AI work, the stock doubles in a year, and I'm not on board. That fear I accept: **missing out doesn't lose money.**"

## Q: What if I already hold it?

"For those holding it, I would ask: why did you buy in the first place?

If the reason was 'it's cheap, Chinese ADRs will rebound,' then I suggest you think it through — that is not a reason, it is a wish. **If the reason was 'asset discount + AI optionality,' then you can hold, but keep the position small — no more than 5% or 6%.**

Then set two lines for yourself: if advertising falls back below 25%, or capex climbs back above RMB 10 billion, get out, don't hesitate. This has nothing to do with the share price. **In all my years investing, the most important thing is not what to buy, but what not to do.** No leverage, no shorting, no businesses you don't understand. Baidu right now falls into 'worth watching, no need to act.'

Stay level-headed. The market offers opportunities every day; there is no need to force a rationale in a place you don't understand."
