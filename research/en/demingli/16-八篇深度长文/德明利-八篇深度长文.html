Article 01

🔐 The Memory Cycle Code: Wealth and Destruction on a 3-4 Year Rotation

The memory chip industry has a classic saying: "Three years without a sale, and one sale feeds you for three years." This seemingly exaggerated industry adage precisely captures the cruelty and allure of the memory cycle. Price swings in NAND Flash and DRAM work like a precision wealth harvester—each cycle makes some people rich overnight and leaves others wiped out.

Demingli (001309.SZ) is one of the most contested beneficiaries of the current cycle. In the first quarter of 2026, revenue surged to RMB 7.54 billion, net profit reached RMB 3.35 billion and the net margin hit 44.39% — striking figures in any industry. But to understand Demingli, one must first understand the "memory-cycle code" behind it.

The Mechanics of the Cycle

The cyclicality of memory chips stems from highly concentrated supply and pulse-like demand expansion. The NAND Flash market is an oligopoly of four players: Samsung (~35%), SK hynix (~30%), Micron (~15%) and Kioxia (~12%). Capacity decisions at these giants are highly synchronized—collective expansion during upcycles, concentrated capacity release 18-24 months later, a price collapse from oversupply, then collective cuts, and the cycle repeats. A full NAND cycle typically lasts 3-4 years.

📊 Four-stage memory cycle model:

① Recovery phase:Prices have bottomed, demand is recovering, and output cuts by manufacturers are taking effect. Gross margin has rebounded from single digits to 15-20%.

② Boom period:Supply falls short of demand, prices rise rapidly, and module makers' gross margins soar to 30-50%. Capacity is fully loaded, and players are stockpiling in anticipation of further price gains.

③ Inflection point period:Demand growth slows, new capacity from manufacturers begins to come online, and prices stop rising (the market may be at exactly this stage in Q2 2026).

④ Recession phase:Oversupply, prices collapse, module makers take inventory write-downs, gross margins fall back to single digits or even into losses.

Current cycle position

This upcycle began in 2024, entered an acceleration phase in 2025, and reached a phase peak in Q1 2026. Historical review: in the 2016-2018 supercycle, after Samsung's profit surged, memory prices plunged more than 40% in 2019. In the 2020-2022 cycle, NAND prices fell more than 50% in the second half of 2022. The current cycle has run up for about 1.5-2 years and, based on historical patterns, has entered its latter half or is even nearing a top.

Subtle shifts have emerged in leading industry indicators: some downstream customers are turning cautious in building inventory, data-center capex growth is slowing, and original manufacturers' capacity utilization is gradually recovering. Although NAND prices carried their momentum higher in 2026Q1, the pace of gains is already flattening.

Impact on Demingli

Demingli's business model is essentially "buy low, sell high"—stockpiling NAND wafers at low prices and selling them as modules at high prices. The company's gross margin was under 15% in 2025 and surged to 57.42% in 2026Q1, almost entirely driven by rising NAND prices. This means that once the cycle reverses, Demingli's gross margin could quickly fall back to 15% or even lower. The company posted a loss of RMB 28 million in 2023 and a loss of RMB 118 million in the first half of 2025—these figures remind us that on the other side of the cycle, Demingli's profitability could completely invert.

Understanding the memory cycle is a prerequisite for understanding Demingli's investment value. The current position in the cycle determines the risk-reward ratio over the next 12 months—and every signal points to one warning: the cycle is nearing its peak.

Article 02

💰 The mystery of Demingli's profitability: where does the 44% net margin come from?

RMB 3.346 billion net profit and a 44.39% net margin—put those two numbers in front of any A-share company and they draw stares. Moutai's net margin was about 52% in 2025, but Moutai's business model is "Chishui River water + brand premium + pricing power." Demingli makes storage modules, a midstream assembly business long known for thin gross margins—so how did it suddenly come to earn a margin close to Moutai's?

To solve this puzzle, we need to analyze three dimensions: the NAND price cycle effect, the inventory appreciation mechanism, and shifts in product mix.

Layer one: the multiplier effect of the NAND price-upcycle

In Demingli's cost structure, NAND Flash wafers account for more than 80% of procurement costs. When NAND prices rise 50% from a low and module product prices rise 50% in tandem, gross margin expansion is not linear—it has leverage. Put simply: if 80% of an SSD's cost is NAND wafers (assume a cost of RMB 100, with wafers at RMB 80), gross margin at the cycle trough is 15% (a selling price of about RMB 118). If wafer prices stay flat and the selling price rises to RMB 200, gross margin becomes (200-100)/200=50%. In reality, however, NAND prices rose even more from late 2024 to Q1 2026, and combined with the low-cost inventory Demingli built up at the cycle trough, this unlocked striking profit elasticity.

📊 Gross margin trajectory (modeled):

2024: gross margin 17.75% — early-cycle recovery, moderate NAND price increases

2025 (H1): gross margin 5.03% — interim report figure, possibly dragged down by product mix

2025 (full year): gross margin 14.81% — marked improvement in the second half

2026Q1: gross margin 57.42% — inventory appreciation effect released in concentrated fashion

Second layer: inventory appreciation — a hidden source of profit

Inventory surged from RMB 7.058 billion at end-2025 to RMB 12.19 billion in 2026Q1, an increase of more than RMB 5.1 billion in a single quarter. A substantial portion of these additions was NAND wafers purchased at the lower prices of 2025. When NAND prices rose sharply in 2026Q1, this low-cost inventory was processed into products and sold, generating large "inventory appreciation" gains. But note: this profit is one-off in nature—once the low-cost inventory is exhausted, newly procured wafers will carry high prices, and gross margin will naturally decline going forward.

In other words, a substantial share of the striking margins in 2026Q1 came from "time-lag arbitrage" — buying low and selling high. That arbitrage is unsustainable unless NAND prices keep rising (which requires the cycle to be extended indefinitely, something that has never happened historically).

Tier 3: Product mix upgrade — can it last?

Demingli is indeed driving structural change: enterprise SSD mix is rising, its in-house storage controller chips are ramping, and memory module gross margin holds above 25%. If these trends persist, even with NAND prices falling, the company's gross margin midpoint could rise from 15% in the past to 25-30%. But the 44% net margin in 2026Q1 is far beyond what product-mix upgrading can explain in any case — cyclical factors undoubtedly dominate.

⚠️ Core view:The 44% net margin in 2026Q1 is "a fleeting peak-of-cycle phenomenon," driven by both NAND price increases and inventory appreciation. Its sustainability is very low. The reasonable expectation: full-year 2026 net margin falls back to the 15-20% range, and in 2027, if the cycle reverses, it could return to 5-10% or even lower.

The answer to the Demingli profit mystery: it did earn real paper profits, but the driver of its windfall in 2026Q1 was the cycle, not a moat. Only when the tide goes out do we see who has been swimming naked.

Article 03

💣 Hidden inventory time bomb: RMB 12.2 billion of stockpiles at the cycle peak

At the end of March 2026, Demingli's balance sheet carried RMB 12.19 billion of inventory. What does that number mean? It equals 66.3% of total assets, 1.84x net assets and 1.13x full-year 2025 revenue. If that inventory were cash, it would be as if an entire year of the company's revenue had turned into unsold stock.

In the memory industry, inventory is never an "asset" but a "risk gauge." When the cycle turns down, NAND Flash prices often plunge far faster than companies can take impairments — NAND prices fell more than 40% within a single year in 2019 and more than 50% in the second half of 2022. Based on Demingli's current inventory scale, a 30% decline in NAND prices would generate roughly RMB 3.7 billion in inventory impairment losses. That is enough to wipe out a full year of the company's net profit (RMB 688 million for full-year 2025), and even exceeds its windfall in 2026Q1 (RMB 3.346 billion).

Stockpiling to Bet on Price Increases: A High-Leverage Gamble

Demingli's inventory growth curve closely tracks the NAND price curve. Inventory stood at RMB 7.058 billion at the end of 2025 and surged to RMB 12.19 billion in Q1 2026—an increase of RMB 5.1 billion in a single quarter. Over the same period the company added about RMB 300 million in short-term borrowings and about RMB 1.1 billion in long-term borrowings, with much of the capital used to stockpile. This is a big bet: a bet that NAND prices keep rising, and a bet that demand stays strong.

• Inventories/total assets: 66%Extremely high

• Inventory/net assets: 184%Above net assets

• Inventory turnover days: 270 days (annualized for Q1 2026)Slow turnover

• Inventory growth: +72.8% (QoQ)Sharp growth

Impairment impact under three scenarios

We stress-tested inventory impairment. If NAND prices correct modestly by 10% (base case), inventory impairment of about RMB 1.2 billion would consume 25-40% of full-year 2026 forecast profit. If prices fall 20% (bearish case), impairment of about RMB 2.4 billion could push 2026 to breakeven or a small loss. If prices plunge 30% (extreme case, not uncommon historically), impairment of RMB 3.7 billion could leave the company with a net loss of more than RMB 2 billion. Net assets per share would shrink sharply from RMB 29.20, and PB would climb further from 18x, triggering a Davis double-kill on the stock.

Why stockpile so much inventory?

The inventory level needed for normal operations is roughly RMB 3–5 billion (based on annualized revenue of RMB 30 billion and 60–90 days of turnover). The excess is "speculative inventory." Subtracting normal operating inventory from RMB 12.19 billion leaves about RMB 7 billion in speculative stockpiling. This money is not the company's own—monetary funds stand at only RMB 1.851 billion, and most of the stockpiling capital comes from borrowings. That means the company is using borrowed money to bet on NAND price increases. If the bet pays off (the cycle continues), it reaps huge profits; if the bet goes wrong (the cycle peaks), it faces a double squeeze of debt repayment plus inventory write-downs.

⚠️ Risk warning:Demingli's inventory has reached dangerous levels. Historically, memory module companies have gone bankrupt from inventory write-downs and negative cash flow more often than not. The current RMB 12.19 billion of inventory is the company's biggest hidden time bomb—if the cycle turns in the second half of 2026, the damage could far exceed market expectations.

When will this hidden time bomb go off? Key indicators to watch: whether inventory starts to decline in the 2026 interim report (signaling the company is actively destocking), whether gross margin retreats from 57% (signaling the price-hike dividend is fading), and whether operating cash flow turns positive (signaling slower depletion from stockpiling). Failure on any one of these is a warning.

💧 Cash flow truth: why did RMB 3.3 billion of net profit not turn into cash?

In the first quarter of 2026, Demingli reported net profit of RMB 3.346 billion, but operating cash flow of -RMB 240.8 million. The gap between net profit and operating cash flow reached RMB 3.587 billion. That is a signal enough to alert any value investor—profit is on paper; only cash is real.

The root of this "scissors gap" is that inventory and receivables growth has completely swallowed the company's profit growth. Put simply, the company did sell products and book revenue, but the cash never came back—it turned into more inventory in the warehouse and receivables owed by customers.

Cash flow breakdown: where did the money go?

Let us run a simple "cash flow" exercise. 2026Q1 net profit was RMB 3.346 billion, but inventory rose from RMB 7.058 billion to RMB 12.19 billion, an increase of RMB 5.13 billion. Even accounting for the rise in accounts payable (money owed to suppliers), operating cash flow remained negative. This means that for every RMB 1 of profit earned, the company must take about RMB 1.07 out of its pocket to stock inventory. This model is clearly unsustainable—the faster profit grows, the wider the cash flow gap.

📊 Cash flow comparison (RMB billion):

• 2024: net profit +RMB 351 million → operating cash flow unknown (but operating cash flow per share -RMB 7.81)

• 2025: net profit +RMB 688 million → operating cash flow-2.241 billion

• 2026Q1: net profit +RMB 3.346 billion → operating cash flow-USD 240.8 million

• Cumulative (past two years + Q1): net profit ~RMB 4.4 billion → operating cash flow ~-RMB 3.0 billion

A "boom" propped up by borrowing

Since operating cash flow is negative, where does the company's money come from? The answer is financing (borrowing). For full-year 2025, net cash flow from financing activities was an inflow of RMB 2.576 billion, and in 2026Q1 it inflow a further RMB 1.450 billion. The company's interest-bearing debt rose from RMB 4.424 billion at end-2025 to RMB 5.787 billion in 2026Q1. This means the current high growth and high profit are entirely propped up by borrowing—not generating cash, but receiving transfusions.

More interesting: in 2026Q1 the company paid interest expense of roughly RMB 70–100 million (estimated), while interest income was minimal. Net interest expense keeps rising as borrowing scales up, another drag on profit.

When will cash flow turn positive?

Turning operating cash flow positive requires one of two conditions: either stop large-scale stockpiling (inventory no longer grows), or accelerate cash collection from sales. For Demingli, flat inventory would mean management judges NAND prices will not rise much further and is entering a "destocking" phase—a major strategic shift for a management team currently extremely bullish on the cycle.

⚠️ Key takeaways:Persistently negative operating cash flow is one of Demingli's most fatal financial signals. It exposes the "inflated" nature of current profit—a large portion of profit has not yet been realized as cash but sits in high-risk inventory. If the cycle reverses suddenly, this "paper profit" could instantly become a "paper loss," while the company still has to repay borrowings. Cash flow is the "blood" of a business, and Demingli's current situation resembles "pale blood." Investors are advised to closely track cash flow data in the 2026 interim report—if operating cash flow still has not turned positive, the risk of holding the stock will escalate sharply.

🔗 CXMT and Demingli: the overrated "two-heroes linkage"

In July 2026, CXMT applied for a STAR Market IPO at a valuation of about RMB 579.2 billion, making it one of the most closely watched new listings in the A-share market this year. Meanwhile, shares of memory module leader Demingli surged in the first half of 2026, giving rise to a market narrative: "CXMT's listing benefits the domestic memory supply chain, and Demingli, as a midstream module maker, will benefit directly."

The narrative sounds plausible, but the reality is far more complex. The market has largely overstated the linkage between ChangXin and Demingli.

Different categories: DRAM vs NAND

The core issue: CXMT makes DRAM (dynamic random-access memory), while more than 90% of Demingli's revenue comes from NAND Flash-related products. These are two entirely different categories—different production processes, different suppliers, different customers and different price cycles. According to Demingli's 2025 annual report, revenue from its memory module products (DRAM modules) was only RMB 1.051 billion, or 9.74% of total revenue. So CXMT's DRAM capacity expansion can affect at most less than 10% of Demingli's business.

Demingli's flagship products—SSD (42.46%) and embedded storage (33.95%)—rely on NAND Flash wafers mainly sourced from Samsung, SK hynix, Micron and Kioxia. The company's largest supplier (34.17% of purchases) is a NAND manufacturer, not ChangXin. There is almost no direct supply-chain relationship between Demingli and ChangXin.

📊 Comparison:

• ChangXin Technology:DRAM wafer manufacturing (asset-heavy, high barriers, only 4 players globally)

• Demingli:NAND modules + controllers (midstream, asset-light, highly competitive)

• Direct linkage:Limited — only Demingli's 9.74% DRAM module business may benefit

• Indirect linkage:Both belong to the broader memory industry, but their cyclical correlation outweighs any business linkage

Future synergy potential: limited but worth watching

Over the long run, CXMT's continued DRAM capacity expansion (targeting a 17% global market share by 2028) does help improve China's memory supply-chain ecosystem. The linkage would strengthen only if Demingli raises the share of its DRAM module business, or if CXMT enters NAND (currently not planned). For now, however, the investment theses for the two names are relatively independent — investing in CXMT is a bet on the success of China's DRAM localization, while investing in Demingli is a bet on the NAND cycle continuing and on the company's own earnings power improving.

The "CXMT concept" rally in Demingli's share price is driven more by sentiment than by fundamentals. Investors should be wary of the "sector narrative" that conflates the two companies, and avoid taking a heavy position in Demingli out of optimism about CXMT, or overvaluing CXMT because of Demingli's surge.

📌 Correct interpretation:ChangXin and Demingli both sit in the memory industry, but one is DRAM wafers (ChangXin) and the other is NAND modules (Demingli). There is almost no overlap between them in the supply chain. The market's linking of the two is more a sector-speculation logic than an industrial one. The two names should be analyzed and valued independently.

Article 06

🏢 Competitors: a survival game in the module segment

The memory module industry has low barriers to entry, high upstream dependence and severe product homogenization. In this race, Demingli's competitive position can be summed up in one sentence:"Squeezed by the manufacturers above, undercut by peers below, and picked over by customers in between."

Demingli's main competitors fall into three tiers. The first tier comprises international giants: the module divisions of original manufacturers such as Samsung and SK hynix (own wafers, in-house packaging, own brands), which hold absolute supply-chain advantages and brand premiums. The second tier comprises listed domestic module makers: Longsys (301308), Biwin Storage (688525) and Netac Technology (300042), which compete directly with Demingli. The third tier comprises small and mid-sized module makers and channel distributors: hundreds of them, locked in relentless price wars.

Longsys: the module leader

Longsys is the leader in China's memory module industry, with revenue exceeding that of Demingli (approximately RMB 15–18 billion in 2025) and stronger brand recognition (its Lexar brand carries substantial influence in the consumer market). Longsys has a deep partnership with SK hynix, giving it a clear edge in supply-chain stability. Demingli's biggest differentiator is its in-house memory controller chips—it is one of the few module makers in China with controller design capability. Longsys has no in-house controllers and relies more on controller solutions from Silicon Motion (SMI) and Phison.

Biwin Storage: A Close Competitor

Biwin Storage (688525) and Demingli are highly similar in business structure—both make SSDs, embedded storage and mobile storage, with high customer overlap and comparable industry standing. Biwin also saw revenue scale up in 2025, but its gross margin is on par with Demingli (both fluctuating around 15%). In their competition at the top of the cycle, which company can maintain better cash flow at the bottom of the cycle will be the decisive factor.

Netac Technology: A Veteran Brand

Netac Technology is a long-established player in the storage industry, focused on consumer-grade products such as USB flash drives and memory cards. The Netac brand enjoys high consumer recognition, but the company's overall scale and market influence have fallen behind Demingli and Longsys. Netac's strengths lie in its brand moat and mature distribution channels; its weaknesses are a product mix skewed toward consumer products and slow expansion into the enterprise segment.

📊 Competitive matrix comparison:

• Demingli:In-house controller ✅ / Full product line ✅ / Enterprise SSD ✅ / High debt ⚠️

• Longsys:Strong brand ✅ / OEM partnerships ✅ / Large scale ✅ / Proprietary controller ❌

• Biwin Storage:Similar business structure / comparable scale / synchronized cycles / no significant divergence

• Netac Technology:High brand recognition / consumer-centric / limited growth elasticity

Moat assessment: moderately weak

Demingli's core competitiveness rests on two points: its in-house memory controller chips, and full-chain service that responds quickly to customer needs. But this does not constitute a "wide moat" — controller chips can be imitated or substituted by competitors, the scale effects of module production are limited, and customer stickiness is weak (low brand-switching costs). In an industry upcycle, everyone makes money; but in a downcycle, only companies with supply-chain advantages and solid cash flow can ride out the cycle. Demingli's high leverage plus negative cash flow will be a significant competitive disadvantage in a downcycle.

Overall, the moat in the memory module segment is generally narrow, and competition among companies hinges more on who can survive the bottom of the cycle than on who earns more at the top.

Management: grassroots entrepreneurship and the capital chessboard

Demingli's founder and chairman, Li Hu, is a quintessential Chinese grassroots entrepreneur. Public records show that Li Hu was born in 1969 and began his career in electronic component trading before founding Demingli's predecessor in 2008. From a trading start to in-house controller chip development and then a listing on the A-share market, Li Hu has led Demingli along the classic Chinese technology company growth path of "trade, manufacture, then technology."

Li Hu: three traits of the man at the helm

The first characteristic isStrategic foresightLi Hu invested in storage controller chips earlier than most peers, making Demingli one of the few domestic module makers with in-house controller capability. That positioning let the company offer differentiated solutions during the NAND price upcycle and strengthened customer stickiness.

The second trait isA spirit of adventureLi Hu displays an exceptionally high risk appetite in running the company—evident both in inventory management (RMB 12.2 billion of inventory, 66% of total assets) and in capital operations (a high debt ratio of 64%, with heavy borrowing to stockpile goods). This "gambling instinct" proved wise during the upcycle, but remember: Li Hu is betting the company's money, while shareholders bear the risk of stock-price volatility.

The third trait isHighly concentrated shareholdingLi Hu directly holds 12.34%, and together with concert parties controls roughly 25-30%, making him the company's actual controller. This means Li Hu has absolute control over the company, and management decisions are essentially led by him. A highly concentrated ownership structure facilitates fast decision-making, but it also carries the risk of one-man rule—at cyclical inflection points, if Li Hu misjudges, the cost of correction will be extremely high.

General Manager Du Tiejun: Technology and Execution

Du Tiejun serves as general manager and oversees daily operations. Public information shows that Du Tiejun has a deep background in the storage industry and, under Li Hu's macro strategy, is responsible for product implementation and execution. The management team overall presents a pattern of "a strong founder plus a professionalized execution team," which is fairly common among A-share growth companies.

📊 Key management metrics:

• Li Hu's stake: 12.34% (direct) + indirect holdings through other entities, totaling approximately 25-30%

• Chairman's stake change: +22.06% QoQ in 2026Q1 (increased) — positive signal

• Headcount: 1,183 — typical scale for the module industry

• Employee stock ownership/equity incentives: the company has equity incentive arrangements in place, and core technical staff hold substantial market value in shares

Interpreting management behavior

Li Hu's 22% increase in holdings in 2026Q1 is worth noting—it shows he personally has confidence in the company's prospects. On the other hand, the heavy borrowing to stockpile inventory also shows management holds a strong conviction that NAND prices will keep rising. That conviction reinforces itself during an upcycle, yet often becomes a blind spot at the cycle's turning point. Historically, many executives at cyclical companies have followed a similar pattern: most confident and betting biggest at the cycle top, then suffering the largest losses.

Management's appetite for risk is a double-edged sword. In the startup phase it drives rapid growth; in the mature phase it can expose the company to unnecessary risk. Demingli is now at a critical juncture in its transition from a "growth cyclical stock" to a "mature technology company" — whether management's risk appetite adjusts accordingly will directly affect the company's long-term value.

Article 08

🎯 Investment decision: finding certainty in extreme divergence

Demingli is one of the most "polarized" names in the A-share market today—extreme growth coexisting with extreme risk. Revenue up 6.0x in three years, a 44% net margin in Q1 2026, and a dynamic PE of just 9x—these numbers excite growth investors. Yet operating cash flow remains negative, inventory of RMB 12.2 billion accounts for 66% of total assets, PB is 18x and static PE 177x—signals enough to keep value investors at bay.

How can investors make rational decisions amid this divergence? We need to run scenario analysis.

Scenario 1: continuation of the bull market (probability about 15%)

Assuming NAND prices continue to rise or stay elevated in the second half of 2026, Demingli's full-year net profit could reach RMB 8-12 billion. At a fair PE of 15x, that implies a market cap of RMB 120-180 billion, offering 0-48% upside from the current RMB 121.7 billion. But this scenario requires the NAND cycle to extend beyond the consensus—which history suggests is uncommon.

Scenario 2: A normal cyclical pullback (probability about 50%)

Most likely scenario: NAND prices begin a moderate decline in the second half of 2026, Demingli's gross margin falls from 57% to 25-35%, and full-year net profit comes in at roughly RMB 3-5 billion. At 15x PE, fair market cap is RMB 45-75 billion, implying 38-63% downside from the current market cap. Inventory impairment pressure also begins to surface, and operating cash flow may improve slightly but is unlikely to turn positive. Under this scenario the company's fundamentals remain healthy, but the current share price has clearly run ahead of this expectation.

📊 Three-scenario valuation:

• Optimistic (15% probability):Net profit ~RMB 12 billion × 15x PE = RMB 180 billion → +48%

• Neutral (50% probability):Net profit ~RMB 3 billion × 15x PE = RMB 45 billion →-63%

• Bear case (35% probability):Net profit ~-RMB 1.0–1.0 billion → market cap RMB 15–30 billion →-75~-88%

Scenario 3: Cyclical slump (probability around 35%)

If NAND prices collapse (as they have repeatedly in history), Demingli could face a double hit of "collapsing net profit plus massive inventory write-downs." In a pessimistic scenario, the company could post a net loss of RMB 1–3 billion, with market cap shrinking to RMB 15–30 billion. With a PB of 18x, net assets per share of only RMB 29.20, and the stock hitting the 10% daily limit down in July 2026 (RMB 536.54 to limit-down), the market has already shown signs of profit-taking.

Key validation milestones

The interim report (due by end-August) will be the key window to validate Demingli's investment thesis. Three core indicators: ① Will gross margin stay above 30%? A fall below 20% would signal the cycle has turned. ② Will operating cash flow turn positive? If still negative, the stockpiling model remains unchanged. ③ Will inventory start to decline? Further growth would mean the company is still raising its bet.

Final recommendation

Demingli is a company that is "highly capable but heavily inclined to gamble." Its in-house controllers and full-chain service capabilities do constitute differentiated competitiveness, but its high leverage, high inventory and negative cash flow leave it dangerously positioned in a downturn. The current market cap of RMB 121.7 billion already embeds extremely optimistic expectations—about 63% downside in the neutral scenario, versus only 48% upside in the optimistic scenario. The risk-reward ratio is unfavorable.

📌 Investment decision:Stay on the sidelines; do not participate. Wait for interim results to validate the data before making a decision. If the interim report shows gross margin holding above 30%, operating cash flow turning positive and inventory starting to decline—that is the kind of investment opportunity worth serious consideration. Buying a highly leveraged module maker at 18x PB at the top of the NAND cycle has rarely produced good outcomes. Patience is an investor's most powerful weapon.
