2. Moat — competitive barriers for memory chip design companies

Demingli's core business is the design and sale of memory chips (NAND Flash / DRAM). The moat for companies of this type typically comes from:

  • Technological barriers: Chip design capability, advanced process adaptation
  • Customer stickiness: Long certification cycles and high switching costs
  • Supply chain relationships: Long-term stable cooperation with wafer foundries
  • Domestic substitution dividend: Policy support and downstream customers' demand for "independent and controllable" technology

Buffett would focus on evaluating: Demingli's moat is"Broad and enduring"(such as Samsung Memory), or"Track cyclical fluctuations"(such as most second-tier chip design companies).

What would Buffett ask?"The cyclicality of the memory chip industry means all players' profits swing sharply. What unique competitive advantage does Demingli have that would let it outperform rivals in a downturn?"

Munger-Style Close Reading: Cycles, Human Nature and Contrarian Thinking

MungerPerspective: cyclical fluctuations, incentive mechanisms, contrarian investing, interdisciplinary thinking

Memory chips: the cyclical industry Munger most loved to study

Munger had a deep understanding of cyclical industries—he commented repeatedly on the cyclical nature of the semiconductor industry. Memory chips are among the most cyclical categories in semiconductors, because:

  • Supply side: Fab construction cycles are long (2-3 years), creating a mismatch between capacity coming online and demand
  • Demand side: Volatile on AI, servers, consumer electronics and other factors
  • Price mechanism: When supply and demand fall out of balance, prices can spike or crash (NAND Flash prices can swing ±50%)
Munger would cite his favoriteContrarian thinking"If Demingli's net profit is RMB 13 billion this year, what is its net profit most likely to be three years from now? — Taking historical cycles into account, the answer could be any number between RMB 3 billion and RMB 30 billion."

Incentive mechanism: what will management do at the cycle peak?

Munger paid close attention to whether incentive structures distort behavior. A classic "Munger-style trap" is this:

  • Strong industry conditions → company profits surge → management receives huge bonuses → confidence inflates → aggressive capacity expansion or unrelated diversification → industry turns cold → massive losses

2026Q1 net profit RMB 3.346 billion, annualized above RMB 13 billion. Munger would press further:"Will management think they are smart because of good results, and then do something stupid?"

The "plight" of contrarian investing

Munger's contrarian investment philosophy is:"Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful."

Dinglong is now in the "others are turning greedy" phase (gross margin jumped from 14.8% to 57.4%, and the market is starting to chase it). Munger would ask:

  • Has the contrarian entry point passed?The cyclical bottom in 2025 (gross margin 14.8%) is the time for contrarian buying
  • Chase the rally now?Munger was typically cautious about buying strong cyclical stocks during their high-profit phase.
  • Waiting for the next low?Munger might choose to wait for the next cycle bottom before considering it
Munger's inversion"If memory chips enter a downcycle next year and Demingli's net profit falls back to RMB 1 billion, the stock could drop more than 70% — can you stomach that? If the answer is no, you shouldn't buy it now."

Conclusion

Demingli is a classic "Munger-style puzzle" — it could be an excellent investment opportunity, or it could be a cyclical trap. The key lies in understanding the cycle position of the memory chip industry and the company's true competitiveness within it. The current high-gross-margin phase may be better suited to holding than to buying.

Why did gross margin surge from 14.8% to 57.4%?

A "nuclear-scale" reversal in gross margin usually stems from a combination of the following factors:

  1. Price-driven: AI-driven storage chip demand surges, NAND Flash/DRAM prices rise sharply
  2. Product mix upgrade: Rising share of high-gross-margin products (such as enterprise SSDs and storage for AI servers)
  3. Scale effects: Dilution of unit fixed costs from substantial revenue growth
  4. Supply chain optimization: Lower upstream procurement costs or optimized foundry fees

Core question: which of these four drivers are sustainable and which are cyclical. The price driver (factor 1) is the least sustainable (prices eventually mean-revert), while product-mix upgrading (factor 2) is the most sustainable.
