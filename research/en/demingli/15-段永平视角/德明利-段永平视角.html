Q&A: Duan Yongping on Demingli

Q: What is your view on Demingli's business?

What does Demingli do? Put simply, itBuy NAND Flash wafers, assemble them into SSDs and memory cards, then sell them。

This is not the kind of business I like.

A good business model has several characteristics: first, a moat—others find it hard to replicate you; second, strong cash flow—the money earned is real cash; third, predictability—you can roughly judge the trajectory over the next few years.

Demingli meets none of these three criteria.

What is its moat? Some say it has in-house controller chips. But R&D spend in 2025 was only RMB 290 million, 2.7% of revenue—at that level of investment,How high a technical barrier can be built?What stops Jiangbolong or Biwin Storage from doing something similar?

Did its cash flow turn positive? Full-year 2025 operating cash flow was -RMB 2.24 billion; Q1 2026 net profit was RMB 3.35 billion, while operating cash flow was still -RMB 240 million.It earned RMB 3.35 billion in profit, yet cash in hand fell by RMB 240 million — what kind of money is that?

Predictable? Its gross margin can swing between 14.8% and 57.4%, entirely dependent on NAND Flash prices. This is not a business, this is aBets on price swings。

Q: 2026Q1 earnings exploded — revenue of RMB 7.5 billion, net profit of RMB 3.35 billion, net margin of 44%, and a forward PE of just 9x. Isn't that cheap?

That gets right to the heart of the matter. Let me break it down for you.

How did the 44% net margin in 2026Q1 come about?

Full-year 2025 gross margin was 14.8%, then suddenly became 57.4% in 2026Q1. A 42-percentage-point gross margin jump within a single quarter—this is not an improvement from normal operations, this isInventory appreciation from the price-hike cycle。

What does this mean? The company stockpiled heavily when NAND prices were low (inventory surged from RMB 7 billion to RMB 12.2 billion), then sold at high prices in the quarter when NAND prices spiked, releasing profit in one go. This follows the same logic as buying the dip in stocks and cashing out at the top.

ButThis is not sustainable.Prices cycle up and down. When NAND prices turn lower, what happens to that RMB 12.2 billion of inventory? A massive writedown. A writedown of RMB 3.5-4 billion would be enough to wipe out the entire year's profit.

A forward PE of 9x is based onAn unsustainable extreme high-price cycleThat is on top of it. If profit reverts to a normal-year level — say RMB 3 billion — then a 9x PE becomes 40x.

Duan Yongping-style questioning:Calculate Demingli's average net profit over the past three years—roughly under RMB 300 million. Using that figure, the PE is 400x. Now tell me: is this a cheap company at 9x PE, or a sky-high company at 400x PE?The answer depends on whether you believe sky-high profits can last. I don't.
Q: Is the memory chip industry a good business? Where does Demingli stand in the industry?

The memory chip industry has two tiers.

Upstream: wafer/chip makers.Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, YMTC, CXMT—these are the true "memory chip companies." They require tens of billions of dollars in capital expenditure, more than a decade of technology accumulation, and tens of thousands of patents. This business has a moat—an extremely wide one. But it is highly cyclical and capital-intensive.

Downstream: module/solution providers.Demingli, Jiangbolong, Baiwei Storage — these areBuy granules, assemble them into products, and sell them.Yes. This business has essentially no moat. Anyone can buy NAND chips, anyone can assemble SSDs. The only differences are scale, customer relationships and quality control.

As Duan Yongping put it:Upstream is real expertise; downstream is arbitrage。

I don't invest in middleman businesses. Why? Because your upstream (Samsung, SK hynix) is far stronger than you — when they raise prices, your costs rise with them; when they cut prices, your inventory value falls with them. Your downstream (customers) is stronger than you too — big customers can switch suppliers and squeeze prices. You are caught in the middle, squeezed from both sides.

This is not a good business—this isA tough business。

Q: Some people benchmark Demingli against Silicon Motion (SMI) — a storage controller chip maker, not a pure assembly business. What is your view?

I need to respond carefully to that claim.

Silicon Motion (SMI) is indeed a fabless company making storage controller chips—it designs the chips, outsources manufacturing to TSMC, and sells to module makers. Its gross margin has long ranged between 45-55%, with R&D expense at 15-20% of revenue, making it a company with real technological barriers.

Demingli also claims in-house controller chips. Let me look at their R&D spending — 2025 R&D expense was RMB 290 million, 2.7% of revenue. Silicon Motion spends roughly USD 150-200 million a year on R&D (about RMB 1-1.5 billion), an R&D expense ratio of 15%+.

RMB 290 million vs RMB 1.0–1.5 billion. A gap of more than 7x.

I cannot judge how much real value Demingli's controller chips hold. But the R&D spending data tells me—If it truly had a technical moat comparable to Silicon Motion's, it should not be investing so little in R&D.。

In addition, Demingli's 2025 revenue was RMB 10.79 billion, of which SSDs accounted for 42%, embedded storage 34%, mobile storage 14% and memory modules 10%. If its core competitiveness really rests on "in-house controllers", why not pursue pure chip licensing (as Silicon Motion does) instead of asset-heavy module assembly?

My judgment is:Dingmingli is essentially still a memory module maker; its in-house controller is a means of differentiated competition, not its core profit model.Its profit source still mainly relies on buying low and selling high on NAND chips.

Q: Is Demingli's management "dutiful"?

Let me share my thoughts.

What does "Benfen" mean? It meansDo the right things, then do things rightDoing the right thing is direction; doing things right is execution.

What is Demingli's management actually doing, judging by the data?

First,Large-scale stockpilingInventory rose from RMB 7.06 billion at end-2025 to RMB 12.19 billion in 2026Q1 — an increase of RMB 5.1 billion in a single quarter. The money was borrowed — short-term borrowings of RMB 3.74 billion, long-term borrowings of RMB 2.05 billion, and interest-bearing debt totaling nearly RMB 6 billion.

Second,Operating cash flow remains negativeNot one year but several consecutive reporting periods: RMB -2.24 billion for full-year 2025 and RMB -240 million for Q1 2026. This shows the company's profits are not converting into cash and are all tied up in inventory.

Third,Debt-to-asset ratio 64%, this leverage ratio is very high for a highly cyclical business.

What is this called? This is calledBetting on the cycleBet on NAND prices continuing to rise. Win the bet, and everyone is happy, with profits exploding. Lose the bet, and it's inventory write-downs, debt defaults, and a stock price collapse.

A company that "knows its place" would not do this. A management team that "knows its place" should control risk, run the business prudently, and turn profit into real cash—not stake the company's fate on NAND prices.

Duan Yongping said:"Running a business requires 'benfen' — don't cheat shareholders, don't cheat yourself, don't do things beyond your circle of competence." Is Demingli's management doing things beyond their circle of competence? Perhaps not — they may simply be in the memory business. ButHoarding RMB 12.2 billion of inventory in the warehouse to bet on price increases is itself not "staying true to one's roots". This is not running a business; this is speculation.
Q: If the NAND price upcycle lasts 2-3 years, won't Demingli make a lot of money?

This is the biggest cognitive trap.

You ask me "if the price increases can last 2-3 years"—that "if" itself says it all.An investment built on "ifs" is not an investment—it is a gamble.

NAND Flash is a highly cyclical commodity. Historically, its price cycles have generally consisted of a 12-18 month upswing followed by a 12-18 month downswing. Prices double on the way up and halve on the way down. This is an inherent property of the industry—supply rigidity (long capacity build-out cycles) and demand volatility dictate it.

From the second half of 2025 to the first half of 2026, NAND was indeed in a price-upcycle. But how long had prices been rising? For about a year already. By historical patterns,We may already be near or at the top of the cycle.。

And you should know — Demingli's current profit rests on an extreme assumption:① NAND prices are at highs; ② Demingli stockpiled large volumes at lower cost; ③ Inventory carries no impairment riskAny one of these three changes, and profits collapse.

This kind of business would keep me awake at night. The first principle of investing isDon't lose moneyHanding your fate to NAND prices is far too risky a business.

Q: How should we understand "dare to be last in the world" in the memory segment? Does Demingli have an opportunity in AI memory demand?

"To dare to be last in the world" means—Don't chase trends blindly, but once you have conviction, do it better than the first movers。

AI-driven demand for storage is real. Large-model training requires vast amounts of SSD storage for data, and AI inference servers also need high-speed storage. The enterprise SSD market is indeed expanding rapidly.

If Demingli can genuinely build an edge in enterprise SSDs—deepening performance, stability and customer relationships—it has a real opportunity. Its SSD revenue reached RMB 4.58 billion in 2025, nearly doubling year on year, showing it is capturing the dividends of this market.

But:

First, the biggest beneficiaries of incremental AI storage demand are upstream wafer fabs (Samsung, SK hynix, YMTC), not module makers.It is like the gold rush—the people selling shovels make more money than the people digging for gold. In the memory supply chain, the upstream wafer fabs are the ones "selling shovels."

Second, does enterprise SSD production really require no deep technical moat?Yes. But the technical barriers lie in the controller chip, firmware algorithms and reliability validation. All of these require long-term R&D investment. Is Demingli's 2.7% R&D expense ratio enough? I have my doubts.

Third, competitors are no pushovers.Longsys is also moving into enterprise-grade, Biwin Storage is doing the same, and the international giants laid their plans long ago. On what basis can Demingli win?

AI storage is a real opportunity, but opportunity does not equalMoatMy judgment: Demingli is now benefiting from the upcycle in prices, not from a technology lead.

Q: Many say Demingli is a "small Jiangbolong." What is Jiangbolong's market cap? How should the two be compared?

I cannot evaluate Longsys because I have not studied it closely. But I can explain how to look at companies of this kind.

Domestic memory module makers essentially do the same thing: buy chips, build modules, sell them. The difference lies in customer mix, product portfolio and scale.

Demingli 2025 revenue RMB 10.79 billion, market cap ~RMB 121.7 billion, P/S 11.3x. Longsys 2025 revenue roughly RMB 15-20 billion; I didn't check its market cap.

But I want to ask a more fundamental question:What PE multiple should storage module companies command in the A-share market?

In the US, Kingston is not a listed company. Seagate is a hard-drive company and is a different story. SMI is a pure-play chip design company, valued at 15-25x PE.

If Demingli is essentially a cyclical module maker, look at historical cyclicals in the A-share market: when peak-cycle profit arrives, the market typically assigns only 8-12x PE, because everyone knows the profit is unsustainable. The current dynamic PE of 9x sits right in that range. In other words:The market has not entirely ignored its cyclical nature, but it has also not fully priced in inventory and cash-flow risk.。

Q: If Duan Yongping had RMB 10 million, would he buy Demingli now?

No.

Let me be clear: Demingli is not, to me, a qualifying "Duan Yongping-style investment target." There are three reasons:


First, I do not understand the business model, and I do not invest in what I do not understand.

A good business model is predictable, has a moat, and turns profit into cash. Demingli has none of the three. Its "competitive barrier" is its own claim, not something I can verify with common sense. Look at Moutai—it is simply the best baijiu, no explanation needed. Look at Apple—it is simply the best smartphone ecosystem, no explanation needed. But Demingli—is it a chip company, a module maker, or a trader? If I cannot even explain it clearly myself, I should not buy it.


Second, management's behavior makes me uneasy.

RMB 12.2 billion in inventory, RMB 6 billion in interest-bearing debt, and operating cash flow negative for consecutive periods—this is not the "Benfen" way. A "Benfen" management team would control risk and run the business prudently. Demingli's management is betting on the cycle, betting on price increases, betting that its own judgment is right. Perhaps they are right, but the behavior itself is not "Benfen."


Third, there is no margin of safety.

PB 18.37x. Static PE 176x. If NAND prices fall 20%, the RMB 12.2 billion of inventory would take a RMB 2.4 billion impairment. Full-year 2025 net profit was only RMB 688 million. In other words,A 20% price decline would wipe out three and a half years of the company's profitsThis risk is not worth taking.


My answer is:

No. Whether it is RMB 10 million or RMB 10,000, do not buy.

I would rather put RMB 10 million into Apple, Tencent and Moutai, collect steady dividends each year and watch them grind higher, than put it into a cyclical stock that could soar or crash. Investing is forSleep soundly, not to chase excitement.

But—if you have a very deep understanding of the memory industry, can judge NAND price trends more accurately than the market, and can better assess Demingli's technical capabilities—then you can buy. I won't. This is not my circle of competence.

Q: What is Demingli's biggest risk?

The biggest risk isThe double hit of inventory write-downs and cash-flow rupture。


First line: inventory.RMB 12.2 billion of inventory = 66% of total assets. If NAND prices fall, impairment could reach RMB 3-4 billion. That would wipe out all of 2025 and 2026Q1 profit.


Second thread: cash flow.Operating cash flow stays negative. The company survives on borrowed money — RMB 3.74 billion in short-term borrowings and RMB 2.05 billion in long-term borrowings. If banks pull their loans or financing conditions tighten, the company could face a liquidity crisis.


If both lines occur at the same time— NAND prices fall → inventory write-downs → profit collapse → stock price collapse → banks demand additional collateral or early repayment → cash flow breaks → forced to dump inventory at low prices → even bigger write-downs — this is the Davis double-kill, and it is terrifying.


Several secondary risks also apply:

- Supplier concentration risk — the largest supplier accounts for 34%, so any supply disruption would have a significant impact

- Exports account for 64% of sales — exposed to international trade friction

- Lock-up expiry — 1.82 million shares unlocked on 23 July; small in proportion but a strong signal


In one sentence:The risk with this company is not "not knowing what will happen" but "knowing what will happen without knowing when."This risk is the hardest to price.

📌 Final conclusion

Duan Yongping's one-line operational advice

Conclusion: ❌ Demingli is not a good business. Do not buy under any circumstances.


Demingli's problem is not "whether it is a good company"—it isIts business model is fundamentally not the kind I like.。


The kind of business I like is this:

✅ You can explain in one sentence how it makes money (Moutai = sells the best baijiu, Apple = sells the best phones)

✅ It has a moat that others find hard to replicate (brand, technology, network effects, economies of scale)

✅ Its profit converts into cash (strong operating cash flow)

✅ Its future is broadly predictable (no need to guess NAND prices)

✅ Management stays "true to its roots" (no gambling, no deception, no stepping outside its circle of competence)


Demingli meets none of them.


Its business cannot be summed up in one sentence—is it a chip company? A module maker? A trader?

Its moat does not exist—a 2.7% R&D expense ratio is not in the same league as Silicon Motion's 15%+.

Its profit is paper profit — net profit RMB 3.35 billion, cash flow negative RMB 240 million.

Its future is unpredictable—it hinges on NAND prices, and no one can predict NAND prices.

Its management is betting the farm—RMB 12.2 billion in inventory equals 66% of total assets. That is not operations; it is speculation.


Some might say: "Duan Yongping, you are too conservative. Demingli is up 10x this year, and you missed a huge bull stock."

True, I missed it. But investing is not about who catches more big winners; it is about who lasts longer.


I have missed many big winners—PDD, Tesla, Nvidia—but I have no regrets. Those companies fall outside my circle of competence; even if I bought them, I could not hold on. Rather than make money on stocks I do not understand and then give it all back, I would rather earn steadily on stocks I do understand.


Companies like Demingli probably keep even their own shareholders awake at night, wondering every evening whether NAND prices will fall tomorrow.


Investing is meant to make life better, not to keep you on edge every day.


If you must play the memory cycle—buy equity in YMTC (if you can get it), or wait for Demingli to fall to a reasonable valuation (PB< 3x，经营现金流转正，存货开始下降）再考虑。现在的价格和风险，不值得。
