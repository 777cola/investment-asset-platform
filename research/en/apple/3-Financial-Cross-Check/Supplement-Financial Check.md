## Basis gap of 1.4%: three ways to calculate share count

Market cap is the only line item above 1%, for a mundane reason: Apple's share count comes in three measures—145.3 billion basic shares outstanding, 147.1 billion diluted shares, and 147.9 billion actual shares outstanding at quarter-end (including unvested RSUs). Applying the same share price to different denominators can swing market cap by USD 60 billion.

We use 145.3 billion shares throughout, as that is the denominator for per-share metrics and the measure most widely used by the market; the trade-off is a market cap slightly below the figures shown on some terminals.This difference does not affect any conclusion—34x or 33.6x makes no difference to the judgment of whether it is expensive.

Note that the numerator and denominator of P/E must be on the same basis: if market cap is calculated on diluted shares, diluted EPS must be used; mixing the two creates a 1% valuation gap out of thin air.This report uses one consistent basis throughout: basic-share market cap of RMB 4.41 trillion ÷ TTM net profit of RMB 129.6 billion = 34.0x.

## Two substantive expectation gaps matter more than the data deviation

Services revenue of RMB 30.739 billion came in 2.0% below the RMB 31.36 billion consensus — a gap of USD 620 million. Note the magnitude: the "USD 6.2 billion below expectations" figure circulating in the market is a magnitude error that turns 620 million into 6.2 billion; this report treats it as USD 620 million. Yet even at just 2%, the nature of the miss matters: this is the first quarter in more than three years that services revenue has fallen short of expectations.

Greater China revenue was RMB 18.816 billion against RMB 19.58 billion expected, 3.9% below, or roughly USD 760 million. The gap itself is not large, but set beside a flattering +22.4% YoY print it is easy to overlook. The market had looked for +25% or more; the actual 22.4% shows share gains are not as fast as imagined.

How to handle it: cite actuals in the body, list expectations separately under "Assessment", and do not mix them into the financial tables. These two items are among the main explanations for the "9% drop in the three days after earnings" in m12 and cannot be written up as merely "slightly above expectations".

## EPS of USD 0.11: one-off or recurring

Of the reported USD 2.02, USD 0.11 came from a tariff refund (about USD 1.6 billion; rough math: 0.11 × 14.7 billion shares). That money is neither core revenue nor recurring: excluding it, EPS is USD 1.91, up 21.7% YoY and just USD 0.02 above consensus.

Hence the paradox of results "0.5% above consensus" alongside a 7.35% share price fall: the market is not focused on the GAAP number but on how much is left once one-offs are stripped out. Revenue 0.5% above consensus, EPS propped up by a tax refund, guidance below consensus—put the three together and the valuation warrants a de-rating.

Final treatment: the body text and tables retain USD 2.02 (the company-reported figure), with a note at every occurrence that it "includes a USD 0.11 refund; USD 1.91 excluding it"; all valuation work uses the USD 1.91 continuing basis to derive FY2026E, avoiding capitalizing one-time gains.

## Cross-check by aggregation: both independent equations hold up

Line-item reconciliation only proves the numbers were copied correctly; aggregate reconciliation alone proves they were broken down correctly. Two equations. First: product gross margin 40.1% × 786.78 = RMB 315.5 billion; services 75.6% × 307.39 = RMB 232.4 billion; total RMB 547.9 billion. Reported gross profit of USD 54,770 million = RMB 547.7 billion. The USD 20 million gap comes from rounding the gross margin; the structure holds.

Second: regional revenue discloses only the Americas at RMB 457.81 billion, Europe at RMB 293.95 billion and Greater China at RMB 188.16 billion, totaling RMB 939.92 billion. Total revenue is RMB 1,094.17 billion; the RMB 154.25 billion difference is Japan plus the rest of Asia-Pacific. This derived figure can be checked against the year-earlier period — the same-basis difference in FY2025Q3 was RMB 134.3 billion, up 14.8% YoY, slower than the 16.4% overall, indicating Japan and Southeast Asia were the weak spots this quarter.

Both derivations point to the same conclusion: the segment data in this report is internally consistent. The problem is not the statistics but the structure — growth is overly concentrated in iPhone and in the Americas and Europe, while Japan, Asia-Pacific and Greater China all grew below the overall rate. Only two regions outgrowing the average is normal for a global company in a year of currency headwinds; in a year of currency tailwinds it is not.

One item cannot be cross-verified: operating cash flow. Apple does not disclose quarterly cash-flow detail, and few in the media calculate it; this report uses an approximation derived from last year's cash-flow ratio. This is the only soft data in the reconciliation, so wherever the main text touches cash flow and free cash flow it uses qualitative language ("covers profit", "net cash position") without decimal precision.
