Earnings Read — Apple

Core thesis

Read this earnings report on four levels. First, revenue: iPhone +21.7% and Mac +28.7%, the strongest June quarter in six years. Second, gross margin: 50.1% is an all-time high, but guidance for next quarter is 47%–48%. Third, costs and profit: operating margin 32.6%, net margin 27.2%, and EPS includes a USD 0.11 tax refund. Fourth, cash: roughly USD 103 billion of free cash flow over the year, all returned to shareholders.

Of the four levels, the first three are "good but peaking"; the fourth is the only one beyond dispute. That also explains the 7.35% drop in the stock after the report — the market is not doubting this report, it is doubting the next one.

Tier 1 Revenue: iPhone and Mac the twin engines; iPad the sole weak spot

Total revenue was USD 109.417 billion, up 16.4% YoY and the highest ever for the June quarter. Growth came almost entirely from two lines: iPhone at USD 54.252 billion, +21.7%, and Mac at USD 10.352 billion, +28.7%. Together the two contributed USD 11.8 billion of incremental revenue, 77% of the total increase of USD 15.3 billion.

The iPhone's +21.7% splits into volume and price. Global shipments grew by a single-digit percentage YoY, so most of the increase came from average selling price: mix continued to shift toward Pro and Pro Max, and the absence of a foldable pushed high-end users onto the Pro line. That mix helps gross margin and hurts volume elasticity: once the high-end replacement cycle ends, volume and price will fall together.

Mac's +28.7% is the direct result of the M5 transition. The M5-series MacBook Pro has been supply-constrained since launch, with lead times reaching 6 weeks at one point. This is a rare quarter in which product strength directly drove revenue for Apple: over the past few years Mac growth came mainly from enterprise refreshes, whereas this time consumers upgraded on their own initiative.

iPad was the only line to decline: USD 6.191 billion, −5.9%. The cause is not competition but the replacement cycle — iPads last too long (5 years on average), and there was no form-factor change in 2026. The line has limited impact on the whole (5.7% of revenue), but it is evidence that Apple too can stop selling.

Services deserves separate treatment, because it is the only bad news in this report. USD 30.739 billion, +12.1% YoY, below the consensus of 2.0%, and down 0.8% sequentially — the first time in more than three years. Of the four revenue blocks, commissions (App Store) are constrained by regulation and the default search slot (Google) is threatened by AI search; only subscriptions and advertising are still growing at a double-digit rate. The two high-margin blocks are being eroded, and the two growing blocks are not large enough.

Second layer, gross margin: 50.1% is the ceiling; the product's 40.1% warrants scrutiny

Gross margin 50.1%, up 3.6pct YoY, gross profit USD 54.77 billion. By segment: products 40.1%, services 75.6%. Services held flat at 75.6% YoY, so the entire 3.6-point gain came from products.

Backing out last year's product gross margin: product revenue 66.613 billion, services 27.423 billion; against a 46.5% total gross margin and services at 75.6%, product gross margin works out to about 34.5%. Up 5.6 percentage points in a year — the number this quarter most demands scrutiny. Management's only explanation is the word "mix," but mix cannot explain 5.6 points.

Three possible sources: (1) higher Pro mix (about +2 points from mix); (2) roughly USD 1.6 billion of tariff refunds booked into product cost (about +2 points); (3) lower channel subsidies and promotions (about +1.5 points). The three sum to roughly 5.5 points, but this is my own decomposition — the company did not disclose it.

That makes next quarter's 47%–48% guidance easy to read: refunds are gone, memory costs start hitting the P&L, and the marginal lift from Pro mix slows. The 47.5% midpoint means "back to normal," not "deteriorating" — 50.1% was the abnormal quarter.

One layer deeper: how much this memory cycle actually takes. Memory is about 12% of iPhone BOM (rough estimate); DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% in early 2026 and NAND doubled, a weighted increase of about 75%, implying BOM up 9% and device gross margin potentially down 4.5 points. Apple's gross margin fell only 2.6 points, meaning it absorbed half, with the other half offset by Pro mix and long-term contract pricing. How much buffer is left? If prices rise another round in 2027, there is no buffer left.

Tier 3 Costs and Profit: 32.6% Operating Margin, Cost Control as an Invisible Moat

Gross profit of RMB 54.77 billion and operating profit of RMB 35.695 billion imply total expenses of RMB 18.952 billion, a 17.3% expense ratio. R&D accounted for about RMB 9.9 billion (9.0% of revenue) and SG&A about RMB 9.0 billion (8.2%). A company with RMB 460 billion in revenue that holds SG&A below 8% is an outlier in hardware—Samsung runs about 17%, Xiaomi about 9% (albeit on a gross margin of only 19.8%).

R&D at 9% of revenue is a historical high. Where does the money go? Three areas: A-series and M-series chips, in-house 5G baseband (C-series), and AI models and on-device inference. All three are bets where spending does not guarantee success but not spending guarantees falling behind, and they are the structural reason gross margin stays under pressure over the long run.

Net income was RMB 29.789 billion, a 27.2% net margin, up 27.1% YoY. But quality needs spelling out: of the USD 2.02 EPS, USD 0.11 came from a tariff refund, leaving USD 1.91, up 21.7% YoY. Revenue 0.5% above consensus, EPS propped up by a one-off, guidance below consensus—those three together fully explain the 7.35% drop after the results.

One more detail: diluted shares fell 1.9% YoY, so EPS growth (+28.7%) outpaced net income growth (+27.1%). Buybacks automatically add about 2% to per-share growth each year, the most reliable "hidden growth" in Apple's financials.

At the operating profit line there is an overlooked comparison: a 32.6% operating margin versus about 15% at Samsung Electronics over the same period (rough estimate, based on the structure before memory price increases). Both make hardware, yet Apple's operating margin is more than double Samsung's—that is the difference between an integrator and a component supplier. Conversely, Samsung benefits from the memory upcycle while Apple absorbs the cost, and the two margins are converging.

Layer 4 Cash flow and balance sheet: RMB 100 billion a year returned to shareholders

Operating cash flow runs at roughly USD 115 billion a year, capex at about USD 12 billion and free cash flow at about USD 103 billion (rough calculation). Nearly all of that goes to buybacks and dividends: roughly USD 100 billion of buybacks and about USD 15.6 billion of dividends over the past four quarters, which together exceed free cash flow. The gap is funded with cash on hand and debt issuance — Apple's standard playbook for over a decade.

The balance sheet is clean: about USD 130 billion in cash and securities, about USD 98 billion in long-term debt and about USD 30 billion in net cash. Net cash has fallen for three consecutive years — not because operations deteriorated, but because buybacks are drawing it down too fast. If that trend continues, Apple turns from a net-cash company into a net-debt company in 2028, and the buyback pace must then slow.

The asset-light structure is the most overlooked strength in these statements. Capex is only 2.6% of revenue, so nearly every extra USD 10 billion of revenue converts into free cash flow. Against TSMC (capex above 30% of revenue) and Samsung (above 20%), Apple earns its own gross margin on other people's capacity.

One thing to watch: whether AI changes this structure. If Apple starts building its own large-model training clusters, capex could rise from USD 12 billion to USD 30 billion and free cash flow would immediately fall 15%. This is the financial variable most worth tracking in 2027, more important than gross margin.

Conclusion

Conclusion: split into four layers, the first three are "good but peaking," the fourth "no debate." Revenue mix is the strongest in history; gross margin is the highest in history and unsustainable; expense control is a hidden moat; and cash flow is thick enough to fund RMB 100 billion of buybacks a year.

Three things to verify next quarter: whether gross margin really lands at 47%–48%, whether services growth stops falling, and whether Greater China is still above 20%.If two of the three fall short, the FY2027 EPS consensus of USD 10.0 will need to be cut.
