Investment summary

We initiate coverage with a Neutral rating and a 12-month target price of USD 290, based on FY2027 EPS of USD 10.0 × 29x, implying 4% downside from the 2026/08/03 close of USD 303.42. The mismatch between company quality and price is the sole reason we do not recommend buying now.

FY2026Q3 revenue was USD 109.417 billion, up 16.4% YoY, with iPhone revenue of USD 54.252 billion, up 21.7% YoY, both record highs for a June quarter. But three marginal signals weakened simultaneously: services growth slowed from 16% to 12.1%, Greater China came in below the consensus of 3.9%, and next-quarter gross margin guidance of 47%–48%.

Key assumptions: FY2026 revenue of USD 469 billion (+12.7%), FY2027 revenue of USD 506 billion (+7.9%); FY2027 EPS of USD 10.0; gross margin midpoint falling from 49% to 48%. Three scenarios: bull case USD 373, base case USD 290, bear case USD 215.

Chapter 1 Company Overview: From Hardware Company to Rent-Collecting Platform

Apple was founded in 1976 and listed in 1980. It became the first listed company to top a USD 1 trillion market cap in 2018, and by August 2026 its market cap stood at USD 4.41 trillion.Four hardware segments (iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables and Home) plus one services segment (App Store, iCloud, subscriptions, advertising).

FY2026Q3 revenue mix: iPhone USD 54.252 billion (49.6%), Services USD 30.739 billion (28.1%), Mac USD 10.352 billion (9.5%), Wearables and Home USD 7.883 billion (7.2%), iPad USD 6.191 billion (5.7%).Services rose from 17.8% five years ago to 28.1% — the most important structural shift of the past five years.

Geographic mix: Americas USD 45.781 billion (41.8%), Europe USD 29.395 billion (26.9%), Greater China USD 18.816 billion (17.2%), and Japan and rest of Asia-Pacific combined USD 15.425 billion (14.1%, derived as total revenue less the three disclosed regions).Greater China fell from 19% in 2021 to 17.2% but remains the third-largest market.

Management hands over on September 1, 2026: Cook steps down as CEO, with Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering John Ternus succeeding him.Under Cook, revenue grew from USD 108 billion to USD 416.2 billion, and Services went from zero to USD 123 billion annualized.

Scale: about 160,000 employees worldwide, about 530 directly operated retail stores, operations in 175 countries and regions.R&D staff account for about 15%, with annual R&D spend of about USD 40 billion—more than the revenue of most semiconductor companies.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis: A Flat Market, Tiered Competition

Global smartphone shipments will reach about 1.2 billion units in 2026, up slightly YoY. Apple holds 20% share, Samsung about 19%, Xiaomi about 14%, Transsion about 9%, and OPPO/vivo about 16% combined.No single player exceeds 20%; the structure is "tiered competition" rather than "homogeneous competition."

Industry profit is highly concentrated: Apple takes about 70% of operating profit, Samsung about 20%, and the rest less than 10% (rough estimate).Apple occupies both the brand end and the rent-collection end, while Samsung and Xiaomi mainly earn manufacturing and hardware spreads.

The cost side has entered an upcycle: in early 2026 DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70%, NAND doubled, and prices rose another 15%–20% in the third quarter; TSMC raised prices for advanced processes by 5%–10%.Institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027, with relief only possible after new production lines come on stream in the second half of 2027.

Two new battlegrounds: foldables (about 30 million units in 2026, with Apple absent and the market expecting it to enter in 2027) and AI hardware (glasses and earphone form factors).Apple leads neither, the biggest concern at the industry level.

Upside/(Downside) in the services market: the global App Store ecosystem generates about USD 1.3 trillion in annual transaction value (company basis, including third-party payments), with Apple's commission covering part of it; the global subscription market is about USD 500 billion, and Apple covers 1.5 billion paying users.The space is large, but regulation is compressing the commission rate—a "volume up, price down" situation.

Relative position versus the S&P 500: Apple has the largest weight (about 7%) and is the representative of the "quality factor"—20 consecutive years of positive free cash flow, 13 consecutive years of dividend increases, and ROIC above 40% over the long term (rough estimate).Such assets are chased when rates fall and de-rated when rates rise; its volatility is essentially "duration" volatility, not operating volatility.

Chapter 3 Business Model: Three-Tier Fee Structure

Layer one, selling hardware: iPhone ASP is roughly USD 1,050, USD 200–300 above comparable Android flagships, and the premium reflects switching costs rather than specs. Hardware gross margin is 40.1%, the lowest of the three layers.

Layer two, collecting annual fees: 1.5 billion paid subscriptions at roughly USD 80 per user per year (rough calculation), with services gross margin of 75.6%. iCloud and AppleCare renewal rates exceed 80%, and marginal cost is close to zero.

Layer three, collecting rent from third parties: App Store commissions of 15%–30%, plus roughly USD 20 billion a year for the default Google search slot. Gross margin here exceeds 85%, but the legal foundation is being eroded by the EU DMA and US court rulings.

Stacked together, the three layers produce a 50.1% blended gross margin and a 27.2% net margin. The moat's core is not any single product but the distribution network formed by "2.3 billion devices + 1.5 billion subscriptions."

Unit economics: 2.3 billion active devices and 1.5 billion paid subscriptions translate into about USD 80 of services revenue per paid user per year, plus hardware (amortized over a 5-year replacement cycle, about USD 210 per year), for a per-user annual value of about USD 290 (rough calculation). This number underpins every Apple valuation model.

The mutual support among the three layers needs to be stated clearly: hardware provides the entry point (2.3 billion devices), services provide cash flow (USD 123 billion a year), and the platform provides gross margin (above 85%). No single layer on its own justifies 34x; only the three stacked together do—which is why "services stalling" is so damaging to the valuation: it does not remove one layer, it removes the second floor of the entire building.

Chapter 4 Financial analysis: record June quarter and peak gross margin

FY2026Q3 revenue RMB 109.42 billion (+16.4%), gross profit RMB 54.77 billion (gross margin 50.1%, +3.6pct), operating profit RMB 35.70 billion (operating margin 32.6%), net income RMB 29.79 billion (net margin 27.2%), diluted EPS USD 2.02 (+28.7%). Every line item hit a record high for the period.

The quality of the beat warrants a discount: the USD 2.02 includes USD 0.11 of tariff refunds (about USD 1.6 billion). Excluding that, EPS is USD 1.91, up 21.7% YoY and just USD 0.02 above consensus of USD 1.91. Revenue 0.5% above consensus, EPS carried by a one-off and guidance below expectations — the combination drove a 7.35% single-day drop after the results.

Gross margin by segment: products 40.1%, services 75.6%. Services were flat YoY; products rose 5.6 points from about 34.5%, of which about 2 points came from tariff refunds, about 2 points from Pro mix and about 1.5 points from fewer promotions. Refunds and promotions are not sustainable, hence guidance of 47%–48% for next quarter.

Cash flow: TTM operating cash flow about RMB 115.00 billion, capex about RMB 12.00 billion, free cash flow about USD 103.00 billion (rough calculation); buybacks about RMB 100.00 billion and dividends about RMB 15.60 billion, together exceeding free cash flow. Net cash fell from over RMB 80.00 billion three years ago to about RMB 30.00 billion.

One line item deserves separate attention: deferred revenue. Service subscriptions are "cash first, recognition later", and this sits on the liability side. Consecutive growth in deferred revenue is the source of visibility for service revenue; once it declines, new subscriptions are shrinking — an earlier signal than the current-quarter growth rate.

Chapter 5 Management and Governance

Cook took over in 2011. Over fourteen years revenue nearly quadrupled, services grew from zero to 123 billion, and cumulative shareholder returns exceeded USD 80 billion. Strengths: supply chain and capital allocation. Weakness: product definition (slow starts in AI and foldables).

Ternus joined in 2001 and led the Mac transition to in-house silicon, with a documented product track record; but he has no record of capital allocation or cycle management, and his CEO compensation package is yet to be disclosed. The handover is orderly: announced in advance, unanimous board approval, and Cook staying on as advisor.

Three governance marks: the 2024 Irish back-tax ruling of EUR 13 billion upheld on final appeal, the 2024 EU antitrust fine of EUR 1.84 billion, and the 2019 cut to revenue guidance, the first in a decade (the open letter conceded pricing was too high). None involves integrity issues, but all reflect a business model repeatedly tested by regulators and cycles.

Score: management 7/10 (Cook 9, Ternus 5, weighted). Watch: the new CEO's price discipline on the first buyback decision, and whether AI capex shifts toward self-built capacity.

Incentive structure: 80% of Cook's pay is stock tied to shareholder returns; the executive team's annual bonus rests mainly on three metrics: revenue, operating margin and services revenue. This rewards "hold the margin, grow services," consistent with the strategy of the past decade; it does not reward "taking risks on new categories," one of the institutional reasons foldables and AI hardware got off to a slow start.

Chapter 6 Growth drivers: two of the three legs are softening

Driver 1: iPhone ASP. The +21.7% this quarter came mainly from Pro mix and ASP, with unit shipments up only single digits. A 2027 foldable launch could lift ASP from USD 1,050 toward USD 1,800; without one, ASP growth stalls at 3%–5%.

Driver 2: Services. Growth slowed from 16% to 12.1%. Take rates and search placement face regulatory pressure and competition from AI search, while subscriptions and advertising still grow double digits, albeit off too small a base. The key variable is whether AI can become a paid service — management says it has "not yet generated revenue at scale."

Driver 3: Emerging markets. India share is below 5%, with revenue hitting a record high in 2026 (rough estimate), driven by older models, local assembly and instalments. But India's mainstream price band is USD 200–300, and deeper Apple penetration requires GDP per capita to double again — a slow variable on a ten-year horizon.

Combining the three drivers, we forecast FY2027 revenue +7.9% and EPS +8.8%. That growth justifies 26–30x; 34x requires an additional 4–8x of "AI option" value, an option we cannot currently reconcile.

AI charging sensitivity: at USD 5 per user per month for Siri AI and 20% penetration of 1.5 billion subscribers, annual revenue would be about USD 18 billion, adding roughly USD 0.85 to EPS at a 70% gross margin. This is not a forecast but an order-of-magnitude reference — it shows that AI monetization would contribute around 8% of EPS, not enough to support a 6-turn P/E expansion.

Chapter 7 Competitive landscape: memory as a cost rival, Huawei as a share rival

Samsung: No. 1 in global shipments and self-supplies memory, so it benefits relatively in a cost-inflation cycle; it is losing high-end share, and its AI strategy (Galaxy AI) has yet to create differentiation. Its threat to Apple is concentrated in the European and Southeast Asian high end.

Huawei: No. 1 in China's above-RMB 6,000 market on three legs — foldables, HarmonyOS and self-supplied chips. The damage to Apple is "carving away a high-margin market," and Greater China coming in below the consensus of 3.9% this quarter is the evidence.

Xiaomi: Value for money plus a human-car-home ecosystem, with a 19.8% gross margin and no room to maneuver when costs rise. Its threat to Apple is "diverting young users," not "taking the high end."

Google: Pixel's share is small, but Gemini is free for Android, which amounts to turning AI into a public good. This is the deepest threat to Apple's valuation — if AI is a public good, Apple cannot charge a premium for AI.

One more point on memory's competitive structure: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron together hold more than 90% of DRAM, and capacity expansion takes 18–24 months. That means the 2026 price increases extend at least into the first half of 2027, and Apple's cost pressure is not a "one-off shock" but a "two-year cycle."

Chapter 8 Risk factors

① Memory costs (already materialised): DRAM up 60%–70%, NAND doubled; gross margin guidance cut to 47%–48%. A further round of increases in 2027 could take gross margin to 45%–46% and EPS down 8%–10%.

② Services stall (already materialised): 16%→12.1%, with structural decline in take rates and search placements. A break below 10% would warrant a hardware multiple (below 25x).

③ Regulatory breakup (30% probability): The EU DMA and the US Epic case already require opening external payments. A systemic cut in take rate to 15% would trim EPS 6%–8% and the multiple by 5x.

④ Greater China (40% probability): Growth has stepped down for three consecutive quarters (38%→28%→22.4%), with Huawei foldables applying sustained pressure. A revenue drop to RMB 15 billion would shave 3 points off global growth.

⑤ Form-factor shift (20% probability / ten years): If interaction moves from screens to voice and glasses, the iPhone goes from gateway to accessory and the moat needs re-rating of −30%. Also watch FX (a 2.5pct drag next quarter) and rates (interest income on cash).

Geopolitics and tariffs: about 80% of Apple's assembly is in China, with India capacity below 20% (rough estimate). A tariff policy change hits cost and delivery simultaneously; the company hedges by expanding in India and Vietnam, but yield losses during ramp-up will eat 0.5–1 point of gross margin. This is a slow variable, but the impact persists.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecasts and Key Assumptions

FY2026E revenue RMB 469.0 billion (+12.7%): Q1 about RMB 138.0 billion (+11%), Q2 about RMB 108.7 billion (+14%), Q3 actual RMB 109.417 billion (+16.4%), Q4 about RMB 113.0 billion (+10.3%, midpoint of guidance). Q1/Q2 are derived from YoY growth rates, not company-disclosed figures.

FY2026E net profit RMB 133.3 billion, EPS USD 9.06 (147 billion shares); FY2027E revenue RMB 506.0 billion (+7.9%), net profit RMB 144.4 billion, EPS USD 10.00 (144 billion shares, buybacks about −1.9% per year). Gross margin assumed at 49.0% for FY2026 and 48.0% for FY2027.

Sensitivity: each 1-point deviation in revenue growth moves EPS by about USD 0.09; each 1-point deviation in gross margin moves EPS by USD 0.27. Gross margin is therefore the more important variable — which is why this report lists it as the primary metric to track.

Risks: if memory prices rise another 30% in 2027, gross margin falls to 45%, FY2027 EPS is about USD 8.6, and 25x that is USD 215. Conversely, if AI monetization succeeds and services return to 15%, EPS is USD 11.3 and 33x that is USD 373.

Quarterly split: Q1 (December quarter) is the largest quarter of the year at about 29% of full-year revenue; Q2 (March quarter) 23%; Q3 (June quarter) 23%; Q4 (September quarter) 25%. The Q1/Q2 figures in this report's FY2026E are derived from YoY growth rates, with ±3% error; do not use them as company-disclosed figures.

Chapter 10 Valuation: a neutral target price of USD 290

Method: anchor on FY2027 EPS of USD 10.0 and apply the midpoint of the company's 25–35x range over the past five years (29x), giving USD 290. That implies FY2026E P/E of 33.5x and FY2027E P/E of 30.3x, 4% downside from the current price of USD 303.42.

Why not the sell side's 33–35x: that multiple requires "services back to 15% + AI starts charging," and both premises need at least two quarters of validation. Pricing on unverified assumptions swaps research for belief.

Sum-of-the-parts cross-check: gross-profit weighted (products 57.6% × 26x + services 42.4% × 44x) gives 33.6x, in line with the current price. The current price therefore already fully reflects the optimistic "44x services" assumption, with no margin of safety.

Historical range: Apple's P/E has fluctuated between 22x and 38x over the past five years, with a median of 28x. The current 34x sits at the 78th percentile, leaving limited upside and opening downside.

DCF cross-check: assuming free cash flow grows 6% annually for the next five years, 2.5% perpetual growth and an 8% discount rate, enterprise value is about USD 4.2 trillion, or roughly USD 289 per share (back-of-envelope). Almost identical to the USD 290 from relative valuation—two methods give the same answer, confirming that 290 is a solid anchor.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Rating Neutral, 12-month target price USD 290. Recommended position 0%–2% as a watch position; buy zone below USD 260 (26x FY2027); trim zone above USD 373 (33x).

Three buy triggers (any one): ① share price falls below USD 260; ② services growth returns above 15% with AI revenue broken out separately; ③ gross margin holds at 47% for two quarters and Greater China returns to 20% growth. None of the three requires forecasting—only reading the quarterly reports.

Three sell/downgrade triggers (any one): ① gross margin below 46% for two consecutive quarters; ② services growth below 10%; ③ Greater China turns negative YoY. Any of these three falsifies the buy thesis, regardless of price.

Suitable investors: long-term capital able to withstand a 30% drawdown with a holding period of three years or more. Not suitable: trading-oriented capital chasing quarterly results and needing catalysts—what this stock lacks most right now is a catalyst.

Key divergence from sell-side: we agree with Morgan Stanley's FY2027 EPS of USD 10.00 but not its 36x target multiple. A 7x P/E gap equates to USD 70 of share price—that is the distance between Neutral and Buy.

Time frame: this report's target price corresponds to 12 months. Extending the horizon to three years, buying below USD 260 implies annualized returns of roughly 10%–12% (assuming 8% annual EPS growth and a multiple re-rating to 30x; FY2029 EPS of about USD 11.7 × 30x ≈ USD 350); buying at USD 303 yields only around 5% annualized over three years, below the index's long-term return.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Neutral, target price USD 290. Business quality grade A (moat 34/50, gross margin 50.1%, free cash flow RMB 103 billion); price grade C (34x TTM against 8%–10% growth).

Buy conditions: below USD 260, or Services growth back to 15% with AI revenue disclosed separately. Until then, hold a watching position of 0%–2% and take no action.
