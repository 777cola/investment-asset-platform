Apple's earnings look too good to be true — and too expensive to be true — Apple

Earnings team analysis · Publishable version

Core view

A publish-ready reading note, conclusion first: Apple delivered its strongest June quarter ever, and then the stock fell 9% in three days. The two facts are not contradictory.

First, the good part: it turned a no-growth industry into a pricing-power business

FY2026Q3: Apple revenue USD 109.417 billion, +16.4% YoY; iPhone USD 54.252 billion, +21.7% YoY; Mac USD 10.352 billion, +28.7% YoY. The June quarter is normally the weakest of the year; Apple turned it into its highest ever.

How it got there matters more. Global smartphone shipments rose only slightly in the first half, yet Apple's share climbed to 20%; iPhone unit shipments grew only in the single digits, but revenue grew 22%. Flat volume, rising price — those four words are the entire case for pricing power. In an industry where everyone is cutting prices, only Apple can still raise them.

Gross margin 50.1%, up 3.6 percentage points YoY; net profit USD 29.789 billion, net margin 27.2%. A company that sells phones has a higher net margin than most software companies. That is not luck but ecosystem rent accumulated over twenty years: App Store commissions, iCloud subscriptions, Google's default search placement, and 1.5 billion paying users.

Now the bad part: three numbers turn at the same time

First, Services: USD 30.739 billion, up 12.1% YoY, below consensus and down 0.8% sequentially — the first sequential decline in more than three years. The market pays 34x for Apple rather than 20x precisely because of the "software company growing inside a hardware company" story. With Services growth slowing from 16% to 12%, that story gets marked down.

Second, Greater China: RMB 18.816 billion, up 22.4% YoY — looks decent, but 3.9% below consensus, and that 22% rests on a low base in the year-earlier period. Huawei is carving out the above-RMB 6,000 segment bit by bit with foldables and HarmonyOS, and Apple's share has stopped expanding.

Third, and most critical: gross margin guidance of 47%–48%. This quarter came in at 50.1%; next quarter steps down to a 47.5% midpoint. On the call, Cook said the company is "struggling hard on the supply chain, with only three core memory suppliers" — a structural problem on the cost side. DRAM contract prices are up 60%–70% this year and NAND has doubled; Apple absorbed half and offset the other half through Pro models and reduced promotions. The buffer is exhausted.

One further detail merits separate mention: of the USD 2.02 EPS, USD 0.11 came from tariff refunds. Excluding that, EPS is USD 1.91, just 2 cents above consensus. Revenue 0.5% above consensus, EPS propped up by a one-off, and guidance below consensus — together those three explain the entire 7.35% post-earnings drop.

So is the stock expensive: paying 34x for 10% growth

At USD 303.42 the stock trades on 34x TTM and 30x FY2027 consensus EPS of USD 10.0. Management guides next-quarter revenue to +9%–11%, already excluding a 2.5-point FX drag. Paying 30x for 10% growth discounts 2027 AI monetisation into today's price.

Three scenarios: bear case USD 215 (gross margin compressed to 45%, EPS 8.6, 25x), base case USD 290 (EPS 10.0, 29x) and bull case USD 373 (AI fees plus foldables, EPS 11.3, 33x). Upside 23%, downside 29%, base case −4% — the risk/reward is inverted.

Sell-side target prices now sit at USD 335–360, implying 33–35x and premised on services growth returning to 15%+ and AI starting to contribute revenue. Neither premise appears anywhere in this earnings report. Justifying today's price on things that have not yet happened is the most fragile part of the bull case.

Finally: what are we waiting for

Two buy triggers; act only when either is met. First, the price falls back below USD 260 (26x FY2027 EPS). Second, Siri AI subscription revenue becomes separately disclosed in the financials for the first time, with services growth back above 15%.

Do nothing in the middle zone (USD 280–300), where the risk-reward on buying is 1:1 — not worth deploying capital.

To close: Apple is one of the companies I would most want to hold for ten years, and USD 303 is one of the prices I would least want to buy at. Price is what separates a good company from a good stock.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the business is fine, the price is not. Paying 34x for 10% growth inverts the risk-reward. Wait for USD 260, or for AI revenue to show up in the statements.
