Munger Reads Financial Reports: Which Three Numbers Lie — Apple

Earnings team analysis · Part 3

Core view

Munger read financial reports in reverse: not looking for good news, but for "dressed-up numbers". This report contains three.

First: EPS USD 2.02

USD 2.02, up 28.7% year on year — looks good. But USD 0.11 of that is a tariff refund; strip it out and you get USD 1.91, up 21.7%. That USD 0.11 looks like only 5%, but it turns "above expectations" into "exactly in line" — the market had expected USD 2.00, so including the refund it is above the consensus of USD 2.00 by 1%, and excluding it, it is below by 4.5%.

Munger would say: one-off gains are where things are easiest to hide. They need no explanation, no recurrence, and can still be capitalized by the P/E multiple. If a company dresses up EPS with one-off gains in its "best quarter," it usually means it knows the next quarter will not look good.

Second: product gross margin 40.1%

This number rose 5.6 points in a year, and the company offered only one word: "mix." The explanation Munger trusted least was "mix"—it is an outcome, not a cause. The real cause must be one of three: price increases, cost declines, or a change in accounting treatment.

My breakdown is "2 points from tax rebates + 2 points from mix + 1.5 points from reduced promotions," but the company has not confirmed it. That is the problem: when a change in a key metric cannot be explained, the safest approach is not to extrapolate it into next year.

Third: Greater China +22.4%

This is the most easily misread number in the report. Year-over-year +22.4% looks good, but it is below the consensus of 3.9%, and the base was last year's low point (FY25Q3 fell on Huawei's return). In other words, this is "climbing out of a hole," not "winning new share."

Munger's judgment would be blunt: if a market's growth needs "last year was terrible" to explain it, then it isn't growth. He would want to see two things: share, and how the next-generation product (iPhone 18) performs in its China debut. Until those two data points are out, Greater China's 22% cannot be treated as the norm.

Following this logic, he would extend the "packaging" point to the entire earnings narrative: the company highlights "record June quarter" in its best quarter, and describes its worst metric as "below expectations" rather than "declining." Word choice is itself information—if a statement needs a qualifier to hold, it doesn't hold. This is the most practical trick in Munger's earnings-reading toolkit: strip out the adjectives and look only at the numbers.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: all three numbers are "right," but all three are dressed up. EPS includes one-offs, gross margin lacks explanation, and Greater China relies on a low base. The right way to read the report is to strip all three back, then decide what multiple to pay — after stripping them back, 34x does not hold up.
