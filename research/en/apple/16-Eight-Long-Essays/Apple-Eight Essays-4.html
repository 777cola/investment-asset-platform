Core view

The most important number in this earnings report is not revenue of RMB 109.4 billion, nor EPS of 2.02. It is product gross margin rising from 34.5% to 40.1% — 5.6 percentage points in a year. This piece unpacks that number, because it determines next year's EPS.

Start with subtraction: where the 5.6 points come from

Product revenue RMB 78.678 billion, services RMB 30.739 billion; total gross profit RMB 54.770 billion, gross margin 50.1%. Services margin of 75.6% was flat year on year, so the entire 3.6-point increase in total gross margin came from products. Backing out last year's product gross margin gives 34.5% versus 40.1% this quarter, a 5.6-point gap.

Three sources, my breakdown: ① Pro model mix up about 2 points (structural upgrade); ② tariff refunds about 2 points (USD 1.6 billion ÷ RMB 78.678 billion product revenue = 2.0%); ③ lower channel subsidies and promotions about 1.5 points (last year carried trade-in subsidies). The three sum to 5.5 points, 0.1 off actual.

The company's explanation is a single word: "mix." But mix cannot explain 5.6 points — even a higher Pro mix contributes only about 2 points structurally. The remaining 3.5 points must come from refunds and reduced promotions, neither of which is sustainable. That is why next quarter's guidance drops straight to 47%–48%.

This breakdown has one testable point: if the margin gain truly came from mix, next quarter's gross margin (before the iPhone 18 launch) should hold above 49%; if it came from refunds and promotions, it will fall to around 47%. The company's own guidance has already given the answer.

Now the multiplication: for every 1-point drop in gross margin, how much does EPS fall?

Based on FY2026E revenue of USD 469 billion: every 1 percentage point change in gross margin moves pre-tax profit by USD 4.69 billion, after-tax by about USD 4 billion, and EPS by about USD 0.27. Falling from 50.1% to 47.5% (midpoint) is 2.6 points, cutting EPS by USD 0.70.

What does that USD 0.70 mean? It wipes out 7% of the FY2027 consensus EPS of USD 10.0. That is why the first thing sell-side does after earnings is cut EPS (Morgan Stanley from 10.39 to 10.00), not cut the target price—the target price is EPS × multiple; EPS moves first, the multiple follows.

If memory prices rise another round in 2027 and gross margin falls to 45%–46%, EPS drops another USD 0.4–0.7. EPS would then be USD 8.6–9.0, which at 25x gives a share price of USD 215–225. That is where the bear case comes from—it is not guesswork.

The other direction also needs calculating: if services return to 15% and AI starts charging, gross margin holds at 48% and EPS is 10.4–10.6. Upside is only USD 0.4–0.6, downside is USD 1.4–1.8. That asymmetry is the source of the "inverted risk-reward."

Finally, cash: why it can keep buying back stock

Operating cash flow is about RMB 115 billion a year, capex about RMB 12 billion, free cash flow about RMB 103 billion (rough calculation). Capex is only 2.6% of revenue — Apple's most underappreciated advantage: it does not need to build fabs; its capacity is other people's.

Almost all of this money goes back to shareholders: TTM buybacks of about RMB 100 billion and dividends of about RMB 15.6 billion, together exceeding free cash flow. The gap is covered by cash on hand and debt issuance, so net cash has fallen from over RMB 80 billion three years ago to about RMB 30 billion.

How long can this model last? As long as operating cash flow holds up, it can keep running. But if gross margin drops 5 points and net profit falls 15%, free cash flow would fall from RMB 103 billion to RMB 88 billion — and then the RMB 100 billion buyback would have to shrink, and the per-share accretion story would break. This is the subtle link between "gross margin" and "buybacks."

One more number is worth watching: the average buyback price. The TTM average is about USD 270 (rough calculation); buying stock at 34x earnings is only one-third as efficient as in 2016 (10x). Whether capital allocation is done well depends not on how much you buy, but on the price you pay.

The accounting effect of buybacks: why EPS outpaces net profit

Net profit +27.1%, EPS +28.7% — the 1.6-point gap comes entirely from share count reduction. Apple buys back about USD 100 billion a year, cutting shares outstanding by 1.9%, which automatically adds 1.9% to EPS growth each year. This is "hidden growth," and a key reason many underestimate Apple.

But it comes at a cost: buybacks require cash, and that cash comes from free cash flow. When gross margin slides and free cash flow falls from USD 103 billion to USD 88 billion, buybacks must shrink. So the "buyback-accretive EPS" engine is ultimately constrained by gross margin.

Take it to the limit: if gross margin stays at 46% long term and net profit grows 3% a year, but the company still needs 1.9% share count reduction, the cash required for buybacks would exceed free cash flow — at that point Apple must either cut buybacks or add leverage. Either path ends the "automatic 2% annual accretion" story.

An easily overlooked line item: other income

Operating profit of RMB 35.695 billion and net profit of RMB 29.789 billion leave a RMB 5.9 billion gap — not tax, but the net of "other income and expenses + tax." The largest item in Apple's "other income" is interest income on cash and securities, about USD 5 billion a year.

This is why interest rates affect Apple's profit: with USD 130 billion of cash on the books, every 1-point rate cut reduces interest income by USD 1.3 billion and EPS by USD 0.08. In a rate-cutting cycle, this line turns from a tailwind into a headwind.

The comparison makes it clearer: Apple's operating margin is 32.6% and net margin 27.2%, a 5.4-point gap that is tax (about 15%) plus interest. The structure is clean, with nowhere for one-off items to hide — except the USD 0.11 tariff refund.

Conclusion

Conclusion of Part Four: of the 50.1% gross margin, about 3.5 points are unsustainable. Lower rebates and promotions were one-off, so a return to 47%–48% next quarter is "normalization", not "deterioration".

The real focus is 2027: if memory prices rise another round, a 45% gross margin and EPS of USD 8.6 imply a share price of USD 215.The financial truth is one chain: gross margin → EPS → buyback capacity → value per share.
