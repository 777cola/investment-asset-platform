⑦ Risk list: five items, two already materializing — Apple

Core view

This risk section does not list "potential challenges"; it lists only "five things whose losses can be calculated." Each item gives a probability, an impact and an observable indicator.

Two things now unfolding

① Memory costs (100% probability, −2.6pct gross margin impact). DRAM up 60%–70%, NAND doubled, with three suppliers accounting for over 90%. Apple has already absorbed 2.6 points; next quarter's 47%–48% guidance is the evidence. Observable: quarterly gross margin guidance.

② Services stall (100% probability, −3x P/E impact). Services growth 16%→12.1%, below the consensus for the first time in over three years. Commission rates compressed by regulation and search placements threatened by AI search — together these two account for more than half of services revenue. Observable: quarterly services revenue growth and deferred revenue.

The two differ in nature: the first hits earnings, the second hits the multiple. The first will pass (memory capacity expansion in H2 2027); the second will not (regulation is irreversible). So over the long run, the second is more costly.

One more detail: the first is already priced in (stock fell 7.35% after earnings); the second has only just begun to be priced in (services stalled for only one quarter). If services growth is still 12% next quarter, the market will re-rate the multiple.

Three points on probability events

③ App Store systematically broken up (30% probability, EPS impact −6% to −8% plus −5 on the multiple). The EU DMA has already forced open external payments, and the US Epic case has already required allowing external links. If the commission rate falls systematically from 30% to 15%, services revenue stays flat but gross profit halves. Observable: EU enforcement rules, US state legislation.

④ Continued share loss in Greater China (40% probability, revenue impact −3 points). This quarter RMB 18.816 billion, +22.4%, but below the consensus of 3.9%, and driven by a low base. Huawei foldables plus the HarmonyOS ecosystem keep applying pressure above RMB 6,000. Observable: quarterly Greater China revenue, IDC share data.

⑤ Form-factor shift (20% probability over ten years, moat re-rating impact −30%). If AI moves interaction from screens to voice and glasses, the iPhone goes from "entry point" to "accessory." Vision Pro is still at the million-unit level and cannot support a second curve. Observable: AI hardware shipments, iPhone replacement cycle.

Of the three, the fourth deserves the most attention: it is not "might happen," it is "slowly happening." Greater China growth fell from 38% to 28%, then to 22.4% — three consecutive quarters of stepping down. The market attributes it to the "base" every time, but the base will not help you for three quarters running.

Combined expected loss of the five

Multiply probability by impact: memory −2.6% (already occurred, booked in the current period), services −3x P/E (about −9%), regulation −3.6%, Greater China −1.6%, form factor −6%. Total about −22%, of which the already-occurred portion is about −12%.

In other words: the market's current price has already priced in the "memory + services" pair, but has not priced in the "regulation + form factor" pair.

This is also why the bear case in this report is USD 215 (−29%) rather than −22% — the extra 7 points are the compounding effect of "two tail risks occurring simultaneously." The value of a risk list is not prediction; it is letting you know how much you would lose in the worst case, and then deciding position size.

One more thing to be clear about: Apple's risk resilience is extremely strong. Net cash of about USD 30 billion, free cash flow of USD 103 billion a year, and brand pricing power intact. So these risks do not change "whether you can hold it," only "at what price you hold it."

The timetable of risk: when to worry

The five risks do not occur simultaneously; only when placed on a timeline do they become useful. Next two quarters: gross margin (already occurring), services growth (already occurring). First half of 2027: Greater China share (settled after the iPhone 18 launch). Second half of 2027: regulatory enforcement details (EU DMA), whether memory prices ease. 2028 and beyond: form-factor change.

The implication of this ordering: near term, watch gross margin; medium term, watch China; long term, watch form factor. Trading long-term risk in the short term is the most common mistake—for example, selling out at USD 300 because "AI may disrupt the phone," then missing the 20% gain in 2026.

The reverse is equally true: ignoring gross-margin decline because of "long-term bullishness" is another mistake. A risk timetable essentially tells you which risk is worth money now and which only later.

The overlooked sixth point: interest rates and exchange rates

Beyond the five, there is one more risk that is unglamorous but real: foreign exchange. Management's guidance explicitly states that FX will drag revenue growth by about 2.5 percentage points next quarter. More than 60% of Apple's revenue comes from outside the US, so a stronger dollar directly compresses reported revenue.

The interest-rate effect is more subtle: interest income on the USD 130 billion of cash on the balance sheet runs about USD 5 billion a year. A 1-point rate cut reduces EPS by USD 0.08. And falling rates usually come with "high-multiple asset expansion" — two things that pull Apple in opposite directions.

Neither changes the investment thesis, but they explain the part of "why the stock fell even though fundamentals did not change." When attributing performance, isolating FX and rates avoids mistaking macro swings for company problems.

Conclusion

Conclusion of Part Seven: five risks — two already materializing (memory, services), three probabilistic (regulation, China, form factor). Combined expected loss is about 22%, of which 12% has already occurred.

None of them would make Apple insolvent. So these risks do not change "whether you can hold it," only "at what price you hold it."
