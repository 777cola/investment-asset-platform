⑦ Risk list: five items, two already materializing — Apple

Core view

This risk section does not list "potential challenges"; it lists only "five things whose losses can be calculated." Each item carries a probability, an impact and an observable indicator.

Two things now unfolding

① Memory costs (100% probability, −2.6pct gross margin impact). DRAM is up 60%–70% and NAND has doubled, with three suppliers accounting for over 90% of supply. Apple has already absorbed 2.6 points, as next quarter's 47%–48% guidance confirms. Observable: quarterly gross margin guidance.

② Services stall (100% probability, −3x P/E impact). Services growth slowed from 16% to 12.1%, missing consensus for the first time in over three years. Regulatory compression of commission rates and AI search threatening search placements together account for more than half of services revenue. Observable: quarterly services revenue growth and deferred revenue.

The two differ in nature: the first hits earnings, the second hits the multiple. The first will pass (memory capacity expansion in H2 2027); the second will not (regulation is irreversible). Over the long run, therefore, the second is more costly.

One further detail: the first is already priced in (the stock fell 7.35% after earnings), while the second has only just begun to be priced in (services have stalled for just one quarter). If services growth is still 12% next quarter, the market will re-rate the multiple.

Three points on probability events

③ App Store forced unbundling (30% probability, −6% to −8% EPS impact plus −5 on the multiple). The EU DMA has already forced open external payments, and the US Epic case has already required allowing external links. If the commission rate falls systematically from 30% to 15%, services revenue stays flat but gross profit halves. Observable: EU enforcement rules, US state legislation.

④ Continued share loss in Greater China (40% probability, −3pts revenue impact). This quarter RMB 18.816 billion, +22.4%, but 3.9% below consensus and driven by a low base. Huawei foldables plus the HarmonyOS ecosystem keep applying pressure above RMB 6,000. Observable: quarterly Greater China revenue, IDC share data.

⑤ Form-factor shift (20% probability over ten years, −30% moat re-rating impact). If AI moves interaction from screens to voice and glasses, the iPhone goes from "entry point" to "accessory." Vision Pro is still at the million-unit level and cannot support a second curve. Observable: AI hardware shipments, iPhone replacement cycle.

Of the three, the fourth deserves the most attention: it is not "might happen," it is "slowly happening." Greater China growth fell from 38% to 28%, then to 22.4% — three consecutive quarters of stepping down. The market attributes it to the "base" every time, but the base will not help you for three quarters running.

Combined expected loss of the five

Probability multiplied by impact: memory −2.6% (already occurred and booked in the current period), services −3x P/E (about −9%), regulation −3.6%, Greater China −1.6%, form factor −6%. Total about −22%, of which the already-occurred portion is about −12%.

Put differently, the current share price already discounts the "memory + services" pair, but not the "regulation + form factor" pair.

That is also why our bear case is USD 215 (−29%) rather than −22%: the extra 7 points reflect the compounding of "two tail risks occurring simultaneously." A risk list is valuable not for prediction but for showing how much you would lose in the worst case — and then sizing your position accordingly.

One further point: Apple's resilience to these risks is exceptionally strong. Net cash of about USD 30 billion, free cash flow of USD 103 billion a year, and brand pricing power intact. These risks therefore do not change "whether you can hold it," only "at what price you hold it."

The timetable of risk: when to worry

The five risks do not occur simultaneously; only when placed on a timeline do they become useful. Next two quarters: gross margin (already occurring), services growth (already occurring). First half of 2027: Greater China share (settled after the iPhone 18 launch). Second half of 2027: regulatory enforcement details (EU DMA), whether memory prices ease. 2028 and beyond: form-factor change.

The implication of this ordering: near term, watch gross margin; medium term, watch China; long term, watch form factor. Trading long-term risk in the short term is the most common mistake—for example, selling out at USD 300 because "AI may disrupt the phone," then missing the 20% gain in 2026.

The reverse is equally true: ignoring gross-margin decline because of "long-term bullishness" is another mistake. A risk timetable essentially tells you which risk is worth money now and which only later.

The overlooked sixth factor: rates and FX

Beyond the five, one further risk is unglamorous but real: foreign exchange. Management's guidance explicitly flags FX as a ~2.5pp drag on revenue growth next quarter. More than 60% of Apple's revenue comes from outside the US, so a stronger dollar directly compresses reported revenue.

The interest-rate effect is subtler: interest income on the USD 130 billion of cash on the balance sheet runs at about USD 5 billion a year, and a 1-point rate cut reduces EPS by USD 0.08. Falling rates, moreover, usually come with "high-multiple asset expansion" — two forces pulling Apple in opposite directions.

Neither changes the investment thesis, but together they explain part of "why the stock fell even though fundamentals did not change." When attributing performance, isolating FX and rates avoids mistaking macro swings for company problems.

Conclusion

Part Seven conclusion: five risks — two already materialising (memory, services), three probabilistic (regulation, China, form factor). Combined expected loss is roughly 22%, of which 12% has already occurred.

None of these would render Apple insolvent. They therefore do not change whether you can hold the stock, only the price at which you hold it.
