⑧ Decision framework: three questions, two lines, one action — Apple

Core view

The previous seven pieces were research; this one is the decision. Compress two hours of reading into three questions, two price lines and one action.

Three questions to answer before deciding whether to buy

Question one: will this business still be here in ten years? Yes. 2.3 billion devices, 1.5 billion subscriptions, 50% gross margin, RMB 103 billion of free cash flow a year. Answer: yes.

Question two: how fast is it growing now? FY2026 revenue +12.7% (estimated), FY2027 about +8%. Services growth slows from 16% to 12%, Greater China laps a low base, and iPhone ASP is left carrying the load. Answer: high single digit to low double digit — not a growth stock.

Question three: how much is the market paying for that growth? 34x TTM, 30x FY2027. Answer: it is paying for 15% growth when the reality is 8%–10%. Three questions answered, and the conclusion follows: good company, expensive price.

The order of these three questions cannot be swapped. Ask about the business first (is it a good company), then growth (what multiple does it deserve), then price (is it expensive now). Ask in reverse and you usually fall in love with the company first, then find reasons for the price.

Two price lines, fixed and immovable

Buy line USD 260 = FY2027 EPS USD 10.0 × 26x. 26x marks the low end of Apple's five-year valuation range excluding the bubble phase—the level at which "even if growth slows to 5%, you don't lose money." At this line, start building the position in tranches.

Trim line USD 373 = EPS USD 11.3 × 33x. That price fully discounts the optimistic scenario: AI monetisation succeeds, the foldable ramps, and services growth returns to 15%. At this line, cut the position by half, however compelling the story.

In between (260–373), do nothing. This range absorbed 70% of the past year's time and 90% of investors' mistakes. Not acting isn't laziness—it's admitting you can't predict short-term moves.

Within the range, a further layer of buffer: USD 280 (28x) supports a 3% base position, USD 240 (24x) allows adding to 8%. The point of tranching is that you don't have to guess the bottom—whichever tranche the price reaches, that's the capital you deploy.

One action: what to do now

Do three things now, none of which involve buying or selling.

First, build a 0%–2% tracking position. Not to make money, but to force yourself to read the financials carefully each quarter, calculate gross margin and track services growth. Research without a position easily becomes armchair theorizing.

Second, put three readings on the calendar. Gross margin (47% is the psychological line, 46% the warning line), services growth (is 12% the bottom or a midpoint), and Greater China (RMB 15 billion is the warning line).All three are visible quarterly; no need to wait for a narrative.

Third, wait. Wait for the price to reach 260, or for services to return to 15% with AI revenue broken out separately.If either condition is met, act; if neither, do nothing. That is the entire operating discipline.

A real-world mistake: buying at the 2021 peak

Apple's P/E peaked above 30x at end-2021 before falling to 21x in 2022, a maximum drawdown of 27%. Fundamentals did not deteriorate that year — revenue was still growing and services were still accelerating — yet the multiple compressed from 30x to 21x and the stock fell nearly 30%.

The lesson is not "don't buy expensive" but "when you buy expensive, your return depends on the multiple not contracting." The multiple is also the least controllable variable, set by interest rates, sentiment and narrative — none of which you can predict.

Today's setup is more delicate than in 2021: 2021 was "20% growth + 30x," 2026 is "10% growth + 34x." The growth buffer is thinner and the multiple higher. Hence our buy point of USD 260 — at that level, even with a further 5x of multiple compression, there is still a 10% margin of safety.

A final methodological note: the value of a decision framework lies not in "calculating accurately" but in "writing it down early." Price lines must be drawn when sentiment is calm, so that on the day earnings collapse you will not change your mind on the spot.

Write it on a card and stick it on the edge of your monitor

Once the research is done, it has to fit on a single card. Five lines only: Company = the world's best consumer-electronics business; moat = ecosystem (9 points); growth = 8%–10%; fair multiple = 26–30x; buy point = USD 260.

Why a card rather than a report? Because reports get forgotten; cards do not. When the stock runs to 350 and the market is all shouting AI, one glance at "fair multiple 26–30x" on the card tells you what to do.

The card also needs a blank line: "what would make me change my mind." Three things would: gross margin below 46% for two consecutive quarters, services growth below 10%, and AI revenue broken out separately with gross margin above 60%. The first two make me sell; the third makes me buy.

Conclusion

Part Eight conclusion: good company, expensive price, no catalyst. Answer three questions, draw two lines, execute one action—a 0%–2% observation position, build below USD 260, halve above USD 373.

Final word: Apple is worth two hours of your research, but not worth acting on at USD 303. Research and buying are two different things; keeping them separate is the only value of this framework.
