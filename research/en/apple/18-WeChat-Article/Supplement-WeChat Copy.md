Title: Apple delivered its strongest June quarter ever, then fell 9% in three days

On August 3, Apple closed at USD 303.42, down 10.8% from USD 340.08 on July 28.

Just four days earlier, it had reported a near-flawless quarter: revenue USD 109.417 billion, +16.4% YoY; iPhone USD 54.252 billion, +21.7% YoY; net income USD 29.789 billion, +27.1% YoY; EPS USD 2.02.The June quarter is normally the weakest of the year, and Apple turned it into its highest ever.

Good earnings, falling stock. The two are not contradictory — the market is not looking at this quarter, but the next.

First number: 50.1%.That is this quarter's gross margin, up 3.6 percentage points YoY, one of the best levels in Apple's history. A company that sells phones has a higher gross margin than many software companies — not luck, but two decades of accumulated ecosystem rent: App Store commissions, iCloud subscriptions, Google's default search slot, 1.5 billion paying users.

Second number: 12.1%.That is services revenue growth YoY. Looks decent, but it was 16% last quarter and 16% a year ago.This is the first time in more than three years that services revenue has come in below consensus.

Why is this the most damaging? Because the market gives Apple a 34x P/E rather than 20x on the strength of the story that "a software company is growing inside a hardware company." Services growth falling from 16% to 12% discounts that story.

Third number: 47%–48%.That is management's gross margin guidance for next quarter, versus 50.1% this quarter. Cook said something very blunt on the call: "We're struggling with the supply chain — memory has only three core suppliers."

Translation: DRAM is up 60%–70% this year, NAND has doubled, Apple absorbed half itself and offset the rest through Pro models and reduced promotions. The buffer is used up.

One detail that is easy to miss: of the USD 2.02 EPS, USD 0.11 came from a tariff refund. Strip it out and EPS is USD 1.91, just two cents above the consensus of USD 1.89.Revenue above the consensus of 0.5%, EPS propped up by a one-off, guidance below consensus — those three together explain the entire 7.35% post-earnings drop.

So is the stock expensive? USD 303 corresponds to 34x TTM and 30x next year's expected earnings. And management's guidance for next quarter is revenue growth of 9%–11%.

Paying 30x for 10% growth means discounting 2027 AI monetization back to today.

Run three scenarios: bear case USD 215 (gross margin compressed to 45%, earnings USD 8.6, 25x), base case USD 290 (earnings USD 10, 29x), bull case USD 373 (successful AI charging + foldable ramp, USD 11.3, 33x).Upside 23%, downside 29%, base case still a 4% loss.

That is why the stock fell from 340 all the way to 303 post-earnings and still has not stopped — not because the business collapsed, but because the risk/reward is wrong.

So what to do? Two lines, fixed in stone.

Line one: buy below USD 260.That is 26x next year's earnings — a level where "even if growth falls to 5% you don't lose money."Line two: trim above USD 373.That is the price at which the bull case is fully realized; when it gets there, you leave, no matter how good the story sounds.

Between 260 and 373, do nothing. That range accounted for 70% of the past year's time and 90% of investors' mistakes.

What I am doing now: holding a 2% tracking position, not adding.Not to make money, but to force myself to read the earnings carefully, calculate gross margin and watch services growth every quarter.

One last point that may not be popular:Apple is one of the companies I most want to hold for ten years, and USD 303 is one of the prices I least want to buy at.

Between a good company and a good stock lies a price. That price is now 260.

(Data from Apple's FY2026Q3 earnings report and earnings call; stock price as of the August 3, 2026 close. Not investment advice.)

One more note on Greater China. This quarter was USD 18.8 billion, +22.4% YoY — looks great, but below the consensus of 3.9%.And that 22% is built on last year's low base — in the year Huawei returned, Apple's China revenue was declining.Climbing out of a hole does not mean winning new share.

Another number worth remembering: services revenue USD 30.7 billion, 28% of revenue, gross margin 75.6%.This segment contributes USD 93 billion of gross profit a year, more than all of Samsung Electronics, with almost zero capex.This is Apple's real money printer, and the segment regulators most want to break up.

As for AI, my view is rather cold: Siri AI launched in June to decent public beta reviews, but the company itself says "paid value-added services have not yet formed scaled revenue."Translation: you can't see it in the financials.Investors paying 30x for revenue they cannot yet see is the most fragile part of this bull case.

So the play now is simple: don't chase, don't short, don't guess.If it falls to 260 I buy, if it rises to 373 I trim, in between I just watch.Four earnings days a year, read carefully four times — far more useful than watching the tape every day.

One final reminder: do not confuse "I am bullish on Apple long term" with "I should buy Apple now."The former is a judgment on the company; the latter is a judgment on the price.Between the two lies a number.
