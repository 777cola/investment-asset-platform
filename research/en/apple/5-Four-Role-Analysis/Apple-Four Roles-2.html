Financial analysis: highly efficient at making money, but gross margin has peaked — Apple

Core view

Verdict first: this is a money printer, but this quarter's report contains one clear turning signal — gross margin of 50.1% is a historic high, with guidance of 47%–48% for next quarter. It has peaked.

Income statement: all three lines improving, except services

Revenue RMB 109.42 billion, +16.4% YoY; gross profit RMB 54.77 billion, gross margin 50.1% (+3.6pct YoY); operating income RMB 35.70 billion, operating margin 32.6%; net income RMB 29.79 billion, net margin 27.2%; diluted EPS USD 2.02, +28.7% YoY. Every line is a record high for the period.

But break down the mix: product revenue RMB 78.68 billion, +18.1%; services RMB 30.74 billion, +12.1%. Services growth fell from 16% a year ago to 12%, the first stall in this line in more than three years. Half of Apple's valuation premium rests on the assumption that services always grow above 15%, and this quarter breaks that assumption.

Costs also merit a look: operating income of RMB 35.70 billion implies total costs of RMB 18.95 billion (revenue less gross profit less operating income). Extrapolating from last year's cost ratios, R&D is roughly RMB 9.9 billion and SG&A roughly RMB 9.0 billion. R&D at 9% of revenue is a record high—AI spending is real money going out, it just has not yet turned into revenue.

Cash flow and capital allocation: USD 100 billion of buybacks in one year

Apple's cash machine needs no complicated explanation: roughly USD 115 billion in annual operating cash flow (rough estimate), about USD 12 billion in capex, and about USD 103 billion in free cash flow. Nearly all of this money goes to shareholder returns—about USD 100 billion of buybacks and about USD 15.6 billion of dividends over the past four quarters, totaling more than free cash flow.

The buyback effect is direct: shares outstanding fell 1.9% year over year, equivalent to an automatic 1.9% boost to earnings per share. This is also why EPS growth (+28.7%) exceeds net income growth (+27.1%). What Buffett likes most is exactly this kind of company—one that can grow per-share value without additional capital.

The risk lies in the "value for money" of the buybacks. Apple bought back a large amount of stock at high prices in 2024–2025, at an average cost of about USD 230; if it continues large buybacks above USD 300 in the future, that amounts to buying its own stock at 34 times earnings—that is not capital allocation, that is handing money to the sellers. This is the first thing to watch once the new CEO takes over.

The ceiling on gross margin: how much can memory price hikes add

Let's do a rough calculation. Memory (DRAM+NAND) accounts for about 12% of the iPhone BOM (rough estimate, based on mainstream teardown reports); in 2026 DRAM contract prices rise 60%–70% and NAND doubles, which weighted together lifts memory cost by about 75%. 12%×75% = BOM up 9%, corresponding to a gross margin decline of about 4.5 points.

Apple's actual guidance is 50.1%→47.5% (midpoint), a drop of only 2.6 points. Where does the gap come from? Three sources: a higher Pro model mix, long-term contract price locks with suppliers, and a structural hedge from a rising services mix. In other words, Apple absorbed about half of the memory price increase, and offset the other half through product mix and structure.

The implication of this estimate: if memory prices rise another round in 2027, Apple's buffer will be exhausted, gross margin could fall to 45%–46%, corresponding to 8%–10% EPS cuts. This is not speculation; it is a direction already written into next quarter's guidance. Buffett would say: a good company can withstand cost inflation, but withstanding it is not the same as being unaffected.

A sensitivity check: for every 1 percentage point change in gross margin, based on FY2026E revenue of RMB 469 billion, pre-tax profit changes by RMB 4.7 billion and after-tax by about RMB 4 billion, with EPS changing by USD 0.27. Falling from 50.1% to 47.5% is 2.6 points = USD 0.70 less EPS, which erases exactly 7% of the FY2027 consensus of USD 10.0. That is why the first thing sell-side does after earnings is cut EPS, not the target price.

Conclusion

Financial verdict: 8/10. Every income-statement line hit a same-period record; cash flow covers buybacks and dividends, automatically lifting per-share value. Marks deducted for peak gross margin and services deceleration—both appearing together means FY2027 EPS consensus (USD 10.0–10.4) risks a downward revision.
