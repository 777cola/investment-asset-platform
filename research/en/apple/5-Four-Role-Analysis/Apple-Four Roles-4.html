Risk assessment: five risks, two already materializing — Apple

Core view

Verdict first: Apple carries no risk of "dying," only the risk of "getting cheaper." That is good news for risk management, bad news for the odds.

Two things now unfolding: gross margin and share

The first is already in the financials: gross margin guidance cut from 50.1% to 47%–48%, with Cook conceding on the call that the company is "struggling for supply chain," and memory has only three core suppliers. This is structural, not a one-quarter disturbance. 100% probability, already happened, roughly −2.5pct hit to gross margin.

The second is also in the data: Greater China 18.816 billion, +22.4% YoY but 3.9% below consensus; Services 30.739 billion, +12.1% YoY but 2.0% below consensus. Both are "still growing but short of expectations" — the most easily overlooked risk, because it isn't frightening, just disappointing.

Combined effect: revenue still growing at a double-digit rate, but the margin ceiling and the growth ceiling appear at the same time. Will the market pay 34x for a "double top"? This quarter's answer: no — down 9% in three days.

Three probability events: regulation, geopolitics, structural change

Regulation is the biggest risk. The EU Digital Markets Act requires opening up external payments and app stores, and the US court in the Epic case requires allowing external payment links. If the commission rate falls systematically from 30% to 15%, services revenue stays flat but gross profit halves, implying a 6%–8% EPS cut and a 5-turn de-rating—the only risk that hits earnings and valuation at the same time.

Geopolitics is the second. Apple's supply chain is highly concentrated in China (assembly) and Taiwan (chips); any tightening of tariffs or export controls on either front delivers a double blow of higher costs and delivery delays. Apple is already shifting capacity to India and Vietnam, but India's share of capacity will still be under 20% in 2026 (rough estimate), so there is no near-term hedge.

Form-factor change is the third, and the longest-term risk. If AI shifts interaction from screens to voice plus glasses, the iPhone's standing will be diluted. Apple has the Vision Pro line, but 2026 sales remain in the million-unit range—not enough to support a second curve. Low probability (20% within ten years), enormous impact (moat re-rating).

The price implications of risk

Li Lu's framework converts risk into a "required discount," weighting three probabilistic events: regulatory 30% probability × −12% impact = −3.6%; geopolitical 20% × −8% = −1.6%; technical pattern 20% × −30% = −6%. That totals an expected discount of about −11%, implying a fair price of USD 270 (303×0.89).

The figure aligns with the judgments of Duan Yongping and Buffett, whose purchase prices fell between USD 250–280—precisely the level reached after "folding in all three risks." Four people using four methods arrived at the same range: no coincidence, but the same price anchor.

One addition: Apple's resilience to risk is extremely strong, with net cash of about USD 30 billion, free cash flow of 100 billion a year and brand pricing power intact. These risks should not make one "avoid" the stock, only "wait for the price"—which is also why this report's rating is "Watch" rather than "Avoid."

Finally, one risk easily mistaken for good news: tariff refunds. This quarter's EPS includes USD 0.11 from refunds; if tariff policy reverses again in 2027 (re-imposing duties), this money becomes a reverse one-off loss. Using one-off gains to support current-period EPS, then capitalizing it at a P/E multiple, is the most common mispricing in bull markets—it has already happened once this quarter, and is worth remembering.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: 5/10. Two of these risks are already materialising (gross margin, share) and three are probabilistic (regulation, geopolitics, form). None would render the company insolvent, but together they warrant an 11% discount that the current price does not embed.
