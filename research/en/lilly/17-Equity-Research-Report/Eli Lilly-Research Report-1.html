Core thesis

Eli Lilly's 2026Q3 was a clean sweep on all three metrics: revenue 7.9% above consensus, adjusted EPS 19.5% above consensus, and a third guidance raise. Fundamentals are among the best in pharma. But at USD 1,411.95 the stock trades on 48.2x TTM and 33.6x 2027E, and our neutral target price of USD 1,344 implies 5% downside. Conclusion: good company, bad price. Rating: Watch, wait for a pullback; buy line USD 1,200.

I. Company Overview

Eli Lilly and Company (NYSE: LLY) was founded in Indianapolis in 1876 and is the world's largest pharmaceutical company by market capitalisation. The stock closed at USD 1,411.95 on November 9, 2026, implying a market capitalisation of about USD 1.33 trillion (941 million shares × USD 1,411.95), on TTM EPS of USD 29.32 and a P/E of 48.2x.

The revenue mix is highly concentrated. Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was USD 67.17 billion, of which tirzepatide (Mounjaro for diabetes plus Zepbound for weight loss) contributed USD 43.768 billion, or 65%; the balance came from Verzenio (breast cancer), Taltz (psoriasis), Kisunla (Alzheimer's) and others. By geography, the US accounts for 62% and overseas markets 38%, with China at USD 2.582 billion for the quarter, up 75% YoY.

The share price now sits close to its highs: the November 9, 2026 close of USD 1,411.95 was up 4.28% on the day and within a 52-week range of USD 851.21–1,417.99, just 0.4% below the high. Over the past three months the stock has risen from USD 1,215.02 to USD 1,411.95 (+16.2%), and is up 26.6% from the September 11 interim low of USD 1,115.70. In other words, the market has priced in the good news from a single earnings report over two months.

For context, Eli Lilly's 2024 revenue was USD 45.043 billion with Non-GAAP EPS of USD 12.99; 2026 guidance is USD 91–92 billion with EPS of USD 35.00–36.00. Revenue doubling and EPS nearly tripling in two years is the entire basis for the 48x P/E. The problem is that such growth cannot persist over the long term.

II. Industry analysis

Global GLP-1 is the fastest-growing major category in pharmaceutical history: in the first three quarters of 2026, tirzepatide at USD 43.768 billion and semaglutide at USD 26.864 billion combined for USD 70.6 billion. On demand, more than 100 million obese Americans meet treatment criteria but only a few million are on therapy, implying single-digit penetration; on supply, peptide manufacturing capacity is the bottleneck, and 2023–2024 saw global shortages.

2026 is the "year of oral GLP-1": oral Wegovy launched in January and Foundayo (orforglipron) was approved in April, shifting the industry from a "duopoly selling injectables" to a "duopoly selling tablets plus injectables." On policy, the November 2025 MFN agreement set the Medicare net price at USD 245 per month with a USD 50 patient out-of-pocket cap, putting prices on a one-way downward path.

Three policy dates matter: November 2025, the MFN agreement with the US government (Medicare net price USD 245 per month, USD 50 patient out-of-pocket cap); January 2026, launch of the TrumpRx platform; July 2026, a temporary Medicare pilot bringing weight-loss GLP-1s into reimbursement. Together, these three policies trade "lower prices" for "higher volume" — the key to reading all 2026 volume and pricing data.

III. Business model

Business model in one sentence: collect rent on an exclusive molecule during the patent term. The same drug trades at four prices — list price USD 1,000–1,350 per month, Medicare net price USD 245, cash-pay channel USD 149, and a patient out-of-pocket cap of USD 50. Eli Lilly earns the spread between net price and cost, with a gross margin of 84.4% and a net margin of 26.4% in Q3 2026.

On cost structure, Eli Lilly is pursuing vertical integration: it is building its own peptide API and fill-finish capacity (USD 27 billion announced in February 2025 to build four sites) and using LillyDirect direct sales and telehealth (partnership with Hims & Hers in November 2026) to bypass some PBMs. In Foundayo's early launch, 45% of prescriptions came from LillyDirect, 35% from telehealth and 20% from traditional pharmacies.

A rough unit-economics pass: at a Medicare net price of USD 245 per month, one patient generates USD 2,940 a year; at an 84.4% gross margin, gross profit is about USD 2,480; after sales expense (15%–18%) and R&D (over 15%), net profit is about USD 780 a year. More than 100 million people in the US are eligible; 5 million on long-term therapy means USD 39 billion of net profit — that explains the source of the USD 1.33 trillion market cap.

IV. Financial analysis

2026Q3 revenue USD 24.397 billion, +38.6% YoY (base 17.601 billion), 7.9% above consensus; net profit 6.451 billion, +16% YoY; Non-GAAP EPS USD 7.48, 19.5% above consensus; Non-GAAP gross margin 84.4%, +0.8pct YoY. First three quarters revenue 67.170 billion.

Profit growth trails revenue by 22.6 percentage points, mainly on a one-off USD 4.2 billion in-process R&D charge from an acquisition (USD 4.57 per share). The balance sheet is clean: interest-bearing debt under USD 30 billion, interest coverage above 50x, ROE 74.6% in 2024. The cost is capex at over 20% of revenue, leaving free cash flow at only 0.6–0.7x net profit (rough calculation).

By region, US Q3 revenue USD 15.1 billion (+34%, volume +41%, price -7%), overseas USD 9.3 billion (+47%, volume +76%, price -29%), China USD 2.582 billion (+75%). The overseas volume-price mix is the biggest warning sign: a 29% price cut bought 76% volume growth, and gross margin is being held up by scale, not pricing. Overseas share rose from 36% to 38%, an overlooked downward slope.

One more detail: 2026Q3 diluted EPS USD 7.22, +16% YoY, in line with net profit growth, indicating no change in share count and no large preferred dividend. The income statement's transmission chain holds—revenue, net profit and EPS reconcile across all three lines, the basic test of financial statement credibility.

V. Management and governance

Chairman and CEO David Ricks joined Lilly in 1996 and took over in 2017, an internally grown manager. Three key decisions in his tenure—betting on the GIP/GLP-1 dual target, building USD 27 billion of capacity in-house and selling direct through LillyDirect—each took three to five years to pay off, and with hindsight all three were right.

On capital allocation, capital goes mainly into capacity and M&A (Verve at about USD 1.3 billion and Adverum at about USD 225 million in 2025), while shareholder returns stay conservative: an annual dividend of about USD 4.5 per share, a 0.3% yield, and buybacks that only offset dilution. Governance is single-class stock with no dual voting rights. The risk is a blank succession plan: Ricks turns 60 in 2026.

The M&A list deserves a closer look: Verve Therapeutics (base editing, about USD 1.3 billion) and Adverum Biotechnologies (ophthalmic gene therapy, about USD 225 million) in 2025, plus about USD 4.2 billion of acquired in-process R&D expensed in one hit in Q3 2026. Such deals are expensed in the current period and pay back three to five years later; the benefit is no goodwill buildup, the drawback is quarterly earnings distortion. The company does not disclose ROIIC for the three years after an acquisition, a governance gap.

The incentive structure also merits a look: more than 80% of Ricks's pay package is stock and performance shares, and the metrics are usually revenue growth, EPS and pipeline milestones, rarely relative shareholder return. That explains why the company dares to spend USD 27 billion on capacity—revenue and share are the metrics, return on capital is not.

VI. Growth drivers

Three growth drivers, ranked by certainty. ① Penetration: more than 100 million eligible people in the US, only a few million on treatment — volume has ample room, and Q3 sales +53% is the evidence. ② New indications: sleep apnea (Foundayo has already shown improvement; the company says it will file before year-end), cardiovascular risk, fatty liver — the same molecule monetised repeatedly. ③ Next-generation pipeline: retatrutide, a triple agonist, delivered 20.8%–25% weight loss at 80 weeks in Phase III, with a filing planned for Q1 2027; it is the mainstay of the 2030s.

The downside: oral drug Foundayo posted just USD 184 million in Q3 (USD 124 million in the US), about USD 50 million below consensus, and the sell-side has cut 2026 estimates from USD 3 billion to USD 1.3 billion. Oral is the bridge for the post-2028 period, and that bridge is not yet built.

The indication-expansion timeline is worth tracking: Foundayo has already shown improvement in sleep apnea, and the company says it will file for the new indication with the FDA before year-end; tirzepatide's cardiovascular benefit and metabolic fatty liver data are accumulating; beyond TRIUMPH-2 (type 2 diabetes with obesity, 20.8% weight loss at 80 weeks), retatrutide's Phase III programme has several obesity indications reading out. Selling the same molecule more than once is the most cost-effective growth in this kind of business.

One contrarian angle: Eli Lilly is putting money into capacity and M&A at its most profitable moment rather than buying back stock, which is right at 48x P/E (a buyback would mean buying itself at a high price). But if growth falls below 10%, the same "invest in capacity" playbook becomes capital misallocation; whether management then pivots to buybacks is the key test of their honesty.

VII. Competitive landscape

Duopoly: in Q1 2026 Eli Lilly held 60% of the global weight-loss market and Novo Nordisk 40%, together above 95%. Lilly has already overtaken Novo in injectables, with Zepbound's new US obesity prescriptions exceeding Wegovy's; in oral GLP-1s Novo leads the first round (Q3 oral Wegovy USD 811 million and 183,000 weekly prescriptions vs Foundayo USD 184 million and 63,000).

The second tier is Amgen (MariTide, monthly dosing, Phase III) and Pfizer (oral GLP-1 pipeline), both still in clinical stages. The third force is Chinese manufacturers: semaglutide's China patent expires in 2026, and generics and improved new drugs are queuing up. The most critical competition comes from payers — Medicare, commercial insurers, PBMs — which do not chase share, only set prices.

Capacity comparison reveals the physical basis of competition: in February 2025 Eli Lilly announced at least USD 27 billion to build four US sites (three API, one injectable, completed before 2030), with the Concord plant alone at USD 1.7 billion and completion in 2027, and more than USD 50 billion invested in the US since 2020; Novo is responding with tenfold-scale expansion, but by 2026 its capacity utilization is already below full.Capacity is the dividing line in this round of competition, and the source of potential oversupply in 2028.

The time dimension of competition must be kept clear: injectables are "a war already won," orals are "a war being fought," and retatrutide is "a war not yet begun."The market counts all three as wins, which is the core reason valuations are too high. Losing any one of them makes the 48x multiple untenable.

VIII. Risk factors

Risks ranked by probability × impact.① Valuation risk (high):48.2x TTM; any quarter below expectations could trigger a 20% drawdown; annualised volatility 31.6%, implying a probability above 60% of a 20% drawdown within one year.② Payer price pressure (high):Medicare at USD 245/month, overseas pricing -29%; price cuts only go one way.③ Oral competition (medium):losing the oral market would dismantle the bridge to the post-2028 transition.

④ Capacity impairment (medium-low):USD 27 billion of capacity may run into leftover demand growth in 2028.⑤ Patent cliff (certain but distant):US expiry in May 2036.⑥ Regulation (low):on November 9 the FDA required a NAION eye-risk warning; impact is close to zero, but it opens a gap.

A sensitivity test: if 2027 EPS is cut 5% from USD 42 to USD 39.9 while the market simultaneously compresses the multiple from 32x to 28x, the target price falls from USD 1,344 to USD 1,117 (-21%).This is the leverage effect of a 48x valuation — a small earnings miss, a large price correction.Conversely, a 5% EPS upgrade with the multiple unchanged yields only a 5% gain. The odds are inherently unfavourable.

One more structural risk: tirzepatide accounts for 65.9% of revenue, at USD 43.768 billion for the first nine months.A clinical, regulatory or supply incident for this one molecule would strike the core of the market cap, and such incidents are unpredictable.Concentration risk cannot be resolved through diversification, only through position control.

9. Earnings forecast

Forecast assumptions: 2026 uses the midpoint of company guidance (revenue USD 91.5 billion, Non-GAAP EPS USD 35.50); 2027 revenue +18% to USD 108 billion, with tirzepatide growth slowing to 15% and Foundayo ramping to USD 4–5 billion; 2028 +15% to USD 124.2 billion. Gross margin declines each year—84.0% in 2026, 82.0% in 2027 and 79.0% in 2028—as US net prices converge toward overseas levels and depreciation rises.

Corresponding Non-GAAP EPS: USD 35.50 in 2026, USD 42.00 in 2027 and USD 48.00 in 2028. Conservative versus sell-side—RBC raised its 2027 EPS forecast to USD 49.33 in July 2026. Two assumptions drive the gap: we see the 2027 oral ramp as slower than the sell-side expects, and gross margin declining faster.

Segment forecast logic: tirzepatide at USD 60 billion in 2026 (USD 43.768 billion for the first three quarters plus about USD 16.2 billion in Q4), +18% to USD 71 billion in 2027 and +13% to USD 80 billion in 2028; Foundayo at USD 1.3 billion in 2026 (consensus after sell-side cuts), USD 5.5 billion in 2027 and USD 8.5 billion in 2028; other products growing at a low +4%. The key variable is the slope of tirzepatide growth, which determines whether 2027 is +18% or +12%.

Sensitivity also hinges on gross margin: if 2027 gross margin falls from 82% to 78% (down 4 percentage points), that is USD 4.3 billion less gross profit on revenue of USD 108 billion and about USD 3.8 less EPS after tax—the target price drops 12% outright. Gross margin is the most sensitive single variable in this model, more so than revenue growth.

X. Valuation

Valuation: 25–38x P/E on 2027 Non-GAAP EPS. Base case USD 42 × 32x = USD 1,344, 5% below the current price; bear case USD 33 × 25x = USD 825 (-42%); bull case USD 47 × 38x = USD 1,786 (+26%). Equal-weighted expected return across the three scenarios is -7%, a payoff ratio of 0.6 to 1.

Why not a higher multiple? The patent expires in May 2036 and the lease has only 9.5 years left. An asset that must compete again in 9.5 years should not command a "perpetual growth" multiple. The current price implies 33.6x 2027 earnings, already more than double the 10–15x of comparable blockbuster drugmakers; the premium rests on growth and pipeline, and both are decelerating.

DCF cross-check: assume free cash flow grows from USD 35 billion in 2027 to USD 70 billion in 2035 (9% CAGR), then continues in perpetuity at one-third of the margin after the 2036 patent expiry, discounted at 8%, giving an intrinsic value of about USD 1,250–1,350 per share. This aligns with the relative valuation (USD 1,344), confirming the base-case target is not arbitrary. At a 7% discount rate, intrinsic value rises to USD 1,450—the source of the rate sensitivity.

Comparables: Novo Nordisk 10.6x, Pfizer 9–10x, Merck around 10x, Amgen around 15x, Eli Lilly 48.2x. Lilly's premium rests on two things: 39% growth and the post-2027 pipeline. If either weakens, the premium converges toward 25–30x.

XI. Investment recommendation

Rating: Watch, wait for a pullback (equivalent to "Neutral"). 12-month target price USD 1,344, -5% downside from the current price of USD 1,411.95. This is not a bearish call on the company but a bearish call on the price. Of the six gates, the first four (circle of competence, good business, moat, management) all pass with high scores; the fifth (margin of safety) and sixth (discipline) do not, for a composite of 41/60.

Two alternative buy conditions: ① the stock falls below USD 1,200 (28.6x 2027, payoff ratio flips to about 1.4 to 1); ② Foundayo quarterly sales exceed USD 500 million with two consecutive quarters of sequential growth (oral franchise succession holds, 35x acceptable). Position sizing: 3% at USD 1,200, 6% at USD 1,050, 8% cap at USD 825.

Portfolio role: Eli Lilly is "defensive growth"—rigid demand, stable cash flow, high valuation. It has very low correlation with the AI compute chain (NVIDIA, TSMC), high correlation with Novo Nordisk and pharma ETFs, and negative correlation with long-end rates (long duration). If the portfolio already holds Novo or a pharma ETF, buying Eli Lilly merely doubles the bet on the same factor and provides no diversification.

Finally, "what would change my conclusion": if the 2027 revenue guidance comes in above USD 115 billion (growth of 25%+), neutral EPS would be revised up to USD 47–50, and USD 1,411.95 would correspond to 28–30x, at which point the current price is reasonable and the rating would be upgraded from "Watch" to "Buy." So this is not a fixed bearish judgment but a judgment pending data.

Conclusion

Core conclusion: Eli Lilly is the undisputed global leader in metabolic, with 2026 revenue set to reach USD 91–92 billion and Non-GAAP EPS of USD 35–36. But a 48x TTM valuation already discounts 2027–2028 smoothly, and our neutral target price of USD 1,344 sits 5% below the current price — a payoff ratio of 0.6 to 1. Rating: Watch, wait for a pullback; buy line USD 1,200; single-position cap 8%. Three tracking indicators: Foundayo quarterly sales, the actual US selling-price decline, and 2027 revenue guidance.
