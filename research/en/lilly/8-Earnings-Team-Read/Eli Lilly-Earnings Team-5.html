Four consensus views

Four people read the same earnings report and agreed more than they disagreed. There were four points of consensus: 1) Operations were flawless — revenue of RMB 24.397 billion, +38.6%, adjusted EPS of USD 7.48 above the consensus of 19.5%, and guidance raised for the third time; 2) The report was clean — the USD 4.2 billion acquisition cost, the oral drug miss and overseas price cuts, all three pieces of bad news were laid out in the open; 3) Growth came from volume, not price — volume +53%, price -15%, and this is the basis for every conclusion; 4) The price is too expensive — 48x TTM, 39.8x 2026 guidance, and none of the four was willing to buy at this level.

One Disagreement and One Signal

There is only one disagreement, but it is critical: is the 84.4% gross margin a matter of "capability" or "timing." Duan Yongping and Buffett lean toward capability—production dilution, scale effects, cost control—these are sustainable; Munger and Li Lu lean toward timing—dilution can only happen once, price declines are one-way, and 84.4% is the peak of this cycle.

My verdict leans toward the latter. The reason: Eli Lilly's own guidance already sets the 2026 operating margin at 52%–52.5%; if the gross margin could still hold above 84%, the margin should be higher. Management's conservatism is often closer to the truth than analysts' optimism. So in modeling I assign a 2027 gross margin of 82% and 2028 of 79%.

There is also a judgment everyone agrees on but no one wrote into the table: the most important information in this earnings report is not this quarter's numbers, but management's "only saying strong, giving no numbers" for 2027. A company accustomed to giving precise guidance that suddenly offers only qualitative description usually means it is not confident itself.

One methodological note: when reading quarterly reports, distinguish "operating signals" from "accounting signals." This quarter's operating signals are sales volume, prescription volume, penetration rate—all moving in a good direction; the accounting signals are gross margin, expense ratio, one-off items—the good-looking gross margin comes from dilution, not price increases. Operating signals determine where the company will be in three years; accounting signals determine how the stock price moves next quarter. Investing uses the former; trading uses the latter.

Conclusion

The synthesized conclusion is firmer than any single piece: this is not a question of whether the earnings are good, but of what good earnings are worth. The earnings score 9; the price scores 2; overall, it fails. If 2027 guidance arrives with growth of 18% or more, the neutral target price would be revised up to around USD 1,500 — only then would USD 1,411.95 count as "reasonable" rather than "cheap."
