Eli Lilly's flawless earnings, paired with an imperfect price — Eli Lilly

Core view

A report with revenue above the consensus of 7.9% and a third guidance raise, paired with a stock at 48x earnings and 0.4% from its all-time high. A good company meeting a bad price leaves only one response: wait.

One molecule supports two-thirds

Start with the good part.

Eli Lilly delivered a quarterly report with almost nothing to fault. Q3 2026 revenue was USD 24.397 billion, up 38.6% year on year and 7.9% above the consensus. Adjusted EPS was USD 7.48, nearly 20% above the expected USD 6.26. Full-year revenue guidance was raised from USD 85–87 billion to USD 91–92 billion, the third upgrade this year.

Behind the numbers is a simple fact: tirzepatide is still selling like crazy. Mounjaro did USD 10.723 billion in the quarter and Zepbound USD 5.352 billion, for a combined USD 16.075 billion, or 65.9% of total company revenue. In the first three quarters the molecule sold USD 43.768 billion.A drug three years on the market, annualizing above USD 60 billion and still growing more than 50% year on year — there is no second example in pharmaceutical history.

Two unseemly details

But the earnings report hides two unflattering details. The first is the oral drug Foundayo: approved in April, it sold only USD 184 million in the third quarter, of which USD 124 million came from the US, below the consensus of about USD 50 million. Over the same period, Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy sold USD 811 million, with 183,000 weekly prescriptions, nearly three times Foundayo's 63,000. On this new oral track, Lilly is for now the one losing.

The second detail is price. Company-wide volume rose 53% while realized price fell 15%; broken down, US volume rose 41% with price down only 7%, while overseas volume rose 76% with price down 29%. Overseas is already in a price war; the US just hasn't had its turn yet. In November 2025, Lilly reached a most-favored-nation drug pricing agreement with the White House, under which Medicare will cover the drug at a net price of USD 245 per month from 2026, with patient out-of-pocket costs capped at USD 50 — the price cut is an institutional arrangement, not a promotional campaign.

Price: the odds are inverted

Together, these two things point to an uncomfortable question: how much longer can Eli Lilly's good times last? Gross margin was 84.4%, up 0.8 percentage points from a year earlier. That happened even as prices fell 15%, driven by cost dilution from higher capacity utilization. That dilution is one-off; the price cuts are ongoing. Once capacity nears full utilization and prices keep falling, gross margin will start to turn down.

So where does the stock stand? USD 1,411.95, a market cap of USD 1.33 trillion, a TTM P/E of 48x, about 40x on the 2026 guidance of USD 35–36, and 33.6x on a neutral 2027 assumption of USD 42. It sits just 0.4% below its 52-week high.

Discount in everything going right in 2027 — EPS of USD 42 at 32x — and the fair value is USD 1,344, 5% below the current price. The bear case is USD 825 (-42%), the bull case USD 1,786 (+26%), a risk-reward of 0.6 to 1 — upside down.

Conclusion: Hold

The most striking moment came on November 9. The US FDA required Mounjaro, Zepbound, Ozempic and Wegovy to add a risk warning for a rare eye condition (NAION) to their labels. That is bad news, yet Eli Lilly rose 4.28% that day and Novo Nordisk gained 2.65%.The market traded the risk warning as "all the bad news is out" — a textbook reading of overheated sentiment.

My conclusion is simple: watch, wait for a pullback. This company is worth owning, but not at this price. Two trigger conditions, either one suffices — the stock falls below USD 1,200 (28.6x 2027 earnings), or Foundayo sells more than USD 500 million in a single quarter with two consecutive quarters of sequential growth, proving the oral drug can carry the baton.

Until then, any rationale of "it's the leader, so I should hold a little" substitutes a judgment about identity for a judgment about price. Overpaying for a good company is still a bad investment.

Conclusion

Watch, wait for a pullback. Only consider buying below USD 1,200, or once Foundayo posts USD 500 million in a single quarter — either condition will do. This company does not need your urgency; it needs your patience.
