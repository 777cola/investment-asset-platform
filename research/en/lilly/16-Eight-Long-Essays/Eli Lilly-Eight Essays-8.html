Part Eight: Decision Framework — Eli Lilly

Three-step assessment, four-tier position sizing, three indicators
Research date 2026/11/09  ·  Price $1,411.95 (+4.28%)  ·  Market cap About USD 1.33 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 48x

Core view

Sequence matters: first judge the business (worth owning), then value it (fair value USD 1,344), and only then look at the price (USD 1,411.95 is too expensive). Action: 0 position; build 3% at 1,200; add to 6% at 1,050; cap at 8% at 825.

Three steps: business, value, price

Condense the previous seven pieces into a three-step decision framework. Step one: is this business worth owning? Yes — gross margin 84.4%, inelastic demand, moat 38/50, management 8 points. Step two: what is it worth now? Base case USD 1,344 (2027 EPS USD 42 × 32x), bear case 825, bull case 1,786. Step three: what should be done at the current price of USD 1,411.95? Nothing. Wait.

The order cannot be reversed — that is the key to the framework. Judge the business first, then calculate value, then look at price. Reverse it — look at the share price first and then hunt for reasons — and you are speculating, which is the order that loses money for 90% of retail investors.

One counterexample: if I had held for five years at a cost of USD 400, would this report's recommendation differ? No. Holding and buying decisions rest on the same yardstick — "at today's price, does it still deserve this position". Cost basis is a mental account, not a valuation basis.

Four position tiers and one alternative

Specific action table: 0 position at the current price; 0%–1% at USD 1,344 (neutral target price); build 3% at USD 1,200 (28.6x 2027); add to 6% at USD 1,050 (25x); cap at 8% at USD 825 (19.6x, contingent on confirming the thesis is intact). Space the tranches more than 15% apart: with annualised volatility at 31.6%, tranches set too close together amount to trading in noise.

Alternative if not adding: if Foundayo sells more than USD 500 million in a single quarter and grows sequentially for two consecutive quarters, build 3% at USD 1,300–1,400. That pays for the "oral successor" hypothesis — if it holds, the growth curve after 2028 looks completely different, and 35x is reasonable.

A word on the cost of "waiting": waiting costs nothing, but waiting requires a reason. Mine is the odds — expected return is -7% buying at the current price, +15% waiting for USD 1,200, and +35% waiting for USD 1,050. These three numbers need not be precise, only directionally right. If Eli Lilly rises to USD 1,700 a year from now, I will concede the call was wrong, but I will not change the method.

Three monthly indicators and one timing indicator

Three metrics to watch, reviewed monthly: 1) Foundayo quarterly sales (RMB 300 million is a pass, RMB 500 million means the baton has been passed, RMB 1 billion is a success); 2) the decline in actual US selling prices (-7% is where we are now, -12% is the alarm); 3) 2027 revenue guidance (RMB 105 billion is the dividing line, corresponding to 15% growth). If two of the three improve, our neutral target price rises to around USD 1,500; if two deteriorate, it falls to USD 1,100.

There is also a time metric: remaining patent life. Every year that passes shortens the lease by a year, and the fair multiple should de-rate in step. By 2029, if growth has slowed to 15% and only 6.5 years of patent life remain, 25x is the ceiling. This is not a forecast, it is arithmetic — and the market will do this math sooner or later.

One more execution discipline: put these three metrics on the calendar, check them once a quarter, and in the months in between, do not look at the share price. A 48x company generates news every day, and looking every day means being dragged around by sentiment every day. The value of a framework is not that it "gets the numbers right" — it is that it "spares you from re-deciding every day".

What if I'm wrong?

Finally, "what if I'm wrong?" Wrong by being too conservative: Eli Lilly opens the next leg with retatrutide (20.8% weight loss at 80 weeks, filing in Q1 2027), oral flips the game on convenience, and 2028 revenue reaches USD 130 billion — in which case I miss the move from 1,411.95 to above 2,000. Wrong by being too optimistic: price war arrives early, oral fails outright, 2027 growth drops to 10% — and even 825 is not cheap.

Either way, the cost of my error is asymmetric. The conservative error costs "earning less"; the optimistic error costs "losing 40%". At 48x, choosing conservatism is not cowardice — it is having done the math.

One last note on "missing out": if Eli Lilly runs straight to 2,000 without looking back, this report is wrong, and publicly wrong. I accept that outcome — because buying at 48x and running into one -42% bear case is the more expensive mistake. Investing is not about who catches more bull stocks; it is about who survives longer.

Conclusion

Decision: watch, wait for a pullback. This is less a "smart" call than a "don't lose money" one. Buying Eli Lilly at 48x means the return comes from sentiment; buying at 25x means it comes from the company's earnings. This report only underwrites the latter.
