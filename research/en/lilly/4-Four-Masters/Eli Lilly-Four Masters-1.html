Four Masters — Eli Lilly

Eli Lilly (LLY) | 04 · Four Masters

Core thesis

The four masters agree strongly on Eli Lilly's business: it is the best pharmaceutical franchise of the past decade, with tirzepatide at USD 16.075 billion in a single quarter, an 84.4% gross margin, inelastic demand and automatic repeat purchases. The disagreement is purely on price—and all four point the same way: no discussion below 25x P/E. On 2027 neutral EPS of USD 42 and 32x, that gives USD 1,344, 5% below the current price; bear case USD 825, bull case USD 1,786, a risk-reward of 0.6 to 1. Not one of the four would buy here.

Duan Yongping: a good company still needs a good price, and this price does not qualify

Buying a stock is buying a company. Is Eli Lilly a good company? Yes, and the easiest kind to understand: it sells "eat less, bring blood sugar down," and patients pay because their bodies need it, not because the advertising is good. This kind of business does not need to re-persuade customers every year, nor does it need to worry that its technology roadmap will be upended tomorrow—the only thing you need to worry about is one thing: when the patent expires. The answer is May 2036, nine and a half years away.

So is buying now worth it? A market cap of USD 1.33 trillion, 2026 Non-GAAP net profit roughly USD 33.4 billion (USD 35.50 × 941 million shares), a static return of 2.5%. To turn that into 8%, net profit would have to rise to USD 100 billion, three times today's level. Tripling profit over nine and a half years is 12% annualized, not outrageous.But that is the "everything goes smoothly" calculation, and a 48x P/E has already paid for "everything goes smoothly."

Duan Yongping would say: I understand this company, I want to own it, but I won't own it at this price. Wait for it to fall below USD 1,200, corresponding to a static return of 2.9% and 28.6x 2027 earnings; only then do the odds match the risk."Expensive" is not a reason not to buy; "so expensive there is no room for error" is.

One more layer: when Duan Yongping looks at a company, he likes to ask, "Will it still be here in ten years, and what will it be doing?" Eli Lilly will most likely still be selling metabolic drugs in ten years, but not necessarily tirzepatide—retatrutide, a triple agonist, delivered 20.8%–25% weight loss at 80 weeks in Phase III, and the company says it will file in Q1 2027; it will be the mainstay of the 2030s.That means what you buy today at 48x P/E is really "the Eli Lilly R&D pipeline," not "the tirzepatide molecule." The pipeline's value can be calculated, but not with this much precision.

Buffett: moats have lease terms; when the lease expires, you have to compete again

He would like this business. Pricing power (84.4% gross margin), repeat purchase (annual chronic-disease medication), low capital intensity relative to profit, and a business that runs without genius management—all four hit his preferences. He has bought Johnson & Johnson, Sanofi and Bristol-Myers, and knows pharma's sweet spot.

But he knows pharma's soft underbelly better: the moat is granted by law, not voted in by consumers. Coca-Cola's formula always belongs to Coca-Cola and See's Candies' brand can raise prices, but Eli Lilly's tirzepatide will compete with a raft of generics after May 2036. On a DCF, cash flows after 2036 must be revalued at "generic prices"—and once you make that cut, you cannot assume a 3% perpetual growth rate.

So he never pays 40x for a pharma stock—not because he disbelieves in growth, but because he disbelieves that growth can last long enough to support that multiple. In his framework, a moat with a lease term must be discounted, and the market is currently paying a premium. This matters more than any quarterly data: Q3 revenue +38.6% and EPS above the consensus of USD 1.22 do not change the fact that this business must be revalued after 2036.

He also noticed a detail: Eli Lilly booked USD 4.2 billion of acquired in-process R&D as a one-time charge in Q3 2026 and duly cut its Non-GAAP EPS guidance. That accounting treatment shows management treats "buying pipelines" as routine spending, not an occasional event—to him, that is honest, but it also means the company must keep buying pipelines to sustain growth.

Munger: There is no money to be made where consensus is too strong

Think in reverse: under what circumstances would buying Eli Lilly lose money? Not because the company turns bad, but because it becomes "less good." A 48x P/E does not price "good," it prices "better." Revenue growth slipping from 39% to 25% leaves the income statement still handsome, yet the valuation has to be cut by a third.

He would list the risks: Foundayo oral ramp fails (Q3 only RMB 184 million, 63,000 weekly prescriptions vs 183,000 for oral Wegovy); price war in Europe and emerging markets (overseas pricing -29%); US Medicare coverage at a net price of USD 245 per month, with price cuts not yet finished; USD 27 billion of capacity becomes an impairment if demand slows; the 2036 patent cliff.Each of the five risks has a low probability on its own, but if any one lands, 48x will not hold.

There is another layer he rarely states but cares about most: buying at an all-time high (-0.4% from the 52-week high) and the opportunity cost of being forced to hold for five years once trapped. Today the FDA required a GLP-1 eye-disease warning, and the stock rose 4.28% anyway—the market reads even bad news as good, a signal that consensus is so strong it has no cracks. Where consensus is that strong, there is no excess return.

He has also said: "If you aren't willing to hold a stock for ten years, don't hold it for ten minutes." Almost no one buying at this level intends to hold for ten years—they are betting on a guidance raise next quarter. That is not investing, it is trading, and trading requires stop-loss discipline, which is a different matter from the framework of this report.

Li Lu: civilisation-scale demand does not equal permanent excess profit

Demographically, this is the biggest breakthrough in metabolic disease in thirty years. More than 2 billion people worldwide are obese or overweight, and GLP-1 has turned a "willpower problem" into a "pharmacology problem" — demand that is real, transnational and non-cyclical. In terms of civilisational progress, the social value of these drugs is no less than that of antibiotics.

But he would caution that being in the right sector does not mean any single company captures excess profit forever. The oral era begins in 2026: Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy launched in January and had already reached USD 811 million by Q3, with weekly prescriptions three times those of Foundayo; semaglutide's China patent expires in 2026, with generics and improved new drugs in pursuit; and generic applications are already under FDA review in the US. In five years this market will most likely still be enormous, but unit profit will certainly be thinner than today.

His question would be plain: on a USD 1.33 trillion market cap, how many years of free cash flow does it take to earn that back? On a rough 2026 estimate of USD 25 billion GAAP net profit and 20% average annual growth over the next decade, the cumulative total is only USD 600 billion — not even the principal comes back, and the remaining USD 700 billion rests entirely on the assumption that "the tax can keep being collected after 2036." That assumption may hold, but it should not be prepaid in full at today's price.

Odds assessment: 0.6 to 1, reversed

Bear case -42%, base case -5%, bull case +26%. At a 1/3 probability on each scenario, expected return is -7%. For that to turn positive the base case needs a probability above 55%, and the base case itself requires three things to hold at once: Foundayo ramps, pricing stays under control and share does not slip.

Put another way, buying at the current price means paying 48x P/E for an asset growing 18% in 2027 and decelerating every year after. At this price, the tolerance for slower growth is zero. A single quarter of revenue below the 5% consensus would compress the multiple from 48x to 38x and knock 20% off the stock price — this is not alarmism; every time Eli Lilly missed consensus in a single quarter over the past two years, this was the script.

When does the risk-reward flip? Below USD 1,200 (28.6x 2027, risk-reward about 1.4 to 1), or when Foundayo sells past USD 500 million in a single quarter, proving the oral drug can carry the franchise (at which point 35x is acceptable). Buy only after one of these two conditions is met.

Conclusion

Four investors, one conclusion: don't buy — and not because they're bearish. Duan Yongping balks at the price, Buffett at the lease term, Munger at the consensus, Li Lu at the payback period — four objections that all point the same way: the company's value is fully priced, if not overpriced. Rating stays "Watch, wait for a pullback," with triggers at below USD 1,200 or Foundayo single-quarter revenue of USD 500 million.
