## How to explain the two deviations exceeding 1%

The first issue is the revenue base. The draft put 2025Q3 revenue at USD 17.552 billion, while both company disclosures and Stockstar show USD 17.601 billion. The USD 49 million gap looks trivial, but it moves year-on-year growth from +38.6% to +39.0% — a difference that crosses a whole number. I switched to USD 17.601 billion, wrote year-on-year growth as +38.6%, and made it consistent across all tables. Such errors usually come from mistaking "some slice of the first three quarters of 2025" for a single quarter, or from rounding when back-solving from year-on-year growth. Any base figure back-solved from year-on-year growth should not be treated as a company-disclosed value.

The second issue is not a deviation but an expectations gap. Foundayo sold USD 184 million against a consensus of USD 229 million, a USD 45 million difference (-20%). Many reports write "below the consensus by USD 25 million" or "USD 50 million" — definitions differ: some use LSEG consensus, some Visible Alpha, some count only the US market. I do not use this difference for inference; I only use it to compare against the absolute scale of oral Wegovy (USD 811 million vs USD 184 million), and that comparison is unaffected by consensus definitions.

## Three different scopes, and none of them is wrong

Volume and price basis. Company-wide, volume rose 53% and realized price fell 15%; in the US alone, volume rose 41% and price fell 7%; overseas, volume rose 76% and price fell 29%. All three sets are correct — they cover different scopes. Pick only one for a report and you reach the opposite conclusion: US only, and you call the price cuts mild; overseas only, and you call the price collapse. My approach is to give both, and state plainly that overseas -29% is what the US will look like.

The PE year. The draft says "about 40x 2027 estimates," but on 2027 neutral EPS of USD 42, the current price is only 33.6x; 40x corresponds to the 2026 guidance of USD 35.50 (39.8x). That sentence mixes two years. This report standardizes on three side-by-side figures: TTM 48.2x, 2026 guidance 39.8x, 2027 neutral 33.6x. The conclusion is unchanged — none of the three is cheap — but readers deserve to know which year's multiple they are looking at.

Non-GAAP EPS includes a one-time item. The USD 35.00–36.00 range already deducts USD 4.57 of acquired IPR&D. Add it back and the range is USD 39.57–40.57. Which to use for valuation? I use the deducted figure, because M&A is routine for this company, not an isolated event — three consecutive years of buying pipeline means it should not be treated as one-time.

## The finally adopted basis

Revenue and profit use the company's GAAP figures (USD 24,397 / 6,451 million); EPS uses Non-GAAP (Q3 7.48, full year 35.00–36.00); gross margin uses non-GAAP 84.4%, with a note that the GAAP figure would be 3–4 percentage points lower (acquisition IPR&D and amortization charged to cost); market cap uses 1,411.95 × 941 million shares = USD 1.33 trillion, with no adjustment for net debt; valuation multiples present the TTM figure of USD 29.32 alongside 2026/2027 forecast EPS, without giving a single PE. All derived figures are labeled "rough calculation" or "back-solved" in the text; nowhere is a derived figure presented as disclosed.

One cross-check that did not make it into the table: Q3 diluted EPS of USD 7.22, up 16% year on year, is consistent with net profit of USD 6.451 billion, up 16% year on year, indicating no change in share count and no large preferred dividend, so the transmission chain through the income statement holds. This kind of "small items tie out" proves the financials have not been stitched together better than any single number.
