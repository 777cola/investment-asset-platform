Core view

The conclusion is clean: no position now; wait for price. At USD 1,411.95 against a neutral target price of USD 1,344, expected return is -5% and the payoff ratio 0.6 to 1, so any position carries negative expectancy. Build the position in four tranches: 3% below USD 1,200 (a probe), 6% below USD 1,050 (25x 2027), 8% below USD 825 (bear case; requires confirming the thesis is intact). Cap any single position at 8%: this is a single-molecule company, and concentration risk cannot be waved away with the word "leader."

Why it must be 0 now, not "start with a small allocation"

"Just buy a small position first" is the most common way discipline breaks down. The logic is "what if it goes up," not "is this price worth it." Do the math: at the current price the neutral case is -5%, the bear case -42% and the bull case +26%; equal-weighting the three gives an expected return of -7%. A 3% position contributes an expected -0.2% to the portfolio — not much, but it consumes a slot and creates the psychological anchor of "I already own Li Auto," which makes you hesitate at the price where you should actually be buying.

Another version of the argument is "I'm bullish long term, short-term volatility doesn't matter." That holds at 20x P/E; it does not hold at 48x — 48x means the next three years of returns have been pulled forward, and a long-term holding period does not make this price any cheaper.

Specific price levels for adding to, trimming and stopping out positions

Adding to the position follows four tiers, with the thesis re-examined at each: USD 1,200 (3%), USD 1,050 (6%), USD 825 (8% cap). Tiering is not about averaging down the cost — it is about letting the price do the judging for you. If the stock falls to 825, the market is already pricing a bear case; what needs confirming then is whether the bear case can get worse, not that it has fallen this far and therefore warrants an add.

Trimming does not look at price; it looks at the thesis. Three triggers: Foundayo below USD 300 million for two consecutive quarters; US realized selling price down more than 12% in a single quarter; 2027 revenue guidance below USD 105 billion (growth <15%). Trigger any one, halve the position; trigger two, cut to a tracking position. Long-term investing does not use price stops, because price stops will shake you out at the best buying points.

One practical point: do not set the intervals between tiered entries at 5%–10%; set them at 15% or more. Eli Lilly's annualized volatility is 31.6%, and within three months it has seen both a -8.2% and a +26.6% move; tiers set too close together amount to trading in noise. 1,411.95 to 1,200 is -15%, 1,200 to 1,050 is -12.5%, and 1,050 to 825 is -21% — only this spacing matches its volatility.

Correlation and portfolio role

Eli Lilly's role in the portfolio is "defensive growth": rigid demand (chronic disease) and stable cash flow, but an expensive valuation. It has almost zero correlation with the AI compute chain (NVIDIA, TSMC)—one tracks patient penetration, the other cloud capex; the two drivers do not overlap. If the portfolio already holds a pile of AI hardware, Eli Lilly is a good diversifier; if it already holds Novo or a pharma ETF, Eli Lilly is just doubling down on the same factor.

Rate sensitivity deserves separate mention: an asset at 48x P/E has very long duration, and for every 50bp rise in long-end rates, its valuation center shifts down 5%–8%. So the timing to buy Eli Lilly often coincides with "rates peaking"—that is not a coincidence; it is the discount rate at work.

One last thing: if the portfolio already holds a pharma ETF, the right way to add Eli Lilly is to "replace the Eli Lilly weight inside the ETF with the single stock," not to add another layer on top of the ETF. Otherwise you are just levering up the same factor.

Conclusion

Position: 0. Add levels 1,200/1,050/825, cap 8%. Trimming is thesis-driven, not price-driven. If already held, there is no need to sell at this level—fundamentals are intact, and selling only incurs taxes and fees—but do not add either. Wait for February 2027 guidance; that is the repricing point.
