## Notes

Metric definitions follow the seven quality-screen elimination criteria (10-year ROE, 5-year cumulative FCF, interest coverage, gross margin, OCF/net profit, net margin, share count dilution) and three exemption rules; Eli Lilly required no exemptions. Financials are sourced from Eli Lilly's 2026Q3 report (2026/10/29); ROE and net assets are on a 2024 annual report basis; capex plans are taken from the company announcement dated 2025/02/26 (USD 27 billion, four sites, 3,000 jobs). ② and ⑤ are rough estimates, as flagged in the table.

## Four points that need no discussion

A gross margin of 84.4% and a net margin of 26.4% are best-in-class for pharma. Novo Nordisk's gross margin is also around 84%, but price wars and idle capacity have pushed its net margin below 30%. Lilly held 26.4% while delivering volume +53% and price -15%, showing that costs were diluted as volumes scaled.

Interest coverage and share count are equally flawless. 2026 operating margin guidance of 52%–52.5% implies operating profit of about USD 48 billion, against interest expense of less than USD 1 billion—this company's borrowing capacity far exceeds its borrowing needs. Share count shrank 1% over five years, meaning buybacks offset equity incentives and shareholders were not diluted.

## Three items that require disclosure of methodology

Read the ROE line with care. Eli Lilly booked a one-off charge in 2017 related to tax reform and reported a loss that year, which dragged down the ten-year average. Excluding that year, ROE has exceeded 20% every year since 2018, reaching 74.6% in 2024 (net income RMB 10.59 billion / net assets RMB 14.19 billion). Half of the high ROE reflects buybacks and dividends thinning the equity base; only the other half is the true return on capital. This line passes comfortably, but it cannot be used as evidence of an "asset-light" model.

I have marked the FCF and OCF/net income lines as "rough estimates." In February 2025 Eli Lilly announced USD 27 billion to build four manufacturing sites in the US (three for API, one for injectables, completed by 2030), pushing capex to historic highs and absorbing a large chunk of free cash flow. Both lines remain positive and still clear the threshold, but "clearing the threshold" and "having ample headroom" are two different things; as capacity comes online in concentrated fashion in 2027–2029, depreciation will push both lines lower.

One more note on the 2017 treatment: that year, US tax reform required multinationals to pay a one-off tax on retained overseas profits, and Eli Lilly booked a charge of about USD 2 billion, reporting a loss. Calculating the ten-year average directly would pull ROE down to just over 20%; excluding it, the average is around 35%. Either method is far above the 8% threshold, so the conclusion is unaffected, but the disclosure must state this clearly; otherwise readers will think this company earns only a 20% return on capital.

## What seven passes in a row does and does not tell us

What this shows: Eli Lilly earns real money, not paper wealth. Revenue of RMB 24.397 billion and net profit of RMB 6.451 billion, with cash flow keeping pace; growth is not propped up by leverage; R&D is not funded by issuing stock. These three things rule out 90% of companies that "look great."

What this does not show: whether buying now makes money. Not one of these seven criteria touches valuation. A company can pass all seven while its share price has already discounted five years of smooth sailing—and that is exactly where Eli Lilly stands: 48x TTM, about 40x 2026 guidance EPS, 33.6x 2027 neutral EPS. The conclusion of negative screening is "should not be excluded," not "should be bought"—the two statements are separated by an entire valuation step.

One aside: among the seven metrics, the easiest for a company like Eli Lilly to "score high" on are gross margin and net margin. They are high because the numerator is exclusive; and exclusivity is a legal status, not a capability status. On the day the US patent expires in May 2036, these two metrics will fall from 84% to below 60% within a year or two—a time dimension the screening system cannot measure.
