## Gate one: circle of competence, 8 points

The business is easy to sum up in one sentence: **during the patent protection period, sell an exclusive molecule at a high price and spread R&D costs across patients worldwide.** Eli Lilly's revenue structure is simple too — revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was USD 67.17 billion, of which tirzepatide accounted for USD 43.768 billion, or 65%. The remaining 35% comes from familiar names such as Verzenio (breast cancer), Taltz (psoriasis) and Kisunla (Alzheimer's).

What is understandable is the mechanism and the demand: the GIP/GLP-1 dual target produces greater weight loss than a single target, and once patients respond they repurchase annually — this is physiological demand, not marketing-driven demand. What is not understandable is the pipeline valuation — retatrutide (triple target) has strong Phase III data, with 20.8%–25% weight loss at 80 weeks, and the company plans to file in the first quarter of 2027, but how much it is worth and how much share it will take from the company's own products after launch cannot be calculated. That is where the 2 points are deducted.

## Second gate: good business, 10 points

What 84.4% non-GAAP gross margin means: for every USD 100 sold, USD 84 is gross profit, and after sales expenses and R&D, USD 26.4 of net profit remains (Q3 net profit RMB 6.451 billion / revenue RMB 24.397 billion). **Fewer than twenty companies worldwide can achieve this structure.**

More critical is this quarter's volume-price mix: company-wide volume +53%, realized selling price -15%, yet revenue still +39%. This shows the demand curve is far from exhausted—a 15% price cut bought 53% volume, price elasticity above 1. US market volume +41% with price down only 7%; overseas volume is large and price low (volume +76%, price -29%). **The same drug sells at completely different price curves in two markets—evidence of pricing power, and a hidden risk to future margins.**

## Gate 3 and Gate 4: Moat 9 points, Management 8 points

Four legs of the moat: mechanism (dual target, beat semaglutide head-to-head), capacity (global peptide fill-finish lines are scarce; in February 2025 Lilly announced USD 27 billion to build four US sites—three for API, one for injectables—completed by 2030), prescribing habits (Zepbound has already overtaken Wegovy in new US obesity prescriptions), and patents (US tirzepatide patents expire in May 2036). **All four are in place; no near-term fix for competitors.** 1 point deducted because the fourth leg is written on a patent certificate and has a lease term.

Management did something counterintuitive but correct this quarter: revenue guidance raised from USD 85–87 billion to USD 91–92 billion (midpoint +USD 5.5 billion), operating margin guidance raised from 49%–50.5% to 52%–52.5%; but Q3 recognized USD 4.2 billion of acquired IPR&amp;D expense (USD 4.57 per share), cutting Non-GAAP EPS guidance from 35.50–36.50 to 35.00–36.00. **Raising revenue and cutting EPS amounts to admitting "the money went into the pipeline"—no propping up the share price.** Points deducted on capital allocation: more and more M&amp;A (Verve and Adverum in 2025, this USD 4.2 billion deal in Q3 2026), with returns only visible in three to five years.

## Gate 5 and Gate 6: Margin of safety 2 points, discipline 4 points

USD 1,411.95, 941 million shares, market cap USD 1.33 trillion. TTM EPS USD 29.32, PE 48.2x. At the midpoint of 2026 guidance of USD 35.50, 39.8x; at the neutral 2027 assumption of USD 42, 33.6x. None of the three measures is below 25x.

Discount all of 2027's smooth sailing: neutral-case EPS USD 42 x 32x = USD 1,344, **5% below the current price**. Bear case USD 825 (-42%), bull case USD 1,786 (+26%). Odds of 0.6 to 1 — betting on "everything goes right" still yields a negative return.

The sixth gate fails because the reasons to buy at this level all look the same: good sector, leader, above the consensus. All true, but these are **company attributes**, not **price conditions**. Discipline demands verifiable premise-based trade-offs — for example, "act only below USD 1,200 (about 34x 2026, 28.6x 2027)". Today's 4.28% gain came on the very day the FDA required a GLP-1 eye-disease warning; the market traded a risk notice as good news, and that sentiment reading is itself evidence not to buy.

## Veto items and items to be verified

**Only one veto: price.** Not slightly expensive on valuation, but expensive in the sense of "a 5% loss even in the smooth scenario." A neutral target price of USD 1,344 means that at the moment of purchase the market must hand out another re-rating gift, and that is beyond our capability.

Four items to verify: ① Foundayo sold only USD 184 million in Q3 (USD 124 million in the US), about USD 50 million below the consensus; oral Wegovy did USD 811 million in the same period, with 183,000 weekly prescriptions vs Foundayo's 63,000 — a threefold gap. ② Actual US selling prices -7%, overseas -29%; Medicare covers it at a net price of USD 245 per month from 2026, and the price cuts are not over. ③ USD 27 billion of capacity comes online progressively before 2030; if growth falls below 20%, depreciation will bite into gross margin. ④ On 2027 guidance management said only "strong" without a number — the biggest missing piece of the valuation puzzle.
