Core view

The buy case rests on six verifiable assumptions, each checked against the current state: tirzepatide tracking toward USD 60 billion for the full year, gross margin above 82%, the scale of US price cuts, and retatrutide filing in Q1 2027 are all on track; 2027 growth remains unverified (management gave no figure), and the oral drug line has clearly diverged — Foundayo at USD 184 million in a single quarter, USD 740 million annualized, against the market's prior expectation of USD 3 billion for the full year. Five of the six hold, but the one that broke is precisely the 2028 growth engine.

Four swing factors: tirzepatide, gross margin, US pricing, pipeline

The assumption of USD 60 billion in full-year tirzepatide sales largely holds. The first three quarters totalled USD 43.768 billion, leaving Q4 with only USD 16.2 billion to find — Q3 alone was already USD 16.075 billion, and Q4 typically carries seasonality from insurance switches and early-year stocking. Mounjaro is still growing 65% and Zepbound 49%. The risk to this assumption lies not in demand but in supply and price cuts.

Gross margin and pricing are locked in a mutual squeeze: the Q3 gross margin of 84.4% was achieved with selling prices down 15%, supported by capacity dilution. As long as dilution is not yet complete (the four sites will not be fully operational until 2030), holding 82% in 2027 is reasonable. The 7% US price decline is the most fragile: Medicare covers the drug at a net price of USD 245/month from 2026, and if coverage expands to commercial insurance in 2027, the 7% decline will quickly become 12%.

The mapping between assumptions and valuation must be spelled out: all six on track → 2027 EPS of USD 47, 38x multiple, USD 1,786; four on track with oral franchise off track → EPS of USD 42, 32x multiple, USD 1,344; three or more off track → EPS of USD 33, 25x multiple, USD 825. The current position is the second — the neutral scenario, with a target price 5% below the current price.

The outlier is the oral drug — and a fatal one at that

Foundayo's numbers warrant close scrutiny. Q3 USD 184 million, USD 281 million cumulative and USD 740 million annualised, while the sell-side has cut its full-year 2026 forecast from USD 3 billion to USD 1.3 billion. Oral Wegovy, by comparison, posted Q3 USD 811 million on 183,000 weekly prescriptions versus Foundayo's 63,000 — a threefold gap. This is not "a bit slower" — it is an order-of-magnitude gap.

Why does this matter more than anything else? Because tirzepatide's US patent expires in May 2036, and the oral drug is the bridge across the patent cliff. If the oral franchise fails to connect, Lilly in the 2030s must rely on retatrutide alone — and retatrutide is also an injectable. In other words, Lilly currently has no second answer in the "no needle" direction. Should this assumption be disproven, the growth story after 2028 has to be rewritten.

One piece of counter-evidence deserves mention: Foundayo's convenience advantage is real (any-time dosing, no fasting required), and channel rollout is not slow (45% from LillyDirect, 35% from telehealth, plus a November partnership with Hims & Hers). What it is losing on is not the product but the "muscle memory of doctors and insurers" — oral Wegovy saves on education costs by leveraging the established semaglutide name. That disadvantage should narrow over time, but it needs four or more quarters of observation, not one.

Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar

Four falsification conditions; any one triggers a re-rating:

  • Foundayo quarterly revenue below USD 300 million for two consecutive quarters → the 2028 growth narrative collapses and the stock de-rates below 25x.
  • US realized price falls more than 12% in a single quarter → gross margin peaks early, voiding the 82% assumption for 2027.
  • 2027 revenue guidance below USD 105 billion (growth <15%) → the 48x multiple necessarily compresses below 30x.
  • retatrutide filing delayed beyond the second half of 2027 → the pipeline premium disappears, equivalent to the second condition also failing.

Watch calendar: February 2027 annual results and 2027 guidance (most important — the missing piece of the valuation puzzle); April 2027 Q1 results (Foundayo's first full quarter-on-quarter); monthly IQVIA oral prescription data (150,000 weekly prescriptions is the watershed); June 2027 ADA and September 2027 EASD (more retatrutide data); approval milestone for the sleep apnea indication (a new indication for Foundayo; the company says it will file before year-end).

Conclusion

The thesis holds for now — not on the oral drug, but on tirzepatide. Five of the six assumptions remain intact; the most fragile one (oral) is already diverging, and diverging clearly. This is another reason to "watch" rather than "buy": it is not a question of price, but that one key assumption has yet to be validated. Once the February 2027 guidance is out, three of the six will be settled outright.
