Core view

Top-tier print: revenue beat consensus by 7.9% and adjusted EPS by 19.5%, while gross margin expanded 0.8pp against the trend. The one caveat is capex—USD 27 billion of capacity investment will compress FCF to 0.6–0.7x net profit, with depreciation to follow.

Income statement: three-tier structure

Start with the three-tier structure of the income statement. Tier one: revenue of USD 24.397 billion, up 38.6% YoY on a base of USD 17.601 billion, and USD 67.170 billion for the first three quarters. Tier two: non-GAAP gross margin of 84.4%, 0.8pp above last year's 83.6%—with selling prices down 15%, gross margin still rose, showing that volume-driven dilution outweighed the price cuts, evidence of high capacity utilisation. Tier three: net income of USD 6.451 billion, up 16% YoY, trailing revenue growth by 23 percentage points.

Why is profit growth only four-tenths of revenue growth? Two reasons. First, a one-time USD 4.2 billion charge for the IPR&D acquisition (USD 4.57 per share); second, selling expenses rose with channel expansion. Adjusted EPS of USD 7.48 came in 19.5% above the consensus of USD 6.26—showing that operations are fine and that accounting and M&A ate the profit.

Cash flow and capex: a sweet burden

Read cash flow and capex together. In February 2025 Eli Lilly committed at least USD 27 billion to build four US sites — three for API, one for injectables, completed by 2030 — plus a USD 1.7 billion single-site plant in Concord, completed in 2027. It is the largest single manufacturing investment in the history of the US pharmaceutical industry. Capex intensity has risen from just over 10% in 2023 to above 20%, compressing free cash flow to 0.6–0.7x net income (our rough calculation).

That is not a bad thing, but the cost has to be understood: depreciation will climb year by year over the next three to five years, and an 84.4% gross margin cannot hold indefinitely. Management raised 2026 operating margin guidance from 49%–50.5% to 52%–52.5%, leaning on scale effects rather than price increases — once growth falls below 15%, fixed costs will bite back.

Balance sheet and shareholder returns

The balance sheet is clean: interest-bearing debt below USD 30 billion, operating profit in the USD 48 billion range and interest coverage above 50x, with ROE of 74.6% in 2024. This is the classic "good company statement" — no leverage, no equity issuance, profit compounding on its own. Shareholder returns lean conservative: an annual dividend of about USD 4.5 per share, a 0.3% yield and modest buybacks, with cash mainly going into capacity and M&A.

My read: the only "flaw" in this report is that it is too good — so good there is no debate. Revenue of RMB 24.397 billion came in 7.9% above the RMB 22.61 billion consensus; EPS beat by 19.5%; guidance was raised for the third time — all three ahead. Buffett would like this statement, but he would add: the better the statement, the more optimism is baked into the price.

One easily overlooked detail: Q3 revenue beat consensus by 7.9%, yet net profit rose only 16%, and still the stock moved +6.63% on the day (October 29) and +3.19% the next day. That says the stock is not rising on profit but on guidance being raised again. When a company is priced mainly on guidance rather than current profit, its volatility rises systematically.

Conclusion

We award the report 9 points, deducting 1 for capex certainty. If volume growth holds above 25% in 2027–2028, the USD 27 billion capacity is a moat; below 15%, it becomes an impairment risk. This is a question of demand, not accounting—and demand has yet to show cracks.
