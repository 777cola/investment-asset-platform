Core view

Four "don't buy" votes, but for different reasons. The sharpest disagreement concerns the oral drug: is it temporarily behind or structurally beaten, and that call determines growth beyond 2028. I come down on "undecided," with Foundayo's quarterly USD 500 million as the validation line. Final rating: watch, wait for a pullback, trigger price below USD 1,200.

Four points of disagreement

All four agreed on the conclusion — a unanimous "don't buy." The reasons, however, diverge, and those differences dictate what to watch from here. I rank the disagreements by importance into four:

  • Disagreement one: is the oral drug "temporarily behind" or "structurally beaten"? The commercial view holds that Foundayo's convenience (dose at any time, no fasting required) will turn the 12–24 month retention rate around, and Lilly already reversed a once-behind injectable with Zepbound in 2023. The risk view counters that 63,000 weekly prescriptions vs 183,000 — a threefold gap — with Novo treating oral as a "battle it cannot lose" and pouring in resources, is not a one-quarter gap. The financial view adds a cut: analysts have already revised Foundayo's 2026 revenue forecast down from RMB 3 billion to RMB 1.3 billion.
  • Disagreement two: is the 84.4% gross margin the ceiling. Commercial and financial argue scale effects remain and volume dilution can offset price cuts; the industry view points out that overseas prices are already -29% and the US Medicare net price is USD 245/month — price declines run one way.
  • Disagreement three: is the USD 27 billion capacity a moat or a burden. Financial sees it as "trading today's FCF for tomorrow's share," which is correct; risk argues that if capacity is 30% oversupplied in 2028, impairments will hit gross margin directly.
  • Disagreement four: is Novo's price cut good or bad for Lilly. The industry view holds that the patent cliff is forcing Novo to cut prices and share will keep shifting to Lilly; the risk view holds that the price war is industry-wide and Lilly will have to follow whether it wants to or not.
Rule-by-rule rulings

Ruling one: Treat the oral drug as "undecided" — neither a reason to buy nor a reason to sell. The validation metrics are demanding: Foundayo at USD 500 million in a single quarter and 150,000 weekly prescriptions. Until then, any "convenience wins" argument is just a story. This single item directly determines the growth curve from 2028 onward and is the most important unresolved variable in this report.

Ruling two: Model gross margin as "slowly declining." 82% in 2027 and 79% in 2028, as US net prices converge toward overseas levels and depreciation rises. That is enough to support the neutral-case EPS of USD 42 without assuming gross margin holds at 84%.

Ruling three: Treat capacity as "neutral to slightly positive." Lilly is expanding with high demand visibility — tirzepatide sold RMB 43.77 billion in 9M and is still growing above 50% — not expanding blindly. Still, keep a pessimistic tier in the model of "70% capacity utilization in 2028."

Ruling four: Novo's price cuts help Lilly near term and hurt it long term. Share will keep shifting to Lilly (the 60/40 split is still widening), but the industry's price center is being pulled down, and unit profit five years out will certainly be thinner than today. Do not net these two effects against each other; state them separately.

Final verdict and trigger conditions

Put the four perspectives together and only one thing is certain: the operations are fine; the problem is entirely the price. Revenue of USD 24.397 billion, +38.6%, guidance raised for the third time to 91–92 billion, operating margin guidance of 52%–52.5%, adjusted EPS 19.5% above consensus — none of these figures need explaining or defending.

On the price side: 48x TTM, 39.8x 2026 guidance, 33.6x 2027 neutral. The neutral target price of USD 1,344 sits 5% below the current price; bear case USD 825, bull case USD 1,786, odds 0.6 to 1.The "good company + bad price" combination has only one response: wait.

Final verdict: Watch, wait for a pullback. Either of two conditions triggers a buy — the stock falls below USD 1,200 (28.6x 2027, odds flip to 1.4 to 1), or Foundayo sells more than USD 500 million in a single quarter with two consecutive quarters of sequential growth (at which point 35x is acceptable). Until then, any "it's the leader so we should hold a little" thinking is a lapse in discipline.

Conclusion

Watch and wait for a pullback. This is not a bearish call — it is bullish, but not at this price. Watch three things: Foundayo's quarterly sales, the pace of decline in realised US selling prices, and 2027 guidance. If two of the three improve, we would revise up the USD 1,344 neutral target, at which point USD 1,411.95 may be justified — but not yet.
