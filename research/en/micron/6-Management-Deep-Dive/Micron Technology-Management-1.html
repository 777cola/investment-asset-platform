Core thesis

Mehrotra is one of the best CEOs in this memory cycle: he did not stop HBM in 2023 when the whole industry cut capex, and in 2026 he used 16 take-or-pay long-term contracts to shift price risk to customers. Capital allocation is also disciplined—net cash, no share dilution in five years, and dividends raised only 30% rather than a lavish payout.

There are two demerits. First, governance: the CEO also serves as chairman, removing one layer of checks and balances; on August 21 he sold 4.0 million shares under a 10b5-1 plan for USD 38.76 million, cutting his stake to 0.08%—not a problem, but not a plus either. Second, succession: born in 1959, he will be 67 in 2026, and the company has announced no succession arrangement, while the core judgments of this cycle (the HBM bet, the SCA long-term contracts) depend heavily on him personally.

Two bets, one paid off, one still unproven

The first was in 2023. When industry-wide capex was cut, Micron itself laid off about 10% of staff and posted a net loss of USD 5.8 billion for FY23, HBM R&D and production-line conversion did not stop. The mainstream market view at the time was that "HBM is SK hynix's business" and Micron was merely a follower. The result: in 2025 HBM3E won Nvidia certification, in 2026 12-layer HBM4 entered mass production in sync with Vera Rubin, and in 2026Q2 it captured 18% of global HBM share. This bet was on "AI training demand for bandwidth growing exponentially," and it paid off.

The second was the June 2026 SCA long-term contracts. 16 agreements, five-year terms, take-or-pay, with price floors and ceilings; 14 of them at floor prices correspond to about USD 100 billion in revenue, and customers have paid about USD 22 billion in cash deposits. This is the first time anyone in the memory industry has tried to commoditize contracts. But it has not yet been tested by a downcycle—take-or-pay is a talisman when supply is short and a starting point for negotiation when supply is in surplus.

What the two bets share: both were made when others were bearish, and both used "long-term contracts/long-term capacity" to counter short-term prices. This fits the profile of good management, and also fits the fate of management at cyclical stocks—their judgment can only be validated by the next cycle, and the next cycle always comes.

Capital allocation: restrained, but with one huge bet

The restraint shows in three places. First, net cash at the top of the cycle: cash and investments of USD 30.2 billion and liquidity of USD 32.2 billion, with no legacy of high-interest debt issued during a downturn. Second, share count essentially flat at 1.13 billion shares over five years, with no reliance on equity issuance to stay afloat. Third, the dividend raised only 30% to USD 0.15 per quarter, with no large increase in the payout commitment when profit surged — which is right, because next year's profit could be a third of this year's.

The huge bet is capex. USD 27 billion in FY26, over USD 40 billion in FY27, and USD 250 billion invested in the US through 2035. Taken together, these three numbers mean: management believes the shortage will last until 2028, and is willing to stake all the money earned in this cycle on that judgment. If right, Micron in 2028 is the lowest-cost memory maker with the most dispersed capacity; if wrong, 2028 depreciation will crush the income statement.

This is not "reckless spending" but a function of the industry's structure — not investing means losing share, investing risks oversupply. The right yardstick is not "invest or not" but "is the pace right": the ramp from USD 7.1 billion in Q3 to about USD 10 billion in Q4 shows gradual escalation rather than a one-off all-in, which deserves credit.

Integrity and governance: no fatal flaws, but two areas to watch

No fatal flaws. SCA's disclosure quality ranks high for the industry—price floors and ceilings, take-or-pay terms, customer deposit amounts and coverage periods are all public, with no vague filler such as "letters of intent" or "framework agreements." GAAP and Non-GAAP net income differ by only 2.2% (RMB 28.24 billion vs RMB 28.86 billion), indicating no dressing up of profit through adjustments. A single-class equity structure, one share one vote, and no dual-class governance discount.

The first thing to watch is the CEO also serving as chairman. Micron in 2026 is at a stage where "one person decides the direction of USD 250 billion in investment," and the chairman and CEO being the same person means the board's check on the pace of capital expenditure rests mainly on the self-discipline of independent directors. This is not a scandal, it is a structural issue, especially at the top of the cycle, when management is naturally inclined to give higher guidance.

The second is insider trading. On August 21, Mehrotra sold 4.0 million shares under a 10b5-1 plan adopted on January 30, 2026, at an average price of about USD 968.90, cashing out about USD 38.76 million, leaving him with 871,600 shares (87.16%). The plan was pre-set and the timing compliant, but the direction is clear: insiders chose to sell in the USD 950–990 range, not to buy.

Succession: the biggest single-point risk in this cycle

Mehrotra was born in 1959, is 67 in 2026, and has served as CEO for nine years. The company has announced no succession plan. At other companies this is a single line on the governance checklist; at Micron it is a substantive risk—because both key judgments of this cycle (the 2023 bet on HBM, the 2026 push into SCA) depend heavily on his personal read of the industry's rhythm.

There are three internal candidates: CFO Mark Murphy (negotiator of the capital structure and customer deposits), global operations head Manish Bhatia (executor of USD 27 billion in capex), and Chief Business Officer Sumit Sadana (negotiator of the long-term SCA contracts). What the three share is that all have participated in this playbook; the weakness is that none has independently steered a full cycle.

The implication for investors: if a CEO transition occurs in 2027–2028 and lands in a downcycle, the market will apply an "uncertainty discount." This is not a reason to sell now, but it is a variable that must be logged for the duration of the holding period.

Conclusion

Conclusion: management 8/10, pass, but not full marks. Mehrotra's 2023 HBM bet and the 2026 SCA long-term contract are the two key moves that shifted this company from a "pure cyclical" toward "contracted cash flow" — right direction, solid execution; capital allocation is restrained, with net cash, no dilution and a modest dividend. Two deductions: the CEO also chairs the board, weakening checks and balances; the 67-year-old CEO has no succession plan, and this cycle's judgment rests heavily on him personally. During the holding period, watch two things each quarter: whether capex pace gets out of control, and whether a succession announcement appears.
