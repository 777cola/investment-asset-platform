## Two bets, one paid off, one still unproven

The first came in 2023. As industry-wide capex was cut, Micron laid off roughly 10% of staff and posted a net loss of USD 5.8 billion for FY23—yet HBM R&D and production-line conversion never stopped. Consensus at the time held that "HBM is SK hynix's business" and that Micron was merely a follower. Instead, HBM3E won Nvidia certification in 2025, 12-layer HBM4 entered mass production in 2026 in sync with Vera Rubin, and by 2026Q2 Micron had captured 18% of global HBM share. The bet was on "AI training demand for bandwidth growing exponentially," and it paid off.

The second was the June 2026 SCA long-term contracts: 16 agreements on five-year terms, take-or-pay, with price floors and ceilings. The 14 at floor prices correspond to about USD 100 billion in revenue, and customers have paid about USD 22 billion in cash deposits. It is the first time anyone in the memory industry has tried to commoditize contracts. But it has not yet been tested by a downcycle—take-or-pay is a talisman when supply is short and a starting point for negotiation when supply is in surplus.

What the two bets share: both were made when others were bearish, and both used "long-term contracts/long-term capacity" to counter short-term prices. That fits the profile of good management—and also the fate of management at cyclical stocks: their judgment can only be validated by the next cycle, and the next cycle always comes.

## Capital allocation: restrained, but with one huge bet

The restraint shows up in three places. First, net cash at the top of the cycle: USD 30.2 billion of cash and investments and USD 32.2 billion of liquidity, with no legacy of high-interest debt issued during a downturn. Second, the share count is essentially flat at 1.13 billion shares over five years, with no reliance on equity issuance to stay afloat. Third, the dividend was raised only 30% to USD 0.15 per quarter, with no big step-up in the payout commitment when profit surged — which is right, because next year's profit could be a third of this year's.

The huge bet is capex: USD 27 billion in FY26, over USD 40 billion in FY27, and USD 250 billion invested in the US through 2035. Taken together, these three numbers mean management believes the shortage will last until 2028, and is willing to stake all the money earned in this cycle on that judgment. If right, Micron in 2028 is the lowest-cost memory maker with the most dispersed capacity; if wrong, 2028 depreciation will crush the income statement.

This is not "reckless spending" but a function of the industry's structure — not investing means losing share, investing risks oversupply. The right yardstick is not "invest or not" but "is the pace right": the ramp from USD 7.1 billion in Q3 to about USD 10 billion in Q4 shows gradual escalation rather than a one-off all-in, which deserves credit.

## Integrity and governance: no fatal flaws, but two areas to watch

No fatal flaws. SCA's disclosure quality ranks high for the industry: price floors and ceilings, take-or-pay terms, customer deposit amounts and coverage periods are all public, with none of the vague filler such as "letters of intent" or "framework agreements." GAAP and Non-GAAP net income differ by only 2.2% (RMB 28.24 billion vs RMB 28.86 billion), indicating no dressing up of profit through adjustments. A single-class equity structure, one share one vote, and no dual-class governance discount.

The first thing to watch is the CEO also serving as chairman. In 2026, Micron is at a stage where "one person decides the direction of USD 250 billion in investment," and combining the chairman and CEO roles means the board's check on the pace of capital expenditure rests mainly on the self-discipline of independent directors. This is not a scandal, it is a structural issue, especially at the top of the cycle, when management is naturally inclined to give higher guidance.

The second is insider trading. On August 21, Mehrotra sold 4.0 million shares under a 10b5-1 plan adopted on January 30, 2026, at an average price of about USD 968.90, cashing out about USD 38.76 million, leaving him with 871,600 shares (87.16%). The plan was pre-set and the timing compliant, but the direction is clear: insiders chose to sell in the USD 950–990 range, not to buy.

## Succession: the biggest single-point risk in this cycle

Mehrotra was born in 1959, turns 67 in 2026 and has been CEO for nine years. No succession plan has been announced. At most companies that is a single line on the governance checklist; at Micron it is a substantive risk, because both key judgments of this cycle—the 2023 bet on HBM and the 2026 push into SCA—rest heavily on his personal read of the industry's rhythm.

There are three internal candidates: CFO Mark Murphy (negotiator of the capital structure and customer deposits), global operations head Manish Bhatia (executor of USD 27 billion in capex) and Chief Business Officer Sumit Sadana (negotiator of the long-term SCA contracts). All three have participated in this playbook; the weakness is that none has independently steered a full cycle.

The implication for investors: if a CEO transition occurs in 2027–2028 and lands in a downcycle, the market will apply an "uncertainty discount." That is not a reason to sell now, but it is a variable that must be logged for the duration of the holding period.
