Core view

Screen the global memory names through a funnel: Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, ChangXin, Nanya, Winbond. The rough cut eliminates four at the first pass — Samsung's business is too mixed, SK hynix's valuation already prices in the HBM premium, ChangXin is unlisted and policy-priced, and Nanya and Winbond play only in niche DRAM with no HBM.

That leaves Micron alone. The reason is not that "it is the best" but that "it is the only name that lets you hold this cycle purely": 92% of revenue from DRAM+NAND, the lowest cost base of the three, the only one offering take-or-pay long-term contracts, and the best US equity liquidity. The drawbacks are equally clear: smallest scale, third in HBM share.

Initial screen: four grounds for elimination
Samsung: Market cap USD 1.08 trillion, DRAM share 39.4%, HBM 33% — the strongest in the industry. Memory, however, is only part of the revenue mix (the rest is phones, panels, appliances, foundry), so buying Samsung means buying a Korean conglomerate with diluted leverage to the cycle. Its foundry business has also lost money for years and drags on the overall valuation — you want the memory cycle; it gives you a conglomerate. SK hynix: 50% HBM share, the biggest winner of this cycle. But its valuation already reflects that: 2026E P/E 4.8x, 2027E 3.6x, which looks cheaper than Micron — the reason it is cheap is that the market has already priced in 2027 peak earnings, and Korean equities carry a "chaebol discount" and FX risk. For overseas investors, there is an added layer of FX and liquidity cost. ChangXin Memory: The fastest grower (revenue +99.3% QoQ, 9.5% share), but unlisted — its STAR Market IPO passed review on 27 May and is still in the registration process. Even once listed, its pricing will be shaped by domestic-substitution policy and local capital, not purely by the market. Nanya Technology / Winbond: Both are niche DRAM (consumer electronics, networking, automotive), with 2026 gross margins of 67.9%/70% — strong leverage, but no HBM, no leading-edge process, and scale a few dozen times smaller than Micron. They are "cyclical beta", not "AI alpha".
Final pick: Why Micron

First, purity. 92% of revenue comes from DRAM and NAND — no foundry, no handsets, no advertising. When DRAM rises 60%, it shows up in the numbers immediately; Samsung cannot do this.

Second, cost. Its bit cost on the 1γ/1δ nodes is the lowest of the three. In shortage years that means earning more; in glut years it means being the last one to lose money. For a cyclical stock, the latter matters more — it determines whether you survive the bottom and buy the dip.

Third, long-term contracts. 16 SCAs, five-year terms, take-or-pay, USD 100 billion minimum revenue, USD 22 billion of customer deposits — of the three, only Micron has turned this into a publicly disclosed business model. That does not mean it will succeed, but it does mean it is the only one trying to change the industry's rules.

Fourth, investability. USD 1.22 trillion market cap, US equity liquidity, a single share class, high-quality disclosure (GAAP/Non-GAAP gap of 2.2%). For the vast majority of investors, this is the only pure memory name you can buy, sell and understand.

Make its disadvantages versus competitors equally clear

Scale: DRAM share 23.3% versus Samsung's 39.4%; HBM share 18% versus SK hynix's 50% and Samsung's 33%. Micron ranks third of the three, not first. Owning it is a bet on elasticity and cost, not on leadership.

Technology gap: HBM4 12-layer mass production trailed Samsung, which got there first globally in February 2026; only Micron's ramp is faster. HBM5 resets the race, with no first-mover advantage carried over.

Valuation: 6.5x 2027E, an 80% premium to SK hynix at 3.6x. On pure cheapness, SK hynix wins; the case for Micron rests on purer elasticity, more transparent disclosure and better liquidity—a preference, not a mathematical conclusion.

Another option: buy equipment, not memory

For those seeking exposure to the memory capex cycle, equipment makers offer a steadier vehicle. Micron's FY26 capex is USD 27 billion and FY27 above USD 40 billion, with Samsung and SK hynix expanding in tandem; that money ultimately flows to ASML, Applied Materials, Lam Research and KLA. Equipment makers run gross margins of 48%–60% through the cycle, far less volatile than memory fabs, and investors need not call 2027 DRAM contract prices.

The trade-off is less upside: equipment makers will not earn 85% gross margins in a shortage year. This is a substitute that trades upside for certainty—if you accept that the memory shortage will last another 4–6 quarters but are unsure how high prices can go, buying the equipment makers is more logical than buying Micron. It is also the Duan Yongping-style "sell the shovels" choice.

Conclusion

Conclusion: of the six, only Micron is investable — but "investable" does not mean "buy now." It is the only pure way to own this memory cycle: 92% of revenue from memory, the lowest cost base, the only one with long-term contracts and the best liquidity. Yet it also ranks third of three — smaller than Samsung, with HBM share an order of magnitude below SK hynix. At 6.5x 2027E it is more expensive than SK hynix at 3.6x, and that premium pays for "purity and transparency." Buying it and holding it are both fine, but adding at this level means paying an 80% premium for "purity."
