## Body

Three numbers to start.

First: USD 41.46 billion. That is Micron's revenue for the third quarter of fiscal 2026, up 346% YoY and 74% QoQ — a record high for the fifth consecutive quarter. The top end of company guidance was USD 34.3 billion; the actual came in USD 7.9 billion above it.

Second: 84.9%. That is this quarter's gross margin. Put it in context: Nvidia's gross margin in its latest quarter was around 73%. A memory-chip maker is running a gross margin 12 percentage points above the company selling AI chips. Net margin 68%, net profit of USD 28.2 billion in a single quarter.

Third: 6.5x. That is the multiple of the USD 1,082 share price to expected 2027 earnings. The S&P 500 averages 22x P/E and semiconductor peers generally sit above 25x. At 6.5x, Micron trades in the cheapest tier in the entire market.

Put the three together and anyone's first reaction is: this makes no sense — either the market is blind, or I am missing something.

## What was left out is the "denominator"

The market is not blind. The 6.5x denominator is earnings at an 84.9% gross margin — a margin that is not normal but a cyclical extreme.

Micron's own disclosure breaks it down: DRAM shipments rose only "low single digits" QoQ this quarter while prices rose just over 60%; NAND shipments rose mid single digits while prices rose about 85%. In other words, absent the price increases, revenue this quarter would have been roughly USD 25 billion, not 41.4 billion. The extra 15-plus billion was entirely price.

Prices rose because of shortages, and the shortages stem from AI data centres' need for HBM, which consumes a large share of wafer capacity — on the same wafer, HBM bit output is only one-third to one-half that of ordinary DRAM — leaving ordinary DRAM short as well. In 2026Q1, DRAM contract prices rose 90% to 95% YoY, and HBM more than 200%.

So 6.5x is not the market making a mistake; it is the market saying: I know this profit is unsustainable. Swap in a mid-cycle 45% gross margin and the same share price implies 24x to 28x — not cheap at all.

## What is truly worth examining is that contract.

The headline number in this earnings report is not the RMB 28.2 billion net profit; it is the 16 long-term contracts.

Micron has signed five-year strategic customer agreements with 16 customers — take-or-pay, committed volumes, and price floors and ceilings. Of these, 14 are measured at the minimum price, corresponding to about USD 100 billion of revenue; customers have already paid about USD 22 billion in cash deposits.

In plain terms: customers pay upfront, lock in five years of volume, and accept that "even if prices fall, they will not go below a certain level." For the first time in the storage industry's decades-long history, someone has moved pricing from "set by the market" to "set by contract."

If this works, Micron's revenue visibility extends from 3 months to 5 years, and its valuation multiple could re-rate from 6x to more than 10x. But there is a catch: coverage is only 20%. USD 100 billion over five years is USD 20 billion a year, against FY26 revenue of about USD 129 billion. The remaining 80% of revenue is still exposed to price volatility.

So the long-term contracts "shave the peaks," not "fill the troughs." They can reduce losses in a downturn; they cannot prevent one.

## So should we buy now?

Three scenarios; the math is yours to do.

Bear case: supply catches up in 2027, AI capex peaks, EPS falls back to USD 80 at a 5x multiple — USD 400, down 63%.

Base case: still in shortage in 2027, EPS USD 166 at 6.7x — USD 1,120, up 3%.

Bull case: long-term contracts cover more than half of output, EPS USD 180, multiple re-rates to 9x — USD 1,620, up 50%.

Weighted 25%, 50% and 25%, the expected value is USD 1,065, 1.6% below the current price. This is not a trade to chase.

Nor is it a trade to sell. If you already hold it with a cost basis below USD 500, selling triggers capital gains tax and forfeits the free option that long-term contracts change the industry's rules. Hold, don't add.

If you hold no position, wait for one of two signals: the stock falls back below USD 800 (4.8x 2027 earnings), or the company explicitly discloses long-term contract coverage above 30% in its results.

## One final counterpoint.

On September 22, Michael Burry said he had significantly increased his short position in Micron. His reasoning: this round of DRAM tightness is not a genuine demand surge but a supply gap created by three players shifting capacity to HBM. Once HBM capacity catches up, ordinary DRAM flips to oversupply in an instant.

The argument is not necessarily right, but it is worth hearing. Bulls and bears are working from the same data set; the only disagreement is how long this shortage can last. My judgment: fundamentals hold up through the first half of 2027, while the share price may peak earlier, in 2026Q4 to 2027Q1.

So my conclusion is "hold, do not add" — not because the business is poor, but because at this level the risk-reward is unattractive.(This article is a research note and does not constitute investment advice.
