Six-Gate Screen — Micron Technology

Micron Technology (MU) | Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/09/25  ·  Price $1,082.28 (+0.16%)  ·  Market cap About USD 1.22 trillion (on a rough estimate of 1.13 billion shares)  ·  PE(2027E) ~6.5x on consensus (5.77x in mid-September)

Core view

Four of six gates passed, 37/60. Micron is the purest play on this memory cycle: FY26Q3 revenue of USD 41.46 billion, +346% YoY, gross margin 84.9%, DRAM at 76% of revenue. Price increases flow straight to profit, with no other business diluting the operating leverage.

The first four gates pass cleanly: the business can be explained in one sentence, pricing power sits with the seller in a three-player oligopoly, HBM4 is already in volume shipment, and management did not cut HBM investment in 2023, the industry's worst year. The fifth gate fails — the 6.5x denominator is peak profit at an 85% gross margin; on a rough normalised 45% gross margin, the same price equates to 24–28x. The sixth gate also fails: the buy case rests on "this cycle is different", and the September 30 earnings report is just five days away.

USD 1,082.28 is not a bad price, it is a bad position. Hold, don't add.

Gate one: the business is understandable, the price is not

Score: 9. Micron's business model in three sentences: buy equipment and build fabs, turn silicon wafers into DRAM and NAND dies, sell them at contract and spot prices to server makers, phone makers and automakers. In FY26Q3, DRAM revenue was USD 31.3 billion, 76% of the total, and NAND USD 9.9 billion, 24%, with no foundry, handset or advertising business to dilute the leverage — DRAM rises 60% and profit multiplies more than tenfold; that is the advantage of its business model.

The 1 point deducted sits at the second layer of judgment: buying it is essentially a bet on 2027 DRAM contract prices. No one can see that layer clearly, management included — on the call they said they "can't see when supply will catch up with demand," not "we know what the price will be in 2027." Understanding the business is not the same as understanding the price, and on memory stocks the two must be assessed separately.

Stage two: just two years of evidence that this is a good business

A marginal pass at 6 points. The bull case is real: global DRAM has consolidated to Samsung, SK hynix, Micron and a single CXMT, and in 2026Q2 those three accounted for 88.7% of industry revenue (TrendForce basis). With three oligopolists and one AI customer buying at any cost, pricing power rests entirely with sellers for now, and gross margin climbed from 39% in FY25Q3 to 84.9%.

The bear case is equally real: memory has never posted three consecutive years of high gross margin. In the last cycle gross margin was 45% in FY22 before falling below 10% in FY23; revenue halved from USD 30.7 billion to USD 15.5 billion and the full year delivered a net loss of USD 5.8 billion. Cash from this business must also go straight back into capacity: FY26 capex is USD 27 billion, FY27 guidance exceeds USD 40 billion, and Q3 alone absorbed USD 7.1 billion. What the company earns is not free cash flow; it is the entry fee for the next round.

Gate three: three moats, none of which can stop price cuts

7 points. The first is cost: bit cost on the 1γ/1δ nodes is the lowest of the three, so in a downturn it is the last to lose money. The second is qualification: 12-layer HBM4 (36GB) has shipped in volume to Nvidia's full Vera Rubin accelerator line, 16-layer (48GB) engineering samples have gone to leading cloud providers, and the 12-layer HBM4 ramp is twice as fast as the prior generation's 12-layer HBM3E. The third is US capacity: USD 250 billion invested in New York, Idaho and Virginia before 2035, targeting 40% of DRAM production in the US, plus USD 6.165 billion in CHIPS Act subsidies.

But none of these three stops prices from falling. Low cost only guarantees being "the last to lose money"; qualification only protects one product generation—in June 2026 Jensen Huang confirmed in Seoul that Samsung, SK hynix and Micron had all passed HBM4 qualification, so exclusivity is gone; US capacity is a strategic asset and also a cost burden. The moat's job is to keep it alive, not to make it non-cyclical.

Stage four: management got two big bets right

Score 8, the highest in the entire company. The first instance came in 2023: while the whole industry cut capex and laid off 10% of staff, Micron kept HBM R&D and production line conversion running; in 2025 HBM3E won Nvidia certification, and in 2026 HBM4 ramped alongside Vera Rubin. At the time the decision looked like "burning money against the tide"; now it looks like the most valuable step of the entire cycle.

The second came with the June 2026 SCA long-term contracts: 16 strategic customer agreements on five-year terms (three years for automotive), take-or-pay, with committed volumes and price floors and ceilings. Of these, 14 correspond to roughly USD 100 billion in revenue at the floor price, and customers have paid about USD 22 billion in cash deposits and financial commitments.This is the first time the memory industry has shifted from "price set by the market" to "price set by contract", and the only legitimate reason the valuation can be re-rated.

The caveat: on August 21, CEO Mehrotra sold 40,000 shares under a 10b5-1 plan, cashing out about USD 38.76 million and cutting his holding to 871,600 shares (about 0.08%). This is routine under a pre-set plan and does not constitute a governance issue, but it also shows insiders will not add at this price.

Gate Five: The 6.5x Scam Sits in the Denominator

3 points — a fatal flaw. The current USD 1,082.28 corresponds to 6.5x the 2027 consensus EPS of about USD 166 — in mid-September it was still 5.77x, so the multiple was passively pushed up 13% in a month because the share price rose faster than earnings expectations. On the surface this is the cheapest semiconductor stock in the S&P 500.

But swap the denominator for a normal year: assume a return to mid-cycle in 2029, revenue of USD 150 billion (about 1.16x FY26), a 45% gross margin and a 30% net margin, giving EPS of about USD 39 and 28x the current price; if revenue only returns to USD 110 billion with a 35% gross margin, EPS is about USD 19, or 57x. The difference between 6x and 28x is not a valuation method, it is the cycle position. The market gives it 6x precisely because it knows this E is unsustainable; if the market believed 2027 profits could hold, it would not give only 6x.

The margin of safety on cyclicals is the cruelest: in a downturn EPS and the multiple contract at the same time. In the 2022 cycle, the share price fell more than 50%, revenue was halved, and the gross margin dropped from 45% to below 10%.

Gate 6: The buy case is a conviction, not a premise

Four points. There is only one reason to buy Micron now: the belief that AI demand keeps memory in shortage through 2028. That call may prove right—SK hynix's CEO said in July that 2027 would be the tightest year for supply, with DRAM bit growth ex-HBM at about 15% versus demand growth of 22%, a 7 percentage point gap, and meaningful supply growth does not arrive until 2028. But no earnings report can verify it.

Timing is the bigger problem. FY26Q4 results land after the close on September 30. Consensus expects revenue of about USD 50.8–51.4 billion and EPS of USD 31.5–31.75; guidance is USD 50 billion ±1 billion and EPS USD 31 ±1. One number five days out decides moves on the order of "USD 800 or USD 1,200." Staking an add-to-position decision on an earnings report five days away is not discipline, it is gambling.

Meanwhile the other side of the market is active: on September 22 Michael Burry said he had "substantially increased" short positions in Micron, SOXX and Nebius, arguing that current DRAM tightness stems from a supply-side effect as capacity is diverted to HBM, not from structural demand. The thesis may not be right, but it is a reminder: bulls and bears use the same data set; the disagreement is over how long it lasts.

Veto items and items to be verified
Veto item: margin of safety. On FY26E net profit of about USD 83 billion, the stock trades at 15x; on 2027 consensus, 6.5x. The entire gap between the two multiples hinges on whether 2027 profit can still grow. That is not a valuation question but a cycle call, and a margin of safety offers no protection on a cycle call—you cannot discount an E that is peaking.
  • FY27Q1 guidance at the September 30 earnings: whether revenue and EPS land above the roughly USD 35–37 consensus, and whether gross margin peaks near 86%
  • SCA coverage: management says long-term agreements carry minimum volumes at minimum prices; can actual coverage rise from roughly 20% of DRAM volume today to 50%
  • 2027 industry bit supply growth: the three players' plans combined already approach demand growth; above 20% would signal a cycle reversal
  • HBM share: Counterpoint 2026Q2 puts Micron at 18%, down 3pct QoQ, with Samsung up from 21% to 33%; continued share loss would indicate the HBM4 certification dividend has not been captured
  • Capex discipline: whether FY27 guidance above USD 40 billion is raised again, and the 2028 supply consequences

Conclusion

Conclusion: Hold, do not add. Four of six gates passed, 37/60. At the company level there is almost nothing to fault: the purest memory play, the lowest cost, the fastest HBM4 ramp and an industry-first take-or-pay long-term contract. At the price level, however, two hurdles cannot be cleared: 6.5x E is on peak earnings, and the September 30 report is five days away. Existing holders should stay put—cost basis is far below the current price, and neither the tax nor the opportunity cost of selling justifies an exit. New positions should wait for one of two things: the stock back below USD 800 (about 4.8x 2027E), or SCA coverage explicitly disclosed above half, with the valuation framework shifting from "cyclical stock" to "contracted-revenue stock."
