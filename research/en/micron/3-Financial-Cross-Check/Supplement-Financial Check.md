## The biggest deviation: Q4 guidance is 16% above consensus

On June 24 the company guided FY26Q4 to USD 49–51 billion and EPS of USD 30–32, against a pre-print consensus of USD 43.1 billion and USD 25.3. No one miscalculated — sell-side models did not anticipate how sharp the price increases would be before the Q3 print. Q3 itself came in USD 7.9 billion above the top end of guidance (guidance 33.57±0.75 billion, actual 41.46 billion), scrambling everyone's models.

By late September the consensus had been revised up to revenue of USD 50.8–51.4 billion and EPS of USD 31.5–31.75, essentially hugging the upper half of company guidance. Treat the "company guidance of USD 49–51 billion" with care: the market has already taken guidance as the floor. This is a classic case of guidance losing its meaning after being revised up — a print even slightly below USD 51.4 billion could be read as below expectations.

## The few within 1% deviation show the data is clean

Revenue, gross margin, EPS and DRAM revenue all tie exactly to company filings, broker research and media citations (deviation 0–0.01%). That level of consistency is unusual for memory names, where segment revenue, bit shipments and ASP are often calculated differently across sources. Micron's disclosure quality this quarter is solid: the four segments (CDBU 11.5 billion, CMBU 13.77 billion, MCBU 11.52 billion, AEBU 4.63 billion) sum to 41.42 billion, a 0.036 billion gap versus total revenue of 41.456 billion, reflecting rounding.

Net income carries two measures: GAAP 28.24 billion and Non-GAAP 28.86 billion, a 0.62 billion (2.2%) difference from stock-based compensation and restructuring charges. This report uses GAAP for the "68% net margin" and Non-GAAP for "EPS 25.11" — because sell-side consensus and company guidance both use Non-GAAP, and mixing the two produces wrong multiples.

## Why share data cannot be mixed

The 18% HBM share is from Counterpoint (revenue basis, 2026Q2) and the 23.3% DRAM share from TrendForce (also revenue basis, same period). The two differ in methodology, sample and timing, and HBM sits within DRAM, so combining 18% and 23.3% to derive an "HBM penetration rate" is wrong.

We cite them separately: the HBM competitive landscape draws only on Counterpoint (SK hynix 50%, Samsung 33%, Micron 18%), while total DRAM and the CXMT threat draw only on TrendForce (Samsung 39.4%, SK hynix 24.9%, Micron 23.3%, CXMT 9.5%). TrendForce industry revenue of USD 154.73 billion × Micron's 23.3% = USD 36.05 billion, a 15% gap versus the company's disclosed DRAM revenue of USD 31.3 billion. The gap is definitional: TrendForce measures DRAM brand revenue (including HBM), whereas Micron's reported DRAM revenue excludes some HBM-related packaging revenue — a definitional difference, not a data error — so we make no forced reconciliation.

## The four finally adopted metrics

- Financial data: raw company filings in USD million; GAAP and non-GAAP presented separately and clearly labelled

- Share price and market cap: batchdata close of USD 1,082.28 on 2026/09/25 × 1.13 billion shares = USD 1.22 trillion; undiluted share count used to avoid conflict with the "P/E (2027E) 6.5x" basis

- Industry share: TrendForce for DRAM, Counterpoint for HBM, each with quarter and methodology noted

- Consensus: used for P/E and target-price comparison, sourced late September (EPS 2027E of about USD 166, FY26Q4 revenue of USD 50.8–51.4 billion), with timing labelled "late September"

## Two careful checks on share price and share count

The share-price series can only come from one source: batchdata daily bars. The 2026/09/25 close was USD 1,082.28, up 0.16% on the day. The 52-week high is USD 1,213.56, 10.8% above the current price, and the year-to-date gain is about 226%, which implies a price of about USD 332 at the start of the year. That is consistent with media reports of "a cumulative gain of about 14% since September" and "a close of USD 1,096.16 on 22 September", indicating no gaps in the price series.

The sequential share shifts have to be added up manually. Counterpoint's 2026Q2 HBM figures are SK hynix 50% (58% in the prior quarter), Samsung 33% (21%) and Micron 18% (21%), totalling 101%, i.e. 100% after rounding. The key point in this data is not that Micron lost 3 points, but that Samsung grabbed 12 points in a single quarter — proof that HBM4 qualification is not an exclusive pass.
