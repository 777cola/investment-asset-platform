Core thesis

Size positions by holding cost, not by view on the company: the risk/reward is entirely different at different cost levels. Low-cost positions (<600 USD): hold, do not add. High-cost positions (>950 USD): cut to half. No position: 0%, wait for below 800 USD.

On correlation, Micron trades closely in line with Nvidia, SK hynix and SOXX, so treat it as beta on the AI trade rather than a diversification tool. At the portfolio level, no single cyclical stock should exceed 10%–15% of equity positions, and do not overweight it alongside Nvidia, AMD and SOXX.

Three price-level rules
Trim line USD 1,300 (+20%): about 7.8x 2027E, close to the low end of sell-side targets (Citi 1,300). At that level the 7x neutral case is already priced in, so risk/reward deteriorates — cut a third. Entry line USD 800 (-26%): about 4.8x 2027E, slightly richer than SK hynix's 3.6x, but 26% cheaper than the current price — a reasonable entry point before the cycle turns. Hard stop USD 700 (-35%) with contract prices turning negative QoQ: both conditions must be met to exit in full. Price alone is not enough — if the stock falls to 700 but contract prices are still rising, that is sentiment, not fundamentals; if contract prices turn negative but the stock is still at 1,000, the market has not caught up and you should actively trim.
Correlation: the beta of the AI trade, not a diversification tool

Micron's correlation with Nvidia, AMD, SK hynix and SOXX exceeds 0.8, and this logic played out on 22 September 2026 — the same day Burry announced his short on Micron and SOXX. Correlation with TSMC is moderate (around 0.6), reflecting TSMC's more diversified customer base, while correlation with consumer stocks and utilities is very low.

The implication: if you already hold Nvidia + TSMC + a semiconductor ETF, adding Micron will not reduce portfolio volatility — it will only amplify exposure to the single factor of "AI capex". Treat it as part of total exposure to the "AI hardware" bucket, not as a new diversifying position.

Correlation with Samsung and SK hynix is higher (the same variable in the same industry), but they trade in different markets and currencies and offer no real hedge — they rise and fall together, only by different magnitudes.

One concrete suggestion: replace adding to the position with an options structure

If you are genuinely bullish but reluctant to chase at USD 1,082, consider selling a put with an USD 800 strike: collect the premium, take delivery if the stock falls to 800, and keep the premium if it does not. That converts "waiting for a pullback" into a paid wait.

Two caveats. First, option liquidity — Micron options are active, so this is not a major issue. Second, position sizing: selling a put is a commitment to buy at USD 800, so work out in advance whether you will have the cash and whether you are willing to hold at that price.Without that preparation, do not sell the put — just wait for the price.

Rebalancing discipline: quarterly reviews, not monthly

Position sizing in cyclicals should not be adjusted daily on price; adjust it quarterly, in line with earnings reports. The cadence: after the September 30 earnings report, watch gross margin and FY27Q1 guidance; after the mid-December FY27Q1 report, check whether SCA coverage is disclosed; after the March 2027 FY27Q2 report, check whether gross margin is still above 85%. Each time, look at one thing only, and do not add or trim on share-price moves.

The only exception is a "price trigger": a break below 800 or a move above 1,300 is executed on discipline regardless of earnings dates. The benefit is that it separates "judgment" from "emotion"—judgment is updated quarterly; emotion has no trading authority.

Conclusion

Conclusion: hold, do not add; manage in cost-based tiers. Keep low-cost positions, halve high-cost positions and otherwise stay in cash. Three price disciplines: cut one-third at USD 1,300, build one-third at USD 800, and exit fully at USD 700 if contract prices turn negative. On correlation, count it within total "AI hardware" exposure, capping any single memory stock at 10%–15% of equity positions. Core principle: do not use "cheap" as a reason to add in the back half of the cycle; the 6.5x denominator is illusory.
