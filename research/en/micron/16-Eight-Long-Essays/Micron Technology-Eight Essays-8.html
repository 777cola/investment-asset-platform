Core view

Compress the judgments from the previous seven pieces into one decision framework: three price levels, two fundamental triggers, one time window. Price levels determine "expensive or not," fundamental triggers determine "whether the thesis still holds," and the time window determines "whether to act now."

Step one: separate "expensive or not" from "good or not"

These two questions must be answered separately, or the conclusion will be wrong. The answer to whether Micron is "good" is yes: lowest cost, HBM4 in mass production, long-term contracts locking in five years, net cash of USD 30.2 billion. The answer to whether it is "expensive" is yes: the 6.5x EPS is peak profit; on a normalized basis it is 24–28x.

The combined conclusion can only be "hold, do not add": a good company should not be sold, and an expensive price should not be bought. Using either answer alone leads to error—looking only at "good" means chasing the high, while looking only at "expensive" means missing the 226% gain.
Step two: price on "normalized" rather than "current" earnings

Normalized algorithm: revenue USD 150 billion (mid-cycle level after AI raises the centerline), gross margin 45%, net margin 30%, 1.13 billion shares → EPS of about USD 39. At 15x = USD 585; at 20x = USD 780; at 25x = USD 975.

On that basis, the current USD 1,082 corresponds to about 28x normalized earnings—that is "reasonably expensive," not "cheap."The market assigns 6.5x current PE because it knows current E is unsustainable; an investor who insists "6.5x is cheap" is betting against the market that this time E can persist.

The key assumption in normalization is the 45% gross margin. If you believe long-term contracts can lift mid-cycle gross margin to 55%, then EPS is USD 52, and 20x = USD 1,040—close to the current price.So the core disagreement in this entire investment is "whether mid-cycle gross margin is 45% or 55%."

Step three: set the trigger conditions, then wait

The most expensive mistake in cyclical investing is not "buying the wrong thing" but "buying the right thing at the wrong time." So the framework's focus is not prediction but "trigger + action": price triggers (build at 800, trim at 1,300), fundamental triggers (30% coverage, contract price turning negative, gross margin falling below 80%).

The advantage of these rules is that they turn decisions from "daily judgment" into "conditional triggers." You don't need to forecast 2027 prices; you only need to act when the conditions appear. This is also the operational version of what Duan Yongping calls "ordinary mind"—not the absence of emotion, but letting rules bear the emotion for you.

What would change my mind

Upside: ① SCA coverage is disclosed above 30%, with management laying out a path to 50%; ② 2027 contract price increases hold at QoQ +5% or more, with the shortage extending into 2028; ③ HBM share returns above 20% and HBM5 secures first-launch certification; ④ free cash flow exceeds USD 15 billion for three consecutive quarters without relying on a low capex ratio.

Downside: ① contract prices turn negative QoQ for two consecutive months; ② gross margin guidance falls below 80%; ③ Samsung's HBM share exceeds 40% and Micron falls below 15%; ④ the three major manufacturers raise 2027 capex in aggregate by more than 30% (a precursor to oversupply).

Of these eight conditions, any one of the first four allows us to raise the valuation center; any one of the last four means trim. If none appear, maintain "Hold, do not add".
Execution checklist: what to do this week, what to do this quarter

This week (9/25–10/3): after the September 30 earnings release, watch only three numbers — whether revenue exceeds RMB 51.4 billion, whether gross margin holds at 86%, and whether FY27Q1 EPS guidance exceeds USD 37. If all three are above, hold; if two are above, hold; if one or none is above, start trimming.

This quarter (October–December): track the month-over-month DRAM contract price for October and November, Counterpoint's Q3 HBM share, and whether management discloses SCA coverage on the earnings call. If any one of these three turns, act by the rules.

What not to do: do not add on strength because the stock rises to 1,200, do not bottom-fish because it falls to 950, do not build a position the day before earnings. These three are discipline, not judgment — discipline exists to protect you when your judgment is wrong.
A counterexample: if there really is a surplus in 2028

Assume that in 2028 all three players' capacity is released at once, DRAM contract prices fall 50%, Micron's revenue returns to USD 70 billion, gross margin is 35% and net margin 20%; EPS would then be about USD 12. At 12x that is USD 145, at 15x USD 180 — a -83% to -86% decline from the current USD 1,082.

This counterexample is not a forecast but a stress test. Its purpose is to remind you that the bear-case USD 400 (-63%) is already a mild assumption, and the true cycle bottom is lower still. In 2019 Micron's stock fell from USD 60 to USD 28 (-53%), and in 2022 from USD 98 to USD 48 (-51%) — both were halvings; neither was a 20% drop.

So the right way to size a position is not "I'm bullish, so I go heavy" but "I could be wrong, so the position must withstand -60%." That is why the final recommendation is hold, do not add, rather than buy — not because the outlook is poor, but because the risk-reward does not permit a heavy position.

Conclusion

Decision framework summary: three price levels (build at 800, trim at 1,300, exit at 700 if contract prices turn negative), two fundamental triggers (30% coverage, contract prices turning negative), one time window (September 30 earnings). There is only one core disagreement: whether mid-cycle gross margin is 45% or 55% — the former implies USD 585–780, the latter USD 1,040. The current price of USD 1,082 has already priced in the optimistic 55% assumption, so buying now means paying for "the best case."
