# Micron Technology（MU）— Quality Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/09/25
- Price：1082.28 USD
- Market cap：About USD 1.22 trillion (on a rough estimate of 1.13 billion shares)

## Notes

The seven metrics apply the AI Berkshire negative-screening criteria at the framework's default thresholds. FY26E revenue is a rough calculation: Q1 USD 13.64 billion + Q2 USD 23.86 billion + Q3 USD 41.46 billion + Q4 guidance midpoint USD 50 billion = about USD 129 billion. Ten-year ROE is the arithmetic mean of annual net income / ending equity, not the geometric mean.

## The three that fall short all point to the same thing

Ten-year average ROE of 9%: FY18 was 44%, driven by DRAM price increases, while FY23 was -13%, driven by DRAM price declines. Averaging the two years tells you nothing; what matters is the signal that this company's return on capital is set by price, not by operating efficiency. Same fabs, same engineers: FY23 lost RMB 5.8 billion, FY26 earned over RMB 80 billion.

Gross margin of 9%–85%: a ten-year range spanning 76 percentage points. Any valuation model that treats an "85% gross margin" as the norm will be wildly wrong. Judge this industry on the direction of change in gross margin, not its level — 2026Q4 guidance is 86%, and any quarter in 2027 that falls below 80% QoQ marks the top of this cycle.

Cumulative ten-year free cash flow near zero: this is the most painful point. Micron is not unprofitable; the money it earns turns into fabs and equipment. FY23 operating cash flow was about USD 1.6 billion and capex USD 7.7 billion, for free cash flow of -USD 6.1 billion; FY24 capex remained elevated, with free cash flow of about -USD 1.2 billion. FY26 single-quarter free cash flow of USD 18.3 billion is a historical extreme for this cycle, but FY27 capex guidance exceeds USD 40 billion, meaning the money earned gets plowed back in.

## The four points it passed show it is not a bad company.

Net cash: cash and investments of USD 30.2 billion, liquidity of USD 32.2 billion, and Q3 single-quarter operating cash flow of USD 25.39 billion. Every historical downturn in memory has buried someone under debt — Elpida's 2012 bankruptcy being the case in point. Micron enters this cycle's peak with net cash, and that is the fundamental reason it can survive the next downturn.

Five-year revenue CAGR of 36%: from USD 27.7 billion in FY21 to about USD 129 billion in FY26E, nearly fivefold. That reflects price, HBM structural upgrades and genuine incremental AI demand. It cannot simply be dismissed as "all price increases" — HBM went from zero to an 18% global share in Q2 2026, which is real share gain and product upgrade.

Operating cash flow/net profit of 0.90x: below 1, because receivables and inventory expand in step with revenue; Q3 revenue rose 74% QoQ, so working capital absorption is inevitably large. This is not a fraud signal but normal for a high-growth period — though it should not earn a "superior cash flow quality" assessment either.

Share count broadly flat over five years at around 1.13 billion shares, with no reliance on equity issuance to stay alive, and in March 2026 the quarterly dividend was raised 30% to USD 0.15. For a capital-intensive cyclical, not diluting is a plus.

## But it is not fatal: it does not determine the valuation method

For a consumer goods company, failing three of the seven criteria would be an automatic reject. Micron, however, is a cyclical stock, and screening it against the seven criteria for compounders is a methodological mismatch—Buffett himself bought PetroChina and BYD, both cyclical or policy-driven; what matters is not whether the metrics look good, but where in the cycle the purchase price sits.

The right way to use the seven criteria is therefore this: the four it passes tell you "it won't die," and the three it fails tell you "it can't command a high multiple." Together they yield this report's conclusion—holdable, but not to be priced as a growth stock; 6.5x 2027E earnings is not cheap, it is the market's normal discount for "unsustainable E."

The real cause for caution lies elsewhere: if Micron one day lifts SCA coverage above 50%, keeps free cash flow positive for three consecutive years and stops breaking below a 25% gross margin trough, these three failed metrics would improve simultaneously—and only then would a 12–15x multiple hold up. That day has not arrived.
