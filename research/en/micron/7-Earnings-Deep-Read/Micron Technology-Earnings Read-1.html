Earnings Read — Micron Technology

Core view

FY26Q3 (March–May 2026) was Micron's best quarter ever: revenue USD 41.456 billion, +345.7% YoY and +73.8% QoQ, Non-GAAP gross margin 84.9%, +10pct QoQ, GAAP net income USD 28.24 billion at a 68% net margin, and quarterly free cash flow of USD 18.3 billion, a record high.

Strip it down and there is one story: almost every dollar this quarter came from price increases. DRAM bit shipments rose only low single digits QoQ while ASPs climbed just over 60%; NAND bits rose mid single digits with ASPs up about 85%. Q4 guidance is USD 49–51 billion at a gross margin of about 86% — prices are still rising, but clearly decelerating.

Tier-one revenue structure: flat volumes, rising prices, all four segments jumping together

Total: revenue RMB 41.456 billion, +345.7% YoY and +73.8% QoQ, the fifth consecutive record quarter, above the top end of guidance (RMB 33.57 billion ± 0.75 billion) by RMB 7.9 billion. The consensus ahead of the print was about RMB 35.69 billion; actual came in 16% above it.

Breakdown: DRAM revenue RMB 31.3 billion, 76% of total, +343% YoY and +67% QoQ; NAND revenue RMB 9.9 billion, 24% of total, +361% YoY. DRAM bit shipments rose only low single digits QoQ, with ASP up just over 60%; NAND bits rose mid single digits, with ASP up about 85%. At unchanged prices, revenue this quarter would have been only about RMB 26 billion — the extra RMB 15 billion is entirely price.

One signal in the mix deserves separate note: data-center NAND bit shipments rose 28% QoQ with ASP up 42% QoQ, and global enterprise SSD share climbed to second place in the industry. NAND here is rising on both volume and price — higher quality than DRAM's pure price gain.

Second-tier gross profit: all categories jump to 79%–87%

Gross margin 84.9%, up 45.9pct YoY and 10pct QoQ, the highest in company history. More telling are the segment gross margins: CDBU rose from 38% to 87%, MCBU from 24% to 87%, and AEBU from 26% to 79%. All four segments now sit in a narrow 79%–87% band, showing that the price increases are not a localized phenomenon in one product but shortage pricing across all categories and all customers.

Costs are helping too. Revenue rose 74% QoQ while depreciation and fixed costs were nearly unchanged, diluting unit costs; meanwhile, HBM4 12-layer yield ramped twice as fast as the prior-generation HBM3E, meaning early yield losses were smaller than expected. Combined, roughly 3–4 percentage points of the QoQ gross margin gain came from cost dilution, with the rest from price.

Q4 guidance is about 86%, only +1pct QoQ—the first guidance in this cycle in which gross margin barely improves. This is not a peak but a signal of approaching the top; once it declines QoQ in 2027, that will be the first domino of a cycle reversal.

A simple stress test: if ASPs return to 2025 levels in 2027 (about 45%–50% of current), quarterly revenue would fall to around USD 20 billion, gross margin would return to the 45%–55% range, and net margin would be around 30%. That is not a disaster but a return to normal—but for a share price priced on an 84.9% gross margin, it is a disaster.

Layer three, costs and profit: how the 68% net margin comes about

Operating expenses were fully diluted by revenue growth. A gross margin of 84.9% and an operating margin near 75% mean that sales, R&D and G&A combined account for only about 10% of revenue—in a normal year that ratio is 25%–30%. This is not better cost control; it is a bigger denominator.

Down to net income: GAAP net income of RMB 28.24 billion, a net margin of 68%; Non-GAAP of RMB 28.86 billion, EPS of USD 25.11. GAAP and Non-GAAP differ by only 2.2% (USD 620 million), the gap being stock-based compensation and restructuring charges. That gap is very clean for a tech company—at many semiconductor companies the gap runs 10%–20%.

What to watch is not the tax rate or expenses, but "the 68% net margin itself." The storage industry's historical peak net margin was about 35% in the 2018 cycle and about 25% in the 2022 cycle. 68% is an extreme with no historical reference point; it can only be explained as "supply gap + pricing power," not as "operating capability."

One more layer: net income contains no large non-operating gains. Interest income rose on USD 30.2 billion of cash, but it is a rounding error against RMB 28.2 billion of net income; there were no asset disposals, no investment gains, no tax rebates. This is a set of statements with clean profit sources and fragile sources.

The fourth layer: cash flow and balance sheet — RMB 18.3 billion of free cash flow, but at a discount

Operating cash flow RMB 25.39 billion (RMB 11.9 billion last quarter, RMB 4.61 billion a year earlier), less net capex of RMB 7.084 billion, gives adjusted free cash flow of RMB 18.304 billion — a record high for a single quarter. Cash and investments RMB 30.2 billion; liquidity RMB 32.2 billion.

One cross-statement tie-out needs explaining: net profit RMB 28.24 billion against operating cash flow RMB 25.39 billion, a ratio of 0.90x. The gap comes from working-capital absorption — revenue up 74% QoQ, with receivables and inventory expanding in step. That is normal in a high-growth phase, not a fraud signal. But neither does it warrant a "high-quality cash flow" verdict; the real test is the next down quarter: when revenue falls 40%, does inventory turn into impairments?

Another item easily misread: 16 SCA long-term contracts bring roughly USD 22 billion of customer cash deposits and financial commitments. On the balance sheet this sits as contract liabilities — not current revenue, and not free cash flow. What it means is that customers have locked in supply with real money, not that the company earned an extra RMB 22 billion.

Put this quarter in historical context: comparing three peaks

Put FY26 alongside the prior two peaks and an uncomfortable fact emerges: this cycle's margins are more than 1.5x the historical extreme. At the FY18 peak, gross margin was 58.9% and net margin 46%; at the FY22 peak, 45.2% and 28%; in FY26Q3, 84.9% and 68%. The higher the margin, the larger the absolute magnitude of the reversion.

The last cycle's outcome is well documented: FY19 revenue -23%, net profit -55%; in FY23 revenue halved from RMB 30.8 billion to RMB 15.5 billion with a net loss of RMB 5.8 billion, and the stock fell about 50% from its peak. This cycle starts from a higher base with more cash (net cash RMB 30.2 billion), so the probability that "the company doesn't survive" is very low, but the probability of "the stock halving" is not lower.

The one difference is the SCA long-term contract. Neither of the prior two peaks had contractual protection; this cycle has 20% of revenue locked up under a five-year contract. That is the only reason Micron may fall less this time than history suggests, and the only reason holders have to wait.

Three things to watch most closely
  • Whether gross margin has peaked: Q4 guidance of 86%, only +1pct QoQ. Any quarter of sequential decline in 2027 would be the first hard signal of this cycle topping out
  • SCA coverage: currently about 20% (RMB 100 billion minimum revenue ÷ five years ≈ RMB 20 billion per year, against FY26 revenue of RMB 129 billion). Whether it can reach 50% determines whether the valuation can re-rate from 6.5x to 10x
  • Capex cadence: RMB 27 billion in FY26, over RMB 40 billion in FY27. If all three expand capacity sharply at the same time in 2027, depreciation and supply will hit together in 2028

Conclusion

Conclusion: a very high-quality earnings report with very low sustainability. High on — clean disclosure (GAAP/Non-GAAP gap of 2.2%), real cash flow (OCF RMB 25.39 billion, FCF RMB 18.3 billion), price increases across all four segments and product categories, NAND volume and price both rising, enterprise SSD climbing to global No. 2. Low on — the entire USD 15 billion of growth came from price, with bit shipments barely moving; gross margin of 84.9% and net margin of 68% have no historical reference point; Q4 guidance shows gross margin is already near the top. What to watch next quarter is not "how much they earn" but "whether gross margin can still rise sequentially."
