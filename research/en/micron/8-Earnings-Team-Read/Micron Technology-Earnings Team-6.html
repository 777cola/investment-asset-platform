Core view

(Edited and polished for publication. Data as of the 2026/06/24 financial report and the 2026/09/25 close.)

USD 41.4 billion, the fifth record quarter

Micron FY26Q3 revenue was USD 41.456 billion, up 345.7% YoY and 73.8% QoQ, the fifth consecutive record quarter and USD 7.9 billion above the upper end of the company's own guidance. Non-GAAP gross margin was 84.9%, up 10 percentage points QoQ and the highest in company history; GAAP net income was USD 28.24 billion, a 68% net margin; single-quarter free cash flow was USD 18.3 billion, also a record.

Set this report against Nvidia, and the gross margin is a notch higher still. That is no exaggeration but an accurate picture of the 2026 memory shortage: AI data centers need HBM, HBM consumes the wafer capacity of ordinary DRAM, ordinary DRAM runs short in turn, and so prices rise across every category.

But look closely at how that RMB 41.4 billion came about

DRAM revenue was USD 31.3 billion, or 76% of the total; bit shipments rose only low single digits QoQ, while ASP climbed just over 60%. NAND revenue was USD 9.9 billion, with bits up mid single digits and ASP up about 85%. In other words, without the price increases, revenue this quarter would have been only about USD 25–26 billion. The extra USD 15 billion-plus came entirely from pricing.

This is not nitpicking; it is the dividing line for valuation. Earnings from shipment growth can command 15x; earnings from price increases can command only 5x. The market now values Micron at 6.5x 2027 expected earnings, which essentially says: I know most of this is luck.

The real change sits in the contracts, not the income statement

The most valuable number in this earnings report is not the RMB 28.2 billion net profit; it is the 16 SCA long-term contracts: five-year terms (three years for automotive), take-or-pay, fixed volumes, with price floors and ceilings. Of these, 14 priced at the floor imply roughly USD 100 billion of revenue, and customers have already paid about USD 22 billion in cash deposits and financial commitments.

This is the first time anyone in the memory industry has shifted from "prices set by the market" to "prices set by contract." If this path works, Micron's valuation center will rise from 5–8x to above 12x—because its revenue visibility extends from 3 months to 5 years. But coverage is currently only about 20% (USD 20 billion of minimum annual revenue against FY26 revenue of USD 129 billion), not yet enough to change its cyclical nature.

What to watch next

The FY26Q4 results on September 30 are the first test. Management guides revenue of USD 49–51 billion, gross margin of about 86% and EPS of USD 30–32; the consensus has already been revised up to USD 50.8–51.4 billion and EPS of USD 31.5–31.75 — meeting guidance is no longer a positive; only above USD 51.4 billion counts.

The second test is DRAM contract pricing in the first quarter of 2027. In 2026Q1 prices rose 90%–95% year on year; by Q4 the sequential gain is down to 10%–15%, so the pace is narrowing. Narrowing gains are not the problem; a sequential turn negative is — historically the share price has always peaked 3–6 months before contract prices turn negative.

The third is capital expenditure. FY26 is USD 27 billion and FY27 guidance exceeds USD 40 billion, with all three expanding at once. Supply lands in a concentrated wave in 2028, and 2028 depreciation corresponds precisely to the orders being placed today.

Conclusion: good company, bad setup

At USD 1,082, the stock trades at 6.5x 2027E earnings, one of the cheapest semiconductor names in the S&P 500. But that multiple is not the market being wrong; it is the market knowing the denominator is at a peak. On a normalized gross margin of 45%, the same price equates to 24-28x.

Existing holders should keep holding and not add; those with no position should wait for below USD 800, or for SCA coverage to clearly exceed half.Three scenarios: bear case USD 400 (-63%), base case USD 1,120 (+3%), bull case USD 1,620 (+50%), probability-weighted USD 1,065 — zero expected return, with 63% downside. This is not a trade worth chasing.

Conclusion

In one line: Micron delivered the best earnings report in memory-industry history, but what it proves is "how severe the shortage is," not "how strong the company is." Long-term contracts are real progress, but coverage is still insufficient; the price really is cheap, but the denominator is fake. Holding is fine; wait for a pullback to add.
