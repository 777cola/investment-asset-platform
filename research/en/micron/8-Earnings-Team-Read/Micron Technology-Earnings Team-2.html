Reading financial reports, level two: return on capital and reinvestment needs

Core view

Buffett would divide the USD 18.3 billion of single-quarter free cash flow by invested capital to derive a return on invested capital. Micron's equation: cash generated in FY26 roughly equals cash to be spent in FY27. In his taxonomy, this is a "very profitable in good years, very expensive in bad years" business, and valuation must rest on normalized earnings, not current-year earnings.

The quality of free cash flow

Operating cash flow of RMB 25.39 billion, net capex of RMB 7.084 billion and free cash flow of RMB 18.304 billion look excellent. But capex is only 17% of revenue, versus 35%–40% in a normal year for Micron—this quarter's "low capex" is an illusion: equipment orders have been placed, but the cash has not yet gone out. Q4 capex is about RMB 10 billion, and FY27 full-year capex exceeds RMB 40 billion.

In other words, roughly RMB 3–4 billion of the RMB 18.3 billion in free cash flow is "deferred capex" flattering the number. True sustainable free cash flow must be recalculated as "revenue × normal capex rate."

Return on Invested Capital: Cycle-Peak Numbers Can Be Deceptive

On FY26E net income of about USD 83 billion and estimated shareholder equity of USD 90–100 billion, ROE is absurdly high (above 80%). The number is meaningless—it divides peak profit by equity accumulated through the cycle. Buffett would take the ten-year average: about 9%, below his 15% hurdle.

The metric that actually works is cumulative free cash flow through the cycle. Micron's cumulative free cash flow over the ten years FY17–FY26 is close to zero; everything it earned turned into fabs. That is not management squandering money—it is the nature of the industry—but the conclusion is the same: it does not have the characteristics of "a business that can grow without further reinvestment."

Accounting quality: A- is warranted

GAAP and non-GAAP net income differ by only 2.2%, no one-off gains were funnelled into adjusted profit, there was no large-scale inventory write-down reversal, and receivables moved in line with revenue—no gimmicks in this earnings report. The half-point deduction is for the "customer deposits" disclosure: the USD 22 billion figure covers "cash deposits and related financial commitments," bundling two different things together, which strictly speaking is not transparent enough.

What would satisfy Buffett is the balance sheet: net cash entering the top of the cycle, USD 30.2 billion in cash and investments—even if prices collapse in 2027, no equity issuance will be needed. That is the "ability to survive" he values most.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: the cash flow is real, its sustainability is not. The RMB 18.3 billion of free cash flow rests on a 17% capex ratio and would fall by 50-70% once normalised; ROE of about 9% over ten years falls short of first-tier. Holding is fine, but do not price it at "free cash flow × 20x" — use "normalised earnings × 10-15x".
