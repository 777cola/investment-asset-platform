Core view

Buffett looks at three things first in a financial report: whether the earnings are cash, whether continuous investment is required, and whether there are accounting tricks. Micron's answers to these three questions this quarter are: yes, it is cash (operating cash flow USD 25.39 billion), massive investment is required (FY27 capex above USD 40 billion), and there are no tricks (GAAP and Non-GAAP differ by only 2.2%). The problem lies in the fourth thing: whether this cash will still be there next year.

Income statement: every dollar is price

Revenue USD 41.456 billion, +345.7% YoY, +73.8% QoQ; gross margin 84.9% (+45.9pct YoY, +10pct QoQ); GAAP net income USD 28.24 billion, net margin 68%; Non-GAAP net income USD 28.86 billion, EPS USD 25.11. The key to these numbers is not the absolute level but the breakdown: DRAM bit shipments rose only low single digits QoQ with ASP up just over 60%, while NAND bits rose mid single digits with ASP up about 85%.

In other words, holding prices flat and relying on volume alone, revenue this quarter would have been roughly USD 26 billion, not USD 41.4 billion. The extra USD 15 billion is entirely price. Buffett would draw a line here: money earned from price increases disappears once prices revert; only money earned from volume has a chance of staying.

The cost side is clean: R&D and SG&A were diluted by revenue growth, and operating margin approached 75%. No one-off gains, no asset disposals, no capitalizing R&D. Earnings quality this quarter is high, but the "quality of the source" of those earnings is low.

Cash flow: operating cash flow of RMB 25.39 billion, but not free cash

Operating cash flow was USD 25.39 billion, less net capex of USD 7.084 billion, giving adjusted free cash flow of USD 18.304 billion — the highest for any single quarter in history, several times the full-year FY24 free cash flow. The number is real, and the cash is on the books: cash and investments of USD 30.2 billion, liquidity of USD 32.2 billion.

But it is not "free cash you can take out." FY26 full-year capex is USD 27 billion, FY27 guidance exceeds USD 40 billion, and Q4 alone will consume about USD 10 billion. At the FY27 pace of USD 40 billion, even if operating cash flow holds at USD 25 billion a quarter, full-year free cash flow is about USD 60 billion — which looks fine, provided revenue does not fall. If revenue returns to USD 120 billion and gross margin to 45%, operating cash flow drops below USD 30 billion, while the USD 40 billion of capex is already backed by signed equipment orders and cannot be cut.

This is the cash flow trap of the memory business: capex is "last cycle's decision," cash flow is "this cycle's price," and the two are mismatched by two years. The 2028 depreciation peak corresponds precisely to equipment orders placed in 2026–2027.

Balance sheet and accounting quality: no gimmicks this time

Entering the cycle top in a net cash position is the most important financial fact of this round. In the 2018 cycle, Micron went into the downturn carrying more than USD 10 billion of debt and in 2022–2023 was forced to cut 10% of its workforce, slash capex and post a FY23 net loss of USD 5.8 billion. This time it holds USD 30.2 billion of cash and USD 32.2 billion of liquidity, so even if prices collapse in 2027, it will not need to sell assets or issue equity.

Accounting quality passes: GAAP net income of USD 28.24 billion vs Non-GAAP USD 28.86 billion, a 2.2% gap, with the difference consisting only of stock-based compensation and restructuring charges—no one-off gains were pushed into Non-GAAP; there were no large inventory write-down reversals; receivables growth matches revenue growth (revenue +74% QoQ, and receivables expanding in step is normal).

The one item to flag is inventory. The leading indicator at every memory-industry peak is "inventory days turning up + contract price increases narrowing." In Q3 the absolute inventory value will expand with revenue, but the company did not disclose days; if inventory days rise while price increases narrow next quarter, that will be the first warning at the financial-statement level.

Conclusion

Financial verdict: this quarter's numbers are real; next quarter's numbers are the problem. Operating cash flow of RMB 25.39 billion, free cash flow of RMB 18.3 billion and cash of RMB 30.2 billion show no sign of window dressing; but at the same time, FY27 capex above RMB 40 billion has already locked in 2028 depreciation. The Buffett-style conclusion: this is a company with "good cash flow but fragile free cash flow" — it prints money in good years and pays for decisions made two years earlier in bad years. Holding is fine, but it cannot be priced with a formula like "RMB 18.3 billion free cash flow × 20x".
