Core view

Li Lu's risk framework does not ask "how likely is it" but "if it happens, can I still survive". Micron's balance sheet can survive (net cash USD 30.2 billion), but the stock price may not—because the stock price is priced on peak earnings, and peak earnings rest on five supports, each of which will be tested within the next 24 months.

The first risk: price reversal, the only one that truly matters

Every risk in memory stocks ultimately converges on a single variable: DRAM contract prices. In Q1 2026 they rose 90%–95% year on year, kept climbing sharply quarter on quarter in Q2, and are up another 10%–15% quarter on quarter in Q4 — the magnitude of the increase is narrowing. A narrowing increase is not the problem; turning negative is.

Reversal signals appear in advance, roughly in this order: contract price growth narrows → spot prices fall first → inventory days rebound → customers delay pickups → contract prices turn negative. This sequence played out once in 2018 and once in 2022, with roughly 3–6 months from the first signal to the share-price peak each time.

We are now in the early stage of "narrowing increases": Q4 commodity DRAM contract prices are expected to rise 10%–15% quarter on quarter, versus a year-on-year gain of more than 90% in Q1. If any quarter in 2027 posts zero quarter-on-quarter growth, that is the cue to cut exposure — no need to wait for the earnings report.

Second risk: the illusion of long-term contracts

The SCA long-term contract is the most overrated thing in this cycle. USD 100 billion, five years, take-or-pay, price floor—it sounds like the cycle has been locked in. But do the math: USD 100 billion over five years = USD 20 billion a year, against FY26 revenue of about USD 129 billion, a coverage ratio of 15%–20%. The remaining 80% of revenue is still exposed to spot and quarterly contracts.

More critical is the enforceability of "take-or-pay." In a downturn, customers do not default outright; they soften terms in three ways: change procurement specifications (buy cheaper models), request deferred delivery, renegotiate the price range. A price ceiling in the contract means customers can demand execution at the ceiling when prices rise; conversely, when prices fall, customers will demand renegotiation at the new spot price. The real function of a long-term contract is to "shave the peaks," not "fill the troughs."

Li Lu's judgment would be: this is the right direction, but treating it as evidence that "the cycle has disappeared" mistakes direction for outcome. Only after it has been executed through a downturn can it command a valuation premium.

The third to fifth risks: share, capital, sentiment

Share risk is materializing. Counterpoint 2026Q2: SK hynix 50% (58% prior quarter), Samsung 33% (21% prior quarter), Micron 18% (21% prior quarter). Samsung grabbed 12 percentage points in a single quarter, powered by the world's first mass production of HBM4 in February plus Nvidia certification. In June 2026 Jensen Huang confirmed all three had passed HBM4 certification—the exclusive advantage is over, and the fight now is yield and capacity.

Capital risk is a timing mismatch. FY27 capex above USD 40 billion maps to 2028 depreciation; if prices decline in 2028, that is a double hit of "rising depreciation plus falling prices." The 2022–2023 script: FY23 revenue halved and net loss of USD 5.8 billion, just as the prior year's capex had set a record.

Sentiment risk is the most immediate near-term issue. On September 22, Michael Burry publicly said he had substantially increased short positions in Micron, SOXX and Nebius; the same day Citi raised its target price to 1,300 and Wells Fargo cut its to 1,400, while BofA reiterated 1,550 on September 25 and UBS reiterated 1,625. Target prices spanning 1,300 to 2,000 dollars are themselves a signal of deep disagreement. The stock is up 226% year to date, and any broad drawdown in the AI trade will amplify its decline.

Conclusion

Risk assessment: the company will survive, but the stock can halve. Net cash of RMB 30.2 billion and liquidity of RMB 32.2 billion let it ride out any downturn; but the share price rests on peak profit, and of the five supports under peak profit, two — price and share — are already deteriorating (price gains narrowing, HBM share -3pct). The Li Lu-style conclusion: this is not a question of "can it be held for the long term" but of "are you willing to pay a cyclical-top price for a three-year business." Holding is fine, but a de-risking trigger must be set: contract prices turning negative month on month, or gross margin guidance falling below 80%.
