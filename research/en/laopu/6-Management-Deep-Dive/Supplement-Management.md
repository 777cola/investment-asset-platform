## Key figure: sold fish, ran tourism, and ended up running a gold shop

Xu Gaoming's résumé contains not a single day of jewelry experience. He graduated in 1984 with a degree in freshwater fisheries, joined the Yueyang Municipal Bureau of Animal Husbandry and Aquaculture as a clerk, and eight years later became general manager of its subsidiary Aquaculture Building. In 1995 he left the system and took over Yueyang Hongqiao Tourism Investment, staying until 2017.

In 2009 he opened the first Laopu Gold store in Beijing's Wangfujing Gongmei Building. The location says it all—the Gongmei Building is a long-established arts-and-crafts mall in Wangfujing, whose clientele comes to buy "things with culture," not gold bars. From day one he had no intention of running a gold business; he intended to run a culture business, with gold merely as the vehicle.

Everything that followed can be explained from this starting point: insistence on direct-operated stores (to control the in-store experience), per-piece pricing (to break free of gold-price anchoring), 2–3 price increases a year (to reinforce symbolic value), entry only into top-tier malls (to use location to screen the clientele), and holding the R&D director title himself (the product reflects his own aesthetic). This playbook has not changed in seventeen years; this is not a company whose strategy swings.

The weaknesses are equally obvious: one man holds three roles, and the company has no second person who can speak publicly. Key-person risk is structural.

## Equity: 58.10% family control, with clear trade-offs on both sides

Xu Gaoming directly holds 18.1%, controls 31.7% through Hongqiao Jinji (70% stake), and his son Xu Dongbo holds 8.1%, giving the three parties a combined 58.10%. This is a family-controlled structure, common among Hong Kong-listed consumer stocks.

The good side: fast decision-making, no pandering to short-term results, and the willingness to proactively close and optimize 10–12 stores in 2026 with gold prices at highs. At its 2024 listing it issued very few shares (net proceeds HKD 713 million); the major shareholder is unwilling to dilute, which is good for minority shareholders.

The bad side: a small free float amplifies share-price swings. From a historical high of HKD 1,065 in July 2025 to HKD 337 in October 2026, a 68% drop in fifteen months. On identical fundamentals, the valuation can swing between 140x and 7x — that is not a company problem, it is a shareholding-structure problem. Minority shareholders have no say in this structure; they can only choose to buy or not to buy.

On 19 May 2026, Xu Dongbo increased his holding by 33.57 million H shares, lifting the three parties' combined stake to 58.10%. Buying rather than selling after the lock-up expired is a positive signal, albeit a small one (roughly HKD 100 million at the then-prevailing share price).

## Capital allocation: no financing, no M&A, no dilution — all cash goes to dividends

List the capital actions of the past three years and a profile emerges that is deeply "anti-growth stock."

Fundraising: Listed in Hong Kong on June 28, 2024 at an offer price of HKD 40.5, raising net proceeds of HKD 713 million on 582x subscription. For a company with RMB 8.5 billion in revenue that year, HKD 700 million amounts to raising nothing. No further fundraising since listing.

Expansion: 7 new stores in 2024, 10 new stores and 9 optimized/expanded stores in 2025, and a plan to optimize 10–12 stores in 2026. Total store count of 45, up only about a dozen over three years. Capex went mainly to store fit-outs and workshop equipment—no self-built factories, no mine acquisitions, no brand M&A.

Returns: Interim 2026 dividend of RMB 18.02 per share, totaling RMB 3.185 billion, a payout ratio of 74.6%, exceeding operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion for the period. Cumulative dividends in the two years since listing are nearly five times the IPO proceeds.

The implication of this combination: management believes the business does not need much incremental capital, and cash flow from the existing 45 stores is enough to fund both expansion and dividends. If the judgment is right, this is excellent capital allocation; if wrong—say, overseas expansion requires heavy investment—the ammunition will have been spent too early.

## Integrity and governance: three key decisions all showed restraint

The first attempt: its 2017 A-share IPO failed, the company withdrew its filing and switched to Hong Kong, a gap of seven years. Seven years without listing, without external financing, and carrying inventory expansion on its own balance sheet demanded cash-flow discipline.

The second: its 2024 Hong Kong listing issued very few shares and did not use a high valuation to raise large amounts. Many new consumer companies ran private placements at peak prices and issued convertible bonds over the same period; Laopu did not.

The third: in July 2025, one year after listing, the lock-up expired, with the share price falling from its HKD 1,065 peak. Major shareholders have no record of reduction; instead, in May 2026 Xu Dongbo increased his stake by 33.57 million shares.The lock-up expiry is the moment that tests the character of major shareholders, and this one passed.

Points to deduct: family seats dominate the board, and the checks and balances of independent directors have not been publicly tested; related parties include Yueyang Hongqiao Tourism Investment and other entities controlled by Xu Gaoming, which do not directly compete with the listed company's business, but the boundaries of related-party transactions require continued monitoring. No major litigation, penalties or financial fraud allegations appear in public channels.

## Succession: the biggest governance gap

Xu Gaoming was born in 1964 and turns 62 in 2026. Xu Dongbo is already in place at the shareholder level—signing a joint-control confirmation with his father in November 2023 and increasing his stake in May 2026—but the company has not disclosed his specific management role or a succession timetable.

This matters more at Laopu than at other companies because the founder is also the product definer. The aesthetics, forms and craft standards of Gufa gold largely reflect Xu Gaoming's personal judgment. Whether a different R&D director would change the product style, and whether customers would accept it, is unknown.

We give management 7 points (the fourth of six gates), and 2 of the 3 points deducted come here: not a lack of confidence in ability, but the absence of a structural backup. Investors can do only one thing—treat "Xu Gaoming's departure" as a trigger event requiring re-evaluation and write it into the thesis tracking.
