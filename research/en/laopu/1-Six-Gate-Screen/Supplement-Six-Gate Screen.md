## Stage one and two: easy to understand, but "easy to understand" and "easy to profit from" are two different things

I score the circle-of-competence test 7, not 9. The business boils down to three sentences: open stores in top-tier malls, sell piece-priced traditional-method gold jewellery, and earn fabrication fees and brand premium. No technology roadmap, no major customers, no policy subsidies. Only one variable is complex — the gold price. Spot gold hit USD 5,419/oz in January 2026 before falling below USD 4,000 by June, and domestic pure-gold retail prices were cut by more than 25% in tandem. When a company's core cost swings 25% within six months, that sits outside the circle of competence.

The second test also scores 7, and the numbers are hard: gross margin 41.3%, up 3.2pct YoY; net margin 21.5%, up 3.1pct. For comparison, Chow Tai Fook posted a gross margin of 32.3% and a net margin of 9.5% in FY2026, while Lao Feng Xiang generated revenue of RMB 52.8 billion and net profit of only RMB 1.755 billion in 2025, a net margin of 3.3%. Both sell gold, yet Laopu keeps RMB 21.5 of net profit per RMB 100 of revenue, while Lao Feng Xiang keeps RMB 3.3. That gap comes from brand and pricing method (per piece rather than per gram), and it is real.

The 3 points deducted are for the asset structure. Inventory of RMB 19.018 billion and 271 days of turnover mean every RMB 1 of profit ties up RMB 4.5 of inventory. When gold rises, inventory amplifies profit; when it falls, inventory becomes a source of impairment — the same balance sheet magnifies in both directions. That is not the standard shape of a "good business" — a good business should make money without betting on direction.

## Gate three: the brand is real; the moat is location, not product

This is the easiest test to misjudge. The criterion is not whether Laopu invented gufa gold, but whether rivals can make the same thing within two years. The answer has two layers.

Product layer: yes. Chasing, filigree and enamelling carry no patents; Shenzhen Shuibei's copycat cycle runs in weeks, and Chow Tai Fook's "Heritage," Lao Feng Xiang's gufa line and Chow Tai Seng's filigree products all sit in the same aesthetic range. In late February 2026 Laopu raised prices 20%–30% across all channels, with some products topping RMB 2,500 per gram. Peers did not follow — not because they cannot, but because they dare not: their customer base will not pay that premium.

Channel layer: no. All 45 stores sit in top-tier commercial centres such as SKP, MixC, Plaza 66 and Taikoo, spanning 16 cities and 35 centres. Ground-floor space in top malls is limited supply; a mall reserves room for only one "China's No. 1 gufa gold brand," and whoever takes it first is not easily displaced. Laopu's moat is therefore channel scarcity plus customer perception: products can be copied, locations cannot. Score 6: adequate, but not enough to support a luxury valuation — the luxury barrier is "if you don't buy this, you can't buy an equivalent status symbol," and Laopu has not reached that point.

## Stage four: no franchising and a 75% payout say more about management than growth does

Score 7. Three things matter.

First, commitment to direct-operated stores. All 45 stores are self-operated, with no franchising and no wholesale — an outlier in China's gold and jewellery industry, where Chow Tai Fook runs 5,305 mainland stores and Lao Feng Xiang thousands of outlets, using franchising to scale at the cost of control over pricing and brand positioning. Laopu forwent the fastest route to expansion: its 2024 IPO raised net proceeds of only HKD 713 million, none of which went into rolling out stores.

Second, a 75% payout. The 2026 interim dividend is RMB 18.02 per share, totalling RMB 3.185 billion — about 75% of net profit and above operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion for the period. A payout at this level is rare for a growth-stage company and reads as two signals: expansion does not need that much capital, and management would rather hand cash to shareholders than leave it on the balance sheet as inventory. Both are positive.

Third, proactive store closures. The 2026 plan calls for optimising 10–12 stores, with the count unchanged at 45 in the first half. Deliberately shrinking rather than expanding into strength at high gold prices shows management knows per-store quality matters more than store count.

3 points deducted for governance: Xu Gaoming and Xu Dongbo, father and son, together with Hongqiao Jinji hold a combined 58.10%, leaving a small free float that amplifies share-price swings — the stock fell from HKD 1,065 to HKD 337 in just fifteen months, and there is no counterbalancing force in governance to smooth such volatility.

## Level five: the 6.9x P/E denominator was stacked up in Q1

This is the crux of the report. A market cap of HKD 59.6 billion against TTM net profit of RMB 7.93 billion on the working-paper basis gives 6.9x. At first glance, 6.9x for a company still growing revenue at +60% looks absurdly cheap.

Open the interim report and it no longer is. H1 2026 net profit was RMB 4.267 billion, of which Q2 contributed only RMB 510–760 million, down more than 80% QoQ and 34%–56% YoY, while Q1 alone contributed over RMB 3.5 billion. The reason is simple: a 20%–30% across-the-board price increase in late February, layered on a buying rush ahead of the January peak in gold prices, pulled high-end customers' full-year purchases forward into the period around Chinese New Year; in Q2 gold prices reversed lower and domestic pure-gold retail prices fell more than 25%, flipping the "buy on the way up, not on the way down" consumer mentality outright.

Part of the denominator behind 6.9x is therefore non-recurring. Annualise Q2 (roughly RMB 2.0–3.0 billion) as the norm and P/E is 20–30x; use the RMB 4.5–5.7 billion likely in 2027 as the denominator and P/E is 9.5–12x. The working paper's "9–10x on sustainable profit" holds up.

## Gold price is not a company variable — gate six, one correction

Gate six scores 5 points, and the deduction is straightforward: the buy case rests on "the gold price does not fall," which is neither within the company's control nor verifiable. Discipline requires that "the buy rationale must be testable by data," and the direction of the gold price cannot be tested — it is only known after the fact. Fail this gate, and no amount of prior scoring justifies a heavy position.

One technical correction to the working paper here, without changing the conclusion. The working paper reads the 41.3% gross margin as "a peak inflated by the timing gap from low-cost inventory," but over a longer horizon: 41.2% in 2021, 41.9% in 2022, 41.9% in 2023, 41.2% in 2024, 37.6% in 2025, and 41.3% in 2026H1. The 41%–42% range is the norm; the 37.6% in 2025 is the anomaly. The low-cost inventory benefit therefore mainly restores the gross margin that the gold price squeezed in 2025 — it does not create a new high.

The real peak is not in gross margin but in revenue: Q1 tax-inclusive sales came to about RMB 19 billion, twice the full year of 2024 in a single quarter. That demand was pulled forward, not incremental. So the call that "profit has peaked" holds, but the mechanism must be stated correctly — it is revenue pull-forward.
