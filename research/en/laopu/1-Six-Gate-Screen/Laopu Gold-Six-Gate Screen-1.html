Six-Gate Screen — Laopu Gold

Laopu Gold (06181) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/10/09  ·  Price HK$337.00 (+4.85%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 59.6 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 6.9x
← Back to the hub

Core view: good brand, good store economics, but the cheapness is only on paper

The six-gate score is 38 out of 60, a fail; the rating carries over from the working paper as "Watch, wait for gold prices to stabilize." The first four gates are largely uncontested: 45 directly operated stores, 16 cities, 35 top-tier commercial centers, tax-inclusive sales of RMB 22.779 billion in the first half of 2026, up 60.6% year on year, and annualized sales per store of about RMB 1.01 billion — store productivity in the global jewelry industry's top tier. Gate three earns only 6 points because the brand premium is real but the product moat is not — ancient-method gold carries no patent, Shuibei can ship in a week, and what is truly hard to replicate is the location in top-tier malls.

The last two gates are where the entire problem lies. The denominator of the 6.9x PE is the RMB 4.267 billion net profit of the first half of 2026, and of that RMB 4.267 billion, Q1 contributed more than RMB 3.5 billion: second-quarter revenue was only RMB 2.3–3.95 billion and net profit RMB 510–760 million, down 80% quarter on quarter. Gold peaked at USD 5,419 in January and broke below USD 4,000 in June; the company itself did not expect the second quarter to be this steep. The low PE of the current period is not a margin of safety; it is the reflection of peak profit.

Stage one and two: easy to understand, but "easy to understand" and "easy to profit from" are two different things

I give the circle-of-competence test 7 points, not 9. The business can be summed up in three sentences: open stores in top-tier malls, sell piece-priced traditional-method gold jewelry, and earn fabrication fees and brand premium. No technology roadmap, no major customers, no policy subsidies. Only one variable is complex — the gold price. In January 2026 spot gold hit USD 5,419/oz; by June it fell below USD 4,000, and domestic pure-gold retail prices were cut by more than 25% in tandem. When a company's core cost swings 25% within six months, that is not inside the circle of competence.

The second test gets 7 points, and the numbers are hard: gross margin 41.3%, up 3.2pct year on year; net margin 21.5%, up 3.1pct. For comparison, Chow Tai Fook posted a gross margin of 32.3% and a net margin of 9.5% in FY2026, while Lao Feng Xiang generated revenue of RMB 52.8 billion and net profit of only RMB 1.755 billion in 2025, a net margin of 3.3%. Both sell gold, yet Laopu keeps RMB 21.5 of net profit per RMB 100 of revenue, while Lao Feng Xiang keeps RMB 3.3. That gap comes from brand and pricing method (per piece rather than per gram), and it is real.

The 3 points deducted are for the asset structure. Inventory of RMB 19.018 billion and 271 days of turnover mean every RMB 1 of profit ties up RMB 4.5 of inventory. When gold rises, inventory amplifies profit; when it falls, inventory becomes a source of impairment — the same balance sheet magnifies in both directions. This is not the standard shape of a "good business" — a good business should make money without betting on direction.

Gate three: the brand is real; the moat is location, not product

This is the easiest test to misjudge. The criterion is not "did Laopu invent gufa gold," but "can rivals make the same thing within two years." The answer has two layers.

Product layer: yes. Chasing, filigree and enamelling carry no patents; Shenzhen Shuibei's copycat cycle runs in weeks, and Chow Tai Fook's "Heritage," Lao Feng Xiang's gufa line and Chow Tai Seng's filigree products all sit in the same aesthetic range. In late February 2026 Laopu raised prices 20%–30% across all channels, with some products topping RMB 2,500 per gram. Peers did not follow — not because they cannot, but because they dare not: their customer base will not pay that premium.

Channel layer: no. All 45 stores sit in top-tier commercial centres such as SKP, MixC, Plaza 66 and Taikoo, spanning 16 cities and 35 centres. Ground-floor space in top malls is limited supply; a mall reserves room for only one "China's No. 1 gufa gold brand," and whoever takes it first is not easily displaced. Laopu's moat is therefore channel scarcity plus customer perception: products can be copied, locations cannot. Score 6: adequate, but not enough to support a luxury valuation — the luxury barrier is "if you don't buy this, you can't buy an equivalent status symbol," and Laopu has not reached that point.

Stage four: no franchising, 75% payout — these two things say more about management than growth does

Score 7. Three things are enough to look at.

First, commitment to direct-operated stores. All 45 stores are self-operated, with no franchising and no wholesale. This makes it an outlier in China's gold and jewelry industry — Chow Tai Fook has 5,305 stores in the mainland and Lao Feng Xiang has thousands of outlets, using franchising to scale up at the cost of losing control over pricing and brand positioning. Laopu gave up this fastest route to expansion; its 2024 IPO raised net proceeds of only HKD 713 million, and it did not use the money to roll out stores.

Second, a 75% payout. The 2026 interim dividend is RMB 18.02 per share, totaling RMB 3.185 billion, about 75% of net profit and higher than operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion for the period. Paying out at this level is rare for a growth-stage company and can be read as two signals: expansion does not need that much capital, and management is willing to hand over cash rather than leave it on the balance sheet as inventory. Both signals are positive.

Third, proactive store closures. The 2026 plan calls for optimizing 10–12 stores, with the store count unchanged at 45 in the first half. Shrinking deliberately rather than expanding into strength at high gold prices shows management knows per-store quality matters more than store count.

3 points deducted for governance: Xu Gaoming and Xu Dongbo, father and son, together with Hongqiao Jinji hold a combined 58.10%, leaving a small free float that amplifies share-price swings — the stock fell from HKD 1,065 to HKD 337 in just fifteen months, and there is no counterbalancing force in governance to smooth such volatility.

Level five: the denominator of the 6.9x PE was piled up in Q1

This is the core of the report. A market cap of HKD 59.6 billion against TTM net profit of RMB 7.93 billion on the working-paper basis gives 6.9x. At first glance, 6.9x for a company still growing revenue at +60% looks absurdly cheap.

Break open the interim report and it is no longer cheap. H1 2026 net profit was RMB 4.267 billion, of which Q2 contributed only RMB 510–760 million, down more than 80% QoQ and 34%–56% YoY, while Q1 alone contributed over RMB 3.5 billion. The reason is simple: a 20%–30% across-the-board price increase in late February, layered on a buying rush ahead of the January peak in gold prices, pulled high-end customers' full-year purchases forward into the period around Chinese New Year; in Q2 gold prices reversed lower and domestic pure-gold retail prices fell more than 25%, so the "buy on the way up, not on the way down" consumer mentality flipped outright.

So part of the denominator behind 6.9x is non-recurring. Annualise Q2 (roughly RMB 2.0–3.0 billion) as the norm and PE is 20–30x; use the RMB 4.5–5.7 billion likely in 2027 as the denominator and PE is 9.5–12x. The working paper's "9–10x on sustainable profit" holds up.

Gate six and one correction: the gold price is not a company variable

Gate six scores 5 points, and the deduction is straightforward: the buy case rests on "the gold price does not fall," which is neither within the company's control nor verifiable. Discipline requires that "the buy rationale must be testable by data," and the direction of the gold price cannot be tested — it is only known after the fact. Fail this gate, and no amount of prior scoring justifies a heavy position.

One technical correction to the working paper here, without changing the conclusion. The working paper reads the 41.3% gross margin as "a peak inflated by the timing gap from low-cost inventory," but over a longer horizon: 41.2% in 2021, 41.9% in 2022, 41.9% in 2023, 41.2% in 2024, 37.6% in 2025, and 41.3% in 2026H1. The 41%–42% range is the norm; the 37.6% in 2025 is the anomaly. So the low-cost inventory benefit mainly restores the gross margin that the gold price squeezed in 2025 — it does not create a new high.

The real peak is not in gross margin but in revenue: Q1 tax-inclusive sales came to about RMB 19 billion, twice the full year of 2024 in a single quarter. That demand was pulled forward, not incremental. So the call that "profit has peaked" holds, but the mechanism must be stated correctly — it is revenue pull-forward.

Conclusion

Fails the six-gate score of 38, Watch, wait for the gold price to stabilise. Only one veto item: earnings quality unproven — Q2 already showed that same-store sales stall when the gold price falls, and management has no hedge against this. Three items to verify: ① whether same-store sales can return to positive growth after the gold price trades sideways at USD 4,000–4,300 for a quarter; ② whether inventory turnover days can fall from 271 days back below 220 days; ③ whether overseas stores can grow from 1 in Singapore to more than 3. If all three pass, the score can be recalculated above 44, and that is the buy point.
