Core view

Buffett reads bottom-up: first ask "what are this company's assets," then ask "how much profit can those assets generate." Laopu's assets are RMB 19 billion of gold; its profit is RMB 4.267 billion. Asset quality determines profit quality, not the reverse.

Income statement: 41.3% gross margin is the norm; 21.5% net margin marks the peak

Gross margin needs a longer view: 41.2%–41.9% in 2021–2024, 37.6% in 2025, 41.3% in 2026H1. 41% is the norm; the 21.5% net margin is the all-time high.

Net margin expansion comes from expense dilution: revenue +60.3%, period expense ratio down from 18.2% (2024) to about 15% (rough calculation). That is no bad thing — it shows operating leverage at work. But leverage cuts both ways: Q2 revenue fell 80%, the expense ratio rebounded immediately, and net profit fell 34%–56% YoY.

Buffett would draw a line here: only a margin that can be extrapolated deserves a valuation multiple. Laopu's net margin cannot be extrapolated, because its denominator (revenue) is pulse-like. That dictates the market is willing to give it only a single-digit to low-teens P/E, not the 25–30x of a consumer growth stock.

A jewellery company's balance sheet is essentially a long position on gold

Inventory of RMB 19.02 billion, 271 days of turnover. Put another way: this company effectively runs a RMB 19 billion long position in gold, monetised through a retail business. Stores, brand and design are simply the distribution channels for that position.

From this angle, much becomes clear. Why did gross margin fall to 37.6% in 2025? Because the gold price rose faster than selling prices, and accounting rules consumed the mark-to-market gains on the long position. Why did it recover to 41.3% in 2026H1? Because long positions built at the end of 2025 were sold after prices rose. Why did Q2 collapse? Because the gold price fell, end customers would not take delivery, and the position could not be converted into cash.

Buffett would say: if a company's profit direction is set by a price it cannot control, its stock is a commodity substitute, not a consumer stock. Commodity companies are valued on cycle position and replacement cost, not on P/E.

Margin of safety: 4% is not a margin of safety

Base case: 2027 net profit of RMB 5.7 billion at 10x P/E gives HKD 352, implying 4% upside from the current HKD 337. Bear case: RMB 4.5 billion at 8x gives HKD 222, or 34% downside.

Buffett's position-sizing rule: downside must be backstopped by "asset value", not by "growth expectations". So what is Laopu's asset value? RMB 19 billion of gold at market, plus the locations of 45 stores, plus the brand. Capitalising rent puts the locations at roughly RMB 50–80 billion; the brand cannot be priced separately. That totals about RMB 25–28 billion, or HKD 27.3–30.6 billion, equivalent to HKD 154–173 per share.

The implication: at HKD 337, the stock trades more than 100% above asset value. Buyers today are therefore not buying assets but three expectations: "gold price stabilises + same-store recovery + overseas expansion succeeds". That is not what Buffett buys.

Conclusion

Buffett-style verdict: a good company, a bad cycle, and a cyclical stock that is not cheap. He would only engage near asset value (HKD 154–173), or buy it as a growth stock once the industry consolidates down to this one player. At HKD 337, the stock sits in the "understandable but won't act" bucket.
