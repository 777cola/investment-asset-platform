Net profit up 88%, stock down 60%: the market does the math better than the company — Laopu Gold

Core view

Laopu Gold 2026 interim results: revenue RMB 19.81 billion, up 60.3% year on year; net profit RMB 4.27 billion, up 88.2%; net margin 21.5%, a record high; operating cash flow turned positive for the first time in three years. Over the same period, the share price fell from HKD 1,065 to HKD 337, a 68% drawdown. Either the financials or the share price must be wrong.

I. First, a quarter that has been overlooked

The interim report gives only half-year figures, but broken down by quarter the story changes completely. In Q2 2026, Laopu generated revenue of RMB 2.30–3.95 billion and net profit of RMB 510–760 million, down more than 80% QoQ and 34%–56% YoY. This is its first single-quarter profit decline YoY since listing.

The reason is not complicated: at the end of February it raised prices 20%–30% across all channels, with per-gram prices for some products exceeding RMB 2,500/g. Once the price-hike announcement landed, existing customers rushed to buy before it took effect, and together with the buying spree ahead of gold's January peak, demand was compressed into Q1—tax-inclusive sales of about RMB 19 billion in Q1, one quarter doing twice the full-year 2024 total. In Q2 gold prices reversed and domestic pure-gold retail prices fell more than 25%, so these buyers naturally stopped buying.

So the "+60.3% for the first half" figure is the average of Q1's +200% and Q2's -40%. This is not growth, it is a pulse.

II. The 41.3% gross margin is widely misread

The market treats the 41.3% gross margin as a "record high." It is not. Look at the history: 41.2% in 2021, 41.9% in 2022, 41.9% in 2023, 41.2% in 2024, 37.6% in 2025. 41% is this company's norm; the 37.6% in 2025 was the anomaly.

Why was 2025 anomalous? Gold prices rose fast, and the company's two price hikes (in August and October, +10%–15% and +20%–30%) both failed to keep pace with costs, squeezing the gross margin by 3.6 percentage points. The return to 41.3% in the first half of 2026 rests on the RMB 16 billion of low-cost inventory built at the end of 2025, plus the price increase in late February.

This correction matters because it flips the direction of the risk: 41.3% is not "too high to hold," it is "back to normal." The real risk is not that the gross margin collapses, but that revenue does—the gross margin can hold at 39%–41%, but revenue will not return to the Q1 level.

III. RMB 19 billion of inventory is the real face of this company

Laopu holds RMB 19.02 billion of inventory on its books, turning over in 271 days, 55 days more than the 216 days at end-2025. In one sentence: what sells in stores today is on average gold stock purchased nine months ago.

When gold prices rise, this inventory amplifies profit; when they fall, it becomes a source of impairment. Operating cash flow in the first half of 2026 was +RMB 2.01 billion, positive for the first time in three years, but the improvement came not from earning more but from buying less—inventory growth slowed from 290% to 18.5%.

Dividends of RMB 3.19 billion exceeded operating cash flow by RMB 1.18 billion, with the gap covered from cash on the books. A company paying out more in dividends than the cash it generates can only be explained by "there is still money on the books," not by "cash flow is strong."

IV. Is 7x PE a trap or an opportunity

Current price HKD 337, market cap HKD 59.6 billion. On TTM net profit of RMB 6.9 billion, stitched from full-year 2025 plus first-half 2026, PE is 7.9x, not the 6.9x the market cites. Even at 7.9x, it is cheaper than Chow Tai Fook's 14x and Chow Sang Sang's 12x.

But that cheapness has a precondition: the denominator is peak profit. Annualize Q2 and PE is 20–30x; use the RMB 4.5–5.7 billion that may emerge in 2027 as the denominator and PE is 9.5–12x.So "7x" and "12x" are two statements about the same company; the only difference is which denominator you use.

Three valuation scenarios: bear case HKD 222 (-34%), base case HKD 352 (+4%), bull case HKD 595 (+77%). A 2:1 payoff ratio looks decent, but weighted 40/40/20 the expected price is HKD 348.6, just 3.4% above the current price.That is not an opportunity; it is no room for error.

V. Conclusion: waiting for a quarter of falling gold prices

Laopu is not a company that will die. All 45 stores sit in top-tier malls, all are directly operated, there is no net-debt crisis, and the brand remains. Although the 2026 National Day period saw "price cuts without volume," the stores are still there and so is the customer base.

Its problem is not "whether it is good" but "when it will get good." Gold peaked at USD 5,419 in January, broke below 4,000 in June, and returned to 4,300 in early October — this is the middle of a high-level range, not the bottom.

So the rating is "Watch, wait for gold to stabilize." Two trigger conditions are fixed: gold trades sideways between USD 4,000–4,300 for a quarter and same-store sales turn positive; or the share price falls below HKD 250. If either occurs, rerun the numbers.

One honest point to close: a company that can push gross margin to 41% and single-store sales to RMB 1 billion while gold rises has real skill. But the hard part of investing is never identifying skill — it is judging what that skill is worth. The market is now pricing that skill at 10x PE; at this price you do not lose, but you do not gain either.

Conclusion

Publishable conclusion: Good company, bad timing, fair price. Not buying is not a bearish call on the company; it is a bearish call on the assumptions embedded in the current price. Waiting two quarters and validating with two data points — same-store sales and inventory turnover — is more reliable than guessing the gold price now.
