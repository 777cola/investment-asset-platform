Masterclass Close Read (Li Lu): can a 17-year-old brand survive 30 years? — Laopu Gold

Laopu Gold: ancient-method gold through a civilisational and generational lens

Core view

Li Lu would set the financial report aside and start with a bigger question: is "gufa gold" a three-year fad or a thirty-year category? The answer determines whether the stock deserves 8x P/E or 20x P/E — and matters more than any quarterly data.

The long-term case: pricing power over cultural identity is rising

Over the past decade China's consumer market has seen one clear structural shift: from "buying function" to "buying identity." Gold jewellery was once a wedding necessity and a store of value, priced by the gram, with buyers chasing the cheapest price. A segment of customers now pays for "cultural symbols," which opens room for per-piece pricing.

Laopu sits on this shift. Its engraving, filigree and enamelling techniques carry intangible-cultural-heritage endorsement; its forms draw on Palace Museum pieces and traditional motifs; packaging and store fit-out are unified into "Eastern elegance" — a complete symbol system rather than a single product. The Singapore store generated RMB 3.32 billion of overseas revenue in the first half of 2026, +107.8%, proving the system is legible beyond the Chinese-speaking cultural sphere.

Li Lu would accept this: cultural products have longer life cycles than functional products, because identity shifts slowly. That is Laopu's most valuable asset and its hardest to replicate.

The bear case on the long-term view: categories mature and brands rotate leadership

But "gufa gold" is a category, not a brand — and categories have life cycles. It emerged around 2019, exploded in 2023 and by 2025 had become an industry standard, with Chow Tai Fook Heritage, Lao Feng Xiang Gufa, Chow Sang Sang and CHJ Jewellery all offering it. By the norms of Chinese consumer goods, a category's dividend period lasts roughly three to five years; Laopu is now in year four.

The next wave could be hard gold, coloured gemstones, pearls, IP collaborations or something else entirely. Laopu's response is to "not chase the wave, but hold the high-end positioning of gufa gold" — the right strategy during a category upcycle, but passive during a downcycle.

Li Lu would caution: over seventy years the luxury industry has cycled through several leaders, and fewer than one in ten brands survive thirty years. Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels survived because they decoupled "brand" from "category" — Cartier does not sell a single craft, it sells an entire aesthetic system. Laopu today is still "gufa gold = Laopu"; the decoupling has not begun.

Valuation implication: a category leader's multiple, not a luxury brand's

That judgment maps directly onto valuation: category leaders command 10–13x, luxury goods 25–35x. Laopu trades at 7.9x on a recalculated basis, below even category leaders—the market is already pricing in "category peaking."

Li Lu's approach would be to anchor at the 10x midpoint, then test whether two things can push it higher: first, whether overseas stores can go from 1 to 5; second, whether products can expand from "gufa gold" into other materials under "Eastern aesthetics." If both succeed, the multiple can re-rate to 18–20x; if only one succeeds, it holds at 12x; if neither does, it falls back to 8x.

On 2027 net profit of RMB 5.7 billion: 8x = HKD 282, 12x = HKD 352, 18x = HKD 528. The current price of HKD 337 sits exactly at the "only one succeeds" position. That is what the market is pricing now—neither pessimistic nor optimistic.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: a brand with long-term value, but that value is not yet proven. Rated "Watch" rather than "Buy" because the category dividend is in year 4, the overseas expansion sample is only 1 company, and decoupling has not yet begun. He would reassess once more than 3 overseas locations are open, or once the category completes its generational shift.
