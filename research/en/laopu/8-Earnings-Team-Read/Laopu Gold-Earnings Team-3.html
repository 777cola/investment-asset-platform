Core view

Munger's approach to reading financial statements is to look for "where things can go wrong." In Laopu's interim report, there are three structural fragilities: revenue relies on advances, profit relies on inventory, and cash relies on buying less goods. All three point in the same direction—the gold price.

Fragility one: revenue is borrowed from the future

First-half revenue of RMB 19.808 billion, +60.3%, looks strong. Break it down: Q1 RMB 15.86–17.51 billion, Q2 RMB 2.3–3.95 billion. Q1 alone did twice the full year 2024. This is not demand growth; it is demand pulled forward.

The mechanism is clear: a 20%–30% price increase at the end of February. Once announced, existing customers rushed to order before the hike; add the "buy the rally" sentiment ahead of gold's January peak, and the two forces compressed two quarters of future demand into one. Once these buyers are done, Q2 is empty.

Munger would say: borrowed revenue looks identical to real growth on the statements — that is accounting's limitation, and management's room to maneuver. To spot it, you can only look at quarterly data and same-store figures, not half-year numbers.

Fragility 2: Profit depends on inventory, and inventory depends on the gold price

Part of the 41.3% gross margin comes from RMB 16 billion of low-cost inventory at the end of 2025. That stock was sold at high gold prices, and the spread flowed into gross profit. It is a legitimate accounting result, but it is not operating capability.

Run the numbers the other way: gold fell from USD 5,419 on 5 January to under USD 4,000 in June, a 26% decline. If 60% of the RMB 19 billion of inventory is gold, the theoretical impairment exposure is on the order of RMB 2 billion. The company has not booked a large impairment, because finished gold jewellery is measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value, and selling prices remain above cost — but another 15% fall in gold and that buffer is gone.

Munger would say: inventory is this company's leverage — it magnifies profit on the way up and magnifies losses on the way down, and management does not decide which way the leverage points. That is why he never bought commodity processors.

Vulnerability 3: Cash flow relies on "buying less inventory"

Operating cash flow went from RMB -6.848 billion (2025) to +RMB 2.008 billion (2026H1). The improvement came not from earning more but from buying less — inventory growth slowed from 290% to 18.5%.

This improvement is unsustainable: to maintain the SKU range across 45 stores and the "always in stock" experience, inventory must track revenue. If revenue recovers in 2026H2, inventory must rise again and cash flow could turn negative once more; if revenue does not recover, inventory days will keep climbing (271 days → above 300 days), which is deterioration of another kind.

Neither path is easy, and that is the essence of "cash flow propped up by buying less": it is not improvement, it is delay. Munger would say: a business model that needs constant transfusions to keep running is not a good business — it has simply not blown up yet.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: All three vulnerabilities point to the gold price, and the gold price is unpredictable. His move would be clean-cut — don't buy, put it in the "too hard" pile. Not because the company is bad, but because the question lies beyond what he can calculate. The only thing he would do is wait: wait for the industry to clear inventory, wait for the gold price to flatten out, wait for a quarter in which the financials show profit "without relying on inventory."
