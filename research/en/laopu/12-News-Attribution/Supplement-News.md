## Signal one: on July 28, the market saw Q2 clearly for the first time

Start with positioning: at HKD 337 the stock sits 60.3% below its 52-week high of HKD 849.5 and only 17% above its 52-week low of roughly HKD 288 — the upside and downside are asymmetric, the classic "mid-stage bad-news pricing" setup.

The profit alert on the evening of 27 July was good news: H1 net profit guided up more than 80% YoY. The next day the stock fell 23.76%, wiping out about HKD 16.7 billion of market cap. That divergence marked the turning point of the entire decline.

The cause was brokers' back-solving: subtract the already-disclosed Q1 figures from the profit alert's growth range, and Q2 revenue comes to just RMB 2.3–3.95 billion with net profit of RMB 510–760 million — down more than 80% QoQ and 34%–56% YoY. For the first time the market realized that the "+80% half-year growth" concealed a "-40% second quarter."

This is a textbook case of headline totals masking structure. Without comparing the profit alert against the Q1 data, the Q2 collapse would not have surfaced until the interim report on 24 August. The value of the disclosure lies not in whether it was good or bad, but in the structure it exposed ahead of time.

## Signal two: on August 9 and October 9, it rose alongside both gold price rebounds

On August 9, gold prices posted their largest weekly gain of the year, Laopu rose more than 8%, and a Morgan Stanley survey said some stores saw queues again in early August. On October 9, gold rose more than 4% in a single day, and Laopu rose 4.85% to close at HKD 337.

The two rebounds make one thing clear: its share price is a shadow of the gold price, not of its earnings. When gold falls, it falls more than gold (down 46% from January to July, versus a 26% drop in gold); when gold rises, it rises more than gold (4.85% in a single day vs 4%). This is a classic high-beta asset.

The implication for investment decisions is clear: if you buy it, you are essentially buying the direction of the gold price. The talk of "bullish on the brand, hold for the long term" does not hold up against these two trading days—a genuine long-term investor would not buy 8% because gold rose 4% in a single day.

## Signal three: October 8, the National Day "price cuts without volume"

This is the most important and most overlooked item of the past three months. During the National Day holiday, the Laopu store in Beijing's Wangfujing commercial district offered discounts on iterated products for the first time, with some items at 9% off and VIPs eligible for a further discount. The result was "price cuts without volume" — foot traffic was flat, dominated by inquiries and try-ons, with none of the queues seen in prior years.

Why does it matter? Because it simultaneously invalidates two assumptions: first, that "demand was merely pulled forward by price increases and can be pulled back with discounts"; second, that "brand power is strong enough that price is not an issue." Discounting is the last lever on the demand side, and its failure shows the problem is not price but expectations.

Compare with Shuibei over the same period: foot traffic at the Shenzhen Shuibei gold and jewelry market rose markedly, with queues at some merchants to buy gold, led by wedding gold jewelry and low-make-fee plain gold. At the same time, in the same city cluster, low-priced gold sells and high-priced gold does not. This is the real meaning of the "new consumption retreat" — it is not that no one is buying gold, it is that no one is willing to pay a premium for a symbol anymore.

## Noise: a sell side that changed its target price three times in three months

Goldman Sachs cut its target price from 1,108 to 650 on July 12, cut again to 560 on July 28, and maintained 560 on September 1. Three moves in three months, each following the share price, none leading it.

This is not Goldman's problem but a structural one for sell-side research: target prices rest on earnings forecasts, earnings forecasts rest on the latest disclosures, and the latest disclosures are always lagging. At a commodity-price-driven company like Laopu, that lag is amplified — because the gold price moves far faster than the financials.

The August 24 interim results are noise too. The market had already guessed the numbers from the July 27 profit alert; the interim results merely confirmed them. The genuinely new information sits in the interim report text of September 22 (inventory of RMB 19.02 billion, turnover of 271 days), yet the share price did not react that day — the market always reacts late to slow variables such as deteriorating inventory.
