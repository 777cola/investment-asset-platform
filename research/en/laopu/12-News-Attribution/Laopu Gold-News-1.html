Core call: the 60% de-rating reflects three rounds of repricing, not sentiment

Between July and October 2026 Laopu's share price fell from HKD 350 to HKD 302 before rebounding to HKD 337 — a 60.3% drawdown from the 52-week high of HKD 849.5, with nine recordable events along the way. Only three carried real information: the quarterly structural exposure on 28 July, the gold-price beta on 9 August and National Day sell-through on 8 October. The other six — three broker target-price revisions, one profit alert, one interim report and one interim report text disclosure — were largely noise.

Decomposing the attribution, roughly 60% of this 60% drawdown came from beta to gold falling from 5,419 to 4,000, 30% from Q2 results disproving the "sustainable high growth" thesis and 10% from valuation mean-reverting from 140x toward 10x. Gold price was the main cause, results the trigger and the valuation de-rating the outcome.

Signal one: on July 28, the market saw Q2 clearly for the first time

Start with positioning: at HKD 337 the stock sits 60.3% below its 52-week high of HKD 849.5 and just 17% above its 52-week low of roughly HKD 288 — asymmetric upside and downside, the classic "mid-stage bad-news pricing" setup.

The profit alert on the evening of 27 July was good news: H1 net profit guided up more than 80% YoY. The next day the stock fell 23.76%, wiping out about HKD 16.7 billion of market cap. That divergence marked the turning point of the entire decline.

The cause was brokers' back-solving: subtract the already-disclosed Q1 figures from the profit alert's growth range, and Q2 revenue comes to just RMB 2.3–3.95 billion with net profit of RMB 510–760 million — down more than 80% QoQ and 34%–56% YoY. For the first time the market realized that the "+80% half-year growth" concealed a "-40% second quarter."

This is a textbook case of headline totals masking structure. Without comparing the profit alert against the Q1 data, the Q2 collapse would not have surfaced until the interim report on 24 August. The value of the disclosure lies not in whether it was good or bad, but in the structure it exposed ahead of time.

Signal two: the stock rallied on both gold price rebounds, on August 9 and October 9

On August 9 gold posted its largest weekly gain of the year, Laopu rose more than 8%, and a Morgan Stanley survey noted queues at some stores again in early August. On October 9 gold rose more than 4% in a single day, and Laopu closed up 4.85% at HKD 337.

The two rebounds make one thing clear: the share price tracks the gold price, not earnings. When gold falls, Laopu falls harder (down 46% from January to July against a 26% drop in gold); when gold rises, it rises harder (4.85% in a single day against 4%). A classic high-beta asset.

The implication for investment decisions is clear: buying the stock is essentially buying the direction of the gold price. The "bullish on the brand, hold for the long term" line does not survive these two trading days—no genuine long-term investor buys 8% because gold rose 4% in a single day.

Signal three: 8 October, the National Day "price cuts without volume"

This is the most important and most overlooked development of the past three months. During the National Day holiday, the Laopu store in Beijing's Wangfujing commercial district discounted iterated products for the first time — some items at 9% off, with VIPs eligible for a further discount. The result was price cuts without volume: foot traffic was flat and dominated by inquiries and try-ons, with none of the queues seen in prior years.

Why it matters: it invalidates two assumptions at once — first, that demand was merely pulled forward by price increases and can be pulled back with discounts; second, that brand power is strong enough that price is not an issue. Discounting is the last lever on the demand side, and its failure shows the problem is not price but expectations.

Compare with Shuibei over the same period: foot traffic at the Shenzhen Shuibei gold and jewellery market rose markedly, with queues at some merchants to buy gold, led by wedding gold jewellery and low-make-fee plain gold. In the same city cluster, low-priced gold sells and high-priced gold does not. That is the real meaning of the "new consumption retreat" — not that no one is buying gold, but that no one is willing to pay a premium for a symbol anymore.

Noise: a sell side that changed its target price three times in three months

Goldman Sachs cut its target price from 1,108 to 650 on July 12, again to 560 on July 28, and held 560 on September 1. Three moves in three months, each chasing the share price rather than leading it.

The problem is not Goldman's but structural to sell-side research: target prices rest on earnings forecasts, earnings forecasts rest on the latest disclosures, and the latest disclosures always lag. At a commodity-price-driven company like Laopu, that lag is amplified — the gold price moves far faster than the financials.

The August 24 interim results are noise too. The market had already inferred the numbers from the July 27 profit alert; the interim results merely confirmed them. The genuinely new information sits in the interim report text of September 22 (inventory of RMB 19.02 billion, turnover of 271 days), yet the share price did not react that day — the market always reacts late to slow variables such as deteriorating inventory.

Conclusion

Attribution: This selloff reflects three stacked factors — gold-price beta, Q2 falsification and valuation mean reversion — not indiscriminate selling. On the news flow alone, July 28 looks like panic; decomposed, it was the first day the market got real data. Looking ahead, only three things can move the share price: the direction of the gold price, same-store data and inventory turnover. Broker reports, the wording of earnings announcements and management commentary do not matter.
