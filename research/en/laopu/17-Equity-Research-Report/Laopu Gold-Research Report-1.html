Investment Research Report: Good Company, Bad Timing, Reasonable Price

Laopu Gold (06181.HK) defines China's high-end traditional-craft gold jewellery category, with 45 directly operated stores, 2025 revenue of RMB 27.303 billion and net profit of RMB 4.868 billion. 1H26 revenue was RMB 19.808 billion, +60.3%, with net profit of RMB 4.267 billion, +88.2%, a gross margin of 41.3% and a net margin of 21.5%.

By quarter, however, Q2 revenue was only RMB 2.30–3.95 billion and net profit RMB 510–760 million, down 80% QoQ — the "high growth" reflects pre-emptive price increases and panic buying rather than sustained demand expansion. Gold fell from USD 5,419 in January to below USD 4,000 in June, turning the company's RMB 19.0 billion of inventory from a profit amplifier into a source of risk.

We rate the stock "Watch" with a neutral target price of HKD 352 (2027 net profit of RMB 5.7 billion × 10x), 4% upside from the current HKD 337; bear case HKD 222, bull case HKD 595. Triggers for a Buy: gold trading sideways at USD 4,000–4,300 for a quarter with same-store sales turning positive, or the share price falling below HKD 250.

Chapter 1 Company Overview

Lao Pu Gold was founded in 2009 by Xu Gaoming in the Wangfujing Gongmei Building in Beijing and listed on the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on 28 June 2024 (06181.HK) at an offer price of HKD 40.5, raising net proceeds of HKD 713 million on 582x subscription. The company positions itself as "China's No. 1 ancient-method gold brand", operates entirely through directly operated stores, and had 45 stores across 16 cities and 35 leading commercial centres at the end of June 2026.

Ownership is highly concentrated: Xu Gaoming directly holds 18.1%, controls 31.7% through Hongqiao Jinji (in which he holds a 70% stake), and his son Xu Dongbo holds 8.1%, giving the three parties a combined 58.10% (after the 19 May 2026 increase).

By product mix, the company focuses on ancient-method gold jewellery, covering bracelets, necklaces, pendants and ornamental pieces, plus a small amount of gold inlay products. Pricing is fixed-price, i.e. priced per piece rather than by gram weight plus a fixed labour fee, which is the core reason its gross margin is significantly higher than peers. Production runs through in-house workshops, with key processes such as engraving, filigree and enamel firing kept in-house.

All channels are directly operated. At the end of June 2026, its 45 stores spanned 16 mainland cities plus Singapore, with mainland revenue of RMB 16.49 billion (83% of the total) and overseas revenue of RMB 3.32 billion (17%). In 2026 the company shifted strategy from volume expansion to optimisation of existing stores, planning to optimise 10–12 stores over the year.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's gold jewellery demand in 2025 was 360 tonnes, down 25% year on year, while consumer spending reached RMB 281.4 billion, up 8%. In the first quarter of 2026, consumption volume was 85 tonnes, down 32%, with spending of RMB 92.8 billion. The core of this volume-price divergence is the gold price: spot gold peaked at USD 5,419/oz in January 2026, fell below USD 4,000 in June, and domestic pure gold retail prices dropped more than 25%.

The industry is barbell-shaped: the low end (Shuibei, e-commerce) relies on processing fees, the high end (international hard luxury) relies on brand premium, and the middle ground is squeezed from both sides. In fiscal 2026 Chow Tai Fook closed nearly a thousand stores and lifted net profit to HKD 9.004 billion (+52.2%) through price increases, while revenue grew only 5.3% — a contraction-driven improvement. On policy, the November 2025 adjustment to VAT on gold jewellery raised end-market costs and accelerated industry concentration toward branded players.

Globally, China is one of the largest gold jewellery consumer markets, but per-capita consumption in grams remains below India and the Middle East; the long-term runway lies in "pieces per capita" rather than "grams per capita". Domestic gold jewellery spending reached RMB 281.4 billion in 2025, near a historical peak, but this is a gold-price-driven "value peak", not a "demand peak" — consumption volume in the same period was only 360 tonnes, down 25% year on year.

The historical pattern: in the first year of a rapid gold price rise, volume falls and value rises; in the second year, if gold prices move sideways, volume recovers modestly; if gold prices keep rising, volume declines accelerate. 2026 sits in the transition from the first year to the second; gold price action from the fourth quarter through the first quarter of 2027 will determine whether the industry recovers or double-dips.

Chapter 3 Business model

Three businesses stacked. Layer one, retail: 45 directly operated stores, annualised sales of RMB 1.01 billion per store, average ticket of RMB 30,000–100,000, efficiency in the global top tier of jewellery. Layer two, brand: priced per piece, 2–3 price increases a year, gross margin 41.3%, built on symbolic strength rather than the spread on grams. Layer three, inventory: RMB 19.02 billion of stock, 271 days of turnover, using the balance sheet to absorb gold-price swings.

Value distribution is clear: brand labour fees accrue to the company, location premium to the mall (rent deduction of about 15%–25%), gold-price swings to shareholders. Zero bargaining power over upstream (SGE), bargaining power over consumers, weak against malls.

The single-store model is quantifiable: annualised sales of RMB 1.01 billion, revenue RMB 880 million, gross profit RMB 360 million, net profit RMB 190 million, against inventory of about RMB 420 million. In other words, one store uses RMB 420 million of goods to generate RMB 190 million of net profit, an inventory return of about 45% — high, but it also means a 20% fall in the gold price wipes about RMB 80 million of inventory value off a single store's books, close to half a year's net profit.

The model has another feature: cash flow first, profit later. Selling goods brings in cash, but profit depends on the gap between the gold price at sale and at purchase. That time lag averages 271 days — this company's "profit black box".

Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

Ordinary growth quality. 2026H1 revenue rose 60.3%, but Q1 contributed RMB 15.86–17.51 billion against only RMB 2.3–3.95 billion in Q2, down 76%–87% QoQ. Growth came from a 20%–30% price increase in late February and pull-forward buying rather than store expansion, with store count held at 45.

Excellent but unstable profitability. Gross margin was 41.3% (41%–42% average in 2021–2024, 37.6% in 2025, back to normal in 2026H1); net margin reached a record high of 21.5%, driven by expense dilution (period expense ratio about 15%).

Cash flow just turned positive, and quality is weak. Operating cash flow was +RMB 2.008 billion (2025: -RMB 6.848 billion), helped by buying less inventory; inventory stood at RMB 19.018 billion, turnover 271 days; dividends of RMB 3.185 billion exceeded operating cash flow.

Cross-section vs peers: gross margin 41.3% (Chow Tai Fook 32.3%, Chow Sang Sang 32.62%, Lao Feng Xiang about 9%); net margin 21.5% (9.5%, 7.6%, 3.3%); period expense ratio about 15% (Chow Tai Fook about 22%–23%); inventory turnover 271 days (Chow Tai Fook over 200 days). Best on three of four metrics and worst on one — this combination shows its high profit comes from pricing, not efficiency.

Five-year view: revenue grew from RMB 1.294 billion (2022) to RMB 27.303 billion (2025), up 21x; net profit from RMB 95 million to RMB 4.868 billion, up 51x. Profit outgrew revenue as net margin expanded from 7.3% to 17.8% — scale effects are real, but they were stretched to the limit in 2026H1 (21.5%).

Chapter 5 Management and Governance

Xu Gaoming was born in 1964 in Yueyang, Hunan, and holds a diploma in freshwater fisheries. He worked at the Yueyang Municipal Bureau of Animal Husbandry and Fisheries from 1984 to 1991, then served as general manager of Yueyang Hongqiao Tourism Investment from 1995 until 2017, opening the first store in 2009. He currently holds four roles: chairman, executive director, general manager and R&D director.

Capital allocation is restrained: no M&A, no dilution, a 75% interim dividend payout for 2026, and proactive optimisation of 10–12 stores. Governance risks: the family controls 58.10%, the free float is small, and there is no public timetable for succession. Key-person risk is structural, because product aesthetics are defined by the founder personally.

On incentives, the company has not introduced a large-scale equity incentive plan in public disclosures; management and shareholder interests are aligned mainly through the 58.10% stake itself. This is common in family businesses: the major shareholder will not spend recklessly because it is his own money. The drawback is a thin professional-manager layer, with opaque turnover risk among mid- and senior management.

On board composition, family seats dominate, and the checks and balances of independent directors have not been publicly tested. Related parties include entities such as Yueyang Hongqiao Tourism Investment, controlled by Xu Gaoming; these do not directly compete with the listed company's business, but the boundaries of related-party transactions require ongoing monitoring. Public channels show no major litigation, penalties or financial fraud allegations, which is clean by the standards of Hong Kong-listed consumer stocks.

Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three drivers, in descending order of certainty. Same-store growth: driven by price increases (2–3 times a year, 10%–30% each) and average ticket size, but this failed in 2026Q2. Store expansion: from 45 to 60 stores, with declining location quality, contributing roughly RMB 0.8–1.0 billion in annualized revenue per store. Overseas replication: 1 store in Singapore, roughly RMB 1.0 billion annualized per store, overseas revenue +107.8%, but the sample is only 1 store.

Mid-term, the revenue ceiling is RMB 36–60 billion (45–60 stores × RMB 0.8–1.0 billion), corresponding to net profit of RMB 6.5–12.5 billion.

How much room is left for price increases? A rough calculation: after the February 2026 price adjustment, the per-gram price of some products exceeded RMB 2,500/g, while the domestic retail price of pure gold over the same period was about RMB 1,000–1,100/g, implying a brand premium of about 1.3–1.5x. By comparison, international hard luxury typically carries a premium of 3–5x the gold price. This means there is still room to move higher, but each price increase narrows the customer base by another layer, and 2026Q2 has already proven that price increases have a limit.

Room for store expansion: from 45 to 60 stores, contributing RMB 0.8–1.0 billion in annualized revenue per store, but location quality declines and new stores take 12–18 months to ramp up. Overseas is the biggest variable and the thinnest evidence.

Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Five comparables: Laopu (gross margin 41.3%, net margin 21.5%), Chow Tai Fook (32.3%, 9.5%), Chow Sang Sang (32.62%, 7.6%), CHJ Jewellery (about 25%, 5.3%) and Lao Feng Xiang (about 9%, 3.3%). Laopu is the smallest and most efficient of the five, and the most exposed to the gold price.

The direct threat comes from Chow Tai Fook's Heritage collection, which is pushing gufa gold across 5,305 stores, and from Shuibei copycats. The longer-term threat is category substitution and second-hand resale prices, where brand premium goes to zero in the resale channel.

Run the five forces. Supplier bargaining power: very strong — the gold price is set by the market and the company has zero pricing power. Buyer bargaining power: moderate — high-net-worth customers are insensitive to price but sensitive to "value retention". Threat of new entrants: moderate — no craft barrier and a low capital threshold, though prime mall locations are hard to secure. Threat of substitutes: strong — hard gold, coloured gemstones and investment gold bars all divert demand. Competitive rivalry: strong — Chow Tai Fook, Lao Feng Xiang and Chow Sang Sang are all moving into gufa gold.

Three of the five forces rank "strong" or "very strong", which explains why the high gross margin cannot hold over the long term: the moat is deep enough, but there are too many enemies on the far bank.

Chapter 8 Risk factors

① Gold price decline: a further 15% fall in gold prices would trigger inventory impairment and gross margin compression, potentially halving profit; ② Demand pull-forward: already visible in Q2; two consecutive quarters of negative same-store growth would invalidate the base case; ③ Inventory: turnover exceeding 300 days would turn free cash flow negative; ④ Category cycle: traditional gold craftsmanship is now in its fourth year; ⑤ Governance: family ownership of 58.10%, one person holding multiple roles, no succession plan; ⑥ Overseas expansion: a sample of just one store, impossible to disprove.

We run a sensitivity analysis. Base case assumes 2027 revenue of RMB 30 billion, gross margin of 41% and net profit of RMB 5.7 billion. If gold prices fall 15%: inventory impairment of about RMB 1.5 billion, gross margin down to 37%, same-store sales -10%; the three combined cut net profit to RMB 4.0–4.5 billion, a decline of 21%–30%. If gold prices rise 15%: inventory appreciation, gross margin up to 43%, same-store sales +5%; net profit rises to RMB 7.0–7.5 billion, an increase of 23%–32%. Upside and downside elasticity are roughly symmetric, showing this is a business where direction decides everything.

We also flag liquidity risk: the public float is about 41.9%, daily turnover is limited, and large inflows or outflows will move the price. The single-day drop of 23.76% on 28 July 2026, which wiped HKD 16.7 billion off market value, was liquidity amplifying the move.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026: revenue RMB 34–36 billion (H1 RMB 19.808 billion + H2 RMB 14–16 billion, with H2 an inventory digestion period), net profit RMB 6–6.5 billion. 2027: bear case RMB 4.5 billion (gold price -15%, negative same-store sales), base case RMB 5.7 billion (gold price flat, revenue +15%, net margin 19%), bull case RMB 7.4 billion (gold price recovery, 3 overseas stores).

The forecast hinges on a 19% net margin in 2027, below the 21.5% in 2026H1, as the expense ratio rebounds and gross margin reverts to 39%–40%.

Quarterly assumptions: 2026Q3 revenue RMB 5.5–6.5 billion (Qixi and Mid-Autumn peak season plus gold price stabilization), Q4 revenue RMB 7.5–9.5 billion (pre-Chinese New Year stocking plus possible price increases), full year RMB 34–36 billion. Net margin 18% in Q3 and 19% in Q4, full-year net profit RMB 6–6.5 billion.

The three 2027 scenarios differ on two variables: same-store sales and gold price. Bear case: same-store -10%, gold at USD 3,600. Base case: same-store 0%, gold at USD 4,200. Bull case: same-store +8%, gold at USD 4,800. All three assumptions are falsifiable — and that is a good thing, because investors know what to watch.

Chapter 10 Valuation

Applying 8–13x P/E to 2027 forecast profit (Hong Kong-listed jewellery peers trade at 10–15x; the low end reflects cyclicality, the high end growth) yields HKD 222/352/595. At the current price of HKD 337, that implies 4% upside in the neutral scenario.

Reference points: Chow Tai Fook about 14x, Chow Sang Sang about 7x. Laopu has higher gross margin and faster growth but stronger cyclicality, so 10x is a reasonable midpoint. On asset value (RMB 19 billion in gold + 45 locations), the floor is HKD 154–173.

Cross-check with DCF (rough calculation): assuming free cash flow grows from RMB 4.5 billion in 2027 to RMB 7 billion in 2031, terminal growth of 2% and a 10% discount rate, enterprise value is about RMB 60–70 billion, corresponding to equity value of RMB 55–65 billion, or HKD 60–71 billion, implying a share price of HKD 340–400. This broadly matches the neutral range of the relative valuation (HKD 352), showing the 10x P/E is not arbitrary.

But DCF is extremely sensitive to "2% terminal growth": if the category fades and terminal growth falls to 0%, valuation drops below HKD 250. That is why this report gives a range rather than a point estimate.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Rating: Under Review; target price HKD 352. We do not recommend buying at the current price: earnings are at a peak, demand has been pulled forward, there is no valuation discount, and downside is 34%.

Upgrade triggers: gold holds in a USD 4,000–4,300 range for a quarter with same-store sales turning positive; or the share price falls below HKD 250 without any falsification trigger being hit. Downgrade triggers: two consecutive quarters of negative same-store growth, inventory turnover above 320 days, gross margin below 37%, a dividend payout cut, or the founder's departure.

Who it suits: investors who can tolerate volatility of 30% or more, are willing to hold for two to three years, and have their own framework for judging the gold price. Who it does not suit: those seeking stable dividends, those unable to withstand a halving of quarterly profit, and those who treat a "low P/E" as a margin of safety.

Time frame: two quarters for the data (2026Q3, the 2026 annual report), one year for the logic (whether the gold cycle has bottomed), three years for the category (whether gufa gold has completed its upgrade).Three time scales map to three position sizes: 0%, 1.5%, 3% — do not skip a step and go straight to a heavy position.

Conclusion

Investment conclusion: Watch, target price HKD 352. Laopu is the most efficient operator in China's gold jewellery industry: 45 stores generate RMB 27.3 billion of revenue at a 41.3% gross margin and 21.5% net margin, and those numbers are real. That said, current earnings rest on elevated gold prices and pulled-forward demand, while RMB 19 billion of inventory doubles the cyclical risk. A good company, but not one to buy at this level: the price already embeds an "all goes well" assumption. Better value lies in waiting two quarters and validating the thesis against same-store sales and inventory turnover.
