Management: a former fish seller who spent seventeen years getting one thing done — Laopu Gold

Core view

Born in Yueyang, Hunan in 1964, Xu Gaoming graduated in freshwater fisheries and spent eleven years at the livestock and aquatic products bureau before leaving in 1995 for tourism investment. He opened the first Laopu store in the Beijing Wangfujing Gongmei Building in 2009. With not a single day of jewellery experience behind him, he did the least jewellery-like thing in the industry: he did not sell by the gram.

I. Three choices that determined today's 41.3%

Choice one: direct-operated stores only. Incorporated in 2016 and listed in 2024, the company has never opened a franchise in seventeen years. Over the same period Chow Tai Fook grew to 5,305 stores in the mainland and Lao Feng Xiang to thousands of outlets, scaling through franchising. Laopu forwent the fastest route to expansion in exchange for full control over pricing and store experience.

Choice two: price by piece, not by gram. This is the most contrarian step. Selling by gram lets consumers calculate the price per gram, leaving brands to earn only a fabrication fee; selling by piece lets the brand set the price, and consumers buy the overall experience. This lifted gross margin from the industry average of 9%–30% to 41%.

Choice three: top-tier malls only. All 45 stores sit in commercial centres such as SKP, MixC, Hang Lung and Swire, on the ground floor or in core positions. Rent is high, negotiations are hard and expansion is slow, but location itself is a filter—it screens out the customers willing to pay for a symbol.

Stack these three choices together and you get annualised revenue of RMB 1 billion per store and a net margin of 21.5%.This is not luck; it is the result of doing one thing for seventeen years.

II. Capital allocation: no equity raises, no M&A, high dividends — an outlier

Listed in Hong Kong on 28 June 2024 at an offer price of HKD 40.5, raising HKD 713 million net on 582x subscription. For a company with RMB 8.5 billion of revenue that year, HKD 700 million is a rounding error. Since listing: no placement, no convertible bonds, no M&A.

Where did the money go? 7 new stores in 2024; 10 new stores plus 9 upgrades and expansions in 2025; 10–12 upgrades planned for 2026. Capex is mainly store fit-outs and workshop equipment — no self-built plants, no mine acquisitions.

Where do the earnings go? An interim dividend of RMB 3.185 billion for 2026, a payout ratio of 74.6%, exceeding operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion for the period. Cumulative dividends in the two years since listing are nearly five times the IPO proceeds.

This capital allocation is an outlier among consumer stocks: no financing-led expansion, dividends funded by internal cash flow. Upside: no dilution, no aggression, no foolish moves. Downside: no second growth curve — every bet sits on one category, one brand, one person.

III. Governance and succession: 58.10% family control

Xu Gaoming directly holds 18.1% and controls a further 31.7% through Hongqiao Jinji (70%-owned), while his son Xu Dongbo holds 8.1%, giving the three parties a combined 58.10%. On 19 May 2026, Xu Dongbo added 0.3357 million H shares, lifting the combined stake to 58.10% — buying rather than selling after the lock-up expiry is a positive signal.

Three negatives stand out. First, the chairman also serves as general manager and R&D director — three roles in one person, leaving no second executive able to speak externally. Second, the free float is small (public shareholding about 41.9%), which amplifies share-price elasticity: the stock fell from HKD 1,065 to HKD 337 in fifteen months. Third, there is no public timetable for succession — Xu Dongbo is in place at the shareholder level, but his management role is undefined.

For investors, the last point matters most, because the aesthetics, vessel shapes and craft standards of gufa jin are largely Xu Gaoming's personal judgment. Whether product style would change, and whether the customer base would accept it, under a different R&D director is unknown. "Xu Gaoming's departure" must therefore be written into the thesis's tracked trigger events.

IV. Organization: How to manage 45 stores

45 directly operated stores, RMB 1 billion annualised per store, a very small management span. That is both an advantage and a constraint.

The advantage is control: every store manager reports directly to headquarters, with unified display, pricing and service standards and no franchisee bargaining. The disadvantage is replication speed: each new store requires site selection, negotiation, fit-out, hiring and training — a 6–12 month cycle — and top-tier malls release only a handful of prime locations a year.

People matter more. High-end retail depends on the professionalism and service of sales staff; Laopu's staff must understand craftsmanship, styling and the status needs of clients. Such people take a long time to train and are concentrated in a few cities. That is why the company chose to optimise the existing base rather than open stores rapidly in 2026 — not for lack of money, but because people cannot keep pace.

From an investment perspective, this is good: slow expansion means no cash burn, no dilution and no foolish moves. It is also bad: growth has a physical ceiling, and the ceiling for 45 stores is RMB 30–40 billion in revenue.

Conclusion

Management conclusion: Excellent capital allocation, thin governance structure, succession a blank. Score 7/10. On the plus side: seventeen years focused on one business, no dilution, no M&A, 75% payout. On the minus side: one person holding three roles, family control of 58%, a small free float. Not a disqualifier, but it rules out a heavy position—if the key person runs into trouble, there is no backup.
