Core view

The interim print looks solid at the headline: revenue RMB 19.808 billion, +60.3%, and net profit RMB 4.267 billion, +88.2%. The quarterly breakdown tells a different story. Q1 revenue came in at RMB 15.86–17.51 billion against just RMB 2.3–3.95 billion in Q2, while Q1 net profit exceeded RMB 3.5 billion versus RMB 510–760 million in Q2. The average masks the cliff.

I. How the cliff formed

The timeline is clear. In January 2026, spot gold surged to a record USD 5,419/oz, triggering a gold-buying frenzy in China. On 28 February, the company raised prices 20%–30% across all channels, with some products topping RMB 2,500/g. The price-hike announcement was itself a promotion—existing customers rushed to buy before it took effect.

The two forces combined to drive Q1 tax-inclusive sales of about RMB 19 billion. By April, gold prices had turned, and in June they fell below USD 4,000, with domestic pure-gold retail prices cut by more than 25%. Once the "buy on the way up, not on the way down" psychology reversed, nobody was buying in Q2.

This cliff-edge drop is not an operational failure but a structural inevitability. When 40% of a company's revenue comes from "stock-up expectations," revenue falls off a cliff when those expectations vanish. The only question is whether it can climb back.

II. The Truth About Gross Margin: 41% Is the Norm

The market is reading 41.3% as a "record high" — that is a misunderstanding. The historical series: 41.2% in 2021, 41.9% in 2022, 41.9% in 2023, 41.2% in 2024, 37.6% in 2025, 41.3% in 2026H1. Gross margin has consistently sat at 41%–42%; 2025 was the anomaly.

Why did it fall in 2025? Gold prices rose too fast, three rounds of price increases could not keep pace with costs, and gross margin was squeezed by 3.6pp. The 2026H1 recovery rests on two things: a 20%–30% price increase in late February (sustainable), and RMB 16 billion of low-cost inventory from end-2025 released during the high-price period (one-off).

This distinction matters. If 41.3% were a "historical high", it would inevitably fall back and the risk would be large; if 41.3% is a "return to normal", it can most likely hold at 39%–41%, and the risk sits on the revenue side rather than the gross margin side. This report leans toward the latter, and therefore places the focus of risk on same-store sales and inventory.

III. Cash flow: borrowed improvement

Operating cash flow swung from -RMB 6.848 billion in 2025 to +RMB 2.008 billion in 2026H1 — on the face of it a qualitative turn. Unpacked, the improvement comes from buying less inventory: inventory growth slowed from 290% to 18.5%, saving roughly RMB 3 billion.

Dividends paid, however, were RMB 3.185 billion, RMB 1.177 billion more than operating cash flow, with the gap covered from balance sheet cash.A company paying out more in dividends than the cash it generates is drawing on its stock of cash to sustain a "high dividend" image — one that can hold for a year or two, not five.

The bigger problem is inventory turnover at 271 days, 55 days more than at end-2025. If goods don't sell, turnover stretches; stretched turnover ties up cash; tied-up cash forces less buying; less buying shrinks the SKU count; fewer SKUs drag down store efficiency.This is a closed negative loop, and 271 days is where it starts.

IV. Cross-checking the three statements: where did the profit go

Net profit came in at RMB 4.267 billion, yet operating cash flow was only RMB 2.008 billion. Where did the missing RMB 2.259 billion go? Into inventory and other working assets.

Inventory rose from RMB 16.044 billion to RMB 19.018 billion, up RMB 2.974 billion, while accounts payable and contract liabilities moved in opposite directions for a net impact of about -RMB 700 million. 2.974 − 0.7 ≈ RMB 2.27 billion, which roughly bridges the gap between profit and cash. This is not a financial flaw but a necessity of the business model: growth requires stockpiling, and stockpiling consumes cash.

Dividends: RMB 3.185 billion paid out against RMB 2.008 billion of operating cash flow, with the RMB 1.177 billion shortfall funded from balance sheet cash. So in the first half of 2026 the cash cycle ran: earn RMB 4.267 billion in accounting profit → RMB 2.974 billion turns into inventory → RMB 2.008 billion returns to the balance sheet → RMB 3.185 billion distributed to shareholders → net decrease of RMB 1.177 billion in cash. That cycle works only if the cash cushion is thick enough; if not, the company must either stockpile less or cut the dividend.

In one sentence: this report contains no bad news, only bad news hidden by averages. Understanding the quarterly tables matters ten times more than understanding the interim report.

Conclusion

Financial truth: profit is the product of two things — inventory revaluation and expense dilution — while cash comes from buying less inventory. The three statements are not fraudulent, but they do not support the narrative of sustainable high growth. Looking ahead, same-store sales and inventory turnover are the only two metrics that matter; everything else is explanation.
