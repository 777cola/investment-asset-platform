Core view

The previous seven pieces covered "what it is"; this one covers "how to do it". There are only four rules: what price to buy at, what signal to add on, what signal to sell on, and how large the position should be. Setting them down clearly is meant to stop you changing your mind when sentiment swings.

I. Buy: two ways to buy, not to be conflated

First, buy cheap. A share price below HKD 250 implies 2027 profit of RMB 4.5 billion on a pessimistic view, or 9–11x P/E, close to the top of the asset value range (RMB 25–28 billion). At that price, even if the thesis is half wrong, downside is limited — provided none of the falsification conditions has been triggered.

Second, buy certainty. Gold holds flat at USD 4,000–4,300 for a full quarter, same-store sales turn positive and inventory turnover falls back below 250 days; the share price may still be HKD 300–350, and you can still buy. At that price the stock is not cheap, but the logic has been validated.

The two cannot be mixed. The most common way to lose money: buy at the "buying cheap" price but hold on the "buying certainty" rationale — and when it falls, say you are "bullish on the brand long term".

II. Exit discipline: sell on thesis invalidation, not on price stops

Cyclical stocks swing too widely for price-based stops, which are easily shaken out. In 2026 Laopu fell from 350 to 288 before rebounding to 337; a 10% stop set at any point would have been swept.

Use logic-based stops instead. Five falsification conditions: ① same-store sales negative for two consecutive quarters with the decline widening; ② inventory turnover exceeding 320 days; ③ gross margin falling below 37%; ④ operating cash flow turning negative again alongside a cut to the payout ratio; ⑤ Xu Gaoming departing with no clear succession plan. If any one triggers, exit the position entirely, regardless of cost basis.

Set trim conditions separately: the share price returning above HKD 500 (equivalent to more than 15x 2027 neutral profit), or gross margin declining for two consecutive quarters even without breaking 37%. Trimming is valuation discipline; exiting is logic discipline — keep the two separate.

III. Watch only three numbers

Too much information leads to bad decisions. This company needs only three numbers.

Same-store sales growth. Total revenue is distorted by new stores, overseas expansion and price increases; same-store sales reflect true brand demand. It turned negative in 2026Q2, and Q3 and Q4 will determine whether this is a "rhythm" issue or a "structural" one.

Inventory turnover days. 271 days is the warning line, 300 days the red line. It captures sell-through, the gold price and cash flow all at once — the most condensed metric.

Operating cash flow. If it turns negative again in 2026H2, the "buy less inventory" improvement has run its course and the company is back to expanding on debt.

If two of the three deteriorate, downgrade; if all three improve, upgrade. Everything else — broker reports, management remarks, new product launches, store openings — is noise.

IV. Position sizing: why the cap is only 5%

The 5% single-position cap reflects volatility rather than a lack of conviction. Beta is high (gold rose 4% in a day and the stock rose 4.85%), free float is small (public shareholding 41.9%) and cyclicality is pronounced (profit can halve in a year). The right way to own this type of asset is "small position + long wait," not "heavy position + frequent trading."

Its role in the portfolio also needs to be clearly defined: not a core holding (too cyclical), not a hedge (beta too high), but purely a "cyclical reversal option" — buy 3% when gold prices stabilise, same-store sales turn positive and valuation is 8–10x; sell above 15x.

One last point: the value of a decision framework is not to make you earn more, but to keep you from panicking when it drops 60% and from getting greedy when it rises 80%. For a stock like Laopu, emotional management matters more than analytical ability.

V. If We Are Wrong: Three Error Scenarios and How to Respond

Every framework must assume it will be wrong. The three most likely errors:

Error one: gold prices return above USD 5,000 in 2027. Laopu's inventory would then carry huge unrealised gains, profit could return to RMB 8 billion and the stock could double. Response: do not chase. Gold prices sit outside our circle of competence; money made on luck is lost on skill. Missing out is acceptable.

Error two: ancient-method gold is displaced by a new category, but the company transitions successfully. Hard gold or coloured gemstones, for example, become mainstream and Laopu enters via brand and location. Response: track the revenue share of new products and reassess once it exceeds 30%. At that point the valuation logic shifts from "category leader" to "brand platform," supporting a multiple of 15x.

Error three: management does the right thing but the market disagrees. It proactively cuts prices to clear inventory, for example, sacrificing a year of profit for turnover, and the stock falls another 20% near term. Response: this is an opportunity to add, not a reason to sell — as long as the falsification conditions are not triggered. Distinguishing "the logic is broken" from "the price has fallen" is the one thing this framework must protect.

Conclusion

Decision framework locked: 0% now; initiate a 1.5% position below HKD 250; add to 3% once same-store sales turn positive; halve above HKD 500; exit fully if any of the five falsification conditions triggers. The rules are fixed—all that remains is to wait. Waiting is itself a position: it protects you from your own emotions.
