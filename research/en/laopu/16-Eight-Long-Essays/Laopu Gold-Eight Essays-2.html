Core view

Laopu's revenue in the first half of 2026 was RMB 19.808 billion, gross profit RMB 8.174 billion and net profit RMB 4.267 billion. Breaking down RMB 100 of revenue: RMB 58.7 is gold material cost, RMB 41.3 is gross profit, RMB 19.8 is expenses and RMB 21.5 is net profit. That RMB 41.3 of gross profit is the entire secret of this business.

I. RMB 58.7 is gold; RMB 41.3 is a symbol

Gold is a standardised commodity with a globally uniform price per gram. For the same gram of gold, Chow Tai Fook charges RMB 800 and Laopu RMB 1,200. The extra RMB 400 is not the value of the gold; it is the value of the symbol.

What does that RMB 400 contain? Design (Forbidden City motifs, traditional vessel forms), craftsmanship (chasing, filigree, enamel work, with hand labour taking several times as long as machine-made pieces), location (SKP ground-floor rent allocated to each item), service (one-on-one, customisable) and, most importantly, the social signal that others know you can afford it.

The first four can be quantified; the fifth cannot—and the fifth is precisely the main source of the premium. Hence Laopu's gross margin of 41.3% versus just 32.3% at Chow Tai Fook, which also sells traditional-craft gold: not a 9-point gap in craftsmanship, but a 9-point gap in the strength of the symbol.

Two, three layers of business stacked together

The first layer is retail. 45 stores, annualised sales per store of RMB 1.01 billion, average ticket size of RMB 30,000–100,000. Efficiency at this layer ranks first in the industry, built on location and display. Retail is replicable and scalable, but its ceiling is the number of locations.

The second layer is brand. A 41.3% gross margin, 2–3 price increases per year, and member repurchases. This layer determines how much money it can make, driven by symbolic strength. Brand is slow, stable and hard to replicate, but it is exposed to category cycles.

The third layer is inventory. RMB 19.018 billion of inventory, 271 days of turnover. This layer determines when and how much profit is realized, driven by the direction of the gold price. Inventory is highly volatile and uncontrollable, but it is the foundation of the first two layers—without goods, neither retail nor brand works.

Return on capital differs completely across the three layers: 20%+ for retail, 40%+ for brand, and ±50% for inventory. The market usually sees only the first two layers and ignores that the third is the one that drives the share price.

III. The ceiling of this business

The ceiling is location, not demand. China can support roughly 100–150 ground-floor locations for high-end ancient-method gold stores — about 60–80 malls at the SKP, MixC, Hang Lung, Swire, IFS and Intime tier nationwide, each offering 1–2 locations. Laopu already holds 45 locations across 35 commercial centres.

Few sites remain, and the further out you go, the worse the location quality and the lower the store efficiency. Hence the 2026 shift in strategy from "quantity expansion" to "stock optimisation" — not for lack of appetite to open stores, but because the good locations are gone.

On 45–60 stores at RMB 0.8–1.0 billion per store, the revenue ceiling is about RMB 36 billion–60 billion; at an 18%–21% net margin, that implies net profit of RMB 6.5 billion–12.5 billion. The current price corresponds to 4.4–8.4x 2027 expectations. The read-across: the current price is not expensive, but nor does it offer "10x upside".

IV. Why the net margin can reach 21.5%

A 21.5% net margin is nearly unmatched in jewelry retail. Chow Tai Fook posts 9.5%, Chow Sang Sang 7.6%, Lao Feng Xiang 3.3%. The gap cannot be explained by gross margin alone (Laopu 41.3% vs Chow Tai Fook 32.3%, a spread of just 9 points); the key lies in the expense ratio.

Rough calculation: Laopu's period expense ratio in the first half of 2026 was about 15% (38.1% gross margin − 21.5% net margin + tax and other income adjustments), versus about 22%–23% for Chow Tai Fook. The 7–8 point gap stems from three things: fewer stores (45 vs 5,305, lower management costs), higher sales per store (RMB 1 billion vs RMB 16 million, greater rent dilution), and no franchise system (no margin concessions to franchisees, no supervisor team to fund).

In other words, the 21.5% net margin is the direct result of "fewer but better."The cost of this model is slow expansion and a low ceiling; the benefit is extremely high per-store efficiency and controllable expenses. It is not an accident; it is a choice.

Conclusion

What kind of business is this? It monetises the symbol, runs on inventory as its principal, and answers to the gold price. Of the three layers, the brand is the most valuable and inventory the most dangerous. Judging this company therefore means judging how long the "strength of the symbol" can last—no financial metric answers that; only same-store sales and second-hand resale prices can.
