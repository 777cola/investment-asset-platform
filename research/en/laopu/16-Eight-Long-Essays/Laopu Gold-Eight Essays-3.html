Core view

Break the moat down item by item: brand 7, craftsmanship 7, channel position 8, supply chain 5, pricing power 6. The hardest thing to take is not the craftsmanship, but the position. Craftsmanship can be learned; position cannot be replicated.

I. Location economics: one mall supports only one heritage-gold flagship

A ground-floor spot in a top-tier mall is a scarce resource. Beijing SKP, Shanghai Plaza 66, Shenzhen MixC — each has a limited number of ground-floor units, with only one or two brands per category. The mall's logic is "complementary categories, non-competing brands" — two high-end traditional gold brands with identical positioning will not appear on the same floor.

Laopu's 45 stores span 16 cities and 35 commercial centers, all directly operated, all on the ground floor or in core positions. This layout took seventeen years to build.A new brand has only two ways in: wait for Laopu to vacate, or go to a second-tier mall.

This is a time barrier, not a capital barrier. Money can buy advertising, hire the best designers, and pay higher rent, but an exclusivity agreement signed with a mall is signed. Chow Tai Fook has money, yet its "Heritage" collection can only be sold in its own 5,305 stores — it cannot get in next door to Laopu.

So the channel score is 8, the highest of the five. The remaining 2 points are deducted for "position quality will dilute" — opening 15 more stores means positions can only move toward second-tier commercial districts, and store efficiency will fall.

II. Brand: 17 years, just enough to prove "pricing power"

The evidence for a brand is not "name recognition" but "whether people still buy after a price increase." Laopu's evidence here is solid: +10%–15% in August 2025, +20%–30% in October, and +20%–30% in February 2026 — all three price increases landed, and gross margin still rose 3.2pct in the first half of 2026.

But the brand is only 17 years old (first store in 2009). It has not lived through a full down-cycle in gold prices, nor through a generational shift in consumer taste. The barrier in luxury is not product; it is time — Cartier 1847, Van Cleef & Arpels 1906, Bvlgari 1884. Laopu needs another twenty years to prove it is not "this generation's fad."

Score 7: adequate, enough to support 10–13x PE, not enough to support 25x.

III. What could break it

Three fronts.

Product: Ancient-method gold craftsmanship holds no patents. Engraving, filigree and enamel firing are all public techniques, and Shenzhen Shuibei's copycat cycle is measured in weeks. Laopu's response is "handmade + limited edition + high ticket size," sustaining differentiation through experience and scarcity—but that does not stop substitutes that "look about the same and cost half as much."

Channel: Chow Tai Fook's "Heritage" collection is the most direct threat. In fiscal 2026, Chow Tai Fook lifted net profit to HKD 9.004 billion, +52.2%, with a gross margin of 32.3%, by closing nearly a thousand stores, raising prices and upgrading products. It pushes ancient-method gold across 5,305 stores at once, spreading marketing costs over scale—something Laopu cannot do.

Perception: The sharpest cut comes from the secondhand market. An ancient-method bangle bought for RMB 35,000 (3.5 ten-thousand yuan) fetches only its weight in gold on resale, about RMB 20,000 (2.0 ten-thousand yuan). Brand premium goes to zero at the recycling counter. Once consumers start calculating "value retention," a 41.3% gross margin looks glaring. The 2026 National Day "price cuts without volume" is the first signal of softening perception.

IV. The Real Test of the Moat: Does It Hold When Gold Prices Fall?

The standard moat test is not "how much you earn in a tailwind" but "how little you lose in a headwind." Laopu has run one and a half tests: in 2025, gold prices rose fast, and three price hikes failed to keep pace with costs, pushing gross margin from 41.2% to 37.6% — a drop of just 3.6 percentage points, not a collapse. This shows the brand's pricing power is "partially effective."

The real test has yet to come: when gold prices fall, can it still sell without cutting prices? National Day 2026 gave half an answer — a 9.0% discount and no queues. This shows "it can't sell without discounting," but also "discounting doesn't necessarily mean it can sell."

If gold prices fall 15% and it is forced to cut prices 10%, gross margin would drop from 41.3% to below 36%, and net margin to 15%. Its moat would still be there (the 45 locations are still there, the brand is still there), but profitability would fall back to 2023 levels. That is the meaning of "holding the business, but not the profit."

Conclusion

Moat conclusion: 6.6 points. It can defend its customer base, but not the gold price; it can defend its locations, but not its category. The company's most valuable assets are its 45 locations and the brand recognition built over 17 years; its weakest link is that the answer to "why are consumers willing to pay RMB 400/gram more" is weakening.
