Core view

China's gold jewellery consumption in 2025 was 360 tonnes, down 25% year on year, while spending reached RMB 281.4 billion, up 8% year on year. Volumes are falling and values are rising; this combination can only be sustained by the gold price. Q1 2026 was more extreme still: 85 tonnes, -32%. The industry is shrinking volumes while Laopu is raising prices — these two things cannot both hold over the long term.

I. The mechanism behind the volume-price divergence

In a normal year, gold jewellery consumption volume fluctuates mildly with the gold price, while consumption value grows with household income. 2025 saw a rare divergence: volume fell 25% while value rose 8%. There is only one reason — the gold price rose faster than demand fell. In January 2026, spot gold stood at USD 5,419/oz, and domestic pure-gold retail prices briefly approached RMB 1,300/gram.

This divergence hits different business models in opposite ways. Companies selling by gram (Lao Feng Xiang, China Gold) are hit hardest, because their revenue equals grams multiplied by the gold price, the fabrication fee share is squeezed below 5%, and they earn a processing fee — so lost volume is a real loss. Lao Feng Xiang's 2025 revenue was RMB 52.823 billion, -7.0%, and net profit RMB 1.755 billion, -10.0%.

Companies selling by piece (Laopu) actually benefit, because their customers are insensitive to the per-gram price, and price increases reinforce the "store of value + scarcity" narrative. Laopu's 2025 revenue rose 221%, and 2026H1 rose 60.3%.

But the benefit has a ceiling. When the gold price rises so high that "stocking up turns into standing on the sidelines," piece-sellers also lose volume — which is what happened in Q2 2026.

II. Barbell structure: the middle ground is the most dangerous

The industry is dumbbell-shaped. At one end is low-priced plain gold from Shuibei and e-commerce, which earns processing fees: high volume, thin gross margin. At the other end are international hard luxury players like Cartier, Bulgari and Van Cleef & Arpels, which earn brand premiums: low volume, fat gross margin. In the middle sit Chow Tai Fook, Lao Feng Xiang, Chow Sang Sang and CHJ Jewellery — brands that also need volume, squeezed from both sides.

The data confirms it: in fiscal 2026 Chow Tai Fook closed nearly a thousand stores and lifted net profit to HKD 9.00 billion, +52.2%, on store closures and price hikes, while revenue rose just 5.3%. Profit growth came from fewer stores and higher per-store efficiency, not from demand. This is a textbook contraction-driven improvement.

Laopu sits at the upper edge of the middle ground, trying to squeeze toward the hard-luxury end. Average ticket size is close to hard luxury (RMB 30,000–100,000), and the store experience is close to hard luxury (single floor, one-on-one service), but brand history, global recognition and secondary-market liquidity still lag.

Opening at Marina Bay Sands in Singapore in June 2025 was the first step. Overseas revenue in the first half of 2026 was RMB 3.32 billion, +107.8%, or roughly RMB 1.0 billion annualized per store — a figure that shows the overseas Chinese market recognizes it. But the sample is just one store.

III. Cycle position and policy variables

Three coordinates define the cycle position: gold price, demand, inventory. Gold fell from USD 5,419 to 4,000 and has rebounded to 4,300 — the middle stage of a pullback from highs. Demand was down 25% by volume in 2025 and down 32% in Q1 2026 — at a low. Inventory at Laopu stands at 271 days, Chow Tai Fook is closing stores to destock, and Shuibei merchants queued for raw materials over National Day — all three point to the "mid-to-late stage of the cycle."

Policy adds another variable: the gold jewelry VAT adjustment took effect in November 2025, raising end-market costs — one reason gold jewelry consumption volume fell 32% in Q1 2026. The impact is small on brands that price by piece at high gross margin, and large on brands that sell by gram. It accelerates industry consolidation toward branded players — a near-term headwind to demand, a medium-to-long-term positive for leaders.

Overall judgment: industry clearing needs another four to six quarters, achieved through store closures, price cuts and inventory clearance. Laopu's current strategy — optimizing 10–12 stores and discounting only on iterated products — is directionally right but insufficient. True clearing requires a round of "proactive price cuts to destock," and that would hurt gross margin.

IV. Endgame scenarios: three possibilities

Endgame 1: Category upgrade succeeds. Ancient-method gold shifts from a fad to an evergreen, settling into a long-term aesthetic niche like hanfu or guochao. Laopu, as the category definer, grows revenue above RMB 50 billion, holds net margin at 18%–20%, and trades at 15x. Probability: about 25%.

Endgame 2: Category fades, brand survives. Ancient-method gold is displaced by new techniques; Laopu leverages its brand and 45 locations to pivot into new categories (hard gold, colored gemstones, Eastern aesthetics), revenue stalls around RMB 30 billion, net margin slips to 12%–15%, and the multiple compresses to 8–10x. Probability: about 50%. This is the most likely outcome.

Endgame 3: Brand dilution. Peers use the same techniques to wage a price war, forcing Laopu to cut prices; gross margin converges toward 30%, net margin falls to 8%, and the multiple drops to 6–8x. Probability: about 25%.

Weighting the three endgames: 0.25×15 + 0.5×9 + 0.25×7 ≈ 10x — consistent with this report's central valuation.The probability distribution itself says it all: this is a business that will be neither too bad nor too good.

Conclusion

Industry view: Shrinking volume with rising prices is unsustainable; the shakeout needs another four to six quarters. Laopu's relative advantages will let it fare better than peers, but its absolute growth will fall to single digits. The best company in the industry is not the same as the best stock in the industry right now.
