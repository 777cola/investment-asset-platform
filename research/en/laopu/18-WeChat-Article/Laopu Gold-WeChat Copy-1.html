Net profit up 88%, stock down 60%: the market does the math better than the company

Start with an uncomfortable fact. Laopu Gold's 2026 interim report: revenue of RMB 19.81 billion, up 60.3% year on year; net profit of RMB 4.27 billion, up 88.2%; net margin of 21.5%, a record high. Over the same period the stock fell from HKD 1,065 to HKD 337, a 68% drawdown.

On one side, the best-looking interim report in history; on the other, the worst share-price performance in history. Both are true at once, which means the market is pricing something that does not show up in the financials.

That something is "peak profit".

I. First, a quarter that got averaged out

The interim report only gives half-year figures. Broken down by quarter, the story changes completely.

In Q2 2026, Laopu's revenue was RMB 2.3 billion to RMB 3.95 billion, with net profit of RMB 510 million to RMB 760 million, down more than 80% QoQ and 34% to 56% YoY. This is its first single-quarter profit decline since listing.

The reason is not complicated. At the end of February it raised prices 20% to 30% across all channels, with per-gram prices for some products exceeding RMB 2,500. Once the price-increase announcement landed, existing customers bought ahead of the change, and combined with the gold rush when gold hit an all-time high of USD 5,419/oz in January, these two forces compressed demand from the next two quarters into one—Q1 tax-inclusive sales were about RMB 19 billion, twice the full-year 2024 figure in a single quarter.

By Q2, gold prices reversed, falling below USD 4,000 in June, and domestic pure-gold retail prices dropped more than 25%. Once the "buy on the way up, not on the way down" psychology flipped, these buyers stopped buying.

So "H1 +60.3%" is the average of Q1's +200% and Q2's -40%. This is not growth; it is a pulse.

II. The 41% gross margin has been misread

Many say Laopu's 41.3% gross margin is "a record high and unsustainable." That is only half right.

Look at the history: 41.2% in 2021, 41.9% in 2022, 41.9% in 2023, 41.2% in 2024, 37.6% in 2025. 41% is this company's norm; the 37.6% of 2025 is the anomaly.

Why was 2025 anomalous? Gold prices rose too fast, and its two price hikes in August and October both failed to catch up with costs, squeezing the gross margin by 3.6 percentage points. The return to 41.3% in the first half of 2026 rests on RMB 16 billion of cheap gold stockpiled at the end of 2025, plus the price increase in late February.

This correction matters. If 41.3% were a historical peak, the risk would sit on the gross margin side; if it is a return to normal, the risk sits on the revenue side. I lean toward the latter—what truly cannot hold up is not the gross margin but that RMB 19 billion quarterly sales figure.

III. The RMB 19 billion inventory is the real story behind this company

RMB 19.02 billion of inventory on the books, 271 days of turnover, 55 days more than at end-2025. In plain terms: what the stores sell today was, on average, gold stock purchased nine months ago.

When gold rises, it amplifies profit; when gold falls, it becomes a source of impairment. The same balance sheet magnifies in both directions.

First-half operating cash flow was +RMB 2.01 billion, positive for the first time in three years — which sounds good. But on closer inspection, the improvement came not from earning more but from buying less: inventory growth slowed from 290% to 18.5%.

More striking is the dividend: RMB 3.19 billion paid out, RMB 1.18 billion more than operating cash flow, with the gap covered from cash on the books. A company paying out more in dividends than the cash it generates can only be explained by "there is still money on the books," not by "cash flow is strong."

IV. Is 7x PE a trap or an opportunity

Current price HKD 337, market cap HKD 59.6 billion. On TTM net profit of RMB 6.9 billion, stitched from full-year 2025 plus first-half 2026, PE is 7.9x. That is cheaper than Chow Tai Fook's 14x and Chow Sang Sang's 7x.

But the cheapness has a precondition: the denominator is peak profit. Annualizing Q2 gives a PE of 20x to 30x; using the RMB 4.5 billion to 5.7 billion likely to emerge in 2027 as the denominator gives a PE of 9.5x to 12x.

For the same company, both "7x" and "12x" are correct — the only difference is which denominator is used.

I ran three valuation scenarios: bear case HKD 222 (-34%), base case HKD 352 (+4%), bull case HKD 595 (+77%). A 2:1 payoff ratio looks decent, but weighted 40/40/20 the expected price is HKD 348.6, just 3.4% upside to the current price.

Taking on 34% downside for 3.4% expected return is not an opportunity — it is zero room for error.

V. My conclusion: wait for a quarter of falling gold prices

Laopu is not a company that will die. All 45 stores sit in top-tier malls, all are directly operated, there is no net-debt crisis, and the brand remains. Although the 2026 National Day period saw "price cuts without volume," the stores are still there and so is the customer base.

Its problem is not "whether it is good" but "when it will get good."

So my rating is "Watch, wait for gold prices to stabilize." Two trigger conditions are fixed: gold holds in a USD 4,000 to 4,300 range for a quarter with same-store sales turning positive; or the share price falls below HKD 250.

One honest point to close. A company that can push gross margin to 41% and single-store sales to RMB 1 billion while gold prices rise has real skill. But the hard part of investing is never recognizing skill — it is judging what that skill is worth.

The price the market is offering now says this skill is worth 10x PE. At that price you do not lose, but you do not gain either.

So I keep my money in my pocket and the company on my list. Let it prove itself.
