## The five that passed show this is a genuinely profitable business

A 41.3% gross margin is the clear No. 1 in China's gold and jewellery industry. Over the same period, Chow Tai Fook posted 32.3%, Chow Sang Sang 32.62%, Lao Feng Xiang about 9% (gram-based pricing, net margin 3.3%) and Chow Tai Seng about 25%. Many players make traditional gold, but Laopu is the only one with a gross margin above 40%. A 21.5% net margin is even more striking; globally, only the likes of Tiffany and Cartier reach 20% in jewellery retail.

ROE of roughly 55%–65% is abnormally high, and the cause is not profitability but a small equity base: over the past three years the company funded inventory expansion with debt and operating float, leaving shareholders' equity thin relative to revenue of RMB 27.3 billion. This high ROE carries a leverage component and should not be counted directly as a moat.

The debt-to-asset ratio is about 51%, normal for retail. It must be read against inventory of RMB 19 billion: a large part of liabilities is accounts payable and contract liabilities, and the true scale of interest-bearing debt is not disclosed in enough detail, so this item only marginally passes.

## But the two points are two sides of the same coin

Operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion against net profit of RMB 4.267 billion gives a ratio of 47%. That is progress in itself: operating cash flow was -RMB 6.848 billion in 2025 and negative in both 2023 and 2024, so this year marks the first positive turn in three years. But 47% means only RMB 47 of every RMB 100 of reported profit came back as cash; the other RMB 53 went into inventory and other working assets.

The second cut is harsher: interim dividends of RMB 3.185 billion exceed operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion by RMB 1.177 billion, with the gap funded from cash on hand. Free cash flow therefore covers dividends about 0.6 times. A company paying out more than the cash it earns admits only two explanations: a very thick cash balance, or strong confidence in future cash flow. Laopu is the former, but the state is not sustainable — if profit halves in 2027, the 75% payout ratio shrinks automatically.

The third cut is inventory: RMB 19.018 billion, turnover of 271 days, 55 days more than the 216 days at end-2025. Stockpiling at high gold prices and weakening sell-through in the second quarter compounded each other. At 271 days, what sells in stores today was on average gold purchased nine months ago. As gold fell from 5,419 to 4,000, that stock kept depreciating.

## Two misses — are they fatal? No, but not negligible

The verdict: not fatal, but position-defining. Not fatal because there is no solvency crisis: all 45 stores are leased with no heavy assets, the company could still distribute RMB 3.185 billion of cash at the interim stage, pointing to ample liquidity, and operating cash flow has turned positive — a step in the right direction.

Failing the two tests, however, points to the same risk: profit comes from inventory revaluation, and cash has not caught up. The 37.6% gross margin in 2025 was squeezed by the rapid rise in gold prices; the return to 41.3% in 2026H1 rests on low-cost inventory built at the end of 2025. The company does not disclose what share of the RMB 19 billion this inventory represents. If gold prices fall another 15%, the same inventory turns from a source of profit into a source of impairment, and cash flow and profit deteriorate together.

These are therefore not "financial blemishes" but "cyclical exposure". The seven-point screen is designed to exclude non-first-tier companies. Laopu sits between first-tier and non-first-tier: first-tier business, second-tier financial discipline.
