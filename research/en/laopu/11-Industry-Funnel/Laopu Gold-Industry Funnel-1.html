Core call: the most efficient player is also the most fragile

The industry funnel leaves only one name standing: Laopu. Of the six, it is the only one with gross margin above 40%, the only one with net margin above 20%, and the only one with annualised sales per store above RMB 500 million. The coarse-screen logic is simple: gram-sellers (Lao Feng Xiang, China Gold) run net margins around 3% and are essentially processing plants, undeserving of a brand multiple; scale players (Chow Tai Fook) in a store-closure cycle are growing through price hikes and closures, not demand.

But the other half of "why it" cuts the other way: it is also the only one of the six whose quarterly revenue can fall 80%. Its model rests on high ticket size, scarce locations and purchase-scramble expectations, and all three failed at once in Q2 2026. The conclusion is therefore "it is the best, but now is not the entry point."

Rough screening: split by gram or by piece first, cutting half outright

Start with business models, not financials. Gram-sellers — Lao Feng Xiang, China Gold and most regional brands — earn processing fees at ~9% gross margin and ~3% net margin, making them effectively gold OEMs plus channel distributors. They will never command a brand premium and should not be valued as consumer brands.

Pricing power sits only with piece-sellers: Laopu, Chow Tai Fook's high-end line, Chow Sang Sang and CHJ Jewellery. Four companies remain.

Next, store efficiency. Laopu's annualised sales per store are about RMB 600 million (2025 revenue of RMB 27.3 billion ÷ 45 stores), versus about RMB 16 million for Chow Tai Fook's mainland stores, RMB 10 million for Lao Feng Xiang, RMB 20 million for Chow Sang Sang and RMB 9 million for CHJ Jewellery. Laopu is three times the runner-up. Only one company remains.

The four eliminated players and how each one died

Lao Feng Xiang: 2025 revenue RMB 52.823 billion, -7.0% YoY; net profit RMB 1.755 billion, -10.0%. With over 5,000 outlets and gram-based pricing, the higher gold prices go, the more consumers hold back — the model is squeezed on both volume and price. This is the death of a model, not the death of a business.

Chow Tai Fook: FY2026 revenue HKD 94.398 billion, +5.3%; net profit HKD 9.004 billion, +52.2%. Headline profit growth looks impressive, but on closer inspection it came from closing nearly a thousand stores, lifting per-store efficiency and raising prices. Store closures deliver a one-off margin repair, not sustained growth.

Chow Sang Sang: 2025 revenue HKD 22.446 billion, +6%; net profit HKD 1.717 billion, +113%; gross margin 32.62%. It is the healthiest name on this list apart from Laopu, but it is design-driven and positioned in the mid-to-high price band; its customer base overlaps partly with Laopu's, yet it lacks Laopu's "cultural symbol" label, and store efficiency is an order of magnitude lower.

Chao Hong Ji: 2025 revenue RMB 9.318 billion, +43%; net profit RMB 497 million. Fast growth off a small base, with a 5.3% net margin; it follows a "fashion jewellery + franchising" route and does not compete in the same price band as Laopu.

Why it: three conditions that cannot be replicated

The first condition is the power to define the category. Laopu is the only brand certified by the China Gold Association as the first to promote the ancient-method gold concept, and only one brand can hold that position. Later entrants can execute better, but they cannot claim to be "the starting point of ancient-method gold."

The second condition is location. All 45 stores sit in top-tier commercial centres — 16 cities, 35 commercial centres. Ground-floor space in top-tier malls is in limited supply; whoever takes it first forces later brands to wait for it to exit. This is a time barrier, not a capital barrier — money cannot buy it.

The third condition is the pricing method. Pricing by piece rather than by gram lets it price independently of the gold price. In 2019 this looked like a risk; in 2026 it looks prescient — it lifted gross margin from the industry average of 9%–30% to 41%.

Stack the three conditions together and you have the answer to "why this one." But that answer comes with a footnote: when all three hold, it is a money printer; when the first condition (category) fails, the other two can only make it die more slowly.

After the Final Selection: Why We Still Won't Buy

The funnel screens for "the best company," not "the best stock." Price sits between the two.

At the current HKD 337 and a market cap of HKD 59.6 billion, a neutral 2027 profit of RMB 5.7 billion at 10x P/E gives a fair value of HKD 352, or 4% upside. On a pessimistic RMB 4.5 billion at 8x, that is HKD 222, or 34% downside. The funnel says "worth watching"; the valuation says "not yet a buy."

This module's conclusion therefore matches the rating: the best company in the industry, first on the watchlist, waiting for price or waiting for data.

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: Of the six, only Laopu qualifies — but qualifying is not the same as buying it now. Its three non-replicable conditions (category definition power, location, per-piece pricing) are all real, but the cost is that all three hinge on a single assumption: "ancient-method gold is still in fashion." If that holds, the stock is worth 15x; if not, 8x. The current price implies 10x — the price of an assumption still pending.
