Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Laopu Gold

The report looks impressive, but all the impressive parts are in inventory.

Core view

Buffett's order for reading financial statements: cash first, then asset quality, and profit last. Apply it to Laopu and the conclusion inverts: cash has only just turned positive, asset quality is deteriorating, while profit is hitting new highs. Of those three, the first two matter more than the third.

Cash at first glance: from -RMB 6.8 billion to +RMB 2 billion — the direction is right

Operating cash flow was -RMB 6.848 billion in 2025 and +RMB 2.008 billion in H1 2026. That swing is the most substantive improvement in the interim report: over the past three years the company funded inventory expansion with financing and debt, and inventory rose from RMB 4.088 billion to RMB 16.044 billion in 2025, up 290%, as cash was tied up in gold materials and finished goods.

In H1 2026 inventory rose only 18.5% (RMB 16.044 billion to RMB 19.018 billion), a sharp slowdown, while sales collections kept pace and turned operating cash flow positive. The turn to positive, in other words, came not from earning more but from buying less.

Dividends of RMB 3.185 billion, however, exceeded operating cash flow of RMB 2.008 billion, with the gap covered from cash on the books. Buffett would immediately ask: can they still pay it next year? If profit halves and inventory keeps building, the 75% payout ratio will have to come down.

Second look at assets: RMB 19 billion of inventory is the entire risk in this business

Inventory of RMB 19.02 billion — and its share of total assets — is the single most critical number for this company. It plays three roles:

In a rising market, it is a source of profit. Gold stockpiled cheaply at the end of 2025 was sold at high prices in the first half of 2026, lifting gross margin from 38.1% to 41.3%. That profit was not generated by current operations; it is an inventory timing gap.

In a falling market, it is a source of impairment. Gold fell 26% from USD 5,419 in January to under USD 4,000 in June. Assuming raw materials are 60% of the total, RMB 11.4 billion of the RMB 19 billion is gold, implying theoretical impairment pressure on the order of RMB 2 billion—though actual accounting treatment would be far more conservative, since finished gold jewellery is measured at the lower of cost and net realisable value and would not be written down all at once.

On turnover, it is a cash black hole. Turnover of 271 days, 55 days more than at the end of 2025. Second-quarter revenue was only RMB 2.3–3.95 billion, down 80% quarter on quarter; goods are not selling, so turnover days are naturally stretched. All three roles can hold true in the same quarter.

A third look at profits: can the 21.5% net margin hold?

The 21.5% net margin is the highest on record, up from 7.3% in 2022, 13.1% in 2023, 17.3% in 2024 and 17.8% in 2025, with 21.5% in 2026H1. The trajectory looks impressive, but it tells the same story as the revenue curve—scale dilutes fixed costs.

Breaking it down: the period expense ratio was 18.2% in 2024 and fell to roughly 14%–15% in 2026H1 (rough estimate; rent and labour are relatively rigid, with revenue up 60% while expenses rose only about 30%). The faster revenue grows, the more the expense ratio falls and the higher the net margin. That is good operating leverage, but it cuts both ways—when Q2 revenue fell 80%, the same leverage crushed profit, which is why Q2 net profit was only RMB 510–760 million, down more than 80% QoQ.

So 21.5% is not a number that can be extrapolated. It is the product of two conditions holding at once—peak revenue and rigid expenses—and both changed in Q2.

Buffett's three questions

Question one: If gold prices fall 20%, does this company still make money? Yes, but far less. Under our pessimistic scenario, 2027 net profit is RMB 4.5 billion and net margin falls back to around 15%. It remains a profitable business rather than a loss-making one — which puts it ahead of most cyclical stocks.

Question two: Where does management put the money it earns? In 1H26 it earned RMB 4.267 billion, paid dividends of RMB 3.185 billion and left RMB 1.082 billion on the balance sheet, while inventories rose RMB 2.974 billion. In other words, the money for expansion came mainly not from profit but from debt and cash on hand. That structure is fine in a growth phase, but becomes pressure when growth slows.

Question three: How much margin of safety does the current price offer? On 2027 neutral profit of RMB 5.7 billion and 10x P/E, fair value is HKD 352 against a current price of HKD 337 — a 4% margin of safety. That is not a margin of safety; it is zero room for error.

Conclusion

Financial assessment: Reporting quality is above average, but earnings sustainability is the weakest. Cash flow has only just turned positive, asset quality is deteriorating and margins sit at a peak—three signals pointing the same way: current earnings are cyclical, not a reflection of underlying capability. The one financial safeguard is the absence of a solvency crisis: all stores are leased, so the footprint can be scaled back at any time.
