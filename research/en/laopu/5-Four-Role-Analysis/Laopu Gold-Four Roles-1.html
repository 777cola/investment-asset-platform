Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — Laopu Gold

The essence of this business: it sells processing fees and status, not gold

Core view

Strip Laopu's business down to its foundations: it does not sell gold; it sells pricing power over the act of turning gold into a cultural symbol. For the same gram of gold, selling by weight earns only RMB 10–30 in fabrication fees, while selling by piece earns over RMB 300. With 45 stores and annualized sales of RMB 1.01 billion per store, this store-level productivity comes not from traffic but from ticket size.

Pricing method determines gross margin: per item vs per gram

China's gold jewellery industry has two ways of doing business. The first is selling by gram: the gold price plus a fixed fabrication fee. Lao Feng Xiang and China Gold take this route, with gross margins of around 9% — essentially a processing plant. The second is selling by piece: one fixed price per item, with fabrication and design accounting for 30%–50% of the retail price. Chow Tai Fook's high-end line and Laopu take this route. Laopu has taken the second model to its extreme: a 41.3% gross margin, more than four times that of Lao Feng Xiang.

The source of that price gap is not gold but "the power to define". For the same traditional-craft bangle, consumers will pay RMB 1,500 more for "intangible-heritage engraving, made by Laopu", because the piece is recognisable in social settings. This is the plainest definition of a brand business: what you sell, others cannot price-compare.

In late February 2026 it raised prices 20%–30%, pushing the per-gram price above RMB 2,500/g, and peers did not follow. That is no coincidence — following would have cost them volume, because they lack the customer base willing to pay for a symbol. Laopu has it.

How did store efficiency reach RMB 1 billion?

Annualized sales per store are RMB 1.01 billion, a figure that ranks in the top tier of the global jewellery industry. For comparison: Chow Tai Fook has 5,305 stores in the mainland and FY2026 revenue of HKD 94.398 billion, or roughly RMB 16 million annualized per store; Lao Feng Xiang has over 5,000 outlets and RMB 52.8 billion in revenue, or about RMB 10 million per store. Laopu's per-store efficiency is six times that of Chow Tai Fook.

It rests on three things: location, ticket size and repeat purchase. Locations are ground-floor spots in top-tier commercial centres such as SKP, MixC, Hang Lung and Swire, where footfall quality is high; ticket sizes run from RMB 30,000 to 100,000, so one sale equals ten elsewhere; repeat purchase relies on the membership system and 2–3 price increases a year that create the expectation that buying early pays off.

But this model has a precondition: foot traffic has to be there. During the October 2026 National Day holiday, the Laopu store at Beijing Wangfujing offered 9.0% off iterative products for the first time, with a further discount for VIPs, and the result was that price cuts did not move volume: store traffic was flat and skewed toward enquiries and try-ons, with none of the queues seen in previous years. This shows the other side of store efficiency: fragility. It rests on the expectation of a scramble to buy, and once that expectation fades, the RMB 1 billion does not hold up.

The three hard constraints of this business

The first constraint is the gold price. Raw materials account for 60% of costs, and the company has no pricing power, so it can only accept the Shanghai Gold Exchange price passively. Price increases lag. In 2025 the company learned this the hard way: gold rose fast, and three rounds of price increases still could not keep up, with gross margin falling from 41.2% to 37.6%.

The second constraint is inventory. To sustain the experience of "always in stock, full range of styles," the company must hold inventory. RMB 19 billion of inventory and 271 days of turnover mean a third of a year's sales are locked in the warehouse. This is not mismanagement; it is the model cost of this business.

The third constraint is location. Prime mall ground-floor space is limited, and 45 stores have already taken most of the best spots. To open another 45 stores, the company can only move into tier-two commercial districts, and store efficiency immediately drops a notch. So the ceiling on growth is not demand; it is location. This also explains why in 2026 the company shifted its strategy from "quantity expansion" to "optimizing the existing base," with plans to optimize 10–12 stores for the full year.

How would Duan Yongping judge this?

In his framework, a good business must meet three criteria: product differentiation, user stickiness, and no need for continuous investment. Laopu clears the first, half-clears the second, and fails the third.

Product differentiation is real (craftsmanship, design, symbolism); user stickiness is half-real (members repurchase, but they wait and see the moment gold prices fall); no need for continuous investment is false (inventory must rise every year, stores need renovation, overseas markets require trial and error).

So he would say: this business is understandable, but not easy to run. "A good brand does not equal a good business" — Laopu is the best case in point. He might buy a little at a very low price — say 5x PE, on sustainable profit — but not at this level, because he cannot answer the question "can it still make money if gold prices halve?"

Conclusion

Business judgement: Pricing power is real but incomplete; the growth ceiling lies in positioning, not demand. The ceiling for this business is "China's number one in high-end gold jewellery", with annual revenue of RMB 30–40 billion — not a business that can grow to RMB 100 billion. Valued at this ceiling, a 10x PE is a reasonable midpoint; 13x requires overseas results above expectations.
