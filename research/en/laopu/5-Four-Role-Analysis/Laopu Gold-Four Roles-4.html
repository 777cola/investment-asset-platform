Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Laopu Gold

Six risks, two of them fatal
Research date 2026/10/09  ·  Price HK$337.00 (+4.85%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 59.6 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 6.9x

Core view

Risks must be tiered: the fatal ones are the gold price and pulled-forward demand; the damaging ones are brand life cycle and inventory; the disruptive ones are governance and free float. The first two already occurred together in the second quarter of 2026; the latter three are still to come.

Risk one: the gold price — already happening, just not acknowledged

This is the only risk that "requires no imagination." Spot gold peaked at USD 5,419/oz in January 2026, fell below 4,000 by June, and domestic pure-gold retail prices were cut by more than 25%. The company holds RMB 19.02 billion of inventory, roughly 60% of it raw gold, implying a theoretical impairment exposure on the order of RMB 2 billion.

The bigger problem is the two-way mechanism in gross margin. When gold prices rose fast in 2025, gross margin was squeezed to 37.6%; in H1 2026, low-cost inventory built at the end of 2025 lifted it back to 41.3%.The same mechanism reverses when gold falls: high-cost inventory meeting low selling prices makes a drop in gross margin below 35% entirely possible.

Li Lu would say the frightening part of this kind of risk is not its magnitude but its unpredictability. You can calculate the impact of a 15% decline, but you cannot calculate when it will fall or how long it will last. Value investing demands "predictability," and the gold price offers no such promise.

Risk 2: Demand pull-forward — Q2 already gave the answer

In Q2 2026, Laopu's revenue was RMB 2.3–3.95 billion and net profit RMB 510–760 million, down more than 80% QoQ and 34%–56% YoY. This is the company's first single-quarter profit decline since listing.

The cause is not vanishing demand, but demand pulled forward. A 20%–30% price increase in late February, combined with panic buying before gold prices peaked in January, concentrated high-end customers' full-year purchases into Q1, when tax-inclusive sales reached about RMB 19 billion—one quarter equal to twice all of 2024. When gold prices reversed in Q2, these customers naturally stopped buying.

The danger of this mechanism: it makes "growth" and "pull-forward" look identical on the financials.H1 2026 revenue rose 60.3%, which looks like strong growth; broken down by quarter, it is the average of Q1 +200% and Q2 -40%. Without brokers' back-calculation after the profit alert, the market could not see what Q2 looked like at all—which is why the stock fell 23.76% in a single day on July 28: investors saw the quarterly structure clearly for the first time.

Risks 3-5: brand, inventory, governance

Brand life cycle. Traditional gold has been hot for three years. It took off in 2023, broke into the mainstream in 2024 and became an industry standard in 2025, with Chow Tai Fook Heritage, Lao Feng Xiang Gufa and Chow Tai Seng Filigree all in the category. A category's boom typically lasts three to five years, and Laopu is now in year four. If consumers shift to hard gold, coloured gemstones or IP collaborations in the next cycle, the 41.3% gross margin will converge toward 30%.

Inventory. 271 days of turnover is the warning line. With gold prices high and sell-through weakening, the two factors combined mean that if turnover exceeds 300 days by the end of 2026, operating cash flow will turn negative again — the lesson of RMB -6.848 billion in 2025 is right in front of us.

Governance. Xu Gaoming and Xu Dongbo, father and son, together with Hongqiao Jinji, hold a combined 58.10%, leaving a small free float. The lock-up expired in July 2025, one year after listing, and the share price has been sliding from its HKD 1,065 peak; in May 2026 Xu Dongbo raised his stake by 33.57 million H shares, lifting the holding to 58.10%, a positive signal. But the family controls 58%, and the chairman also serves as general manager and R&D director — one man running three functions — with no public timetable for succession.

Risk 6: Going global — the best story, the thinnest evidence

The Marina Bay Sands store opened in Singapore in June 2025; overseas revenue reached RMB 3.32 billion in H1 2026, up 107.8% YoY, or roughly RMB 1 billion annualized per store. This is the most optimistic passage in the whole piece, and also the thinnest evidence — just one store.

A single store's success could be location (Marina Bay Sands is one of the highest-traffic malls in Asia), could be a opening honeymoon, could be one-off spending by Chinese tourists. To prove the brand is replicable requires at least three stores, across two markets, operating for a full two years. The sample size is currently 1.

Li Lu would caution: the success rate of Chinese brands going overseas is far below what the market imagines. Over the past two decades, only a handful of Chinese consumer brands have established a firm footing abroad. If Laopu's overseas push succeeds, the valuation could command above 15x; if it fails, overseas investment becomes a drag.

Conclusion

Risk assessment: Of six risks, two have already materialized, three are on the way, and one cannot be falsified. The two that have materialized (gold price, demand pull-forward) happen to be the two most critical, showing that this company's risks are not "possible in the future" but "happening now." This also explains why the stock fell from HKD 1,065 to HKD 337—the market is not panicking, it is repricing.
