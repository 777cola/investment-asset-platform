Core view

All four independent reports agree on the facts: revenue of RMB 19.808 billion, net profit of RMB 4.267 billion, inventory of RMB 19.018 billion, a Q2 single-quarter collapse, and gold falling from 5,419 to 4,300. They diverge on three judgments: whether Q2 is temporary or structural, whether gross margin can hold at 40%, and whether the overseas expansion is replicable. Our item-by-item verdict follows.

Disagreement one: is Q2 temporary or structural?

The commercial and financial read leans "temporary": the Q2 decline mainly reflects pull-forward demand ahead of price increases, and once that demand is absorbed (roughly two to three quarters), same-store sales should recover of their own accord. The industry and risk read leans "structural": gold fell from 5,419 to 4,000, domestic retail prices dropped 25%, and consumers' buy-on-rising-prices psychology has reversed—a question of direction, not timing.

Verdict: structural factors account for 70%.Three independent pieces of evidence support this. First, in October 2026 Laopu offered a 10% discount (9.0 discount) on iterative products for the first time, and the result was "price cuts without volume"—had this been a timing issue, price cuts should have revived sales, and they did not. Second, a Citi survey in September showed that nearly half of purchases occurred after April, confirming that Q1 stockpiling was indeed pull-forward, but no second wave of buyers followed. Third, at the industry level, gold jewellery consumption fell 25% in 2025 and 32% in Q1 2026—Laopu cannot buck the industry indefinitely.

The 30% assigned to "temporary" reflects gold price stabilisation from August, queues reappearing at some stores in early August, and some recovery in the traditional Q3 peak season (Qixi, Mid-Autumn).But the recovery is not strong enough to support the full-year 2026 revenue expectation of RMB 38 billion.

Divergence 2: Can the 41.3% gross margin hold?

The workpaper's judgment is that "the 41.3% includes a low-cost inventory timing gap that cannot repeat." The financial perspective supports this; the commercial perspective dissents: gross margin ran 41.2%–41.9% in 2021–2024, so 41% is the norm, not an anomaly.

Ruling: 41.3% sits within the normal range, but it trends down in 2027. Both are right; they just operate on different time scales. Historically 41% held because gold prices moved moderately and the company raised prices 2–3 times a year to catch up with costs. The 2026 problem is that gold price swings are too wide—from 5,419 to 4,000 to 4,300 within a year—and no pricing cadence can keep up.

So the gross-margin risk is not "a fall back to 37%" but "oscillation between 37% and 41%." The oscillation alone is enough to kill the valuation—because the market will only pay a low multiple for a margin it cannot extrapolate.

Divergence 3: Can overseas expansion be replicated?

The risk view is the thinnest (one store only), the industry view the most bearish (Chinese brands have a poor record overseas), and the commercial view the most bullish (the Singapore single store annualising at RMB 1 billion proves product strength).

Verdict: treat overseas expansion as option value, not as a valuation input. The bull case in our three-scenario valuation (2027 net profit of RMB 7.4 billion, 13x P/E, HKD 595) already assumes 3 overseas stores, while the base case (RMB 5.7 billion, 10x, HKD 352) assumes only 1–2. The market is therefore paying nothing for overseas expansion: success is a free option, failure costs no valuation.

This is the only bullish conclusion in this report, and it rests on one premise: don't pay for the option. At HKD 337, the stock sits just 4% below our base case of HKD 352, confirming the market indeed ascribes no premium to overseas expansion.

Overall verdict: why "Watch" rather than "Avoid"

Of the four perspectives, Munger's is the harshest (avoid), Li Lu's the mildest (watch), and Duan Yongping and Buffett sit in between (don't buy, wait on price). The Team Lead adopts "watch" for three reasons.

First, the company will not die. There is no net-debt crisis, all stores are leased, all 45 generate positive cash flow, and the brand remains intact. A company like this deserves to stay on the list: opportunities in cyclical stocks tend to appear when bad news is most concentrated.

Second, the price has not arrived. The neutral case of HKD 352 offers only 4% upside to the current price, while the bear case of HKD 222 implies 34% downside. The payoff ratio is 2:1 but the probability skews bearish, leaving expected value at just +3.4% — not worth taking on 34% downside risk now.

Third, the verification window is right ahead. Terminal sell-through in October–December 2026 (National Day, Double 11, Christmas) will deliver the first full-quarter reading after gold prices stabilize; the March 2027 annual report will provide full-year same-store data. Waiting for these two data points costs a possible 20% rebound but avoids a 34% decline. The math works.

The rating is therefore "watch, wait for gold prices to stabilize," with hard-coded triggers: gold holding flat in the USD 4,000–4,300 range for a quarter with same-store sales turning positive, or the share price falling below HKD 250.

Conclusion

Overall verdict: Watch, wait for the gold price to stabilise. The disagreement now comes down to one sentence: 70% of the Q2 collapse is structural and 30% is timing; gross margin will swing between 37% and 41%; overseas expansion is a free option we will not pay for. Together these three point to "do not buy now, but keep watching". Next steps: set alerts for the January 2027 positive profit alert and the March 2027 annual results, and track only two numbers: same-store growth and inventory turnover days.
