Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — Laopu Gold

The industry is shrinking even as it raises prices—those two things cannot both hold over the long term

Core view

China's gold jewellery demand fell 25% YoY to 360 tonnes in 2025, yet consumer spending rose 8% YoY to RMB 281.4 billion. Falling volumes against rising prices — a classic "gold-price-driven recession." 2026Q1 was more extreme still: consumption of 85 tonnes, down 32% YoY, on spending of RMB 92.8 billion. The industry is shrinking volumes while Laopu raises prices. How long this combination can hold is the core question for industry research.

What does the price-volume divergence signal

In a normal year, gold jewellery consumption volume moves mildly with the gold price while consumption value rises with income. 2025 saw a rare divergence: volume fell 25% and value rose 8%. There is only one explanation — the gold price rose faster than demand fell. Spot gold hit USD 5,419/oz in January 2026, and domestic pure-gold retail prices briefly approached RMB 1,300/gram.

That divergence is bad for the industry, good for high-end brands and a disaster for mid- and low-end players. The reason: the higher the gold price, the more a business selling by gram resembles "custodying gold for customers," with fabrication fees compressed below 5%; businesses selling by piece instead benefit, because their customer base is less price-sensitive and price increases reinforce the "store of value + scarcity" narrative.

The data confirms it: in 2025 Laopu revenue rose 221%, while Lao Feng Xiang fell 7% with net profit down 10%; Chow Tai Fook closed nearly a thousand stores and lifted net profit 52.2% through closures and price increases. The industry is consolidating, and the survivors are of two kinds — extreme value for money (Shuibei) and extreme brand (Laopu).

Competitive landscape: the middle ground is the most dangerous

The industry is barbell-shaped. At one end sits low-priced plain gold from Shuibei and e-commerce, which earns processing fees on large volumes and thin gross margins; at the other, international hard-luxury players such as Cartier, Bulgari and Van Cleef & Arpels earn brand premiums on small volumes and fat gross margins. In the middle sit Chow Tai Fook, Lao Feng Xiang, Chow Sang Sang and CHJ Jewellery — brands that also need volume and are squeezed from both ends.

Laopu's position is unusual: it sits at the upper edge of the middle ground, trying to squeeze into the hard-luxury end. Average ticket size is close to hard luxury and so is the store experience, but brand history, global recognition and secondary-market liquidity still lag. The Singapore store opened in June 2025 marked a first step toward hard luxury; overseas revenue in 1H26 was RMB 3.32 billion, +107.8%, or RMB 1 billion annualized per store — evidence that the overseas Chinese market recognizes it.

But Munger would point out: squeezing in and standing firm are two different things. The barrier to hard luxury is not product or stores but time — Cartier was founded in 1847, Van Cleef & Arpels in 1906, Laopu in 2009. For a brand to become a symbol that "needs no explanation" takes two or three generations of accumulation. Laopu still needs explaining.

Cycle position: this is the single most important point

Three indicators frame the cycle position: the gold price percentile, industry inventory and consumer willingness.

Gold is mid-pullback from an all-time high. January's USD 5,419 marked the historic peak; June broke below 4,000; by early October it had recovered to near 4,300 — down 21% from the peak but still well above the 2024 average. This is not a bottom; it is high-level turbulence.

Industry inventory is elevated. Laopu carries RMB 19 billion, or 271 days; Chow Tai Fook is closing nearly a thousand stores to clear stock; Shuibei merchants queued to buy gold over the 2026 National Day holiday — gold, not jewellery, which tells you the end market is betting on a gold price rebound, not a consumer recovery.

Consumer willingness is at a low. Over the 2026 National Day holiday Laopu discounted iterative products by 10% (9.0 off) for the first time, and the result was "price cuts did not move volume" — customers mostly came to ask questions and try pieces on. This is the most direct read on the demand side: panic buying when prices rise, standing on the sidelines when prices fall — this is not consumption, this is speculation. Munger would say margins propped up by speculative demand cannot be used for valuation.

Munger-style conclusion: this is a cyclical industry

Gold jewellery is an industry that has not disappeared in three thousand years, yet the brand rankings turn over every twenty years. Industries do not die; companies do.

Laopu's current edge is that it "defines the gufa gold category"; its risk is that "the gufa gold category will go out of fashion." Gufa gold has been hot for three years since 2023, and no one knows what the next trend will be—hard gold, coloured gemstones, or IP collaborations. Chow Tai Fook is contesting this position, turning its "Heritage" collection into a RMB 10 billion-level product line in fiscal 2026.

He would give this industry a 6: demand is real, the landscape is clear, and the cycle is obvious. Obvious means—every few years it hands you a chance to buy cheap, so there is no need to rush.

Conclusion

Industry view: Shrinking volume with rising prices is unsustainable; the cycle sits in the upper-middle range. From Q4 2026 to 2027, the industry most likely faces a double hit to volume and price: flat-to-falling gold prices, consumption volume still declining, and brands leaning on price hikes to sustain revenue until those hikes stop working. Laopu's relative advantages should let it outperform peers, but absolute growth will slow to single digits.
