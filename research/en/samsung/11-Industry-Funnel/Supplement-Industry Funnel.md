## Rough screening: first cut half with three hard criteria

Three criteria: ① Can it price on its own (top three in share); ② Does it have a balance-sheet buffer (net cash or low debt); ③ Is valuation below its historical high. All three must pass to advance to the next round.

Kioxia fails the first: NAND-only, no DRAM, a single-product structure and fully a price taker. ChangXin fails the second and cannot be judged on the third: unlisted, no public valuation, and burning cash through capacity expansion. Both are eliminated at the rough-screen stage — not because they are bad companies, but because they do not meet the "investable, valuetable" conditions.

TSMC passes all three, but it is a different business — the foundry moat is process technology and customer trust; the memory moat is cost and capacity. Running them through the same funnel yields the wrong conclusion, so at the final-selection stage it serves as a "reference", not a "candidate".

## Final pick: Samsung vs SK hynix vs Micron

SK hynix: elasticity is the strength, an expensive multiple the weakness. 2026Q1 operating margin was 71.5%, the highest of the three, and its 50% HBM share is tied to Nvidia. But at 17.2x P/E (TTM) it is the most expensive of the three, and the stock has already fallen 37.8% in July — the market is pricing in a cyclical peak; the price simply has not fallen far enough yet.

Micron: purity is the strength, the absence of a buffer the weakness. FY26Q3 gross margin 84.9%, revenue +346% YoY — the pure-play memory name with the greatest elasticity, and at 6.5x P/E (2027E) it looks the cheapest. But with no foundry and no end market, profit falls vertically once prices turn down; and the stock has already risen 226% in 2026, carrying the greatest drawdown risk.

Samsung: longevity is the strength, low elasticity the weakness. P/E 7.5x, P/B 1.8x, net cash, four divisions backstopping one another. In its best years it earns less than SK hynix (dragged by end markets); in its worst years it loses the least (cushioned by end markets). If the call is that the cycle is near a top, this trait is worth more than elasticity.

## Why it: three reasons, one precondition

Reason one: downside protection. Samsung's bear case is KRW 110,000 (-60%), but that implies 0.72x P/B — a level it has never breached in history, with the trough at 0.8x. SK hynix's bear case is KRW 880,000 (-52%), at which P/E has already lost all meaning. For the same downside, Samsung has net assets as a floor; SK hynix has only "valuation mean reversion."

Reason two: share is improving. HBM from 21% to 33%, DRAM from 33% to 39.4% — both lines are moving in the right direction. That is rare at a cycle peak: peaks usually bring frozen share and reliance on price hikes, whereas Samsung has "price hikes + share gains" running simultaneously.

Reason three: the valuation discount has room to narrow. The 7.5x P/E embeds a triple discount: "governance discount + cycle discount + messy business mix." If HBM share climbs above 40% and foundry turns profitable, one of these layers disappears, and valuation can recover to 6–6.5x (bull case).

One precondition: this choice only holds when "the cycle is judged to be near a peak." If the cycle has two more years to run, you should buy SK hynix or Micron, which offer far more upside beta. The funnel's conclusion depends on the cycle call, not on company quality — this must be stated clearly.

## The two eliminated players deserve a spot on the watchlist

CXMT is worth watching — not as a secondary-market buy (it is unlisted), but for its capacity and pricing. Every DDR5 line it adds lowers the price floor for consumer-grade DRAM by one notch and shaves a slice off Samsung's ordinary DRAM profit, making it a "short indicator" for Samsung's earnings.

Kioxia is also worth watching as a pure read on NAND prices. Samsung does not disclose NAND profit separately, but Kioxia's quarterly results serve as a proxy — the moment Kioxia's gross margin turns down, Samsung's NAND runs into trouble first.

Neither is an investment candidate; both are "information sources." The funnel screens out "what to buy," but what remains includes "what to watch."
