## Three signals: all point to "peak profit"

The first was the 7 July Q2 preliminary guidance: operating profit of KRW 89.4 trillion, above consensus. By any normal reading that was a clear positive, yet the stock gave back the entire gain within a week — the good news had been bought ahead of the guidance, and the 6 July level of KRW 362,500 was where expectations were fully priced in.

The second was the 30 July earnings call. The numbers were fine; the tone was the problem: Q3 guidance came in on the conservative side, the MX division swung to a loss, and management publicly flagged "pressure from memory costs on the device business" for the first time. The market got the message — if even Samsung says its device business cannot absorb it, room for further price increases is limited.

The third was the 2 September disclosure of a 33% HBM share. This was the only event that was both positive and accepted by the stock, because it answered a question that had hung over the company for two years: whether Samsung is still viable in HBM. The answer is yes. But it drove only a one-day gain and did not change the trend — the share improvement was structural, while the market at that point was trading a cyclical peak.

## Three noise items: misread as positives or negatives

The union strike is noise, but not irrelevant noise. Production was unaffected over the three days (memory lines are highly automated) and the stock did not react; but it marks the end of Samsung's "union-free" era, and labour costs will rise rigidly. The impact will be felt in three years, not within three months.

TrendForce's September 7 data is noise: it is a retrospective tally of Q2 that the market already knew from July earnings reports. Such "lagging-confirmation" data typically produces only one day of volatility and does not change the trend.

The +5.77% on September 21 also warrants a calmer read. There was no company-specific news that day; the entire memory sector rose, with foreign capital re-entry, short covering and quarter-end rebalancing all possible factors. A single-day surge in cyclical stocks is often a "bounce" rather than a "reversal"; the test is whether the price can hold above its moving averages for three consecutive days.

## Down 24%: valuation, not news

Decompose the three-month move: earnings upgrades contributed + (profit rose), multiple compression contributed − (valuation fell). Q2 operating profit rose 1814% YoY, yet the stock fell 24%. The only explanation: the valuation de-rated from "10x mid-cycle" to "7.5x cycle peak."

Is that de-rating reasonable? Versus history: in the 2018 cycle, Samsung fell 38% from its high while operating profit was still hitting new records; in the 2022 cycle it fell 40%, with the decline starting two quarters before profit peaked. This cycle's 24% drop is the shallowest of the three — a sign the market gives some credence to "structural AI memory demand."

In other words, −24% has already priced in "the cycle will turn," but not "how deep the turn will be." If 2027 brings merely flat prices (not a decline), the current price is cheap; if prices fall 30%, there is still 30%–40% downside. That is why the position can only be 5%, not 15%.

## What to watch next: three dates

Q3 preliminary guidance in early October is the first checkpoint. Consensus looks for operating profit above KRW 95 trillion. Below KRW 90 trillion would signal that price-hike pass-through has run its course, and the stock would step down again; above KRW 100 trillion would signal Q3 is still accelerating, with a near-term rebound to follow.

The formal Q3 report in late October is the second checkpoint. The focus is not profit but three structural data points: HBM as a share of memory revenue, whether the MX division stops losing money, and whether capex guidance is raised. A capex increase is bad news—it means more supply in 2028.

The HBM4E certification result in December is the third checkpoint and the most important of the year. A pass puts 2027 share at 40% and the bull case holds; a fail caps share at 33%, making the base case the ceiling.
