Core call: the "eliminate the weak" stage does not weed it out — it flags it

Six of seven hard criteria pass. The only failure is earnings stability: semiconductors lost KRW 4.36 trillion in Q2 2023 and earned KRW 89.2 trillion in Q2 2026, a twentyfold swing over three years. This failure is not fatal—it defines a cyclical stock, not an operational misstep—but it dictates every position rule that follows.

The other six pass easily: gross margin 69.6%, net margin 41.8%, annualized ROE about 24%, net cash, operating cash flow covering net profit, and interest coverage not applicable (no net interest expense). The purpose of negative screening is to exclude companies whose business itself is unsound; Samsung is not one of them. It belongs to the category of "sound business, difficult timing."

What do the six approvals show

All three profitability metrics clear the bar by a wide margin: gross margin 69.6% (threshold 40%), net margin 41.8% (threshold 10%), ROE about 24% (threshold 15%). This is not a bare pass — it is more than triple the required cushion. The driver is price: DRAM average selling price rose 44% quarter on quarter and NAND 53%, while costs barely moved, so the price gains flowed straight into gross profit.

The three balance-sheet metrics are even cleaner. Samsung has long been a net cash company, with net profit of KRW 118.7 trillion in the first half of 2026, and that money has not gone to repay debt — because there is no debt to repay. Operating cash flow covers net profit comfortably: memory is asset-heavy, with depreciation and amortization running above KRW 30 trillion a year, so adding D&A back to net profit leaves cash flow higher than earnings.

The conclusion of the negative screen is therefore clear: this is not a company that needs to be excluded. The seven hard metrics were designed to filter out names that look cheap but are value traps — high leverage, low gross margin, revenue propped up by receivables. Samsung matches none of these traits.

The only one that fails: profit stability

In Q2 2023, Samsung's semiconductor division posted an operating loss of KRW 4.36 trillion, one of the ugliest single-quarter figures in the company's history; three years later, in Q2 2026, the same division earned KRW 89.2 trillion. The peak-to-trough ratio exceeds 20x, failing any standard of "stability."

Why this is not fatal: the loss year was not an operating failure but industry-wide prices falling below cost. Samsung's DRAM share did not slip, its technology node did not fall behind, its customers did not leave; the price cycle simply hit bottom. Using "profit stability" to reject it is equivalent to using "no cycles allowed" to reject the entire memory industry.

But this must be flagged because it changes the valuation method. Stable companies can be valued on PE or DCF; cyclical companies only on PB, normalized earnings, or replacement cost. Every target price below uses cyclical multiples, not growth multiples, precisely because this test fails.

If only four had passed, the conclusion would be different.

Run a reverse test: assume gross margin of only 45%, net margin of 12%, and net debt of RMB 3 trillion. Even at a PE of just 7.5x, you should not buy it, because it would be the first to die in a downturn. Samsung is not that case; its net cash and 39.4% share are proof that it "won't die."

What truly warrants caution is a different combination: all seven criteria passed, but valuation at 20x PE. At that point it is a "good company at an expensive price," carrying more risk than now. Now it is a "good company at a cyclical peak, at a discounted price." The sources of risk differ, and so does the handling: the former means waiting for a pullback; the latter means buying in tranches and controlling position size.

Actions after weeding out the weak

Six of seven criteria passed, move to the next layer: is it worth buying, and at what price. These two questions are not answered by negative screening; they are answered by valuation and cycle position. The only conclusion negative screening delivers is this: it is worth spending time on.

Operationally, that conclusion means: allow it to take 3%–5% of the portfolio, allow adding on declines (because it is not a value trap), but do not take a heavy position just because "the PE is only 7.5x" (because the denominator is unsustainable). That is the difference between "flagging" and "eliminating".

Conclusion

Six of seven criteria passed; not eliminated, flagged as a cyclical name. The one failure (earnings stability) is not an operating issue but an industry characteristic—yet it locks the valuation method into the "PB + normalized earnings" framework.
Implication for action: it is buyable, and can be added to using a cyclical-stock approach, but it must not be sized like a growth stock.
