## Six items with zero deviation: the results themselves are uncontested

Revenue of KRW 171.5 trillion, operating profit of KRW 89.49 trillion, net profit of KRW 71.62 trillion, gross margin of 69.6%, memory revenue growth of +471% and DS operating profit of KRW 89.2 trillion — these six figures are identical across Chinese media, Korean newswires and the company press release, matching to one decimal place. The financial statements themselves therefore leave no room for manipulation, and there is no question of "which outlet got the math wrong."

Why so clean? Samsung's quarterly disclosure is granular: the consolidated income statement gives operating profit, the segment table gives the five major divisions, and memory revenue is broken out separately. The numbers come from the same table; the media merely copy them. The real risk is not whether the numbers are right, but how they are read.

## Share metrics: does a 1 percentage point difference matter?

On the 33% HBM share: Counterpoint puts it at 33%, some media cite 32% and others 34%. The discrepancy is methodological — revenue versus shipment bits, HBM3E and HBM4 combined or separate, and whether samples and mass-production shipments are included.

The conclusion: a 1pp difference does not change the judgment. Whether the starting point is 21% and the end point 32% or 34%, the direction is identical — "more than ten points gained in a single quarter" — and that is the decisive fact. I use 33% consistently throughout and flag the Counterpoint methodology at each citation.

The DRAM figure of 39.4% carries a smaller deviation (within 0.5pp), since TrendForce's DRAM revenue ranking is the industry-standard methodology and media outlets cite it directly. Note that this is a revenue basis, not a bit basis — in a price upcycle, revenue share naturally inflates, and Samsung's bit share is in fact somewhat below 39.4%.

## The real issue is definitional: Q1's figure is derived by subtraction

H1 net profit of KRW 118.7 trillion and Q2 net profit of KRW 71.62 trillion leave Q1 at just KRW 47.1 trillion — a figure never disclosed separately and derived by subtraction. Operating profit follows the same logic: Q1's KRW 57.4 trillion is back-solved from the company's "+756.1% YoY" and the 2025Q1 base.

Both derived figures matter. H1 operating profit of KRW 146.9 trillion, annualised to KRW 293.8 trillion, anchors the full-year forecast. A KRW 2 trillion gap between Q1 actuals and the back-solve shifts the full-year forecast by roughly KRW 4 trillion — about 1% of net profit in the KRW 350 trillion range. Acceptable, but the text must clearly flag these as derived figures.

## The three ROE measures: 24%, 30% and 40% are all correct

The working paper cites "ROE approaching 40% at the cycle peak." I calculate 24% on ending net assets — a wide gap that warrants explanation. The 40% figure uses average net assets for 2023–2025 as the denominator (bottom-of-cycle net assets depressed by losses); 30% uses end-2025 net assets; 24% uses end-2026Q2 net assets.

All three are correct, but they serve different purposes. Average net assets (40%) is the right basis for judging "how much this business can earn when times are good"; ending net assets (24%) is required to judge "how much return the price I pay today corresponds to," because what you buy today is today's net assets. This report uses 24% throughout and explains the difference where the Buffett perspective is involved.

## Final adopted methodology

All income statement figures are company-disclosed. Market share draws on third-party data (Counterpoint, TrendForce), with methodology noted; Q1 data is marked "derived". ROE assumes a period-end net asset base of 24%, and net assets of KRW 996 trillion are derived from 1.8x PB and marked "derived".

We use an exchange rate of 1,500 KRW/USD (the level around the report date; rough calculation), putting market cap of KRW 1,793 trillion at approximately USD 1.20 trillion. All cross-market comparisons (with SK hynix, Micron, TSMC) convert market cap into USD first, so that exchange-rate error does not contaminate the conclusions.
