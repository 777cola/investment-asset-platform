## Title and standfirst (ready to use)

**Title: A KRW 89 trillion quarterly profit and the stock is down 24% — what is wrong with Samsung**

**Subtitle:** HBM share rose from 21% to 33% in a single quarter, DRAM ranks first globally, and HBM4 reached mass production first. There is no crack in the fundamentals; the crack is in the valuation.

**Lead:** On September 21, 2026, Samsung Electronics closed at KRW 275,000, up 5.77%, still 24% below its July 52-week high of KRW 362,500. Its second-quarter report had been out for nearly two months: revenue of KRW 171.5 trillion, operating profit of KRW 89.49 trillion and net profit of KRW 71.62 trillion, all three at record highs. **Earnings at records, the stock at a discount — the classic picture of a cyclical stock.**

## Main text 1: What RMB 89 trillion means

First, how big this number is. KRW 89.49 trillion, about USD 59.7 billion at KRW 1,500/USD — in a single quarter. Samsung earned more in one quarter than it did across all of 2023 to 2025 combined.

Gross margin 69.6%, operating margin 52.2%, net margin 41.8%. **All three are extremes for global manufacturing** — TSMC's gross margin over the same period was 67.7%, already called a historic peak for foundry, and Samsung is two points above that. Yet Samsung sells memory chips, theoretically a commodity.

Why so profitable? In one word: **shortage.** DRAM average selling prices rose 44% quarter on quarter, NAND 53%, with bit shipments up only slightly. Memory revenue rose 471% year on year, almost entirely from price. This is not management improvement or a technology breakthrough; a supply-demand gap has pushed prices to levels unseen in two decades.

## Body 2: What really matters is the 33%

Look at the 89 trillion alone and you would conclude this is a one-off windfall. But one line in the report is worth more than the 89 trillion: **HBM market share of 33%, up from 21% last quarter.**

HBM is high-bandwidth memory for AI chips; each GPU takes 6–8 of them. Its gross margin runs 15–20 percentage points above ordinary DRAM, and switching suppliers forces customers through more than 12 months of joint qualification—**the only product in memory with genuine "customer relationship" characteristics.**

From 2024 to 2025, Samsung trailed SK hynix in this segment by a full year, Nvidia's orders went almost entirely to its rival, and the market briefly wrote it off in AI memory. In 2026 it has reversed course to become the world's first maker to ship HBM4 in volume, recovering 12 percentage points of share in a single quarter.

The point is not "12 points more sold" but that **"both engineering capability and speed of correction remain intact."** Prices will give back; capability stays.

## Main text 3: Handset losses are a signal

One inconspicuous number in the earnings report: the mobile division posted KRW 33.2 trillion in revenue and KRW 0.7 trillion in operating loss. Phone sales were not bad; the loss came from memory costs — **Samsung raised its own prices and ate its own mobile division's profit.**

This is intra-group price transfer within the same company, invisible in consolidated statements. But it sends an important signal: **memory price increases are starting to squeeze downstream.** When memory costs for a flagship phone rise from 12% of bill-of-materials cost to over 20%, phone makers have only two choices — raise prices or cut specs. Samsung chose to absorb it; Apple and Xiaomi can only raise prices or cut specs.

For judging where the cycle stands, this is more direct than any industry report: **when upstream price increases start to squeeze downstream, the turning point is near.** Every historical memory cycle began with this signal.

## Body 4: So should you buy at KRW 275,000?

Three sentences.

First, **the company is fine**. Share is rising, HBM4 leads, DRAM ranks first globally, and the balance sheet is net cash. This is not a company with cracks.

Second, **the price is not expensive, but not cheap either**. 7.5x PE is based on annualized first-half profit, which looks cheap; 1.8x PB is based on net assets, which looks normal. The gap between the two numbers is the definition of a "cycle top."

Third, **the denominator is at the top**. A single-quarter profit of KRW 89 trillion is unsustainable; once prices turn down in 2027, the PE will rise on its own. The market's 7.5x is not a mispricing—it is a discount priced in advance for a cyclical peak.

So the conclusion is: **buy, but buy it as a cyclical stock.** Build one-third at the current price, add a second tranche below KRW 240,000, and wait for the third tranche until October's Q3 guidance and December's HBM4E certification. Total position no more than 5%; once 2027 DRAM contract prices turn down, cut half the position, regardless of profit or loss.

## Closing: one unpalatable remark

Buying Samsung is not buying AI; it is buying **the DRAM price cycle + HBM share catch-up**. Both can be verified, and both can fail.

If you cannot say where these two variables stand right now, you should not buy it, no matter how low the PE. **The most expensive moment for a cyclical stock is often when its PE is lowest.**
