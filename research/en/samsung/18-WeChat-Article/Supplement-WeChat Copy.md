## Title and standfirst (ready to use)

Title: A KRW 89 trillion quarterly profit and the stock is down 24% — what is wrong with Samsung

Subtitle: HBM share rose from 21% to 33% in a single quarter, DRAM ranks first globally, and HBM4 reached mass production first. The fundamentals show no crack; the crack is in the valuation.

Lead: Samsung Electronics closed at KRW 275,000 on September 21, 2026, up 5.77% but still 24% below its July 52-week high of KRW 362,500. Its second-quarter report had been out for nearly two months: revenue of KRW 171.5 trillion, operating profit of KRW 89.49 trillion and net profit of KRW 71.62 trillion, all three at record highs. Earnings at records, the stock at a discount — the classic picture of a cyclical stock.

## Main text 1: What RMB 89 trillion means

Start with the scale: KRW 89.49 trillion, about USD 59.7 billion at KRW 1,500/USD — in a single quarter. Samsung earned more in one quarter than it did across all of 2023 to 2025 combined.

Gross margin 69.6%, operating margin 52.2%, net margin 41.8%. All three are extremes for global manufacturing — TSMC's gross margin over the same period was 67.7%, already hailed as a historic peak for foundry, and Samsung sits two points above that. Yet Samsung sells memory chips, theoretically a commodity.

Why so profitable? In one word: shortage. DRAM average selling prices rose 44% QoQ and NAND 53%, with bit shipments up only slightly. Memory revenue rose 471% YoY, almost entirely on price. This is not management improvement or a technology breakthrough; a supply-demand gap has pushed prices to levels unseen in two decades.

## Body 2: What really matters is the 33%

An 89 trillion figure alone would suggest a one-off windfall. But one line in the report matters more than the 89 trillion: HBM market share of 33%, up from 21% last quarter.

HBM is high-bandwidth memory for AI chips, with each GPU carrying 6–8 of them. Its gross margin runs 15–20 percentage points above ordinary DRAM, and switching suppliers puts customers through more than 12 months of joint qualification—the only product in memory with genuine "customer relationship" characteristics.

Through 2024 and 2025 Samsung trailed SK hynix in this segment by a full year, Nvidia's orders went almost entirely to its rival, and the market briefly wrote it off in AI memory. In 2026 it has reversed course to become the world's first maker to ship HBM4 in volume, recovering 12 percentage points of share in a single quarter.

The point is not "12 points more sold" but that "both engineering capability and speed of correction remain intact." Pricing will give back; capability stays.

## Main text 3: Handset losses are a signal

One number buried in the earnings report stands out: the mobile division posted KRW 33.2 trillion in revenue and KRW 0.7 trillion in operating loss. Phone sales were not the problem — the loss came from memory costs. Samsung raised its own prices and ate its own mobile division's profit.

This is an intra-group transfer price within a single company, invisible in consolidated statements. But it sends an important signal: memory price increases are starting to squeeze downstream. When memory costs for a flagship phone rise from 12% of bill-of-materials cost to over 20%, phone makers have only two choices — raise prices or cut specs. Samsung chose to absorb it; Apple and Xiaomi can only raise prices or cut specs.

For judging where the cycle stands, this is more telling than any industry report: when upstream price increases start to squeeze downstream, the turning point is near. Every historical memory cycle began with this signal.

## Body 4: So should you buy at KRW 275,000?

Three points.

First, the company is fine. Share is rising, HBM4 leads, DRAM ranks first globally, and the balance sheet is net cash. There are no cracks here.

Second, the price is not expensive, but not cheap either. 7.5x P/E on annualised first-half profit looks cheap; 1.8x P/B on net assets looks normal. That gap is the definition of a cycle top.

Third, the denominator is at the top. A single-quarter profit of KRW 89 trillion is unsustainable; once prices turn down in 2027, the P/E will rise on its own. The market's 7.5x is not a mispricing—it is a discount priced in advance for a cyclical peak.

The conclusion: buy, but buy it as a cyclical stock. Build one-third at the current price, add a second tranche below KRW 240,000, and hold the third until October's Q3 guidance and December's HBM4E certification. Cap the total position at 5%; once 2027 DRAM contract prices turn down, cut half the position, regardless of profit or loss.

## Closing: one unpalatable remark

Buying Samsung is not a bet on AI; it is a bet on the DRAM price cycle plus HBM share catch-up. Both are verifiable, and both can fail.

If you cannot say where those two variables stand right now, you should not buy the stock, however low the P/E. The most expensive moment for a cyclical is often when its P/E is at its lowest.
