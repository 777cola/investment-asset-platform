Six-Gate Screen — Samsung Electronics

Core call: the business and share are both improving; the only issue is the denominator implied by the purchase price

Six gates 43/60, rating "Buy (staged)". The score is not evenly distributed—the first three gates score near full marks, the fourth gate loses points, and the fifth gate scores only 5. The first three gates' points come from facts: 2026Q2 revenue KRW 171.5 trillion, operating profit KRW 89.49 trillion, gross margin 69.6%, DRAM share 39.4% ranking first globally, HBM share rising from 21% to 33% in one quarter, and HBM4 the first to ship in volume.

The fourth gate loses points on history: in 2024–2025 HBM3E certification trailed SK hynix by a year—the lag is structural. The fifth gate loses points on arithmetic: the 7.5x PE denominator is profit after DRAM average prices rose 44% QoQ, and on a normalized basis it is not cheap.The conclusion is that it is buyable, but buy it as a cyclical—staged, position-capped, and with the 2027 price downturn written into the base case rather than treated as a black swan.

Gate one: circle of competence — all three lines are understandable; the hard part is the cycle position

Samsung's money comes from three places: selling DRAM and NAND at contract prices to server, phone and PC makers; selling leading-edge capacity by the wafer to design companies; and selling phones and TVs by the unit to consumers. Three sentences and you're done — no technical background required. This gate scores 9; the 1 point deducted is not for failing to understand the business but for failing to understand the cycle.

Judging where memory stocks sit requires three data sets: contract price change quarter on quarter, channel inventory in weeks, and the capex cadence of the big three. All three are available, but all three lag — contract prices are published monthly, and by the time you see them turn down, the stock has already fallen 20%. So "understanding the company" and "understanding the stock price" are two different things, and the edge of the circle of competence lies in the latter.

Foundry is messier: 2nm yield, customer mix, the ramp at the Taylor fab — from the outside you can only infer these from order changes at Nvidia, Qualcomm and Tesla. I rate myself "half competent" here, so none of the valuation that follows prices foundry separately; I treat it as an option.

Stage two: a good business — the 69.6% gross margin is a reading of oligopoly pricing

2026Q2 gross margin 69.6%, versus 34.2% a year earlier. Gross margin up 35 percentage points in a year is not management improvement, it is price. DRAM average selling price rose 44% QoQ and NAND 53%; with all three suppliers running at full utilization and still short of supply, pricing power sits entirely with the seller.

A good business meets three tests: customers cannot do without it, competitors cannot get in, and the seller sets the price. Memory satisfied the first two in 2017–2018, when Samsung's peak quarterly operating profit was KRW 17.6 trillion; this cycle it reached KRW 89.49 trillion, five times that level. The difference is HBM: the same wafer sold as HBM to Nvidia generates four to five times the revenue of ordinary DDR5.

The 2-point deduction reflects unstable cash flow. A good business should still earn money in a downturn; memory cannot: the semiconductor division lost KRW 4.36 trillion in 2023Q2 and earned KRW 89.2 trillion in 2026Q2. A twentyfold swing in three years makes this a commodity, not a consumer product.

Stage three: the moat lies in cost and capacity, not technological leadership

DRAM revenue share 39.4%, 14.5 percentage points above second-place SK hynix at 24.9%; HBM share 33%, up 12 percentage points from 21% last quarter; HBM4 is the world's first to ship in mass production. Taken together, these three numbers show it has at least not fallen behind.

But the nature of the moat needs to be clear. Samsung's advantage is that it "can afford to lose": vertical integration (in-house procurement of materials other than equipment, in-house packaging, its own end devices to absorb capacity), a net-cash balance sheet, and the largest single-fab scale in the world. When prices fall below cash cost, it can hold out until rivals cut output first, then trade share for the next cycle. This moat guarantees it survives, not that it is immune to price cuts.

Its technology lead is "six months", not "three years". It trailed SK hynix by about a year on HBM3E, has moved half a step ahead on HBM4, and is still awaiting results on 12-layer verification for HBM4E. So the third gate scores 8, with the 2-point deduction for technology uncertainty.

Gate four: management — quick to correct mistakes, but the half-step lag is structural

From 2024 to the first half of 2025, Samsung trailed SK hynix by a full year on HBM3E, with Nvidia awarding almost all orders to its rival. The reasons are not complicated: yields were resolved late, internal qualification took long, and the DS division changed leadership (Jun Young-hyun took over in May 2024). A decision that could have been made in one quarter took three.

2026 reverses this: first to mass-produce HBM4, share recovering from 21% to 33%, and the Taylor fab's 2nm progressing on schedule. This shows the capacity for course correction remains, but the method is "change people + spend more," not "make processes faster." The same half-step-behind will recur at the next technology transition.

Another layer is capital allocation. First-half 2026 net profit reached KRW 118.7 trillion, yet dividends remain at the KRW 9.8 trillion floor, a payout ratio of about 4%. The money stayed on the company's books rather than returning to shareholders—at the top of the cycle this is conservative, and possibly wasteful. This item should be read alongside the sixth gate, so it scores only 6.

Gate Five: Margin of safety — what is cheap is the PE, not the business

7.5x PE, 1.8x PB, market cap KRW 1,793 trillion (about USD 1.20 trillion). On the numbers alone, this is one of the cheapest large tech companies in the world: on the same 2026Q2 profit, Nvidia's valuation is more than six times its own, TSMC's four times.

The problem is the denominator. Of the KRW 89.49 trillion in single-quarter operating profit, the vast majority is price contribution — memory bit shipments grew only modestly, yet revenue rose +471% year on year. Prices can turn; capacity does not disappear. If DRAM contract prices fall 30% in 2027, profit on the same capacity drops below KRW 40 trillion, and 7.5x instantly becomes more than 15x.

So the margin of safety does not score "cheap"; it scores "downside is cushioned": backing out from 1.8x PB gives net assets of about KRW 996 trillion, or book value per share of about KRW 153,000. The bear-case target price of KRW 110,000 implies 0.72x PB — Samsung has never fallen below 0.8x PB in its history. This is not cheap valuation; it is a floor provided by the balance sheet.

Gate 6: Discipline — can the buy thesis be falsified?

The question this gate asks is: if three months from now prove me wrong, will I know where I went wrong. For Samsung the answer is yes: HBM share (reported quarterly), DRAM contract prices (reported monthly), DS division margin (reported quarterly) — all three metrics can falsify my thesis.

The reason to buy is not "AI will keep growing" but two specific things: HBM share continuing to climb from 33%, and HBM4E entering Nvidia's mass-production list within the year. The first is already in the data; the second is yet to be verified. If both fail, the investment should be exited, regardless of whether the stock goes up or down.

The 3-point deduction is because discipline with cyclical stocks is hard to keep: when it rises, it is easy to forget it is a cyclical; when it falls, it is easy to mistake the cycle for growth. So the entry rules must be written down upfront — three tranches, at least one month apart, with a hard cap on total position size.

Veto items and items to be verified

Veto items: none. None of the six gates is a hard veto — that is what separates this from a theme stock: not "a good story," but "real profit, real share, real cash."

Three items remain to be verified. ① Q3 preliminary guidance in early October: the market expects operating profit above KRW 95 trillion; below KRW 90 trillion would signal that price-hike pass-through has run its course. ② Whether HBM4E 12-layer clears Nvidia certification within the year: the precondition for share to reach 40% in 2027. ③ Whether foundry actually turns profitable after excluding one-off performance bonuses: disclosure is opaque, so the only read is next quarter's internal DS structure.

The biggest risk is not the company, it is the price. The current market cap is under 5x expected 2027 profit, and that number is only cheap if "no shortage in 2027" holds. Once contract prices turn down in the first half, 5x becomes 15x. That is why the rating is "Buy (staged)" rather than "Buy."

Conclusion

Six gates 43/60, Buy (staged). The scores on the first three gates are solid, and so is the 5 points on the fifth gate: at its best, this business at this price is cheap for a cyclical, but what is cheap is a denominator that is not sustainable.
Action: open a first position of one-third at the current price, add a second tranche on a pullback below KRW 240,000; Q3 guidance and HBM4E certification are the two checkpoints. If DRAM contract prices turn down in 2027, cut the position by half regardless of profit or loss.
