## Notes

Financial data comes from Samsung Electronics' 2026Q2 report (2026/07/30); the 2027 net profit of KRW 350 trillion is this report's neutral assumption (implying operating profit of about KRW 425 trillion and a net margin of 48.6%), not company guidance; the three-scenario multiples of 4–6.5x reference the actual valuation ranges of the 2018 and 2022 downturns.

ROE basis: this report uses 24% on ending equity (annualized net profit of KRW 237.4 trillion ÷ equity of about KRW 996 trillion). On 2023–2025 average equity, ROE approaches 40%, which measures "how much the business earns when times are good"; the purchase price corresponds to the former.

Historical drawdown data: Samsung fell about 40% from its peak in 2022 and about 38% in 2018, based on public price history and not precise to the day.

## Duan Yongping: You can understand a business without being able to value it

Duan Yongping would ask three questions first. Will DRAM still be needed in ten years? Yes, and more than today — AI training, inference and on-device models all need memory. Will Samsung still be in the top three in ten years? Most likely, since it has the lowest cost and the largest capacity of the three. The third question he could not get past: how much will it earn in a year ten years from now?

Memory profit is a function of price, not of capability. In Q2 2023 the semiconductor division lost KRW 4.36 trillion; in Q2 2026 it earned KRW 89.2 trillion — same fabs, same engineers, the only difference is contract prices.Duan Yongping's stance on this kind of business has always been clear: he can understand it, but he cannot value it, so he only buys when it is "cheap enough that no valuation is needed."

Does KRW 275,000 count as "no valuation needed"? His standard is roughly a market cap below net current assets or replacement cost. Samsung's market cap is KRW 1,793 trillion against net assets of KRW 996 trillion, a PB of 1.8x — still half a turn away from "no valuation needed."So he would say: good company, I'll wait for a better price, or I'll buy a little as tuition.

## Buffett: A cyclical stock at 1.8x PB—cheap enough, but not good enough

Buffett would not value Samsung on PE; he would flip to the right side of the balance sheet. Net cash, no net interest-bearing debt, 1.8x PB, annualized ROE of 24% — a respectable set of numbers for a cyclical. He would also note one thing: Samsung's DRAM share rose from 33% to 39.4% over three years, showing it was still gaining share when the industry was at its worst. That is the classic "strong get stronger."

But he would stop at two points. The first is pricing power: a true moat is "the ability to raise prices without losing customers." Samsung can raise prices in 2026 because of shortages, not because customers cannot do without it. The second is capex: memory consumes tens of trillions of Korean won a year on equipment, and most of the profit must be reinvested, leaving free cash flow far below net income.

His logic would run roughly: "This is a good company, but the industry it operates in means even good companies earn only average returns." If he bought, he would wait for PB to fall below 1.2x — roughly around KRW 190,000.

## Munger: invert the question — what would make it halve?

Munger does not forecast upside; he only asks about downside. Samsung has fallen from 275,000 to 140,000 — what would it take to go further? Three triggers: (1) DRAM contract prices turn down in 2027, and the market reprices the stock on downcycle earnings; (2) CXMT ramps DDR5 at scale and breaks consumer DRAM pricing; (3) SK hynix beats Samsung to HBM4E certification again, leaving its share stuck at 33%.

If any one of these occurs, a 40%–50% decline is the historical norm: Samsung fell 40% from its peak in 2022 and 38% in the 2018 memory downturn.In other words, the current -24% is just "one leg down," not "the bottom."

He would also ask the reverse: under what conditions does it not fall? The answer is "the shortage lasts into 2028." That answer depends on capex at the four cloud players, and their capex depends on whether AI revenue materializes.The chain is too long, and Munger would not pay for it.His conclusion is usually "too hard — put it in the too-hard basket."

## Li Lu: AI memory is a structural change, but it shifts the center, not the volatility

Li Lu would look at a longer layer: how this demand cycle differs from the 2017 one. 2017 was phone replacement plus data-center restocking, one-off demand; 2026 is rigid demand for memory bandwidth from AI training and inference—HBM's share of DRAM wafer capacity rising from under 5% to over 20%, each GPU needs 6–8 HBM stacks; this is a structural change, not an inventory cycle.

A structural change means the profit center has been raised: over the past decade Samsung's quarterly operating profit centered around KRW 10 trillion; this cycle, even if prices fall 30%, the center may land at KRW 30–40 trillion. That is why he would pay a premium for Samsung—not paying for this year's KRW 89 trillion, but for "the bottom of the next downturn being higher than the last."

But he would equally point to the boundary: structural demand changes the center, not the volatility. Memory remains a capex-driven, three-year-cycle business. For long-term holders, Samsung's real value is that it is the only player in the industry with memory, foundry and end devices all at once—the most resilient, at the cost of never capturing the elasticity of a pure memory company.

## Deliberation: how four votes become one action

Put four people at one table to vote: Duan Yongping votes "wait a bit longer," Buffett votes "talk to me at 1.2x PB," Munger votes "too hard," and Li Lu votes "worth paying a small premium for a core asset." Three of the four votes say "don't buy now," one says "you can buy" — that is not a directly executable conclusion.

The key to the verdict is whether they are rejecting the same thing. Duan Yongping and Munger reject "the valuation method doesn't hold"; Buffett rejects "the price isn't low enough"; only Li Lu is answering "has the long-term value of this business changed." In other words, all three are rejecting the price; none is rejecting the company. Price can be solved with tranches and patience; the company cannot.

So the action is designed in three stages: the first buys now, accepting the risk of buying at the cycle top, using one-third of the position to buy "not being absent"; the second waits for below KRW 240,000 (1.55x PB), a discount to Buffett's 1.2x PB; the third goes to full position only when both conditions hold — HBM4E certification passes and supply is still tight in 2027. The virtue of this design: whoever is right, I am never entirely wrong.

One reverse discipline must also be written down: if the stock rises above KRW 330,000 within three months, do not add, do not chase, and instead cut half of the first tranche. The easiest way to lose money in cyclicals is to be persuaded by "this time is different" when earnings are at their best and to size the position to the maximum. Of the four people's consensus, this is the only undisputed point.

## Odds assessment: 45% upside, 60% downside — why still buy

Neutral-case target price KRW 268,000, almost equal to the current price; bull case 399,000 (+45%), bear case 110,000 (-60%). On these three numbers alone, the risk/reward is negative — the standard shape of a cyclical stock at the top, where upside is capped by "unsustainable profit" and downside is opened up by "the moment prices turn down."

So why still rate it "Buy (staged)"? Three reasons. First, the probability distribution is not uniform: HBM share went from 21% to 33% in a single quarter, and if that slope continues, the bull case carries no small probability. Second, the downside has net-asset support: the bear case of KRW 110,000 corresponds to 0.72x PB, and Samsung has never broken below 0.8x in its history — meaning -60% is a theoretical figure and actual downside may be only -35%. Third, staged entry spreads the cost to around 250,000, giving a positive return even in the neutral case.

Conversely, if the price rises above 330,000 (2.2x PB, 6x 2027), this risk/reward no longer holds — at that point one should trim, not chase.
