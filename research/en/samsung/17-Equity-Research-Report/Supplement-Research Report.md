## Chapter 1 Company Overview: A Memory Company Disguised as Consumer Electronics

Samsung Electronics was founded in 1969, is headquartered in Suwon, and is listed on the Korea Exchange (005930), with total share capital of about 6.52 billion shares (including preferred shares) and a market cap of KRW 1,793 trillion (about USD 1.20 trillion). Its business is divided into four major divisions: **semiconductor (DS), mobile and network (MX), display (SDC), and TV and appliances (CE).**

Revenue structure in 2026Q2: DS KRW 127.5 trillion (74.3%), MX KRW 33.2 trillion (19.4%), CE KRW 14.5 trillion (8.5%), SDC KRW 7.5 trillion (4.4%). **The profit structure is even more extreme: DS KRW 89.2 trillion, accounting for 99.7%, while the other three divisions combined were negative.**

On ownership, Lee Jae-yong maintains control through the Samsung C&T—Samsung Life—Samsung Electronics shareholding chain, and foreign investors hold about 50%, making them the main marginal buyers in this rally. **The company has long maintained net cash and has no net interest-bearing debt.**

On production footprint, four domestic campuses in Korea—Pyeongtaek, Hwaseong, Giheung, and Cheonan—carry the main memory and foundry capacity; overseas there are three bases: Xi'an, China (NAND), Austin, USA (foundry), and Taylor, USA (2nm). **The Xi'an plant is the main NAND production base and is also where geopolitical risk exposure lies.** R&D staff have long accounted for more than 20% of employees, and R&D expense in 2026Q2 was KRW 16 trillion, +78% year on year.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis: Oligopoly, AI Demand, Cycle Position

DRAM is an oligopoly: Samsung 39.4%, SK hynix 24.9%, Micron 23.3%, CXMT 9.5%. Global DRAM industry revenue rose 59.5% QoQ in Q2 2026 (TrendForce), the sixth quarter of this upcycle.

Demand is AI-driven: one AI server carries 8–10x the DRAM capacity of a general-purpose server, with 6–8 HBM stacks per GPU. On the supply side, the constraint is the capacity build cycle (2–3 years); no one expanded in 2023–2024, leaving shortages in 2026. **HBM's share of DRAM wafer capacity has risen from under 5% to over 20%, further squeezing conventional DRAM supply.**

Cycle positioning: the QoQ rise in DRAM contract prices narrowed from over 60% in Q1 to 44% in Q2, and all three suppliers raised capex in tandem — two signals pointing to a "peak zone"; HBM's share is still climbing — one signal supporting an "extended plateau." **Conclusion: H2 2026 to H1 2027 is a high-level plateau; H2 2027 is a high-risk inflection window.**

NAND is the other front, and easily overlooked. NAND contract prices rose more than 100% in 2026, with Samsung first at roughly 30% share, followed by Kioxia, SK hynix (including Solidigm) and Western Digital. **NAND's technology roadmap is moving from 176 layers to over 300 layers, and Samsung leads on stack count, but gross margins on this line are below DRAM, limiting its contribution to consolidated profit.**

## Chapter 3 Business model: three revenue lines, one determines profit

Memory sells dies on quarterly contract prices, HBM sells stacks on annual long-term agreements, and foundry sells capacity by wafer. **The three lines have different pricing mechanisms, and 2026 profit comes almost entirely from the first line.**

On unit economics, HBM consumes more than three times the wafer area per GB of DDR5, commands a unit price four to five times higher, and earns a gross margin 15–20 percentage points higher. **Every 5 percentage point increase in the HBM mix raises blended memory gross margin by about 1 percentage point.**

Depreciation accounts for 30–40% of the cost structure, and utilization amplifies gross margin swings more than price does—which explains why memory makers prefer to cut prices and run full utilization during gluts. Samsung's advantage is the largest single-fab scale and the strongest depreciation absorption: **on the same 30% price decline, its gross margin falls 5–8 percentage points less than peers'.**

On customer structure, Samsung does not disclose single-customer concentration, but the supply chain allows an estimate: the largest HBM customer is Nvidia, mainstream DRAM customers are the four major cloud providers and handset makers, and NAND customers are handset makers and module houses. **Customer concentration is highest in HBM—orders from Nvidia alone can affect more than 15% of Samsung's memory revenue (rough estimate).** That is both a source of premium and a source of risk.

One more point on the limits of pricing power: Samsung's pricing power in memory shows up only in the magnitude of quarterly contract price increases, not in the benchmark price. The benchmark is set by the supply-demand curve; when prices rise it rises with them, and when prices fall it can only choose between cutting prices to chase volume or cutting output to defend price. **In 2026 it chose to follow prices up; in 2027, if demand weakens, it will most likely cut output to defend price—that is what separates it from pure commodity producers.**

## Chapter 4 Financial analysis: real profits, intercepted cash, clean balance sheet

2026Q2 gross margin 69.6% (34.2% in 2025Q2), operating margin 52.2%, net margin 41.8%, effective tax rate 20.0%, no one-off gains (FX gain 3.1 trillion, 3.5% of operating profit). **Profit quality is clean, but with no "other income" to prop it up — all of it comes from the core business.**

Cash flow is being intercepted by capex: net profit 71.62 trillion, and operating cash flow is higher after adding back depreciation, but Pyeongtaek P4/P5, the Taylor fab and dedicated HBM lines are all under construction at once, so free cash flow is far below net profit. **First-half net profit 118.7 trillion, dividends only 9.8 trillion (annualized), payout ratio about 4%.**

Balance sheet: net assets of about KRW 996 trillion (backed out from 1.8x PB), net cash, no net interest-bearing debt. **This balance sheet guarantees it needs no financing in any downturn, and explains why it dares to expand capacity counter-cyclically.**

Two more details on the balance sheet are worth watching. First, inventory: in a rising-price cycle, low-cost wafers and finished goods generate inventory valuation gains, which reverse into write-downs when prices turn down; the quarterly report does not disclose inventory turnover days, which is an information gap. Second, depreciation: 2026 capex is at a historical high, and depreciation will concentrate in 2027–2028; **even if prices stay flat, rising depreciation will shave 3–5 percentage points off the operating margin.**

## Chapter 5 Management: First-class execution, zero checks and balances

Chairman Lee Jae-yong (took office October 2022) sets strategy and capex; DS division head Jun Young-hyun (took office May 2024) leads the HBM catch-up; MX's Roh Tae-moon and CFO Park Hark-kyu handle execution. **The decision chain is short and execution fast, but checks and balances are missing — falling a year behind on HBM3E in 2024–2025 is the price of a chain that is too long.**

Capital allocation record: Harman for USD 8 billion in 2016 (strategic promise unfulfilled), the Taylor fab in 2021 (on track for 2026), the KRW 10 trillion buyback in November 2024 (clear value creation), dividends of no less than KRW 9.8 trillion in 2024–2026 (fixed amount, payout ratio passively falls to 4%).

Governance: the 2017 bribery case (special pardon in 2022), the 2015 merger case (acquitted on appeal in February 2025), the first large-scale union strike in 2024 (agreement reached in 2025). **None of the three events affected competitiveness; they affected the valuation discount — one reason Samsung's PE has long trailed TSMC's.**

Organizationally, the three divisions (DX, DS, SDC) each hold independent P&L and capex authority, while the group-level Future Technology Institute makes technology bets three to five years out. **The problem with this structure is coordination: HBM requires storage, packaging and foundry to work together, and if any one division is slow, the whole is slow — that is the lesson of 2024–2025.** Upgrading HBM to a company-wide project in 2026 is the patch for this problem.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers: Three Engines, One Option

Engine one: **HBM share**. 33%→40% is the biggest profit variable for 2027, corresponding to roughly KRW 10 trillion in annual incremental profit (rough calculation). The premise is that HBM4E's 12-layer passes Nvidia certification before December.

Engine two: **AI server DRAM capacity**. DRAM capacity doubles with each server platform generation — this is volume growth that does not depend on price increases. Even if prices stay flat in 2027, bit shipments still grow 15%–20%.

Engine three: **foundry turning profitable**. 2nm customer adoption and the Taylor fab ramp; if it turns profitable in 2027, it could add KRW 2 trillion–3 trillion to consolidated profit and lift the valuation center. **This is an option, not an engine — it should be priced at zero today.**

There are two more underappreciated growth drivers. First, automotive-grade memory: smart driving requires 5–8x the DRAM and NAND capacity of internal-combustion vehicles, and Samsung's automotive-grade products have already entered the supply chains of Tesla and most German automakers. **This business carries higher gross margins than consumer-grade and has smaller cyclical swings.** Second, on-device AI: phones and PCs are starting to run models locally, doubling memory capacity requirements, and Samsung is the only manufacturer supplying both memory and end devices.

## Chapter 7 Competitive landscape: three players coexist, China sets the floor price

The three DRAM players combined hold 87.6%; the three HBM players combined hold 101% (definitional differences); in NAND, Samsung ranks first at about 30%; in foundry, TSMC leads decisively with 67%. **This is a stable oligopoly, not an industry that will be disrupted.**

Samsung's position: first in memory, second in HBM, third in foundry, first in handsets. **An all-round player: strongest in none, but alive in every segment.**

The most dangerous competitor is not SK hynix (which captures the premium), but ChangXin Memory (CXMT), which drives down the floor price. CXMT now holds 9.5% share, trails by two technology generations and prices 20%–30% cheaper. **It will turn Samsung's commodity DRAM from "profitable" into "thin-margin", but will not affect HBM or server DRAM.**

The competitive landscape in NAND and foundry needs to be spelled out. NAND is a five-player contest (Samsung, Kioxia, SK, Western Digital, Micron), with lower concentration than DRAM and more frequent price wars; foundry is dominated by TSMC alone (67%), with Samsung at 10% and SMIC at 6%. **Samsung is not a price-setter in either: NAND follows industry pricing, and foundry follows TSMC's capacity.** Their valuation contribution should therefore be lower than that of the core memory business.

## Chapter 8 Risk Warnings: Two Require Position Changes, Three Simply Need to Be Accepted

Price risk (high probability, high impact): DRAM contract prices turn down in 2027, and operating profit could fall 50%–70%. **Observable: TrendForce monthly contract prices; two consecutive months of declines confirm it.**

Qualification risk (medium probability, medium impact): HBM4E 12-layer fails Nvidia qualification, share stalls at 33%, valuation gap of KRW 130,000. **Observable: one-off announcement.**

Acceptance-type risks: CXMT ramping volume (chronic, suppresses the price floor), geopolitics/tariffs (low probability, high impact, China capacity accounts for more than half), governance discount (slow variable, valuation persistently 30%–50% below TSMC). **None of the three changes the judgment that "the company will not die"; they only change valuation.**

Two more risks are easily overlooked. First, single-quarter volatility: memory quarterly profit can swing ±50% quarter on quarter, and any earnings miss will bring share-price volatility of more than 10%. **This tests holders' psychological tolerance, not a negation of the company's quality.** Second, the inventory cycle: once channel inventory exceeds 8 weeks, prices begin to soften. Third parties track this data, but with a one-month lag.

## Chapter 9 Earnings forecast: peak in 2026, held up by mix in 2027

2026E: revenue 685 trillion, operating profit 345 trillion, net profit 275 trillion. Assumptions are Q3 operating profit 95 trillion (consensus), Q4 103 trillion, full-year operating margin 50.4%.

2027E: revenue 720 trillion, operating profit 425 trillion, net profit 365 trillion. **This forecast appears to show 2027 higher than 2026, contradicting the "cycle peak" thesis — the explanation: first-half 2026 operating profit was only 146.9 trillion (Q1 57.4 + Q2 89.49), dragging down the full year; 2027 is at high levels throughout, with a higher HBM mix.**

Key assumptions: DRAM contract prices flat in first-half 2027, down 10% in the second half; HBM share 35% (neutral); foundry near breakeven. **If prices turn down in first-half 2027, 2027E net profit must be cut to below 250 trillion.**

On sensitivity, price is the most important variable: **every 10% change in DRAM contract prices moves operating profit by about 25%** (rigid costs); every 10% change in bit shipments moves operating profit by about 12%; every 5 percentage point change in HBM share moves operating profit by about 3%. Of the three variables, price has the strongest explanatory power and is the hardest to forecast — a limitation shared by all memory-stock forecasts.

## Chapter 10 Valuation: PB Defines the Floor, Normalized Profit Defines the Center

Three methods cross-checked: **PB method** — net assets KRW 996 trillion, per-share KRW 152,800, historical range 0.8–2.6x, current 1.8x sits above the midpoint; **normalized PE method** — normalized quarterly operating profit KRW 25–35 trillion, full-year net profit KRW 85–120 trillion, 8–10x corresponds to KRW 104,000–184,000; **cyclical multiple method** — 2027E net profit KRW 350 trillion × 5x = KRW 268,000.

The three methods produce a wide range (104,000–268,000); **the reason is differing definitions of "normalized profit"**: count HBM's structural improvement into the norm and the midpoint rises; use the 2015–2024 historical norm and the midpoint falls.

This report adopts the cyclical multiple method as primary (268,000 base, 399,000 bull, 110,000 bear), with the PB method as downside protection (0.72x corresponds to 110,000). **The current price of 275,000 sits near the base-case target, with upside from HBM share and downside cushioned by net assets.**

Historical valuation range: Samsung PB has oscillated between 0.8–2.6x over the past decade, median about 1.4x; PE has oscillated between 6–20x, median about 10x. **The current 1.8x PB sits above the midpoint while 7.5x PE sits at a historical low — this contradiction between the two metrics is precisely the signature of a cycle top.** Dividend yield 0.55%, low among large tech companies.

A final methodological caveat: there are two kinds of "cheap" for cyclicals — the false cheap caused by a denominator about to shrink (now), and the true cheap caused by a denominator that has already bottomed (as in 2023). **Distinguishing the two depends not on PE but on the quarter-on-quarter direction of contract prices.** If QoQ is still rising, no matter how low the PE, it is false cheap; two quarters after QoQ turns negative, no matter how high the PE, it may be true cheap.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendation: Buy (in tranches), cap 5%

Rating: **Buy (staged)**. Open a first tranche of 1.5% at the current price, add a second tranche of 1.5% below KRW 240,000, and fill out to 2% once Q3 guidance is ≥KRW 95 trillion and HBM4E certification is passed. **The three tranches cost about KRW 255,000 on average, implying 5% upside to the neutral target price of KRW 268,000.**

Trim: cut one-third above KRW 330,000 and another half above KRW 400,000. **Logical stop: if DRAM contract prices fall month on month for two consecutive months, halve the position regardless of profit or loss.**

Why not a "Buy"? Because the neutral-case target price is almost equal to the current price, so the payoff is thin. **The case for buying is "downside cushioned by net assets + upside optionality on share gains + staged entry lowers cost", not "clearly undervalued".** All three strengthen below KRW 240,000, which is why staging is key.

Portfolio level: Samsung's correlation with SK hynix and Micron is above 0.85, and 0.6–0.7 with TSMC and Nvidia, **so it is not a diversification tool but another expression of the same bet.** If the portfolio already holds memory or AI chip exposure, Samsung's 5% should be counted within the 15% sector exposure. On FX, KRW assets carry extra volatility for RMB investors, but a 5% position does not need a separate hedge.
