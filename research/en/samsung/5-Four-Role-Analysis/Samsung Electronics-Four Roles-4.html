Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Samsung Electronics

How much could be lost at worst, what would trigger it, and which risks are overstated

One line: it can lose 40% without being wiped out, but it will cost you two years

Downside risk is modeled in three tiers: mild downside (price falls 15%) → share price 200,000–220,000; moderate downside (falls 30%) → 150,000; extreme downside (falls 50% + share loss) → 110,000. The three scenarios correspond to PB of 1.3x, 0.98x and 0.72x, all within the historical range, with no possibility of "insolvency".

The real risk is not the magnitude of the loss but time: downturns in cyclical stocks typically last 4–6 quarters, meaning all of 2027 could be spent in "falling profits + share price grinding along the bottom". During this period every quarterly report looks ugly, and it is easiest to sell at the very bottom. The purpose of risk assessment is not to scare people but to write this process down in advance.

Risk 1: The price inflection point arrives faster than expected

Storage prices are characterized by "slow rises, fast falls." Rises come from quarterly contract negotiations, 10%–20% at a time; falls start with a spot-market collapse—spot prices can drop 10% in a week—and contract prices are then cut the following quarter.The 44% QoQ increase in 2026Q2 has never been sustained for more than two quarters in history.

Samsung's profit is extremely sensitive to price: for every 10% fall in price, operating profit falls roughly 25% (costs are rigid; depreciation does not fall with it). If contract prices fall 30% in the first half of 2027, annualized operating profit drops from KRW 293.8 trillion to around KRW 150 trillion,which at 4x gives a share price of KRW 150,000—down 45% from the current price.

This calculation is not a forecast; it is a stress test. It shows one thing:buying Samsung is not a bet on whether it will fall, but on whether you dare add when it does.

Risk 2: HBM share stalls at 33%

HBM is Samsung's only structural improvement this cycle. Share rose from 21% to 33%, driven by being first to mass-produce HBM4. But 12-layer validation for HBM4E has yet to pass, SK hynix is closing the gap on HBM4 yield, and Micron's HBM4 is already sampling.If Samsung's share stalls at 33% in 2027, it is merely a "second supplier" with no pricing power.

Second-supplier status means: pricing follows the leader, capacity follows orders, and gross margin runs 5–8 percentage points below the leader. That is fine in a shortage, but fatal in a glut—in a glut, customers cut the second supplier's orders first.

The watch indicators are clear: Nvidia's next-generation platform HBM4E supplier list, HBM as a share of Samsung's memory revenue, and the price spread between HBM contract prices and conventional DRAM. Deterioration in any one of the three warrants a re-rating.

Risk 3: China's capacity and geopolitics

CXMT holds a 9.5% DRAM share, focused on the DDR5 consumer market. Its technology trails by two generations, but it has an advantage Samsung lacks: it does not need to make money; it needs to exist. As long as it keeps expanding capacity, it will suppress the price floor for commodity DRAM.

Geopolitical risk cuts both ways: US export controls on equipment to China will cap CXMT's advanced capacity, which helps Samsung near term; but the controls also accelerate substitution toward domestic Chinese equipment and materials, eroding Samsung's cost advantage over time. The more direct risk is memory being drawn into tariffs — more than half of Samsung's memory is produced at its Xi'an and Suzhou plants in China.

I cannot quantify this one, only characterize it: it is a tail risk, not the base case. If it materializes, the impact is "halved profit + valuation discount" at the same time, harder to recover from than a plain price cycle.

Overstated Risks: Foundry Drag and FX

The market often cites two risks that I believe are overstated. The first is foundry losses: Samsung's foundry business is indeed burning cash, but losses are narrowing in 2026 as 2nm customers ramp, and its weight in consolidated results is small—within the DS division's KRW 127.5 trillion revenue, foundry is only about KRW 6.7 trillion.A KRW 1 trillion foundry loss affects 1% of the division's KRW 89 trillion profit.

The second is FX: a weak KRW is a tailwind for Samsung (export-denominated), not a headwind. The real FX risk is sharp KRW appreciation—that would compress translated revenue, but typically by 3%–5%, which does not change the conclusion.

Strike these two risks from the "major risks" list, and what remains are the real risks: price, share, geopolitics. None of the three is fully within the company's control, which is why it merits only a 5% position, not 15%.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: A 40% downside is calculable, not catastrophic; the real cost is time — two years of bottoming out. Of the three real risks (price, share, geopolitics), only price can be observed in advance; the other two can only be confirmed after the fact.
Score from Li Lu's perspective: downside controllability 7/10, time cost 3/10. Recommendation: position cap 5%, scale in tranches, hold cash to handle the KRW 150,000 scenario.
