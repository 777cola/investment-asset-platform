Summary: three debates, three verdicts, one action

Four reports were produced independently, with no communication between them. They agree completely on "company quality" (all consider it the strongest of the three) and split into two camps on "can you buy it now": the commercial and industry reports say "wait", the financial and risk reports say "buy in tranches". The three disagreements need to be adjudicated with verifiable observation points, not with more analysis.

Verdict: Buy (in tranches), first position one third, total position cap 5%. Disagreement one (cycle position) is judged "top zone", disagreement two (whether HBM share can reach 40%) is judged "possible but uncertain", disagreement three (whether shareholder returns will improve) is judged "not within 2026". All three point to the same action: small position, slow build, visible falsification conditions.

Disagreement one: is this the top or the middle of the cycle?

The industry view calls a "top zone," citing two consecutive quarters of narrowing contract price gains and three players expanding capacity in unison; the financial view offers a counterargument: if the top were already in, why did Q2 operating margin still rise sequentially and R&D still grow 78% year on year?

Verdict: top zone, not the top. Both pieces of evidence hold, because they describe different things—margin is the "price already realized," contract price gains are the "marginal change." Marginal change turns first, absolute level turns later; that is the general rule for cyclical stocks. Q2's 52.2% operating margin is the high for this cycle, and even if profit keeps growing in Q3, the margin is unlikely to step up again.

In practice, the verdict means: do not count on "one more rally before selling," and do not bet on "an imminent collapse." Pace entries on the assumption that the high-level plateau lasts 2–4 quarters, with at least one month between tranches.

Disagreement two: can HBM share reach 40%

The bull case is most optimistic: first to mass-produce HBM4, share going from 21% to 33% in a single quarter — the slope shows engineering capability is back. The risk view is most cautious: 12-layer HBM4E has not yet passed Nvidia certification, and SK hynix will not cede share.

Verdict: possible, but not usable as a base-case assumption. Going from 33% to 40% requires two conditions to hold at once — HBM4E passing certification, and SK hynix capacity being diverted to customers other than Nvidia. Samsung controls the first; it does not control the second. So the neutral scenario uses 35% (slightly above current), and only the bull scenario uses 40%.

This verdict directly determines valuation: at a 40% base case, 2027 net profit reaches KRW 400 trillion and the target price is KRW 399,000; at 35%, it is KRW 350 trillion and KRW 268,000. A KRW 130,000 gap, all riding on one certification outcome — which is why the initial position is only one-third.

Divergence 3: Will shareholder returns improve?

The biggest grievance from a financial perspective: a market cap of KRW 1,793 trillion and dividends of KRW 9.8 trillion, a dividend yield of 0.55% and a payout ratio of 4%. At 7.5x PE, buybacks would be obvious value creation, yet the company has not done them. Adding the risk perspective: the more cash on the balance sheet, the greater the risk of reckless investment at the top of the cycle.

Verdict: no meaningful improvement within 2026. Samsung's shareholder return policy for 2024–2026 is "50% of free cash flow used for returns, annual dividend no lower than KRW 9.8 trillion." The policy itself has not changed, but profit rose 20x while the dividend did not keep pace, passively diluting the payout ratio to 4%. Any change must wait for a new policy announcement in 2027.

The impact of this on valuation is a "discount factor": for the same profit, Samsung should trade at a lower valuation than Micron because it does not return profit to shareholders. This is also evidence that the 7.5x PE embeds a governance discount—it is not all cyclical discount.

One thing none of the four perspectives mentioned

None of the four reports discusses foundry adequately. The reason is insufficient data: the DS division discloses only total revenue and total operating profit, without separating foundry from memory. Externally, one can only infer from 2nm customer adoption, Taylor fab progress, and orders from Nvidia/Qualcomm/Tesla.

This is an information gap, and also an option. If foundry truly turns profitable in 2027, Samsung's profit structure shifts from "memory only" to "memory + foundry," and its valuation center could move up 1–2x. If it does not turn profitable, it is a burden burning several trillion won a year. Both outcomes are possible, so we assign it zero in valuation—no premium, no discount.

We write this because the most dangerous thing is not known risk, but the place no one is looking. Foundry is that place.

Final action: three batches, two checkpoints, one iron rule

Three tranches: open one-third of the initial position at the current price of 275,000; build the second tranche below 240,000 (1.55x PB); execute the third tranche only if Q3 guidance is no lower than 90 trillion and HBM4E certification is passed. Once all three tranches are executed, the cost basis will be around KRW 255,000.

Two checkpoints: the preliminary Q3 guidance in early October (whether operating profit continues to grow QoQ) and the HBM4E certification result (whether it enters Nvidia's mass-production list). These two are the switch for "whether to add the third tranche," not the switch for "whether to sell."

One iron rule: once 2027 DRAM contract prices turn down, cut the position by half regardless of profit or loss. This rule requires no judgment, no meeting, no earnings call — just TrendForce's monthly contract prices. Cyclical stocks lose money in only two ways: adding at the top, and telling a "long-term story" on the way down. This iron rule guards against both.

Conclusion

Committee conclusion: Buy (in tranches). All three debates were adjudicated; none changed the rating, but each changed the position — that is the value of four-perspective parallel analysis: same conclusion, different execution.
