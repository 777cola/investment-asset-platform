Synthesis: the disagreement is not about the numbers but about which year's earnings to use as the denominator

The four close readings use identical figures but reach conclusions ranging from "do not buy" to "an initial position can be opened." The sole source of disagreement is the denominator: Duan Yongping and Munger use normalized profit, Buffett uses net assets, and Li Lu uses structural profit after separation.

Synthesized judgment: This is a financial report that "must be read in parts." The consolidated figure (KRW 89.49 trillion operating profit) is misleading because it blends cyclical windfall gains with structural improvement. Once separated, the structural portion is worth 15x and the cyclical portion 4x, summing to a fair value range of KRW 340,000–410,000.

Divergence one: which year's profit to use

Duan Yongping and Munger both use "normalized profit": KRW 25 trillion–35 trillion per quarter, KRW 100 trillion–140 trillion for the full year. On that denominator, an 8–10x valuation implies KRW 104,000–184,000, making the current price 50% expensive.

Li Lu uses "sum-of-the-parts profit": KRW 30 trillion structural plus KRW 55 trillion cyclical, applying a separate multiple to each, to arrive at KRW 340,000–410,000. The difference is that he accepts "the structural portion will not disappear," whereas Duan Yongping and Munger fold it into the cycle.

Who is right? It depends on whether the HBM share can hold. If HBM still accounts for more than 30% of memory revenue in 2028, Li Lu is right; if HBM becomes a commodity and the price premium narrows, Duan Yongping and Munger are right. This is a disagreement that data can settle, not a difference of opinion.

Divergence 2: What is the balance sheet worth

Buffett is the only one who uses PB valuation. His logic: cyclical stocks cannot be valued on PE at the top of the cycle, only on "replacement cost" or "net assets." A 1.8x PB, in his view, is "50% expensive," because Samsung's historical trough was 0.8–1.0x PB.

The other three ignore PB entirely. Duan Yongping's reasoning: "the plants and equipment in net assets may have a replacement value of only half their book value" (semiconductor equipment depreciates quickly). Munger's reasoning: "net assets cannot prevent a price decline." Li Lu's reasoning: "structurally growing companies should be valued on earnings."

In synthesizing, I adopt Buffett's PB as a downside floor, not as the primary method. Reason: PB tells you "how far it can fall in the worst case," not "what it is worth." The bear case of KRW 110,000 corresponds to 0.72x PB, which is the reading on that floor.

Consensus: all three agree now is not the time for heavy positions

List the "actions" from the four deep reads: tracking position ≤1%, watchlist, do not buy, initial position. Not one of them said "heavy position." That is the most important consensus.

A heavy position requires three conditions to hold at once: a good business, a good price, and understandable. Samsung satisfies the first and third; the second is only half met (PE cheap, PB not cheap). So one can only build an initial position, not a heavy one.

This consensus also explains why the market gives it 7.5x PE: the market is not wrong; it is also waiting for a signal of "denominator certainty." That signal is the 2027 HBM mix and contract price trend, and even at the earliest it will not be clear until early 2027.

Conclusion

Synthesis: The four deep-dive fair-value ranges span KRW 104,000–410,000, with the median around 250,000 — less than 10% away from the current price of 275,000. This shows market pricing is efficient, and that the "low PE" is not an obvious mispricing.
There is only one actionable move: open a small initial position and write down clear triggers for adding.
