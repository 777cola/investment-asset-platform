Structural components account for roughly 30%, with the remainder cyclical

The core question Li Lu asks when reading financial statements: how much of this profit comes from "this time is different," and how much from "another cycle"? I split Samsung's 2026Q2 operating profit of KRW 89.49 trillion into two parts: a structural portion of roughly KRW 25 trillion–30 trillion, driven by the HBM-led product-mix upgrade and the durability of AI demand, and a cyclical portion of roughly KRW 60 trillion from price increases.

That split dictates the valuation approach: the structural portion can command 15x, the cyclical portion only 4–5x. Annualising the KRW 30 trillion structural profit at 15x and adding cyclical profit at 4x yields a market-cap range roughly in line with the current price—the mathematical expression of "fair but not cheap."

First, distinguish between the two types of profit

Cyclical profit changes the moment price moves; structural profit is still there when price does not. Of Samsung's KRW 89.49 trillion this quarter, only the product upgrade portion of HBM and high-end DRAM qualifies as structural—even if DRAM prices return to 2025 levels, HBM unit prices remain four to five times those of ordinary DRAM.

Specifically, HBM's share of memory revenue rose from under 15% in 2025 to over 30% in 2026Q2 (estimated by share and industry size), and its gross margin runs 15–20 percentage points above ordinary DRAM. Isolating it corresponds to roughly KRW 25 trillion–30 trillion in quarterly operating profit.

The remaining KRW 55 trillion–60 trillion comes from "the same capacity at higher prices." This portion will disappear in the next downturn—not partially, but largely. In the 2019 cycle, Samsung's quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 17 trillion to KRW 6 trillion, a drop of 65%.

Can the structural component hold up?

Li Lu would ask three questions: Is AI memory demand a one-off buildout? Will HBM's technical barriers erode? Is Samsung a winner in this new landscape?

The answer to the first is "not one-off": training is one-off, inference is continuous. Inference demand grows with user numbers and usage hours, and memory bandwidth requirements only rise.That makes the HBM demand curve flatter and longer than in the 2017 cycle.

The answer to the second is "barriers are thinning but not disappearing": HBM4 can be made by three players—Samsung, SK hynix and Micron—and the barrier shifts from "can it be made" to "yield and capacity."Once all three can make it, competition will eat into part of the excess profit.

The answer to the third is "one of the winners, but not the only one": Samsung holds 33% share, SK hynix 50%. It gets the "second supplier" position, with margins below the leader's but enough to share in industry growth.

Valuation: price the two profit streams separately

Sum-of-the-parts: structural profit of KRW 30 trillion/quarter × 4 = KRW 120 trillion/year, at 15x = KRW 1,800 trillion market cap; cyclical profit of KRW 55 trillion/quarter × 4 = KRW 220 trillion/year, at 4x = KRW 880 trillion. Together that is KRW 2,680 trillion, or KRW 411,000 per share on 6.52 billion shares.

That implies 50% upside to the current price and looks like a buy signal. Apply a discount, though: cyclical profit could go to zero in 2027, so the KRW 880 trillion valuation holds only "if prices stay". Value cyclical profit at just 2x, on the basis that it can vanish at any time, and market cap becomes KRW 1,800 + 440 = KRW 2,240 trillion, or KRW 343,000 — still above the current price.

Li Lu's conclusion would therefore be milder than Munger's: the current price already reflects "the cycle will turn", but not "the structural part is growing". This is a position to enter with a small allocation, not a heavy one.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: of the KRW 89 trillion in profit, only 30% belongs to the new era and 70% to the old cycle. Applying 15x to the 30% and 4x to the 70% gives a fair value of KRW 340,000–410,000, above the current price.
After the discount, however (cyclical profit at 2x), fair value is about KRW 343,000—against a current price of KRW 275,000, that is 20% upside: not enough for a heavy position, but enough to build a first position.
