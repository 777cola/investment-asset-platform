Ask the Samsung question again, the Duan Yongping way

Duan Yongping's test of a company is simple: is it a good business, will it still be here in ten years, do I understand it, at what price do I buy, and what do I fear most. Samsung answers the first three. It cannot answer the fourth, since the denominator behind the price is uncertain, but it has a clear answer to the fifth: the fear is the cycle, not competition.

His conclusion is therefore most likely "understandable, wait for the price, buy a little." That does not conflict with our Buy (staged): the difference is only position size. He might buy just 1%, while we recommend a 5% cap with a 1.5% initial position. The disagreement is over risk appetite, not over the facts.

Q: Is this a good business?

Good — but what makes it good? Customers cannot walk away, new entrants cannot get in, and it can raise prices when supply is tight. A 69.6% gross margin and 52.2% operating margin in 2026Q2 are the best readings in manufacturing.

There are two kinds of "good", however: the kind that earns this much every year (consumer goods, software), and the kind that earns a lot in good years and loses money in bad ones (cyclicals). Samsung is the latter. Its semiconductor business lost 4.36 trillion in 2023Q2 and earned 89.2 trillion in 2026Q2 — same company, same fabs.

The accurate statement, then, is that this is a good business, but not a "buy and hold" business. Buy-and-hold requires stable profit, which Samsung does not have.

Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

Yes. Will DRAM still be needed in ten years? Yes—the bigger AI gets, the more DRAM it needs. Will Samsung still be in the top three in ten years? Most likely. Memory is a capital-intensive industry: only three players survive three cycles, and new entrants must clear three gates—patents, equipment and customer certification.

The only real worry is China. CXMT holds 9.5% share and trails by two technology generations, but it has market, policy support and patience. In ten years it could reach 20% share and turn commodity DRAM into a product "China can make too." That does not touch Samsung's high-end business, but it is fatal to its commodity DRAM.

So the answer is: the company will still be here, but its profit mix will change—high-end (HBM) will account for a growing share, while low-end (commodity DRAM) becomes less and less profitable. That shift is good for valuation (more like a growth stock) and bad for profit (a smaller total).

Q: Do you understand it?

I understand the business; I cannot read the cycle. That is the core contradiction in how I score Samsung.

On the business side — memory, foundry, handsets — three sentences and it is done: gross margin, share and customers are all verifiable. I give this layer 9 points.

On the cycle side, calling 2027 pricing requires the capacity expansion cadence of three manufacturers, the capex of four cloud players, HBM certification progress and China capacity coming online — of these four variables, two I cannot control, one I cannot see (certification) and one I cannot call (capex). I give this layer 3 points.

Duan Yongping's approach: buy only when "the layer you understand is cheap enough." In other words, do not rely on cycle calls; rely on price protection. If the price is low enough, a wrong call will not cost you much.

Q: At what price to buy?

On a rough estimate, a normal year — neither peak nor trough — puts Samsung's quarterly operating profit at KRW 25 trillion–35 trillion, full-year operating profit at KRW 100 trillion–140 trillion, net profit at KRW 85 trillion–120 trillion and EPS at KRW 13,000–18,400. Applying a normal-year P/E of 8–10x gives fair value of KRW 104,000–184,000.

At KRW 275,000, the stock trades 50% above the upper bound, so on this method now is not the time to buy.

Run it in reverse, however: if HBM lifts the midpoint to KRW 40 trillion a quarter (full-year operating profit KRW 160 trillion), EPS is KRW 20,000 and 10x gives KRW 200,000 — still below the current price. Justifying the current price requires 2027 net profit above KRW 350 trillion and a market willing to pay more than 5x. That is our neutral-scenario assumption, and one Duan Yongping would never make.

Q: What is the biggest fear

The biggest risk is not SK hynix, not ChangXin, but still adding to the position in 2027 when prices turn down.

There are only two ways to lose money in cyclicals: adding at the top, and telling a "long-term story" on the way down. The first is greed, the second is self-deception. Samsung is especially good at inducing self-deception, because its long-term logic (AI needs memory) is real.

So write the rules in stone before buying: halve the position once contract prices fall for two consecutive months, regardless of profit or loss. The value of this rule is not how clever it is, but that it requires no judgment — only a rule that requires no judgment can be executed when sentiment is at its worst.

Q: So how much would you buy?

By habit, Duan Yongping would take an observation position of about 1% in a target like this — "understandable business, unpredictable cycle, not cheap" — and then wait. What he waits for is not a falling share price but the "denominator becoming certain": clarity on the 2027 HBM mix and contract price trends.

His disagreement with us is over position size, not the judgment. We see a 1.5% initial position with a 5% cap as reasonable, because: ① the bear case is floored by P/B of 0.72x; ② the slope of HBM share gains is verifiable; ③ scaling in can push the cost basis down to around 255,000. He is more conservative because he does not need to "not miss out" — he only needs to "not lose money."

Both answers are right. Which to choose depends on whether your portfolio can bear "missing out": if missing this AI memory cycle would leave you struggling to explain yourself to clients, the 5% cap is right; if you care only about long-term compounding, the 1% observation position is more logical.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style verdict: a good business, understandable, not cheap, and the biggest risk is adding at the top. Four of the six questions can be answered; price and cycle cannot, so the only answer is to buy a little and wait for the denominator to become certain.
This does not conflict with our Buy (staged): the disagreement is over position size (1% vs 5% cap), not the facts.
