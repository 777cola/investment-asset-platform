Product line overlapDRAM module segment onlyDemingli may procure CXMT DRAM dies for memory modules, but this accounts for a small share of its revenue
Competitive dynamicsNon-direct competitionOne makes wafers, the other makes modules; they are upstream and downstream, not competitors

Future synergy potential: medium

Positive correlation:

  • CXMT's DRAM capacity keeps expanding (targeting a 17% global share by 2028) → more DRAM supply in China → domestic module makers such as Demingli can source domestic DRAM dies → lower costs plus supply stability
  • Complete domestic substitution chain: CXMT (DRAM wafers) + Demingli (modules) = domestic storage solution

Constraints:

  • Demingli's core business is NAND, not DRAM, so its direct benefit from CXMT is limited
  • If Demingli's high profit margin (44%) in 2026Q1 stems from NAND price increases, it has nothing to do with CXMT's DRAM business
  • Demingli's main suppliers remain SK hynix/Samsung/Micron

IV. Final judgment

ChangXin Technology:Strategic value is exceptionally high (China's only DRAM maker), but the current IPO valuation already fully, perhaps excessively, reflects optimistic expectations (308x PE plus market expectations of doubling to RMB 1 trillion+). A more reasonable entry point comes after the DRAM cycle turns down, sanctions risk is fully priced in, and valuation falls back to a reasonable range.

Demingli:The 44% net margin in 2026Q1 is both the biggest highlight and the biggest red flag. If the business mix has fundamentally changed (own controllers ramping + product upgrades), annualized profit of RMB 30 billion+ against under 10x PE is severely undervalued. But if Q1 was a one-off pulse driven by the memory price-up cycle, the current price carries huge correction risk. Waiting for the interim results is a reasonable strategy.

The relationship between the two:Although both sit within the broader memory industry, one is DRAM wafer manufacturing (an asset-heavy oligopoly) and the other is NAND modules/controllers (an asset-light midstream segment), so direct business linkage between them is limited. The logic of "investing in one is equivalent to investing in the other" does not hold.
