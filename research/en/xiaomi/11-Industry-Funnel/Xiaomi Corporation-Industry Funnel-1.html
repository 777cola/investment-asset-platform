Core thesis

Screen six companies in one table: Xiaomi, Apple, BYD, Li Auto, Transsion and Huawei (unlisted). Apple is too expensive, BYD is a different kind of business, Li Auto is a single category, Transsion has thinner gross margins and Huawei cannot be bought — which leaves only Xiaomi.

Being the only one left is not the same as being a good choice. Xiaomi's strength is that it is the only listed company betting on phones, cars and an ecosystem at the same time; its weakness is that none of its lines is the strongest. This is a process of elimination, not a process of selection.

Screening: four of five eliminated

Apple: 46% gross margin and exceptionally strong cash flow, but 30x P/E for 5% growth leaves insufficient upside. Its moat is ten times deeper than Xiaomi's, and its valuation twice as rich. Reason for elimination: expensive.

BYD: The strongest vertical integration and cost control in the field, 20% ROE, and 20x P/E is not expensive. But it is a pure automotive business with no optionality from phones or an ecosystem, and it is caught up in a price war. Reason for elimination: a different kind of business, not directly comparable to Xiaomi.

Li Auto: Single category, single price band, deliveries under pressure in 2026 and an uneven transition to pure electric, with 18x P/E against negative growth. Reason for elimination: product-cycle risk too high.

Transsion: Leader in emerging-market phones, and 12x P/E looks cheap, but gross margin is only 20% and net margin in the single digits; memory price increases hit it harder than Xiaomi, which has IoT and internet services as a buffer. Reason for elimination: a thinner business with weaker resilience.

Huawei: The best company, but uninvestable. Reason for elimination: not listed.

Final pick: why Xiaomi (and how weak that "why" is)

Xiaomi is the only name left. Three reasons, though each needs to be weighted carefully:

  • Uniqueness: the only listed company globally combining phones, IoT, cars and in-house chips. No second name offers this combination, and the option value is real.
  • Execution: took the car to 10.4 units in a single quarter within two years, and Xuanjie O1 shipments exceeded one million units. No peer could have done either.
  • Valuation has come down: from 59.45 to 29.02, a 51% drop, and 19.6x TTM does not look expensive.

Each has a flip side, however: uniqueness does not equal profitability (gross margin 19.8%); execution does not equal profit (net profit -42.6%); and the other side of the valuation pullback is that "profit is falling faster" (28x 2026E). So this is a "least bad" choice, not a "very good" one.

Key cross-sectional finding: ROE is Xiaomi's biggest weakness

Take the ROE column on its own: BYD 20%, Transsion 15%, Apple extremely high (distorted by buybacks), Li Auto 8%, Xiaomi 8.5%. Xiaomi's return on capital ranks second to last among the six.

Not because it fails to make money, but because its asset base is too heavy and its equity base too large: net assets of over RMB 200 billion (including the 2025 share placement) generating RMB 24.6 billion a year, roughly 12%; strip out the impact of investment fair-value swings on equity and it falls to 8.5%.

BYD's 20% ROE comes from fast turnover and heavy use of leverage; Xiaomi's leverage is extremely low (interest-bearing debt is almost zero) — conservatism, but also a loss of efficiency. If Xiaomi were willing to add leverage and buy back shares, ROE could immediately rise to 12%–15% — but it chooses to put the money into cars and chips.

Conclusion: why this name, and why not buy now

Why this name? Of the investable universe, it is the only company that combines brand, ecosystem and manufacturing capability, and its valuation has already fallen from bubble levels into a reasonable range. That still holds.

Why not buy now? A reasonable range is not a cheap range. At 28x 2026E against a 43% decline in profit, the stock is "reasonably expensive"; the real entry point is 15-18x (HKD 15.6-18.7).

Keep the two steps separate: stock selection is done (this is the one), timing has not arrived (wait for gross margin or wait for price). In investing these two decisions must be made separately; mixing them becomes "it is a good company, so buy at any price."

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: six names screened, only Xiaomi survives — but as the "least bad", not the "best". Three factors keep it top of the watchlist: uniqueness, execution and the valuation pullback. Against that, ROE of 8.5%, a 19.8% gross margin and 28x 2026E keep us from buying now. Stock selection done, timing not yet: wait for two consecutive quarters of handset gross margin ≥10%, or for HKD 24 or below.
