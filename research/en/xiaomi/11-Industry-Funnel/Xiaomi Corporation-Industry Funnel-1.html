Core thesis

Screen six companies in one table: Xiaomi, Apple, BYD, Li Auto, Transsion, Huawei (unlisted). Apple is too expensive, BYD is a different kind of business, Li Auto is a single category, Transsion has thinner gross margins, Huawei cannot be bought — only Xiaomi is left.

But "only it is left" does not mean "it is a good choice." Xiaomi's strength is being "the only listed company betting on phones + cars + ecosystem at the same time"; its weakness is that "none of its lines is the strongest." This is a process of elimination, not a process of selection.

Screening: four of five eliminated

Apple: 46% gross margin and exceptionally strong cash flow, but paying 30x PE for 5% growth offers insufficient upside. Its moat is ten times deeper than Xiaomi's, and its price is twice as expensive. Reason for elimination: expensive.

BYD: Vertical integration and cost control are the strongest in the field, ROE of 20%, and 20x PE is not expensive. But it is a pure automotive business, with no optionality from phones or an ecosystem, and it is also engaged in a price war. Reason for elimination: a different kind of business, not directly comparable to Xiaomi.

Li Auto: Single category, single price band, deliveries under pressure in 2026 and an uneven transition to pure electric, with 18x PE corresponding to negative growth. Reason for elimination: product-cycle risk too high.

Transsion: Leader in emerging-market phones, 12x PE looks cheap, but gross margin is only 20% and net margin in the single digits; memory price increases hit it harder than Xiaomi (it has no IoT or internet services as a buffer). Reason for elimination: a thinner business with weaker resilience.

Huawei: The best company, but uninvestable. Reason for elimination: not listed.

Final pick: why Xiaomi (and how weak that "why" is)

Only Xiaomi is left. There are three reasons, but each one's weight needs to be seen clearly:

  • Uniqueness: the only listed company globally doing phones, IoT, cars and in-house chips at the same time. There is no second one with this combination, and the option value is real.
  • Execution: took the car to 10.4 units in a single quarter within two years, and Xuanjie O1 shipments exceeded one million units. No peer could have done either of these.
  • Valuation has come down: from 59.45 to 29.02, a 51% drop, and 19.6x TTM does not look expensive.

But all three have a flip side: uniqueness does not equal profitability (gross margin 19.8%); execution does not equal profit (net profit -42.6%); and the other side of the valuation pullback is that "profit is falling faster" (28x 2026E). So this is a "least bad" choice, not a "very good" one.

Key cross-sectional finding: ROE is Xiaomi's biggest weakness

Look at the ROE column on its own: BYD 20%, Transsion 15%, Apple extremely high (distorted by buybacks), Li Auto 8%, Xiaomi 8.5%. Xiaomi's return on capital ranks second to last among the six.

The reason is not that it fails to make money, but that its assets are too heavy and its equity base too large: net assets of over RMB 200 billion (including the 2025 share placement), earning RMB 24.6 billion a year, which is roughly 12%; deduct the impact of investment fair-value swings on equity, and it falls to 8.5%.

BYD's 20% ROE comes from fast turnover and ample use of leverage; Xiaomi's leverage is extremely low (interest-bearing debt is almost zero), which is conservatism and also a loss of efficiency. If Xiaomi were willing to add leverage and buy back shares, ROE could immediately rise to 12%–15% — but it chooses to put the money into cars and chips.

Conclusion: why this name, and why not buy now

The answer to "why this one" is: among the investable names, it is the only company that combines brand, ecosystem and manufacturing capabilities while its valuation has already come down from bubble levels to a reasonable range. That answer holds.

The answer to "why not buy now" is: a reasonable range is not the same as a cheap range. 28x 2026E against a 43% decline in profit is "reasonably expensive"; the real entry point is 15-18x (HKD 15.6-18.7).

Separate the two steps: stock selection is done (it is the one), timing has not arrived (wait for gross margin or wait for price). These two things must be done separately in investing; mixing them turns into "because it is a good company, buy at any price."

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: after screening six names only Xiaomi remains, but it is the "least bad", not the "best". Three factors — uniqueness, execution, and the pullback in valuation — support its place at the top of the watchlist; ROE of 8.5%, gross margin of 19.8%, and 28x 2026E keep us from buying now. Stock selection done, timing not yet: wait for two consecutive quarters of handset gross margin ≥10%, or for HKD 24 or below.
