## Signal one: June memory contract prices — the real fundamental inflection point

June 26 was the quarter's most important trading day — not for the size of the drop but for the reason behind it: memory contract prices were raised again, and the full-year DRAM price increase was revised up to 60%–70%. That day the market began to recognise that the 11.5% handset gross margin could not hold.

The facts confirmed it quickly. In the August 18 earnings report, handset gross margin came in at 8.5%, down 3.0pct YoY. Between late June and mid-August the share price rebounded from 21.42 to around 26, even as earnings expectations for the handset business were cut all the way down. This is the classic divergence of a share price rebounding while fundamentals deteriorate — and it rarely lasts long.

## Signal 2: Autos priced for volume in August — a change of valuation method

The key takeaway from Q2 is not the -42.6% profit decline but the four auto metrics: deliveries +28.2%, ASP -9.6%, gross margin -7.2pct and an operating loss of RMB 2.6 billion. Together they put a definitive end to the "autos as an option" narrative.

The market reaction is telling: a single day of decline (8/18 actually closed up), then three straight days of gains. The reason is that expectations were already low enough — sell-side analysts had cut 2026 net profit to around RMB 24 billion ahead of the results, and with the actual figure no worse, bad news was fully priced in.

The nature of this rebound, however, is short covering rather than longs building positions. The evidence is simple: volume expanded, yet the share price failed to break the July high of 31.88.

## Noise: none of the three rallies had anything to do with the company

Three rallies — +9.5% on 8 July, +19.3% over 27–29 July and +4.5% on 21 August — were driven by a sector rebound, new-model expectations and an oversold bounce respectively. None was backed by a company announcement; all were classic beta moves.

Such moves are easily misread as a "reversal." The test is to compare the gain in the Hang Seng Tech Index over the same period: if the stock's gain is close to the index's, it is beta; only clear outperformance is alpha. On 29 July Xiaomi rose 9.0% while Hang Seng Tech gained about 3% that day — 6 points of outperformance. That one had an alpha component, but the source was Pengcheng expectations, not current-period results.

Pengcheng is the only thing left in 2026 that can still offer "option value." Its order and delivery ramp will determine whether the auto business can reach 50.0 thousand units in 2027. The late-July rally was therefore not pure noise — it was a "half signal." But expectations cannot be booked as results.

## Third attribution: who is buying, who is selling

Look past the events to the shareholder base. Between May and August 2026, Southbound holdings in Xiaomi fell, foreign active funds trimmed positions, and passive ETFs and retail investors absorbed the selling. That structure dictates the nature of any rebound: passive money will not add on improving fundamentals, only adjust with index weight changes.

Contrast 2025, when Southbound funds were steady net buyers and the stock ran from HKD 15 to 59.45. The shift from "active buying" to "passive holding" is the clearest evidence of this name going from growth stock to value stock.

One indicator is therefore enough to judge whether a rebound can last: whether Southbound funds log two consecutive weeks of net buying. If not, every rally is a zero-sum game.

## Strip out the noise and what signal remains

Three months, seven events, a 51% drawdown, a 35% rebound. Strip out the noise and only two sentences remain:

- Memory has broken smartphone gross margin, and it cannot be repaired in the short term.This is the main driver of the valuation cut.

- Auto has gone from an "option" to a "heavy-asset" business, and the market has switched valuation methods.This is the main driver of the multiple cut.

Both point to the same outcome: an EPS and P/E double kill.And that double kill is not over — memory prices are still rising and autos are still cutting prices.

So the call on the near-term share price: a rebound is possible, a reversal is premature.A true reversal requires smartphone gross margin to recover sequentially, which will not show until the Q3 report around November.
