## I. Company Overview

Xiaomi (01810.HK), founded in 2010 and listed in July 2018, is the world's third-largest smartphone maker. The business has three segments: smartphones (RMB 42.1 billion revenue in 2026Q2), IoT and lifestyle products (RMB 31.3 billion), and smart EV and AI innovative businesses (RMB 24.896 billion, including RMB 23.9 billion from EVs). It also has internet services (advertising, gaming, fintech), with a historical gross margin above 70%, but the scale is small and the company did not disclose standalone figures for this segment this quarter.

The company announced its car-making push in March 2021, launched its first model, the SU7, in March 2024, rolled out the YU7 SUV in June 2025, and surpassed 100,000 units delivered in a single quarter in 2026. The stock closed at HKD 29.02 on 2026/08/21, giving a market cap of HKD 747 billion (25.741 billion shares), down 51.2% from its 52-week high of HKD 59.45.

EV capacity: Phase 1 in Beijing runs at 150,000 units/year (15.0); with Phase 2 in production, the combined total is roughly 300,000 units/year (30.0) (rough estimate). Pengcheng starts deliveries in 2026 and is key to whether capacity utilization can step up to a higher level. R&D intensity: RMB 9.2 billion in 2026Q2 alone, 8.4% of revenue, or RMB 36.8 billion annualized. R&D staff account for more than half of employees (the company does not disclose this quarterly; figure based on historical methodology).

Shareholder structure: Lei Jun holds about 23% (including Class A shares, with majority voting power); institutional investors are mainly mutual funds and foreign institutions, and southbound holdings fell in the first half of 2026.**Under the dual-class share structure, minority shareholders have no veto over major decisions, and this is the part that warrants a valuation discount.**

## II. Industry analysis

Smartphones are a stock market: global annual shipments of about 1.2 billion units, no growth for five years, CR5 at about 70% but no single player above 20%. The 2026 variable is the memory super-cycle — DRAM contract prices up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%, device BOM costs up 9%–15%, and gross margins compressed across the industry. **Thin-margin players are hit hardest; Xiaomi's 8.5% gross margin is already close to contract-manufacturing levels.**

Autos are a market shifting from growth to stock: China's NEV penetration exceeds 55%, growth has slowed to single digits, and the RMB 200,000–300,000 price band is the most fiercely contested, with BYD, Tesla, Huawei AITO, Li Auto, XPeng and Zeekr all present. **The industry break-even point is 300,000–500,000 units a year; Xiaomi's annualized 41.7 million units has only just cleared it.**

Historical reference for memory cycles: the 2017–2019 upcycle lasted about 7 quarters, the 2021–2023 cycle about 5 quarters. The current cycle began rising in early 2026; on a 4–6 quarter estimate, the peak window falls between Q4 2026 and Q1 2027. **But HBM crowding out standard DRAM capacity is the new variable this time and may lengthen the cycle.**

Pace of auto consolidation: China has more than 50 NEV brands, and institutions generally expect consolidation to fewer than 20 by 2027–2028. Until consolidation completes, the price war will not stop. **Xiaomi's opportunity is not "winning the price war" but "surviving until it ends."**

## III. Business model

Xiaomi's model is "acquire customers through hardware, monetize through internet services, retain through the ecosystem" — thin margins on hardware, fat margins on services. But in 2026 this model is under strain: on the hardware side, smartphone gross margin is 8.5% and the auto business lost RMB 2.6 billion; on the services side, revenue tracks the smartphone shipment decline (shipments -26.5%) and cannot offset it.

The new auto business changes the asset profile of the business model: from asset-light (contract manufacturing + channel) to asset-heavy (self-built plants + direct delivery). **The upside is control over manufacturing and quality; the downside is that fixed costs and capacity utilization become new profit variables.**

The unit economics comparison is telling: gross profit of RMB 112 per phone, RMB 44,000 per car (but a net loss of RMB 25,000), and annual ARPU per internet service user in the low hundreds of RMB at a gross margin above 70%. **The "margin rates" of the three businesses differ 10-fold, yet their revenue shares are 39% / 22% / under 10%.**

This structure dictates that the larger the hardware scale, the more overall gross margin is dragged down; only a higher services mix can improve blended gross margin. **And services track the installed base of phones — with smartphone shipments down -26.5%, services cannot grow against the tide.** This is the model's inherent contradiction.

## IV. Financial analysis

2026Q2: revenue RMB 108.92 billion (-6.1%), gross profit RMB 21.57 billion (gross margin 19.8%, -2.7pct), net profit RMB 9.46 billion (-20.3%), adjusted net profit RMB 6.22 billion (-42.6%), R&D RMB 9.2 billion (+18.9%). Gross profit fell RMB 4.52 billion and adjusted net profit fell RMB 4.61 billion, **passing through almost one-for-one, which shows no buffer on the cost side.**

Asset side: interest-bearing debt is minimal and cash is ample; but inventory rose on memory stockpiling and vehicle inventory, operating cash flow conversion declined, and free cash flow is close to breakeven. **The company has paid no dividend in eight years since listing; shareholder returns rely on buybacks (HKD 20 billion in 2026Q1).**

Cost structure: R&D RMB 9.2 billion (8.4% of revenue); selling and administrative expenses are not disclosed separately and total roughly 8%–9% on the historical basis. **Revenue fell 6.1% while R&D rose 18.9%, pushing the expense ratio up about 1.5 points — one reason net profit fell more than gross profit.**

Returns: ten-year average ROE 8.5%; 2026 annualized ROE about 10%–12% (roughly, on RMB 24.6 billion net profit and RMB 200–240 billion equity), still below the 15%+ level of top manufacturers. **Profit decline and placement dilution occurred together, and ROE has not materially improved — if it does not step up, the valuation should not carry a premium.**

Financial comparison with peers: Xiaomi's blended gross margin of 19.8% is below Apple (46%) and Samsung (around 35%) and above pure contract manufacturers (5%–8%); ROE of 8.5% is below BYD (20%) and Transsion (15%); its interest-bearing debt ratio is near zero, the most conservative of the six. **The combination of "low leverage + low gross margin + low ROE" means it cannot have both safety and returns.**

## V. Management

Lei Jun serves as chairman and CEO, holds about 23% and controls more than half the voting rights, and directly oversees autos and chips. Lu Weibing serves as president and president of the phone division, and is the executor of the premiumization push. Capital allocation record: a HKD 42.5 billion share placement at a high in March 2025 and a HKD 20 billion buyback at a low in Q1 2026 — selling high and buying low worked once; but eight years without a dividend, and the buyback amounts to only 2.7% of market cap.

Governance risk centers on "one-man decision-making": four business lines advance simultaneously, there is no credible second-in-command to provide checks and balances, and succession planning is blank (Lu Weibing is the same age as Lei Jun). **Execution scores 9, governance scores 5.**

Core team: Lei Jun (founder, chairman and CEO, born 1969), Lu Weibing (president and president of the phone division, born 1969, former president of Gionee), Lin Bin (co-founder and vice chairman, former engineering director at Google), Alain Lam (CFO, former head of Asia-Pacific investment banking at Credit Suisse, joined 2020), Yu Liguo (vice president of the auto division, former head of BAIC Arcfox).

Capital allocation assessment: a HKD 42.5 billion placement in March 2025 (800 million shares @53.25) and a HKD 20 billion buyback in Q1 2026 — selling high and buying low; but no dividend in eight years since listing, and the buyback amounts to only 2.7% of market cap. **Capital allocation scores 7: right direction, insufficient scale.**

## VI. Growth drivers

Three drivers, ranked by certainty:

- **Vehicle scale (highest certainty):** Q2 104,200 units, 417,000 annualized; after the Pengcheng launch, 500,000 units is within reach in 2027. Scale is the only path to turning the vehicle business profitable.
- **Premiumization (medium certainty):** Domestic mix above RMB 3,000 reached 32.1%, ASP hit a record RMB 1,322, but premiumization rescues revenue, not gross margin.
- **Ecosystem monetization (lowest certainty):** Human-car-home synergy lifts internet services ARPU, but does not hit the financials near term.

**None of the three drivers solves the core problem of gross margin being squeezed from both ends**—that is set by cycle and competition.

Pengcheng is the most important variable for 2026. Its positioning, pricing and delivery cadence determine whether vehicles can reach 500,000 units in 2027. Judging by the SU7 ramp curve (over 80,000 locked orders within 24 hours of launch), orders are not the issue for Pengcheng—**capacity and gross margin are: the ramp speed of the Phase 2 plant determines how fast fixed costs are absorbed.**

Overseas markets are the second front: Xiaomi phones rank among the leaders in Europe, Southeast Asia and Latin America, with vehicle exports (Europe) planned after 2027. **Premiumization overseas is easier than at home because competitive intensity is a notch lower, but compliance and channel costs are higher.**

## VII. Competitive landscape

Smartphones: Samsung, Apple, Xiaomi, Transsion and OPPO/vivo split the market five ways; Xiaomi holds a top-three share but has no pricing power, and Apple and Huawei keep it suppressed in the high end. Autos: BYD presses costs through vertical integration (18% gross margin) and Huawei commands a premium on brand and ADAS, leaving Xiaomi squeezed in between, able to respond only with "3–5 million cheaper for the same spec."

**The truly most dangerous competitor is the upstream memory makers**: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron set prices, and a single quarter can strip 3 points of gross margin from Xiaomi — more damaging than any automaker grabbing share.

Share: Xiaomi ranks a steady top three globally in phones (contesting second with Transsion and OPPO) and top five in China; in autos it entered the top three among new-energy vehicle makers by deliveries in the first half of 2026. **Share is not the problem — share does not convert into gross margin.**

High-end competition: in the above-RMB 4,000 market, Apple and Huawei together take most of it; Xiaomi's 32.1% high-end mix sits mainly in the RMB 3,000–4,000 band. **True "high end" means above RMB 6,000, and Xiaomi's presence there remains weak.**

## VIII. Risk factors

① Memory price increases extend into 2027, handset gross margin falls below 7%; ② EV price war escalates, gross margin falls below 15%, quarterly loss widens to RMB 4 billion; ③ Pengcheng deliveries miss expectations, the scale story breaks; ④ Organization and succession risk (one person across four lines); ⑤ Geopolitics and compliance (India fund freeze, overseas market policy); ⑥ Valuation risk (28x 2026E on a 43% profit decline).

**Of these, ① and ③ are high-probability risks, ② is medium-probability but highest-impact, ④ and ⑤ are low-frequency, high-impact.**

Sensitivity analysis: each 1-point change in handset gross margin, on handset annual revenue of RMB 170 billion (rough estimate), affects gross profit by RMB 1.7 billion and net profit by about RMB 1.3 billion (after tax), equal to ±5% of 2026 net profit. Each 1-point change in EV gross margin, on annual revenue of RMB 95 billion, affects RMB 950 million. **Two 1-point moves together affect net profit by about 9%.**

This means: **gross margin is the company's only high-leverage variable; volume and revenue are secondary.** Read gross margin first in the financials; everything else is explanation.

## Nine, Earnings Forecast

2026E: revenue of about RMB 430 billion, adjusted net profit of RMB 24.6 billion (H1 RMB 12.3 billion annualized), net margin 5.7%, EPS RMB 0.96. Assumes handset gross margin holds at 8.5%–9%, auto gross margin 19%, and the IoT decline narrows.

2027E base case: revenue RMB 470 billion, net profit RMB 30 billion, assuming handset gross margin recovers to 10%, auto gross margin holds at 19%, and deliveries reach 50.0 thousand units. Bear case: net profit RMB 20 billion, assuming another round of memory price increases and further auto price cuts.**The biggest uncertainty in our forecast is not revenue but gross margin—two percentage points swing net profit by RMB 6 billion.**

Key assumptions in detail: 2026E handset revenue of about RMB 170 billion (-8% YoY), gross margin 8.8%; IoT about RMB 125 billion (-12% YoY); auto about RMB 100 billion (+25% YoY), gross margin 19%; internet services about RMB 35 billion, gross margin 72%. Total revenue of about RMB 430 billion, blended gross margin 19.5%.

2027E base case: handset gross margin back to 10%, auto deliveries of 50.0 thousand units (revenue RMB 115 billion), IoT stops declining, revenue RMB 470 billion and net profit RMB 30 billion.**The core of this forecast is not revenue but the two gross margin assumptions—they are the only variables that can materially change the outcome.**

## X. Valuation

Cross-check with sum-of-the-parts: phones + IoT + services at 2027 net profit of RMB 20 billion, 15x = RMB 300 billion; autos at 2027 deliveries of 500,000 units, net profit per vehicle of RMB 50,000 (turnaround assumption) = RMB 2.5 billion, at 30x = RMB 75 billion; total RMB 375 billion ≈ HKD 407.6 billion ≈ HKD 15.8 per share.

Blended PE: 2027 neutral case RMB 30 billion × 20x ÷ 0.92 ÷ 257.41 = HKD 25.3. **The two methods differ by 60%, showing that "what the auto business is worth" is the core valuation disagreement.** We adopt the blended PE method because it more conservatively captures group-level expenses.

Historical valuation range: Xiaomi's PE swung between 15x and 45x in 2021–2025, with a midpoint of about 25x; the 2025 high was 57x (on 2026 net profit) and the 2024 low was 15x. **At 28x today it sits near the historical midpoint, but profit is on a downtrend — "midpoint valuation plus declining profit" is the worst combination.**

Peer comparison: Apple 30x (5% growth), BYD 20x (15% growth), Li Auto 18x (negative growth). **Xiaomi at 28x with negative growth of 43% is the worst mismatch between valuation and growth among the four.**

By investor type: absolute-return investors — wait for below HKD 24; relative-return investors — wait for the handset gross margin inflection (potential outperformance versus the index); long-term allocators — start research now, but do not build a position.**Three investor types, three approaches; the common thread is "do not chase the current price".**

Divergence from the market: the sell-side consensus forecasts 2027 net profit of RMB 32.0–36.0 billion; we use RMB 30.0 billion for our neutral scenario.
