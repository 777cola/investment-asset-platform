I. Company Overview

Xiaomi (01810.HK), founded in 2010 and listed in July 2018, is the world's third-largest smartphone maker. It reports three segments: smartphones (RMB 42.1 billion revenue in 2026Q2), IoT and lifestyle products (RMB 31.3 billion), and smart EV and AI innovative businesses (RMB 24.896 billion, of which RMB 23.9 billion from EVs). Internet services (advertising, gaming, fintech) carry a historical gross margin above 70%, but scale is small and the company did not disclose standalone figures for the segment this quarter.

Xiaomi announced its car-making push in March 2021, launched its first model, the SU7, in March 2024, rolled out the YU7 SUV in June 2025, and surpassed 100,000 units delivered in a single quarter in 2026. The stock closed at HKD 29.02 on 2026/08/21, implying a market cap of HKD 747 billion on 25.741 billion shares, down 51.2% from its 52-week high of HKD 59.45.

EV capacity: Phase 1 in Beijing runs at 150,000 units/year (15.0); with Phase 2 in production, combined capacity is roughly 300,000 units/year (30.0) (rough estimate). Pengcheng starts deliveries in 2026 and is key to whether capacity utilisation can step up to a higher level. R&D intensity: RMB 9.2 billion in 2026Q2 alone, 8.4% of revenue, or RMB 36.8 billion annualised. R&D staff account for more than half of employees (not disclosed quarterly; figure based on historical methodology).

Shareholder structure: Lei Jun holds about 23% (including Class A shares, with majority voting power); institutional investors are mainly mutual funds and foreign institutions, and southbound holdings fell in the first half of 2026.Under the dual-class share structure, minority shareholders have no veto over major decisions, and this is the part that warrants a valuation discount.

II. Industry analysis

Smartphones are a stock market: global annual shipments of about 1.2 billion units, no growth for five years, CR5 at about 70% but no single player above 20%. The 2026 variable is the memory super-cycle — DRAM contract prices up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%, device BOM costs up 9%–15%, and gross margins compressed across the industry. Thin-margin players are hit hardest; Xiaomi's 8.5% gross margin is already close to contract-manufacturing levels.

Autos are a market shifting from growth to stock: China's NEV penetration exceeds 55%, growth has slowed to single digits, and the RMB 200,000–300,000 price band is the most fiercely contested, with BYD, Tesla, Huawei AITO, Li Auto, XPeng and Zeekr all present. The industry break-even point is 300,000–500,000 units a year; Xiaomi's annualized 41.7 million units has only just cleared it.

Historical reference for memory cycles: the 2017–2019 upcycle lasted about 7 quarters, the 2021–2023 cycle about 5 quarters. The current cycle began rising in early 2026; on a 4–6 quarter estimate, the peak window falls between Q4 2026 and Q1 2027. But HBM crowding out standard DRAM capacity is the new variable this time and may lengthen the cycle.

Pace of auto consolidation: China has more than 50 NEV brands, and institutions generally expect consolidation to fewer than 20 by 2027–2028. Until consolidation completes, the price war will not stop. Xiaomi's opportunity is not "winning the price war" but "surviving until it ends."

III. Business model

Xiaomi's model — acquire customers through hardware, monetise through internet services, retain through the ecosystem — runs thin hardware margins against fat services margins. In 2026 that model is under strain. On hardware, smartphone gross margin is 8.5% and the auto business lost RMB 2.6 billion; on services, revenue tracks the smartphone shipment decline (shipments -26.5%) and cannot offset it.

The new auto business changes the asset profile of the model: from asset-light (contract manufacturing plus channel) to asset-heavy (self-built plants plus direct delivery). The upside is control over manufacturing and quality; the downside is that fixed costs and capacity utilisation become new profit variables.

The unit economics comparison is telling: gross profit of RMB 112 per phone and RMB 44,000 per car (against a net loss of RMB 25,000), versus annual ARPU per internet service user in the low hundreds of RMB at a gross margin above 70%. The "margin rates" of the three businesses differ 10-fold, yet their revenue shares are 39% / 22% / under 10%.

This structure dictates that the larger the hardware scale, the more overall gross margin is dragged down; only a higher services mix can lift blended gross margin. And services track the installed base of phones — with smartphone shipments down -26.5%, services cannot grow against the tide. This is the model's inherent contradiction.

IV. Financial analysis

2026Q2: revenue RMB 108.92 billion (-6.1%), gross profit RMB 21.57 billion (gross margin 19.8%, -2.7pct), net profit RMB 9.46 billion (-20.3%), adjusted net profit RMB 6.22 billion (-42.6%), R&D RMB 9.2 billion (+18.9%). Gross profit fell RMB 4.52 billion and adjusted net profit fell RMB 4.61 billion, passing through almost one-for-one — there is no buffer on the cost side.

Balance sheet: interest-bearing debt is minimal and cash is ample, but inventory rose on memory stockpiling and vehicle inventory, operating cash flow conversion declined, and free cash flow is close to breakeven. The company has paid no dividend in the eight years since listing; shareholder returns rely on buybacks (HKD 20 billion in 2026Q1).

Cost structure: R&D RMB 9.2 billion (8.4% of revenue); selling and administrative expenses are not disclosed separately and total roughly 8%–9% on the historical basis. Revenue fell 6.1% while R&D rose 18.9%, pushing the expense ratio up about 1.5 points — one reason net profit fell more than gross profit.

Returns: ten-year average ROE 8.5%; 2026 annualised ROE about 10%–12% (roughly, on RMB 24.6 billion net profit and RMB 200–240 billion equity), still below the 15%+ level of top manufacturers. Profit decline and placement dilution occurred together, and ROE has not materially improved — without a step-up, the valuation should not carry a premium.

Financial comparison with peers: Xiaomi's blended gross margin of 19.8% is below Apple (46%) and Samsung (around 35%) and above pure contract manufacturers (5%–8%); ROE of 8.5% is below BYD (20%) and Transsion (15%); its interest-bearing debt ratio is near zero, the most conservative of the six. The combination of "low leverage + low gross margin + low ROE" means it cannot have both safety and returns.

V. Management

Lei Jun is chairman and CEO, holds roughly 23% and controls more than half the voting rights, and directly oversees autos and chips. Lu Weibing is president and president of the phone division, and is the executor of the premiumisation push. Capital allocation record: a HKD 42.5 billion share placement at a high in March 2025 and a HKD 20 billion buyback at a low in Q1 2026 — selling high and buying low worked once; but eight years without a dividend, and the buyback amounts to only 2.7% of market cap.

Governance risk centres on "one-man decision-making": four business lines advance simultaneously, there is no credible second-in-command to provide checks and balances, and succession planning is blank (Lu Weibing is the same age as Lei Jun). Execution scores 9, governance scores 5.

Core team: Lei Jun (founder, chairman and CEO, born 1969), Lu Weibing (president and president of the phone division, born 1969, former president of Gionee), Lin Bin (co-founder and vice chairman, former engineering director at Google), Alain Lam (CFO, former head of Asia-Pacific investment banking at Credit Suisse, joined 2020), Yu Liguo (vice president of the auto division, former head of BAIC Arcfox).

Capital allocation assessment: a HKD 42.5 billion placement in March 2025 (800 million shares @53.25) and a HKD 20 billion buyback in Q1 2026 — selling high and buying low; but no dividend in eight years since listing, and the buyback amounts to only 2.7% of market cap. Capital allocation scores 7: right direction, insufficient scale.

VI. Growth drivers

Three drivers, ranked by certainty:

  • Vehicle scale (highest certainty): Q2 104,200 units, 417,000 annualized; after the Pengcheng launch, 500,000 units is within reach in 2027. Scale is the only path to turning the vehicle business profitable.
  • Premiumisation (medium certainty): Domestic mix above RMB 3,000 reached 32.1% and ASP hit a record RMB 1,322, but premiumisation rescues revenue, not gross margin.
  • Ecosystem monetisation (lowest certainty): Human-car-home synergy lifts internet services ARPU but does not hit the financials near term.

None of the three drivers solves the core problem of gross margin being squeezed from both ends—that is set by cycle and competition.

Pengcheng is the most important variable for 2026. Its positioning, pricing and delivery cadence determine whether vehicles can reach 500,000 units in 2027. Judging by the SU7 ramp curve (over 80,000 locked orders within 24 hours of launch), orders are not the issue for Pengcheng—capacity and gross margin are: the ramp speed of the Phase 2 plant determines how fast fixed costs are absorbed.

Overseas markets are the second front: Xiaomi phones rank among the leaders in Europe, Southeast Asia and Latin America, with vehicle exports (Europe) planned after 2027. Premiumisation overseas is easier than at home because competitive intensity is a notch lower, but compliance and channel costs are higher.

VII. Competitive landscape

Smartphones: Samsung, Apple, Xiaomi, Transsion and OPPO/vivo carve the market five ways. Xiaomi holds a top-three share but has no pricing power, and Apple and Huawei keep it suppressed in the high end. Autos: BYD presses costs through vertical integration (18% gross margin) while Huawei commands a premium on brand and ADAS, leaving Xiaomi squeezed in between and able to respond only with "3–5 million cheaper for the same spec."

The truly most dangerous competitor is the upstream memory makers: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron set prices, and a single quarter can strip 3 points of gross margin from Xiaomi — more damaging than any automaker grabbing share.

Share: Xiaomi ranks a steady top three globally in phones (contesting second with Transsion and OPPO) and top five in China; in autos it entered the top three among new-energy vehicle makers by deliveries in the first half of 2026. Share is not the problem — share does not convert into gross margin.

High-end competition: in the above-RMB 4,000 market, Apple and Huawei together take most of it; Xiaomi's 32.1% high-end mix sits mainly in the RMB 3,000–4,000 band. True "high end" means above RMB 6,000, and Xiaomi's presence there remains weak.

VIII. Risk factors

① Memory price increases extend into 2027, pushing handset gross margin below 7%; ② EV price war escalates, gross margin falls below 15% and the quarterly loss widens to RMB 4 billion; ③ Pengcheng deliveries miss expectations, breaking the scale story; ④ Organisation and succession risk (one person across four lines); ⑤ Geopolitics and compliance (India fund freeze, overseas market policy); ⑥ Valuation risk (28x 2026E on a 43% profit decline).

Of these, ① and ③ are high-probability risks; ② is medium-probability but highest-impact; ④ and ⑤ are low-frequency, high-impact.

Sensitivity analysis: each 1-point change in handset gross margin, on handset annual revenue of RMB 170 billion (rough estimate), moves gross profit by RMB 1.7 billion and net profit by about RMB 1.3 billion (after tax), equal to ±5% of 2026 net profit. Each 1-point change in EV gross margin, on annual revenue of RMB 95 billion, moves RMB 950 million. Two 1-point moves together affect net profit by about 9%.

The implication: gross margin is the company's only high-leverage variable; volume and revenue are secondary. Read gross margin first in the financials; everything else is explanation.

Total revenue of about RMB 430 billion, blended gross margin 19.5%.

X. Valuation

Cross-check on a sum-of-the-parts basis: phones + IoT + services on 2027 net profit of RMB 20 billion at 15x = RMB 300 billion; autos on 2027 deliveries of 500,000 units and net profit per vehicle of RMB 50,000 (turnaround assumption) = RMB 2.5 billion at 30x = RMB 75 billion; total RMB 375 billion ≈ HKD 407.6 billion ≈ HKD 15.8 per share.

Blended P/E: 2027 neutral case of RMB 30 billion × 20x ÷ 0.92 ÷ 257.41 = HKD 25.3. The two methods differ by 60%, which shows that "what the auto business is worth" is the core valuation disagreement. We adopt the blended P/E method because it captures group-level expenses more conservatively.

Historical valuation range: Xiaomi's P/E swung between 15x and 45x in 2021–2025, with a midpoint of about 25x; the 2025 high was 57x (on 2026 net profit) and the 2024 low was 15x. At 28x today it sits near the historical midpoint, but profit is on a downtrend — "midpoint valuation plus declining profit" is the worst combination.

Peer comparison: Apple 30x (5% growth), BYD 20x (15% growth), Li Auto 18x (negative growth). Xiaomi at 28x with negative growth of 43% is the worst mismatch between valuation and growth among the four.

XI. Investment recommendation

Neutral rating, target price HKD 25.3. Rationale: execution and ecosystem scarcity justify long-term coverage, but 28x 2026E already prices in the optimistic scenario and earnings remain in a downgrade cycle. Odds 1:0.68 (bull case +38%, bear case -56%), expected value HKD 22.6, below the current price.

Action: do not build a position at the current price. Open a 3% initial position below HKD 24 and add to 6% below HKD 19; add to 8% if handset gross margin holds at ≥10% for two consecutive quarters.Red line: exit fully if handset gross margin <7% or auto gross margin <15%.

Timeline: the Q3 report in November 2026 is the first checkpoint (handset gross margin, auto gross margin, auto losses); the full-year report in March 2027 is the second (2027 guidance, timeline for auto breakeven).Rating unchanged until both checkpoints.

By investor type: absolute-return investors should wait for below HKD 24; relative-return investors should wait for the handset gross margin inflection (potential outperformance versus the index); long-term allocators should start research now but not build a position.Three investor types, three approaches; the common thread is "do not chase the current price".

Divergence from the market: sell-side consensus forecasts 2027 net profit of RMB 32.0–36.0 billion; we use RMB 30.0 billion for our neutral scenario. The gap comes from two places — we cut our handset gross margin assumption from 11% to 10% and lower auto ASP by a further 3%.The divergence is small but the direction is the same: both see 2027 better than 2026; the only question is how much better.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Neutral, target price HKD 25.3. Xiaomi combines top-tier execution with a genuine moat, but it remains a thin-margin business. In 2026 it is squeezed on both sides — rising memory prices and the EV price war — with profit down 42.6% YoY. On 28x 2026E against declining profit, the valuation is not cheap. Upgrade triggers: smartphone gross margin ≥10% for two consecutive quarters, or the share price falling below HKD 24.
