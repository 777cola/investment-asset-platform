Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Xiaomi Corporation

Five real risks, ranked by whether they could be fatal
Research date 2026/08/21  ·  Price HK$29.02 (+4.54%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 747 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 19.6x (2026E approximately 28x, estimated)

Core view

Rank risks by probability × impact, not by how frightening they sound. Of Xiaomi's five risks, only the memory cycle is live today; the other four are structural but observable.

Risk 1: Memory price increases (already under way, largest impact)

DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND by more than 100% in early 2026. HBM has crowded out standard DRAM capacity, and institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027. Xiaomi's smartphone gross margin fell from 11.5% to 8.5%, and IoT gross margin is also under pressure.

The risk is cyclical, exogenous and unhedgeable. Xiaomi's procurement volume ranks among the world's largest, so it can secure supply but not price concessions—memory is dominated by the three oligopolists (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) plus YMTC and CXMT, and pricing power sits with the sellers.

Impact estimate: memory accounts for 15%–25% of the smartphone BOM (rough calculation), so a 60% memory price increase implies a 9%–15% rise in total device cost; with Xiaomi's smartphone gross margin at 8.5%, this round of price increases has already consumed all of its profit cushion. When will it ease? Watch two signals: DRAM spot prices turning negative month on month, and capacity expansion by the manufacturers materializing. Neither is in sight yet.

Risk 2: Auto price war (structural, lasting impact)

Xiaomi's ASP fell 9.6% from 253,700 to 229,300, and gross margin dropped from 26.4% to 19.2%. This is not a promotion — it is a downward shift in the price-band benchmark. BYD will keep using vertical integration to compress costs in 2026, Huawei will lean on its smart-driving label to defend a premium, and Xiaomi can respond only on specs and price.

Where is the risk threshold? Gross margin falling below 15%. At that level per-vehicle gross profit is just 34,000, which cannot cover per-vehicle fixed costs and expenses of 69,000; losses would widen from 2.6 billion per quarter to more than 4 billion, and the car business would shift from an "investment phase" to a "cash-bleeding phase."

Li Lu would ask a more fundamental question: does the car business ultimately become like phones, where the most efficient player survives? If so, Xiaomi has a chance — it is an efficiency player. If it becomes a case of "the strongest brand survives" (as with Huawei), Xiaomi is in the wrong position. The answer will only be clear in 2027.

Risk 3: Diversification and organisational complexity (chronic, easily underestimated)

Phones, IoT, autos and chips are advancing on four fronts at once, each world-class in difficulty. R&D in 2026Q2 was RMB 9.2 billion, up 18.9% YoY, and that money has to be split across four battlefields.

Historically, diversification has failed at a startlingly high rate — not for lack of capability but for lack of attention. Lei Jun is chairman, CEO and de facto head of the auto business; in good times that structure is efficiency, in bad times a single point of failure.

What to watch: whether the auto business has produced an independent second-in-command who can speak publicly, and whether the phone business has a successor beyond Lu Weibing. Those two things say more about the health of the organization than gross margin does.

Risk 4: Geopolitics and compliance (low frequency, high impact)

Nearly half of Xiaomi's revenue comes from overseas, with India, Europe and Southeast Asia the key markets. Two shocks have hit before: in 2021 the US added the company to the CCMC list (removed the same year), and in 2022 India's Enforcement Directorate froze about RMB 4.8 billion of its funds, with litigation dragging on since.

Such risks are unpredictable and uncontrollable, but their exposure can be quantified. India accounts for a substantial share of Xiaomi's overseas revenue; in an extreme case the impact is "profit from one region goes to zero", not "the company goes to zero".

In Li Lu's framework, such risks must be compensated by a "price discount" — only a cheap enough entry can withstand a black swan. A 28x P/E offers no discount at all.

Risk 5: Valuation itself (the most certain risk to materialise)

The first four risks may not materialise; this one will—a 28x 2026E valuation requires three consecutive years of profit growth to digest, and the current trend is downward.

Do the math: if 2027 net profit is RMB 30 billion (base case), the P/E drops to 23x at an unchanged market cap; if net profit is only RMB 20 billion (bear case), the P/E rises to 34x.In other words, during earnings downgrades, the share price must fall to keep the valuation reasonable.

This is the standard structure of a "Davis double-kill": EPS down, multiple down, both working at once. This is exactly what drove Xiaomi from 59.45 to 29.02 in 2026.The risk is not "will it fall further" but "at what level do the odds turn positive"—the answer is below HKD 24.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: one unfolding (memory), one structurally persistent (EV price war), two chronic (organisation, geopolitics), one inevitable (valuation). Of the five, the first two determine 2026–2027 profit; the last three determine whether the stock is worth holding long term. The single metric to watch most closely is handset gross margin — the thermometer of the memory cycle and the proof of whether this company can breathe on its own. A fall below 7% is a danger signal; a return above 10% would signal a turning point.
