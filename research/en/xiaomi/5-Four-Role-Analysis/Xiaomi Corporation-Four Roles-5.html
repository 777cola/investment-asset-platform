Team lead summary: disagreements and rulings across the four perspectives — Xiaomi Corporation

The consensus is on price; the disagreement is on the car
Research date 2026/08/21  ·  Price HK$29.02 (+4.54%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 747 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 19.6x (2026E approximately 28x, estimated)

Core view

Four roles ran the same set of financial results and converged on three points while splitting on a fourth. Agreement: good company, bad timing, expensive price. Disagreement: whether autos represent "normal losses during the investment phase" or "a structurally bad business."

Consensus point one: this business's profitability is "below average"

Four analysts, four methods, one set of numbers: RMB 112 gross profit per phone, a 5.7% core net margin, a ten-year average ROE of 8.5%, and a RMB 25,000 loss on every car sold. All four converge on the same conclusion — this is not a business capable of generating excess profit.

That much is settled and needs no further debate. The real question is what a below-average business is worth. For manufacturing, the answer is 15–20x; for ecosystem growth, 25–30x. At 28x (2026E), the market is pricing it as the latter.

Consensus point two: the decline is exogenous rather than a loss of operational control

The most easily misread line in the earnings report is "profit down 43%". All four roles agree this is not a management problem: a 2.7pct decline in gross margin equates to RMB 4.5 billion less gross profit and RMB 4.6 billion less adjusted net profit, almost one-to-one — the entire profit decline comes from gross margin, not expenses, not sales volume, not impairments.

That margin decline stems from memory price increases (exogenous) and autos trading price for volume (semi-exogenous). Judging the future therefore requires no attention to what management says — only two prices: DRAM contract prices and auto transaction prices.

Consensus point three: price is the sole veto factor

All four reach the same conclusion: the company is fine, the price is not. Duan Yongping: "I wouldn't pay 28x." Buffett: "I'd rather buy the one with 50% gross margins." Munger: "Options turn it into a heavy-asset business; the valuation method has to change." Li Lu: "Wait for the bad news to fully play out." Four unanimous votes are rare in this framework.

The probability-weighted outcome: optimistic +38%, neutral -13%, pessimistic -56%, expected value HKD 22.6, 22% below the current price. This is not "not cheap enough" — it is "expensive."

The debate: is the auto business loss-making only during its investment phase, or is it structurally a bad business?

Duan Yongping and Buffett lean toward the "investment phase" view: gross profit per vehicle of RMB 44,000 covers 64% of fixed costs, and profitability follows once annual sales exceed 600,000 units — a question of scale, not of business model. Munger and Li Lu lean toward the "structural" view: this price band drifts lower every year, the risk that larger scale means larger losses is real, and the squeeze from BYD on one side and Huawei on the other will not go away.

Verdict: treat it as "conditionally valid." Three conditions must hold simultaneously — ① gross margin holds at 19%; ② annual deliveries reach 500,000 units; ③ no new round of price cuts after Pengcheng's listing. If all three hold, the auto business turns profitable in 2027 and can command a 25x valuation; if any one breaks, treat it as "structural" and the valuation can only be 15x.

The implication: you cannot pay for the auto business's optimistic scenario today, but you can buy it when that scenario is proven false.

One point of contention remains: the significance of buybacks

Through Buffett's lens, the HKD 20 billion buyback in 2026Q1 is a positive signal: management sees the shares as undervalued. Li Lu's lens cautions that the buyback equals only 2.7% of market cap, too small to offset the pace of profit decline, so its signal value outweighs its financial value.

Verdict: both are right. The buyback proves that "management does not think HKD 30 is overvalued," not that "HKD 30 is undervalued." Treat it as a sentiment indicator, not a valuation basis. If Q3 steps up to above HKD 30 billion, upgrade it to a basis for judgment.

Summary: Watch, three triggers

Final verdict from the four roles plus Team Lead: Rating: Watch — wait for handset gross margin to bottom; no target price, only three trigger conditions.

  • Buy condition A (fundamentals): handset gross margin ≥10% for two consecutive quarters, signalling memory pressure has peaked.
  • Buy condition B (price): share price ≤HKD 24, equivalent to 23x 2026E, turning expected value positive.
  • Veto condition C (red line): auto gross margin falls below 15%, or handset gross margin falls below 7%, signalling the business is deteriorating rather than merely cyclical.

Either A or B alone permits an initial position (no more than one-third of planned position size); if C triggers, exit and watch from the sidelines.That is the entire operating discipline; no need to forecast the share price.

Conclusion

Team Lead ruling: the points of agreement — a middling business, an exogenous downturn, a rich price — justify "do not buy"; the points of disagreement — whether autos are in an investment phase or a structural problem — do not affect the decision today, only the position cap after purchase. The call is "watch" rather than "avoid" because the company's product strength and execution are intact; what is broken is the cycle and the price. Wait for handset gross margin to hold above 10% for two quarters, or wait for HKD 24.
