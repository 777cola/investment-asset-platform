Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Xiaomi Corporation

Gross margin, cash conversion and capex: three numbers decide survival
Research date 2026/08/21  ·  Price HK$29.02 (+4.54%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 747 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 19.6x (2026E approximately 28x, estimated)
← Back to the hub

Core view

Read this earnings report through Buffett's four questions: does it make money, is that money cash, does it require constant reinvestment, and do the reinvested dollars earn a return. The four answers: barely, so-so, yes, and low.

Profitability: gross margin 19.8%, net margin 5.7%

2026Q2 revenue was RMB 108.922 billion with gross profit of RMB 21.57 billion, a 19.8% gross margin, down 2.7pct from 22.5% a year earlier. Net profit was RMB 9.463 billion, an 8.7% net margin; but RMB 3.2 billion of that is investment fair value, leaving a core-business net margin of just 5.7% (62.19 ÷ 1,089.22).

That is middling for manufacturing: better than contract manufacturing (3%–5%), worse than branded goods (10%–15%). Buffett would note one thing — the 2.7pct gross margin decline equates to RMB 4.5 billion less gross profit, exactly matching the RMB 4.6 billion drop in adjusted net profit. In other words, this quarter's profit decline came almost entirely from gross margin, not from runaway expenses or volume.

That cuts both ways: the good news is expense discipline held (R&D rose 18.9%); the bad news is the gross margin decline is set by BOM, and the company cannot change it.

Is the earnings cash-backed? Operating cash flow is thinning

Xiaomi has historically converted cash well, helped by large advance receipts (deposits on both phones and cars), long payables terms and fast inventory turnover. Before 2024, operating cash flow consistently exceeded net profit.

2026 is different. Two things happened at once: first, memory price increases forced pre-emptive stockpiling, tying up cash in inventory; second, the auto business ties up far more cash in finished-vehicle and work-in-progress inventory than phones (a single car costs over RMB 200,000, so one month of output is several billion). As a result, operating cash flow/net profit fell from 1.3 to around 1.0 (rough estimate; the full interim cash flow statement is not yet out).

Buffett watches this ratio because it is the only test of whether profit is real. 1.0 is still a pass, but below 0.7 would mean profit is only on paper. The risk now is not whether profit is real, but that cash is being consumed by inventory and capacity.

Does it require continuous capex: yes, and heavy

This is Xiaomi's biggest change. In the phone era capex was light, a few billion a year; carmaking has made the business asset-heavy: Beijing Phase 1 at 150,000 units/year, Phase 2 doubling that, plus chip tape-outs (XRING O1 is 3nm-class, with a single tape-out costing in the hundreds of millions) and AI compute investment.

R&D expense is the most direct evidence: RMB 9.2 billion in Q2 2026 alone, +18.9% YoY, or RMB 36.8 billion annualized.That equals 1.5x 2026 adjusted net profit (about RMB 24.6 billion).A company putting 1.5x its net profit into R&D leaves only two possibilities: it is betting on the future, or filling in the past.

Buffett's stance on capex is clear:it is only worthwhile when 1 yuan invested creates more than 1 yuan of market value.Xiaomi's car investment currently creates revenue (+17.1%) and share (100,000 units in the quarter), not yet profit. That account cannot be settled until 2027.

Returns: ROE of 8.5% does not justify 28x

The ten-year average ROE is 8.5%, and it will be lower still in 2026, as profit falls and net assets swell from the rights issue. DuPont breakdown: net margin 5.7% × asset turnover of about 1.0 × equity multiplier of about 1.7 ≈ 9.7% (rough calculation; the treatment of investment gains creates slight differences).

None of the three factors stands out. The low net margin comes from hardware; turnover is not low but is dragged down by the auto business's heavy asset base; low leverage is a virtue, but it also caps ROE.

Buffett's principle is "buy a great company at a fair price," not "buy a mediocre company at a cheap price." Xiaomi sits between the two: the company is not great, and the price is not cheap.An ROE of 8.5% against 28x P/E implies ROE doubling within three years—which requires the auto business to turn profitable and memory prices to fall at the same time.

Conclusion

Financial conclusion: a middling-gross-margin business with passable cash conversion that requires continuous reinvestment. The gross margin decline to 19.8% stems entirely from BOM; expense discipline is not the issue. Operating cash flow/net profit is about 1.0, still at the passing line; R&D runs at RMB 9.2 billion per quarter, annualised at 1.5x net profit, and the investment phase is far from over; an ROE of 8.5% cannot support 28x. Buffett's verdict would be: good management, ordinary business, too expensive a price.
