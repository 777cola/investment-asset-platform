Core thesis

This earnings report has only one protagonist: gross margin. Gross margin fell from 22.5% to 19.8%, cutting gross profit by RMB 4.52 billion; adjusted net profit fell by RMB 4.61 billion. The two figures are nearly equal, showing the profit decline is not a cost problem or a volume problem — it is a gross margin problem.

Breaking it down: automotive revenue rose from 17.9% to 22.9% of the total, with the revenue mix shifting toward "more expensive things"; but automotive gross margin fell 7.2pct and smartphone gross margin fell 3.0pct, with the profit mix shifting toward "thinner." This is the concrete shape of "bigger scale, less profit."

Layer one: the revenue mix is improving, but not enough to prop up the total

Revenue of RMB 108.92 billion, down 6.1% YoY, marks Xiaomi's first negative quarterly revenue growth in nearly three years. Breaking it into three parts: automotive and AI innovation RMB 24.90 billion, up 17.1% YoY, with its share rising from 17.9% to 22.9%; smartphones RMB 42.1 billion, down 7.5% YoY; IoT RMB 31.3 billion, down 19.2% YoY.

The arithmetic is clear: smartphones fell RMB 3.4 billion and IoT fell RMB 7.4 billion, a combined RMB 10.8 billion decline; automotive added RMB 3.3 billion, recovering only a third.The growth engine has shifted to automotive, but automotive is not yet large enough to offset the decline in the other two segments.

The good news on structure is that "quality" is improving: the average selling price of automotive is RMB 229,300, 170 times that of smartphones. More expensive, more durable goods should in theory be more stable.The bad news is that gross margin on this new business is trending down, so structural improvement has not translated into profit improvement.

Layer two: gross margin — the whole story is here

Blended gross margin 19.8%, down 2.7pct YoY, translating to RMB 4.52 billion less gross profit. Three segments:

  • Smartphones 8.5% (11.5% last year): memory price hikes hit margins directly. At an ASP of RMB 1,322, gross profit per unit is RMB 112, down 31% YoY (smartphone gross profit about RMB 3.6 billion).
  • EVs 19.2% (26.4% last year): ASP down 9.6% and product mix shifted downmarket, cutting gross profit per vehicle from RMB 67,000 (6.7万元) to RMB 44,000 (4.4万元).
  • IoT and internet services: IoT revenue -19.2%, economies of scale gone; internet services carry high gross margin but track smartphone shipments and are also under pressure.

All three segments falling at once is not a "mix shift" — it is being squeezed from three directions simultaneously. Smartphones squeezed by upstream memory, EVs squeezed by downstream price wars, IoT squeezed by the fading national subsidy.

Tier 3: Costs and profit — R&D rises against the cycle, profit falls passively

R&D RMB 9.2 billion, +18.9% YoY, 8.4% of revenue. Raising R&D 19% in a quarter when revenue fell 6.1% was a deliberate choice: none of the three tracks — auto platform, Xuanjie chip, AI large models — can pause. The money is well spent, but it makes quarterly profit look worse.

Net profit RMB 9.463 billion, -20.3% YoY; adjusted net profit RMB 6.219 billion, -42.6% YoY. The RMB 3.244 billion gap is mainly fair-value changes and disposal gains on investments. In other words, the core business earned RMB 6.2 billion and investments earned RMB 3.2 billion, together producing the RMB 9.4 billion book profit.

To read the core business, watch adjusted net profit: two consecutive quarters of declines above 40% YoY (Q1 about RMB 6.1 billion, Q2 RMB 6.219 billion, first half about RMB 12.3 billion in total). This decline is steeper than the 2022 round.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the layer that most needs watching but is hardest to see clearly

This layer carries the least information. The earnings summary does not provide a full cash flow statement, so direction can only be inferred from three things:

  • Inventory will rise markedly: memory prices are rising, so parts must be locked in early — an industry-wide move. Smartphone makers' inventory turnover has stretched from 30 days to 45 days, tying up cash in the tens of billions.
  • Work-in-progress and finished-vehicle inventory absorb large amounts of cash: 10.4 thousand vehicles in a single quarter at RMB 22.93 ten-thousand per vehicle means one month of output is an inventory exposure of RMB 24 billion. Unlike smartphones, vehicles cannot be pushed onto channels; the inventory stays on the company's own books.
  • Capex remains high: the Phase 2 plant, chip tape-outs, AI compute. A 3nm-class chip like Xuanjie costs hundreds of millions per tape-out, and a failed attempt means starting over.

The conclusion: operating cash flow is most likely still positive, but the cash conversion rate is falling; free cash flow is close to breakeven. This is also why the company has paid no dividend for eight years and can return capital to shareholders only through buybacks.

The balance sheet is relatively healthy: interest-bearing debt is very low and cash on hand is ample (exact figures pending the full interim report). The real risk is not debt service, but cash being locked up in inventory and capacity. If vehicle sales fall short of expectations, that inventory turns from an asset into an impairment.

Three things invisible at this layer

The biggest trap in reading financial reports is mistaking "not disclosed" for "no problem." This summary is missing three key pieces of information:

  • Full P&L for the auto segment: only gross margin and operating loss are given; no per-vehicle cost, no allocation rules for R&D and selling expenses. The RMB 2.6 billion loss is on a "directly attributable" basis; on a fully loaded basis it could be larger.
  • Unit sales and ASP detail for the phone business: the company gives only year-on-year growth rates, not unit volumes. The ASP of RMB 1,322 and shipments of 31.85 million units are both back-solved in this report (rough calculation), with possible error of 3%–5%.
  • Internet services revenue: this is Xiaomi's highest-gross-margin segment (historically above 70%), but this quarter it is folded into "smartphone × AIoT," with no standalone growth rate visible. Its strength directly determines whether overall gross margin can hold at 19.8%.

All three gaps point to one judgment: the "bad" in this report is certain; "how bad" is not. Assigning 28x in the face of uncertainty amounts to trading certain bad news for uncertain good news.

Conclusion

Four layers in, one sentence: this is a report of "structural deterioration, but not yet dangerous." The revenue mix is moving in the right direction (autos at 22.9%); the profit mix is moving the wrong way (gross margin -2.7pct); cash flow has gone from ample to tightly balanced. Three numbers decide what comes next: handset gross margin (8.5%), auto gross margin (19.2%), and the quarterly auto loss (RMB 2.6 billion).Only when handset gross margin returns above 10% and the auto loss narrows to within RMB 1 billion will the deterioration in this report have stopped.
