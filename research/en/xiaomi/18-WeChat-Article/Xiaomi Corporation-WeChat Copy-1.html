Lead-in

Xiaomi reported Q2 2026 results on August 18: revenue of RMB 108.9 billion, down 6.1% YoY, and adjusted net profit of RMB 6.219 billion, down 42.6% YoY. The stock has halved, falling from HKD 59.45 to 29.02.

Oddly, the shares rose for four straight days after the print, gaining 11% in total.Why is the market buying an ugly earnings report?This article addresses three things: where the money went, what the market is betting on, and what to watch from here.

I. Three numbers first

First, vehicle deliveries: 104,199 units, +28.2% YoY, the first quarter above 100,000.That is Xiaomi's best result in more than two years of carmaking, and rare in global automotive history.

Second, vehicle gross margin: 19.2%, against 26.4% a year earlier, down 7.2 percentage points. In the same quarter it sold more and earned less.

Third, smartphone gross margin: 8.5%, against 11.5% a year earlier.A phone selling for RMB 1,322 carries a gross profit of RMB 112.

Put the three together and the answer emerges:selling more (+28.2%), earning less (-42.6%).This is no contradiction; two things are happening at once.

2. Where the money went: upstream and downstream both reaching for it

The first squeeze is upstream: memory prices are rising. DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% in early 2026, and NAND more than 100%. Memory accounts for 15%–25% of handset costs, and against that headwind an 8.5% gross margin leaves almost no buffer.

The second squeeze is downstream: the auto price war. Xiaomi's average vehicle price fell 9.6%, from RMB 253,700 (RMB 25.37 million) to RMB 229,300 (RMB 22.93 million), while gross margin dropped 7.2 points and the operating loss widened from RMB 300 million to RMB 2.6 billion.

One raises costs, the other cuts selling prices. Caught in between, gross profit fell by RMB 4.5 billion and net profit by RMB 4.6 billion—almost one to one.

So don't blame management. R&D spending rose 18.9% to RMB 9.2 billion; expenses are not out of control—all three businesses are being squeezed at once.

III. Why the market is still rising: expectations are already very low

On earnings day the stock rose just 1.2%, then 4.8%, 1.2% and 4.5% over the following three sessions — a gain of 11% in four days.

The reason is simple: sell-side analysts had already cut their 2026 net profit forecast to around RMB 24 billion ahead of the results, and the actual number came in no worse, so the bad news was fully priced in. The stock had also already rebounded from its June low of HKD 21.42, and shorts needed to cover.

The nature of this rebound, however, needs to be seen clearly: it is short covering, not long build-up. The test is simple — volume expanded, but the stock failed to break above its July high of HKD 31.88.

IV. The 19.6x PE is an illusion

Many software platforms show Xiaomi at a P/E of 19.6x, which looks inexpensive. But that multiple is based on trailing four-quarter profit, and two of those quarters pre-date the memory price increases.

On current earning power: adjusted net profit of RMB 12.3 billion in the first half of 2026, annualized at RMB 24.6 billion, against a market cap of HKD 747 billion (RMB 687.2 billion), implies 28x.

The stock fell from 59.45 to 29.02, down 51%, yet the valuation did not get cheaper — because earnings fell faster than the share price.

This is the "Davis double kill": EPS down, multiple down, both working at once. It is not a one-off de-rating but a process.

One detail stands out: over these four days, the Hang Seng Tech Index rose about 6% and Xiaomi rose 11%, outperforming by 5 points. Some capital is clearly buying actively, but it is buying the Pengcheng expectation and oversold recovery, not current earnings. Telling β from α is simple — check whether it outperformed the index, and whether the reason for outperformance is the company's own doing.

Five common misreadings, clarified at once

Misreading 1: "Revenue fell only 6%, so it's not that bad." Revenue blends volume and price; profit is the business. Adjusted net profit fell 42.6% — that is the real picture.

Misreading 2: "Net profit is still RMB 9.4 billion — wasn't it supposed to be halved?" Of the RMB 9.463 billion, RMB 3.2 billion is fair-value gains on investments — asset appreciation, not money earned from operations.

Misreading 3: "Cars are selling well, so the valuation should rise." Cars sell well but lose heavily (RMB 2.6 billion per quarter), turning from an "option" into a "burden" — which means the valuation method has to change.

Misreading 4: "It's down 51%, so it must be cheap." At 28x 2026E, it is not cheap at all. Before the drop it was 57x.

Misreading 5: "Lei Jun is working this hard, so he will surely turn it around." Capability cannot solve a cost cycle. Memory price hikes are set by Samsung and SK hynix, not something Lei Jun can negotiate away.

V. When to look at it

My rating is "Watch — wait for handset gross margin to bottom." This is not a bearish call: the company has not deteriorated, and its product strength, channel efficiency and auto ramp-up speed have all been validated over the past two years.

I am waiting for two signals:

  • Handset gross margin back above 10% for two consecutive quarters, indicating memory pressure has peaked;
  • The stock falling below HKD 24, equivalent to 23x 2026 expected earnings, at which point the risk-reward turns positive.

Either signal can come first. Until then, the best action is no action.

6. Final sentence

Xiaomi's earnings report proves one thing: "success" and "making money" are two different things.

Moving 100,000 vehicles in a single quarter is real success; a -43% profit is also real. The stock rose 11% in four days as the market bet on a cycle reversal, but at 28x 2026 expectations that bet is no longer cheap.

My conclusion is simple: this company deserves a place on the watchlist, but not a purchase at 28x. Wait for handset gross margin to return to 10%, or for HKD 24.

The hardest part of investing isn't understanding a company, it's holding back from buying once you do. Xiaomi is right now the name that requires holding back.

Conclusion

In one line: Xiaomi's halving reflects a squeeze on profits from both ends—upstream memory costs and downstream price wars—not a deterioration in the business; 28x 2026E is not cheap, so wait for gross margin to recover to 10% or for HKD 24.
