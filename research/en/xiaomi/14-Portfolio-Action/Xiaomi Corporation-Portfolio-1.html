Core thesis

This is a name where we "have no position yet," so the position sizing starts from 0: no position at HKD 29.02; build an initial 3% position below HKD 24; add to 6% below HKD 19; if handset gross margin returns above 10%, go to 8%.

The stop-loss trigger is not the share price but gross margin: if auto gross margin falls below 15%, or handset gross margin falls below 7%, exit unconditionally. The logic is simple — a falling share price is market sentiment, a falling gross margin means the business has deteriorated, and the two must be handled separately.

Why no base position: paying 28x for profit down 43%

The usual playbook — "buy a little first, add on dips" — does not work with Xiaomi, because the decline is fundamentally driven: this is not a sentiment selloff but EPS downgrades.

Run the numbers: 2026 net profit of RMB 24.6 billion at 28x implies a market cap of RMB 687.2 billion. If 2027 net profit falls to RMB 20.0 billion (bear case), the same 28x implies a market cap of just RMB 559.0 billion, equivalent to HKD 23.6. In other words, doing nothing while profit simply tracks the bear case leaves 19% more downside in the stock.

"Buy a little first" carries negative expectancy in this structure. A 0 position is not conservatism — it is a decision made after running the numbers.

Why HKD 24 is the first entry point

HKD 24 equates to 23x 2026E and 20x 2027E (base case RMB 30 billion). What that multiple implies: the market is pricing "manufacturing plus a bit of growth," not an "ecosystem premium."

At HKD 24, risk/reward across the three scenarios is: bull 40.1 (+67%), base 25.3 (+5%), bear 12.7 (-47%). Expected value of about HKD 24.5 sits slightly above the entry price — negative expectancy turns marginally positive.

This is not "so cheap you buy with your eyes closed"; it is "the risk/reward is finally no longer a loss." Hence a 3% position only, with no adds.

Why stop-losses should be anchored to gross margin, not the share price

Most investors set stop-losses on price — exit after a 20% drop, say. On cyclicals that rule cuts both ways: a 20% fall can be indiscriminate selling (add) or a broken business (exit), and price cannot tell them apart.

Gross margin can. Smartphone gross margin of 8.5% reflects the cycle (memory price hikes); below 7% it reflects the business (worse than contract manufacturing). Auto gross margin of 19.2% reflects competition (price war); below 15% it reflects the model (fixed costs not covered). Both lines mark the boundary between "structural" and "cyclical."

The discipline follows: when price falls, read gross margin to decide whether to add or wait; when gross margin breaks the line, exit regardless of price.

Correlation with other holdings: offers no diversification benefit

Xiaomi's correlation with several asset classes is roughly as follows (rough estimates based on directional judgment from 2026 daily data):

  • Hang Seng Tech: around 0.8. Highly correlated — owning Xiaomi is effectively levered exposure to Hong Kong tech.
  • New-energy vehicles (BYD, Li Auto, Tesla): 0.6–0.7. The auto business makes Xiaomi look increasingly like an automaker.
  • Memory stocks (Samsung, SK hynix, GigaDevice): around negative 0.3. The relationship runs opposite on the cost side — memory prices rise, Xiaomi's gross margin falls.
  • Apple chain: around 0.5. Both are consumer electronics, but the Apple chain earns contract-manufacturing fees, a different logic.

Conclusion: Xiaomi cannot diversify Hong Kong tech exposure; it only amplifies it. Any allocation must come with cuts to Hang Seng Tech or automaker positions, otherwise total exposure will spiral out of control.

Three portfolio-level disciplines

First, cap any single position at 10%. Xiaomi's governance structure — one-man decision-making — warrants a position cap, however bullish the view.

Second, avoid overlap with similar holdings. If BYD or a Hang Seng Tech ETF is already held, scale the Xiaomi position down accordingly.

Third, hard-code trigger conditions; no judgment calls in the moment. Buy 3% at HKD 24, buy 6% at HKD 19, add to 8% if gross margin holds at 10% or above for two quarters, exit in full on a line break — four rules written in advance, so execution needs no "let me think again."

The core of this discipline: separate "I like this company" from "do I buy it now." The former is a research conclusion (a good company); the latter is an odds calculation (not cheap).

Conclusion

Portfolio conclusion: 0 position at the current price; wait for HKD 24 or wait for gross margin. Build the position 3% → 6% → 8% → 10%, each step on explicit triggers. The stop-loss is gross margin, not the share price: auto below 15% and handsets below 7% mean unconditional exit. Within Hong Kong tech this is a high-correlation name — not a diversifier, only a vehicle for adding or trimming exposure.
