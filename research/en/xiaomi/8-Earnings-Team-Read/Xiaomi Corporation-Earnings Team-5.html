Core view

Four close reads across 4 languages converge on three sentences: the business is below average, the decline is exogenous, and price is the only veto. Views diverge on the fourth: whether autos are in an investment phase or a structural one. That sentence sets the position cap after buying, not whether to buy now.

Consensus 1: This is not a business that can raise prices

Four investors made the same point in different words: Duan Yongping called it "hard-earned money," Buffett said "no pricing power," Munger said "efficiency moats are not pricing power," and Li Lu said "thin profits."

The evidence is stark: handset gross margin 8.5%, auto gross margin 19.2% and down 7.2pct, average selling price per vehicle down 9.6%, gross profit per handset RMB 112.Every pricing action points down; not one points up.

Consensus 2: The decline is not management's fault

Gross profit fell RMB 4.52 billion and adjusted net profit fell RMB 4.61 billion — roughly one for one. That equation says it all: the entire decline came from gross margin, cost discipline held, and R&D spending rose 19%.

Why gross margin fell: memory price hikes (DRAM +60%–70%), the auto price war (ASP -9.6%) and the phase-out of IoT subsidies (revenue -19.2%). All three are industry-level exogenous shocks no company could avoid. The only difference is whose gross margin is thick enough to absorb them.

Consensus 3: 28x is the only problem

Duan Yongping: "would not pay 28x"; Buffett: "should be 15–18x"; Munger: "price it as a heavy-asset business"; Li Lu: "would only buy at HKD 15–19." All four converge on a fair range of 15–18x 2026E, equivalent to HKD 15.6–18.7.

At HKD 29.02, the stock trades 55%–86% above that range. That alone explains the "watch" stance: not a poor company, but a price that has yet to arrive.

Disagreement: what is the auto business worth

Duan Yongping / Buffett: gross profit per vehicle of RMB 44,000 covers 64% of fixed costs, and the business turns profitable at annual sales of 600,000 vehicles — a scale problem. If it holds, the auto business is worth HKD 200–300 billion.

Munger / Li Lu: with the price-band benchmark shifting down and margins squeezed at both ends, there is a possibility that the larger the scale, the larger the loss. If it holds, the auto business is worth 0, or even negative (because it ties up capital).

The panel's approach: do not value the auto business separately; treat it as an "option" — worth 0 now, to be valued once it turns profitable. This yields a target price of HKD 25.3 (neutral scenario, excluding the value of the auto option).

Synthesis conclusions and actions

Committee conclusion: Watch; wait for handset gross margin to bottom. Two triggers — handset gross margin ≥10% for two consecutive quarters (fundamentals), or share price ≤HKD 24 (price).

Position discipline: even if triggered, the initial position must not exceed one-third of the planned position. With the auto dispute still unresolved, a full position would be a bet on an unverified assumption.

Conclusion

Synthesis: the company qualifies, the decline is exogenous, the price is expensive, and the auto business remains unproven. The four analysts' fair-value range of 15–18x implies HKD 15.6–18.7, 55%–86% above the current price. Rating: "Watch, wait for handset gross margin to bottom"; buy trigger: handset gross margin ≥10% for two quarters, or share price ≤HKD 24.
