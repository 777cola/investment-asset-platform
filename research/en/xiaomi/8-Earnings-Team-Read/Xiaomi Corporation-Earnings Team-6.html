Xiaomi's earnings: separating "success" from "making money" — Xiaomi Corporation

Core view

Xiaomi reported second-quarter results on August 18, 2026. The stock rose 4.8% the next day, added 1.2% the day after and 4.5% on the fourth day. A report showing profit down 43% YoY drew an 11% four-day gain. The reaction is more telling than the report itself.

Look at three numbers first.

Revenue RMB 108.922 billion, down 6.1% YoY — the first single-quarter decline in nearly three years. Adjusted net profit RMB 6.219 billion, down 42.6% YoY, marking the second consecutive quarter of a decline above 40%. Vehicle deliveries 104,199 units, up 28.2% YoY, topping 100,000 in a single quarter for the first time.

Put the three numbers together and you get a paradox: selling more, earning less.

The breakdown makes it clearer. Vehicle revenue RMB 23.9 billion, up 15.9% YoY — deliveries rose 28.2% but revenue rose only 15.9%, as average selling price per vehicle fell 9.6% from RMB 253,700 to RMB 229,300. Gross margin dropped from 26.4% to 19.2% over the same period, and operating loss widened from RMB 300 million to RMB 2.6 billion. Volumes up, ASP down, gross margin down, losses widening — a textbook case of buying volume with price.

Smartphones: premiumization rescues ASP, not gross margin

Smartphone revenue RMB 42.1 billion, down 7.5% YoY; shipments down 26.5% YoY, but ASP up 25.9% YoY to a record high. Models priced above RMB 3,000 accounted for 32.1% of domestic volume. The mix shift upmarket is real and deserves credit.

Gross margin, however, fell from 11.5% to 8.5%, and there is only one reason: memory prices. In early 2026, DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% and NAND more than 100%; memory accounts for 15%–25% of smartphone BOM. That squeeze leaves an 8.5% gross margin with almost no buffer.

At an ASP of RMB 1,322, gross profit per handset is RMB 112. After logistics, warehousing and after-sales, only a few dozen yuan of that RMB 112 may reach net profit. A USD 30 increase in the price of a single memory die wipes out a quarter's smartphone profit.

The real dividing line: why the market sold off first and rallied later

Results on August 18 landed with the stock at HKD 26.18, up just 1.2%; it then rose for three straight sessions to 29.02. That is not "good results" — it is "expectations too low."

From the 52-week low of HKD 21.42 on June 26 to 29.02 on August 21, the stock has rebounded 35%. The market is betting on one thing: memory price increases are a cycle, not a trend. If DRAM prices fall back in 2027, handset gross margin can return to 10%–11% and profit will rebound sharply.

The bet makes sense, but it comes with one condition — you have to be able to wait until 2027. And at 28x 2026E, there is no margin of safety while you wait.

Valuation: 19.6x is an illusion

Many software tools show Xiaomi's P/E at 19.6x, which looks inexpensive. But that figure uses TTM profit—the past four quarters, two of which have not yet been hit by memory costs.

On current profitability: adjusted net profit of RMB 12.3 billion in the first half of 2026, annualized to RMB 24.6 billion, against a market cap of HKD 747 billion (RMB 687.2 billion), implies 28x. The stock has fallen from 59.45 to 29.02, down 51%, yet valuation has not become cheaper, because earnings are falling faster than the share price.

This is the shape of a "Davis double-kill": EPS heading down, multiple heading down. It is not a one-off de-rating; it is a process.

Conclusion: wait for two signals

My rating: "Watch — wait for handset gross margin to bottom." Not avoidance, because the company has not deteriorated; product strength, channel efficiency and the pace of the auto ramp-up have all been proven over the past two years.

I am waiting for two signals: first, handset gross margin back above 10% for two consecutive quarters, indicating memory pressure has peaked; second, the share price falling below HKD 24, equivalent to 23x 2026E, at which point the risk-reward turns positive.

Either signal can come first. Until then, the best move is no move. Xiaomi's mistake is not "not working hard enough" but "a business that is too tough" — and a tough business is only worth buying when it is cheap.

Conclusion

One-line summary: Xiaomi's results show that "success" and "making money" are two different things. 100,000 vehicles in a single quarter is real success; a -43% profit is also real. The stock rose 11% in four days on a bet on a cyclical reversal; at 28x 2026E, that bet is no longer cheap. Wait for handset gross margin to return to 10%, or wait for HKD 24.
