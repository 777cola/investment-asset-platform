Masterclass close read (Li Lu): a specimen of manufacturing upgrading, paying too high a price — Xiaomi Corporation

Look at the decade, not the quarter

Core view

Li Lu's framework is "civilizational evolution + margin of safety." Xiaomi is a rare specimen of China's manufacturing upgrade: no more than three companies in China combine brand, supply-chain integration, in-house chip design and whole-vehicle manufacturing. But a rare specimen does not mean it is a buy now.

Why it is a sample: four capabilities present at once

The first is brand. Xiaomi sells phones in more than 100 countries and ranks among the top in European market share — a rare "global consumer recognition" among Chinese brands. The second is supply-chain integration. With the same BOM, Xiaomi can achieve a lower retail price — that is organizational capability.

The third is in-house chip development. The Xuanjie O1 has shipped more than one million units cumulatively, the first time a mainland device maker has put a self-developed SoC into a mass-produced flagship. The significance of this lies not in current-period profit, but in the possibility of "not being strangled at the choke point." The fourth is vehicle manufacturing. From zero to 10.42 vehicles per quarter in two years — rare in global automotive history.

Only with these four capabilities combined does the "human-car-home" ecosystem exist. Apple has the first two and half of the third, but not the fourth; Tesla has the fourth, but not the first two. The scarcity of this combination is real.

But the scarcity is already priced in

Xiaomi's share price rose from HKD 15 to HKD 59.45 in 2025, a 3x gain. What was being priced in? Scarcity — the market suddenly realized the company held four cards at once.

The fall back to HKD 29.02 in 2026 reflects that "these four cards cannot make money." From HKD 59.45 to HKD 29.02, market cap evaporated by HKD 780 billion — equivalent to the market fully withdrawing the "ecosystem premium" and leaving only a hardware valuation.

At 28x 2026E, it is still above the 15–20x for manufacturers. In other words, the market has withdrawn the "ecosystem premium" but not yet the "growth premium." That is why there is still downside.

Margin of safety: when will Li Lu step in

Li Lu bought BYD in 2002, when its market cap was in the low billions; he bought Apple in 2016, at 10x PE. The common thread: buy when a good company is hit by bad news, not when a good company is lifted by good news.

Xiaomi already has plenty of bad news: profit halved, gross margin at a new low, auto losses widening. Yet the stock is down only 51%, with valuation still at 28x. That means the bad news is not fully priced in — a true "bad news exhausted" level should be 15–18x.

Converted to price: 15x × RMB 24.6 billion ÷ 0.92 ÷ 257.41 = HKD 15.6; 18x corresponds to HKD 18.7. That range is 35%–46% below the current HKD 29.02.

Ten years on: will it be worth more?

If the "human-car-home" ecosystem truly works—150 million phones a year, 1 million cars a year, IoT spanning 1 billion devices—its profit structure would change: hardware earns thin margins, while internet services and software subscriptions contribute the bulk of profit. At that point, 25-30x would be reasonable.

But that is an "if." Li Lu's approach: pay a little for the "if," pay a lot for the "already." Today the "already" part (phones + IoT + internet) is worth 15-18x; the "if" part has not happened yet.

So he would most likely: put it on the watchlist, set a price alert, and wait. In value investing, "waiting" is not passive—it is the most common action.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: the scarcity is real, and so is the expensive valuation. All four cards are on the table at once — a sample of China's manufacturing upgrade; but 28x 2026E has already paid in advance for the optimistic "ecosystem works" scenario. A reasonable entry point is HKD 15–19 (15–18x), 35%–46% below the current price. Wait.
