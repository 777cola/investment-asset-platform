## Upstream: memory is the real rent collector in this chain

The most profitable business in 2026 is not phones but memory. DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% early in the year and NAND by more than 100%; HBM is crowding out standard DRAM capacity, and institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027. Gross margins at Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have generally returned above 40% this cycle.

Xiaomi is a "large customer" on the procurement side, but not a "strong customer." Sixth-largest global phone shipments guarantee supply, not price: memory is a standardized product, and quotes follow contracts, not relationships. Memory accounts for 15%–25% of a phone BOM; a 60% price increase translates into a 9%–15% rise in device cost.

That alone explains why Xiaomi's phone gross margin fell from 11.5% to 8.5%. It has no hedge: it cannot lock in memory prices the way automakers lock in lithium (there is no long-term contract market for memory), nor can it pass on costs through brand premium the way Apple does (an 8.5% gross margin leaves no room to pass on costs).

## Midstream: system integrators capture value but earn the thinnest gross margins

The OEM's position is awkward: it must deliver a complete user experience, so it integrates every component, yet it has no pricing power over any supplier because it cannot bypass a single one.

The contrast is stark. Apple's gross margin on finished devices is 46%, as its brand and iOS ecosystem let it squeeze upstream suppliers and raise prices downstream; Xiaomi's is 8.5%, because it holds the high ground at neither end.Both are integrators, but Apple earns an integration premium while Xiaomi earns an integration pittance.

Autos fare slightly better, with vehicle gross margin at 19.2%, 10 points above phones, as cars carry brand attributes, safety-certification barriers and regional channel barriers.But the 2026 price war has stripped 7.2 points off that premium too.

## Downstream: channels and internet services, Xiaomi's only high-margin zone

Internet services — advertising, gaming, fintech and memberships — have historically carried gross margins above 70%, and this is the main reason Xiaomi's blended gross margin holds at 19.8%. In 2026Q2, smartphone x AIoT revenue was RMB 84.03 billion, down 11.3% YoY; internet services track the smartphone installed base and engagement, and are under pressure too.

Distribution is where Xiaomi's real strength lies: Mi Home prices are identical online and offline, inventory turnover leads the industry, and sales per square metre exceed those of traditional retail chains. That distribution efficiency underpins its "same specs at 20% lower prices" attack on the market.

But the fat downstream profits are being diluted: internet services are under 10% of total revenue and cannot carry a RMB 108.9 billion top line, while distribution efficiency saves money without generating gross profit. In one sentence: the downstream is a buffer, not an engine.

## Cycle position: memory in upcycle, autos mid-price-war

The two cycles are moving in exactly opposite directions. Memory is in an upcycle that began in late 2025 and, by industry convention, runs 4-6 quarters, implying a peak between late 2026 and the first half of 2027. Autos are mid-price-war: penetration has passed 55%, growth is slowing, capacity is excessive and the shakeout has not yet begun.

For Xiaomi, the implication is that the second half of 2026 through the first half of 2027 is the most painful window: upstream costs are still rising while downstream prices are still falling, squeezing the company from both ends at once. Only in the second half of 2027 is a dual improvement — "memory prices rolling over + auto shakeout" — likely to appear.

That also explains why the market was willing to give a rally in August: investors are trading the 2027 cycle reversal in advance. But between trading in advance and a real reversal lie two quarters of earnings reports.

## Its position in the chain: strong demand, weak supply

Xiaomi's position in the industry chain, in one sentence: brand power with users, no bargaining power with upstream suppliers.

The financial signature of that position: gross margin swings with upstream costs, revenue swings with downstream competition. Upstream costs were cheap and competition mild in 2024–2025, lifting gross margin to 22.5%; in 2026, upstream prices rose and competition intensified, and it fell to 19.8%. The same company, a 2.7-point gap across two years, entirely determined by external factors.

EVs changed this—they gave Xiaomi heavy assets and large-scale manufacturing capability for the first time, and saddled it with a fixed-cost burden for the first time. From then on, its income statement gained a new line item—capacity utilisation—a variable it never had before.
