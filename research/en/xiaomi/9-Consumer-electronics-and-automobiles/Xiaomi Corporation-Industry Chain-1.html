Core view

In the consumer electronics and auto supply chains, profit distribution is "thick at both ends, thin in the middle": upstream memory and advanced process nodes capture 35%–55% gross margins, downstream brands and software services capture 40%–70%, while assembly manufacturing in the middle gets only 8%–20%. Xiaomi sits squarely in the thinnest segment, and it cannot extend to either end.

What makes 2026 unusual is that upstream is collecting rent: DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70%, directly cutting Xiaomi's smartphone gross margin from 11.5% to 8.5%. Autos put it for the first time into a segment with fixed costs and scale thresholds, but that segment also lacks not capacity, only profit.

Upstream: memory is the real rent collector in this chain

The most profitable business in 2026 is not phones, but memory. DRAM contract prices rose 60%–70% early in the year and NAND more than 100%; HBM is crowding out standard DRAM capacity, and institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027. Gross margins at Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have generally returned above 40% in this cycle.

Xiaomi is a "large customer" on the procurement side, but not a "strong customer." Sixth-largest global phone shipments guarantee supply, not price — memory is a standardized product, and quotes follow contracts, not relationships. Memory accounts for 15%–25% of a phone BOM; a 60% price increase translates into a 9%–15% rise in device cost.

That is the entire reason Xiaomi's phone gross margin fell from 11.5% to 8.5%. It has no hedge: it cannot lock in memory prices the way automakers lock in lithium (there is no long-term contract market for memory), nor can it pass on costs through brand premium the way Apple does (an 8.5% gross margin leaves no room to pass on costs).

Midstream: system integrators capture value but earn the thinnest gross margins

The OEM's role is awkward: it must deliver a complete experience to users, so it integrates every component; yet it has no pricing power over any component supplier, because it cannot bypass a single one of them.

The contrast is clear: Apple's gross margin on finished devices is 46%, because its brand and iOS ecosystem let it squeeze upstream suppliers and raise prices downstream; Xiaomi's is 8.5%, because it holds the high ground at neither end.Both are "integrators," but Apple earns an integration premium while Xiaomi earns an integration pittance.

Autos fare slightly better: vehicle gross margin is 19.2%, 10 points above phones, because cars carry brand attributes, safety-certification barriers and regional channel barriers.But the 2026 price war has stripped 7.2 points off that premium too.

Downstream: channels and internet services, Xiaomi's only "high-margin zone"

Xiaomi's internet services segment has historically posted gross margins above 70% (advertising, gaming, fintech, memberships), and this is the key reason its overall gross margin holds at 19.8%. In 2026Q2, smartphone x AIoT revenue was RMB 84.03 billion, down 11.3% YoY; internet services track smartphone installed base and engagement, and are also under pressure.

Distribution is where Xiaomi's real strength lies: Mi Home offers the same prices online and offline, inventory turnover leads the industry, and sales per square meter exceeds traditional retail chains. This distribution efficiency is the foundation that lets it attack the market with "same specs at 20% lower prices."

But the fat profits downstream are being diluted: internet services account for less than 10% of total revenue and cannot support a RMB 108.9 billion top line; distribution efficiency saves money but cannot generate gross profit. In one sentence: the downstream is a buffer, not an engine.

Cycle position: memory in upcycle, autos mid-price-war

The two cycles are moving in exactly opposite directions. Memory is in an upcycle that began in late 2025; by industry convention a cycle runs 4-6 quarters, so it may peak between late 2026 and the first half of 2027. Autos are mid-price-war: penetration has passed 55%, growth is slowing, capacity is excessive, and the shakeout has not yet begun.

The implication for Xiaomi: the second half of 2026 through the first half of 2027 is the most painful window — upstream costs are still rising while downstream prices are still falling, a squeeze from both ends at once. Only in the second half of 2027 is a dual improvement — "memory prices rolling over + auto shakeout" — likely to appear.

That also explains why the market was willing to give a rally in August: investors are trading the 2027 cycle reversal in advance. But between trading in advance and a real reversal lie two quarters of earnings reports.

Its position in the chain: strong demand, weak supply

Xiaomi's position in the industry chain in one sentence: brand power with users, no bargaining power with upstream suppliers.

The financial signature of this position is "gross margin swings with upstream costs, revenue swings with downstream competition." In 2024–2025, upstream costs were cheap and competition was mild, so its gross margin reached 22.5%; in 2026, upstream prices rose and competition intensified, and it fell to 19.8%. The same company, a 2.7-point gap across two years, entirely determined by external factors.

EVs changed this—they gave Xiaomi heavy assets and large-scale manufacturing capability for the first time, and also saddled it with a fixed-cost burden for the first time. From then on, its income statement gained a new line item, "capacity utilization," a variable it never had before.

Conclusion

Industry-chain conclusion: Xiaomi sits at the thinnest-margin link of the entire chain. Upstream memory takes over 40%; Xiaomi takes 8.5%. Downstream channels and services earn 70%, but the volume is too small. The only way to break out is to extend to both ends — in-house chips (upstream) and software and ecosystem (downstream). Xiaomi is doing both, but neither has yet reached the point of changing its profit structure. Investment implication: the money to be made is from "cyclical reversal + stable share", not from "redistribution of industry-chain profits".
