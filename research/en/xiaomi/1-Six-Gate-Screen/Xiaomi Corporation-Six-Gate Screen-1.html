Six-Gate Screen — Xiaomi Corporation

Xiaomi Corporation (01810) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/08/21  ·  Price HK$29.02 (+4.54%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 747 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 19.6x (2026E approximately 28x, estimated)
← Back to the hub

Core view

Six gates, 38 points, four passed. Product strength, execution and ecosystem are real at Xiaomi; margin of safety and discipline are not — not now.

2026Q2 revenue RMB 108.922 billion, -6.1% YoY; adjusted net profit RMB 6.219 billion, -42.6% YoY. EV deliveries 104,200 units, first quarter above 100,000, but average selling price fell 9.6% to RMB 229,300, segment gross margin dropped from 26.4% to 19.2%, and operating loss widened to RMB 2.6 billion; smartphone gross margin 8.5%, crushed by memory price increases. HKD 29.02 corresponds to 19.6x TTM, but that 19.6x uses four quarters not yet contaminated by memory costs; on 2026 net profit of about RMB 24 billion, it is 28x. Conclusion: watch, wait for smartphone gross margin to recover sequentially, or for HKD 24 or below.

Gate one to gate three: understandable, executable, defensible

First gate: 8 points. All three businesses can be modeled: smartphones RMB 42.1 billion at an 8.5% gross margin; IoT RMB 31.3 billion; EVs RMB 23.9 billion at a 19.2% gross margin. Revenue is broken out by segment, and average selling price can be derived from revenue ÷ deliveries — transparent by hardware-company standards. The 2-point deduction is because no one can reliably estimate the long-run steady-state margin of the EV business — it has been delivering for only two years, capacity is still ramping, and the company has not clarified how much of the RMB 2.6 billion quarterly loss is fixed-cost absorption versus price concessions.

Second gate: 6 points, a bare pass. The test of a good business is "raising prices without losing volume"; Xiaomi is the opposite: smartphone ASP rose 25.9% year on year to a record high, yet gross margin fell from 11.5% to 8.5%; EV deliveries rose 28.2%, but average price fell 9.6% and gross margin fell 7.2pct. Rising volume and price showed up only in revenue, not in profit. The only credit is internet services — high margin, asset-light — but in 2026Q2 combined smartphone × AIoT revenue fell 11.3%, with advertising and gaming also under pressure, and this segment cannot support the RMB 108.9 billion top line.

Third gate: 7 points. A moat is not a concept; it is something you can point to: models priced above RMB 3,000 account for 32.1% of domestic units, showing premiumization has genuinely taken hold; Xiaomi Home keeps online and offline prices aligned and inventory turnover near the top of the industry; human-car-home connectivity creates switching costs. But these three moats block "user churn", not "gross margin decline". Against memory makers, Xiaomi has no pricing power; against BYD and Huawei, it can only match prices.

Stage four: Lei Jun's execution is the only thing that has not been discounted this time

I'd give it 8 points. The 2 points deducted are not about capability but about "one person carrying too much." From the SU7 launch in March 2024 to 104,200 units in a single quarter in 2026Q2, the auto business took just over two years; the Beijing Phase 1 line of 150,000 units/year is running at full capacity, and Phase 2 will double that once production begins (rough estimate). Over the same period, the Xuanjie O1 entered mass production with cumulative shipments exceeding one million chips, making this the first mainland China device maker to put a 3nm-class in-house SoC into a mass-produced flagship.

Capital allocation also holds up: in 2026Q1 it announced a HKD 20 billion buyback, buying its own stock with real money around HKD 30; a HKD 42.5 billion placement in March 2025 diluted shareholders by about 3%, with the proceeds going into autos and chips rather than plugging holes. A hardware company willing to keep spending RMB 9.2 billion on R&D in a quarter when profit fell (+18.9% YoY) is evidence of long-termism, and one reason current-quarter profit looks ugly.

Where it loses points: both key figures in the auto business were brought in from outside, and Lei Jun himself is chairman, CEO and head of the auto unit, so the depth of the organization has yet to be proven. He has said autos are his "last major venture," and the flip side of that is—there is no second chance for error.

Gate 5: 19.6x is an illusion; 28x is the real number

Start with the market cap: HKD 29.02 × 25.741 billion shares = HKD 747 billion, about RMB 687.2 billion (at HKD 1 ≈ RMB 0.92). TTM adjusted net profit is about RMB 35 billion, giving 19.6x — the number is right, but the problem is the denominator.

TTM includes the second half of 2025, when memory prices had not yet risen and auto gross margin was still 26%. Adjusted net profit of RMB 12.3 billion in the first half of 2026 annualizes to RMB 24.6 billion, implying 28x; annualizing Q2's RMB 6.219 billion alone gives just RMB 24.9 billion, also around 28x. In other words: the stock is down 51%, yet the valuation has not gotten cheaper, because earnings fell faster than the share price.

This is the "unstable denominator makes the multiple meaningless" problem. In the bear case, 2027 net profit of RMB 20 billion at 15x gives a target price of HKD 12.7; base case RMB 30 billion at 20x gives HKD 25.3; bull case RMB 38 billion at 25x gives HKD 40.1. The current price sits just above the base case, a 1:1 risk-reward with no margin of safety.

Stage six: the buy case rests in someone else's hands

This question is not about "will it rise," but "if your assumptions are wrong, do you know where they are wrong." The current buy case for Xiaomi rests on two statements: memory prices fall back, and auto gross margin recovers. The deciding power over these two things lies with Samsung/SK hynix/YMTC/CXMT, and with BYD/Huawei/Tesla, not with Xiaomi.

Only three observables can be put into numbers: ① whether handset gross margin can return above 10% (now 8.5%); ② whether the auto segment gross margin can hold at 19% (now 19.2%, versus 26.4% last year); ③ whether the auto operating loss can narrow from RMB -2.6 billion to within RMB -1 billion. All three can be read in the Q3 report around November, with no need to wait a year.

The discipline gate scores 5 because it cannot even specify "how long to wait." By industry convention, the memory upcycle runs 4–6 quarters; having started rising in early 2026, it could ease at the earliest in the first half of 2027. That means "watching" may be a matter of a year, not a month.

Veto items and items pending verification

Veto item: margin of safety.28x 2026E for a business with profit down 43% year on year and likely to fall further next quarter. Xiaomi's valuation anchor has historically had two regimes: 15–20x for a manufacturer, over 30x for an ecosystem grower. It is now moving from the latter to the former, with no support in between.

Three items to verify, ranked by importance:

  • Handset gross margin: whether it holds above 10% in Q3. This is the only hard indicator of whether memory pressure has peaked, and it matters more than shipments.
  • Auto gross margin and losses: whether 19.2% is the bottom; whether the Pengcheng launch triggers another price war that pushes losses back above RMB 3 billion.
  • Buyback intensity: RMB 20 billion already repurchased in Q1; if Q3 steps up further, management sees HKD 25–30 as the value zone.

If two of the three turn, gates five and six of the six-gate test can be rerun.Not yet worth it.

Conclusion

38/60, four gates passed, the conclusion is watch rather than avoid. The reason to avoid is price, not the company — Xiaomi's product strength, channel efficiency and auto ramp speed have all been repeatedly validated over the past two years; it has not deteriorated, its profit is simply squeezed from both ends by upstream memory and downstream price wars. HKD 29.02, 28x 2026E, odds 1:1, and the win rate depends on two exogenous variables. Triggers are fixed: handset gross margin back above 10% (wait for fundamentals), or the share price falling below HKD 24 (wait for price). Until one of the two is hit, watch only, no action.
