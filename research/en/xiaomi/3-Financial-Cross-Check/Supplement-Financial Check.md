## Basis gap 1: RMB 9.463 billion vs RMB 6.219 billion — what drives the RMB 3.2 billion difference?

This is where misreading happens most easily. The same report contains two "profit" figures: net profit of RMB 9.463 billion and adjusted net profit of RMB 6.219 billion. The RMB 3.2 billion gap is mainly fair value changes and disposal gains on investments — Xiaomi holds large positions in ecosystem companies and listed equities, and these assets are marked to market: gains flow into profit, losses into loss.

In 2026Q2 both Hong Kong and A-share markets rebounded (the Hang Seng Tech Index recovered from its lows during the quarter), contributing positive returns. This is not operating income, and it could reverse next year. This report therefore uses adjusted net profit as the denominator throughout: valuation, YoY and annualized figures all use RMB 6.219 billion, not RMB 9.463 billion.

A cross-check: adjusted net profit fell 42.6% YoY, net profit fell 20.3% — a 22-point gap between the two declines. If the market only sees "net profit down 20%" it looks less bad, but the core business was cut in half. That is the real reason the share price fell from 59.45 to 29.02.

## Basis difference 2: whose basis does the -26.5% handset shipment figure use?

The company reported handset shipments down 26.5% YoY. Quarterly data from third-party agencies (IDC, Canalys) often fails to reconcile, as it is unclear whether they capture sell-in (vendor shipments to channels) or sell-through (sales to consumers). Xiaomi is actively destocking in 2026Q2, so sell-in will run noticeably below sell-through, with the two measures diverging by 5–8 points.

The distinction matters for the call: if the -26.5% reflects deliberate destocking, Q3 will claw it back; if demand has genuinely fallen, it is share loss. I lean toward the former — ASP rose 25.9% YoY over the same period to a record high, and the share of domestic sales above RMB 3,000 reached 32.1%. Selling fewer units at higher prices is a classic mix shift upmarket plus channel inventory clearance.

My approach: use only company-reported shipments for YoY comparisons, never mixed with third-party data; leave the share call for m11, to be discussed separately using third-party data.

## The 1.6% gap: automotive operating loss

Company announcement: the automotive and other innovation business segment reported an operating loss of RMB 2.6 billion. Sell-side analysts back out the figure as "segment revenue of RMB 23.9 billion × (1-19.2%) - allocated period expenses," arriving at -RMB 2.64 billion, a 1.6% deviation.

The gap is in expense allocation: sell-side analysts allocated part of group-level R&D and administrative expenses to the automotive segment by revenue proportion, whereas the company announcement counts only directly attributable portions.1.6% is within an acceptable range, but it is a reminder that the RMB 2.6 billion loss is the most conservative number, and the true full-cost loss may be larger.

In valuation, therefore, I use "automotive segment loss of RMB 2.6 billion + a portion of the group's RMB 9.2 billion in R&D" rather than treating RMB 2.6 billion as the full economic cost of the automotive business.

## What data is still missing

Two figures are unavailable this round, and I flag them so they are not mistaken for knowns: first, the specific operating cash flow amount for the first half of 2026 — the results summary gives only balance sheet and income statement highlights, with the full cash flow statement pending the interim report body; second, the per-vehicle cost of the auto segment — the company discloses only gross margin, not a breakdown of BOM and manufacturing expenses.

These two gaps affect the judgment of the "structure of the loss," not the judgment of the "direction of the loss." When the direction is clear but the structure is not, the valuation must be discounted — which is why I dare use only 20x for the neutral scenario.
