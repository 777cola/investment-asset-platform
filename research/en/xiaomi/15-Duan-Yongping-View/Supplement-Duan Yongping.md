# Xiaomi Corporation（01810）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/08/21
- Price：29.02 HKD
- Market cap：About HKD 747 billion

## Notes

The Q&A restates the analysis in Duan Yongping's publicly expressed style — benfen, business attributes, staying within one's circle of competence, price, the stop-doing list — and is not a statement by Duan himself. Figures tie to the working papers: 2026Q2 handset gross margin 8.5%, auto gross margin 19.2%, net profit RMB 24.6 billion (roughly annualised), share price HKD 29.02. Bear case HKD 10–13 = RMB 20 billion × 12–15x ÷ 0.92 ÷ 25.741 billion shares.

## Q: Is this a good business?

Answer: not great, but not bad either. The test of a good business is whether it can raise prices without losing volume; Xiaomi is the reverse — it cuts prices to hold volume, and loses orders the moment it raises them.

Look at the numbers: handsets carry an 8.5% gross margin — sell one for RMB 1,322 and you make RMB 112. Autos carry 19.2% — sell one for RMB 229,300 for RMB 44,000 of gross profit, and after costs it loses RMB 25,000. This is not a question of "how much profit" — it is a question of "earning just enough to survive."

But it has one virtue: the demand is real. The world buys 1.2 billion handsets and 30 million vehicles a year, and this business is not going away. A business that will not disappear plus thin gross margins equals "hard-earned money" — viable, but do not count on windfall profits.

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

A: Yes — and most likely bigger than it is now.

Three reasons. First, phones are a necessity, Xiaomi ranks in the global top three by share, and it has both channel and brand. Second, the car business has proven out: 100,000 units in two years shows both organisation and supply chain are working. Third, once the "human-car-home" ecosystem takes shape, the cost to users of switching brands keeps rising.

But "still here" and "highly profitable" are two different things. Take home appliances: Gree and Midea are both still around 10 years later, and their share prices over 10 years were nothing special. For manufacturing companies, surviving a long time is the norm; making big money is the exception.

So the answer to this question does not affect whether to buy. What really affects whether to buy is the next question.

## Q: Can you understand it?

Answer: I understand eight-tenths of it.

Phones and IoT I understand completely: units sold, price per unit, gross margin — all calculable. Cars I understand seven-tenths: deliveries, average selling price and gross margin are disclosed, but capacity utilisation, fixed cost per vehicle and R&D allocation are not, so I can only infer them.

The two-tenths I cannot understand is "how much the ecosystem synergy is actually worth." The concept sounds appealing, but it never hits the income statement. What I cannot understand, I do not value.

So my approach: value only the three hard businesses — phones + IoT + cars — and treat the ecosystem as a freebie. A freebie gets no value, and that is how I avoid overpaying.

## Q: At what price to buy?

Answer: below HKD 24 is worth considering; below HKD 19 is comfortable.

The math: 2026 net profit of RMB 24.6 billion; HKD 24 implies 23x, HKD 19 implies 18x. Good manufacturers (Midea, Gree) trade at 12–18x year-round; growth names (BYD) at 20–25x. Xiaomi is neither pure manufacturer nor pure growth — it sits in between, so roughly 20x is reasonable.

At HKD 29.02 and 28x today, it carries "a growth stock's multiple on a manufacturer's earnings." That mismatch will correct sooner or later — either the share price comes down or profit goes up. I bet on the former, because profit is not the company's call.

One more thing: do not assume it is cheap just because it "fell 51%." Still 28x after a 51% drop means it was 56x before. Going from 56x to 28x is "mean reversion"; going from 28x to 20x is "cheap."

## Q: What is the biggest fear?

A: The biggest fear is another two years of memory price increases.

This is not alarmism. DRAM rose 60%–70% in early 2026, institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027, and HBM continues to crowd out capacity. If prices rise another round in 2027, Xiaomi's smartphone gross margin falls from 8.5% to below 6%, and the smartphone business swings from profit to loss.

What happens then? The market reclassifies Xiaomi from "hardware + internet" to "pure hardware," assigning 12–15x. On RMB 20 billion net profit, that implies a market cap of RMB 240–300 billion and a share price of HKD 10–13. This is not fearmongering; it is the arithmetic of the bear case.

The second fear is an out-of-control EV price war. If another round of price cuts follows the Pengcheng launch, EV gross margin falls below 15%, and quarterly losses widen to RMB 4 billion, the EV business goes from "investment" to "bleeding," and the entire thesis must be rewritten.

## Q: So what should we do now?

Answer: do nothing, just watch.

This is not passivity. Most of investing is waiting—waiting for a good company to hit bad news, waiting for the price to fall to a level that needs no calculation.Xiaomi's problem right now is not that "the good news hasn't been discovered yet" but that "the bad news hasn't finished coming out."

I'll put it on the watchlist and set two alerts: one at a price of HKD 24, one on the handset gross margin in the Q3 report. Whichever comes first, I look at that first.If both arrive, then I buy.

One last word on my real view of this company: Lei Jun is a product man, not a capital man. This kind of company gets hyped very high in a bull market and slaughtered in a bear market.Your job isn't to judge whether he's up to it—it's to judge when the market hands the price back to him.
