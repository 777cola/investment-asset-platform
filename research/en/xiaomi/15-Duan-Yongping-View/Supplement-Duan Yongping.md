# Xiaomi Corporation（01810）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/08/21
- Price：29.02 HKD
- Market cap：About HKD 747 billion

## Notes

The Q&A restates the analysis in Duan Yongping's publicly expressed style (benfen, business attributes, staying within one's circle of competence, price, stop-doing list); it is not a statement by Duan himself. Figures match the working papers: Q2 2026 handset gross margin 8.5%, auto gross margin 19.2%, net profit RMB 24.6 billion (rough annualized), share price HKD 29.02. Bear case HKD 10–13 = RMB 20 billion × 12–15x ÷ 0.92 ÷ 25.741 billion shares.

## Q: Is this a good business?

Answer: not great, but not bad either. The test of a good business is "raise prices without losing volume"; Xiaomi is the reverse — it cuts prices to hold volume, and loses orders the moment it raises them.

Look at the numbers: handsets carry an 8.5% gross margin — sell one for RMB 1,322 and you make RMB 112; autos carry 19.2% — sell one for RMB 229,300, gross profit RMB 44,000, and after costs it loses RMB 25,000. **This is not a question of "how much profit" — it is a question of "earning just enough to survive."**

But it has one virtue: the demand is real. The world buys 1.2 billion handsets a year and 30 million vehicles a year, and this business is not going away. **A business that will not disappear plus thin gross margins equals "hard-earned money" — viable, but do not count on windfall profits.**

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

A: Yes. And most likely bigger than it is now.

Three reasons: first, phones are a necessity, Xiaomi ranks in the global top three by share, and it has both channel and brand; second, the car business has proven out — 100,000 units in two years shows both organization and supply chain are working; third, once the "human-car-home" ecosystem takes shape, the cost to users of switching brands keeps rising.

But "still here" and "highly profitable" are two different things. Look at home appliances: Gree and Midea are both still around 10 years later, and their share prices over 10 years were nothing special. **For manufacturing companies, surviving a long time is the norm; making big money is the exception.**

So the answer to this question does not affect whether to buy. **What really affects whether to buy is the next question.**

## Q: Can you understand it?

Answer: I understand eight-tenths of it.

Phones and IoT I understand completely: how many units sold, price per unit, gross margin — all calculable. Cars I understand seven-tenths: deliveries, average selling price and gross margin are available, but capacity utilization, fixed cost per vehicle and R&D allocation are not disclosed, so I can only infer them.

The two-tenths I cannot understand is "how much the ecosystem synergy is actually worth." The concept sounds appealing, but it never hits the income statement. **What I cannot understand, I do not value.**

So my approach: value only the three hard businesses — phones + IoT + cars — and treat the ecosystem as a freebie. **A freebie gets no value, and that is how I avoid overpaying.**

## Q: At what price to buy?

Answer: below HKD 24 is worth considering; below HKD 19 is comfortable.

Here is the math: 2026 net profit of RMB 24.6 billion; HKD 24 implies 23x, HKD 19 implies 18x. Good manufacturing companies (Midea, Gree) trade at 12–18x year-round; growth names (BYD) at 20–25x. **Xiaomi is neither pure manufacturing nor pure growth — it sits in between, so roughly 20x is reasonable.**

At HKD 29.02 and 28x today, it carries "a growth stock's price on a manufacturer's earnings." **That mismatch will correct sooner or later — either the share price comes down or the profit goes up. I bet on the former, because profit is not the company's call.**

One more thing: do not assume it is cheap just because it "fell 51%." Still 28x after a 51% drop means it was 56x before. **Going from 56x to 28x is "mean reversion"; going from 28x to 20x is "cheap."**

## Q: What is the biggest fear?

A: The biggest fear is another two years of memory price increases.

This is not alarmism. In early 2026, DRAM rose 60%–70%, institutions say the shortage will persist into 2027, and HBM is still crowding out capacity. **If prices rise another round in 2027, Xiaomi's smartphone gross margin will fall from 8.5% to below 6%, and the smartphone business will go from profitable to unprofitable.**

What happens then? The market will reclassify Xiaomi from "hardware + internet" to "pure hardware," assigning 12–15x. On RMB 20 billion net profit, that implies a market cap of RMB 240–300 billion and a share price of HKD 10–13. **This is not fearmongering; it is the arithmetic of the bear case.**

The second fear is an out-of-control EV price war. If another round of price cuts follows the Pengcheng launch, EV gross margin falls below 15%, and quarterly losses widen to RMB 4 billion, then the EV business goes from "investment" to "bleeding," and the entire thesis must be rewritten.

## Q: So what should we do now?

Answer: do nothing, just watch.

This is not passivity. Most of investing is waiting—waiting for a good company to hit bad news, waiting for the price to fall to a level that needs no calculation.**Xiaomi's problem right now is that "the bad news hasn't finished coming out," not that "the good news hasn't been discovered yet."**

I'll put it on the watchlist and set two alerts: one at a price of HKD 24, one on the handset gross margin in the Q3 report. Whichever comes first, I look at that first.**If both arrive, then I buy.**

One last word on my real view of this company: Lei Jun is a product man, not a capital man. This kind of company gets hyped very high in a bull market and slaughtered in a bear market.**Your job isn't to judge whether he's up to it—it's to judge when the market hands the price back to him.**
