Gross margin fell 2.7 points and profit dropped 43% — Xiaomi Corporation

One earnings report has only one protagonist

Core view

If you remember one number from this earnings report, make it this: gross margin 19.8%, down 2.7pct YoY. Everything else — net profit -42.6%, auto losses of RMB 2.6 billion, phone gross profit of RMB 3.6 billion — follows from it.

Identity: gross profit RMB 4.52 billion lower, net profit RMB 4.61 billion lower

2026Q2 gross profit was RMB 21.57 billion versus RMB 26.09 billion last year, a decline of RMB 4.52 billion; adjusted net profit was RMB 6.219 billion versus RMB 10.83 billion, down RMB 4.61 billion. The two declines are nearly equal — not a coincidence, but structure.

The RMB 4.5 billion gross-profit decline passed through to net profit almost one-for-one. There was no buffer on the cost side: the company did not cut staff, did not cut R&D (R&D rose 18.9% to RMB 9.2 billion), and did not plug the gap with investment gains (the RMB 3.2 billion of investment gains sits in net profit, not in adjusted net profit).

This one-for-one pass-through is typical of thin-margin companies: every 1 point of gross margin lost costs 6–7 points of net profit. Net margin is only 5.7%, so the denominator is too small.

Gross profit breakdown of the three businesses

Smartphones: revenue RMB 42.1 billion, gross margin 8.5%, gross profit about RMB 3.6 billion, down RMB 1.6 billion YoY (last year RMB 45.5 billion × 11.5% = RMB 5.2 billion). EVs: revenue RMB 23.9 billion, gross margin 19.2%, gross profit RMB 4.59 billion; last year RMB 20.6 billion × 26.4% = RMB 5.44 billion, down RMB 850 million. IoT and internet services: revenue RMB 31.3 billion, -19.2% YoY, with scale economies gone and gross profit down in tandem.

Across the three: smartphones -RMB 1.6 billion, EVs -RMB 850 million, IoT about -RMB 2 billion, a total of -RMB 4.45 billion that broadly matches the RMB 4.52 billion decline in total gross profit. This is not one segment going wrong — all three are going wrong at once.

The drivers, moreover, are entirely different: smartphones face upstream price hikes, EVs downstream price cuts, IoT pulled-forward demand. Three independent pieces of bad news landing together — any one alone would not look this ugly.

Two faces of profit: RMB 9.46 billion vs RMB 6.22 billion

Net profit came in at RMB 9.46 billion against adjusted net profit of RMB 6.22 billion — a RMB 3.24 billion gap. That RMB 3.2 billion is largely fair value changes on investments: Xiaomi holds sizeable stakes in ecosystem companies and listed equities, marked to market.

The nature of this money needs to be clear: it is not earned from operations but asset appreciation. The Hong Kong stock rally in Q2 2026 produced gains; should the market fall next quarter, those gains reverse. Leaning on a volatile line item to prop up profit is the least reliable approach.

Xiaomi therefore has to be judged on adjusted net profit. RMB 6.22 billion, down 42.6% YoY — that is the true picture of the core business. The market reads it the same way, which is why the stock reacted to 42.6%, not 20.3%.

Cash flow: the least visible but most important layer

The earnings summary omits a full cash flow statement, so our read is inferential: inventory rises markedly on memory price increases (advance stocking), while work-in-process and finished-vehicle inventory absorb substantial capital (RMB 229,300 per vehicle) and capex stays high (Phase 2 plant, chip tape-out).

Conclusion: operating cash flow is most likely still positive, but the cash conversion rate is declining and free cash flow is close to breakeven. That explains why the company has not paid a dividend in eight years and can only return capital to shareholders through buybacks.

The balance sheet is relatively healthy, with minimal interest-bearing debt and ample cash on hand. The risk is not solvency but cash being locked up in inventory and production capacity. If vehicle sales fall short of expectations, inventory turns from an asset into an impairment — the line item to watch most closely in 2027.

Three items the market has not yet priced in

Beyond gross margin, three line items deserve close attention — and the market has not priced them in:

  • Inventory. Memory price increases force advance stockpiling, so inventory will rise markedly. If Q3 inventory growth exceeds revenue growth by 20 points, stockpiling is excessive and impairment risk lies ahead.
  • Capex. The second-phase plant, chip tape-out and AI compute combined could push full-year capex above RMB 20 billion (rough estimate), directly compressing free cash flow.
  • Fair value of investments. This contributed RMB 3.2 billion in Q2, 34% of net profit. If Hong Kong stocks pull back in Q3, the line item becomes a drag and reported net profit could look worse than adjusted net profit.

What these three share is that they sit outside the market's field of view: sell-side reports rarely break them out, and media flashes never mention them. Yet they determine cash flow quality and earnings sustainability — which matter far more than revenue growth.

Conclusion

Financial reality: a gross-margin story. A 2.7pct decline in gross margin drove profit down 43%, with all three businesses bleeding at once; investment gains masked an ugly core, and cash flow swung from ample to tight. Three numbers decide what comes next: handset gross margin 8.5%, auto gross margin 19.2%, and a quarterly auto loss of RMB 2.6 billion. The bleeding stops only once the first two recover and the third narrows.
