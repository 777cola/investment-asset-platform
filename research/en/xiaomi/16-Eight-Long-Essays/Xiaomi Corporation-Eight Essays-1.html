Cognitive reset: why "auto success" halved the stock price — Xiaomi Corporation

The same thing was an option a year ago and a burden a year later
Research date 2026/08/21  ·  Price HK$29.02 (+4.54%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 747 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 19.6x (2026E approximately 28x, estimated)

Core view

Xiaomi's EV business sold well in 2025 and the stock ran to HKD 59.45. In 2026, EVs sold even better — topping 100,000 units in a quarter for the first time — yet the stock fell to HKD 29.02. The same business went from "option" to "burden," separated by nothing more than one consolidation.

First, one fact: this is not an indiscriminate selloff.

Many attribute the 51% decline to "market sentiment." That is wrong. The market did two entirely rational things: it cut EPS and it cut the multiple.

On EPS: H1 2026 adjusted net profit was RMB 12.3 billion, or RMB 24.6 billion annualised; on the 2025 comparable basis, the market had originally expected 2026 to deliver over RMB 40 billion. The cut was about 40%, in line with the actual profit decline of -42.6%.

On the multiple: the 2025 high of HKD 59.45 implied a market cap of HKD 1,530.3 billion (RMB 1,407.9 billion), which divided by RMB 24.6 billion gives 57x. The current HKD 29.02 implies 28x. EPS down 40% and the multiple down 51% multiply out to -71% — the actual 51% decline shows the market still cut some slack.

The mechanics of option valuation: worth most when it stays off the income statement

How did the market price the auto business in 2025? Not on "deliveries × per-vehicle profit," but on "imagination." The rationale was sound: annual sales of 13.7 thousand vehicles (2024), still ramping, and losses were explained away as "investment." At that stage, the more the auto business lost, the more the market believed it was investing; the more it delivered, the more it proved it could break out.

2026 is different. 10.42 thousand units in a single quarter, 41.7 thousand annualized — that scale can no longer be explained away as "startup." It is a mid-sized automaker and must be examined by automaker rules: what is the gross margin, what is per-vehicle cost, what is capacity utilization, when does it reach break-even.

By those rules, the answer is: gross margin 19.2% (down 7.2pct), per-vehicle gross profit RMB 4.4 ten thousand, per-vehicle loss RMB 2.5 ten thousand, quarterly loss RMB 2.6 billion. The option has become a liability, and the valuation method must change. That is the entire mechanism of "success killing the valuation."

Second-level insight: the smartphone business has not "stabilised" — it has been "punctured"

If cars were the only problem, Xiaomi could hold on. What is truly fatal is two things happening at once.

Handset gross margin fell from 11.5% to 8.5%, and the cause is not competition but memory price hikes — DRAM contract prices up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%. This is an industry-wide cost shock, but it hits Xiaomi hardest because its gross margin is the thinnest. At Apple's 46% gross margin, a 10% cost increase just means earning less; at Xiaomi's 8.5%, a 10% cost increase means zero.

Two businesses: one squeezed by rising prices upstream, the other by falling prices downstream. That is the core of the "cognitive reset": Xiaomi is not "a good company facing difficulties" but "a thin-margin company squeezed from both ends at once."

Third layer of insight: 19.6x PE is a statistical artifact

The 19.6x TTM shown in market software is based on the past four quarters of profit, and 2025Q3 and Q4 were the good days before memory prices rose. Using a denominator that is "good for half the period, bad for half the period" to calculate a future that will be "bad for the entire period" is a statistical illusion.

On current profitability: 2026 profit of RMB 24.6 billion, market cap of RMB 687.2 billion, 28x. On the pessimistic scenario: 2027 profit of RMB 20 billion, 15x, target price HKD 12.7. The true valuation range is 28–34x (if profit continues to be revised down), not 19.6x.

This is the final layer of the cognitive reset: it is not "it fell 51%, so it is cheap"; it is "after falling 51%, it is still not cheap." Because the denominator is falling faster than the numerator.

Fourth-layer thinking: when this judgment would be wrong

Leave room for the bear case. This call could be wrong in three ways:

  • Memory rolls over earlier than expected. If contract prices peak in 2026Q4 and spot prices turn down in 2027Q1, handset gross margin could return to 11% within two quarters and profit would rebound sharply. Both 2019 and 2023 saw fast reversals of the "a year up, half a year down" pattern.
  • Auto volume beats expectations. If Pengcheng replicates the SU7 ramp, quarterly volume could reach 15.0 thousand units in 2027, turning the segment profitable on diluted fixed costs and shifting the valuation framework from "asset-heavy" back to "growth."
  • Ecosystem monetisation is underestimated. Internet services are now under 10% of total revenue. If the human-car-home ecosystem lifts ARPU, overall gross margin could gain 2–3 points — an option the model does not capture.

All three are possible, but all need time to verify. A "cognitive reset" does not mean "permanently bearish"; it means "take the optimistic scenario out of the base case first." Add it back when the evidence appears. The cost is missing the first leg; the benefit is not losing principal.

Conclusion

The cognitive reset is complete: Xiaomi's de-rating is rational, the product of a double hit to EPS and multiples. The more successful the car business, the more it gets valued as a heavy-asset business; smartphone gross margin has been breached, turning "thin profit" into "danger." At 28x 2026E against a 43% profit decline, the stock is expensive, not cheap. Buying it requires a new thesis, not "it has fallen a lot."
