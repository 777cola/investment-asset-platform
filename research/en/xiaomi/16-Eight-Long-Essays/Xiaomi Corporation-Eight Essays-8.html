Step one: compress the conclusion into one sentence

What is this company? A manufacturer with top-tier execution and a real moat, but a thin-margin business now squeezed at both ends by rising upstream costs and falling downstream prices.

What is it worth? A good manufacturing company deserves 15–20x; a growth company 25–30x. Xiaomi sits in between, so around 20x is reasonable. 2026 net profit of RMB 24.6 billion × 20x ÷ 0.92 ÷ 25.741 billion shares = HKD 20.8.

Where is it now? HKD 29.02, 28x. 39% too expensive. That is the entire conclusion. Everything else is execution detail.

Step two: three numbers that replace all narrative

Handset gross margin (8.5%): A thermometer for the memory cycle and proof of whether the company can breathe on its own. Back above 10% means cost pressure has peaked; below 7% means it is worse than contract manufacturing.

Auto gross margin (19.2%): A thermometer for the price war. Holding 19% means "efficiency-driven carmaking" holds; below 15% means fixed costs are not covered and the model is flawed.

Quarterly auto loss (RMB 2.6 billion): A thermometer for scale effects. Narrowing to within RMB 1 billion means capacity utilization has come up; widening to RMB 4 billion means the more it sells, the more it loses.

All three numbers are readable in the quarterly report — no channel checks, no digging required. Everything else — Pengcheng orders, ecosystem synergies, AI strategy — is auxiliary and cannot replace these three.

Step 3: Two trigger conditions to replace "it feels cheap"

Price trigger: HKD 24. Corresponds to 23x 2026E, 20x 2027E (midpoint RMB 30 billion). At HKD 24, the three-scenario payoff becomes +67% / +5% / -47%, expected value HKD 24.5, turning positive from negative. This is "calculated cheapness," not "it feels like it has fallen enough."

Fundamental trigger: handset gross margin ≥10% for two consecutive quarters. This condition matters more than price because it changes the numerator. If gross margin returns to 11%, 2027 net profit could reach RMB 35 billion, and 28x becomes 20x—the valuation comes down on its own.

Either condition can arrive first. But do not buy early because "neither has arrived, but I fear missing out." The cost of missing out is opportunity cost; the cost of buying wrong is principal.

Step 4: Position sizing and red lines

Position path: HKD 24 at 3% → HKD 19 at 6% → gross margin target met 8% → dual trigger 10%. Every step has explicit conditions; no on-the-spot judgment.

Red lines: handset gross margin <7% or auto gross margin <15% — unconditional liquidation. Rationale: these two lines mark the boundary between "cyclical" and "structural" — breaching them signals not cyclical fluctuation but a broken business.

Final discipline: no single name above 10%, and no stacking with similar holdings (BYD, Hang Seng Tech ETF). Xiaomi's correlation with Hang Seng Tech is 0.8 and with automakers 0.6–0.7; it is not a diversification tool but an amplifier.

Bear-case check: if I am wrong, where would I be wrong?

Every framework must state clearly "under what circumstances it fails."

Mistake one: treating a cycle as a trend. If memory peaks in Q4 2026 and handset gross margin returns to 11% in Q1 2027, then 28x is in fact "a reasonable valuation at the cycle bottom," and my fair value of HKD 20.8 at 20x is 30% too low. This is the most likely mistake.

Mistake two: underestimating the non-linearity of the auto business. Autos are a capacity-utilization business; going from 400,000 to 600,000 units, profit does not grow linearly but jumps. If Pengcheng beats expectations, autos could turn profitable on a quarterly basis in 2027, and the valuation method would switch from "heavy asset" back to "growth." At that point 25x is reasonable.

Mistake three: applying too heavy a governance discount. I applied a 10% governance discount, but if Lei Jun's execution keeps delivering, the market may not grant that discount at all—in 2025 it granted none.

What the three mistakes share is that "all of them point to my numbers being too conservative." So the framework keeps one escape hatch: if handset gross margin holds at ≥10% for two consecutive quarters, I can add directly to 8%, regardless of where the share price is. That is the exit I left myself for "I was wrong."

Conclusion

Decision framework: fair value HKD 20.8 (20x), current price 29.02 is 39% expensive; buy points HKD 24 (3%) and HKD 19 (6%); add on handset gross margin ≥10% for two consecutive quarters; red line is handset <7% or auto <15%. Three numbers, two triggers, one red line—the framework is not for forecasting; it lets you follow the table when emotions run highest.
