## What Nadella got right: not technology, but the charging model

Microsoft's problem in 2014 was not a lack of technology but a lack of a charging model—Windows and Office were sold as one-off licenses, customers upgraded on a five-year cycle, and revenue moved with PC shipments. Nadella's first move was to push Office 365 onto every platform (including iPad); his second was to turn Azure from "virtual machine rental" into "an extension of enterprise IT."

The results show up in the numbers: FY2026 revenue of USD 331 billion, Microsoft Cloud at USD 214 billion, up 27% year on year, and commercial RPO of USD 678 billion. This is not luck—it is twelve years in the same direction. **The hardest thing for a USD 3 trillion company is not finding a new story but keeping the old business compounding**—Office Commercial is still raising prices and still adding Copilot surcharges with enterprise seats near saturation, direct evidence of pricing power.

## Capital allocation: three big M&A deals without a misstep, the fourth is a bet

Microsoft's M&A history is clean by tech giant standards. It bought LinkedIn for USD 26.2 billion in 2016, criticized at the time as expensive; today it is the only enterprise-social sample. It bought GitHub for USD 7.5 billion in 2018, an underappreciated deal — its value today lies not in code-hosting revenue but in the distribution channel formed by 225 million developers, where Copilot's first users came from. Activision Blizzard at USD 68.7 billion was held up by regulators for 21 months before closing; the cost was time, not money.

Capital allocation changed in nature in 2026: **from "buying companies" to "building factories"**. FY2026 cash capital expenditure is USD 115.9 billion, with FY2027 planned at USD 175 billion — close to USD 300 billion over two years, more than twice all historical M&A combined. No historical data exists to benchmark the return on this spending; it can only be inferred indirectly from RPO and Azure growth.

## Two deductions: depreciation life tied to OpenAI

**Depreciation life extended from 15 to 25 years**, while the FY2027 capex guidance "becomes" 175 billion from 190 billion (some leased and third-party capacity no longer counted). Neither move breaks any rule — both are accounting estimates and definitional choices — but they point the same way: make the statements look a little better. The amounts are small (roughly 4–5 billion less depreciation per year, about USD 0.5 in EPS); what suffers is credibility — investors dislike seeing accounting policy loosen at a capex peak.

**The OpenAI tie-up** is the bigger structural issue. After the October 2025 restructuring, Microsoft holds about 27% and secures model and IP rights through 2032, while OpenAI is free to choose other clouds (Oracle has already won a large contract). That means one thing: **part of Microsoft's AI revenue is "rented exclusivity."** Fortunately, Q4 data offers a counterpoint — RPO still grew 25% excluding OpenAI, showing non-frontier-model customers are providing support.

## Governance and succession: no Plan B, and none needed

The board has about 12 members, with Nadella also serving as chairman and a lead independent director appointed. Ownership is highly dispersed, dominated by institutional investors, and no shareholder can challenge management—the upside is fast decision-making, the downside is weak checks and balances. In 2024, the EU and UK antitrust reviews of cloud licensing terms ultimately ended with commitments to remedy, and the CISPE settlement was about EUR 20 million, a "pay to make it go away" level outcome.

On succession, Microsoft has no public COO or anointed heir. The internal candidates are CTO Kevin Scott and commercial chief Althoff, but the more likely answer is Nadella serving another five years—he was born in 1967 and will be 59 in 2026. **The implication for investors: over the next five years the company's strategic continuity is extremely high, and there is no need to pay a premium for "what happens if leadership changes," nor to apply a discount for it.**
