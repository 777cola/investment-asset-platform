## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) is one of the world's largest enterprise software and cloud computing companies. FY2026 revenue was USD 331 billion, net income about USD 133.7 billion, market cap USD 3.45 trillion, and P/E 25.8x (TTM). The company has three segments: Intelligent Cloud (Azure, server products, enterprise services), Productivity and Business Processes (Office 365, LinkedIn, Dynamics), and More Personal Computing (Windows, gaming, search advertising).

In FY2026Q4, Intelligent Cloud revenue was USD 39.3 billion, up 32% YoY; Microsoft Cloud totaled USD 59.3 billion, up 27% YoY, and USD 214 billion for the full year. It is the world's second-largest public cloud and the only cloud vendor that also owns an enterprise software distribution channel.

Management: CEO Satya Nadella took office in February 2014, CFO Amy Hood in May 2013; the two have worked together for over twelve years. Under Nadella, market cap rose from USD 300 billion to USD 3.45 trillion; the core moves were converting licenses to subscriptions, moving subscriptions to the cloud, and using capital to buy an AI entry ticket.

One more point on the revenue mix: the two non-Intelligent Cloud segments (Office, LinkedIn, Windows, gaming, search) total about USD 50.7 billion, growing only around 10%. They are the cash flow base but offer no valuation upside.

Share capital and market cap: about 7.42 billion shares (backed out from USD 3.45 trillion market cap ÷ USD 464.72), institutional ownership about 72%, with Vanguard, BlackRock and State Street as the main top ten holders. Average daily turnover is about USD 9 billion, with excellent liquidity, suitable for large-scale capital flows.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

Global cloud computing is an oligopoly: AWS ranks first by share, Azure second, Google Cloud third, Oracle has squeezed into fourth on large AI orders, and new clouds such as CoreWeave are capturing a wave through GPU leasing. The industry's core variable shifted between 2024 and 2026 from "who has the lowest price" to "who has capacity" — GPUs, power and racks became the bottleneck, and supply rather than demand determines growth.

In 2026 the four major North American cloud providers collectively raised capex. Microsoft plans USD 175 billion for FY2027, equivalent to 53% of FY2026 revenue.Historically cloud capex has never exceeded 40% of revenue; the intensity of this round is unprecedented. The industry has shifted from a "steady-state oligopoly" to an "arms race": participants must invest, and those that do not will lose share.

Cycle assessment: orders are still rising (RPO up 84% year on year), prices have not fallen (cloud gross margin 65%), and capacity is not in excess (management says demand still exceeds supply), placing the cycle mid-stage. The top signal is "order growth dropping below 20% while capacity is still coming online," which on the current pace would be around 2028.

No industry ceiling is yet in sight: cloud accounts for about 30% of global enterprise IT spending, incremental AI inference demand has only just begun, and saturation is not visible within five years. The real constraint is power and advanced packaging, not demand.

Policy variables: the US government's push for "sovereign AI" and domestic compute is a positive for Microsoft, one of the few vendors with government cloud credentials. The EU cloud licensing investigation is a negative variable, but its impact is limited to European revenue (about 20% of total revenue).

## Chapter 3 Business model

Three charging models map to three corporate budgets: per-head subscriptions (M365), per-usage compute (Azure), and per-seat AI add-ons (Copilot, list price USD 30/seat/month). The three reinforce each other: M365 customers are Azure's natural customers, Azure's compute lets Copilot run, and Copilot makes seats more valuable. Amazon (compute without applications) and Google (applications without enterprise channels) both lack this closed loop.

The cost is a lower return on capital. M365 carries gross margins above 80% and is asset-light; Azure carries 65% gross margins and is asset-heavy. As the revenue mix shifts toward the latter, blended gross margin must fall—FY2026Q4 already slipped from 68.6% to 67.2%, with cloud gross margin at 65%, down year on year. This is not an operating misstep; it is arithmetic.

The other constraint is "can't stop": the closed loop means Microsoft must always stay ahead on capacity, even at the cost of lower near-term returns. In strong demand this is an advantage; in weak demand it is a burden.

From the customer's perspective, what Microsoft really sells is "the choice you won't be blamed for": a CIO who picks Azure faces no challenge, while one who picks a smaller vendor faces audit questions. This psychological premium is the fundamental reason it costs 30% more than CoreWeave.

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

FY2026Q4: revenue RMB 90.01 billion (+17.8%), gross profit RMB 60.49 billion (gross margin 67.2%, -1.4pct), operating profit RMB 40.6 billion (margin 45.1%), net income RMB 35.77 billion (+31.3%, including a RMB 3.2 billion gain on the Anthropic investment), diluted EPS USD 4.81. Excluding one-off gains, net income grew about +21% YoY.

Cash flow: operating cash flow RMB 55.4 billion (+30%), cash capex RMB 35.76 billion, free cash flow RMB 19.6 billion (-23%). Full-year operating cash flow RMB 182.9 billion, capex RMB 115.9 billion, free cash flow RMB 67 billion, yield 1.94%. Operating cash flow is 1.55x net income, so earnings are high quality; but capex/operating cash flow reached 74% (quarter) and 63% (full year), the only one of the seven hard metrics it fails.

Two quality issues: the effective tax rate of 17% is below the statutory 21% and unsustainable; depreciation lives were extended from 15 to 25 years, which by rough calculation cuts annual depreciation by RMB 4–5 billion, or about USD 0.5 of EPS, and the company did not quantify it.

By segment: Intelligent Cloud operating margin about 40%, Productivity and Business Processes about 55%, Personal Computing about 30%. The mix is shifting toward Intelligent Cloud, which dilutes the overall margin — a long-term trend, not quarterly noise.

Shareholder returns and financing: FY2026 shareholder returns RMB 43 billion, but capex is RMB 115.9 billion, and operating cash flow covers the gap. If FY2027 capex does reach RMB 175 billion, free cash flow will fall to RMB 44.5 billion, squeezing room for dividends and buybacks — a layer that must be factored into valuation.

## Chapter 5 Management

Nadella's capital allocation record: LinkedIn at USD 26.2 billion (2016), GitHub at USD 7.5 billion (2018), Nuance at USD 19.7 billion (2022), Activision Blizzard at USD 68.7 billion (completed 2023); none of the four major acquisitions produced an impairment black hole. Cumulative OpenAI investment of about USD 13 billion; after the October 2025 restructuring, a stake of about 27% and IP usage rights through 2032.

Shareholder returns: USD 43 billion for full-year FY2026 (dividends of about USD 25.8 billion, buybacks of about USD 17.2 billion, rough split). Dividends have grown every year, but buybacks look small against a USD 3.45 trillion market cap.

Two deductions: accounting policy (extended depreciation lives; FY2027 capex guidance adjusted from USD 190 billion to USD 175 billion) and disclosure (Copilot discloses seats only, not ARPU or renewal rates). Neither breaks any rule, but both point to a desire for the market to see growth rather than cost. Governance: about 12 board members, Nadella also serves as chairman, a lead independent director is in place, ownership is dispersed and there is no challenger.

On incentives, Nadella's pay is almost entirely stock, tied to three-year shareholder returns; the executive team's scorecard includes cloud revenue and AI adoption. That gives them an incentive to pile on capex in the short term to buy growth.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

- Azure supply unlock: Faster capacity onboarding and higher GPU utilization lifted Azure growth from 40% to 43%, with guidance of 45% next quarter (constant currency).

- Order book conversion: RPO of RMB 678 billion, up 84% YoY, with 30% to be recognized within 12 months, implying roughly USD 203.4 billion of revenue over the next four quarters (rough calculation); still up 25% excluding OpenAI.

- Copilot penetration: 30 million seats (20 million last quarter), roughly 7.5% penetration of the more than 400 million M365 seats (rough calculation), or about USD 10.8 billion annualized at list price.

- AI consumption-based pricing: Usage in customer-service scenarios rose 4x QoQ; a shift from per-seat to per-seat plus consumption-based pricing would move the TAM from headcount to call volume.

Three-to-five-year growth path: Azure sustains above 30% on capacity and orders, M365 sustains 10%–12% on Copilot price increases, and gaming and search contribute steady single-digit growth. Overall revenue growth of 13%–15% a year is achievable.

One often overlooked driver: price increases. Azure unit prices were raised once in 2026, and Office commercial is also rising. If half of Azure's 43% growth comes from price increases, volume growth is only about 20%, and sustainability should be discounted.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Four main rivals. Amazon: leads in scale and cost, broadest customer base, but weak at the application layer; it is chasing incremental workloads. Google: the only company developing in-house at all three layers — models (Gemini), chips (TPU) and cloud — with the best cost structure and the weakest enterprise sales. Oracle: entered via a mega-deal with OpenAI, high order elasticity, but customer concentration and delivery doubts. OpenAI: largest customer and partner; its multi-cloud strategy erodes Microsoft's exclusivity.

Google is the most dangerous rival. Microsoft's AI stack relies on OpenAI for models and Nvidia for chips — paying at both ends; Google pays at neither. If inference cost becomes the core competitive variable, Microsoft's structural disadvantage will become more evident each year.

The second threat is open-source models: they commoditize the model layer to rock-bottom prices, leaving Azure's premium to come only from compute and compliance, no longer from "exclusive models." These two threats combined are the fundamental reason Microsoft's valuation multiple cannot exceed 30x.

Share data: Azure holds about 25% of global cloud share (rough estimate), AWS about 30%, Google about 12%. This ranking is unlikely to change within three years, but the gap is narrowing — Google's growth has outpaced Azure for four consecutive quarters.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

- Capex mismatch: RMB 290 billion invested over two years, asset payback period of 5–8 years, RPO weighted duration of only 2.3 years — a maturity mismatch.

- AI demand peaking: if demand growth falls to 15% in 2028, capacity sits idle and operating margin could be compressed to 38%.

- Price war: cloud gross margin falling below 60% is the signal; open-source models accelerate commoditization.

- Regulation: the EU cloud licensing probe and UK/US scrutiny of the Microsoft-OpenAI relationship; extreme scenarios call for business separation.

- Accounting policy: extended depreciation lives and changes in reporting basis affect earnings quality and market trust.

- Customer concentration: OpenAI accounts for a high share of RPO growth; a shift to another cloud would visibly slow Azure growth.

Of the six, the first two determine whether this investment makes money; the last four determine how much. The risks are poorly hedgeable — the entire tech sector is betting on the same thing, and the only tool is position sizing.

FX factor: about one-third of revenue comes from outside the US, and a stronger dollar would drag reported growth by 1–2 percentage points. The company guides Azure on a constant-currency basis precisely to strip out this noise.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

FY2027E: revenue RMB 381 billion (+15%), operating profit RMB 170 billion (44.6% margin, 0.5 pt drag from depreciation), net income RMB 150 billion, EPS USD 20.2; capex RMB 175 billion, operating cash flow about RMB 219.5 billion (+20%), free cash flow down to RMB 44.5 billion (trough).

FY2028E: revenue RMB 438 billion (+15%), operating profit RMB 197 billion (45.0%, depreciation peaks and costs dilute), net income RMB 174.5 billion, EPS USD 23.5; capex RMB 185 billion, free cash flow recovering to about RMB 70 billion.

Three key assumptions: Azure growth holds above 35% in FY2027 and eases to 28%–30% in FY2028; effective tax rate returns to 19% from 17%; share count broadly unchanged (buybacks offset option dilution). A deviation in any one of the three moves EPS by about 10%.

Further decomposition: if FY2028 Azure growth is only 20% (rather than 28%), revenue is about RMB 30 billion lower and EPS falls to around USD 21 — exactly the bear case. Valuation is clearly highly sensitive to Azure growth.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

The current price of USD 464.72 corresponds to 25.8x FY2026, 23.0x FY2027E and 19.8x FY2028E; PB 10.1x (USD 3.45 trillion ÷ equity USD 340 billion), PS 10.4x (USD 3.45 trillion ÷ USD 331 billion).

Method: anchor on FY2028 EPS (avoiding the distortion of FY2027 capex peak on profit) and assign 24x — the lower-middle of the company's 22–32x range over the past five years. The lower-middle assignment reflects rising capital intensity, ROIC drifting from 31% to 22%, and a free cash flow yield below 2%.

Cross-check: post-earnings sell-side target prices of USD 560–640 (Citi 600, Goldman Sachs 640), averaging about 561, broadly in line with this report's neutral 564. This shows the valuation assumptions are not aggressive but consensus. Sensitivity tests show multiple elasticity exceeds EPS elasticity: EPS ±10% maps to ±USD 57 on the target price, multiple ±3x maps to ±USD 70.

Historical comparison: Microsoft's median PE was about 30x in 2019–2024, when capex was 12% of revenue. It is now 35%, so the multiple should shift from 30x toward 24x — not a bearish call, but an acknowledgment that the business has become heavier.

Peer valuation comparison: Alphabet about 24x, Amazon about 38x, Oracle about 45x, Salesforce about 24x. Microsoft at 25.8x sits mid-range, but its cash flow quality beats Oracle (no customer concentration) and its profit purity beats Amazon (no retail drag).

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Buy (initiation), 12-month target price USD 564, implying +21%. Bear case USD 400 (-14%), bull case USD 700 (+51%), risk-reward 1.5:1, expected return +8%–10% on a 55%–60% win rate.

Execution in three tranches: build 2% at the current price of 464.72, add to 4% below USD 430 (21x FY2027, risk-reward 4.5:1), top up to 5% after FY2027Q1 results confirm Azure ≥40%; stop adding above 560. Cap the position at 5% because the risk-reward does not justify a heavy position.

Three falsification conditions: Azure below 30% for two consecutive quarters and explained as slowing demand; RPO year-on-year growth falling below 20% with a 12-month conversion rate below 25%; cloud gross margin breaking below 55%. A falling share price alone does not constitute falsification.

Alternative execution: if unwilling to scale in, sell put options near 430 to collect premium and build the position, turning waiting time into return. But this does not suit most people; discipline matters more than technique.

Tracking cadence: update three numbers after each quarterly report — Azure growth, RPO conversion rate, cloud gross margin; update capex guidance every July. If the three numbers hold, the rating holds; if two deteriorate, downgrade to Hold.
