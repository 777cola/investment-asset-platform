The intersection points and genuine disagreements across the four close reads

Core view

Read together, the four deep dives share more consensus than expected: no disagreement on revenue quality, customer stickiness or management capability; depreciation and capex are the sole point of contention; Copilot is the one item all four admit remains unclear.

The value of synthesis lies not in voting but in locating uncertainty to specific line items—only then can it be tested next quarter.

Consensus 1: No softness anywhere on the revenue side

Revenue RMB 90.01 billion, +17.8%; Azure +43%, guidance for next quarter +45%; RPO RMB 678 billion, +84%. All four read these three data sets the same way: demand is real, and it is accelerating. Even Munger, the most conservative of them, concedes that excluding OpenAI, RPO still grew +25%, which shows this is not a story carried by a single customer.

Another piece of evidence on revenue quality: Microsoft Cloud RMB 59.3 billion, +27% year on year, RMB 214 billion for the full year. Only three companies worldwide can sustain 27% growth at that scale.

Consensus 2: Depreciation takes center stage in 2027–2028

FY2026 cash capex was RMB 115.9 billion, with FY2027 planned at RMB 175 billion. On a rough straight-line basis over 6 years for GPU assets, the short-lived assets added in FY2026 alone would add about RMB 13 billion in annual depreciation; with another RMB 175 billion invested in FY2027, total depreciation in 2028 could double that of 2026.

That is why all four anchor valuation on FY2028 rather than FY2027—FY2027 is the investment year; FY2028 is the year it settles. The neutral case gives FY2028 EPS of USD 23.5, implying operating margin holds at 43%.

The real disagreement: is this spending investment or cost

Buffett treats it as cost (1.9% free cash flow yield, 51x free cash flow), Li Lu treats it as investment (expanding with its own cash flow, returns accruing to shareholders), Duan Yongping adds no premium for the parts he cannot understand, and Munger says assume it is cost first; if it later proves to be investment, that is a welcome surprise.

The composite approach is: price it as cost, hold it as investment. That is, the neutral scenario assigns no premium for capex (24x), but if returns prove high in 2028, the optimistic scenario (27x) will capture it. This way we neither bet nor miss out.

The three actions after synthesis
  • Buy, but in three tranches. One tranche at the current price of 464.72; add a tranche below 430 and double it; add the final tranche once FY2027Q1 confirms Azure growth of ≥40%.
  • Watch three numbers, not the share price. Azure growth, the 12-month RPO conversion rate, and Copilot seats and ARPU. Only if all three deteriorate should you consider exiting.
  • Do not add on the AI narrative. Add only on price or data, never on news.
Why not use weighted scoring

Combining the four deep dives by weight (business 40%, financials 30%, industry 20%, risk 10%) yields a tidy 7.8 score and a Buy recommendation—but that conclusion carries no information, because the weights were pulled out of thin air.

A more useful approach is to look for veto points: did any of the four argue this business should not be bought? No. Did any argue the price is unacceptable? Two. So the conclusion is buy, but not fully invested now—this logic is more reliable than any weighted average.

Conclusion

Synthesis: Buy, scale in, neutral at USD 564. All four agree the business is good, diverge on price, and share doubts about capex returns. The synthesized action: bet on what is certain (revenue, moat, management), hedge what is not (depreciation, returns) by scaling in.
