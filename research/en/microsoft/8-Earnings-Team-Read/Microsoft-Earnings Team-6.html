Core view

After the close on July 29, Microsoft reported FY2026Q4: revenue USD 90.01 billion, up 17.8% year on year, Azure accelerating from 40% to 43%, with guidance of 45% for next quarter. The stock gapped up 16% the next day and rose 19% over two sessions.

The market bought the answer that AI can make money. But the same report carries the other half: capex of USD 41 billion and free cash flow of -23%. The real story this quarter is not how good revenue was, but how big the bill is.

I. Azure's acceleration is a supply-side story

The 43% figure is 3 points above last quarter's 40% and 2 points above the consensus of 42%. Management's explanation is specific: faster capacity ramp and higher GPU utilization. Translated, that means — demand did not suddenly improve; we finally have machines to sell.

The distinction matters. Growth bought with price cuts drags down gross margin and renewals; growth unlocked by supply lets pricing move higher. Microsoft's cloud gross margin is 65%, down year on year, but the decline comes from product mix (a higher compute share), not from a price war.

Harder evidence sits in the order book: commercial remaining performance obligation of USD 678 billion, up 84% year on year and up USD 51 billion quarter on quarter, with the incremental almost entirely from customers other than OpenAI. The market's biggest doubt had been whether Microsoft's AI revenue was just moving money between its own pockets — this quarter answered it with orders from non-OpenAI customers.

2. Bill: RMB 41 billion, still not enough

Capex in the same quarter was RMB 41 billion, up 70% year on year, with about two-thirds going to short-lived assets such as CPUs and GPUs. Full-year cash capex was RMB 115.9 billion, and FY2027 is planned at RMB 175 billion—equivalent to 53% of FY2026 full-year revenue.

The result is free cash flow: RMB 19.6 billion for the quarter, down 23% year on year, and RMB 67 billion for the full year, a yield of just 1.94% against a market cap of RMB 3.45 trillion. This is not a financial risk but a valuation constraint—until capex peaks, Microsoft's cash flow cannot support a multiple above 30x.

Three, two accounting details worth noting

First: depreciation life extended from 15 to 25 years. The company did not quantify the impact; assuming affected assets account for half the depreciable base, annual depreciation falls by RMB 4–5 billion and EPS rises by about USD 0.5. The amount is modest, but it comes in a year of record capital expenditure, and the timing is noteworthy.

Second: FY2027 capital expenditure guidance cut from RMB 190 billion to RMB 175 billion. Of the RMB 15 billion reduction, part reflects a definitional change (certain leases and third-party capacity no longer counted as capital expenditure), so cash outlay is unchanged. Together, the two make reported earnings look about RMB 20 billion better than cash.

IV. Copilot: 30 million seats, and then what?

This is the most overestimated number of the quarter. 30 million paid seats, 10 million more than last quarter, net additions doubling quarter over quarter, roughly USD 10.8 billion annualized at list price—3.3% of FY2026 revenue.

The magnitude shows it is no longer an experiment, but it is not yet enough to change the growth rate. The real question is: does this make Office more expensive, or does it make Office better? The former is a price increase, capped by the growth rate of enterprise IT budgets; the latter is a new business, capped by AI call volume. The company disclosed neither ARPU nor renewal rates—until those two numbers are out, any valuation of Copilot is guesswork.

V. How to price it: 25.8x, fair but not cheap

USD 464.72 corresponds to 25.8x TTM and 23x FY2027. The base case applies 24x to FY2028 EPS of USD 23.5, yielding USD 564; bear case USD 400; bull case USD 700. Odds of 1.5 to 1.

Post-earnings, sell-side target prices clustered upward to USD 560–640 (Citi 600, Goldman Sachs 640), averaging about USD 561 — broadly in line with the base case. That means 564 is not an aggressive target but the consensus.The upside of consensus is you won't be embarrassed; the downside is excess returns can only come from exceeding expectations.

The conclusion: buy, but in tranches. Open the first position at the current price, double down below 430, stop adding above 560.Buy it for the certainty; tranche it because the bill isn't fully settled.

Conclusion

One-line summary: Microsoft used this quarter to prove AI can generate revenue, and incidentally proved that the revenue has to be spent right back out. Top marks on revenue, a passing grade on cash flow, fair valuation. For long-term investors, this is a trade that exchanges time for upside; for anyone who only looks at quarterly results, the cash-flow line in this report will keep you up at night.
