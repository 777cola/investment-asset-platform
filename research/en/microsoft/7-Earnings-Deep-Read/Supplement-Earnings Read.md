## Layer one: revenue mix — Azure drives acceleration, Office provides the floor

Of the RMB 90.01 billion in revenue, Intelligent Cloud accounted for RMB 39.3 billion, up 32% year on year, accelerating another notch from last quarter; the other two segments (Office Commercial, LinkedIn, Dynamics and Windows, Gaming, Search) totaled about RMB 50.7 billion, growing around 10%, the "quiet but steady" part of the business. **The importance of this structure: Azure drives the valuation, Office underpins the base.**

Azure accelerated from 40% to 43%; management attributed this to faster capacity coming online and higher GPU utilization rather than a sudden improvement in demand. That line is worth noting — **the bottleneck is on the supply side (power, racks, chips), not the demand side**. Growth constrained by supply is higher quality: no need to cut prices to win volume, and prices can even move higher. Guidance for next quarter is 45% (constant currency), implying the acceleration is not over.

Copilot is the third curve: 30 million paid seats, up 10 million from 20 million last quarter, with net adds doubling sequentially. At a list price of USD 30/seat/month, that implies roughly USD 10.8 billion in annualized revenue, 3.3% of FY2026 revenue — **a meaningful scale, but not yet enough to move overall growth**. The real question is renewal rates and ARPU, and the company has not yet disclosed either.

## Layer two: gross margin is in structural decline, not being priced down

Gross margin 67.2%, down 1.4 percentage points year on year; cloud gross margin 65% and also trending down. Three reasons: a higher mix of Azure and AI infrastructure, front-loaded GPU depreciation, and the power and bandwidth costs of AI inference. **Selling software carries an 80% gross margin, selling compute 60%, selling inference just over 50%** — the more revenue shifts toward compute, the lower the blended gross margin.

The key question: is this bad? No. Gross profit rose 15.4% year on year, still growing in absolute terms; and once built, compute revenue is long-term and contractually locked in (RPO RMB 678 billion). What to watch is price — if the inference price war pushes per-token prices down faster than usage rises, gross margin will fall faster than the mix shift alone implies. Not yet visible, but watch whether cloud gross margin holds 60%.

## Tier three: costs controlled like a mature company

Sales, R&D and G&A combined came to RMB 19.88 billion, +9.9% YoY, well below the +17.8% revenue growth; the expense ratio fell from 23.7% to 22.1%. That is why operating margin still edged up 0.2pct: **mix diluted gross margin while scale diluted expenses, and the two offset each other.**

From operating profit of RMB 40.6 billion to net profit of RMB 35.77 billion, two items sit in between: add RMB 3.2 billion of investment income from Anthropic, subtract about RMB 710 million of net interest expense, giving roughly RMB 43.09 billion pre-tax; an effective tax rate of about 17% yields net profit of RMB 35.77 billion. The 17% rate is low (the US statutory rate is 21%), mainly due to R&D tax credits and the overseas structure — **this rate is unsustainable and will most likely revert to 19%–20% over the next two to three years**, meaning the same operating profit would produce roughly 1.5 points less net profit. The market broadly overlooks this.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the account is still open

Operating cash flow of RMB 55.4 billion, up 30% YoY, is very healthy: net profit was RMB 35.77 billion, and operating cash flow is 1.55x that, showing profit is not being dragged down by receivables or inventory. The problem is on the next line: cash capex of RMB 35.76 billion, or RMB 41 billion including finance leases, leaving free cash flow of just RMB 19.6 billion, down 23% YoY.

The full year makes it clearer: operating cash flow of RMB 182.9 billion, cash capex of RMB 115.9 billion, free cash flow of RMB 67 billion. And FY2027 plans to invest RMB 175 billion—**if operating cash flow grows 20% to RMB 219.5 billion, free cash flow drops to just RMB 44.5 billion, another one-third decline YoY.** That is the valuation ceiling: not insufficient profit, but cash intercepted by capex.

The balance sheet still has room: cash and short-term investments of about RMB 90 billion, long-term debt of about RMB 60 billion, shareholders' equity of about RMB 340 billion, positive net cash and a AAA rating.**In other words, Microsoft can borrow another RMB 100 billion without damaging its credit—which also means the downside protection of "capex stops because the money runs out" does not exist; it will keep investing until demand peaks.**

## The three things to watch most closely

- **Depreciation life extended from 15 to 25 years.** The company did not quantify the impact; a rough estimate is RMB 4–5 billion less depreciation per year, or about USD 0.5 in EPS. The amount is not fatal, but this is a loosening of accounting estimates at the peak of the capex cycle—watch the policy notes in the next earnings report.
- **Price versus volume in Azure growth.** If the 45% guidance comes mainly from price increases (e.g., AI service surcharges), its sustainability deserves a discount; if it comes mainly from usage, demand is real.
- **Copilot ARPU.** 30 million seats is a volume figure; the revenue contribution was not disclosed. Usage-based billing is just starting (customer-service usage up 4x quarter over quarter), and this is the most important variable for 2027—it determines whether Microsoft's TAM is "number of enterprise employees" or "AI call volume."
