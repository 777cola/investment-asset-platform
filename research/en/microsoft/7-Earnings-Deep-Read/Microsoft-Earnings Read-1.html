Core call: revenue accelerating, cash decelerating

FY2026Q4 revenue USD 90.01 billion, up 17.8% YoY; operating income USD 40.6 billion at a 45.1% margin; net income USD 35.77 billion, up 31.3% YoY; EPS USD 4.81. All four beat consensus.

The other half of the same statement: capex USD 41 billion, up 70% YoY; free cash flow USD 19.6 billion, down 23% YoY; gross margin down from 68.6% to 67.2%. These are not contradictory results; they spell out the cost—the growth is real, and so is the cost.

Layer one: revenue mix — Azure drives acceleration, Office provides the floor

Intelligent Cloud generated RMB 39.3 billion of the RMB 90.01 billion in revenue, up 32% YoY and accelerating again from last quarter; the remaining two segments (Office Commercial, LinkedIn, Dynamics and Windows, Gaming, Search) contributed about RMB 50.7 billion, growing around 10% — the "quiet but steady" part of the business. The structure matters: Azure drives the valuation, Office underpins the base.

Azure accelerated from 40% to 43%, which management attributed to faster capacity coming online and higher GPU utilization rather than a sudden improvement in demand. That line is worth noting — the bottleneck sits on the supply side (power, racks, chips), not the demand side. Supply-constrained growth is higher quality: no need to cut prices to win volume, and prices can even move higher. Guidance for next quarter is 45% (constant currency), implying the acceleration is not over.

Copilot is the third curve: 30 million paid seats, up 10 million from 20 million last quarter, with net adds doubling sequentially. At a list price of USD 30/seat/month, that implies roughly USD 10.8 billion in annualized revenue, 3.3% of FY2026 revenue — a meaningful scale, but not yet enough to move overall growth. The real question is renewal rates and ARPU, and the company has not yet disclosed either.

Layer two: the market is not pricing in a structural decline in gross margin

Gross margin 67.2%, down 1.4pp YoY; cloud gross margin 65% and also trending down. Three drivers: a richer mix of Azure and AI infrastructure, front-loaded GPU depreciation, and the power and bandwidth costs of AI inference. Software carries an 80% gross margin, compute 60%, inference just over 50% — the more revenue shifts toward compute, the lower the blended gross margin.

Is that bad? No. Gross profit rose 15.4% YoY and is still growing in absolute terms; and once built, compute revenue is long-duration and contractually locked in (RPO RMB 678 billion). The risk is pricing: if the inference price war pushes per-token prices down faster than usage rises, gross margin will fall faster than the mix shift alone implies. Not yet visible, but watch whether cloud gross margin holds 60%.

Tier three: costs controlled like a mature company

Sales, R&D and G&A combined came to RMB 19.88 billion, +9.9% YoY and well below revenue growth of +17.8%, cutting the expense ratio from 23.7% to 22.1%. That is why operating margin still edged up 0.2pct: mix diluted gross margin while scale diluted expenses, and the two offset each other.

Two items bridge operating profit of RMB 40.6 billion and net profit of RMB 35.77 billion: add RMB 3.2 billion of investment income from Anthropic and subtract about RMB 710 million of net interest expense, giving roughly RMB 43.09 billion pre-tax; an effective tax rate of about 17% then yields net profit of RMB 35.77 billion. That 17% rate is low against the 21% US statutory rate, mainly due to R&D tax credits and the overseas structure — this rate is unsustainable and will most likely revert to 19%–20% over the next two to three years, meaning the same operating profit would produce roughly 1.5 points less net profit. The market broadly overlooks this.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the account is still open

Operating cash flow of RMB 55.4 billion, up 30% YoY, is very healthy: net profit was RMB 35.77 billion and operating cash flow is 1.55x that, so profit is not being dragged down by receivables or inventory. The problem sits on the next line: cash capex of RMB 35.76 billion, or RMB 41 billion including finance leases, leaves free cash flow of just RMB 19.6 billion, down 23% YoY.

The full year makes it clearer: operating cash flow of RMB 182.9 billion, cash capex of RMB 115.9 billion, free cash flow of RMB 67 billion. And FY2027 plans to invest RMB 175 billion—if operating cash flow grows 20% to RMB 219.5 billion, free cash flow drops to just RMB 44.5 billion, another one-third decline YoY. That is the valuation ceiling: not insufficient profit, but cash intercepted by capex.

The balance sheet still has room: cash and short-term investments of about RMB 90 billion, long-term debt of about RMB 60 billion, shareholders' equity of about RMB 340 billion, positive net cash and a AAA rating.In other words, Microsoft can borrow another RMB 100 billion without damaging its credit—which also means the downside protection of "capex stops because the money runs out" does not exist; it will keep investing until demand peaks.

The three things to watch most closely
  • Depreciation life extended from 15 to 25 years. Management did not quantify the impact; a rough estimate implies RMB 4–5 billion less depreciation per year, or about USD 0.5 of EPS. Not fatal, but loosening an accounting estimate at the peak of the capex cycle warrants attention—watch the policy notes in the next earnings report.
  • Price versus volume in Azure growth. If the 45% guidance is driven mainly by price (e.g., AI service surcharges), its sustainability deserves a discount; if it is driven mainly by usage, demand is real.
  • Copilot ARPU. 30 million seats is a volume figure; the revenue contribution was not disclosed. Usage-based billing is only just starting (customer-service usage up 4x QoQ), and this is the most important variable for 2027—it determines whether Microsoft's TAM is "number of enterprise employees" or "AI call volume."

Conclusion

Conclusion: full marks on revenue, a pass on cash. Four core metrics beat consensus: Azure accelerated, RPO rose 84% and net new Copilot seats doubled, confirming AI demand is real; gross margin fell and free cash flow dropped 23%, confirming the cost is real too. The key judgment is not about this quarter but about FY2027: with RMB 175 billion of capex deployed, which arrives first in 2028, depreciation or revenue. Until then, 22–26x is a reasonable valuation and 30x is a luxury.
