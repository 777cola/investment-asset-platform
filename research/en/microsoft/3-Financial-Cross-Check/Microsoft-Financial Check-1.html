Core call: headline numbers are clean; the issues sit entirely in the accounting basis

We cross-checked eight key Microsoft FY2026Q4 metrics against two sources. Revenue, operating income, net income, EPS, Azure growth and free cash flow all match exactly, with deviations within 0.02%.

The two deviations above 1% are definitional rather than errors: capex differs by 12.8% depending on whether finance leases are included, and the 19.5% market cap gap reflects two closing prices, from July 29 and July 31. The real issue is three figures with no second source to cross-check against — Copilot ARPU, the weighted term of RPO and the amount from the change in depreciation lives — all of which rest solely on the company's own account.

Two deviations above 1%, neither of them errors

First: capex of RMB 41.0 billion vs RMB 35.76 billion — a gap of RMB 5.24 billion, or 12.8%. The RMB 41.0 billion is "total investment" including finance leases; the RMB 35.76 billion is cash actually paid for purchases in the cash flow statement, verifiable by working back from free cash flow: operating cash flow of RMB 55.4 billion minus free cash flow of RMB 19.64 billion equals exactly RMB 35.76 billion. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions — one "how many assets were invested in this year," the other "how much cash was spent this year." Use the former for depreciation and capacity, the latter for cash.

Second: market cap of USD 3.45 trillion vs USD 2.97 trillion — a gap of 16.2%. This reflects a 19.5% gain in the two days after the earnings report, not a calculation error. For valuation, use the price on the day of USD 464.72; for year-on-year and earnings comparisons, the 7/29 price is acceptable. Mixing the two produces the false conclusion that "P/E suddenly jumped from 22x to 26x."

One more number that is easy to misread: net profit rose 31.3% YoY, above revenue growth of 17.8%, with the difference mainly from a RMB 3.2 billion gain on the Anthropic investment and operating leverage. Excluding this one-off gain, net profit grew about 21% YoY — the true growth rate of the core business.

Three figures cannot be cross-checked against two sources and can only be flagged as questionable

Copilot ARPU: Management disclosed only 30 million paid seats (20 million last quarter) and gave no revenue figure for those seats. The market has applied the USD 30/seat/month list price to arrive at an annualised USD 10.8 billion, an estimate the company has not endorsed and which serves only as an order-of-magnitude reference. Actual ARPU is likely below USD 30, given discounts to large customers and seats bundled into E5.

RPO weighted duration of 2.3 years, 30% recognised within 12 months: Both figures appear only in the earnings call Q&A; the financial statements contain no table to support them. Calculating "RMB 203.4 billion recognisable over the next four quarters" from them is reasonable, but it requires accepting one premise—that this is the distribution the company itself provided.

Financial impact of extending the depreciation period from 15 years to 25 years: The company did not quantify it. Any claim of "RMB 4 billion or RMB 8 billion less depreciation" is an extrapolation. This article uses RMB 4–5 billion (assuming affected assets account for half the depreciation base) and explicitly labels it a rough estimate.

The finally adopted basis

One convention throughout: revenue, profit and EPS on a GAAP company basis; capex including finance leases, with the cash basis shown in parentheses; free cash flow on a company basis (operating cash flow − cash capex); valuation based on the July 31 closing price and a back-solved share count.

We do not use adjusted (non-GAAP) figures to calculate P/E. The gap between Microsoft's GAAP and non-GAAP numbers is mainly investment gains of the Anthropic type, and including or excluding them does not change the conclusion on whether the stock is expensive — only the decimal. This differs from many China concept stocks: Microsoft's GAAP statements are themselves clean enough that no adjustment is needed to tell the story.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the data is usable, provided the definitions are stated clearly. Six zero-deviation items show no information loss between the financial statements and media retellings; the two large deviations are "whether leases are included" and "which day's price". All subsequent modules adopt the same conventions: GAAP profit, capex including leases of USD 41 billion, company-defined free cash flow of USD 19.6 billion, and the July 31 closing price of USD 464.72. Copilot ARPU, RPO duration and the depreciation change amount are flagged "company-defined, not third-party verified".
