## Two deviations above 1%, neither of them errors

First: capex of RMB 41.0 billion vs RMB 35.76 billion — a gap of RMB 5.24 billion, or 12.8%. The RMB 41.0 billion figure is the "total investment" basis including finance leases; the RMB 35.76 billion figure is the cash actually paid out for purchases in the cash flow statement — the latter can be verified by working back from free cash flow: operating cash flow of RMB 55.4 billion minus free cash flow of RMB 19.64 billion equals exactly RMB 35.76 billion. Both numbers are correct; they answer different questions: one answers "how many assets were invested in this year," the other answers "how much cash was spent this year." Use the former to analyze depreciation and capacity, the latter to analyze cash.

Second: market cap of USD 3.45 trillion vs USD 2.97 trillion — a gap of 16.2%. This stems from a 19.5% gain in the two days after the earnings report, not a calculation error. For valuation you must use the price on the day of USD 464.72; for year-on-year and earnings comparisons you may cite the 7/29 price. Mixing the two produces the false conclusion that "PE suddenly jumped from 22x to 26x."

One more number that is easy to misread: net profit rose 31.3% year on year, above revenue growth of 17.8%, with the difference mainly from a RMB 3.2 billion gain on the Anthropic investment and operating leverage. Excluding this one-off gain, net profit grew about 21% year on year — that is the true growth rate of the core business.

## Three figures cannot be cross-checked against two sources and can only be flagged as questionable

Copilot ARPU: The company only said 30 million paid seats (20 million last quarter) and did not say how much revenue those 30 million seats generated. The market multiplied the USD 30/seat/month list price to arrive at an annualized USD 10.8 billion estimate—a figure the company has not endorsed and that serves only as an order-of-magnitude reference. Actual ARPU is likely below USD 30, because large customers receive discounts and some seats are bundled into E5.

RPO weighted duration of 2.3 years, 30% recognized within 12 months: These two figures appear only in the earnings call Q&A; the financial statements provide no table to support them. Using them to calculate "RMB 203.4 billion recognizable over the next four quarters" is reasonable, but it requires accepting one premise—that this is the distribution the company itself provided.

Financial impact of changing the depreciation period from 15 years to 25 years: The company did not quantify it. All claims of "RMB 4 billion or RMB 8 billion less depreciation" are extrapolations. This article uses RMB 4–5 billion (assuming affected assets account for half the depreciation base) and explicitly labels it a rough estimate.

## The finally adopted basis

One principle: revenue, profit and EPS on a GAAP company basis; capex on a basis including finance leases, with the cash basis noted in parentheses; free cash flow on a company basis (operating cash flow − cash capex); valuation using the July 31 closing price and back-solved share count.

Do not use adjusted (non-GAAP) figures to calculate PE, because the gap between Microsoft's GAAP and non-GAAP numbers is mainly investment gains of the Anthropic type; including or excluding them does not change the conclusion on whether the stock is expensive, only the decimal. This differs from many China concept stocks — Microsoft's GAAP statements are themselves clean enough that no adjustment is needed to tell a story.
