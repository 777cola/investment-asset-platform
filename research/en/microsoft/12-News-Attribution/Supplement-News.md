## Phase 1 (May–June): a stream of bad news; the share price flatlined around 390

Microsoft's stock has slid from its high at the start of the year; the 52-week high of USD 492.02 came in the first half of 2026, and the shares had fallen more than 20% by the time of the earnings release. The cause is not the business but three overlapping factors: repeated upward revisions to capex guidance, rising memory prices pushing up server costs, and doubts over AI monetization.

The market overlooked the most informative item: DRAM up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%.This means Microsoft's cost per unit of compute will rise in FY2027, while its cloud gross margin is already trending down (65%). Nobody paid attention to this news that day, but it partly explains the 1.4-point decline in cloud gross margin in the Q4 results, and it will be a source of margin pressure in FY2027.

## Phase two (July 29): one day overturned every assumption

Three figures from the earnings release answer every prior doubt: Azure accelerated from 40% to 43%, guidance for next quarter is 45%, and RPO reached RMB 678 billion, up 84% year over year (still up 25% excluding OpenAI). The market had worried whether AI revenue was just money moving from one pocket to another; this quarter's orders from non-OpenAI customers prove it is not.

The stock rose 16% after hours, then gapped up the next day and closed at USD 451. The reaction is reasonable—not sentiment, but a change in the denominator of the valuation model: the market had expected FY2027 EPS of about USD 19, and after the report that was broadly raised above USD 20. At the same 23x multiple, the price should rise 5%–8%; add a lower risk premium, and 16% is not excessive.

## Phase 3 (July 30–31): sell-side upgrades are follow-through, not new information

Citi raised its target price to 600 and Goldman Sachs to 640, averaging about 561. Such moves carry little information — they are post-earnings model updates, not the discovery of new facts. The 3.02% gain on July 31 was institutions topping up positions, not someone finding something new.

There is a simple test for whether this kind of news carries information: does it contain a number the market did not previously know. Sell-side target price hikes usually do not; they merely plug known numbers into known models.

## What counts as noise: three criteria

- Anything that does not change the next three years of cash flow is noise. The EU investigation, analyst ratings and index rebalancing all fall into this category.

- Anything that affects only one earnings report is noise. One-off investment gains, FX gains and losses, litigation settlements — the numbers are large, but they do not recur.

- Anything that changes unit economics is signal. Memory price increases (each server costs more), Azure price increases (each token costs more), depreciation schedules (how much less is expensed each year) — these three will keep affecting margins.

Reorder the past three months by this standard: earnings are signal (they changed the growth assumption), memory price increases are signal (they changed the cost assumption, but were ignored), and the other four items are noise.

## Without this earnings report, what would it be worth now?

Work backwards: the July 29 close of USD 388.9 corresponds to FY2027 EPS of about USD 19, or 20.5x. If the results had merely met expectations (Azure holding at 40%, RPO +50%), the market would probably have assigned 21x, or about USD 400.

In other words, this earnings report created about USD 65 of value, or 17% — because the growth assumption shifted from "40% and decelerating" to "43% and accelerating." That is a real valuation change, not a bubble. But remember: that 17% is already in the price, so chasing the stock means paying that money on someone else's behalf.
