Core call: earnings day is the only event that matters over the next three months

Six Microsoft-related developments emerged between late April and late July: two upward revisions to industry capex, one memory price-increase pass-through, one EU regulatory action, one earnings report and one round of collective sell-side upgrades.

Only the earnings report moved the valuation, because it answered a question no one had been able to answer before: is AI revenue real or narrative. Of the other five, the memory price increase was completely ignored by the market, and the regulatory action affected less than two trading days.

Phase 1 (May–June): a steady run of bad news left the share price flat at around 390

Microsoft shares have fallen from their high at the start of the year. The 52-week high of USD 492.02 was set in the first half of 2026, and the stock was down more than 20% by the time of the earnings release. The cause is not the business but three overlapping factors: repeated upward revisions to capex guidance, rising memory prices pushing up server costs, and doubts over AI monetisation.

The market overlooked the most informative item: DRAM up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%.Microsoft's cost per unit of compute will therefore rise in FY2027, while its cloud gross margin is already trending down (65%). The news drew no attention that day, but it partly explains the 1.4-point decline in cloud gross margin in the Q4 results and will be a source of margin pressure in FY2027.

Phase two (July 29): one day overturned every assumption

Three figures from the earnings release settle every prior doubt: Azure accelerated from 40% to 43%, guidance for next quarter is 45%, and RPO reached RMB 678 billion, up 84% YoY (still up 25% excluding OpenAI). The market had worried that AI revenue was simply money moving from one pocket to another; this quarter's orders from non-OpenAI customers prove otherwise.

The stock rose 16% after hours, then gapped up the next day and closed at USD 451. The reaction is reasonable—not sentiment, but a change in the denominator of the valuation model: the market had expected FY2027 EPS of about USD 19, and after the report that was broadly raised above USD 20. At the same 23x multiple, the price should rise 5%–8%; add a lower risk premium, and 16% is not excessive.

Phase 3 (July 30–31): sell-side upgrades follow through rather than add new information

Citi lifted its target price to 600 and Goldman Sachs to 640, an average of about 561. Such moves carry little information — they are post-earnings model updates, not the discovery of new facts. The 3.02% gain on July 31 reflected institutions topping up positions, not anyone finding something new.

A simple test applies to whether this kind of news carries information: does it contain a number the market did not previously know? Sell-side target price hikes usually do not; they merely plug known numbers into known models.

What counts as noise: three criteria
  • Anything that does not change the next three years of cash flow is noise. The EU investigation, analyst ratings and index rebalancing all fall into this category.
  • Anything that affects only one earnings report is noise. One-off investment gains, FX gains and losses, litigation settlements — the numbers are large, but they do not recur.
  • Anything that changes unit economics is signal. Memory price increases (each server costs more), Azure price increases (each token costs more), depreciation schedules (how much less is expensed each year) — these three will keep affecting margins.

Reorder the past three months by this standard: earnings are signal (they changed the growth assumption), memory price increases are signal (they changed the cost assumption, but were ignored), and the other four items are noise.

Without this earnings report, what would it be worth now?

Work backwards: the July 29 close of USD 388.9 implies FY2027 EPS of about USD 19, or 20.5x. Had the results merely met expectations — Azure holding at 40%, RPO +50% — the market would probably have assigned 21x, or about USD 400.

In other words, this earnings report created about USD 65 of value, or 17%, as the growth assumption shifted from "40% and decelerating" to "43% and accelerating." That is a genuine re-rating, not a bubble. But remember: that 17% is already in the price, so chasing the stock means paying that money on someone else's behalf.

Conclusion

Conclusion: of the three months' events, only one merits review — the earnings report. It turned "can AI make money" from narrative into numbers, at the cost of a one-off 19.5% share-price jump. Of the other five items, two are cost signals that will be digested gradually (memory price increases, depreciation) and three are noise to ignore outright. For holders: don't trade on headlines; anchor on the three quarterly metrics (Azure growth, RPO conversion, Copilot seats).
