Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Microsoft

Microsoft FY2026Q4: Real Profits, Tight Cash

Core view

Buffett asks only three things of a financial report: are the profits real, is the cash freely deployable, and has retained money created value. Microsoft passes the first two; the third will not have an answer until 2028. This quarter's statements put that question in unusually sharp relief.

Income statement: the good part is real

Revenue RMB 90.01 billion, operating profit RMB 40.6 billion, net profit RMB 35.77 billion. The relationship among the three is clean: gross margin 67.2%, expense ratio 22.1%, operating margin 45.1%, with no unusual line items between gross profit and operating profit. Investment income of RMB 3.2 billion is disclosed separately; excluding it, net profit still grew 21% year on year, above revenue growth.

The only question mark is the 17% effective tax rate, below the 21% statutory rate. This is not fraud but R&D credits and the overseas structure, and it is not sustainable. If the tax rate returns to 19%–20% over the next two to three years, the same operating profit would yield 1.5 points less net profit. Build this into three-year forecasts.

Cash flow statement: half the money went out the door

Operating cash flow was RMB 55.4 billion, up 30% year on year and 1.55x net profit — very healthy. But cash capex was RMB 35.76 billion (RMB 41 billion including finance leases), leaving free cash flow of just RMB 19.6 billion, down 23% year on year.

Buffett would pause here. He does not oppose capex; he opposes capex with an uncertain return. See's Candies can raise prices without spending money; Microsoft must spend over RMB 100 billion a year to sustain 40% cloud growth — these two businesses are not in the same quality tier. He would say: this is a good business, but not the best business.

The depreciation bill: several tens of billions more each year for the next three years

FY2026 cash capex was RMB 115.9 billion, which will turn into depreciation over the next 6 years (GPU/servers) and 15–25 years (data centers). Roughly applying a 6-year straight-line method to GPU assets, the short-lived assets invested in FY2026 alone will add about RMB 13 billion of depreciation per year (115.9 × 2/3 ÷ 6).

Meanwhile, the company extended the depreciation life of some assets from 15 years to 25 years this year, cutting depreciation by RMB 4–5 billion a year. With RMB 13 billion more on one side and RMB 4.5 billion less on the other, the net effect is still a sharp rise in depreciation. That means FY2027–FY2028 operating margin will be compressed by 2–3 points unless revenue growth outpaces depreciation. That is why he wants a position small enough that even a depreciation blow-up would not hurt.

How would he value it?

He does not use PE; he uses "market cap ÷ free cash flow" and "market cap ÷ owner earnings." Now USD 3.45 trillion ÷ USD 67 billion = 51x free cash flow—placed against his lifetime of investments, this number falls in the "would never touch" range. But Microsoft's free cash flow is artificially depressed (capex front-loaded), so he also looks at adjusted measures such as "market cap ÷ (free cash flow + a portion of capex)."

Even adjusted, the figure sits around 30x. His conclusion would be: good company, price not cheap, buy a little, and revisit after the depreciation peak passes in 2028.

Conclusion

Conclusion: financial quality A, valuation attractiveness C. Earnings are real, cash is healthy, and the balance sheet has headroom, but capex intercepts free cash flow; 51x free cash flow implies a 1.9% yield with no margin of safety. His move would be "small position, long hold", not "buy heavily".
