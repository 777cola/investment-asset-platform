## Title and lead-in

Title: Microsoft earned USD 35.8 billion this quarter, then spent USD 41 billion

Subtitle: Azure accelerated from 40% to 43% and AI has finally proved it can make money — but the bill has arrived too.

## Look at three numbers first.

Microsoft reported FY2026Q4 results after the close on July 29. Three numbers matter most:

Revenue USD 90.01 billion, up 17.8% YoY. Net income USD 35.77 billion, up 31.3% YoY. Azure growth accelerated from 40% to 43%, with guidance of 45% for the next quarter.

The stock gapped up 16% the next day and gained 19.5% over two sessions. The market put its money where its mouth is: Microsoft can actually monetize AI.

## Flame: the order book says more than revenue

Beyond revenue, another number is underappreciated: commercial remaining performance obligations (RPO) of USD 678 billion, up 84% YoY — an increase of USD 51 billion in a single quarter.

That is contracted but not yet recognised revenue. More critically, nearly all of the incremental growth came from customers other than OpenAI.

Why does this matter? For the past two years, the market's biggest question has been whether Microsoft's AI revenue is simply OpenAI's money passed through. With orders from non-OpenAI customers now underpinning the book, that concern can largely be set aside: excluding OpenAI, RPO still grew 25% YoY.

## Seawater: the RMB 41 billion bill

Capex in the same quarter was USD 41 billion, up 70% YoY. Roughly two-thirds went into CPUs and GPUs — kit that becomes obsolete within six years.

Free cash flow tells the story: USD 19.6 billion, down 23% YoY. Against a market cap of USD 3.45 trillion, the full-year USD 67 billion implies a yield of 1.94% — below the ten-year US Treasury.

Management has guided to a further USD 175 billion of investment in FY2027. That equals 53% of FY2026 full-year revenue.

So the real takeaway from this quarter is not that "Microsoft is raking it in" but that "every dollar Microsoft earns goes straight into the ground and comes back as server racks." This is not a financial risk; Microsoft can afford it. It is a valuation constraint: until capex peaks, its cash flow cannot support a P/E above 30x.

## A quick note: don't take that +31% net profit at face value

Net profit rose 31.3% YoY, far outpacing 17.8% revenue growth. That looks impressive, but it includes a USD 3.2 billion one-off investment gain from appreciation of the company's Anthropic stake.

Strip that out and net profit grows about 21% YoY — the true growth rate of the core business, still strong but less spectacular.

The report also contains a quiet adjustment: the depreciation period was extended from 15 years to 25 years. The company did not disclose the financial impact; by conventional estimates, this cuts annual depreciation by RMB 4–5 billion and adds about USD 0.5 to EPS. No extra cash was generated — the books simply look a little better.

## By the way, Copilot: 30 million seats, and then what?

This is the quarter's most overhyped and most underestimated number.

30 million paid seats, up from 20 million last quarter, with net additions doubling QoQ. At a rough list price of USD 30 per seat per month, that is USD 10.8 billion in annualised revenue, or 3.3% of full-year revenue.

The scale shows it is no longer an experiment, but it is not yet enough to move the growth rate. The real question is: does this make Office more expensive, or does it make Office better?

The former is a price increase, capped by the growth rate of enterprise IT budgets; the latter is a new business, capped by AI call volume. The company disclosed neither ARPU nor renewal rates—until those two numbers are out, every Copilot valuation is a guess.

## So is it expensive now?

USD 464.72, 25.8x P/E, 23x FY2027E. For a company that can still grow EPS by 20%, that is not expensive — but not cheap either.

Three scenarios: bear case 400 (-14%), base case 564 (+21%), bull case 700 (+51%). Reward-to-risk 1.5 to 1.

Sell-side targets rose across the board post-earnings (Citi 600, Goldman Sachs 640), averaging 561 — roughly in line with the base case. That makes 564 a consensus number, not an aggressive target. The upside of consensus is you won't be embarrassed; the downside is excess returns can only come from beating expectations.

## Someone asked: with such a good earnings report, why did it rise only 16%

Because the market is doing the math too. A revenue beat earns credit; capex and cash flow lose it. Net the two out and you get 16%.

Compare with 2023–2024, when a single Nvidia earnings report could move the stock 25%: it was a pure beneficiary, and the shovel seller doesn't care who strikes gold. Microsoft is different—it sells shovels (Azure), digs for gold (Copilot) and buys shovels (RMB 175 billion in capex). With three identities stacked together, the market will not value it on one-sided optimism.

That is no bad thing. The price still embeds rationality, which means there is no bubble.

## Finally, three definitions

First, the RMB 41.0 billion capex figure includes finance leases; cash capex is RMB 35.76 billion, a RMB 5.2 billion gap. Second, the RMB 35.77 billion net profit I cited includes RMB 3.2 billion of one-off investment gains; strip those out and it is RMB 33.1 billion. Third, the 25.8x P/E is on GAAP net profit; ex one-offs it is about 27x.

The basis itself does not matter; mixing bases does. The same set of financial statements can tell two different stories depending on the basis — which is why, when reading financials, the first question to ask is "how was this number calculated?"

## One-line conclusion

Buy, but don't buy it all at once.

Open one-third of the position at the current price, double it if the stock falls back below 430, and stop chasing once it rises above 560. The logic is simple: the certainty of this business justifies paying 25x, but the RMB 175 billion bill has not been settled yet, and the price embeds no discount.

Buy it because in ten years enterprises will still need Office and will still rent compute; buy in tranches because neither you nor I know whether AI demand in 2028 will be as large as everyone assumes today.

Earn money from certainty, not from imagination. That is the only thing I am sure of.
