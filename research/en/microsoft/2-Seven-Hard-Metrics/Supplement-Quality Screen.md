## It cleared all six criteria, and not narrowly — by a wide margin

Read these six metrics not just for whether they pass, but for the margin of safety. Net margin of 39.7% against a 10% threshold is a fourfold cushion; ROE of around 39% against 15%; operating cash flow at 1.55x net profit — the last shows profit is no paper figure: depreciation and amortisation depress book profit, so cash actually collected exceeds profit by half.

One item must be stripped out of net profit: a USD 3.2 billion gain on the Anthropic investment, equal to 9% of quarterly net profit. Excluding it leaves net profit of about RMB 33.1 billion and a net margin of 36.8%, still far above the threshold. This adjustment is no nitpick: the gain will not recur, and using it to calculate P/E would understate the valuation.

Gross margin down 1.4 percentage points YoY is the only deteriorating direction among the seven metrics. This is not a price cut driven by competition but a shift in product mix from software to compute. The trend will persist, and gross margin will most likely fall another two to three points over the next three years, but because Azure's absolute gross profit is larger, total profit still trends upward.

## Capex absorbed 74% of operating cash flow — the one metric that fell short

Capex of RMB 41 billion against operating cash flow of RMB 55.4 billion — a ratio of 74%. The full year is more extreme: cash capex of RMB 115.9 billion against operating cash flow of RMB 182.9 billion, or 63%; including finance leases, the ratio is higher still. The only one of the seven metrics that fails is precisely the only one deteriorating fast — two years ago this ratio was still below 45%, and by fiscal 2026 it is already 63%, stepping up every year.

The metric has a very concrete meaning: for every RMB 100 of cash collected, the company must spend RMB 74 on GPUs and data centers. What remains must cover dividends (about RMB 25.8 billion for the full year), buybacks (about RMB 17.2 billion) and a margin of safety. That is why, for the RMB 175 billion of capex in FY2027, the market will certainly ask "how will the remaining gap be funded" — whatever operating cash flow does not cover can only come from the balance sheet.

## One miss — is it fatal?

Not fatal, but it changes the valuation method. Excessively high capex intensity turns disastrous in two scenarios: first, demand falls before depreciation is fully taken, leaving data centres as idle capacity; second, financing costs rise and the debt-financed capex model breaks. The first is business risk, the second interest-rate risk, and neither has materialised yet—Azure growth is accelerating, RPO is up 84% YoY, and the rating remains AAA.

The correct reading of this miss, then: it downgrades Microsoft from "a stable cash cow deserving 30x" to "a capital-intensive growth stock deserving 22–26x." The neutral 24x in the working paper means exactly this. A re-rating is warranted only if capex/operating cash flow falls below 50% while Azure growth stays above 35%.
