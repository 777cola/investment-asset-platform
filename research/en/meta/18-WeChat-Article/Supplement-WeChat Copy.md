## Look at three numbers first.

RMB 64.6 billion, RMB 34.9 billion, -RMB 2.1 billion.

RMB 64.6 billion is third-quarter revenue, up 26% year on year and RMB 1.2 billion above the consensus. RMB 34.9 billion is quarterly capital expenditure, up 85.6% year on year and a record high. -RMB 2.1 billion is free cash flow, negative for the first time since the company listed in 2012.

Read the three numbers together: the company earns plenty, spends plenty, and spends more than it earns. This is not an operating problem — operating cash flow for the same period was RMB 32.79 billion, up 9.3% year on year. The company chose to bury the money in the ground, and what it buried was GPUs and data centers.

## Flaming: the advertising machine is still accelerating

Advertising revenue was USD 63.06 billion, 97.6% of total revenue and a record high for a single quarter. More important is the mix: ad impressions rose 10% year on year and average price per ad rose 14%.

Volume and price rising together is unusual in advertising. Rising volume shows users are still there; rising price shows advertisers are competing for slots.In the past five years this combination has appeared only two or three times, and this is the second consecutive quarter. In the second quarter, impressions rose 14% and price per ad rose 12%.

Compare with peers: Google advertising rose 10.6%, Amazon 10.9%, Meta 26%. The same AI, applied to search, means "users no longer click on ads"; applied to feeds, it means "ads are more accurate". The impact of technology on different businesses can point in opposite directions.

## Seawater: the bill has arrived

RMB 34.9 billion of capex, RMB 83.06 billion cumulative over the first three quarters, full-year guidance of RMB 135–145 billion. This money turns into GPUs, data centers and twenty-year power purchase agreements; it does not become revenue immediately, but from 2027 it becomes depreciation.

Amortized over five to six years, that adds RMB 20–30 billion of cost a year. Covering it requires RMB 60–70 billion of additional annual revenue—equivalent to growing another 25% on top of this year's base. This year's 27% growth is just enough; if it falls to 15% next year, it will not be.

The income statement already shows signs: cost of revenue rose 45.2%, gross margin fell from 82.0% to 79.3%; R&D expense rose 32.4%. Revenue up 26%, costs up 45%—operating leverage was negative this quarter.

## A quick note: don't take that +264% net profit at face value

Net profit of RMB 9.86 billion, up 264% year on year — a headline that intimidates. But in Q3 last year Meta booked USD 18.95 billion of income tax, an effective tax rate of 87%, crushing net profit to USD 2.71 billion. With such a low base, triple-digit growth was inevitable.

This year there is also a USD 9.89 billion litigation settlement — reached in August with the attorneys general of 52 states, totalling up to USD 18.59 billion, payable over ten years. So look at two numbers: GAAP EPS of USD 3.83, and USD 7.40 excluding the settlement. A gap of nearly double — which one to use depends on what question you are asking.

## Incidentally, Muse

Muse, the personal agent launched on September 8, passed 10 million downloads in 40 days, with daily active users at about 25%. On September 21, the day it topped the US App Store, the stock rose 11.3% in a single day and market cap increased by USD 192 billion.

But the math needs to be clear. Morgan Stanley estimates that at 25% of the weekly quota used, monthly service cost per user is about USD 37; subscription tiers are USD 20 and USD 100. That is, for users on the USD 20 tier, Meta loses USD 17 for every month it retains them. Zuckerberg says cost efficiency can improve tenfold by 2027. If that holds, the business works; if not, this is the second metaverse.

## The real turning point: from "returning money to shareholders" to "asking shareholders for money"

On October 21, Meta announced the creation of non-voting Class C shares, effective January 19, 2027, distributed 1:1 — in substance a two-for-one split. Zuckerberg's 60.6% voting power is unchanged.

Five days later, the company put it to use: USD 25 billion of common stock (priced at USD 625), USD 12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred stock with a 6.5% coupon, plus an at-the-market offering of up to USD 60 billion. That totals USD 95 billion, the largest equity raise in the company's history. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom intend to subscribe up to USD 5 billion in aggregate.

Meanwhile, in the first nine months of 2026, Meta's buybacks were zero. Over the past seven years it spent USD 20 billion to USD 30 billion a year on buybacks, the most aggressive of the Magnificent Seven. Within a year, it went from "returning USD 30 billion a year to shareholders" to "asking shareholders for USD 95 billion." This is not financial engineering; it is a strategic pivot.

## So is it expensive now?

Closed at USD 606.73 on November 2, market cap USD 1.575 trillion, 21x TTM, down 22% from the September 24 high of USD 777.59.

Base case: EPS of USD 28 in 2027, 22x multiple, fair value USD 620 — essentially in line with the current price. But that is the share count before the equity raise. The USD 37.5 billion already priced in dilutes 2.3%; the USD 60 billion raise, if fully used, dilutes another 3.9%, for 6% in total. Post-dilution EPS is USD 26.3; at the same 22x, fair value is USD 580.

In other words, buying at the current price implies an expected return of negative 4%, against 32% downside (USD 410 in the bear case). The risk/reward does not work, no matter how good the company is.

## One-line conclusion

A good company at a bad moment, with no discount in the price.

Two triggers for buying: the 2027 expense growth guidance comes in below revenue growth, or the share price falls back below USD 500 (below 18x). Until then, just watch. A good company does not need to be bought at a bad moment; all it needs is patience.
