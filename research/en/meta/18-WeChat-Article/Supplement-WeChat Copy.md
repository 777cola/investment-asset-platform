## Look at three numbers first.

RMB 64.6 billion, RMB 34.9 billion, -RMB 2.1 billion.

Revenue in the third quarter came in at RMB 64.6 billion, up 26% YoY and RMB 1.2 billion ahead of consensus. Quarterly capex reached a record RMB 34.9 billion, up 85.6% YoY. Free cash flow was -RMB 2.1 billion — negative for the first time since the company listed in 2012.

Read the three together: the company earns plenty, spends plenty, and spends more than it earns. This is not an operating problem — operating cash flow for the same period was RMB 32.79 billion, up 9.3% YoY. Management chose to bury the money in the ground, and what it buried was GPUs and data centers.

## Flaming: the advertising machine is still accelerating

Advertising revenue reached USD 63.06 billion, 97.6% of total revenue and a record high for a single quarter. The mix matters more: ad impressions rose 10% YoY while average price per ad rose 14%.

Volume and price rising together is unusual in advertising. Rising volume shows users are still there; rising price shows advertisers are competing for slots.Over the past five years this combination has appeared only two or three times, and this is the second consecutive quarter. In the second quarter, impressions rose 14% and price per ad rose 12%.

Against peers: Google advertising rose 10.6%, Amazon 10.9% and Meta 26%. The same AI applied to search means "users no longer click on ads"; applied to feeds, it means "ads are more accurate". Technology can cut opposite ways across different businesses.

## Seawater: the bill has arrived

Capex of RMB 34.9 billion in the quarter and RMB 83.06 billion cumulative over the first three quarters, against full-year guidance of RMB 135–145 billion. This spending buys GPUs, data centres and twenty-year power purchase agreements; it does not convert into revenue immediately, but from 2027 it converts into depreciation.

Amortised over five to six years, that adds RMB 20–30 billion of cost a year. Covering it requires RMB 60–70 billion of additional annual revenue—equivalent to growing another 25% on top of this year's base. This year's 27% growth is just enough; at 15% next year, it will not be.

The income statement already shows the strain: cost of revenue rose 45.2% and gross margin fell from 82.0% to 79.3%, while R&D expense rose 32.4%. Revenue up 26% against costs up 45%—operating leverage was negative this quarter.

## A quick note: don't take that +264% net profit at face value

Net profit of RMB 9.86 billion, up 264% YoY — an intimidating headline. But in Q3 last year Meta booked USD 18.95 billion of income tax at an effective rate of 87%, crushing net profit to USD 2.71 billion. Off such a low base, triple-digit growth was inevitable.

This year adds a USD 9.89 billion litigation settlement — reached in August with the attorneys general of 52 states, totalling up to USD 18.59 billion and payable over ten years. So focus on two numbers: GAAP EPS of USD 3.83, and USD 7.40 excluding the settlement. A gap of nearly double — which one to use depends on the question you are asking.

## Incidentally, Muse

Muse, the personal agent launched on September 8, passed 10 million downloads in 40 days, with daily active users at about 25%. On September 21, the day it topped the US App Store, the stock rose 11.3% in a single day and market cap increased by USD 192 billion.

The math, however, needs to be clear. Morgan Stanley estimates that at 25% of the weekly quota used, monthly service cost per user is about USD 37, against subscription tiers of USD 20 and USD 100. For users on the USD 20 tier, that means Meta loses USD 17 for every month it retains them. Zuckerberg says cost efficiency can improve tenfold by 2027. If that holds, the business works; if not, this is the second metaverse.

## The real turning point: from returning cash to shareholders to asking them for more

On October 21, Meta announced the creation of non-voting Class C shares, effective January 19, 2027 and distributed 1:1 — in substance a two-for-one split. Zuckerberg's 60.6% voting power is unchanged.

Five days later the company put the structure to work: USD 25 billion of common stock (priced at USD 625), USD 12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred stock with a 6.5% coupon, plus an at-the-market offering of up to USD 60 billion. That totals USD 95 billion, the largest equity raise in the company's history. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom intend to subscribe up to USD 5 billion in aggregate.

Meanwhile, Meta's buybacks were zero in the first nine months of 2026. Over the past seven years it spent USD 20 billion to USD 30 billion a year on buybacks, the most aggressive of the Magnificent Seven. Within a year, it went from "returning USD 30 billion a year to shareholders" to "asking shareholders for USD 95 billion." This is not financial engineering; it is a strategic pivot.

## So is it expensive now?

Closed at USD 606.73 on November 2, a market cap of USD 1.575 trillion, 21x TTM, down 22% from the September 24 high of USD 777.59.

Base case: EPS of USD 28 in 2027 on a 22x multiple gives fair value of USD 620 — essentially in line with the current price. But that is the share count before the equity raise. The USD 37.5 billion already priced in dilutes 2.3%; the USD 60 billion raise, if fully used, dilutes another 3.9%, for 6% in total. Post-dilution EPS is USD 26.3; at the same 22x, fair value is USD 580.

In other words, buying at the current price implies an expected return of negative 4%, against 32% downside (USD 410 in the bear case). The risk/reward does not work, no matter how good the company is.

## One-line conclusion

A good company at a bad moment, with no discount in the price.

Two triggers would make us buyers: 2027 expense growth guidance coming in below revenue growth, or the share price falling back below USD 500 (below 18x). Until then, we watch. A good company need not be bought at a bad moment; all it needs is patience.
