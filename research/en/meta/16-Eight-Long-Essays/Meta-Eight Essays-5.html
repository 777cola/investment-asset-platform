Industry and competition: the only share gainer in a zero-sum market — Meta

Part Five · Who Is Growing, Who Is Bleeding

Core view

Q3 2026 laid bare a rare divergence in digital advertising: Meta ads grew 25.9%, against 10.6% at Google and 10.9% at Amazon. The same AI cut both ways across formats — eroding search through summaries while strengthening feeds through recommendations.

Landscape: three giants plus a long tail

On Q3 2026 single-quarter ad revenue, Meta booked USD 63.06 billion, Google USD 82.01 billion and Amazon USD 19.64 billion — a combined USD 164.7 billion, or roughly USD 658.8 billion annualised. Pinterest at USD 1.17 billion, Reddit at USD 895 million and Snap at USD 1.72 billion (Q2) together amount to less than 6% of Meta.

That structure leaves industry growth dependent on either taking budget share or raising unit prices. Meta did both this quarter, making it the only player gaining share in a zero-sum market.

Why the divergence: AI's opposing effects on the two ad formats

Search advertising is fundamentally about "users arriving with intent." AI summaries answer the query directly on the results page, users do not click the ads, and click-through rates fall. Feed advertising is fundamentally about "the system guessing what you want." The stronger the AI, the more accurate the guess, the higher the conversion, and the more expensive the unit price.

The same technology is destroying inventory in one case and adding value to inventory in the other.This is not a difference in execution but a difference in business model. Grasp this, and you will not read Google's 10.6% as "Google has gotten worse," nor Meta's 26% as "Meta is invincible."

The agent wars: four approaches race ahead at once

OpenAI released Dots on September 30, Google released Gemini agents on October 8 and Anthropic launched Claude Duet on October 22 — but Meta's Muse got there first, on September 8. In China, Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance have all followed suit.

What decides this round is not model scores but three things: distribution (who can reach a billion people), the payment loop (who can complete transactions) and compute cost (who can drive unit task cost to zero). Meta has all three, but leads on none. Google leads on distribution (Android + Search), Apple and Stripe lead on payments, and Google leads on compute cost (TPU).

Cycle: capital-side overheating, demand-side moderate

The four major cloud providers spent a combined USD 207.8 billion of capex in 2026Q3, up 115.5% YoY, with combined free cash flow of -USD 27.31 billion. That is a classic signal of capex overheating: supply is expanding at a doubling pace while demand grows at 10% to 26%.

Overcapacity and price declines are inevitable in between. Memory prices already rose 60% to 70% in early 2026, and such gains cannot persist for three years. When the cycle peaks, Meta's capex will fall first and its profit will recover first—but by then the market will already be worrying about demand. That is the difficulty of investing in cyclical stocks: good news and bad news always arrive at the same time.

Map of Budget Migration

Ad budgets are shifting on three fronts: from traditional media to digital, from the open web to walled gardens, and from brand to performance. Meta captures the latter two and little of the first, since its ads remain primarily performance-driven.

Retail media is the new variable. Amazon and Walmart have turned on-site search placements into ad inventory, and the segment is growing fast. The budget at stake is not Meta's but Google's search advertising budget. So the 2026 divergence is partly AI and partly budgets being reallocated across platforms.

Addendum

On a ten-year view, Meta's ad unit price was essentially flat from 2016 to 2022 (a rising emerging-market user mix dragged the average price down), resumed rising in 2023 and is up 14% in 2026. The inflection came after Reels monetisation and AI recommendations launched, which points to capability-driven pricing gains rather than luck.

Now the other side of Google this quarter: cloud revenue of USD 30.71 billion, up 102.6% YoY and accelerating 20.8 percentage points from the prior quarter. Google's capex has a second outlet; Meta's does not. Both are buying GPUs, but Google can sell compute to customers while Meta can only wait for ad monetisation. That is an industry-structure difference, not an execution difference.

Conclusion

Sector conclusion: Meta is the share winner in a mature market, but the way it wins—heavy capital investment—leaves it more fragile than other winners.The agent battle has only just begun and the outcome is undecided; the capex cycle is near its peak but has not yet peaked. With these two uncertainties overlapping, the sector offers no case to buy.
