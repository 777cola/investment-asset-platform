Financial reality: depreciation takes centre stage in 2027 — Meta

Part Four · Three traps in a single financial statement

Core view

On the surface this report reads "revenue above consensus, profit down"; in reality it is "revenue is fine, the cash is gone, and future costs have not yet begun to be provisioned." RMB 140 billion of capex will turn into RMB 20-30 billion of depreciation a year in 2027 to 2029 — a cost already on the way.

Pitfall 1: +264% net profit

Net profit of RMB 9.86 billion, up 264% year on year, looks impressive. But Q3 2025 included a one-off USD 18.95 billion tax charge that crushed the base to USD 2.71 billion. This is an accounting phenomenon, not an operating improvement.

Two numbers that actually matter: GAAP diluted EPS of USD 3.83, and USD 7.40 excluding the USD 9.89 billion settlement. On 3.83, a 21x PE becomes 40x; on 7.40, it is 20x. Same price, two conclusions — the only difference is the basis.

Pitfall two: the halo of operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was RMB 32.79 billion, up 9.3% year on year, and media headlines all called it "still strong." True, but incomplete. In the same period, purchases of property and equipment were RMB 33.94 billion and finance-lease principal was RMB 970 million, leaving free cash flow at negative RMB 2.12 billion — the first negative quarter in fourteen years as a listed company.

Part of operating cash flow is added-back depreciation, and depreciation is the accounting afterimage of past capital expenditure. For a company in a heavy-capex phase, operating cash flow looks good while free cash flow looks ugly. Which one to watch depends on whether you want to know if it "can operate" or if it "can pay out."

Trap three: depreciation that has not yet begun to be recognized

Capex of USD 135–145 billion in 2026, with USD 83.06 billion already spent in the first three quarters. Assuming a blended life of four to six years for servers and fifteen to twenty years for data centers, that implies roughly USD 23–28 billion of incremental depreciation per year. That cost does not appear in the 2026 statements; it starts weighing on profit from 2027.

To cover that cost, working back from a 40% operating margin requires USD 60–70 billion of additional annual revenue, equivalent to a further 25% increase on top of the 2026 base of USD 256.2 billion. Revenue growth of 27% in 2026 is just enough; if it falls to 15% in 2027, it will not be. This is the only risk that can be verified with arithmetic.

Balance sheet: plenty of cash, even more commitments

Cash and marketable securities of USD 82 billion and operating cash flow of USD 97 billion for the first three quarters look comfortable. But on the other side sit USD 825 billion in lease and purchase commitments, the newly issued USD 12.5 billion of 6.5% coupon preferred stock, and a USD 60 billion ATM facility.

"Ample cash" and "must raise financing" can both be true — the classic state of an asset-heavy transition. Post-earnings CDS spreads have risen to elevated levels; the market is already repricing its credit, a signal more direct than any analyst commentary.

Three profit paths for 2027

2027 EPS under three scenarios: first, revenue up 18% and expenses up 20%, EPS of about USD 26; second, revenue up 15% and expenses up 25%, EPS of about USD 23; third, revenue up 22% and expenses up 15%, EPS of about USD 31.

At the current price of USD 606.73, the corresponding PE is 23x, 26x and 20x respectively. In other words, the 21x the market currently assigns implicitly prices in "the third path." That is an optimistic assumption, not a neutral one. This is why I believe the current price offers no margin of safety.

Addendum

An easily overlooked asset: deferred revenue from advertiser prepayments makes operating cash flow naturally lead profit. This is also why its operating cash flow has long exceeded net profit. The upside is that cash arrives early; the downside is that this advantage masks real changes in earnings quality.

Let me restate the two capex measures, because they affect every number you recalculate: RMB 34.91 billion includes finance lease principal, RMB 33.94 billion is purchases of property and equipment, and the difference is RMB 970 million. Free cash flow of -RMB 2.12 billion is the result after deducting both items. If you see someone calculate -RMB 1.15 billion, they are not fabricating it — they omitted the finance lease item.

Conclusion

Financial conclusion: revenue is real, profit depends on the metric, cash is the problem.Depreciation begins in 2027, expense guidance has not been given, and the financing window is already open—three variables stacked together make any precise earnings forecast false precision. Only one thing to do: wait for 2027 expense guidance.
