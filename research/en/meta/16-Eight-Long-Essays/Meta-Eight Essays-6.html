Management: a one-man company, and a capital-allocation machine running in reverse — Meta

Chapter Six · You Are Buying His Judgment
Research date 2026/11/02  ·  Price $606.73 (-1.34%)  ·  Market cap About USD 1.58 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 21x

Core view

Buying Meta is essentially buying Zuckerberg's judgment. He holds 13.4% of the shares and 60.6% of the voting power; the board cannot stop him. Over the past decade he bet right twice (mobile, short video), bet wrong once (the metaverse), corrected course once (the 2023 layoffs), and is now making his fourth bet.

Track record: three major decisions, two wins and one loss

In 2012, going all in on mobile and moving Facebook from PC to phone worked. After acquiring Instagram in 2016, going all out on Stories and Reels to hold off TikTok worked. In 2021, renaming to Meta and spending tens of billions a year on the metaverse failed — Reality Labs has accumulated losses of over RMB 100 billion over ten years, and the VR strategy is contracting in 2026.

This track record shows two things: he dares to make big bets, and he admits when he loses. The 2023 "Year of Efficiency," which cut more than 20,000 jobs and pulled the operating margin from 25% back to 40%, was the execution version of that admission. Both are rare qualities.

What is different this time: bigger bets, more tools

In the metaverse cycle, the money came from operating cash flow, and free cash flow stayed positive even at the worst point. This cycle, free cash flow has turned negative and the company has started issuing stock: USD 37.5 billion of equity plus USD 60 billion ATM on October 26, the largest ever.

More important is the structural groundwork: non-voting Class C shares were created on October 21, effective January 19, 2027, distributed 1:1. This lets the company print unlimited stock to raise capital without diluting the founder's voting power. Shareholders' economic interest gets diluted; voting power does not.

Capital allocation: from buyback machine to financing machine

From 2019 to 2025, Meta repurchased USD 20–30 billion annually, shrinking its share count by 2%–3% a year. In the first nine months of 2026, buybacks were zero. On October 26 it reversed into share issuance, with USD 37.5 billion priced and USD 60 billion pending launch.

Going from "-2% a year" to "+6% a year" is an 8-percentage-point hit to long-term compound returns.This is the most easily overlooked point: many people only look at PE and ignore changes in share count. Management's capital allocation moves directly change the denominator of per-share value.

No Plan B

The company has no public succession plan. Bosworth is CTO, Olivan is COO and Susan Li is CFO; all three can execute, but none carries Zuckerberg's equity stake or decision-making authority.

That means two things: while he stays, strategy is stable, which is good for decade-long heavy investment; if he leaves or has a health issue, the company instantly loses its decision-making center. The market is not pricing this risk because he is only 42. But governance discounts are never priced in until they happen.

Can his word be trusted?

Look at the track record on three promises. At the end of 2022 he said 2023 would be the "year of efficiency" — delivered, with operating margin pulled back from 25% to above 40%. In 2024 he said "AI will improve ad performance" — delivered, with unit prices rising consecutively. In 2025 he said "capex will double in 2026" — delivered, from RMB 72.2 billion to above RMB 135 billion.

Three promises, three deliveries — that is why he deserves to be believed. But note: what he promises is always "input", not "return". The promises about returns — Muse cutting cost efficiency tenfold next year, Reality Labs losing RMB 15 billion next year — have never yet been delivered.

Addendum

Compensation is not the point. Zuckerberg's base salary is USD 1, and his entire return comes from the value of his shareholding, which aligns his interests with shareholders on "long-term share price." The real divergence is in time horizon: he wants position a decade out; you want returns within three years. Same objective function, different horizons.

There is another detail on the Class C shares: by Oppenheimer's estimate, after the 1:1 distribution Zuckerberg's voting power dilutes from 60.6% to around 56%, still absolute control. In other words, with this split he traded 4.6 percentage points of voting power for unlimited financing capacity—a very good trade for him personally.

Conclusion

Management verdict: execution A, capital allocation C, governance D. Buying this company means handing money to an unconstrained founder and betting on his judgment, with no buyback clause. That is not an impossible bet, but the position must be small and the price low.
