Business essence: the unit economics of attention wholesaling — Meta

Part Two · How the Money Is Made

Core view

The business runs in three steps: capture attention with products, match that attention to ads with algorithms, then price it through auctions. None of the three is asset-heavy, hence the 79.3% gross margin. What changed in 2026 is that step three got stronger (unit price +14%) while step one began to require heavy assets (compute).

Step one: the cost of capturing attention

Meta neither buys content nor rights; content is user-generated. Its costs are servers, bandwidth, content moderation and R&D. In 2026Q3, cost of revenue was RMB 13.37 billion and R&D RMB 20.05 billion, totaling RMB 33.42 billion, or 51.7% of revenue.

The model is highly efficient: 3.64 billion users worldwide at a per-user monthly cost of under USD 3. Netflix's content cost per user, by comparison, is far higher.User-generated content is the biggest cost advantage of this business, and the hardest part to replicate.

Step two: matching efficiency

The same ad slots command a higher price the more accurately they are matched. This is the most tangible use of AI inside Meta—not building chatbots, but improving the accuracy of ads ranking. The 14% increase in unit price in Q3 2026 is the price expression of this efficiency gain.

Matching efficiency has a ceiling: user data. After Apple restricted tracking in 2021, Meta recovered part of that data through its self-built Conversions API and on-platform closed loop. As long as users still browse, click and buy inside its apps, it does not need external data. This is the economics of the walled garden.

Step 3: Auction pricing

Ad slots are not sold at a fixed price; they are auctioned. The system ranks ads by "expected conversion value," and advertisers bid for each impression. Rising prices therefore reflect intensifying competition among advertisers, not unilateral price hikes by Meta.

The auction model also confers cyclical resilience. In a strong economy, advertisers compete for slots and prices rise automatically; in a weak one, prices fall automatically but volume holds. That automatic adjustment makes Meta's revenue less volatile than that of media companies with fixed pricing.

What is the ceiling for this business?

Two ceilings. The first is advertising budgets as a share of GDP: global ad spend runs at roughly 1% of GDP, a ratio that has barely changed in decades, so long-term ad revenue growth tracks nominal GDP plus share shifts.

The second is the total pool of attention—a person only has 24 hours in a day. Meta has already captured a large chunk of it, and daily active user growth slowing to 3% is the evidence. So its long-term growth will step down from 26% to around 10%; this is not a competitiveness problem, it is a math problem. Muse matters because it opens a third ceiling: shifting from "selling attention" to "selling task completion."

Why advertising, not subscriptions

The beauty of advertising is that it monetises every user, whether or not they want to pay. An Indian user who never pays and an American user paying USD 20 a month both carry advertising value for Meta, just at different unit prices.

Subscriptions work the opposite way: they charge only the small slice of people willing to pay. Muse's USD 20 monthly fee is essentially an attempt to charge the 5% of heavy users a second time, on top of the advertising model. It does not replace advertising; it adds a layer to it. Understand this, and you will not overvalue Muse as a "second growth curve."

Addendum

Will advertisers leave? Watch two metrics: advertiser count and spend per advertiser (neither disclosed). Unit price is the observable proxy—if advertisers were leaving, it would fall first. In Q3 2026 unit price rose 14%, which tells us not only that they are staying but that they are paying more. This is the simplest and most effective demand validation.

Conclusion

Business essence: an efficiently run attention-wholesaling machine with a visible ceiling, now attempting to switch lanes. Advertising unit economics remain excellent (gross margin 79.3%, unit price +14%), but the growth ceiling is closing in. Muse is the only attempt that could open a new one, and it has yet to prove itself.
