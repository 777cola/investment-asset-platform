Decision framework: at what price to buy, under what conditions to walk away — Meta

Part Eight · Turning Judgments into Executable Rules

Core view

The previous seven pieces covered "how to think"; this one covers "what to do." Conclusion: do not buy at the current price; build a 3% position below USD 500; fundamental triggers take priority over price triggers; abandon the thesis if any of the three hard conditions is triggered.

I. Rating and Target Price

Rating: Watch, wait for a pullback. Neutral target USD 620 (2027 EPS USD 28 × 22x), bear case 410, bull case 860. After accounting for 6% dilution from the USD 95 billion share issuance, neutral is about USD 580.

Current price USD 606.73, corresponding to 21x TTM, a market cap of USD 1.575 trillion, and -22% from the 52-week high. This price reflects neither the dilution nor the 2027 depreciation, so it is not "cheap" — it is "not yet fully priced in."

II. Two triggers for buying

Price trigger: share price ≤USD 500, corresponding to 19x diluted, -18% in the bear case and +72% in the bull case, so the risk-reward turns positive. Fundamental trigger: 2027 expense growth guidance below revenue growth, overturning the fifth gate, in which case buying at a somewhat higher price is acceptable.

Act when either condition is met; no need to wait for both.Because these two conditions correspond to "price safety" and "fundamental safety" respectively, one is enough.

III. The three hard conditions we dropped

First, ad growth has fallen below 10% for two consecutive quarters (pricing power has failed). Second, the 2027 capex guidance exceeds USD 170 billion (spending has no boundary). Third, depreciation has pushed EPS revisions down by more than 10% for three consecutive quarters (growth cannot absorb the spending).

These three points are written down to avoid making excuses when the time comes. People grow attached to companies they have followed for a long time; rules exist to counter that attachment.

IV. Execution checklist

Ask five questions before buying: is ad pricing still rising? Has capex guidance been cut? Did they give a 2027 expense number? Does Muse have separately disclosed revenue? Has buyback resumed? At least three "yes" answers before acting.

After buying, do three things: reconcile the assumption sheet once a quarter, cap any single position at 5%, halve it above 750. The main cause of investment failure is not misjudging the company, but buying too much of a right company at the wrong price. Rules beat judgment.

A counterexample: what a 2022 buy point looked like

In November 2022, Meta's stock fell to USD 88, a market cap of USD 230 billion, about 10x 2023 EPS. The conditions then: ad growth turned negative, metaverse losses widened, sentiment was extremely bearish. But free cash flow was still positive, buybacks continued, and the balance sheet held ample cash.

Over the next two and a half years, the stock rose more than sevenfold. That was a real buying point: bad news exhausted, cash flow intact, price extremely cheap. Compare with now: bad news just beginning, cash flow already broken, price merely "not expensive." The two are far apart.

Addendum

Last point: when to sell. If bought at USD 500, cut the position in half at USD 750 (27x), and exit fully at USD 860 (the optimistic upper bound); if fundamentals trigger the abandonment conditions, sell regardless of price. Buy on price, sell on rules — that is how you avoid mistakes when sentiment peaks.

Finally, the three most common mistakes. First, treating a "good company" as a "good investment" and ignoring price. Second, treating "down 22%" as "cheap" and ignoring that earnings are also being revised down. Third, treating "AI is the future" as a reason to buy and ignoring how far away that future is. What the three mistakes share: narrative replacing arithmetic.

Conclusion

Framework conclusion: good company, bad timing, no discount on price — so wait. What we wait for is the 2027 expense guidance and a USD 500 price, not for news, a rebound, or others to buy first. Set the rules, and execution needs no courage.
