Risk list: seven risks, two already materialized — Meta

Core view

Frame risks as "how much you lose, and when you will know it," not as "AI carries uncertainty." Of the seven, unclear returns on capex and equity dilution have already materialized, depreciation-driven margin compression is certain to hit, and the advertising cycle, regulation, gateway substitution and competition are queued up.

Two already delivered

First, capex returns are unclear. For 2027, the only guidance on spending is a "significant increase" with no number attached — that is missing information, not risk appetite. Second, equity dilution: USD 37.5 billion is already priced at 2.3% dilution, with a USD 60 billion ATM held in reserve for a further 3.9%, or 6% combined.

The damage from these two is that they move the numerator and the denominator at once: EPS has to come down (dilution), costs have to go up (depreciation), and whether revenue can keep pace is unknown.

What is certain to happen: depreciation

RMB 140 billion of capex amortised over four to six years implies RMB 23 to 28 billion of depreciation a year. From 2027Q1, quarterly operating margins come under visible pressure. This is not a risk; it is a schedule.

The 21x the market currently assigns is based on 2026 earnings, before this depreciation is deducted. On 2027 earnings after full depreciation, the P/E jumps to 24 to 26x. That is the source of the illusion that the stock is "cheap."

What could happen: advertising cycle and regulation

Advertising is a derivative of GDP, and 97.6% of revenue depends on it. Small and mid-sized advertisers are the most rate- and consumption-sensitive; once US consumption weakens, they are the first to disappear. Historically, Meta's revenue growth has fallen to single digits in recessions, and dropped to -1% in 2022.

Regulation is a recurring cost: the teen settlement of RMB 18.59 billion (paid over ten years), the EU antitrust fine of EUR 798 million, and more to come. The real impact is not the fines but the product rules—default time limits for teens will change how the next generation of users engages, an effect that will only become visible five years from now.

What may not happen: gateway substitution and competition

If agents become the primary entry point, Meta is demoted from tax collector to content source. That risk is three to five years out; I put the probability at 25%. Google's threat is more concrete: it has all four pieces—models, chips, cloud, distribution—and its exposure to the same capex risk is only half of Meta's.

The risk checklist is not for scaring yourself; it is for sizing positions. Two already materialized plus one certain to happen map to "no position." The four that have not happened map to "keep on the watchlist." If the first three ever ease, we can revisit the position.

If you could hedge only one risk

Capex is the line of defence. Every other risk can wait; only this one keeps draining cash: RMB 135–145 billion in 2026, more in 2027, and flexibility only in 2028. The three-year cumulative total could exceed USD 450 billion.

What makes this risk frightening is that it is an "active choice" rather than a "passive encounter." The advertising cycle will pass, regulation will adapt, competition will balance out, but capex is management's own decision every year. As long as they decide to keep raising it, free cash flow will not return.

Addendum

The tail risk is two things happening at once: ad growth slowing to 10% while capex is raised again in 2027. EPS would then take a double hit — RMB 30 billion less on the revenue side, RMB 25 billion more in depreciation on the cost side — and profit could be halved. The probability is not high (I put it at 15%), but if it happens, the stock is not -30%, it is -50%.

The regulatory timetable also needs to be worked out: the RMB 18.59 billion settlement is paid over ten years, about RMB 1.86 billion a year, equivalent to RMB 460 million of expense per quarter. It will not land all at once on some day; it deducts like a mortgage payment every month. This kind of "chronic cost" is the easiest for analysts to overlook, because it does not constitute a single-quarter surprise.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: near-term (2027) pressure is certain; the medium term (2028–2029) hinges on whether capex peaks; the long term (2030+) hinges on who owns the entry point.Of the three horizons, only the near term is certain bad news. Hence you cannot buy now — but nor can you take it off your watchlist.
