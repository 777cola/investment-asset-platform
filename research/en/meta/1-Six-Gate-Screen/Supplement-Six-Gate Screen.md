## The first three hurdles: understandable, quantifiable, defensible

The advertising business comes down to one sentence: users return on their own every day, the platform sells that attention by the thousand impressions, and advertisers bid up the price on conversion performance. Q3 2026 advertising revenue was USD 63.06 billion, 97.6% of total revenue, with impressions up 10% YoY and average price per ad up 14%. Volume and price rising together signals advertisers bidding for slots, not the platform cutting prices to buy volume. That combination has appeared only two or three times in the past five years; Q2 2026 saw impressions +14% and average price +12%. Two consecutive quarters of simultaneous volume and price gains is not seasonality.

Gross margin 79.3%, net margin 15.3%, operating cash flow USD 32.79 billion — put those three together and you struggle to find ten companies worldwide. The moat scores 8, not 10, with the deduction for brand: Facebook's brand strength among younger users is slowly aging and Instagram still holds up, but the shift in user mix is a ten-year risk.

## Gate 4: Capability is not the constraint; checks and balances are

Zuckerberg held 13.4% as of October 1 and controls 60.6% of voting power through Class B shares carrying ten votes each. On October 21 the board approved the creation of non-voting Class C common stock, effective January 19, 2027 and distributed 1:1 — in substance a two-for-one split. The split changes no one's voting rights, but it institutionalises the principle that shares can be printed without asking shareholders.

Five days later, on October 26, the company put it to use: USD 25 billion of Class A common stock (priced at USD 625) plus USD 12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred stock (6.5% coupon), for a total of USD 37.5 billion, alongside a Class C ATM program of up to USD 60 billion launching as early as Q1 2027. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom intend to subscribe up to USD 5 billion in aggregate, non-binding. Management says there are "no specific financing plans at present," but the tools and the window are in place.

This is not fraud; it is structure. The founder is betting the advertising cash flow on "personal superintelligence," and shareholders can veto neither the spending nor the financing — only accept the outcome. A score of 5, docked for checks and balances, not capability.

## Gate 5: Margin-of-safety math, recalculated for the share issuance

The working paper's neutral case assumes 2027 EPS of USD 28 at a 22x P/E, implying USD 620 — flat with the current price. The calculation has one flaw: it uses the share count before the equity issuance.

USD 37.5 billion issued at USD 625 adds roughly 60 million shares, or 2.3% of the 2.596 billion base; if the full USD 60 billion ATM is drawn at an average price of USD 600, that adds about another 100 million shares, or 3.9%. Total dilution of 6% takes 2027 EPS from USD 28 to USD 26.4; at the same 22x, fair value is USD 580 — 4% below the current price. In other words, after October 26, the expected return on buying at the current price goes from "zero" to "negative 4%."

The money raised will of course become compute capacity, and compute capacity will become revenue, so value may not be diluted in the long run. But that is a 2028 story. 2027 EPS is a certain subtraction; revenue is a probabilistic addition — which is why there is no margin of safety.

## Stage six: discipline, and two verifiable premises

The sixth gate asks whether the buy thesis can be written as a single falsifiable sentence. Right now it cannot. "Muse will monetise within three years" is unverifiable, and "AI spending will pay off" even more so. Only one claim can be tested: in 2027, total expense growth will be lower than revenue growth.

2026 expense guidance is USD 175–179 billion and capex guidance USD 135–145 billion, of which 83.06 billion was already spent in the first three quarters. For 2027, management said only "significant increase", without a number. Until that number is disclosed, every earnings forecast is a guess.

The discipline gate scores 3 — not because money will necessarily be lost, but because it does not permit a large bet when information is insufficient. Waiting for a number risks missing a 10% rally; not waiting risks a 30% drawdown.

## Beyond the six gates, two easily overlooked details

First, fewer people, more money. Headcount stood at 66,775 at end-September, down about 12,000 (12,000) YoY, while R&D expense rose 32% and capex rose 85.6% over the same period. Meta has swapped labour cost for compute cost — the textbook case of capital substituting for labour. The upside is a more variable cost structure; the downside is that once depreciation starts, it does not stop when revenue slows.

Second, future commitments are already heavy. The company disclosed about USD 825 billion in signed lease and purchase commitments, most tied to AI compute and energy infrastructure. That money is not on the balance sheet, but it must be funded out of cash flow in 2027–2030. Looking at Meta means looking not just at capex but at the total curve of "capex + off-balance-sheet commitments" — it sets the ceiling on free cash flow for the next three years.
