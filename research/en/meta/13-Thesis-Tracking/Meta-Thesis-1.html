Paper tracking: write down where you could be wrong before you buy

You can write a thesis without owning the stock — writing it is how you learn what to wait for. Of the five core assumptions, advertising pricing power holds; a capex peak is unverified (and still deteriorating); expense growth is unverified; Muse is unverified; and no shareholder dilution has already been falsified. One falsified, three blank — that is why you cannot buy now.

The only hypothesis that holds—and the only one worth anything

Advertising pricing power. Two consecutive quarters of simultaneous volume and price growth (Q2 impressions +14%, unit price +12%; Q3 +10%, +14%) — a rare combination in advertising. It proves AI recommendation is genuinely lifting conversion rates, not just raising parameters at launch events.

The value of this thesis: as long as unit price can still rise more than 10%, the revenue side will hold up, and all risk concentrates on the cost side. The investment call thus becomes simple — no need to forecast user growth, only to watch expenses.

Writing research without holding the stock is meant to guard against hindsight bias

There is a human instinct at work here: when a call works, it was sharp judgment; when it fails, the market was irrational. Writing a paper pins "what I thought at the time" to the page, so that when the books are reconciled three months later there is no room to argue.

This paper deliberately uses specific numbers and dates: which year, which metric, what threshold. "Cost growth falls below revenue growth in 2027" can be proven true or false; "long-term bullish on AI" cannot. In investment research, only judgments that can be written as true-or-false sentences have value.

The one that was disproven matters more than expected

"Shareholders are not diluted" was a cornerstone of the buy thesis: Meta has repurchased USD 20–30 billion a year for the past seven years, shrinking its share count 2%–3% annually — a hidden return for long-term holders. In the first nine months of 2026 buybacks were zero; on October 26 the company reverse-issued USD 37.5 billion and reserved USD 60 billion under an ATM program.

From "-2% a year" to "+6% a year" — that shift is worth 8 percentage points of long-term compounding. Many investors look only at the P/E and ignore share-count changes, systematically overstating returns. That assumption has now been falsified, so the old valuation model must be rebuilt.

Falsification conditions: when to abandon the thesis entirely

Three hard stop conditions — any one triggered removes it from the watchlist: ① advertising growth below 10% for two consecutive quarters (pricing power has failed); ② 2027 capex guidance above USD 170 billion (spending has no boundary); ③ depreciation drives EPS cuts of more than 10% for three consecutive quarters (growth cannot absorb the spending).

Conversely, three conditions would accelerate purchases: ① 2027 expense growth guidance below revenue growth; ② free cash flow turns positive; ③ the share price falls below USD 500. The conditions are fixed in advance to avoid deciding on "gut feel" when the time comes.

Watch calendar for next steps

December 2026: Muse launches on smart glasses; watch whether hardware becomes a new distribution channel. January 19, 2027: Class C shares take effect; watch first-day trading volume and whether the ATM is activated. Late January 2027: Q4 earnings — the most important call of the year, as it must provide 2027 expense guidance. April 2027: Q1 earnings; watch whether depreciation growth starts to outpace revenue growth.

Four dates: the first two concern structure, the last two the numbers. Structure determines the story; numbers determine the price.

At each quarterly reconciliation, ask only three questions

First, is ad unit price still rising? That drives revenue; two consecutive quarters below 10% and the story is over. Second, is capex still climbing? That drives cash flow; 2027 guidance above RMB 170 billion and there is no need to wait. Third, does Muse disclose revenue separately? That determines whether the option can convert into cash flow.

The answers combine into only four outcomes: three point to "keep watching", one to "give up". Only one combination truly points to "buy": costs peak + unit price still rising. We are still far from that now.

Conclusion

Thesis status: one holds, three unverified, one falsified. On our discipline, that rules out building a position. One core variable matters: 2027 expense guidance. Until it lands, every reason to buy is narrative; once it lands, either the fifth gate is overturned or the thesis is abandoned outright.
