Earnings deep dive: advertising generates revenue, but the cash vanishes

Read this quarter's earnings in two halves. The first is advertising: USD 63.06 billion, +26%, with both volume and pricing up — the strongest single quarter in history. The second is cash: capex of USD 34.91 billion and free cash flow of -USD 2.12 billion, the first such occurrence in fourteen years as a public company. Both halves are real, but only the second determined the stock price.

Layer one: revenue structure — 97.6% depends on one thing

Advertising revenue reached RMB 63.06 billion, 97.6% of the total and a single-quarter record. Within that, impressions rose 10% and price per ad 14%. The combination matters: advertisers are paying up to secure slots. Volume up with price down would signal a platform discounting to buy growth; volume and price rising together means both supply (user time) and demand (ad budgets) are tilting toward Meta.

Other revenue was RMB 1.097 billion, +59%, on too small a base to signal much. Reality Labs contributed RMB 443 million, -6%, as Quest headset sales declined, partly offset by growth in AI glasses. Beyond advertising, Meta currently has no second pillar.

Layer two: gross margin fell 2.7 points — that is the signal

Operating cost rose 45.2% from RMB 9.21 billion to RMB 13.37 billion, and gross margin fell from 82.0% to 79.3%. Cost grew 19 percentage points faster than revenue, driven by compute, depreciation and content moderation.

R&D expense reached RMB 20.05 billion, +32.4%, also outpacing revenue. "AI efficiency gains" are visible on the revenue side (unit price +14%) but not yet on the cost side. That pattern is common to all early-stage heavy-capex investment: costs come first, returns follow, with a two-to-three-year gap in between.

Tier 3: one-off items and their pitfalls

In August the company settled with the attorneys general of 52 states for up to USD 18.59 billion, payable over ten years. USD 9.89 billion was accrued this quarter and booked to management expenses, lifting that line from USD 3.51 billion to USD 14.86 billion. Operating profit was compressed from USD 22.88 billion to USD 12.99 billion and the margin fell from 35% to 20%.

Net profit of USD 9.86 billion, +264%, stems from a low base: 2025Q3 included a USD 18.95 billion one-off tax charge that left net profit at just USD 2.71 billion. The two one-off items offset each other; read this table on both a GAAP and an adjusted basis, or you will reach completely opposite conclusions.

Breaking down the app family: one business funds the entire company

The family of apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger) generated USD 64.158 billion of revenue in the third quarter, +26.9% and almost equal to total company revenue. Reality Labs revenue was USD 443 million, or 0.7%, with an operating loss of USD 4.13 billion, slightly narrower YoY (a loss of USD 4.43 billion in the same period last year).

Another cut makes it clearer: of the operating profit the family of apps earns each quarter, about USD 4.1 billion goes to plugging Reality Labs' hole and a further USD 34.9 billion goes to buying compute. The former is spending on something already proven a failure but continued anyway; the latter is spending on something not yet proven but on which the company must bet. Together, the two exceed half of the family of apps' operating profit for the quarter.

Layer 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — the only genuine red flag

Operating cash flow of RMB 32.79 billion, +9.3% — cash generation is fine. Purchases of property and equipment of RMB 33.94 billion, +80.3%, plus RMB 970 million of finance-lease principal, left free cash flow at -RMB 2.12 billion. This is not an operating problem — the company is deliberately burying money in the ground: GPUs and data centers go in, depreciation comes out.

The other side of the balance sheet: RMB 82 billion of cash and securities and RMB 97 billion of operating cash flow through the first three quarters, against USD 825 billion of signed lease and purchase commitments. On October 26 it raised a further USD 37.5 billion of equity plus a USD 60 billion ATM. "Cash-rich" and "must raise" hold true at the same time — the classic state of a heavy-asset transition.

Four-quarter trend: growth is slowing but remains the fastest in the industry

Revenue growth runs 24%→33%→28%→26% from 2025Q4 to 2026Q3, with advertising growth of 24%→27%→26%.Growth is stepping down modestly but holds above 25% — the only one of the three major ad platforms in the mid-teens or higher. Over the same period, Google advertising grew 10.6% and Amazon 10.9%.

Q4 guidance of USD 73–76 billion implies roughly +24% YoY at the midpoint, consistent with a "modest slowdown" slope. The real risk is not revenue but expenses: 2026 expense guidance is 175–179 billion, while for 2027 the company offers only the words "increase significantly."

Cost structure: the R&D growth delivered by 12,000 people

Headcount stood at 66,775 at end-September, down roughly 12,000 YoY, while R&D expense over the same period reached RMB 20.05 billion, up 32.4% YoY. Headcount fell by three-tenths as R&D expense rose by three-tenths — the incremental spend went entirely into compute, model training and the restructuring of hardware teams.

This is the textbook case of capital substituting for labour. The upside is a higher variable-cost share: severance is a one-off outlay, after which the company saves tens of billions of dollars in annual labour cost; the downside is rigid depreciation: servers, once purchased, must be depreciated over five to six years and cannot be stopped even if revenue slows. Shifting the cost structure from people to machines trades flexibility for rigidity.

How did the 14% ASP increase come about?

Ad unit prices are set by auction, not by pricing. A 14% increase in unit price can only come from two sources: the same impression delivering higher conversion (more accurate AI recommendations), or advertisers bidding more aggressively for scarce supply. This quarter, both held at once.

On the supply side, daily active users were 3.64 billion, +3%, a moderate pace, indicating ad inventory did not expand much and the price increase came mainly from efficiency. On the demand side, e-commerce and performance ads lowered the barrier to entry with the help of AI creative tools, and the number of small and mid-sized advertisers is rising.The most direct evidence that "AI has already monetized" — not new business revenue, but price increases in the old business.

Q4 guidance and full-year estimates

Q4 revenue guidance of USD 73–76 billion (midpoint USD 74.5 billion) implies roughly +24% YoY, slightly above consensus. On that basis, full-year 2026 revenue is about USD 256.2 billion (USD 181.71 billion for the first three quarters plus USD 74.5 billion), up +27.5% YoY.

On costs: full-year USD 175–179 billion, with capex of USD 135–145 billion. Combined, the two total roughly USD 310–324 billion — already above full-year revenue. Full-year 2026 free cash flow is therefore likely to be negative or close to zero; Q3 was already -USD 2.12 billion, and Q4 capex will only run higher.

Where does it diverge from sell-side expectations?

Revenue beat the RMB 1.2 billion consensus (646 vs 634). Operating profit fell well short on settlement costs, but excluding those it was RMB 22.88 billion at a 35% margin, broadly in line. The initial reaction on report day was therefore a gain (up as much as 6% after hours), which reversed into a 7% drop the next day—the market first treated the settlement as one-off, then grasped that cash flow is the real problem.

At least 13 investment banks raised target prices post-results (Bernstein, Morgan Stanley and others), citing advertising resilience and Muse's potential.Sell-side optimism alongside share-price weakness usually signals that the disagreement is over "timing", not "direction".

Three caveats on the metrics

① Capex is reported on two bases — RMB 34.91 billion including finance-lease principal and RMB 33.94 billion of purchases of property and equipment. The RMB 970 million gap matters: mixing the two definitions turns free cash flow negative at RMB 1.15 billion. ② 2025Q3 includes a one-off tax charge of RMB 18.95 billion, so any YoY comparison built on that quarter will be distorted. ③ Diluted EPS of USD 3.83 is GAAP; adjusted EPS is USD 7.40, nearly double, so any debate over whether the stock is expensive must specify which measure it uses.

Conclusion

Earnings verdict: revenue 9 points, costs 5 points, cash flow 3 points. Advertising pricing power is still strengthening — the strongest part of this report; capex and depreciation are eroding margin flexibility. Three things to watch: 2027 expense guidance, the point at which depreciation growth outpaces revenue growth, and whether Reality Labs losses can narrow to about USD 15 billion.
