Earnings deep dive: advertising makes money, but the money disappears

This quarter's earnings must be read in two halves. The first half is advertising: USD 63.06 billion, +26%, with both volume and pricing up — the strongest single quarter in history. The second half is cash: capex of USD 34.91 billion and free cash flow of -USD 2.12 billion, the first such occurrence in fourteen years as a public company. Both halves are real, but only the second half determined the stock price.

Layer one: revenue structure — 97.6% depends on one thing

Advertising RMB 63.06 billion, 97.6% of total, a single-quarter record. Broken down: impressions +10%, price per ad +14%. What this combination means: advertisers are paying up to secure slots. Volume up with price down would mean the platform is discounting to buy growth; volume and price rising together means supply (user time) and demand (ad budgets) are both tilting toward Meta.

Other revenue RMB 1.097 billion, +59%, too small a base to signal much. Reality Labs RMB 443 million, -6%, with Quest headset sales declining, partly offset by growth in AI glasses. Beyond advertising, Meta currently has no second pillar.

Layer two: gross margin fell 2.7 points — that is the signal

Operating cost rose from RMB 9.21 billion to RMB 13.37 billion, +45.2%, and gross margin fell from 82.0% to 79.3%. Cost grew 19 percentage points faster than revenue, driven by compute, depreciation and content moderation.

R&D expense was RMB 20.05 billion, +32.4%, also outpacing revenue. "AI efficiency gains" are visible on the revenue side (unit price +14%) but not yet on the cost side. This is common to all early-stage heavy-capex investment: costs come first, returns follow, with a two-to-three-year gap in between.

Tier 3: one-off items and their pitfalls

In August, a settlement with the attorneys general of 52 states, totaling up to USD 18.59 billion, payable over ten years, with USD 9.89 billion accrued this quarter, booked to management expenses, pushing that line from USD 3.51 billion to USD 14.86 billion. Operating profit was therefore compressed from USD 22.88 billion to USD 12.99 billion, and the margin fell from 35% to 20%.

Net profit of USD 9.86 billion, +264%, stems from a low base: 2025Q3 included a USD 18.95 billion one-off tax charge, leaving net profit at just USD 2.71 billion. The two one-off items offset each other; read this table on both a GAAP and an adjusted basis, or you will reach completely opposite conclusions.

Breaking down the app family: one business funds the entire company

The family of apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger) generated revenue of USD 64.158 billion in the third quarter, +26.9%, almost equal to the company's total revenue. Reality Labs revenue was USD 443 million, or 0.7%, with an operating loss of USD 4.13 billion, slightly narrower year on year (a loss of USD 4.43 billion in the same period last year).

Another way of calculating makes it clearer: of the operating profit the family of apps earns each quarter, about USD 4.1 billion goes to plug Reality Labs' hole, and a further USD 34.9 billion goes to buying compute. The former is spending on something already proven a failure but continued anyway; the latter is spending on something not yet proven but on which the company must bet. Together, the two exceed half of the family of apps' operating profit for the quarter.

Layer 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — the only genuine red flag

Operating cash flow RMB 32.79 billion, +9.3% — cash generation is fine. Purchases of property and equipment RMB 33.94 billion, +80.3%, plus RMB 970 million of finance-lease principal, leaving free cash flow at -RMB 2.12 billion. This is not an operating problem — the company is deliberately burying money in the ground: what goes in is GPUs and data centers, what grows out is depreciation.

The other side of the balance sheet: cash and securities RMB 82 billion, operating cash flow of RMB 97 billion through the first three quarters, but USD 825 billion of signed lease and purchase commitments. On October 26 it raised a further USD 37.5 billion of equity plus a USD 60 billion ATM. "Cash-rich" and "must raise" hold true at the same time — the classic state of a heavy-asset transition.

Four-quarter trend: growth is slowing but remains the fastest in the industry

From 2025Q4 to 2026Q3, revenue growth runs 24%→33%→28%→26%, and advertising growth 24%→27%→26%.Growth is stepping down modestly but remains above 25%, the only one of the three major ad platforms in the mid-teens or higher. Over the same period, Google advertising grew 10.6% and Amazon 10.9%.

Q4 guidance is USD 73–76 billion, up about +24% year on year at the midpoint, consistent with a "modest slowdown" slope. The real risk is not revenue but expenses: 2026 expense guidance is 175–179 billion, while 2027 offers only the words "increase significantly."

Cost structure: the R&D growth delivered by 12,000 people

Headcount stood at 66,775 at the end of September, down about 12,000 (1.2万) year on year; R&D expense over the same period was RMB 20.05 billion, up 32.4% year on year. Headcount fell by three-tenths while R&D expense rose by three-tenths — the extra money went entirely into compute, model training and the restructuring of hardware teams.

This is the textbook move of capital substituting for labor. The upside is more variable costs: layoffs are a one-time outlay, after which the company saves tens of billions of dollars in annual labor cost; the downside is rigid depreciation: servers, once bought, must be depreciated over five to six years and cannot be stopped even if revenue slows. Shifting the cost structure from people to machines trades flexibility for rigidity.

How did the 14% ASP increase come about?

Ad unit prices are set by auction, not by pricing. For the unit price to rise 14%, there are only two paths: the same impression delivers higher conversion (more accurate AI recommendations), or advertisers compete more intensely (scarce supply). This quarter, both held at once.

On the supply side, daily active users were 3.64 billion, +3%, a moderate pace, indicating ad inventory did not expand much and the price increase came mainly from efficiency. On the demand side, e-commerce and performance ads lowered the barrier to entry with the help of AI creative tools, and the number of small and mid-sized advertisers is rising.This is the most direct evidence that "AI has already monetized" — not new business revenue, but price increases in the old business.

Q4 guidance and full-year estimates

Q4 revenue guidance of USD 73–76 billion, midpoint USD 74.5 billion, up about +24% YoY, slightly above market expectations. On that basis, full-year 2026 revenue is roughly USD 256.2 billion (first three quarters USD 181.71 billion + USD 74.5 billion), up +27.5% YoY.

Cost side: full-year USD 175–179 billion, capex USD 135–145 billion. The two combined total roughly USD 310–324 billion, already exceeding full-year revenue. That means full-year 2026 free cash flow is likely negative or close to zero — Q3 was already -USD 2.12 billion, and Q4 capex will only run higher.

Where does it diverge from sell-side expectations?

Revenue came in above the consensus of RMB 1.2 billion (646 vs 634). Operating profit fell well below expectations on settlement costs, but excluding those it was RMB 22.88 billion at a 35% margin, broadly in line. So the first reaction on report day was a gain (up as much as 6% after hours), which turned into a 7% drop the next day—the market first treated the settlement as one-off, then grasped that cash flow is the real problem.

At least 13 investment banks raised target prices after the results (Bernstein, Morgan Stanley and others), citing advertising resilience and Muse's potential.Sell-side optimism and share-price weakness coexisting usually means the disagreement is over "timing", not "direction".

Three caveats on the metrics

① Capex has two figures: RMB 34.91 billion (including finance-lease principal) and RMB 33.94 billion (purchases of property and equipment); the RMB 970 million gap means mixing the two turns free cash flow into negative RMB 1.15 billion. ② 2025Q3 carries a one-off tax charge of RMB 18.95 billion, and any year-on-year figure based on it will look distorted. ③ Diluted EPS of USD 3.83 is GAAP; adjusted EPS is USD 7.40 — nearly double — so any "expensive or not" discussion must state which one it uses.

Conclusion

Earnings verdict: revenue 9 points, costs 5 points, cash flow 3 points. Advertising pricing power is still strengthening — the hardest part of this report; capex and depreciation are eating into margin flexibility. Three things to watch: the 2027 expense guidance, when depreciation growth outpaces revenue growth, and whether Reality Labs losses can narrow to about USD 15 billion.
