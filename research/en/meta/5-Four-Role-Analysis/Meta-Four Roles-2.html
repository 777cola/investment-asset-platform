Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Meta

The income statement looks fine, but the cash flow statement is flashing a warning for the first time

Core view

Of the four statements, only one is problematic: cash flow. Excluding the litigation settlement, the income statement shows operating profit of RMB 22.88 billion, up 11% YoY; the balance sheet holds RMB 82 billion in cash; the cash flow statement shows free cash flow of -RMB 2.12 billion. That single statement caps the valuation.

Income statement: the good part is real

Revenue RMB 64.60 billion, +26.1%, with advertising at RMB 63.06 billion, +26.0%. Operating profit fell 37% to RMB 12.99 billion, but RMB 9.89 billion of that reflects the August teen-litigation settlement with the 52 state attorneys general (up to RMB 18.59 billion in total, payable over ten years). Excluding the charge, operating profit was RMB 22.88 billion, +11%, a 35% margin and in line with the year-earlier level.

Two cost lines stand out: R&D RMB 20.05 billion, +32%, and cost of revenue RMB 13.37 billion, +45%. Costs grew 19 percentage points faster than revenue — the first evidence that depreciation and compute are starting to bite.

Cash flow statement: negative for the first time

Operating cash flow came in at RMB 32.79 billion — not bad. Purchases of property and equipment were RMB 33.94 billion, plus RMB 970 million of finance-lease principal, leaving free cash flow at negative RMB 2.12 billion. That is the first negative single quarter since the 2012 listing; the prior quarter had already slipped to an "extremely low positive."

Buffett would press one question: if capex stays at the RMB 140 billion level for three years, that is USD 420 billion cumulative — where does the money come from? The answer came on October 26: USD 37.5 billion of equity + USD 60 billion ATM + investment-grade debt + operating cash flow. That is a complete financing package — and a signal that management itself knows cash flow is insufficient.

Balance sheet: room remains, but it is thinning

Cash and marketable securities of USD 82 billion, together with USD 97 billion of operating cash flow for the first three quarters, look ample. Against that sit USD 825 billion of lease and purchase commitments, plus the newly issued USD 12.5 billion of 6.5% coupon mandatory convertible preferred stock—a company with negative cash flow starting to pay a 6.5% preferred dividend is a credit-market signal, not a financial trick.

Meta's CDS spread hit a high after the earnings report, and the market is already repricing its credit. Buffett's first question on a company like this is not the P/E but "how much longer can it feed itself." At the current burn rate, the answer is roughly two years—after that it will have to rely on financing.

The depreciation bill: an extra RMB 20-30 billion a year in each of the next three years

USD 140 billion of capex, depreciated on a blended schedule of four to six years for servers and fifteen to twenty years for data centers, adds roughly USD 23–28 billion of depreciation a year. That charge will not appear in the 2026 cash flow statement, but it will press on the income statement from 2027.

To cover it, working back from a 40% operating margin, the company needs USD 60–70 billion of additional revenue a year—25% on top of the 2026 base of USD 256 billion.Advertising grew 26% this year, just enough; if it falls to 15% next year, it will not be enough.This is the "race between depreciation and growth," and the line to watch most closely in 2027.

Conclusion

Financial verdict: the core business still generates cash; capital allocation spent it all. Three-part score: income statement 7.0, balance sheet 7.0, cash flow statement 3.0. Overall, this is not a name that can be valued on a DCF; only "normalised earnings × a reasonable multiple" works, and even that normalization has to wait for capex to peak.
