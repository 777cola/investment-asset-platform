Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — Meta

The zero-sum game in digital advertising, and an arms race over entry points

Core view

Global digital advertising has entered a zero-sum competition: in 2026Q3, Google advertising grew +10.6%, Amazon +10.9%, and Meta +25.9%. Meta is the only player still gaining share, relying on AI recommendations to lift conversion rates at every placement. But the cost of this arms race is shifting from advertisers to shareholders.

Industry structure: three players capture most of the incremental growth

Based on single-quarter advertising revenue for 2026Q3, Meta posted USD 63.06 billion, Google USD 82.01 billion and Amazon USD 19.64 billion, for a combined USD 164.7 billion, or roughly USD 658.8 billion annualized. At that scale, industry growth can only come from two places: taking budget from others, or making ad inventory more valuable. Meta did both this quarter.

Munger would note one anomaly: Google advertising rose just 10.6%, while Meta rose 25.9%. Both use AI recommendation, so why the gap? The answer is inventory position — search advertising is intent-driven, and AI summaries directly eat the clicks; feed advertising is interest-driven, and the stronger the AI, the more accurate the recommendation and the more valuable the inventory. Technological change affects different advertising formats in opposite ways.

Cycle position: ad budgets are most sensitive to macro

Advertising is a derivative of GDP. Companies always cut budgets in the same order: brand advertising first, then performance advertising, and headcount last. 97.6% of Meta's revenue comes from advertising, with a high share from small and medium-sized merchants—these customers are the most sensitive to interest rates and cash flow, and they are the first to disappear when the economy weakens.

The 2026 macro environment has not reached that point yet: US consumption is still expanding, and AI-related capital expenditure has supported corporate confidence. But Google's 13% plunge after its earnings at the end of October and the combined free cash flow of the four major cloud providers of USD -27.3 billion in the third quarter show that the market is already pricing in the idea that "AI investment does not translate into profit." The industry cycle is not on the advertising side; it is on the capital side.

Competitive landscape: the real rivals are at the model layer

TikTok fought for user time, Google fought for ad budgets—Meta has fought both battles for a decade, with mixed results. The new battleground is the agent entry point: OpenAI released Dots on September 30, Google released Gemini agents on October 8, Anthropic launched Claude Duet on October 22, and Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance are all following suit.

The decisive factor this round is not model capability, but distribution channels + payment loops + compute costs. Meta has distribution across 3.6 billion users, payments via Stripe, and a compute budget of RMB 14 billion—it has all three, and that is the confidence behind its bet. Munger would caution: having all three does not mean all three are enough.

Where pricing power comes from: auction mechanism + AI creativity

Meta's advertising runs on an auction: dozens of advertisers bid on every impression, and the system ranks them by "expected conversion value." AI does two things in this chain: it matches the right ads to the right people (lifting conversion) and auto-generates creative assets (lifting supply quality). Both directly push up the price per impression.

That explains how Meta raised unit prices 14% while impressions grew only 10%—it does not make money by selling more slots; it makes money by selling each slot for more.The limit of that pricing power is advertiser ROI: as long as returns on spend hold, price increases can continue; once ROI falls below the threshold, price increases immediately turn into volume loss.

Conclusion

Sector view: the share winner in a mature market, but an expensive way to win. Meta's competitive edge in advertising is still widening (growth is 2.4x Google's), which is good; the cost is the world's highest capex intensity, which is bad. The industry cycle sits at a stage of "rising capex, stable ad demand", a mix that is unfriendly to cash flow.
