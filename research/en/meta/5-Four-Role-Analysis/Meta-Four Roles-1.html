Core view

In one sentence: Meta wholesales the attention of 3.64 billion people, selling it to advertisers by the thousand impressions at near-zero marginal cost. Of the USD 63.06 billion in advertising revenue in Q3 2026, volume contributed 10% and pricing 14% — it can raise prices without consulting customers, the hallmark of the best business models.

What makes this a good business: both ends depend on it

Free for users, auction-based for advertisers, with algorithms in between. Global ARPU is roughly USD 70 a year (2026 annualised revenue of USD 256 billion ÷ 3.64 billion daily active users), and North American ARPU is more than three times that. The key is that Meta does not set this price—the auction does: the system charges whatever an advertiser is willing to pay for a conversion.

Supply is stable too. Users produce the content and Meta pays no author fees (short-video creators receive revenue shares, but the proportion is small); algorithms handle distribution, so there is no need to buy copyrights or build warehouses. This is the prototype of an "asset-light money printer": 2025 revenue was USD 200.97 billion, while capital expenditure was only USD 72.2 billion.

What has it now turned the money printer into?

Capex of USD 135–145 billion in 2026 equals 55% of revenue. That buys GPUs, builds data centres and signs long-term power contracts, and what it produces is "compute," not "revenue." Compute feeds better recommendations and higher ad conversion, but the path is indirect and lagged.

The Duan Yongping-style question is: will this business still be here in ten years? Yes. Social graphs will not disappear, and neither will advertising budgets. But he would immediately ask a second question: will it still be making money in ten years? That is harder to say—if it has to plow all of operating cash flow back in every year just to stay competitive, the money it earns does not belong to shareholders.

Is Muse a second business model, or a second metaverse?

Muse's pricing is straightforward: the free tier offers roughly 100 million tokens per week, paid tiers sit at USD 20/month and USD 100/month, and there is a transaction take rate on top. Launched on September 8, it passed 10 million downloads in 40 days, with daily actives at about 25%. Shopping is the most frequent use case, used by over 25% of users, with an average order value above USD 200.

Cost is the problem. Morgan Stanley estimates a monthly serving cost of about USD 37 per user under a base case of 25% quota usage per week—while Meta earns only USD 70 per user per year globally. In other words, for a user willing to pay USD 20 a month, Meta still subsidizes USD 17. Zuckerberg says cost efficiency will improve more than tenfold by 2027; if that holds, the business works; if not, Muse is a more expensive metaverse.

Who pays: structural shifts in advertising budgets

Meta's advertiser base is long-tail: a large number of small and medium merchants, plus e-commerce, app downloads and local services. These customers run small budgets, decide fast and are extremely ROI-sensitive—as long as the same money can sell more goods on Meta, they will increase budgets; once performance deteriorates, they withdraw within a week. That explains the 14% rise in unit price: not brand advertising getting more expensive, but conversion rates on performance advertising improving.

The risk lies here too. Small and medium merchants are the most sensitive to financing costs and consumer sentiment; when the macro turns weaker, they are the first to disappear. Google's search advertising customers, by contrast, skew toward large brands and mature e-commerce, giving it somewhat stronger cyclical resilience. Meta grows faster and has greater elasticity, but is also more fragile on the downside.

Conclusion

Business judgment: first-class business, questionable turnaround direction. The unit economics of the advertising cash machine have not deteriorated — pricing is still rising. What has deteriorated is where the cash goes. Muse's unit economics are currently negative, and whether they turn positive in 2027 is the only business question that matters for this business over the next three years.
