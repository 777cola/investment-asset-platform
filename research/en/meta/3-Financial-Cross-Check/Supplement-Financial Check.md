## The only deviation above 1%: the two measures of capex

RMB 34.91 billion is "capital expenditure including finance lease principal repayments"; RMB 33.94 billion is "purchase of property and equipment" in the cash flow statement. The USD 970 million difference is exactly the finance lease principal component. This is not a calculation error by anyone; it is a difference in the boundaries of the two concepts.

Why this matters: free cash flow is calculated as 327.9 − 339.4 − 9.7 = -21.2. If you use only 339.4, you get -11.5 billion and the erroneous conclusion that the company is "close to break-even"; if you use only 349.1, you get -21.2 but cannot explain what was deducted. Both numbers are correct, but only by putting all three together does the account close. This is also why every reference to capital expenditure in this report is labeled "including finance lease principal."

## The 11.6% market-cap gap reflects timing, not an error

Closed at USD 697.52 on 21 October, corresponding to a USD 1.78 trillion market cap; closed at USD 606.73 on 2 November, corresponding to USD 1.575 trillion. The 11.6% difference comes entirely from the share price, not from a change in share count. The share count of 2.596 billion shares stays unchanged until the offering closes — the USD 37.5 billion issuance priced on 26 October, with settlement typically T+2 to T+5, and the new shares will only be counted at the end of the fourth quarter or in early 2027.

This report therefore uses 2.596 billion shares and USD 1.575 trillion throughout, and calculates dilution separately when discussing the offering.Do not assume others are wrong when they write "USD 1.7 trillion market cap" — that reflects the price before 21 October.

## One more figure that is easy to misread: +264% net profit

Net profit was RMB 9.86 billion, up 264% year on year, and media headlines all called it a "surge." That number has no interpretive value: in Q3 2025 Meta booked about USD 18.95 billion of income tax due to a US tax law change, an effective tax rate of 87%, crushing net profit to USD 2.71 billion. The base was artificially depressed, and with the tax rate back at 24% this year, growth naturally runs at triple digits.

What actually matters is the adjusted basis: excluding the USD 9.89 billion litigation settlement, EPS was USD 7.40 on a 17% effective tax rate; GAAP diluted EPS was USD 3.83. The two figures differ by nearly a factor of two, and which one to use depends on the question you are answering — use 7.40 to judge operating quality, and 3.83 to judge what shareholders actually received this year. This report uses GAAP in the income statement and adjusted figures when discussing the core business, noting which is which each time either appears.

## How should we handle the three items that cannot be cross-checked against two sources?

① 2027 total expense guidance: management said only "significant increase," with no numerical source, and the whole market is guessing. Approach: cite only the qualitative statement, give no forecast figure, and flag "author's assumption" in the scenario assumptions.

② Muse revenue: not broken out in the Q3 results; media reports only download and account counts (10 million in 40 days since launch, daily active users about 25%). Approach: no revenue estimate; discuss only as "option value."

③ Reality Labs' USD 15 billion loss target: only an oral commitment from Zuckerberg on the earnings call, with no written guidance. Approach: quote his words and note "management commentary, not formal company guidance"; reconciliation possible only in Q1 2027.

## The three numbers not adopted

① 26.27x P/E: that was calculated on the October 21 closing price and is now stale; this report uses the 21x based on November 2 throughout. ② "AI brings in X billion in incremental ad revenue": the company only said "meaningful increment" without breaking it out, so any specific figure is a third-party estimate. ③ Sell-side 2027 EPS consensus: estimates range from USD 26 to USD 31, too wide a dispersion, so this report uses its own three-scenario assumptions and labels them "author's estimate" in the valuation section.
