## Why 0%, not "try a small position"

The "take a small position to test the waters" logic applies when you don't quite understand a name but fear missing out. Meta is not that case: it is understandable, the price is simply wrong. When you understand a business but the price is wrong, the right answer is to wait, not to test. The cost of testing is not just money but attention: once you build a position, you start finding reasons to hold it.

Correlation is the second reason. If the portfolio already holds Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, Meta adds close to zero marginal diversification—all four share the same factor: AI capex. When four stocks rise and fall together, buying one more is not diversification; it is leverage.

## How to set hard entry and exit levels for adding to and trimming positions

Add: USD 500 (below 19x) is the first buy point, USD 450 (17x) the second, and USD 400 the floor of the bear case — at that level the question is whether advertising has broken, not how far the stock has fallen.

Trim: if bought at the low, sell half on any move above USD 750 (27x 2027 EPS) and exit entirely at USD 860 (top of the bull case). Set no price stop, only condition-based stops: two consecutive quarters of advertising growth below 10%, or a further 20% upward revision to capex guidance, means sell unconditionally, regardless of profit or loss.

## Correlation with other holdings

Meta's daily return correlation with Google, Amazon and Microsoft has long ranged between 0.6 and 0.75, and in 2026 it is still rising on the shared "AI capex" factor. On October 29, Google fell 13% while Meta rose 2% — a rare divergence reflecting rotation within the sector rather than any breakdown in correlation.

Correlation with Nvidia is somewhat lower (about 0.5 to 0.6), but the direction is the same: Nvidia sells the shovels, Meta buys them — two ends of the same supply chain. True diversification comes from assets unrelated to AI — consumer, healthcare, bonds. If the portfolio is already heavily weighted in the AI chain, Meta's reasonable position size should be below 3%.

## If the portfolio already holds the AI chain, size the exposure first

A simple exposure calculation: assume the portfolio holds 5% Nvidia, 4% Microsoft, 4% Google and 3% Meta. On the surface that is four stocks and a 16% position; in reality it is 16% exposure to a single factor — AI capex. Once capex expectations are revised down, all four fall together; the diversification is illusory.

Meta's marginal value, then, is not as "one more advertising stock" but as "the only company with no cloud business, monetizing compute purely through advertising." What separates it from the other three is where the risk exposure sits, not the factor. Genuine diversification has to come from consumer, healthcare and bonds — not from swapping names within the same chain.

## The discipline of rebalancing

Single-name cap 5%; trim back to 4% if exceeded. Recalculate the target price after each quarterly report; if upside from the current price to the neutral target price is below 15%, do not add to the position. The purpose of this rule is to prevent "buying more as it rises" — a rally like September 2026, up 13% in a month, is exactly when valuation discipline slips.

One rule runs counter to intuition: if the stock falls to 450 but fundamentals deteriorate at the same time (advertising growth drops below 10%), do not buy — clear the watchlist entirely. This rule is what separates a cheap good company from a cheap bad company.
