## Five passed: quality is not an issue

Gross margin 79.3%, net margin 15.3%, operating cash flow 3.3x net profit—**fewer than thirty companies across A-shares and US equities satisfy all three at once.** Operating cash flow far exceeds net profit, showing profit is not propped up by receivables: advertisers prepay and settle monthly, so cash collection runs ahead of revenue recognition.

The net cash criterion needs a caveat: cash and marketable securities of USD 82 billion exceed interest-bearing debt, but the company carries roughly USD 825 billion in lease and purchase commitments, plus USD 37.5 billion in newly issued equity and mandatory convertible preferred stock with a 6.5% coupon. On "cash minus interest-bearing debt" it is net cash; on "cash minus all commitments" it is a highly leveraged company.**This criterion passes, but only barely.**

## A historical parallel: this is not the first time it has remade itself

When Meta listed in 2012, capex exceeded 30% of revenue because it had to build data centers; after 2016 it fell to 15%–20%, free cash flow began to gush, and buybacks and the share price followed. The returns on that round of rebuilding were verifiable: two years after capex peaked, operating margin rose from 40% to above 50%.

This round differs in scale. **In 2026 capex is about 55% of revenue, nearly twice the intensity of 2012, and the payback verification period has stretched from two years to three to five.** It has bet right once before, so it cannot be dismissed just because the numbers look ugly now; but nor can this round be assumed correct just because it bet right once. That is all "observe" means here.

## Only two points remain: one is the outcome, the other is the cause

Free cash flow of -USD 2.12 billion is the result. Breaking it down: operating cash flow of USD 32.79 billion, minus purchases of property and equipment of USD 33.94 billion, minus principal repayments on finance leases of about USD 970 million, equals -USD 2.12 billion. This is the first negative quarter since the 2012 listing; the same period last year was +USD 10.63 billion. **Note this is not operating deterioration — operating cash flow still rose 9.3% year on year.**

Capex/operating cash flow of 106% is the cause. Full-year 2025 capex was USD 72.2 billion, with 2026 guidance of USD 135–145 billion, nearly double; the first three quarters have already spent USD 83.06 billion. For comparison: Apple has held this metric below 10% for years, Google is around 60% in 2026, and Meta is the only one of the four major cloud players without a cloud business to fall back on, yet has pushed the ratio above 100%.

## Not fatal, but it changes the valuation approach

Are these two points fatal? No, but **they turn Meta from "a company that can be valued on discounted free cash flow" into "a company that can only be valued on P/E"**. The denominator of a DCF is free cash flow, and that number is now negative; any DCF would have to rest on post-2029 assumptions, which is not valuation but faith.

There is only one genuinely fatal condition: **advertising growth falls below 10% while capex stays at the RMB 140 billion level.** At that point revenue can no longer cover depreciation, profit turns to negative growth for four consecutive quarters, and a 21x PE instantly becomes 30x. That combination has not appeared yet — Q3 advertising still grew 26%, and Q2 grew 27%. So this is a watch item, not a reason to avoid the stock.
