Conclusion on removing it from the portfolio: not a bad company, but one that has remade itself into a heavy-asset business

Five of seven hard metrics pass. The two failures are strategic choices rather than accounting issues: free cash flow of -USD 2.12 billion and capex/operating cash flow of 106%. At other companies these two would be alarm bells; at Meta they are buttons management pressed deliberately. The negative screen therefore concludes "retain and watch," not "eliminate": this remains a first-class business, just one that temporarily no longer generates distributable cash.

Five passed: quality is not an issue

Gross margin 79.3%, net margin 15.3%, operating cash flow at 3.3x net profit—fewer than thirty companies across A-shares and US equities clear all three hurdles at once. Operating cash flow runs well ahead of net profit, so earnings are not being propped up by receivables: advertisers prepay and settle monthly, meaning cash collection leads revenue recognition.

The net cash criterion warrants a caveat: cash and marketable securities of USD 82 billion exceed interest-bearing debt, but the company carries roughly USD 825 billion in lease and purchase commitments, plus USD 37.5 billion in newly issued equity and mandatory convertible preferred stock with a 6.5% coupon. On "cash minus interest-bearing debt" it is net cash; on "cash minus all commitments" it is a highly leveraged company.This criterion passes, but only barely.

A historical parallel: this is not the first time it has reinvented itself

When Meta listed in 2012, capex exceeded 30% of revenue as it built out data centers; after 2016 that fell to 15%–20%, free cash flow began to gush, and buybacks and the share price followed. The returns on that rebuild were verifiable: two years after capex peaked, operating margin rose from 40% to above 50%.

This round differs in scale. In 2026 capex is about 55% of revenue, nearly twice the intensity of 2012, and the payback verification period has stretched from two years to three to five. It has bet right once before, so it cannot be dismissed just because the numbers look ugly now; but nor can this round be assumed correct just because it bet right once. That is all "observe" means here.

Only two questions remain: the outcome and its cause

The result is free cash flow of -USD 2.12 billion: operating cash flow of USD 32.79 billion, less purchases of property and equipment of USD 33.94 billion and principal repayments on finance leases of about USD 970 million. This is the first negative quarter since the 2012 listing, against +USD 10.63 billion in the same period last year. Note this is not operating deterioration — operating cash flow still rose 9.3% YoY.

Capex/operating cash flow of 106% is the cause. Full-year 2025 capex was USD 72.2 billion, with 2026 guidance of USD 135–145 billion, nearly double; the first three quarters have already spent USD 83.06 billion. For comparison, Apple has held this metric below 10% for years and Google is around 60% in 2026; Meta is the only one of the four major cloud players without a cloud business to fall back on, yet has pushed the ratio above 100%.

Not fatal, but it changes the valuation approach

Are these two points fatal? No, but they turn Meta from "a company that can be valued on discounted free cash flow" into "a company that can only be valued on P/E". A DCF is only as good as its denominator, free cash flow, and that number is now negative; any DCF would have to rest on post-2029 assumptions, which is not valuation but faith.

Only one condition is genuinely fatal: advertising growth falls below 10% while capex stays at the RMB 140 billion level. At that point revenue no longer covers depreciation, profit turns to negative growth for four consecutive quarters, and a 21x P/E instantly becomes 30x. That combination has not appeared yet — Q3 advertising still grew 26%, and Q2 grew 27%. So this is a watch item, not a reason to avoid the stock.

Conclusion

Five of seven criteria met; both failures are capital allocation issues, not operating issues. How to play it: do not buy at a discount. Wait for one of two signals — free cash flow turning positive (2027 at the earliest, second half, provided capex peaks), or the share price falling below 18x. Until then, it sits in the "understandable but not actionable" bucket.
