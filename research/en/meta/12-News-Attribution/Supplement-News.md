## Phase 1 (late August to mid-September): bad news lands, share price holds

On August 26, Meta reached a settlement with the attorneys general of 52 states worth up to USD 18.59 billion, payable over ten years, and agreed to make safety settings for teen accounts default-on. It ranks among the largest state-level consumer protection settlements in US history. The stock rose just 1.1% that day—the market had already priced in the fine; the real new information is that product rules have been permanently changed.

The same stretch brought a 4.4% pullback (8/17 to 8/18, from 568.97 to 543.67) on no company-level news, part of broad tech-sector volatility.Such declines need no explanation and no action.

## Phase 2 (September): a single-product valuation re-rating

Muse launched on September 8 and drew 730,000 downloads in 5 days, surpassing 10 million in 40 days. It topped the US App Store on September 21, when the stock rose 11.3% and added USD 192 billion in market cap, while Zuckerberg's net worth rose about 25 billion in a single day. On September 24 the stock set a 52-week high of USD 777.59.

Market cap rose about USD 300 billion over the month, reflecting the narrative that "Meta is back at the AI table" rather than any revenue. Muse revenue in the financials is zero — the Q3 report contains no contribution from it at all. The market is buying an option, not cash flow.

## Phase 3 (late October): cash flow takes center stage

Earnings after the close on October 21: revenue of RMB 64.6 billion beat consensus and the stock rose as much as 6% after hours, but the call was all about capex and 2027 expenses. The next day the stock fell 7.0%, then declined for four straight sessions from 697.52 to 612.49, a cumulative -12.2%.

On October 26, the EU's EUR 798 million fine and a USD 95 billion equity financing were announced on the same day. The stock fell 1.9%. The market barely reacted to the fine (0.05% of market cap); its reaction to the financing was slow-burning — the stock fell another 4% over the next five days.

On October 29, Google's earnings sent its stock down 13%, while Meta rose 2.0%. The day showed money rotating within the sector: out of Google, where investment is dragging on profit, and into Meta, where ad growth is faster. But this is relative strength, not an absolute endorsement.

## One overlooked detail: 13 upgrades, stock still falls

At least 13 investment banks raised target prices post-results, Bernstein and Morgan Stanley among them, citing advertising resilience and Muse's potential. The stock still fell from 697.52 to 606.73, a 13% decline. Sell-side underwrites cash flow 12 months out; buy-side sells the uncertainty inside 12 months.

Such divergence is common in capital-intensive transitions. Analysts work off DCF, and the DCF denominator is long-term growth, so the further the stock falls, the cheaper it looks; traders work off positioning, and positioning tracks next quarter's free cash flow, so the further the stock falls, the less they dare to buy. Two languages coexist, and price is set by the latter.

## Which are noise: three criteria

Start by sizing each item against market cap. The EU's EUR 798 million fine is 0.05% of the EUR 1.58 trillion market cap — noise. The EUR 18.59 billion settlement is 1.2% — a signal, but not fatal. The EUR 95 billion financing is 6% — the signal of signals.

Next, ask whether it changes future cash flow. Muse topping the App Store does not move current cash flow, since revenue is zero — noise. Lifting capex guidance by EUR 5 billion changes depreciation over the next three years — a signal.

Then ask whether there is verifiable follow-through. The settlement carries a ten-year payment schedule and is verifiable; Muse download volumes come with no retention data and are not.

## Without Muse, what would it be worth now

A counterfactual: strip out Muse and the market would price Meta as "an advertising company growing 26% with USD 140 billion of capex." At 2027 EPS of USD 28, that is USD 504 at 18x and USD 560 at 20x.

In other words, USD 50 to 100 of the current USD 606.73 share price—8%–16%—is Muse's option value. That pricing is not unreasonable: USD 20 billion of Muse revenue in 2028 at 10x PS implies a USD 200 billion market cap, which discounts back to precisely this magnitude.

But option value fluctuates; cash flow does not.Most of September's USD 300 billion of market-cap gain falls into the former bucket.
