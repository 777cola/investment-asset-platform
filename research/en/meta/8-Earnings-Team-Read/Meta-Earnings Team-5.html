Core view

Read the four deep dives together and the intersection is clear: advertising pricing power is consensus, unclear returns on capex are consensus, and no margin of safety at the current price is consensus. The only disagreement is whether this spending is worth waiting for.

Consensus 1: Revenue perfect, cash ugly

All four agree on revenue of RMB 64.6 billion and advertising of RMB 63.06 billion, with volume and price each up 26% — the best quarter in history. Capex of RMB 34.91 billion and free cash flow of -RMB 2.12 billion mark a first in history. Two historical extremes on the same table show the company is at a turning point.

Duan Yongping sees the business is still there, Buffett sees the cash is gone, Munger sees the accounting is being dressed up, and Li Lu sees the position is still there but the deposit is expensive. The four statements describe the same thing; they differ only in what each cares about.

Consensus 2: Depreciation takes centre stage in 2027

All four close reads flag depreciation. RMB 140 billion of capex amortised over five to six years adds RMB 23–28 billion of annual cost, which requires RMB 60–70 billion of incremental revenue to cover. Revenue growth of 27% in 2026 is sufficient; 15% in 2027 is not.

This is the only bearish argument that can be verified by arithmetic, and the only quantified risk all four wrote into their conclusions.

Disagreement: is this spending investment or cost

Duan Yongping and Buffett anchor on cost: absent a calculable return, it is not an investment. Li Lu anchors on investment: the contest over position size cannot be settled on short-term accounts alone. Munger sits between the two: if it is an investment, it should carry a return calculation; without one it is gambling, and gambling warrants only a gambling-sized position.

The synthesized conclusion follows Munger: this may be right, but until a calculation exists, it does not merit a full position.

The three synthesized actions

First, don't buy: at USD 606.73 the stock trades on 21x TTM, or 22x diluted, leaving no margin of safety. Second, track: 2027 expense guidance, depreciation growth, narrowing Reality Labs losses, and whether Muse subscriptions are broken out separately. Third, valuation: reassess below USD 500; USD 410 is the floor under the bear case.

Of the three actions, what matters most is not the decision not to buy but what to track — because the buying opportunity will come from a change in a specific number, not from a macro narrative.

Why not use weighted scoring

The four perspectives are not weighted and averaged, because they do not answer the same question: Duan Yongping asks whether the business can be understood, Buffett whether it is worth the price, Munger how one could lose money, and Li Lu whether it will still be around in ten years. Adding up the answers to four different questions yields only a set of numbers, not a judgment.

The synthesis is therefore a "single veto": if any one of them says the business is unintelligible or the price is wrong, we do not buy. Two have now said so, and the conclusion is naturally not to buy.

Conclusion

Synthesis: a good company, a bad entry point, and no discount on the price. The advertising moat is still deepening, but capital allocation is deteriorating; the two offset each other, leaving the share price roughly fair. Fair means no excess return, and therefore no reason to buy.
