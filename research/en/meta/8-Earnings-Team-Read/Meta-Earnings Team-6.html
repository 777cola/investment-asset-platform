Core view

Meta delivered the strongest quarter in advertising history, and also its first negative free cash flow report in fourteen years as a public company. Both are true at once, and it took the market two weeks to work out which one to look at.

I. The advertising machine is still accelerating

Q3 revenue was USD 64.6 billion, up 26% year on year and USD 1.2 billion above the consensus. Advertising came to USD 63.06 billion, 97.6% of total revenue and a record quarterly high. More important is the mix: ad impressions rose 10% year on year and average price per ad rose 14%.

Rising volume and price together is unusual in advertising. Volume growth means users are there; price growth means advertisers are bidding. Over the past five years this combination has appeared only two or three times, and this is the second consecutive quarter. AI recommendation has turned "guess what you like" into "guess what you'll buy"; conversion rates on ad slots rise, and price follows.

That is also why Google's advertising grew only 10.6% and Amazon's only 10.9%, while Meta grew 26%. The same AI reduces clicks when applied to search and raises conversion when applied to the feed. The impact of technology on different business models can point in opposite directions.

2. The bill has arrived too

In the same earnings report, capital expenditure was USD 34.91 billion, up 85.6% year on year, the highest single quarter on record; free cash flow was -USD 2.12 billion, the first negative single quarter since its 2012 listing. Capital expenditure for the first three quarters already totaled USD 83.06 billion, with full-year guidance of USD 135 to 145 billion.

This money turns into GPUs, data centers and long-term power contracts. It does not become revenue immediately, but it starts becoming depreciation from 2027. Amortized over five to six years, that adds RMB 20 to 30 billion of cost a year. To cover that cost, it needs RMB 60 to 70 billion more revenue a year, equivalent to another 25% growth on top of this year.

The income statement already shows the strain: cost of revenue rose 45.2%, gross margin fell from 82.0% to 79.3%; R&D expense rose 32.4%. Revenue up 26%, costs up 45% — operating leverage was negative this quarter.

III. Two one-offs, one positive and one negative

In August, Meta reached a settlement with the attorneys general of 52 states over teen mental health litigation, totaling up to USD 18.59 billion, payable over ten years, with USD 9.89 billion accrued this quarter. Operating profit was thus compressed from USD 22.88 billion to USD 12.99 billion, and margin fell from 35% to 20%.

On the other side, net profit of USD 9.86 billion, up 264% year on year, looks striking, but is actually due to last year's base being depressed by a USD 18.95 billion one-off tax expense.The two one-offs offset each other; looking only at the headline yields a completely opposite conclusion.Excluding the settlement costs, EPS is USD 7.40, versus USD 3.83 on a GAAP basis.

IV. The real turning point lies in the capital structure

On October 21, the board approved the creation of non-voting Class C common stock, effective January 19, 2027, distributed 1:1, effectively a two-for-one split; Zuckerberg's 60.6% voting power is unaffected.

Five days later the company put it to use: USD 25 billion of common stock priced at USD 625, USD 12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred with a 6.5% coupon, plus a Class C ATM of up to USD 60 billion. A total financing framework of USD 95 billion, the largest equity raise in the company's history.

Over the same period, buybacks in the first nine months of 2026 were zero. Over the past seven years Meta spent USD 20 billion to 30 billion a year on buybacks, the most aggressive of the Magnificent Seven. Within a year, it went from returning USD 30 billion a year to shareholders to asking shareholders for USD 95 billion — not a financial technique, but a strategic pivot.

V. How to price it

Current price USD 606.73, market cap USD 1.575 trillion, 21x TTM, 22% drawdown from the September 24 high of USD 777.59.

Base case: 2027 EPS of USD 28 at 22x gives a fair value of USD 620, flat to the current price. But that is the share count before the equity raises. The USD 37.5 billion already priced in dilutes 2.3%, and the USD 60 billion ATM, if fully drawn, dilutes another 3.9%, for 6% combined, putting the diluted fair value at about USD 580. In other words, buying at the current price implies an expected return of negative 4% against 32% downside risk.

The conclusion is therefore watch, not buy. Two triggers: 2027 expense growth guidance below revenue growth, or the stock back below USD 500. Until then, just watch. A good company does not need to be bought at a bad moment.

Conclusion

In one sentence: Meta traded a flawless advertising quarter for a USD 95 billion bill. The math may yet work out, but shareholders pay first and get answers later. While waiting for those answers, they should not pay a premium.
