Earnings Deep Dive (Duan Yongping) — Meta

Check first whether the business makes money, then where the money goes

Core view

Duan Yongping reads financial statements in a fixed order: gross margin, net margin, operating cash flow, capex. The first three tell him whether it is a good business; the fourth tells him whether the business still belongs to shareholders. Meta passed the first three. The fourth is where trouble hit.

Step 1: Gross margin 79.3%, confirming a good business

Gross margin has held in a 78-82% range for several consecutive years, pointing to stable pricing power. In practice, for every 100 of advertising revenue collected, direct costs absorb only 21. Of the remaining 79, R&D takes 31 and sales and administration a large chunk, leaving 20 as operating profit.

Net margin 15.3% (GAAP, including settlement costs), 30.6% adjusted.Duan Yongping would say this business has no problem making money; the question is who the money belongs to.

Step two: operating cash flow of RMB 32.79 billion, 3.3x net profit

The multiple is a healthy signal: advertisers prepay while settlement is monthly, so cash is collected before revenue is recognised, days sales outstanding sits around 40 and bad-debt pressure is minimal.

One detail warrants a flag, however: part of the multiple comes from adding back depreciation and amortisation. The higher the depreciation, the better operating cash flow looks, but that reflects the accounting treatment of past spending rather than newly earned cash. Cash flow must therefore be read alongside capex; looking at half of it alone leads to the wrong conclusion.

Step 3: capex of RMB 34.9 billion — the answer is on paper

Operating cash flow of RMB 32.79 billion, capex of RMB 34.91 billion, free cash flow of -RMB 2.12 billion. Duan Yongping's test is simple: a business that has to plow every dollar it earns back in each year is not a cash-flow business but a growth stock — and a growth stock needs a clear return calculation.

Meta cannot provide that calculation now. Management says Muse will improve cost efficiency tenfold next year and that Reality Labs' losses will narrow to RMB 15 billion next year. Both statements say next year will be better; neither says how much each dollar invested will earn back.

Step four: how would he write the conclusion

I understand this business—advertising is plain for all to see. But with RMB 140 billion being spent, I cannot see where the return comes from. If I cannot see it, I will not touch it; I will wait until the money is spent and the picture is clear. There are plenty of opportunities anyway; I will not miss this one.

He will not buy simply because AI is the future; he will buy only if the business can still make money ten years from now and the price is cheap today. Only one of those two conditions is met now.

Conclusion

Close reading: a good business, but not good cash flow. Gross margin and operating cash flow attest to the quality of the business; capex shows management chose a different path. The Duan Yongping approach is to put it on the watchlist and wait for free cash flow to turn positive or for the price to fall sharply.
