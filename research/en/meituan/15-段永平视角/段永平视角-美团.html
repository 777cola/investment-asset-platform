📌 Preface: Duan Yongping and Meituan, six years of watching from the sidelines

In October 2019, someone asked Duan Yongping what he thought of Meituan. He said one thing:"Wang Xing is an interesting character. Unfortunately, I have not yet dealt with him directly."

In 2020, he began to follow Meituan, saying "I get the sense that Wang Xing is quite impressive, able to face problems rationally." But he also said—"I don't know Meituan, I don't know whether the share price is high or low, and I've never bought it."

At the end of 2023, when someone brought up that Douyin was eroding Meituan's moat, he did not argue.

By 2026, Meituan's share price has fallen 83% from its 2021 peak of HKD 460 to HKD 78. JD.com burned RMB 100 billion on food delivery to grab market share, Alibaba's Taobao Shangou burst onto the scene mid-race, and Douyin is expanding aggressively in in-store services. Meituan swung to a massive loss of RMB 23.4 billion in 2025, plunging from a RMB 35.8 billion profit in 2024.

Duan Yongping still hasn't bought.

In this report, I use Duan Yongping's way of thinking—the three sayings he often repeats—to reason through why he did not buy, and how he would view Meituan if he were looking at it today.

"Buying a stock is buying a company, and buying a company is buying its people. Good business + good price + good management = time to buy."
— Duan Yongping (Da Dao Wu Xing Wo You Xing)

I. Buying a company means buying its people: Is Wang Xing trustworthy?

🎯 How does Duan Yongping view founders?
Duan Yongping's criteria for choosing a CEO:① Integrity and honesty ② Rationality ③ Long-termism ④ Decide within the circle of competenceHe has said: "Good management means doing the right things, then doing things right." He has also said that if he does not like or trust the founder, he will not touch the business no matter how good it is. This is "buying a company means buying its people."

Wang Xing's track record — the resilience of a serial entrepreneur

Wang Xing is the archetype of the Tsinghua graduate who dropped out in the US and returned home to start a business. Xiaonei (sold), Fanfou (shut down), Meituan (built into a success). Three ventures, the first two "failures," yet each time he learned from them and got back on his feet.

Duan Yongping would appreciate this "unkillable" trait. He went through plenty of setbacks himself before founding BBK. Someone who has weathered failure and can stand back up is usually more reliable than someone who has only known smooth sailing.

Deduction: Meituan Select — lessons from RMB 70 billion in tuition

Meituan Select accumulated losses of more than RMB 70 billion from 2021 to 2025. Most operations nationwide were shut down in June 2025, leaving only Guangdong and Zhejiang. It was a massive strategic misstep.

How would Duan Yongping see it? He would say:"This is the wrong thing to have done."

Community group buying is fundamentally a business of burning cash to buy scale. No differentiation, no moat — Pinduoduo, Alibaba and Meituan are all pouring money into it. Duan Yongping would certainly ask:"What makes you think you can win? What is your competitive advantage?"If Wang Xing cannot give a clear, convincing answer, then the decision is "doing the wrong thing"—and in Duan Yongping's framework, that is the most serious demerit for management.

🤔 The question Duan Yongping would ask Wang Xing

① "What was your original purpose in building Meituan? Or was it just about scaling up?"

② "Meituan Select lost RMB 70 billion — what did you learn? If you had another chance, would you do it again?"

③ "JD.com and Alibaba are both burning cash to grab your market. What's your plan? Keep burning or cede ground?"

④ "Is the overseas expansion (Keeta) a direction they genuinely understand, or is it just because the domestic market is too cutthroat?"

Bonus: the ability to face problems rationally

Duan Yongping once said, "Wang Xing is quite impressive—he can face problems rationally." That matters. When Meituan was hit by the pandemic in 2020, Wang Xing responded rationally—no panic, no reckless investing, just a focus on the core business. Facing competition from JD and Alibaba in 2025, Wang Xing's response was to ramp up subsidies to defend share while decisively shutting down Meituan Select to stop the losses.

Able to shut it down after a RMB 70 billion loss—not every CEO has that decisiveness. Duan Yongping would approve of the quality of "admitting mistakes and cutting losses in time."

II. Meituan's Business Model — How Would Duan Yongping Understand It?

🎯 Duan Yongping's three criteria for defining a "good business"

① Strong moat —"What others cannot do, or cannot do as well as you."

② Pricing power —"You can raise prices without losing customers"

③ Strong user stickiness —"Users can't do without you"

What business is Meituan in? The one-sentence version

Meituan = a local life services platform that uses high-frequency food delivery as its traffic gateway and its on-demand delivery network as infrastructure.

Duan Yongping would break this into two layers:Delivery infrastructure + platform monetization model。

Delivery infrastructure is grunt work—capital-intensive, operationally complex (3.36 million riders), and low-margin. But once built, it does confer scale advantages.

The platform monetization model is the logic of a "good business" — once traffic arrives, it is monetized through in-store services, travel and hospitality, advertising and instant shopping, with very low marginal costs.

Duan Yongping-style intuition:"If you break Meituan down—the delivery network is a 'cost center' and the platform business is a 'profit center.' The problem is that competitors are not trying to take your delivery network; they are trying to take your platform profit. If your profit center is taken away, the delivery network becomes a 'public utility'—you are still doing the hard work, but someone else is making the money."

The core question: does Meituan have pricing power?

The one thing Duan Yongping cares about most:Can this business raise prices?

Apple can raise prices (iPhone users won't switch to Android just because it costs USD 200 more). Moutai can raise prices (Moutai drinkers won't switch to Wuliangye just because it costs RMB 200 more). Tencent can raise prices (QQ memberships, game skins).

What about Meituan?

No.The 2025 data has already proven it:

  • • JD.com enters food delivery with "zero commission", forcing Meituan to ramp up subsidies
  • • Delivery service revenue fell 2% year on year — not a price increase, but a price cut
  • • Blended merchant take rate of 15%–18%, already close to the upper limit the restaurant industry can bear
  • • The restaurant industry's average net margin is only 5%-10%, leaving merchants no room to raise prices
Duan Yongping-style conclusion:A business without pricing power is not a good business, no matter how large. Meituan's problem now is that it is not that it does not want to raise prices — it cannot. If it does, users will run to JD.com, to Ele.me, to Taobao Shangou. In this kind of business, you earn hard money, not easy money.

3. What will Meituan most likely still be doing in 10 years?

When Duan Yongping looks at a company, his favorite question is:"What is this company most likely still doing 10 years from now?"

The subtext of that statement: if you cannot clearly picture what a company will look like 10 years from now, you never really understood the business.

🔮 What Meituan will most likely still be doing 10 years from now (2036)

✅ Food delivery — definitely still here.This is social infrastructure. Chinese people cannot go back to the era of cooking for themselves. The frequency will only rise, not fall. But by then, food delivery may be unmanned (drones, autonomous vehicles), and the competitive landscape may have stabilized.

✅ Instant retail — most likely still here.The "everything to your door" trend is irreversible. Delivery within 30 minutes will become a basic service like water, electricity and gas. But competition in this market will intensify—JD.com, Alibaba and even Douyin will all take part.

⚠️ In-store/hotel & travel — probably still there, but Douyin may have taken a big bite out of share.Content-driven consumption decisions (Douyin seeding → in-store spending) are replacing the search-driven model (Meituan search → find a store → spend).

❌ Community group buying — most likely gone.Meituan Select has shut down most of its regions; this direction has been proven unviable.

❓ Overseas (Keeta) — hard to call.Chinese internet companies' overseas expansion has a far higher failure rate than success rate. Success would be a surprise, but it falls outside the range of judgments within their circle of competence.

A Duan Yongping-style 10-year judgment:Ten years from now, Meituan's core asset — its instant delivery network — will certainly still be there, and it may well be a more valuable asset. The question is whether Meituan alone will operate this network, or whether it becomes a "shared network" — used by Alibaba, by JD.com, and by Douyin too. If the latter, Meituan becomes a "delivery company," and its valuation logic changes completely.

The valuation gap between a "delivery company" and a "platform company" can be 3-5x. Duan Yongping would wait until that answer becomes clear before deciding.

IV. Duan Yongping's three core questions for Meituan

🔴 Question one: Is this a "good business" or just a "big business"?

Duan Yongping has repeatedly stressed:"A good business is not necessarily a big business. But a big business is not necessarily a good business."

Meituan is unquestionably a "big business" — annual revenue approaching RMB 500 billion, coverage of 2,800+ cities nationwide, and nearly 700 million annual active users. But is it a "good business"?

The standard for a good business is:High profit margin + high return on capital + low capital investment.Meituan's net margin was 10.6% in 2024 and turned negative outright in 2025. Return on invested capital (ROIC) keeps falling under competitive pressure. More importantly, it must keep committing large amounts of capital to defend its competitive position—not the asset-light model Duan Yongping favors.

Conclusion: Meituan is a big business, but not a good business as defined by Duan Yongping.

🔴 Question two: Did Wang Xing do "the right thing"?

The core philosophy Duan Yongping held when running a business was"Do the right things, and do things right"。

Meituan Select — spending RMB 70 billion to build a community group-buying business with no moat, sustained only by subsidies — is that "the right thing to do"? Duan Yongping would say no. Because the essence of community group-buying is burning cash to buy scale: a business model with no differentiation that creates no real long-term value.

JD and Alibaba burn cash to grab market share—Meituan is forced to fight back—and this too is not "the right thing to do" but "the wrong thing to do" (burning cash and taking losses to grab market share) because rivals leave it no choice.

Conclusion: Wang Xing is doing many "right things" (instant retail, overseas expansion), but he has also done "wrong things" (Youxuan lost RMB 70 billion over 7 years). Duan Yongping needs more evidence of "doing the right things" before he feels reassured.

🔴 Question three: Does this business have a "margin of safety"?

Duan Yongping said:"A good business is worth buying when the price is right."

Meituan now trades at HKD 78.35, down 83% from its high of 460. PS is about 1.3x, PB about 1.4x, and FCF yield reached an striking 10.6% in 2024. These numbers look cheap.

But here is the problem—Meituan posted a loss in 2025. If the 2025 loss carries into 2026, then the current PE is negative and FCF is also negative. You could call it a "value trap" — it looks cheap, but it is actually destroying value.

When Duan Yongping bought Tencent, Tencent was already buying back stock and profits were recovering. When he bought Apple, Apple's ecosystem was already rock solid. When he bought Moutai, Moutai was still raising prices. Meituan's "margin of safety" rests on the assumption that "the competition will end within 1-2 years" — an assumption Duan Yongping would not accept.

Conclusion: If the competitive landscape holds, today's price is low but offers no margin of safety—because profits are disappearing.

V. Would Duan Yongping buy Meituan? A point-by-point review of the investment decision framework

A business you can understand? 50%
Meituan's business logic is not complicated. Duan Yongping would certainly "understand" food delivery, in-store services, and hotel and travel. But what he cannot understand is—When will this competition end? What will the landscape look like afterwards? Can Meituan still restore a net margin of 10%+?If the market is still a three-way standoff with low margins three years from now, Meituan is not a good business. That uncertainty turns "understandable" into "only half-understood."
A good price? 50%
PS 1.3x and PB 1.4x look cheap in absolute terms. But given a loss of RMB 23.4 billion in 2025 and likely continued losses in 2026, the current "cheapness" rests on past (2024) profits. Duan Yongping looks at "discounted future cash flows," not "past PE," when buying. If the company does not make money over the next 2-3 years, the current price is not actually cheap.
Good management? 65%
Wang Xing is a capable founder, and Duan Yongping has publicly said he "quite likes Wang Xing." But Meituan Select's RMB 70 billion loss is a major blemish. When Duan Yongping buys a company, he needs to "trust this management team for 10 years." Wang Xing's misjudgment on Meituan Select will affect Duan Yongping's long-term confidence in him.
Duan Yongping's circle of competence? 40%
This is the most critical point. The area Duan Yongping knows best is:Consumer electronics (Apple, OPPO/vivo affiliates), internet social (Tencent), premium consumer goods (Moutai), e-commerce (Pinduoduo, Alibaba). Local life services plus food delivery—he has never publicly claimed to have done in-depth research on these.

He said plainly on Xueqiu: "I don't understand Meituan, I don't know whether the stock price is high or low, and I've never bought it." Duan Yongping will absolutely not buy a company he "doesn't understand"—no matter how cheap the price.
⚖️ Final verdict: would Duan Yongping buy Meituan?

Short term (within 1 year): will not buy. ❌

The competitive landscape is too chaotic and the profit trend is downward; this is outside my circle of competence. Duan Yongping would not make a move at this point.

Medium term (1-3 years): may pay attention. Wait and see

If the subsidy war between JD.com and Alibaba begins to contract, the competitive landscape stabilizes and Meituan returns to profit, Duan Yongping may revisit his research. But he first needs to "understand" the business — including meeting Wang Xing in person and studying in depth how deep the moat around its delivery network runs.

Long term (3-5 years): possible, but with conditions. Depends on the competitive landscape

If Meituan can still hold 60%+ delivery market share once the competition ends, restore net margin to 8%-10%, keep free cash flow stable at USD 5 billion+, and see PS fall back below 1.5x — if all these conditions are met, Duan Yongping might add it to his "buyable" list. But he would most likely take only a "small position" rather than a heavy bet as he did with Tencent.

Duan Yongping would most likely say:
"Meituan is a good company, but not a good company at a good price. Or rather, this price may be a good price, but I cannot see clearly what the next three years will look like.

Wang Xing is a good CEO, but Meituan's business is too "grueling"—competitors are everywhere, and anyone can step in and pick a fight. With a business like this, there is no rush to look at it until the fighting is over.

I have said before, "don't do what you don't understand." I still don't "understand" Meituan, so I won't do it.
