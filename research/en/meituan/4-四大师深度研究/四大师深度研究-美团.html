Four masters' perspectives

BuffettPerspective

Buffett would see Meituan as a"Consumer monopoly" platform companies. His core question: does Meituan have a "durable competitive advantage (wide moat)"? Fundamentally, Meituan connects hundreds of millions of consumers with millions of merchants, creating a powerful two-sided network effect. Food delivery is a high-frequency necessity—"people have to eat every day"—and that demand does not disappear with the economic cycle. But Buffett would also note cautiously that Meituan is only at the start of a "profit inflection point," having achieved large-scale profitability for the first time only in 2024 (net profit USD 4.98 billion), and its track record is not yet long enough. He might say: "I don't like investing in companies still proving they can sustain profits, but I like this business model."

"Buying a great company at a fair price is far better than buying a mediocre company at a cheap price." Is Meituan a "great company"? Buffett would examine the durability of its moat—not just its current growth rate.

MungerPerspective

Munger has long been an Alibaba investor (through Daily Journal), and he has a deep understanding of the Chinese internet. As for Meituan, Munger would particularly note its"Scale is the moat"characteristics. Munger once said: "If you have a scale advantage, you have a huge competitive advantage." Meituan holds roughly 65-70% market share in China's food delivery market, with daily orders exceeding 50 million; this scale effect makes it nearly impossible for latecomers to challenge its position without incurring astronomical losses. Munger would also appreciate Meituan's culture of "rationality" — Wang Xing is known for data-driven, rational decision-making, which aligns with the value of "rationality" that Munger champions. Munger might say: "Meituan has a very simple business — people eat, they deliver. But behind that simplicity is an extremely complex operating system, and that is the moat."

Duan YongpingPerspective

The core of Duan Yongping's investment philosophy is "a good business model + a good corporate culture + a reasonable price." He would ask: is Meituan a "good business"? Duan Yongping's preferred business model is "product-type" (such as Apple, Moutai) rather than "platform-type." He believes product-type businesses more easily build brand premiums and user loyalty, while platform-type businesses face more intense price competition. For Meituan, Duan Yongping would acknowledge its scale effects and user stickiness, but would also question its"Pricing power"— Users are sensitive to delivery fees and merchants to commission rates, which shows Meituan lacks the pricing power of Moutai or Apple. He might say: "Meituan is a good company, but not the best business. A good business should have a wide moat and strong pricing power." Still, he acknowledges Wang Xing's managerial ability and Meituan's execution.

Li LuPerspective

As a leading figure in Chinese value investing, Li Lu would understand Meituan more deeply in the context of China's consumer modernization. He would view Meituan asThe "infrastructure" of China's services e-commerce— Like Alibaba and JD.com in physical-goods e-commerce, Meituan is defining a category that did not previously exist: instant local life services. Li Lu focuses on structural change: Chinese household consumption is only about 38% of GDP, far below 68% in the US, implying substantial long-term growth headroom. Meituan is a core beneficiary of China's consumption upgrading and modernisation. He has said: "China is undergoing the largest industrialisation and urbanisation process in human history, which creates countless investment opportunities." Meituan is the most representative services-consumption platform in that process. Li Lu's patience and long-term perspective let him tolerate short-term uncertainty, valuing instead the certain trends over 10 years and beyond.

II. Industry analysis

Industry overview

Meituan operates in China's local life services industry, one of the country's largest and fastest-growing consumer sectors. China's restaurant market totaled about RMB 5.7 trillion in 2025, while the in-store services market (hotels, travel, leisure and entertainment) exceeded RMB 4 trillion. The instant delivery industry is expanding from food to "everything to the door," continuously widening the addressable market.

Perspectives of four masters

BuffettPerspective — "Eating" is an evergreen industry

Buffett has repeatedly stressed investing in "things people can't live without." Local life services—especially food and dining—are an absolute necessity. Even in a recession, people do not stop eating. But Buffett would focus on the industry'sCompetitive structure: Is it a "good industry"? After aggressive subsidy competition in the US food delivery market, DoorDash has taken a dominant position, but margins remain thin. Competition in China is fiercer: Alibaba (Ele.me) keeps investing on the strength of its e-commerce ecosystem, and JD.com is also stepping up in instant retail. Buffett would ask: "How high can the return on capital in this industry be over the long term?" He might conclude that local life services are a"Essential but highly competitive"Only players with the strongest operating efficiency can earn excess returns in this industry.

MungerPerspective — "the ultimate expression of scale advantage"

Munger would"Economies of scale sharing"Analyze Meituan's industry position from this angle. The data generated by Meituan's 50 million+ daily orders gives it a deeper understanding of user behavior, merchant operations and delivery efficiency than any competitor. This data advantage is a self-reinforcing flywheel: more orders → better dispatch algorithms → shorter delivery times → better user experience → more orders. For competitors, breaking this cycle would be extremely costly. Munger would cite his oft-repeated line: "Fish where the fish are." China's local life services market is large enough, and Meituan's lead is solid enough. But he would also caution: "Competition is always fiercer than you imagine," especially with capital giants like Alibaba and JD.com circling.

Duan YongpingPerspective — "High frequency vs low frequency, stickiness vs price"

Duan Yongping would take note of Meituan's business"High-frequency consumption"Attributes. He prefers products that users use every day—this builds strong brand recognition and user habits. Food delivery is one such case: once users are accustomed to ordering through Meituan, switching to Ele.me requires changing behavioral habits, and this switching cost is an important factor in his "moat" analysis. However, Duan Yongping would also point out that Chinese users'"Price sensitivity"far above US users, and some users may churn once subsidies stop. This contrasts sharply with Apple users' relative insensitivity to price. He might conclude: "High frequency is an important feature of a good business, but if users come only for the subsidies, then the business is not good enough."

Li LuPerspective — "The core track of China's consumer modernization"

Li Lu fromModernization processview of the local life services industry from a macro perspective. He argues that China is undergoing an economic transition from "manufacturing-driven" to "consumption-driven," and that services consumption as a share of household consumption will continue to rise. In international comparison, China's services consumption share (about 45%) is far below that of developed countries (60-70%), implying room for at least 10-15 percentage points of improvement. Meituan is precisely the leader in the digitalization of services consumption. What Li Lu focuses on is: does the industry have the characteristics of a "long slope with thick snow"? The answer is yes. From food delivery to fresh produce, from retail to pharmaceuticals, from hotels to travel, the market Meituan serves exceeds RMB 10 trillion, while penetration remains at an early stage. Li Lu firmly believes that, under the right structural judgment, short-term volatility is a long-term investor's friend.

III. In-depth analysis of the business model

The essence of the business model

Meituan is aTwo-sided (multilateral) platforms, connecting consumers, merchants and riders. Its core business model is: food delivery (high-frequency traffic driver) → in-store/hotel & travel (profit center) → Instashopping/Select (growth engine). This "high-frequency driving low-frequency" flywheel effect is the key to the business model.

Four masters' perspectives

BuffettPerspective — "Assessing the Moat Across Five Dimensions"

Buffett's conclusion is: Meituan possesses"Wide moat", but the depth and durability of the moat still need time to prove out. His particular concern: when growth slows, can Meituan convert its scale advantage into sustained returns on capital? The 2024 data gave a preliminary affirmative answer — the operating margin jumped from -2.6% to 10.9%, a signal that the moat has begun to "cash in."

MungerPerspective — "Moat Analysis by Thinking in Reverse"

Munger would useContrarian thinking:"What could make Meituan's moat disappear?" The likely answers: ① disruptive technology (e.g., drone delivery, but Meituan is investing in this itself); ② regulation that fundamentally changes the business model (e.g., forced breakup); ③ a competitor subsidizing at any cost (requiring sustained RMB 100 billion-level investment). Munger's judgment: these risks have a low probability of occurring, and even if they do, Meituan's scale and cash reserves (USD 26 billion) give it ample capacity to respond. Munger would particularly emphasize Meituan's"Economic moat"the "economies-of-scale sharing" feature — Meituan's high order density keeps its per-unit delivery cost far below any competitor's, and this cost advantage is sustainable.

Duan YongpingPerspective — "Good businesses vs. average businesses"

Duan Yongping's framework is concise and profound.The core marker of a "good business": (1) Strong brand and pricing power (Apple, Moutai); (2) extremely low user churn; (3) margins that keep improving as scale grows. Meituan partially satisfies these conditions: user stickiness is genuinely high, and margins are genuinely improving. But Duan Yongping would point out: Meituan still has to strike a difficult balance between subsidies and profit, and its pricing power is constrained on both sides by user price sensitivity and merchant bargaining power. He might say: "Meituan is a 'good business,' but not the 'best business.'" In his portfolio, he would allocate first to Apple, Moutai and Tencent; Meituan would be at best his "sixth or seventh choice."

Li LuPerspective — "The infrastructure nature of service e-commerce"

Li Lu would compare Meituan toThe "railway" of service e-commerce. Railway companies do not need to own every train and every piece of cargo on the rails; they only need to own the network itself. Meituan's core value is its "delivery capacity network"—connecting consumers (demand) with merchants (supply) in real time through a rider network. This infrastructure attribute produces several key characteristics: ① once built, replacement costs are extremely high; ② as network density increases, unit costs keep falling; ③ it can carry an expanding range of categories (from food delivery to instant retail to everything). Li Lu argues that Meituan's long-term value lies in the infrastructure it is building for a "30-minute life circle," and that the value of this infrastructure will keep growing with China's consumption upgrade. He says with confidence: "If you see Meituan as infrastructure for China's service consumption rather than as a food-delivery company, its value looks completely different."

Four masters' perspectives

BuffettPerspective — "From loss to profit, inflection point confirmed"

Buffett's financial analysis focuses on three core dimensions:Earnings sustainability, cash flow quality, return on capital。

Profitability: 2024 net profit was USD 4.98B, up 154% year on year — Meituan's first large-scale profit in its history. Buffett would view this turning point with caution: the main driver of the profit improvement was the release of scale effects in the food delivery business (gross margin rising from 23.7% to 38.4%), a sustainable structural improvement rather than a one-off factor. He would also note the slowdown to 9.4% in the first three quarters of 2025, which bears watching, though a lower base against 20%+ growth is normal.

Cash flow: 2024 operating cash flow was USD 7.95 billion (OCF/revenue = 16.9%), free cash flow USD 6.54 billion (FCF/revenue = 13.9%) — Buffett would place heavy weight on this. For him, free cash flow is the measure of a company's true earning power. USD 6.5 billion of free cash flow against a USD 62 billion market cap gives an FCF yield of about 10.6%, a notably attractive figure.

Balance sheet: Cash of $26.1B vs long-term debt of $5.9B, a net cash position — Buffett would be very pleased. This gives Meituan enormous financial flexibility to support long-term investment, withstand competitive pressure, and fund large-scale buybacks.

"Cash plus an excellent business is the best combination." Meituan's $26B cash reserve plus steadily improving profitability fits Buffett's aesthetic of "margin of safety plus growth."

MungerPerspective — "Operating leverage begins to release"

Munger would take noticeOperating leverage— How revenue growth on top of a fixed cost base drives disproportionate profit growth. Meituan's fixed costs (R&D, infrastructure, management systems) are relatively fixed, while revenue grew from USD 27.8 billion in 2021 to USD 47 billion in 2024 (+69%), and operating profit swung from -USD 3.6 billion to +USD 5.1 billion over the same period. This is the power of operating leverage. Munger would also note that SG&A expenses grew from USD 7.6 billion to USD 10.4 billion (+37%), far slower than revenue growth (+69%), indicatingScale effectsIt is working. He would say: "That is the mathematical charm of economies of scale—once you cross the break-even point, a growing share of every incremental dollar of revenue turns into profit."

Duan YongpingPerspective — "Earnings Quality and Sustainability"

Duan Yongping analyzes profitability in depth.Quality: (1) Profit source structure — profit improvement in the food delivery business is the core; new businesses (Select, grocery) are still burning cash, but losses are narrowing; (2) Non-recurring gains — other operating income of $392M in 2024, a small share, with profit growth driven mainly by the core business; (3) Cash flow coverage of profit — operating cash flow of $7.95B far exceeds net profit of $4.98B (coverage multiple 1.6x), indicating high earnings quality. Duan Yongping would be satisfied with these metrics, but his core question remains: "Can this profit level be sustained?" If competitors step up subsidies, must Meituan follow suit and thereby sacrifice profit? That is the uncertainty he worries about most.

Li LuPerspective — "Financial data confirms structural change"

Li Lu fromLong-term structural perspectiveLook at the financial data. Gross margin rose steadily from 23.7% in 2021 to 38.4% in 2024 — not a one-off event, but a structural trend of Meituan's improving scale effects and operating efficiency. The same trend shows up in operating cash flow (from negative to $7.9B) and free cash flow (from -$2B to $6.5B). What Li Lu focuses on is the direction of the trend, not the absolute level — as long as the structural trend holds (revenue growth, gross margin expansion, falling expense ratios), short-term quarterly volatility is not worth over-worrying about. He would also note that revenue in the first three quarters of 2025 was $37.9B (+9.4% YoY): growth has indeed slowed, but sustaining double-digit growth at this scale is itself a demonstration of capability.

BuffettPerspective — "Trust but Verify"

Buffett's assessment of management includes three core criteria: ①Rational(capital allocation capability); ②Candid(attitude toward shareholders); ③Resist industry inertia(no herd mentality). How does Wang Xing perform on these three dimensions?

Rational: Wang Xing is known for "long-term patience" and stayed relatively rational amid cash-burn competition—in 2023-2024 he explicitly shifted strategic focus from scale expansion to profitability improvement, a reflection of rational capital allocation. But Buffett would question: has Meituan overinvested in some new businesses (such as the large-scale cash burn of Meituan Select in community group buying)? Have these investments created value for shareholders?

Candid: Wang Xing communicates with investors relatively transparently, but he is not as skilled as some of the CEOs Buffett admires (such as Roberto Goizueta of Coca-Cola) at explaining complex businesses in simple language.

Resisting inertia: Wang Xing maintained strategic discipline under competitive pressure from Alibaba and avoided getting dragged into a bottomless price war—proof that he has the quality to resist industry inertia.

MungerPerspective — "Rationality is the highest quality"

What Munger valued most was"Rational"Munger said many times: "Rationality is a moral duty." He appreciated Wang Xing's rationality in several respects: (1) a data-driven decision-making style; (2) persisting in investing in infrastructure during loss-making years rather than sacrificing long-term competitiveness for short-term profit; (3) decisively shifting to profit-first in 2023-2024, demonstrating strong strategic flexibility. Munger would also note that Wang Xing is a Tsinghua graduate with a solid technical background, which lets him better understand technology-driven business models. Munger's Alibaba investment experience at Daily Journal gave him a deeper understanding of Chinese internet management — he would place Wang Xing in the "first tier" of Chinese internet CEOs.

Duan YongpingPerspective — "Corporate culture is the foundation of long-term competitiveness"

In Duan Yongping's investment philosophy, "a good corporate culture" matters as much as "a good business model." His core criterion for evaluating management is:"Benfen"(doing the right thing without being told). He would focus on: ① Meituan's values are "consumers first, merchants second, employees third, shareholders fourth" — highly consistent with Duan Yongping's "user-centric" principle; ② Is Wang Xing someone who embodies "benfen"? Duan Yongping has publicly praised Wang Xing as an "outstanding entrepreneur," but he would also question whether certain of Meituan's aggressive expansions deviate from "benfen" — for example, during the period of unchecked expansion in the community group-buying business. Duan Yongping also emphasizes that management must"Do the right thing"— Meituan decisively scaled back its cash-burning businesses once the profit inflection point arrived, which meets the standard of "doing the right thing".

Li LuPerspective — "Entrepreneurial Spirit and Long-Termism"

Li Lu believes the key to investing in Chinese companies is finding management that combines "entrepreneurial spirit with long-termism." Wang Xing stands out in this respect: from founding Meituan in 2010 to its 2018 listing and full profitability in 2024, he has remained focused on long-term goals. Li Lu also values management's"Learning ability"— Meituan evolved from group buying to food delivery to flash shopping to Select, proving the organization's capacity for continuous learning. Li Lu views the founder's stake and control over the company as positive factors—Wang Xing holds a sufficient stake (about 5-8%), aligning his interests closely with shareholders, while retaining enough control to execute a long-term strategy.

VI. Growth driver analysis

Core growth engine

Meituan's growth comes from four levels: ① profit release in the core food delivery business (efficiency gains + monetization rate improvement); ② margin expansion in in-store hotel and travel (deeper advertising monetization); ③ category expansion in instant retail (from food to "everything to the home"); ④ narrowing losses in new businesses (Select, grocery, etc.).

Perspectives of four masters

BuffettPerspective — "Predictability and Certainty"

Buffett's preferencePredictable growthrather than explosive growth. Meituan's food delivery business (about 60% of revenue) is highly predictable—China's restaurant market grows a steady 5-7% a year, and delivery penetration rises 2-3 percentage points annually. These two alone can support average annual growth of more than 10% in food delivery. Add margin expansion in the in-store business and category expansion in Instashopping, and Buffett's growth model would show a "steady but not explosive" growth path. He would ask: among these growth drivers, which areConfirmed, which ones areUncertainWhat is certain is the continued growth and rising penetration of food delivery; what is uncertain is whether community group buying can turn profitable and whether overseas expansion can succeed. Buffett prefers to put capital into the certain part.

MungerPerspective — "The mathematical power of compounding"

Munger is"Compounding"A firm believer in it. He would analyze the foundations of Meituan's compounding growth: ① revenue grew at a compound annual rate of about 25% from 2019 to 2024 — sustaining high growth on a high base; ② profitability crossed from losses to a 10.6% net margin; ③ users grew from about 300 million to nearly 700 million (China has about 1.1 billion internet users, leaving room for further penetration). Munger would use the "Lollapalooza effect" to analyze Meituan — multiple positive factors (scale effects, network effects, the data flywheel, brand mindshare) acting at once, producing non-linear growth outcomes. He might say: "Meituan is in a compounding-growth phase where multiple factors are firing simultaneously; once this effect starts, it can last a very long time."

Duan YongpingPerspective — "user value is the root of growth"

Duan Yongping's logic is simple and direct:Growth comes from creating value for usersMeituan's core value is "making it easier for users to get the goods and services they want." On that basis, the growth directions are: ① getting existing users to use it more frequently (from 2 times a week to 1 time a day); ② getting users to spend more (average order value from RMB 30 to RMB 50); ③ attracting more users (covering more cities and demographics). Duan Yongping would pay particular attention to"User satisfaction"— If Meituan cuts service quality to protect profit (for example, by extending delivery times or raising delivery fees), it erodes user value and ultimately harms long-term growth. He would likely advise Meituan: "Do not sacrifice user experience for short-term margins — that is the most important thing."

Li LuPerspective — "From food delivery to everything to the 30-minute living circle"

Li Lu views Meituan's growth as"The deepening of consumption modernization". Phase 1 (2015-2022): food delivery replaces "cooking at home/dining in"; Phase 2 (2023-2028): flash shopping replaces "supermarket shopping"; Phase 3 (2029+): everything-to-home replaces "all daily consumption that requires going out". This "substitution process" is essentially a structural shift in Chinese consumer behavior from "in-store" to "to-home". Li Lu judges that the ceiling of China's instant delivery market is not food delivery, but the entire daily consumer goods retail market—about RMB 15 trillion. Meituan has currently penetrated less than 5% of it, leaving huge long-term growth space. He says with confidence: "We are witnessing the birth of a brand-new consumption infrastructure, and Meituan is the builder and operator of this infrastructure."

BuffettPerspective — "The price is right, but how big is the margin of safety?"

Buffett's investment decision formula is:Price < intrinsic value × margin of safety coefficientHis valuation centers on "a bird in the hand" (the present value of future cash flows). We attempt to estimate Buffett's valuation framework:

  • Normalized earnings: 2024 net profit USD 4.98B; assuming 10% growth in 2025 to ~USD 5.5B
  • Free cash flow: USD 6.5B, FCF/net profit = 1.3x, high quality
  • Growth rate assumptions: Net profit CAGR of 15% over the next 5 years (conservative), then 8% over the following 5 years
  • Discount rate：10%
  • Intrinsic value estimate: About USD 80-120B (market cap USD 62B, 20-50% upside)

What Buffett would make of current valuationsReasonably low, but the margin of safety is not wide enough—he wants a discount of more than 30% before building a heavy position. The current share price of HKD 78.35 has rebounded 23% from the 52-week low of HKD 63.65, but remains far below the 52-week high of HKD 136.10. If the share price pulls back to the HKD 65-70 range, the margin of safety would improve markedly. He would also consider: if there are too many uncertainties, better to miss out than to buy wrong.

"For a company you are certain is a good business, a fair price is a good price." The question is whether Buffett's "conviction" in Meituan is high enough. Based on available information, he is likely at the stage of "interested but needs more evidence."

MungerPerspective — "Looks undervalued"

Munger may be more optimistic than Buffett. He would see value from several angles:

  • PE 12-15xFor a company still growing revenue (10%+), with profitability now coming through and in a net cash position, it is clearly undervalued
  • PS 1.3xIt is even cheaper for platform companies — Alibaba trades at about 2x PS, Tencent at about 6x PS
  • FCF Yield 10.6%—In his eyes, this is a "very high-certainty rate of return"
  • Net cash USD 20 billion— equal to 32% of market cap; ex-cash, the effective PE is below 10x

Munger is famous for "cheap is the hard truth." He might say: "If you can find a company with a strong moat, piles of cash on the books, reliable management, and a PE of only 12x, you should buy it without hesitation." He also lived through the Alibaba investment and may have a clearer-eyed view of the risks in China ADRs, but Meituan's valuation is clearly more attractive than Alibaba's (when he bought it).

Duan YongpingPerspective — "reasonably priced, but not the best opportunity"

Duan Yongping's valuation approach is more qualitative. He focuses on"Does the price match the quality of the business?"For Meituan, his judgment might be: a PE of 12-15x is reasonable for a company growing over 10% annually with a 10.6% FCF yield, but given that its business model is inferior to Apple's or Moutai's, this price can only be called "reasonable" rather than "undervalued." He would draw comparisons: Apple trades at about 30x PE but has extremely strong pricing power, Moutai at about 25x PE carries luxury-brand attributes, and Tencent at about 20x PE holds a social-network monopoly. At the same price level, he would rather buy more Apple than buy Meituan. But if you asked him "What should Meituan be worth?" he might say: "USD 80-100 billion is reasonable." That implies 30-60% upside from the current price—but he probably would not build a heavy position just for that gain, because "reasonable" does not equal "optimal."

Li LuPerspective — "the ideal vehicle for long-term value investors"

Li Lu's valuation framework focuses least on the current PE multiple and most onIntrinsic value in 10 yearsHe was thinking:

  • What will Meituan's revenue reach in 10 years? Assuming a 12% CAGR, revenue in 10 years would be about $150B
  • What net margin can be reached in 10 years? International peer platforms (such as DoorDash and Booking) have net margins of 15-25%; a conservative estimate is 15%
  • Net profit of about $22.5B after 10 years, applying a 20x PE (reasonable valuation for a growth stock), gives a market cap of $450B
  • That corresponds to an annualized return of about 22%—even halved, it still delivers 10%+ annualized.

For Li Lu, the current USD 62 billion market cap offers a sufficient margin of safety. Even if every assumption is halved (5% growth, 7.5% net margin, 10x PE), market cap reaches USD 150 billion after 10 years, still a 9% annualized return. This is an investment opportunity with "limited downside and enormous upside." He might say: "At the current price, Meituan is an ideal vehicle for long-term value investors. You already have the margin of safety, while also participating in the long-term growth of Chinese consumption modernization."
