Meituan In-Depth Analysis: From a RMB 36.4 billion loss to a RMB 49.8 billion profit — why is this "food delivery stock" undervalued?

Deep research · Data-driven · Independent thinking

If a company could go from an annual loss of USD 3.65 billion to an annual profit of USD 4.98 billion in four years—a swing of USD 8.6 billion (about HKD 67 billion) from trough to peak—while its market cap halved from its high, would you not suspect something is off?

Meituan, 3690.HK, is right now playing out exactly this kind of "fire and ice" script.

On July 7, 2026, Meituan closed at HKD 78.35, down 42% from its 52-week high of HKD 136.10. Yet on the other side, its net profit swung from -USD 3.65 billion in 2021 to +USD 4.98 billion in 2024. Gross margin climbed from 23.7% to 38.4%, and free cash flow turned from -USD 2 billion to +USD 6.5 billion—nearly 10% of market cap in free cash generated each year.

This article does not recommend stocks or make bullish calls. We use data and logic to take the company apart, inside and out.

I. From "cash-burning machine" to "money printer"

To understand Meituan's investment story, you first have to understand what its income statement has been through.

Meituan net profit trend (USD million):
2021: -3,648 (a loss of RMB 3.65 billion)
2022: -993 (a loss of RMB 0.99 billion)
2023: +1,958 (profit RMB 1.96 billion)
2024: +4,981 (profit RMB 4.98 billion)

In four years, net margin went from -13.1% to +10.6%, spanning the full cycle from loss to profit. More importantly, this profit did not come from asset sales or accounting adjustments, but from real operating profit.

In 2024, Meituan posted operating profit of USD 5.125 billion, an operating margin of 10.9%. Two years earlier, in 2022, the figure was -USD 865 million (-2.6%). From -USD 865 million to +USD 5.125 billion — a USD 6 billion swing across two fiscal years — a textbook turnaround even by global internet industry standards.

What is the core driver behind this turnaround? Three words:Scale effects。

Meituan's food delivery business is a classic "two-sided network plus economies of scale" model. More riders mean faster delivery; more merchants mean richer choice; more users mean higher order density—and as order density rises, delivery cost per order falls. From 2021 to 2024, Meituan's revenue grew from USD 27.77 billion to USD 46.96 billion, up 69%. Over the same period, operating costs rose from USD 21.18 billion to USD 28.91 billion, up only 36%. Cost growth was just over half of revenue growth—a direct manifestation of the scale effect.

Economics has a concept called "operating leverage" — once fixed costs are spread across a growing volume of orders, each additional unit of revenue converts into profit at a higher rate. Meituan is in exactly this sweet spot.

II. Growth has slowed, but quality is improving

Look past profit to growth, and the picture shifts subtly.

Meituan revenue growth trend:
2022: +17.7%
2023: +19.6%
2024: +20.1%
2025 first three quarters (estimated): approximately +9.4%

The 20% growth rate came to an abrupt halt in 2024. In the first three quarters of 2025, Meituan's revenue was approximately USD 37.9 billion, with year-on-year growth sliding to single digits. This is an unavoidable signal of deceleration.

The market has drawn two sharply different readings of this:

The bearsSome say: food delivery penetration has peaked, in-store hotel and travel is under siege from Douyin, community group buying keeps burning cash — Meituan has hit its growth ceiling.

The optimistsLook at quality, not just growth. Although the first three quarters of 2025 delivered only 9.4% growth, that 9.4% comes on a much larger base—on a revenue scale of USD 37.9 billion, each 1% of growth equals USD 3.8 billion. And if margins are still rising while growth slows, the absolute amount of "value creation" is actually getting larger.

The financials reveal an interesting structural shift: in 2024, Meituan's SG&A expense (selling and administrative) was USD 10.39 billion, or 22.1% of revenue. In 2021, that ratio was 27.5%. Marketing efficiency is improving, indicating the company no longer needs to "burn cash for growth" to acquire users, but instead retains existing users through service quality. This shift matters more than short-term growth figures.

III. Cash piles up, free cash flow yield above 10%

If the income statement is Meituan's "face," the cash flow statement is its "substance."

Meituan cash flow overview (USD million):
Operating cash flow: 7,949 (2024)
Capital expenditure: -1,405
Free cash flow: +6,544
Free cash flow yield (FCF Yield): ~10.6%

What does a free cash flow yield of 10.6% mean? Within the Hong Kong tech sector, that figure ranks near the top. Tencent's FCF yield currently sits at around 3-4%, and Alibaba's at around 5-6%. Meituan's figure is almost two to three times that of its peers.

Now look at the balance sheet: as of the end of 2024, Meituan held USD 26.12 billion in cash and equivalents and only USD 5.89 billion in long-term debt—over USD 20 billion in net cash. And the market cap? About USD 62 billion. That means, after deducting net cash, Meituan's "operating business" is effectively worth only USD 42 billion.

USD 42 billion corresponds to a business with nearly USD 5 billion in annual profit and USD 6.5 billion in annual free cash flow. That is an actual PE of under 9x and an FCF yield above 15%.

It is like a prime-location storefront where nearly half of the price you pay is the cash in the safe, and the storefront itself is essentially free.

IV. The "triple shadow" behind the low valuation

But the market is smart. Meituan's low valuation naturally has its reasons.

The first shadow: deteriorating competitive landscape.Alibaba's Ele.me, Amap and Sun Art Retail are forming an "iron triangle" in local life services; JD.com made a high-profile entry into the food delivery market in 2024, touting "zero commission" merchant recruitment; Douyin's in-store group buying keeps pressure on Meituan's in-store business. Attacked on three fronts, how wide is Meituan's moat?

The second shadow: the cloud of negative EPS.According to Google Finance data, Meituan's current EPS is -HKD 7.64, which conflicts with its full-year 2024 profit of USD 4.98 billion. This negative figure likely includes a non-recurring loss from one quarter of 2025 (such as a large impairment on community group buying), but in fundamental analysis, a negative EPS will scare off many quantitative stock-selection models and passive investors.

The third shadow: systemic bias in the Hong Kong stock market.Chinese ADRs have retraced from their 2021 peak, and market sentiment still assigns a high risk premium to the Hong Kong tech sector as a whole. Combined with China-US geopolitical uncertainty, Meituan's valuation discount is not just company-specific but market-wide.

Each of these three shadows deserves serious attention. Investing is not about looking only at the bright side.

V. If Meituan Were a Bond, It Would Be a High-Yield Bond

Here is an interesting analogy.

If we treat Meituan as a "bond," its coupon is the free cash flow yield — 10.6%. The competitive and market risks it faces are its credit risk. For comparison: the yield on China's 10-year government bond is about 2.5%, so the yield on this Meituan "bond" is more than 4 times that of the government bond.

The question is: will Meituan's "credit" default? That is, will its profitability slump sharply or even return to losses?

The answer hinges on two variables: how deep the delivery moat really is, and whether losses in new businesses (Meituan Select, Instashopping) spiral out of control.

Food delivery's moat runs deeper than most people imagine. Not because of technological barriers, but because of an "operational complexity barrier": delivering tens of millions of orders a day requires simultaneously dispatching hundreds of thousands of riders and matching the location data of millions of merchants and hundreds of millions of users. The scheduling efficiency and delivery cost of this system cannot be replicated by burning subsidies. JD.com and Ele.me can poach a few executives, but they cannot poach the scheduling-algorithm optimization experience accumulated over tens of millions of deliveries a day.

As for new businesses, one notable detail in the 2024 financial report: Meituan's "other operating income" line posted a positive gain of USD 392 million, and USD 765 million in 2023. This means losses at new businesses such as Meituan Select are narrowing rapidly. Management also repeatedly mentioned on the earnings call a strategic shift toward "loss reduction ahead of growth."

VI. Bottom-line thinking: a safety cushion below, upside waiting to be triggered

Investing, at its core, is about finding asymmetry within uncertainty—an odds structure where downside is limited and upside is substantial.

Where is Meituan's downside cushion?

First, net cash of USD 20 billion, one third of market cap. Even if the business shrinks, this cash reserve is enough to fund years of transition. Second, on-demand delivery is a countercyclical service — the worse the economy, the more people cook at home and order takeout. Third, Wang Xing's management ability as a serial entrepreneur has been proven over the past decade.

Where are Meituan's upward catalysts?

First, if margins expand further once the competitive landscape stabilizes (moving from 10% toward 15-20%), a doubling of profit would correspond to a doubling of the share price. Second, Meituan Instashopping (30-minute delivery to the door) is expanding from fresh produce into higher-ticket categories such as mobile phones, digital devices and beauty products, which could be the next trillion-level growth engine. Third, if sentiment in the Hong Kong stock market recovers and the valuation re-rates from single-digit PE to the 15-20x normal for tech stocks, combined with profit growth, the upside from a Davis double-play would be considerable.

VII. Risk factors

Finally, the risks must be addressed. Each of the following deserves serious weight:

1. Competition intensifies beyond the consensus.If JD.com, Alibaba and Douyin launch a new round of RMB 10 billion-scale subsidy wars, Meituan's profit could be heavily eroded. SG&A expenses already reached USD 10.4 billion in 2024; there is still room to push them higher, but doing so would severely pressure margins.

2. Regulatory policy uncertainty.Social insurance for delivery riders, algorithm compliance and data security: tighter regulation in any one of these areas could raise the cost structure.

3. Revenue growth continues to decline.If full-year 2025 growth falls below 5%, the market may reprice Meituan's growth premium, treating it as a "value trap" rather than a "value opportunity."

4. Geopolitical risk.ADR delisting risk for China concept stocks and the structural shrinkage of Hong Kong stock liquidity are macro factors that may weigh on valuations for a long time to come.

5. Negative EPS acts as a technical drag.The current EPS of -HKD 7.64 will continue to weigh on allocation weights among index funds and quantitative strategies, creating persistent passive selling pressure.

In closing

Is Meituan a good company? Most likely yes. Is it a good company at a good price? That question you need to answer yourself.

At HKD 78, Meituan holds USD 20 billion in cash, earns USD 5 billion a year and is still growing. Its moat is widening (operating efficiency keeps improving), and its moat is being eroded (competitors on every front). This is the most agonizing "gray rhino" — every piece of good news you see stands next to a piece of bad news.

But the beauty of investing lies precisely in this:Without disagreement, there is no discount; without uncertainty, there is no excess return.

The Meituan story is not a "buy or not buy" story; it is a story about how large a discount you are willing to pay for how much uncertainty. The data has already laid its cards on the table—what remains is your eye for reading them and your steadiness in holding them.
