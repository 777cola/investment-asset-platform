1.1 🔗 Network effects — the strongest moat (Score: 9.5/10)

Core thesis:Meituan operates China's largest three-party platform connecting riders, merchants and consumers, with a classic two-sided (in practice three-sided) network-effect flywheel.

Three-party network flywheel mechanism:

More consumers →More order volume→Higher delivery density→Lower delivery costs, faster delivery times→ More high-quality merchants onboarded →Wider selection of products and dishes→ More consumers(cycle)

Rider-side scale effect:More orders mean shorter average delivery distance per rider, more deliveries per unit time and steadier rider income → attracts more riders to join → ample delivery capacity → better consumer experience.

Merchant-side agglomeration effect:More consumers drive growth in merchants' online order volume; merchants are willing to commit more resources to online operations and advertising → Meituan's advertising revenue grows → more capital flows into R&D and subsidies → consumer-side experience improves.

Data support:Meituan had about 600 million+ annual active consumers, about 7 million active merchants and about 60 million instant-delivery orders per day in 2024. At this scale of network effect, any new entrant would need to invest tens of billions of US dollars just to build an initial foothold. Ele.me, backed by more than 10 years of Alibaba investment, still holds only ~25% food-delivery share, which confirms the defensive strength of Meituan's network effect.

1.2 📊 Economies of scale — delivery density economics (Score: 9.0/10)

Core thesis:On-demand delivery is a textbook "density economy" industry — per-order delivery cost is directly inversely correlated with order density.

The mathematics of density economics:Consider a 3-square-kilometer area with 100 orders per day versus 1,000. In the former, riders wait a long time to batch orders and travel long distances round-trip, putting per-order delivery cost at about RMB 7-8; in the latter, minute-level smart order batching and route optimization bring per-order cost down to RMB 4-5. At Meituan's scale of 60 million orders per day, order density in core commercial districts of top-tier cities can reach 3-5x that of competitors.

The new entrant's dilemma:New entrants such as JD Hourly Delivery and Douyin Waimai face a "low-density trap" in their early stages: low order volume leads to high delivery costs, which require larger subsidies to attract users; once subsidies fall, users churn, and the business never reaches breakeven. JD restarted instant retail in 2024 through "JD Miaosong," but its delivery team Dada handles fewer than one-tenth of Meituan's average daily orders, with per-order costs at least 30-50% higher.

💡 Investment takeaway:Meituan's 2024 delivery-related costs were USD 28.9 billion (about RMB 208 billion). If its per-order cost runs RMB 2 lower than competitors' on density advantages, the annualized cost advantage reaches about RMB 40 billion. That structural cost edge is the most powerful weapon in a subsidy war — Meituan can sustain the same subsidy intensity at a lower absolute cost.

1.3 🔐 Switching costs — moderately strong (score: 7.0/10)

Consumer side (moderate stickiness):

  • Habit stickiness:Meituan's membership system (monthly passes/coupons) creates some lock-in: after buying a monthly pass, users tend to order through Meituan to "get their money's worth." Overall, however, switching costs between food-delivery apps are low, and differences in subsidies can easily prompt users to migrate.
  • Dianping stickiness:Reviews written on Dianping, saved stores, and VIP tiers create emotional and practical switching costs. Dianping's UGC content ecosystem is distinctive; competitors such as Douyin struggle to replicate the depth and credibility of its reviews.

Merchant side (higher stickiness):

  • Merchants' operational assets invested in Meituan—storefront design, menu design, review management, advertising placement—are non-transferable.
  • Meituan's SaaS tools for merchants (Meituan Butler, Meituan Restaurant System) are deeply embedded in merchants' daily operations, making switching costs high.
  • Merchants, however, are broadly dissatisfied with Meituan's commission rates (typically 18-26% for food and beverage) and are keen to operate across multiple platforms (Douyin group buying, Ele.me), so stickiness to a single platform is weakening.

Rider side (low stickiness):

  • Riders typically take orders across multiple platforms at once and show almost no loyalty to any single platform. Meituan's advantage lies in higher order volume: riders earn more on Meituan and therefore choose to stay.
  • This is "structural loyalty" rather than "emotional loyalty"—once Meituan's order volume falls, riders will quickly move to other platforms.

1.4 🏷️ Brand moat — moderate (score: 6.5/10)

Dianping–Meituan dual-brand matrix:

  • Dianping:China's leading local-services consumption decision gateway, with IPs such as the "Must-Eat List" and "Black Pearl" carrying strong brand pull. Dianping's UGC content ecosystem has built up over more than 15 years, and content platforms such as Douyin will struggle to replicate its content authenticity and trust in the short term.
  • Meituan:The brand's core perception is "cheap, convenient, has everything," giving it strong utility attributes but little emotional brand premium. Compared with Pinduoduo's "great value" and Douyin's "fun" brand images, Meituan's emotional connection with users is relatively weak.
💡 Brand monetization capability:Meituan's annualized advertising revenue from merchants in its in-store business has exceeded RMB 40 billion, reflecting the value of brand traffic. But Douyin is carving into in-store advertising budgets through content-driven recommendations (short video plus livestream group buying); in 2024 Douyin's in-store GMV already exceeded half of Meituan's in-store GMV.

1.5 🤖 Data/technology moat — Strong (score: 8.5/10)

Core thesis:Meituan's AI delivery dispatch system is one of the most complex real-time operations-research optimization systems in China, and its integration with the LongCat-2.0 large model forms a distinctive technical moat.

AI delivery dispatch system:

  • Meituan's self-developed "Super Brain" dispatch system handles real-time routing, rider matching, smart order batching and estimated time of arrival (ETA) prediction for 60 million daily orders.
  • The system must simultaneously optimize dozens of variables: consumer wait times, rider income fairness, merchant food-preparation times, real-time traffic conditions, weather factors and more.
  • LongCat-2.0 large model upgraded in 2025: uses large models to improve natural-language customer service, intelligent recommendations, meal preparation time prediction, rider voice interaction and other scenarios, expected to raise delivery efficiency by 5-10%.

Multiplier effect of data accumulation:

  • Meituan has accumulated more than 10 years of delivery data, merchant operations data, consumer behavior data and traffic conditions data.
  • This data is the core fuel for training AI models. Competitors, especially Ele.me, are constrained in algorithm optimization because their smaller order volumes leave data quality and diversity insufficient.
Data facts:Meituan's 2024 R&D expense was USD 2,929M (about RMB 21 billion), 6.2% of revenue, mid-to-high among technology platforms. Meituan leads in China and globally in AI algorithm talent pool and patent count in the instant delivery space.

🥇 Ele.me (Alibaba) — the most enduring competitor

Background:Fully acquired by Alibaba in 2018 (USD 9.5 billion); in 2024 Alibaba carried out a strategic restructuring of Ele.me, further integrating it into Alibaba's local services group (including Amap, Fliggy and others).

  • Advantages:Backed by the Alibaba ecosystem (Taobao/Alipay traffic gateways, Cainiao logistics network, Alibaba Cloud technology), it has penetration potential in lower-tier cities, and Alibaba's entry into instant retail (Hema/Tmall Supermarket) can create synergies with Ele.me.
  • Disadvantages:Delivery share has long stagnated at ~25%, with cumulative losses exceeding RMB 50 billion over the past decade, and a muddled brand identity ("Ele.me" = delivery, "Koubei" = in-store, but both are weaker than Meituan).   • Strategic evolution:After 2025, Alibaba adjusted its strategy for Ele.me to "limited investment, focus on profitability," no longer pursuing market-share expansion and concentrating instead on UE improvement and synergy value within the Alibaba ecosystem.
Assessment:The Ele.me threat is fading. Although Alibaba's local-life group narrowed its losses in 2024, they remain at the RMB 10 billion level, and Alibaba's core e-commerce business faces fierce competition from Pinduoduo and Douyin, making it hard to keep funding Ele.me's subsidy war.

🥈 Douyin Local Life — the most threatening disruptor

Background:It launched its local services business in 2021, pushed hard on it in 2023, and by 2024 its group-buying plus food-delivery GMV exceeded RMB 400 billion, making it the fastest-growing local services platform of the past three years.

  • Advantages:More than 600 million daily active users (one of China's largest traffic pools); content-driven recommendations (short video plus livestreaming) showcase products far better than text-and-image reviews; a short user decision path (see, desire, order); and penetration in lower-tier cities far exceeds Meituan's.
  • Disadvantage:Weak food-delivery capability (currently reliant mainly on third-party capacity or merchant self-delivery), unstable fulfillment experience and an order completion rate below Meituan's; Douyin users mainly "scroll content" rather than actively search for stores, so that mindset has yet to take hold; and shallower local-merchant operations and service capabilities than Meituan.
  • Strategic direction:In 2025, Douyin's strategy shifted from "expanding market share" to "improving fulfillment quality," but building its own delivery network requires massive investment and years of accumulation. Douyin is more likely to pursue a "platform model" (traffic matching only, with delivery outsourced) rather than an "asset-heavy self-operated model."
⚠️ Biggest threat:Douyin's in-store group-buying business is materially eroding Meituan's profit base (in-store is Meituan's profit center, contributing roughly 40-50% of its operating profit). If Douyin pushes further into food delivery and solves the delivery problem, it would pose a fundamental challenge to Meituan. For now, however, the capital and time barriers to building an in-city delivery network from scratch are extremely high.

🥉 JD Hourly Purchase / Miaosong — a head-on competitor in instant retail

Background:In 2024, JD.com elevated instant retail to a Tier-1 strategic business, integrated Dada Group (controlled by JD.com), and launched "JD Instant Delivery," a 30-minute delivery service.

  • Advantages:JD.com's supply-chain advantages in 3C, home appliances and general merchandise (authenticity guarantees, brand partnerships, warehouse network), JD Logistics' intra-city delivery capability (Dada's 1.3 million active riders), and the high spending power of JD.com's users.
  • Disadvantage:Food delivery is largely absent (JD.com has no strategic plan to enter food delivery), instant retail order volume is far below Meituan Instashopping, and JD.com's supply-chain advantage in the supermarket and convenience category is less pronounced than imagined (supermarkets in tier-1 cities already have mature O2O solutions).
  • Differentiated strategy:JD.com focuses on "quality instant retail"—high-ticket categories such as phones, computers, beauty and maternal products—avoiding Meituan Instashopping's main battleground of fresh groceries and supermarkets. But in early 2025 JD.com began raising subsidies on supermarket categories, competing head-on with Meituan.
Assessment:JD.com is the most closely watched competitor in the instant retail race. Its brand trust and supply-chain capabilities in the 3C/home appliance category are several times those of Meituan. But JD.com lacks the high-frequency consumption scenario that food delivery provides—food delivery is the foundation of daily active users and delivery density, and an instant retail delivery network without high-frequency scenario support has weaker economics.

3.2 The moat's self-reinforcing mechanism

Meituan's biggest moat advantage lies in its self-reinforcing nature (Self-reinforcing moat):More orders lead to lower costs, which lead to a better experience, which leads to more users, which leads to more orders. This is a self-reinforcing feedback loop that runs continuously and is nearly irreversible in a steady-state market.

Key self-reinforcing path:

  1. Data flywheel:More orders → more accurate demand forecasting/delivery scheduling → higher delivery efficiency → higher user satisfaction → more orders.
  2. Merchant stickiness flywheel:More consumers → higher merchant online revenue → merchants willing to invest more resources (advertising, discounts, exclusive dishes) → more consumer choice → more consumers.
  3. Rider capacity flywheel:More orders → higher per-order rider income → adequate rider supply → stable delivery times → consumers willing to use the service → more orders.

3.3 Summary of Moat Vulnerability Assessment

Meituan's core moat—three-sided network effects and delivery-density economics—isNo material risk of disruption within a foreseeable 3-5 yearsThe biggest uncertainty comes from regulation and breakthrough innovation by Douyin.

  • Regulatory risk:If the Chinese government introduces policies similar to the EU's Digital Markets Act (DMA), requiring platforms to mandatorily open up data or restricting "self-preferencing," this could erode Meituan's moat. But the probability currently looks low.
  • Douyin risk:Douyin is the only competitor capable of bypassing Meituan's network effects through a "dimensional strike" — reshaping how local-life consumption decisions are made via its content ecosystem. The key question is whether Douyin can solve delivery fulfillment; if it cannot, Douyin can only erode the in-store business and cannot shake the core food-delivery base.

5.4 Final conclusion

💡 Investment view: Meituan holds one of the most defensible structural moats in China's internet sector.

The core logic is:"Delivery density economics plus three-party network effects" form a nearly impossible-to-replicate double safeguardEle.me, backed by Alibaba for 10 years, has failed to crack the core food-delivery base (share stuck at ~25%); Douyin succeeded in in-store services but struggles to enter food delivery (delivery-capacity bottleneck); JD.com competes on differentiation in instant retail but lacks high-frequency scenarios to support delivery density.

But Meituan's investment case has shifted from a "growth story" to a "value story" — at a PS of only ~1.3x and an FCF yield of ~10.6%, the market is already valuing it as a value stock. If Meituan can sustain roughly 10% revenue growth while continuing to lift margins and free cash flow, the current valuation offers a margin of safety.

Key metrics to watch:(1) The gap between in-store GMV growth and Douyin's; (2) whether food-delivery order growth holds steady (target: 10-15% annual growth); (3) whether instant retail (Shanbuy) can become a second growth engine; (4) changes in the intensity of the subsidy war.
