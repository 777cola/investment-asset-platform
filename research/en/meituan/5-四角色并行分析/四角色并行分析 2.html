Business analyst report

Duan Yongping's lens — business model · core competitiveness · pricing power

Meituan's core competitiveness

1. Flywheel effect of high-frequency consumption

Food delivery is a classic high-frequency, low-margin business. Meituan handles about 70 million orders a day, making it China's largest on-demand delivery network. High-frequency delivery demand brings a huge user base and daily app-open frequency, which naturally converts into a traffic source for higher-margin businesses such as in-store services, travel and hotel booking, and flash shopping.This is not simple "traffic monetization" but "service as channel"—every delivery is a chance to reach the user.

2. Scale barriers in instant-delivery infrastructure

Meituan has about 1 million active riders, covering 2,800 counties and districts nationwide. This delivery network cannot be replicated in the short term with capital alone: dispatch algorithms (AI optimized across hundreds of millions of orders), network density (per-order fulfillment cost falls as density rises), and merchant coverage (about 13 million active merchants). This forms a virtuous cycle: more merchants → more choices → more users → more orders → better delivery efficiency.

3. The "China uniqueness" of the local services ecosystem

No competitor in China matches Meituan's full-scenario coverage across food delivery, in-store services, travel and hospitality, instant retail and community group buying.

The essence of the business model

The answer isBoth, but at its core it is "retail infrastructure driven by high-frequency services"Meituan's food delivery business matches users with restaurants, but delivery fulfillment is an asset-heavy operation. Meituan Instashopping (30-minute delivery) is expanding its categories from food to supermarkets, pharmacies, daily necessities and electronics. In essence, this isA retail platform built on an instant-delivery network as infrastructure, similar to Amazon's FBA system, but focused on "30-minute delivery" rather than "next-day delivery".

Meituan vs Costco

Meituan

Revenue growth slows

From 20% to 10% — a red flag?
Buffett-style analysis: look at the base effect—at a $47B scale, a 10% growth rate produces an absolute increment ($4.7B) higher than 2021's 17% growth on $27.8B ($4.7B). Earnings growth > revenue growth is what matters—in 2024, revenue +20%, net profit +154%, FCF +36%. Revenue decelerates while profit accelerates—the mark of a good business.
Buffett-style summary:"The best businesses are those where investing $1 today produces far more than $1 in the future. Meituan spent 8 years losing money and investing, and starting in 2024 it has finally entered a payback period. $6.5B in free cash flow, $26B in cash reserves, 20% ROE—these numbers are real. The only thing that gives me pause is that Wang Xing has not yet shown a willingness to return cash to shareholders. I will keep watching."

In-depth analysis of the four major competitors

1. Alibaba local services (Ele.me + Amap + Koubei)

"Alibaba's investment in Ele.me is 'incompetent capital allocation.' It spent USD 9.5 billion on the acquisition and burned over RMB 10 billion in subsidies, only for market share to fall from 40% to 30%. This shows that:Capital accumulation without core competitiveness is waste."

Threat level: ⭐⭐Persistent losses, no structural competitive advantage

2. JD Daojia (Dada)

JD.com's Dada is a business "with a position but a low ceiling." Instant retail is a 30-minute delivery business, while JD.com's core brand is next-day delivery—two completely different consumer mindsets. JD.com Dada's scale is only one-tenth of Meituan's, and its delivery network density is an order of magnitude worse.Second place never makes money."

Threat level: ⭐⭐⭐Focused on instant retail, with some differentiation

3. Douyin local life services

Douyin's approach to local services is "traffic monetization thinking," fundamentally different from service-fulfillment thinking. Douyin's problem is:Without a delivery network, there is no moat.Short-video ROI is falling and traffic costs are rising. Douyin is "goods seeking people"; Meituan is "people seeking goods" — the two models cannot substitute for each other.

Threat level: ⭐⭐⭐⭐The threat in the in-store segment is real, but it has a ceiling

4. Ele.me

The problem with Ele.me is not a matter of money. Alibaba has already invested over RMB 100 billion, yet market share keeps falling. This shows that Ele.me hasStructural competitive disadvantage: Meituan was built by an independent startup team, while Ele.me was acquired—two completely different DNAs.

Threat level: ⭐⭐"Zombie competitors" — alive but not a threat

Has the scale effect reached a turning point?

Munger-style synthesis:The "sweet spot" of scale effects still has 3-5 years of runway. Meituan's scale effects are now shifting from "delivery cost decline" to "profit release." In 2024, FCF rose from $4.8B to $6.5B, driven not by a revenue surge (+20%) but by margin expansion (net margin from 5% to 10.6%).This indicates that scale effects are shifting from "burning cash for scale" to "trading scale for profit".

Risk Assessor Report

Li Lu's lens — regulation · competition · overseas · technology · key figures

Risk 1: Regulatory risk

Probability: medium-high | Impact: medium to high
The peak of the 2021 regulatory storm has passed, and the current policy environment has eased markedly. The remaining risks are mainly: rider social insurance costs (if 100% social insurance is mandated, an additional RMB 20-30 billion per year) and food delivery pricing controls. The probability of another antitrust fine is low.
Li Lu-style judgment:The period of greatest regulatory risk (2021-2023) is over. Meituan's biggest regulatory risk now is not "crackdown" but "cost" — rider social insurance and possible pricing controls will erode margins, but will not destroy the business model.

Risk 2: Competitive subsidy war

Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium to High
Probability of an all-out subsidy war: 15% (very high impact); probability of a localized subsidy war: 50% (medium impact); probability of subsidy cooling: 35% (low impact).
Li Lu-style judgment:"The subsidy war is Meituan's biggest short-term risk, but not a long-term one. Meituan's cash reserves are more than twice those of its competitors, its per-order unit economics are already profitable, while Ele.me still loses RMB 5-8 per order. Those burning cash can never catch up with those making money."

Risk 3: Uncertainty over overseas expansion

Probability: High (expansion decision) | Impact: Low to medium
Keeta's pilots in Hong Kong and the Middle East are tiny in scale (less than 1% of total revenue), so the near-term impact is limited.
A Li Lu-style judgment:"I would rather invest in a company that penetrates the Chinese market deeply and thoroughly than in one that wastes resources on overseas experiments. If Wang Xing decides to enter overseas markets at scale, I will reassess."

Risk 4: Technology substitution risk

Probability: Low | Impact: Medium
Advances in AI and drone delivery favor Meituan, which has already built out its capabilities, but pose a threat if competitors are first to cut costs substantially.

Risk five: key-person risk

Probability: Low to medium | Impact: Very high
Wang Xing holds about 10% of shares but 60% of voting rights, with no clear succession plan. If Wang Xing were to leave for any reason, the stock could plunge 30-50%!
A Li Lu-style judgment:"This is my biggest concern. Meituan is far too dependent on Wang Xing. Alibaba found Daniel Zhang after Jack Ma stepped back, and JD.com has been trying decentralized management since the Liu Qiangdong incident. Meituan has no such backup. If anything happens to Wang Xing, the investment thesis for Meituan would be severely undermined."

📌 Key monitoring indicators

The following metrics will determine whether Meituan's investment thesis holds:

  1. 2025 full-year revenue growth: If it holds at 8-12%, growth slows but remains manageable; if it falls below 5%, the growth narrative needs reassessment
  2. FCF/revenue ratio: If it keeps rising (toward 15-18%), it confirms economies of scale are materializing; if it falls, competitive pressure is intensifying
  3. Management capital allocationWill it launch a buyback? Will it pay a dividend? That will determine whether the market grants it a "mature platform" valuation premium
  4. Douyin local life GTV growth rate: If Douyin's in-store GTV growth stays above 50%, Meituan's in-store business will come under pressure
