I. Investment Thesis (Rationale for Buy)

Core thesis:Meituan is China's largest local life services platform and has built a hard-to-dislodge network-effect moat in food delivery. The profit inflection is confirmed, FCF is strong (USD 6.5 billion a year), yet the market still values it as tech retail rather than a high-growth platform (PS just 1.3x). As margins keep improving, new businesses narrow losses, and in-store/hotel-travel and instant shopping grow into a second curve, the re-rating upside is significant.


1. Extremely deep moat in food delivery— Daily orders exceed 60 million, covering 2,000+ cities, with over 9 million active merchants and over 7 million riders. Two-sided network effects (more riders → faster delivery → more users → more merchants) have formed an efficient closed loop. New entrants (Douyin, JD) cannot replicate this fulfillment infrastructure in the short term.

2. Earnings turnaround confirmed— From a USD 1 billion loss in 2022 to net profit of USD 5 billion and FCF of USD 6.5 billion in 2024. Operating margin surged from -2.6% to 10.9%, and gross margin rose from 28.1% to 38.4%. Earnings quality shifted from "cash-burning expansion" to "monetizing scale," and the trend is sustainable.

3. In-store, hotel and travel as the second growth curve— Meituan's in-store + hotel & travel business is China's largest local life in-store platform (merged with Dianping) and has shown resilience against Douyin's local life competition. GTV exceeds the trillion scale, and the monetization potential from commissions plus advertising remains far from fully tapped.

4. Instant retail offers a massive opportunity— Meituan Instashopping (30-minute delivery for everything) is expanding from food delivery into categories such as supermarkets, convenience stores, pharmaceuticals and 3C electronics. The instant retail market is expected to reach the trillion-yuan scale in 2025, and Meituan holds a first-mover advantage on the strength of its delivery network.

5. Valuation is highly attractive— PS just 1.3x, FCF yield 10.6%, PE about 14x, net cash above USD 20 billion. Against global peers such as DoorDash (PS ~6x) and Uber Eats (PS ~3x), Meituan trades at a pronounced valuation discount, reflecting market risk aversion toward Chinese assets rather than any flaw in the underlying business.

V. Reverse View and Weaknesses in the Thesis (Honest Self-Questioning Required)

1. Double-kill risk of "slowing growth + intensifying competition":Meituan's core dilemma: food delivery is maturing (growth slowing from 20%+ to single digits) while competitors (Douyin, JD.com, Ele.me) all ramp up investment. Meituan may face twin pressures of "decelerating growth" and "margin compression" — revenue growth slowing while it is forced to raise subsidies to defend against competition. This is the risk scenario that most warrants vigilance today.

2. Is a PS of 1.3x really undervalued?PS 1.3x looks cheap, but Meituan's core business (food delivery) is structurally lower-margin than pure internet platforms (advertising/gaming). With a net margin of about 10%, the "value" implied by PE 14x is far less extreme than PS suggests. And the slowing revenue growth trend means the current 1.3x may be fair.

3. Douyin local life is a structural threat:Meituan's core moat in the in-store business is the user mindset of "find a store → read reviews → place an order" (Dianping), whereas Douyin's approach of seeding demand through short-video content → buying group-buy vouchers directly is changing how consumers decide on in-store visits. If the "first stop" for in-store consumption shifts from Dianping to Douyin, Meituan's in-store moat will be substantially weakened.

4. Instant retail (flash sales) may be a big concept rather than a big business:30-minute supermarket delivery sounds appealing, but instant retail carries extremely high fulfillment costs (delivery cost of roughly RMB 5-7 per order), and its requirement for SKU breadth makes it hard to compete with traditional e-commerce (next-day delivery). Whether Instashopping can become a profitable growth engine rather than a "burn cash for GMV" story remains to be proven.

5. No dividend/buyback culture:Meituan has never paid a dividend, and its buyback is minimal (all but negligible against a USD 62 billion market cap). In this new phase of slower growth, if management does not shift toward shareholder returns, a re-rating will be hard to achieve—especially against the backdrop of Alibaba having already launched dividends plus buybacks.

6. China ADR risk cannot be diversified:Meituan faces no ADR delisting risk (it is primarily listed in Hong Kong), but the overall China-concept valuation discount still weighs on the stock. International investors' underweight in Chinese assets is systemic and not something Meituan can resolve on its own. A substantial part of Meituan's valuation discount stems from the "China discount" rather than business fundamentals.

📋 VI. Current conclusion

🟢 Thesis largely holds, but watch the growth slowdown
The core of the Meituan investment thesis —Food-delivery moat + margin improvement + multi-business growth— Largely holds up so far. FY2024 net profit of USD 5 billion and FCF of USD 6.5 billion confirm the earnings inflection. But three things need continued verification:

① Can revenue growth hold above 10%?(slowed to 9.4% in the first three quarters of 2025)
② Do Douyin/JD pose a substantive threat to market share?
③ Can flash sales/in-store hotel and travel become a scalable second growth curve?

The current FCF yield of 10.6% offers a sufficient margin of safety, but the low PS valuation needs growth to support it. If growth continues to decelerate without a commensurate margin improvement, there is a risk of a "growth-valuation double downgrade."
