04 Financial Reality: Where the 56% Gross Margin Comes From — AMD

Core view

The 56.0% gross margin is the most important number to break down in this earnings report. It rose 12.8 percentage points year on year, driven not by price increases but by mix; yet mix improvement has a ceiling and is being eroded by rising HBM prices. This piece breaks the gross margin down far enough to see the components.

Three sources, one hidden risk

Source 1: Product mix. Instinct accounts for more than 40% of revenue at a gross margin of 60%+; client CPU gross margin is 45%. As the mix shifts from "CPU-led" to "accelerator-led," the weighted gross margin naturally moves up. This contributed about 8 percentage points.

Source 2: Pricing. MI355X and MI450 are quoted 15%–20% higher than the prior generation, and customers accept it because the benchmark is Nvidia. This contributed about 3 percentage points.

Source 3: Cost. Chiplet lets AMD use N6 for the IO die, and the savings flow straight to gross profit. This contributed about 2 percentage points.

The risk is HBM.MI450 carries 432GB of HBM4 per card, and HBM accounts for more than 40% of bill of materials. HBM contract prices are up 60% year over year in 2026; another 30% increase in 2027 would take 3–5 points off gross margin. That is why management guides 2027 gross margin at "around 55%," not "60%."
Cash and capital: why an "asset-light" company spent RMB 800 million

The benefit of fabless is not having to build fabs. TSMC spends USD 20 billion on a single 3nm fab; AMD's annual capex is only USD 3 billion. That structurally gives it a higher ROE than IDMs — provided profits are large enough.

Q2 2026 capex was USD 808 million, 2.7x the consensus of USD 299 million. That number is highly unusual for a fabless company. Two possibilities: prepaid CoWoS capacity, or building a system integration line (ZT Systems). Either way, management is preparing for 2027 orders — a more informative signal than guidance.

Cash flow: operating cash flow of about USD 3 billion and free cash flow of about USD 2.2 billion, covering net income 1.3x. Healthy but not ample — growth requires inventory build. This is the norm for growth-stock earnings: profit on the books, cash in inventory.

How to read a 10% ROE

TTM net profit of RMB 6.4 billion, net assets of RMB 63 billion, ROE 10%. That figure trails first-tier semiconductor companies (NVIDIA 80%, TSMC 35%, Broadcom 25%) because the 2022 Xilinx acquisition was paid in stock, inflating net assets from RMB 20 billion to RMB 55 billion.

If net profit reaches RMB 33 billion by 2027 (EPS USD 20), ROE would hit 45%—only then would it deserve the "first-tier" label.In other words, AMD's low ROE today is a legacy of the past, not a present-day ailment.But investors paying for the future must recognize they are buying the "ROE recovery" story.

The 19-percentage-point gross margin gap breaks into three parts: software and services at roughly 8 points (NVIDIA's CUDA Enterprise, NVAIE, three-year support contracts), scale procurement at roughly 6 points (on identical HBM, NVIDIA pays 10%–15% less), and system integration at roughly 5 points (the assembly and testing premium on full racks).Of these three, AMD can only close the third (Helios); the first two are out of reach near term.

The expense structure also matters. R&D of roughly RMB 2.4 billion and SG&A of roughly RMB 1.1 billion total RMB 3.5 billion, or 30% of revenue. Within R&D, GPU-related work is about 40% (RMB 960 million), CPU about 35%, and FPGA and other 25%. That allocation shows AMD has not gone "all in GPU"—it must fund CPU and FPGA at the same time, the cost of running multiple product lines.NVIDIA can throw its entire RMB 12 billion R&D budget at a single line; AMD cannot.

The tax rate is a small but real tailwind. The effective tax rate this quarter was about 12%, below the 15% statutory rate, thanks to R&D tax credits and the overseas income mix. That rate is unsustainable; if it returns to 15% in 2027, EPS would fall 3%–4%.This is not a risk—it is a reminder not to treat the most optimistic EPS as the norm.

The relationship between segment profit and corporate expenses needs to be clear. The data center segment's 31.3% margin is calculated without allocating corporate R&D and administrative costs. Corporate R&D of about USD 3 billion a year (architecture, verification, software) and USD 1 billion in administrative expenses, allocated back to the four segments by revenue, would cut the data center's true margin to 22%–24%.That figure is still decent, but it is not 31%.

Conclusion

Financial reality: the 56% gross margin is structural and sustainable through 2027, but strip out 3–5 points from HBM price increases; the 10% ROE is post-merger fallout that will correct on its own; the RMB 800 million in capex is a leading signal of orders. There are no landmines in this report, only price.
