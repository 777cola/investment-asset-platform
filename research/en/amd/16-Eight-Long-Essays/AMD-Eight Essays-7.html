07 Risk list: six items, three outside the company's control — AMD

Core view

Classify risks by who controls them: two AMD can control (yield, cost) and four it cannot (Nvidia pricing, cloud capex, in-house substitution, geopolitics). If any one of the four uncontrollable risks materializes, a 125x multiple will not hold.

Most likely scenario: cloud capex decelerates

A 35% probability, the highest of the six. The reason is simple: North American cloud capex reaches USD 80 billion in 2026, up 60% year on year, the third year of the run. No prior cycle has sustained four consecutive years of growth. If growth falls to just 10% in 2027, the AI accelerator market drops from +60% to +20%.

Direct impact on AMD: the share-gain assumption from 9% to 22% is cut to 16%, and market contraction will absorb half of revenue growth. Data center revenue may grow only 40% in 2027, not 100%. EPS falls from 19 to 14, and at 25x the stock is 350 — that is how we arrived at our entry level.

The most lethal: cloud providers' in-house chips + geopolitics

In-house substitution is structural. It will not cut AMD's revenue; it will cap its share at 10%–15% permanently. For a company priced on "share reaching 25%", that is a valuation killer: the same profit commands only 18x rather than 25x.

Geopolitics is a tail risk. A 5% probability, but the impact is "valuation halved plus delivery disruption". Such risk cannot be handled through analysis, only through position sizing: no single stock above 5%, and no stacking with other Taiwan Strait exposure.

Together the two mean this: AMD's risk is not "will it lose money" but "will it fail to make money for a very long time". At a 125x valuation, the latter hurts more.
The most easily overlooked: customer concentration

In 2026Q2 revenue, the top five customers accounted for more than half. That concentration is high for the semiconductor industry (TSMC's top ten customers account for 70%, but they are spread across four areas: phones, PCs, AI and autos). AMD's concentration is concentration in "the same area, the same demand" — once AI capex turns, all five customers pull back at the same time.

That is also why this report lists "customer concentration" as a risk rather than a strength. Diversified customers are a moat; concentrated customers are leverage. Leverage magnifies gains in a tailwind and magnifies losses in a headwind.

A historical analogy offers a reference. In the 2018 cryptocurrency crash, AMD's GPU revenue halved in two quarters and the stock fell from USD 34 to USD 16 (-53%), while its CPU business was growing at the time. That episode proved: when demand for a business line is "derived demand", its volatility far exceeds that of the company as a whole. Today's data center business is the same in nature as the mining cards of that year — demand driven by external capex.

Sensitivity analysis: each 1 percentage point decline in gross margin cuts EPS by about 3% (gross profit is 56% of revenue, operating margin 17%); each 3 point decline in share cuts revenue by 6% and EPS by about 8%; each 5 turn decline in the multiple cuts the stock price by 20%. Of the three variables, the multiple has the largest impact and is the least controllable. That is why this report ranks "valuation compression" as the highest-probability risk.

Tail risk must be handled by hard rules: no single stock above 5%; combined exposure with TSMC and NVIDIA holdings no more than 12%; no leverage. None of the three is an analytical conclusion; they are discipline. Geopolitical risk cannot be predicted; all we can do is limit its potential to cause permanent loss.

Customer concentration can be quantified. In 2026Q2 revenue, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, OpenAI and Anthropic together account for about 45%–50%, plus three OEMs — Dell, Lenovo and Hon Hai — at about 20%. That means the top eight customers account for 70% of revenue. Compare TSMC: its top ten customers account for 70%, but they are spread across five areas — phones, PCs, AI, autos and industrial — so a decline in any one area affects only 15%. AMD's concentration is concentration in "the same area", and the risk cannot be diversified.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: three of the six are high-probability (in-house silicon 40%, capex 35%, price cuts 30%), and three are low-probability, high-impact (order cuts, supply, geopolitics). This distribution means AMD is more likely to "gradually stop making money" than to "suddenly blow up." The impact on valuation is slow and persistent, not one-off.
