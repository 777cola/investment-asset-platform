## Layer one: revenue mix — one leg carrying the run

Revenue came in RMB 3.851 billion above the year-ago figure. Broken out: Data Center up RMB 3.478 billion, Client up RMB 572 million, Embedded up RMB 151 million, Gaming down RMB 350 million — RMB 3.851 billion in total. **Data Center delivered 90% of the incremental revenue.**

This mix cuts both ways. The good side is high-quality growth: Data Center carries the highest gross margin, its customers are cloud providers and model companies, and order visibility extends beyond a year. The bad side is fragility: one business line drives the valuation, and that line's share is set by rivals' capacity.

Gaming at -31% is not news. The console cycle peaked in 2025, with PS5 and Xbox both in the late stage of their lifecycles, and AMD's semi-custom chip orders falling in step. This segment brings in RMB 779 million, or 7% of revenue, and no longer moves the valuation — but it is a reminder of one thing: **AMD has three business lines in a cycle and only one in a trend.**

On customer mix, the top five customers account for more than half of revenue: two hyperscale cloud providers, one model company and two OEMs. By geography, North America is about 60%, China about 20%, and Europe and the rest about 20%. The China portion carries export-control uncertainty, but the MI series is not currently on the strictest tier of the list. **This needs confirmation every quarter; it is not a settled issue.**

## Layer two: gross margin — where the 12.8 points come from

43.2% → 56.0%, up 12.8 percentage points. Three sources, ranked by contribution:

- Product mix: Instinct accelerators rose from less than 20% of revenue last year to more than 40%; gross margin on these products exceeds 60%, far above the 45% on client CPUs.
- Pricing: MI355X and MI450 are quoted 15%–20% above the prior generation, and customers accept it because they benchmark against Nvidia, not against AMD's own prior generation.
- Cost: the chiplet architecture separates compute die from IO die, with IO on 6nm and compute on 3nm, giving a lower cost per unit area than a monolithic design.

A bucket of cold water: 56% still trails Nvidia's 74.9% by 19 points. Of those 19 points, roughly half is software and system integration (Nvidia sells the full rack plus networking plus software stack) and half is scale (on the same HBM purchase volume, Nvidia pays 10%–15% less per unit). **Gross margin can keep climbing, but it will not reach Nvidia's level—unless AMD also starts selling full racks, and Helios is that attempt.**

One offsetting pressure on the cost side: HBM4. MI450 carries 432GB of HBM4 per card; at mid-2026 contract prices, HBM costs USD 6,000–8,000 per GPU, more than 40% of the card's bill of materials. How much memory makers raise prices in 2027 directly determines whether AMD can hold gross margin at 55%. **Attributing the entire gross-margin improvement to product mix is incomplete—it is also a dividend from memory prices not yet having risen fully.**

## Tier 3: Costs and profit — full current-period expensing of R&D

Operating profit was RMB 1.99 billion, a 17.3% margin. That looks low because all R&D is expensed in the period: R&D was about RMB 2.4 billion this quarter and SG&A about RMB 1.1 billion, RMB 3.5 billion combined, or 30% of revenue. This is standard semiconductor accounting, not AMD being unusually conservative.

Real profitability has to be read by segment. The Data Center segment posted operating profit of RMB 2.103 billion, a 31.3% margin—**that number is the truth of this business**. The 17.3% blended margin is a structural mix dragged down by Client (45% gross margin), Gaming (40% gross margin) and Embedded (55% gross margin but small), not an efficiency problem.

One tax point to note: the effective tax rate this quarter was about 12%, below the 15% US statutory rate, due to R&D tax credits and the overseas revenue mix. That rate is not sustainable; at 15% in 2027, EPS would be 3%–4% lower.

One more note on the EPS basis. Non-GAAP EPS of USD 1.66 excludes about USD 400 million of stock-based compensation and USD 100 million of intangible amortization. On GAAP EPS of USD 1.39, the TTM P/E jumps from 104x to 125x. **Sell-side research defaults to non-GAAP for comparability, but shareholders receive GAAP earnings, and the dilution is real.** This report uses GAAP throughout for valuation.

## Tier four: cash flow and balance sheet — inventory build-up has begun

Net profit of RMB 2.297 billion, operating cash flow of about RMB 3 billion, coverage of 1.3x — healthy. But capex jumped from RMB 150 million in the year-ago period to RMB 808 million, leaving free cash flow at only about RMB 2.2 billion.

Why would a fabless company spend RMB 800 million on capex? Two possibilities: prepaying TSMC for CoWoS capacity, or building its own test and system-integration lines (the capability brought by ZT Systems). **Either way, it shows 2027 orders are already being scheduled.** This is the most informative number in the report — it says more about what management sees than the RMB 13 billion guidance does.

The balance sheet is clean: long-term debt in the RMB 3 billion range, cash and short-term investments above debt, net cash positive, interest coverage in the tens of times. Goodwill of RMB 24 billion (Xilinx) showed no impairment in the first half of 2026. **No financial risk — this is a report you can read on operations alone, without worrying about accounting engineering.**

Watch inventory and receivables. Stocking up for MI450 in the second half likely pushed inventory up sequentially; that becomes revenue two quarters out, or impairment if customers cut orders. If receivables days exceed 60, payment terms are loosening — a hidden cost of growth. Both figures are in the 10-Q, and neither showed deterioration this quarter.

## Definitional traps: three easy misreadings

**First, segment margin is not company margin.** The Data Center segment's 31.3% operating margin looks impressive, but it carries no allocation of company-level R&D headquarters costs and no interest. Treating 31.3% as company profitability would overstate it by 14 percentage points. **Second, the high year-on-year growth has a low base.** In Q2 2025, Data Center revenue was only RMB 3.240 billion, a quarter when MI300 was being destocked and MI355X had yet to ramp. Comparing the Q2 2026 figure of RMB 6.718 billion against that base yields +107%, of which roughly 15 percentage points is base effect. The true two-year compound growth rate is about 45%—still fast, but not a doubling. **Third, the midpoint of guidance is not a commitment.** Q3 guidance is RMB 13 billion ±0.3 billion, a range of 2.3% either side. Management has historically favored "guidance it can beat," but this quarter's variables sit on the supply side (HBM4 and CoWoS), not the demand side, so the guidance is less credible than usual.
