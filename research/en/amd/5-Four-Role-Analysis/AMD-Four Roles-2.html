Core thesis

Buffett's three questions: can the margin hold, can profit turn into cash, and is capex money well spent. AMD's answers this quarter: yes, mostly yes, and well spent but hard to see through. The 56.0% gross margin is structural, operating cash flow covers net income 1.3x, and capex of RMB 80.8 million is 2.7x the consensus.

Profit margin: 56% is structural, not cyclical

AMD's gross margin was 45% in 2022, 49.4% in 2024 and 56.0% in Q2 2026. That is an 11-percentage-point gain over three years, driven by one factor: data center share rising from 25% to 58%. This is not a pricing cycle; it is a product-mix shift.

Its sustainability therefore hinges on one thing: whether data center share holds. If MI450 ramps in 2027, share reaches 65% and gross margin has another 2–3 points of room; if share stalls and falls back, gross margin retreats to 52%. The direction of gross margin is the direction of share; the two numbers are two sides of the same coin.

R&D is the other thread. AMD will spend about USD 9.5 billion on R&D in 2026, one-third of Nvidia's and half of Intel's. That money must support four lines at once — CPU, GPU, FPGA and DPU — leaving only about USD 4 billion for GPUs. Nvidia puts its entire USD 12 billion into a single line. No amount of organizational efficiency can close a threefold resource gap.

Cash flow: real money earned, but partly eaten by inventory build

Net profit of RMB 2.297 billion and operating cash flow of about RMB 3 billion, a coverage ratio of 1.3x. That ratio is healthy — semiconductor equipment companies run below 0.8 year-round, while Fabless companies typically exceed 1.2 because depreciation is light and receivables are short.

Capex of RMB 808 million, 2.7x the consensus. For a Fabless company this is an outlier. The money likely goes to two places: prepaid CoWoS capacity, and the systems integration lines that come with ZT Systems. The former buys capacity, the latter buys delivery capability; both point to orders for 2027 already being scheduled.

Buffett would like this move — locking in capacity ahead of time when demand is certain is the same structural move as See's Candies expanding capacity in 1973.But he would also ask one question: if this RMB 800 million ultimately has no matching revenue, whose impairment is it? The answer is the shareholders'.

Shareholder returns deserve separate mention. AMD pays no dividend, and buybacks run about USD 1–1.5 billion a year, just offsetting dilution from equity incentives (about USD 400 million of equity incentives a year × 4 quarters). That means 100% of the return from holding AMD comes from the share price, with not a cent of cash return.For a company at 125x earnings, that puts the entire risk on "the valuation not compressing."

Conclusion

Financial verdict: A-. Earnings are real, cash is real, the balance sheet is clean. The only thing that remains opaque is the customer commitment behind the RMB 808 million of capex—if it is dedicated to a single customer, then that money has swapped "demand uncertainty" for "customer uncertainty."
