Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — AMD

AMD (AMD) | Which risks are quantifiable, which are not

Core view

AMD's risk is not financial, it is structural. The finances are clean, cash is abundant and debt is low; structurally there are four hard risks: NVIDIA's capacity and pricing, cloud providers' in-house ASICs, customer concentration, and the Taiwan Strait supply chain. Three of the four are outside AMD's control.

Quantifiable risk: valuation and price cuts

Valuation compression is the easiest to quantify. With 2027 EPS unchanged at USD 19, the multiple falling from 25x to 17x takes the share price from 475 to 323, -33%. This scenario does not require anything to go wrong at the company; it only requires market sentiment to shift from "AI faith" back to "semiconductor cycle." The same kind of compression hit memory stocks in February 2026 and software stocks in July 2026.

Nvidia's price cuts are also quantifiable. If Rubin's capacity is fully ramped in the first half of 2027, Nvidia has ample motive to block AMD with price—its gross margin is 74.9%, so a 15-point cut still leaves 60%, while AMD has only 56% and cannot match it. The result of a price war is not AMD losing share, but AMD losing gross margin. Gross margin falls from 56% to 50%, and EPS drops 20%.

There is another underappreciated risk: accounts receivable and inventory rising in tandem. Fast-growing companies tend to run into trouble here—goods ship, cash does not come back; revenue is real, cash is not. AMD currently shows no sign of this (operating cash flow covers net profit 1.3x), but it needs to be checked every quarter. This is the single most effective indicator for identifying "fake growth."

Unquantifiable risks: customers and geopolitics

Customer concentration cannot be quantified. OpenAI's 6GW agreement and Anthropic's 2GW agreement depend on whether these two companies can keep raising financing. The AI financing environment in 2026 is still fine, but the "circular financing" doubts of November 2026 show that market sentiment can turn overnight—once financing stalls, orders get delayed rather than cancelled, but a delay means zero revenue for that year.

Geopolitical risk cannot be priced. All of AMD's advanced process capacity sits with TSMC, and all of its HBM with three Korean suppliers. If either end breaks down, AMD has no alternative—it lacks Apple's ability to cultivate a second supplier, and lacks Nvidia's standing to lock up an entire production line. The way to handle this kind of risk is not analysis but position sizing: no single stock exceeds 5% of the portfolio.

Conversely, under what circumstances would we be wrong? If data center revenue really reaches USD 150 billion in 2027, with a 60% gross margin and EPS of USD 25, then the current price of USD 483 is only 19x, and our "too expensive" call would be wrong. That scenario requires three things to hold at once: Nvidia capacity stays tight, cloud capex rises another 40%, and Helios takes 20% of rack share. We put the probability of all three happening simultaneously at 20%.

Conclusion

Risk assessment: four hard risks, three external. An acceptable price is one where "the pessimistic scenario is already priced in," i.e., the USD 240–300 range. The current price of USD 483 discounts the optimistic scenario by 30%, offering insufficient risk compensation.
