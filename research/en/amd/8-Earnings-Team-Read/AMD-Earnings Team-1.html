Core view

Read this earnings report and Duan Yongping would likely say two things: "The person is still the same person, and the business is still the same business." The person is Lisa Su, who has not missed in twelve years. The business is "selling the substitute": data center doubled to USD 6.7 billion, and the driver was Nvidia's inability to deliver, not any capability unique to AMD.

First, look at where the money comes from and how it is made

Revenue was RMB 11.536 billion, with data center at 58%. The mix shift is substantive: four years ago data center was only 22%, the company lived off PC processors and gross margin was 45%. Now it lives off accelerators, and gross margin is 56%. The same company, but what it sells has changed, and so has how it makes money.

How does it make money? Data center posted operating profit of RMB 2.103 billion, a 31.3% margin. That is the margin of "selling chips," not "selling systems." Nvidia sells full racks and its segment margin is above 60%. The difference is not manufacturing — it is whether the customer is buying a component or a solution.

Now let's see whether this business has improved.

What improved: gross margin from 43% to 56%, a customer base shifting from PC makers to cloud providers, and order visibility extending from 3 months to 12 months. All three are qualitative shifts, not incremental ones.

What hasn't changed: pricing power. Of the RMB 6.7 billion in data-center revenue, every dollar of pricing is set against Nvidia — customers come to the table with Nvidia's quote sheet. Duan Yongping's "benfen" means not doing things beyond one's competence; AMD has achieved that. But "benfen" does not equal "moat" — those are two different things.

His judgment is roughly: this is a 7-out-of-10 business paired with a 9-out-of-10 CEO, worth tracking long term; but buying it at 125x P/E means paying a 10-out-of-10 price for a 7-out-of-10 business.
Final question: what will it be selling in ten years?

A decade from now AMD will most likely still be around, still selling the same thing: "the second option besides Nvidia." That position will not disappear — as long as Nvidia is around, customers need a second supplier — but it will not become more valuable either.

This is exactly the point Duan Yongping values most: if a business is still doing the same thing in the same position ten years from now, what it is worth can be calculated.On 2027 EPS of USD 19–20 and a 25x multiple, that gives USD 475–500, roughly in line with the current price. The result is "fair," not "cheap."

Conclusion

Close-reading verdict: business 7, people 9, price 0. Lisa Su turned a near-bankrupt company into USD 800 billion of market cap — real skill. But that skill monetises through share gains rather than pricing power, which caps the multiple the valuation can support.
