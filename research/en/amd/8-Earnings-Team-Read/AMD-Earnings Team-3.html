Core thesis

Munger's approach requires just one move: assume a competitor wrote this earnings report, then look for its flaws. I found three: the base effect, the non-GAAP basis, and the attribution of segment profit. None is fatal, but all three flatter the "+107%" figure.

Flaw one: the +107% includes a low base

Q2 2025 data center revenue was just RMB 3.24 billion — MI300 was being destocked that quarter and MI355X had yet to ramp, making it the lowest base of the past eight quarters. Against that base, roughly 15 percentage points of the +107% is base effect.

Run a second calculation: Q2 2024 data center revenue was about RMB 2.28 billion (per public quarterly reports), giving a two-year CAGR of (67.18/22.8)^0.5 - 1 = 71%. Still fast, but not double. "Double" gets thrown around too casually during earnings season — investors should run the math themselves.

Flaw 2: Non-GAAP looks 20% better than GAAP

Non-GAAP net income of RMB 2.76 billion and EPS of 1.66, against GAAP net income of RMB 2.297 billion and EPS of 1.39. The RMB 463 million gap is mainly RMB 400 million of stock-based compensation and RMB 100 million of intangible amortisation.

Stock-based compensation is a real cost: it dilutes shareholders. Intangible amortisation is also a real cost: it is the premium paid to acquire Xilinx that year. Stripping out both amounts to saying "this money isn't money." Munger had a line for this: if the compensation is stock, is it not an expense?

Flaw 3: Segment profit does not allocate head-office costs

Data center delivered operating profit of RMB 210.3 million at a 31.3% margin, yet total operating profit was just RMB 199.0 million, a 17.3% margin. The segments sum to more than the whole because client, gaming and embedded are loss-making, and corporate R&D and administrative costs sit outside the segments.

This is not fraud; it is standard segment reporting. But it misleads: 31.3% makes the business look highly profitable, while 17.3% shows the company as a whole is still building capability. You need both numbers; on either one alone you overvalue the stock.

Munger-style conclusion: no accounting fraud here, but plenty of "selective presentation." Management led with the three best-looking numbers (data center growth, gross margin, EPS above consensus) and buried the capex jump from an expected RMB 299 million to RMB 808 million in the notes. Read the notes, not the press release.

Conclusion

Close-reading conclusion: the financials are credible; discount the narrative. None of the three gaps undermines the call that "the company is improving," but each shapes the call on "how much it has improved." Adjusting for the base effect, the accounting basis and the allocation, real growth is 71%, the real net margin is 19.9% and real profitability is 17.3%—still decent, but not as simple as "doubling."
