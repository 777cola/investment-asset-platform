AMD Q2 2026: Making money, but not yet earning its position
Research date 2026/08/07  ·  Price $483.36 (-1.21%)  ·  Market cap About USD 802 billion  ·  PE(TTM，GAAP) Approximately 125x
← Back to the hub

Core thesis

AMD delivered a flawless earnings report, and the stock still fell 7%. That reaction is the conclusion: the market is not paying for this quarter's USD 11.5 billion, it is paying for the doubling in 2027.

A good earnings report

AMD reported second-quarter 2026 results after the close on August 4: revenue of USD 11.536 billion, up 50% YoY to a record high; data center revenue of USD 6.718 billion, up 107% YoY and 58% of total revenue, with segment operating profit of USD 2.103 billion; gross margin of 56.0%, up 12.8 percentage points YoY; GAAP net income of USD 2.297 billion and non-GAAP EPS of USD 1.66, above consensus.

Guidance was equally strong, with third-quarter revenue guided to USD 13 billion ±300 million versus consensus of USD 12.5 billion. Capex of USD 808 million came in at 2.7 times expectations — for a fabless chip designer to spend at this level, there is only one explanation: locking in capacity for 2027 orders.

Lisa Su gave two targets on the call: double data center revenue in 2027, and EPS above USD 20. On those targets, the USD 483 share price is 24 times 2027 earnings — not expensive.

But the position has not changed.

The problem: AMD is still selling "the second option to Nvidia." Customers buy for three reasons—20% cheaper, half the lead time, and second-supplier policy—none of which is "because it is better."

This matters. It determines three things: share has a ceiling (cloud providers typically allocate 20%–30% to a second supplier), price has a benchmark (customers negotiate with Nvidia's quote sheet in hand), and gross margin has a cap (56% vs Nvidia's 74.9%). A company whose gross margin trails its rival by 19 percentage points is not selling the same thing.

More pressing is the timing window. MI450 and Nvidia's Vera Rubin launch in the same six-month period, pitting AMD's best product against its rival's next generation. MI450's specs are no weakling (432GB HBM4, N2 process, Helios rack with 72 GPUs), but Rubin is a system-level solution: NVLink, Spectrum-X and CUDA as a full stack. A single card against a rack has never won in history.

The answer is already priced in

125x TTM, RMB 802.4 billion market cap, RMB 6.4 billion annual profit. Even on the company's own most optimistic target — USD 20 EPS in 2027 — that is 24x; on our base case of USD 19, it is 25.4x.

Three scenarios: bear case USD 240 (share stagnation, EPS 12, 20x), base case USD 475 (doubling delivered, EPS 19, 25x), bull case USD 720 (above expectations, EPS 24, 30x). Probability-weighted expected price USD 442, 8.6% below the current price. Upside requires "better than the company's own target"; downside only requires "industry normalization." These odds do not warrant a heavy position.

The conclusion is therefore watch, not avoid. This company's execution deserves a place in the pool: Lisa Su's twelve years, spinning off manufacturing, acquiring Xilinx, betting on chiplet, securing OpenAI's 6GW and Anthropic's 2GW — every step right. What it lacks is not capability but position; and position can only be changed by time and a generation of products.

Two signals, wait for one of them

First, price: below USD 350, equivalent to 18x 2027 neutral EPS, by which point half the bear case is already priced in.

Second, evidence: MI450 quarterly revenue above USD 3 billion with gross margin holding at 55%. Both numbers will appear in the October and next January earnings reports — no guesswork required.

Until then, the best move is to do nothing. Not because the outlook is poor, but because the price isn't there.

One final risk to flag. Every "wait for USD 350" in this report rests on one assumption: that MI450 will ramp, and that this is only a question of timing. If it cannot ramp at all — yields stay low, HBM4 supply is unavailable, ROCm cannot run mainstream models — then USD 350 is not the floor either; USD 240 is. The difference: the former is a valuation issue, the latter a business issue.Telling these two apart matters more than nailing the target price.

Conclusion

In one sentence: AMD made money this quarter, but it has not earned its valuation. At 125x you are paying for the thesis to change, and no earnings report can validate that change. Wait for USD 350, or wait for MI450 revenue.
