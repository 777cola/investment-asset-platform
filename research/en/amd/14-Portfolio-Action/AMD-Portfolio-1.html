Core view

Do not build a position here. This is not a negative call but a risk/reward one: +49% upside requires the company to beat its own targets, whereas -50% downside requires only industry normalisation. At that asymmetry, any position is paying for the story.

Why you cannot buy now: the risk-reward does not add up

Current price 483.36. Bear case 240 (-50%), base case 475 (-2%), bull case 720 (+49%). Probability-weighted at 35/45/20, expected value 442, 8.6% below the current price.

Put differently, making money at the current price requires 2027 EPS above USD 20 (management's target) and a multiple the market is willing to pay above 25x. We put the probability of both at 30%. Losing money requires only that EPS stall at USD 15 or the multiple de-rate to 18x, a 50% probability. A 30% win rate at 1:1 odds is the worst table in the casino.

Correlation: Buying it does not equal diversification

AMD's daily return correlation is about 0.85 with Nvidia, about 0.9 with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index and about 0.7 with Broadcom. The implication is straightforward: if the portfolio already holds Nvidia or a semiconductor ETF, adding AMD simply amplifies the same risk 1.3x without reducing volatility.

Narrative overlap is the bigger problem. Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD and TSMC are all driven by the same variable — AI capex — so when that variable turns, all four fall together and the diversification proves illusory. True diversification must span industries, not stocks.

For those who must allocate within semiconductors, this report's ranking is: TSMC (certainty) > Nvidia (ecosystem) > Broadcom (valuation) > AMD (elasticity). AMD ranks last not because it is the worst business, but because it is the most expensive.

Stop-loss and rebalancing discipline

If you built a 2% position at USD 450 on the "evidence first" basis, set the stop-loss at USD 340 (-24%) and exit fully on trigger, no questions asked. Rationale: -24% corresponds to 17x 2027 EPS, meaning the market is already pricing in "share stagnation" and the thesis premise has changed.

Rebalancing: trim to 6% when a single position exceeds 8% of the portfolio; halve the position when AMD rises above USD 700 (29x 2027 EPS), because at that point the price is already pricing 2028, and visibility into 2028 is zero.

What not to do: don't add the day before earnings, don't bottom-fish on an unexplained selloff, don't use leverage. These three rules matter especially for high-beta growth stocks — the -13.2% over two days on 28–29 July would turn into a permanent loss with leverage.

One more practical constraint: cash and concentration. If the portfolio already holds 5% in Nvidia and 3% in TSMC, adding 5% in AMD takes semiconductor exposure to 13%. That number shrinks across the board when AI capex turns.Position sizing isn't about a single stock; it's about total exposure to the same risk factor. The 5% cap in this report assumes AMD is the only semiconductor holding.

When to sell? Three conditions: ① the thesis is disproven (gross margin <52%); ② valuation peaks (above 29x 2027 EPS); ③ a better opportunity appears (the same money can buy a higher-certainty asset).Don't sell because you're "up 30%", and don't sell because you're "down 20%". Sell for a reason, not an emotion.

One practical addition: if you built 3% at USD 350 and it later rises to 500, don't rush to trim. 500 corresponds to 26x 2027 EPS, not yet "expensive"; trim by half only at 700 (29x).The criterion for taking profit is valuation, not the size of the gain.

Conclusion

Portfolio conclusion: 0% position, top tier of the watchlist. Two paths to building a position: price reaches USD 350 (3%), or evidence reaches "MI450 quarterly revenue of USD 3 billion + gross margin of 55%" (2%). At a correlation of 0.85 it cannot diversify risk—it only amplifies the single variable of AI capex. Position cap 5%, no exceptions.
