①
Understandable — the essence of the business
✅ Passed

Core view: Alibaba's core business logic is not hard to grasp.

In plain terms, Alibaba is a company that "helps Chinese businesses do business + helps Chinese factories sell to the world + sells cloud computing services." Its revenue comes from three parts:

  • China e-commerce (Taobao and Tmall Group)— Taobao + Tmall + 1688 account for ~65% of revenue. The model is "rent collection": merchants pay advertising fees, transaction commissions and store fees. This is Alibaba's breadbasket, but Pinduoduo and Douyin are raiding it.
  • ☁️ Cloud computing (Alibaba Cloud)— About 12% of revenue. Sells compute power and data services, No. 1 in China (market share ~34%), but growth is slowing and it is still in a price war with Huawei Cloud and Tencent Cloud.
  • 🌍 International e-commerce (Lazada/AliExpress/Trendyol)— About 12% of revenue. Southeast Asia, Turkey and Latin America: burning cash for growth, not yet meaningfully profitable.

Buffett's perspective:Alibaba is not like Apple (which consumers can see and touch), but it is essentially "commercial infrastructure"—the same kind of thing Berkshire boughtrailroads and utilitiesThere are similarities: both are pipes through which the economy runs. The difference is that Alibaba's pipes are both physical (Cainiao logistics) and digital (Alibaba Cloud plus payments).

The complicated part:Alibaba also owns Ele.me, Amap, digital media and entertainment (Youku/Alibaba Pictures), Freshippo, Intime Retail… a pile of "side businesses." If these cannot be cleanly separated, valuation becomes difficult. The good news is that Alibaba is "returning to its core" and gradually divesting non-core assets.

Conclusion:The core business (e-commerce + cloud) is understandable, but part of the "opaque odds and ends" needs to be ignored. ✅

②
Moat — competitive barriers
⚠️ To be examined

Core call: the moat remains, but its walls are getting lower.

The parts that remain solid:

  • 🕸️ Network effects (the widest part):Taobao + Tmall have ~1 billion annual active buyers and millions of merchants—a massive two-sided market. Merchants come for the buyers, buyers come for the selection, creating a flywheel. Pinduoduo and Douyin have not yet broken this flywheel; they have only captured incremental growth.
  • 🏢 Merchant ecosystem/infrastructure:Merchants have invested substantial resources in Alibaba's ecosystem (store design, data tools, customer service teams, Cainiao logistics integration). Switching costs are high; opening a store on Pinduoduo is no substitute.
  • ☁️ Cloud computing's first-mover advantage:Although Alibaba Cloud's growth has slowed, its domestic IaaS+PaaS market share remains close to 34%, far above second-placed Huawei Cloud (~19%). The switching costs for enterprise customers migrating cloud providers are extremely high.
  • Cainiao Logistics Network:Although it does not fully own them, the delivery network controlled by Cainiao is a moat for e-commerce fulfillment. JD.com has its own in-house logistics, but Cainiao covers a broader merchant base.

Where the city wall is thinning:

  • 🔻 PDD's asymmetric attack:Having started in lower-tier markets, Pinduoduo now attacks Tmall's core base directly (branded goods). Pinduoduo's "low price" positioning is too strong, forcing Alibaba to follow with a price war at the cost of its profits.
  • 📱 Douyin/Kuaishou interest-based e-commerce:Livestream e-commerce is cannibalizing traditional shelf-based e-commerce. In 2025, Douyin e-commerce GMV approached RMB 3.5 trillion; though only ~40% of Taobao Tmall's, its growth rate is 3x Taobao's.
  • ☁️ Cloud computing price war:Alibaba Cloud cut prices sharply several times in 2024-2025, with some core products down 55%. This is direct evidence of intensifying market competition.
  • 🔧 The "crack" in the moat:Alibaba's historical moat, built on first-mover advantage, is being eroded by more aggressive competitors (PDD) and algorithm-savvier rivals (Douyin) as the mobile internet dividend fades.

Conclusion: the moat remains (wide but not deep), yet competitive pressure has clearly intensified. Market share shifts warrant close attention. Measured against Buffett's "wide and deep" moat standard, Alibaba currently rates only "above average." ⚠️

④
Management integrity — capital allocation and governance
⚠️ Mixed signals

Core view: management is sincere but its competence is questioned, and its capital allocation record is mixed.

✅ Doing the right things:

  • Large-scale buyback:FY2024 buybacks were RMB 88.7 billion (about USD 12.2 billion), and FY2025 buybacks were RMB 86.7 billion. Management did honor its commitment to "buy back when undervalued." But FY2026 plunged to RMB 7.6 billion (-91%); the official reason is that cash is needed to invest in AI infrastructure—understandable, but disappointing to shareholders.
  • Initiating dividend:Started paying dividends for the first time in FY2024, with FY2026 dividends of RMB 33.7 billion (+16% YoY). This is uncommon among internet companies and reflects a shareholder-return mindset.
  • Organizational split (1 into 6):The "1+6+N" organizational overhaul announced in 2023 sought to make each business unit operate independently and be responsible for its own profits and losses. The direction was right, but execution has been slow.
  • Cutting non-core:Gradually exit non-core retail assets (Intime, RT-Mart and others) and return to the main track of e-commerce + cloud + AI.

⚠️ Things done wrong / in doubt:

  • FY2026 margin collapse:SG&A exploded from ~RMB 166.0 billion in FY2025 to RMB 278.1 billion (+68%), while revenue grew just 2.7%. This shows management is "buying growth" to grab market share — exactly the "burn cash for scale" that Buffett detests.
  • Capital allocation priority question:Net cash plunged from RMB 197.4 billion to RMB 56.9 billion (-71%), while CapEx surged to RMB 126.1 billion (+90% YoY). A massive amount of money went into AI infrastructure buildout. The direction is right (AI is the future), but the timing and pace are debatable—investing at scale while competition intensifies puts heavy pressure on near-term profit.
  • Frequent management turnover:The CEO changed 3 times between 2019 and 2023 (Daniel Zhang → Wu Yongming → Wu Yongming concurrently CEO). Alibaba Cloud's CEO also changed in 2025. Senior-management turmoil undermines long-term strategy execution.
  • Political/regulatory risk treatment:Ant Group's IPO was halted (2020), an antitrust fine of RMB 18.2 billion (2021), and the "choose one of two" rectification—although these are largely macro factors, Alibaba's management was clearly not forward-looking enough in its communication with regulators.

Wu Yongming (CEO)'s style:With a technical background (founder of Alibaba Cloud), he set out a clear "user first, AI-driven" strategy upon taking office. He drove a series of organizational slimming measures in 2024-2025, showing he saw the problems. But the profit collapse in FY2026 raises doubts about his execution.

Conclusion: management is not "fraudulent," but frankly—the capital allocation record is mixed. Some things were done right (buybacks, dividends, focus on the core), and some big mistakes were made (margin discipline lost, cash burn too fast). ⚠️

⑥
Margin of safety — room for error
⚠️ Margin of safety exists but is not thick enough

Core call: a margin of safety exists (share price near its 52-week low + PB below book), but the FY2026 results expose the risk that "profits are more fragile than they appear."

🛡️ Sources of margin of safety:

  • Below-book trading:PB 0.22x. Even under liquidation (an extreme assumption), shareholders could theoretically recover book value of ~$441/share; the current share price of $98.40 means the market is pricing in a "heavy discount." Alibaba holds substantial monetizable assets (equity stakes, real estate, data centers).
  • Value of non-core assets:Alibaba's stakes in Ant Group (~33%), Cainiao (~64%), Alibaba Health, Alibaba Pictures and other listed/unlisted equity are worth a conservatively estimated USD 80-120 billion. Strip these out, and the core e-commerce plus cloud business trades at an actual PE of perhaps only 10-12x.
  • Buyback support:Although the FY2026 buyback drops sharply, the board still has USD 31 billion of unused buyback authorization (dual-listed in US and Hong Kong shares). Management can step in to support the stock at any time.
  • Cash flow basis:Even if FY2026 FCF turns negative, operating cash flow still reaches RMB 76.2 billion. The negative FCF stems mainly from surging CapEx (AI infrastructure). If a downturn forces management to scale back investment, FCF could quickly turn positive.

⚠️ Risk points for margin of safety:

  • Margins may deteriorate further:If the price war between PDD and Douyin persists, Alibaba may need to keep burning cash. The 68% surge in FY2026 SG&A may be just the beginning. If the operating margin slides further from 4.9% to 2-3%, the PE will quickly balloon to 20x+.
  • Negative FCF is unsustainable:Free cash flow of -RMB 49.8 billion is a red alert. Buffett's "margin of safety" concept rests on a company's ability to self-fund. If Alibaba posts negative FCF for 2-3 consecutive years, it must either cut spending, halt buybacks/dividends, or issue debt/equity — none of which is good for shareholders.
  • Net cash burn too fast:Net cash plunged from RMB 197.4 billion at the end of FY2025 to RMB 56.9 billion (-71%). At this pace, it could turn to net debt by the end of FY2027. Balance-sheet flexibility is diminishing.
  • Systemic risk for China concept stocks:A PB of 0.22x already prices in the extreme risk of delisting or expropriation. This double discount may be an opportunity for value investors, or it may be a value trap.

Buffett's margin of safety calculation (rough):

  • Conservative scenario (profit keeps declining 10% a year):Cumulative profit over the next 3 years of about RMB 250 billion, current market cap ~RMB 1.6 trillion. ~15x PE, margin of safety is average.
  • Bull case (margin recovery + incremental cloud/AI contribution):Cumulative profit over the next 3 years is about RMB 400 billion, implying the current market cap pays back in roughly 4 years. The margin of safety is wide.
  • Extreme scenario (uncontrolled competition + halved profit):Profit falls to RMB 50 billion a year, pushing PE to 32x. The margin of safety is breached.

Conclusion: there is a margin of safety, but it is not "thick" enough for Buffett to buy with confidence.Low valuation offers downside protection, but deteriorating FY2026 financials make upside uncertain. Alibaba looks more like a "distressed turnaround" than a classic Buffett-style buy of "a great company facing temporary trouble." ⚠️
