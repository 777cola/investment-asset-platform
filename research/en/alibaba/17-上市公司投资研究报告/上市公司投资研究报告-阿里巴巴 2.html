Company overview

Alibaba Group Holding Limited is one of the world's largest retail commerce ecosystems and the leader in China's cloud computing market. Jack Ma founded the company in Hangzhou in 1999. It listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2014 (NYSE: BABA) and completed a secondary listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2019 (HKEx: 9988).

Business model

Alibaba is not an "e-commerce company" but a super ecosystem aggregator—the core of its business model is "connection" and "enablement": connecting consumers, merchants, logistics, computing resources, financial services and content producers, and continuously optimizing the efficiency of those connections through data and algorithms.

3.2 Monetization methods in detail

Core monetization (Taobao and Tmall):Alibaba is essentially a "traffic monetization platform." Merchants buy traffic keywords (Zhitongche) and display slots (Zuanzhan/Super Recommendation) through Alimama (its advertising platform), and Alibaba charges advertising fees on a CPC/CPM/CPS basis. In FY2026, advertising revenue accounted for about 46% of Alibaba's total, and commission revenue about 15%.

Monetizing technical services (Alibaba Cloud):Charge based on resource usage (compute/storage/network) and the number of AI model invocations. MaaS (Model-as-a-Service) is an emerging new monetization approach.

Monetizing ecosystem services (Cainiao/Ant Group):Service revenue such as logistics service fees and payment processing fees.

3.3 Ecosystem flywheel effect

Alibaba's business model has a distinctive positive flywheel:

E-commerce transactions → massive data → AI and algorithm upgrades → better user experience → more transactions → growing cloud demand → improved technical capabilities → empowering e-commerce/logistics → more efficient ecosystem

But the flywheel is at risk: growth in the core engine (e-commerce transactions) is slowing, reducing the flywheel's speed. Alibaba Cloud is pushing for "flywheel decoupling"—reducing its reliance on Taotian's internal orders and raising the revenue share from external customers.

Core tension in the business model
Alibaba is growing in scale, but its profit efficiency is declining.Revenue grew 2.7%, but SG&A rose 47.7% — every additional RMB 1 of revenue required RMB 17.7 of selling expense. CapEx rose from RMB 32.1 billion to RMB 126.1 billion, while operating cash flow fell from RMB 182.6 billion to RMB 76.2 billion. Scale expansion failed to deliver economies of scale.

Competitive analysis

Alibaba faces multi-front competition in its two core battlegrounds of e-commerce and cloud computing. In e-commerce, Pinduoduo, Douyin and JD.com form a "four-way rivalry"; in cloud computing, Huawei Cloud and telecom operator clouds continue to erode Alibaba Cloud's market share.

Pinduoduo: The Most Structural Threat

  • Price anchor reset:Pinduoduo has led users to perceive Alibaba as "overpriced" — the hardest shift in user mindset to reverse
  • C2M factory-direct connection:Under a disintermediation model, Alibaba's "brand-merchant-led" model cannot be replicated
  • Traffic logic innovation:Social fission bypasses Alibaba's search traffic distribution, creating a new customer-acquisition paradigm
  • TEMU's overseas spillover effects:Pinduoduo has proven the global viability of its low-price model overseas

Douyin e-commerce: the fastest eroder

  • Interest-based recommendations vs search-based recommendations:Douyin has upended e-commerce's underlying logic, shifting it from "people seeking goods" to "goods seeking people"
  • Live-streaming GMV ~RMB 1.5 trillion:Far above Taobao Live's ~RMB 500 billion
  • Brand self-broadcasting becomes the norm:Brands are shifting budget from Tmall to Douyin
  • Shelf-based transformation:Douyin Mall plus search is extending "impulse spending" into "intentional shopping"

Risk factors

Alibaba's current risk matrix is more complex than at any point in its history—risks across five dimensions exist simultaneously: competition, regulation, governance, financial, and macro. The following is ranked by severity.

8.1 Competitive risk (🔴 High)

  • Pinduoduo continues to erode:If Taotian's GMV share falls below 35% without stabilizing, core e-commerce's profit contribution will decline markedly
  • Douyin e-commerce rises rapidly:Livestreaming and content e-commerce are eroding the foundation of Alibaba's advertising revenue (the Alimama platform)
  • Cloud computing price war:Aggressive price cuts by Huawei Cloud and telecom carrier clouds are squeezing Alibaba Cloud's profit margins
  • TEMU hits overseas business:Pinduoduo's rapid overseas expansion creates direct competition for AliExpress

8.2 FCF and balance sheet risk (🔴 High)

  • FCF turns negative:FY2026 FCF was -JPY 49.9 billion; if this trend continues, the company faces the risk of accelerating cash burn
  • Net cash shrank sharply:From RMB 400.3 billion in FY2024 to RMB 56.9 billion in FY2026, a decline of 86%
  • CapEx return uncertainty:Whether the massive RMB 126.1 billion capital expenditure (mainly on AI infrastructure) can generate sufficient future returns is highly uncertain
  • Interest-bearing debt increased:Rising from RMB 170.8 billion to RMB 260 billion (+52%); if FCF stays negative, the company may be forced to take on debt

8.3 Regulatory and policy risk (🟡 Medium-high)

  • Delisting risk for Chinese ADRs:The PCAOB audit working paper issue was temporarily resolved in 2023, but uncertainty in China-US relations persists
  • Antitrust scrutiny tightens again:If regulators tighten their stance again, Alibaba could face new compliance challenges.
  • Data security and privacy legislation:China's Data Security Law and Personal Information Protection Law continue to constrain Alibaba's ability to monetize its data.
  • Ant Group's subsequent rectification:Ant Group's financial holding license progress and valuation changes affect Alibaba's equity-method investment income

8.4 Governance risk (🟡 Medium)

  • Management distraction:Joseph Tsai also owns the NBA's Brooklyn Nets, and Wu Yongming is the founder of Vision Plus Capital
  • Unclear succession plan:No clear next-generation CEO candidate has emerged after Wu Yongming
  • Partnership system transparency:Partner election criteria are not publicly disclosed, creating a governance gray area
  • Partner pipeline gap:A clear experience gap separates the founding generation of the 1960s-1970s from the post-1985 cohort

8.5 FX and Macro Risks (🟡 Medium)

  • RMB exchange rate volatility:Alibaba reports in RMB but trades in USD, so exchange-rate swings directly affect its financial statements and US share price
  • US-China relations and tariffs:Cross-border e-commerce business (AliExpress/Trendyol) is heavily affected by tariff policy
  • China's economic growth is slowing:Weak consumption directly weighs on e-commerce GMV growth and advertising spend
