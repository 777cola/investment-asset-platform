The deeper reason behind the surge in SG&A:
① Marketing spend on international business expansion:AIDC (Lazada/AliExpress/Trendyol) sharply increased overseas marketing and subsidy spending to counter Temu/Shein. Customer acquisition costs keep climbing in Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Eastern Europe.
② Domestic e-commerce "price war":To counter competition from Pinduoduo and Douyin, Taobao and Tmall Group increased consumer subsidies and merchant incentives, driving a marked rise in its marketing expense ratio.
③ Organizational adjustment and personnel restructuring:The 1+6+N restructuring brought one-off workforce optimization costs (severance and reorganization expenses), while aggressive hiring in AI and cloud computing pushed up compensation spending.
④ Local life continues to receive cash support:The food-delivery battle between Ele.me and Meituan continues, and the local-services segment still requires heavy subsidies to defend share.
How should we view this SG&A crisis?This is not merely a short-term expense-control problem but a structural predicament: in a market of stock competition, Alibaba must spend heavily to defend market share, while rivals (Pinduoduo, Douyin, Temu) are burning cash just the same. Whoever burns cash more efficiently survives to the end.

Three reasons FCF turned negative:
① Operating cash flow halved (-53.4%):The sharp decline in operating profit was the main driver, while higher working capital tied up (buildup in receivables and inventory) further worsened cash collection.
② Capital expenditure doubled (+46.6%):CapEx surged from RMB 85.97 billion to RMB 126.06 billion, mainly for AI/cloud infrastructure buildout (GPU clusters, data centers) and Cainiao logistics network expansion.
③ FCF/net profit ratio 0.75×:A ratio below 1 means net profit is not adequately backed by cash—earnings contain substantial non-cash items (depreciation, amortization, share-based compensation), while actual cash collection efficiency deteriorates.

EightManagement strategy review: the cost of "slimming down" and "going global"

8.1 Background of the strategic transformation

FY2026 is Alibaba's first full fiscal year following the "1+6+N" organizational restructuring. Core strategies include:

  • Focus on the core:Divested non-core retail assets including Intime and SunArt Retail, recovering capital
  • Overseas expansion:AIDC (Lazada/AliExpress/Trendyol) is investing heavily to capture overseas markets
  • AI-driven:Alibaba Cloud + Qwen large model, focused on AI infrastructure investment
  • Low-price strategy:Taobao and Tmall Group returns to a "low-price" strategy to counter Pinduoduo's challenge

🔍 Core contradiction: "Too few people making money, too many people spending it"

Alibaba's problem is not a simple matter of "cost control" but a structural business-model predicament:

  • Taobao and Tmall Group onlyThe real profit driver; the other 6 major business segments (AIDC/cloud/Cainiao/local services/digital media and entertainment/others) are either marginally profitable or deeply loss-making
  • Domestic e-commerce's "passive defense":Taobao and Tmall Group spends tens of billions annually to counter Pinduoduo and Douyin, attackers that by nature carry no "historical baggage" (no need to maintain legacy systems)
  • Internationalization is a "necessary gamble":Stay domestic and you slowly die; go overseas and you face 3-5 years or more of losses
  • AI's "arms race":Skip AI and you get left behind; invest in AI and you must strike a painful balance between CapEx and FCF
Impact on the investment thesis:Alibaba's FY2026 annual report strongly confirms the "growth fading" and "moat erosion" thesis. It passes only 3/7 of the seven hard metrics. The key things to watch in FY2027 are: (1) whether SG&A can decline; (2) whether FCF can turn positive; (3) the true profit level after interest income falls back. Unless FY2027 brings a clear operating inflection, Alibaba's "value trap" risk is rising.
