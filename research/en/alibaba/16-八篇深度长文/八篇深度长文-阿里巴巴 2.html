Part One

Cognitive reset: what kind of company is Alibaba, really?

 ▼
Research date:2026/07/07 ｜ Share price:$98.40 ｜ Market cap:~USD 225 billion

I. Introduction: Perception Lags Reality

When investors pull up Alibaba's ticker (BABA.NYSE), the first word that comes to most minds is "e-commerce company." This is Alibaba's "original sin" — Taobao and Tmall were so successful that the entire market understands the company through a definition from 20 years ago.

But the data does not lie. In FY2026, Taobao and Tmall Group (core e-commerce) accounted for about 54% of Alibaba's RMB 1.02 trillion revenue, versus more than 70% five years ago. Alibaba Cloud contributed over RMB 120 billion in revenue, Cainiao about RMB 80 billion, international business about RMB 70 billion, and local services about RMB 50 billion—beneath Alibaba's "e-commerce" shell, a completely different business skeleton has long since grown.

The first lesson of the cognitive reset:What you see as an "e-commerce company" is only a silhouetteThe real Alibaba is a digital economy assembled from six distinctly different businesses, each with its own industry characteristics, life-cycle stage and valuation logic.

II. Asset Overview: Map of the Six Major Segments

2.1 Taobao and Tmall Group — cash cow (but thinning)

Taobao and Tmall Group (Taobao + Tmall + 1688 + Xianyu) is the "engine" of the Alibaba ecosystem, contributing about 54% of revenue and more than 70% of operating profit. In FY2026, Taobao and Tmall revenue is about RMB 550 billion, growing only 2-3%, roughly in line with retail sales growth.

Key characteristicsChina's largest e-commerce platform, with GMV of about RMB 8 trillion; monetization rate of about 3.5%—between Pinduoduo (~4.5%) and JD's marketplace (~1%); brand flagship stores' "brand mindshare" remains one of Alibaba's strongest moats; but share has fallen from ~70% in 2020 to ~45% in 2025, and the trend has not reversed.

2.2 Alibaba Cloud — the second curve (making a big bet)

Alibaba Cloud FY2026 revenue of about RMB 120 billion, growth of about 3%. China IaaS+PaaS market share of about 34% (ranked first); global ranking #4 (~4% share); profitable in FY2026 but with a single-digit margin. Of the RMB 126.1 billion in cash consumed by capital expenditure, most went to AI/cloud infrastructure.

2.3 Cainiao — logistics infrastructure (in the process of becoming independent)

Cainiao's FY2026 revenue is about RMB 80 billion (growth of about 15%), handling about 80 million parcels per day. Its distinctive value lies in an "asset-light operating model" — it owns no warehouses or couriers, instead connecting logistics companies through data and technology.

2.4 International business — growth engine (currently burning cash)

International digital commerce (Lazada/AliExpress/Trendyol/Daraz) FY2026 revenue of about RMB 70 billion, growing around 20%. This is one of Alibaba's fastest-growing segments, but it remains in a "burn cash for scale" phase, with losses of RMB 10-15 billion.

2.5 Local life — tactical positioning (strategic loss)

Alibaba's local services segment—Ele.me, Amap, Fliggy and Koubei—posted FY2026 revenue of about RMB 50 billion and a loss of about RMB 10 billion. Alibaba has shifted from head-on confrontation to a differentiated playbook: focusing on Amap's map-ecosystem traffic funnel rather than a subsidy war.

2.6 Greater Entertainment — peripheral assets (may be divested at any time)

Youku + Alibaba Pictures + Damai generate combined revenue of about RMB 20-30 billion, the smallest in scale, with losses persisting but narrowing. The market ascribes essentially zero valuation to the broader entertainment segment.

III. Why do people always misunderstand Alibaba?

Misconception 1: Alibaba is "the Amazon of China"— Amazon follows a "self-operated" model, buying and selling to earn the spread; Alibaba follows a "platform" model, connecting buyers and sellers to earn transaction commissions and advertising fees.

Misconception 2: Alibaba is "China's eBay"— eBay is simply a C2C auction platform, whereas Alibaba's ecosystem extends far beyond "e-commerce."

Misconception 3: Alibaba is already a "sunset company"The core problem Alibaba faces is "slowing growth" and "declining efficiency," not "business death." Revenue of RMB 1.02 trillion, a 40% gross margin, net cash of RMB 56.9 billion—by any industry standard these numbers mark a giant, not a sunset business.

Four, a more accurate label: digital economy infrastructure operator

Compare Tencent (social + gaming), Pinduoduo (extreme low-price e-commerce) and JD.com (self-operated retail + logistics): Alibaba is the only company covering the full-chain digital economy of "transaction (e-commerce) + computing (cloud) + payment (Ant) + logistics (Cainiao) + location (Amap) + content (digital media and entertainment)".

The key judgment for investors is:Will "comprehensive" translate into "all-capable"?

V. Operational checklist for the cognitive reset

  1. Stop viewing Alibaba through an "e-commerce stock" lens
  2. Accepting "slow growth" as the new normal
  3. Distinguish "company quality" from "stock quality"

Part Two

E-commerce battlefield: how does Alibaba fight a defensive battle?

 ▼
Keywords: Pinduoduo, Douyin e-commerce, JD.com, market share, low-price strategy, livestream e-commerce

I. Introduction: From "unrivaled" to "besieged on all sides"

In 2019, Alibaba held about 65% of China's e-commerce market—a dominant position. Five years later, in 2025, that figure has fallen to about 45%, with Pinduoduo and Douyin E-commerce capturing roughly 20% and 17% respectively. Behind the 20-percentage-point share loss lies the most intense reshaping of competitive dynamics in the history of Chinese e-commerce.Alibaba has shifted from attacker to defender。

II. Pincer Movement on Three Fronts: The Three "E-Commerce Species" Alibaba Faces

2.1 Pinduoduo: the mind war of the price war

Pinduoduo's impact on AlibabaThe biggest damage is not the loss of share but the reset of the price anchor.Pinduoduo = once the ultra-low-price user mindset is established, Alibaba faces a dilemma: follow (cut prices → damage to brand relationships) or not follow (user churn → accelerated share loss).

Pinduoduo's structural advantages: the C2M (factory-to-consumer) model, WeChat social virality, and extreme organizational efficiency (13,000 employees vs Alibaba's 130,000).

2.2 Douyin e-commerce: a different e-commerce logic

Traditional e-commerce (Taobao/JD.com) ="Search-compare-purchase"; livestream e-commerce (Douyin) ="Scroll, get hooked, buy on impulse"Douyin e-commerce GMV already exceeded RMB 3.5 trillion in 2025, growing more than 10x faster than Alibaba.

2.3 JD.com: an old friend, a new threat

JD.com has pushed its "RMB 10 billion subsidy" program since 2024. While Alibaba fights on two fronts—moving upmarket with Tmall's brand focus and downmarket with Taobao Tejia and low prices—JD.com holds its mid-range price segment and waits for its opening.

III. The gains and losses of Taobao's "low-price strategy"

In 2023, Jack Ma called for "return to Taobao, return to users, return to the internet." The low-price strategy defended GMV but at the cost of a falling monetization rate and damaged relationships with brand merchants. The core contradiction: Alibaba wanted to "defend" with a low-price strategy, but that strategy raised the cost of defense, eroded its brand-merchant advantage, and turned the defender into a "follower."

IV. Livestream e-commerce: the structural crisis of shelf-based e-commerce

China's livestream e-commerce GMV grew from about RMB 1 trillion in 2020 to about RMB 5 trillion in 2025, with its share of total e-commerce GMV rising from about 8% to about 25%. Taobao Live can serve as a defensive tool, but it is hard for it to go on the offensive in livestream e-commerce — its recommendation algorithm lags Douyin, its content ecosystem is insufficient, and traffic costs are high.

V. Conclusion: Can the defense hold?

Time dimensionJudgmentCore conditions
Short term (1 year)Share continues to decline to ~42-43%Pinduoduo and Douyin still growing faster than Alibaba
Medium term (2-3 years)Share stabilizes at ~40-42%AI plus organizational reform boosts efficiency
Long term (3-5 years)UncertainNew formats such as AI-powered personalized shopping and cross-border e-commerce

Core view: Share will keep declining, but the slope will flatten. The key is margin recovery once share stabilizes—if the operating margin returns to 12-15%, even with only 40% share, Alibaba's e-commerce remains a very good business.

Part Three

Alibaba Cloud: Second Curve or Capital Black Hole?

 ▼
Keywords: cloud computing, AI large models, Qwen, CapEx, break-even

I. Introduction: A RMB 126.1 billion gamble

FY2026 capex surged to RMB 126.1 billion—up 46.6% year on year and 3.7x FY2023. The RMB 126.1 billion equals 119% of Alibaba's net profit attributable to shareholders that year and exceeds Pinduoduo's full-year revenue.

II. China's Cloud Computing Market: Alibaba Cloud's Core Franchise

RankingCloud providersIaaS + PaaS shareGrowth rate

#2Huawei Cloud~19%~20%+
#3Tencent Cloud~15%~10%
#4-#6The three major telecom operators' cloud businesses~21% (total)~40%+

Alibaba Cloud's operating margin is only in the single digits, a huge gap versus AWS's ~30% operating margin. Reasons for the low margin: price wars, disadvantages in the government and enterprise market, high customization costs, and scale effects not yet fully realized.

III. AI large models: Alibaba Cloud's "X factor"

Qwen sits in the first tier of Chinese large models. Alibaba's distinctive AI advantages: vertical e-commerce data that competitors cannot access, a cloud-plus-AI closed loop, and rich application scenarios.

AI-related revenue estimate: about RMB 20 billion in FY2026 (~17% of total), growing ~100%+. But AI revenue carries a low gross margin (AI compute only 10-15%), and the overall AI business gross margin is ~25-30%, below the ~35-40% of traditional IaaS.

IV. Rationality analysis of the RMB 126.1 billion CapEx

CapEx allocation: data center construction ~40%, GPU servers ~45%, network equipment ~15%. Capex-to-revenue ratio (CIR) about 1.5-2x (versus AWS's historical CIR of 2-3x).

Warning sign: CapEx/revenue jumped from ~4% in FY2023 to ~12.3% in FY2026. If CapEx fails to translate into proportional profit growth within 3-5 years, Alibaba will slide from an asset-light platform to an asset-heavy infrastructure provider.

V. Conclusion: Did Alibaba Cloud bet right or bet too big?

Core viewAlibaba Cloud's "AI big bet" is directionally right. But the metrics to track are not absolute CapEx, but the "AI revenue share" and the "cloud business operating margin".

Part Four

Management upheaval: from Jack Ma to Joe Tsai to Eddie Wu

 ▼
Keywords: Joseph Tsai, Eddie Wu, 1+6+N, partnership system, corporate governance

I. Introduction: Alibaba Management's "Genetic Code"

Alibaba has historically been a company where "rule by people" outweighs "rule by system." Three genetic codes: founder culture, the partnership system, and an organizational evolution from "centralized power" to "decentralization → recentralization."

II. The Jack Ma era (1999-2013): founding and foundation

Jack Ma made three core decisions: in 1999, he rejected the MBO proposal and insisted on building a platform; in 2003, he founded Taobao to counter eBay China; in 2008, he decided to build Alibaba Cloud.

III. The Post-Jack Ma Era (2013-2023): Trial and Error Under Professional Managers

Daniel Zhang was Alibaba's most successful professional-manager CEO—he drove the "All in Wireless" strategy, created Tmall's Double 11, commercialized Alibaba Cloud, and launched the "1+6+N" organizational split. But his fatal mistakes: reacting too slowly to Pinduoduo's rise, letting the organization grow more complex the more he managed it, and stretching the investment front too wide.

FY2026 revenue RMB 1.02 trillion (+2.74%), net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 105.9 billion (-18.2%), operating margin 4.90% (14.14% a year earlier). But a deeper story lies behind these headline numbers.

2. Signal one: SG&A surged 47.7%

FY2026 SG&A expense: RMB 278.1 billion, up about RMB 89.9 billion year on year (+47.7%). SG&A growth is 20 times revenue growth.

Interest income equals 82.6% of net profit attributable to shareholders — more than 80% of Alibaba's net profit comes from interest earned "lying flat." Investors should focus on "operating profit excluding interest income."

V. Signal 4: buybacks plunge from RMB 86.7 billion to RMB 7.6 billion

III. PB 0.22: The Deeper Meaning of Trading Below Book

I. Executive summary of conclusions

Three major advantages: Asset value is severely undervalued (PB 0.22x, SOTP discount of 40-60%); the AI + cloud closed loop genuinely exists; management is doing the right things.

Three major disadvantages: Negative FCF is the hard flaw (-RMB 49.9 billion); continued e-commerce share loss; organizational efficiency problems behind runaway SG&A.

One-line summary:Alibaba is a "cheap but flawed" name: its asset value is deeply undervalued, but deteriorating operating efficiency could offset the margin of safety from the asset discount. It suits a small position while waiting for a signal, not a heavy bet on the bottom.
