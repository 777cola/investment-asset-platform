Q&A: Duan Yongping on Alibaba

Q: How do you view Alibaba's business?

Alibaba is a complex business. And complexity is not something I like.

Look at Apple: it can be summed up in one sentence—make the best phones and ecosystem. Moutai is even simpler—China's best baijiu. But Alibaba? Is it an e-commerce company? A cloud company? An investment company? Or a logistics company?

If a company cannot explain in one sentence how it makes money, its business model has a problem.Alibaba's biggest problem right now isn't competition — it's that Alibaba itself no longer knows what it is.。

E-commerce is its core business, but growth there has clearly slowed. FY2026 revenue grew just 2.74%, which for a tech company is essentially stagnation. GMV is still growing, but Pinduoduo has left it far behind on growth.

Alibaba Cloud has a story, and AI has a story. But a story is not profit. Alibaba Cloud still accounts for a very small share of the group's profit.

Q: Is platform e-commerce a good business model?

Platform e-commerce is inherently a good business model—asset-light, high-margin, network effects, strong cash flow. Look at Amazon in the US and Pinduoduo in China—both are good businesses.

ButA good business model does not mean anyone can run it well.The key is to look at the moat.

What is Alibaba's e-commerce moat? It used to be the network effect of "more sellers → more buyers → even more sellers." But that effect has now been broken. Pinduoduo tore open a gap at the "low price" end, and Douyin captured traffic at the "content e-commerce" end.

Alibaba's moat is not technological—the technical barrier to building an e-commerce platform is actually not high. Its moat isScale— Number of merchants, product variety, logistics network (Cainiao), payment system (Alipay).

But the problem is that these moats are narrowing. Pinduoduo has drawn away many merchants and users with cheaper supply and more efficient matching. Douyin has captured user time with livestreaming and short videos.

A business model with a narrowing moat is not a good business—at the very least, not one you can hold with peace of mind.

Q: How valuable is Alibaba Cloud in the AI era? What is the competitive landscape?

Alibaba Cloud is China's first cloud. That position has value.

Cloud computing, in my view, is a good business model: once customers use your cloud, switching costs are high and stickiness is strong. Amazon AWS has proven this.

But the competitive landscape in domestic cloud differs from overseas. Overseas, AWS is the dominant player, with Azure and GCP chasing from behind. In China, Alibaba Cloud, Huawei Cloud and Tencent Cloud compete head-on, alongside carrier-backed players such as Tianyi Cloud, and the price war is fierce.

Alibaba Cloud has an opportunity in the AI era—large models require massive compute, and compute means cloud. ButCompetition is too intenseHuawei Cloud is strong in the government and enterprise market, and ByteDance Cloud (Volcano Engine) is rising. Whether Alibaba Cloud can stay ahead depends on technology, service and pricing.

Besides, Alibaba Cloud still contributes limited profit to the group. In FY2026, Alibaba Cloud revenue will be around RMB 100 billion, but profit only a few billion, contributing less than 10% of Alibaba's total profit. It is the future, not the present.

Duan Yongping-style questioning:If you were to spin off Alibaba Cloud on its own, what valuation would you be willing to give it? The A-share market gives companies like Yonyou Network 30-40x PE, but Alibaba Cloud currently has only single-digit margins. Is it worth anything? Yes. But how much? Right now that is unclear.
Q: Do the new management team of Joseph Tsai and Eddie Wu live up to "benfen"?

Joseph Tsai is Alibaba's co-founder, comes from a finance background, and commands high prestige within Alibaba. Eddie Wu comes from a technical background and is also an Alibaba veteran. With these two taking over, the leadership is more "Alibaba" than Daniel Zhang was.

But"Whether one knows one's place" is not judged by a resume but by actions。

I see several positive signals:

First, they began toFocus on core businessThat's it. The "1+6+N" split is essentially about making each business generate its own cash and take responsibility for itself — the thinking is right. That is exactly how Duan Yongping ran BBK — each business unit accounted for itself independently, and was cut if it failed.

Second, they beganBack to usersJoseph Tsai publicly admitted that Alibaba had "forgotten the user" over the past few years. That willingness to own the mistake reflects benfen. When you get it wrong, admit it; once you admit it, fix it.

Third, they are inWorkforce reduction to boost efficiencyLayoffs are never good, but keeping idle staff is worse. Alibaba cut its workforce from 250,000 to 130,000. Painful, but the direction is right.

But consider the other side:

Alibaba's corporate culture was once criticized by outsiders as a "KPI culture"—willing to do anything to hit targets. This runs entirely counter to "benfen." "Benfen" means knowing what should and should not be done, not resorting to any means for the sake of GMV.

Can the new management reinfuse Alibaba with a culture of "doing the right thing"? For now, there is hope, but it will take time to verify.

Q: Alibaba now trades at PE=15x and PB≈0.22 (below book). Is it undervalued?

First, a correction: a PB of 0.22x does not mean the stock trades below book value. Alibaba's book value per share is RMB 441, and the share price converts to roughly RMB 700-plus (USD 98 x 7.2), which actually puts PB at around 1.6x. It is unclear how the 0.22 figure was derived; most likely it stems from a conversion error between the US ADR and the Hong Kong shares.

Now, back to the question of 15x PE.

A 15x PE for Alibaba,On the numbers alone, it is indeed not expensiveVersus its own history: Alibaba's PE reached 30-40x in 2021. Versus peers: Pinduoduo now trades at a PE of 9x, Tencent at 15x. Alibaba sits in roughly the same range.

ButCheap and good value are not the same thing。

Alibaba trades at 15x PE, against a backdrop of an 18% decline in net profit. If profit keeps falling in FY2027, today's 15x becomes 20x next year.

And Alibaba's free cash flow has now turned negative — FY2026 FCF was -RMB 49.8 billion. The reason is a sharp increase in capex to RMB 126 billion (mainly into AI and cloud infrastructure). This investment is necessary, but it will weigh on profits in the near term.

So my judgment is:Alibaba at 15x PE is not expensive, but it may not offer a margin of safetyMargin of safety comes from how deeply you understand the business. If you are confident that Alibaba's profit can return to growth over the next five years, then it is undervalued now. If you are not sure, then even 15x could be a value trap.

Q: Pinduoduo has taken market share from Alibaba. How do you view this competition?

Pinduoduo is a company I follow, and I know Colin Huang well. What Pinduoduo does isExtreme efficiency—Cut out every intermediary and connect factories directly to consumers. The direction is right, and Pinduoduo has executed it exceptionally well.

But competition is not a zero-sum game.

China's e-commerce market is large enough. In 2025, China's total retail sales of consumer goods will be roughly RMB 50 trillion, with e-commerce penetration at about 30%, leaving substantial room for growth. Pinduoduo focuses on "cheap", Alibaba on "comprehensive", and Douyin on "content" — these three propositions may serve different customer groups and different scenarios.

The problem lies inAlibaba's "conglomerate" positioning is being erodedPinduoduo has captured price-sensitive users, and Douyin has captured content-driven users. Alibaba is caught in the middle—not cheap enough, and not fun enough.

If Alibaba cannot find an irreplaceable positioning—such as "the most reliable quality plus the best service"—its market share will keep eroding.

Duan Yongping says: "Dare to be last under heaven" means that being a late mover is not necessarily a disadvantage — what matters is whether you can do it better than the first mover.Alibaba was the e-commerce pioneer, but the problem now is that it has not stayed ahead on "doing it better." Pinduoduo overtook it from behind, showing that a latecomer can pull it off. Alibaba itself has now become the one being chased by latecomers.
Q: How should the "1+6+N" organizational transformation be viewed from the perspective of "Benfen"?

The direction of this reform is right.

The first layer of meaning in "Benfen" is—Each finds its placeEach business segment is accounted for independently and responsible for its own profits and losses: keep the good ones, cut or spin off the bad ones. This is the same approach I took with BBK back in the day—a divisional structure where whoever makes money stays and whoever loses money is held accountable.

What was Alibaba's old problem? The "iron rice bowl." Taobao's profits had to feed Alibaba Cloud, and Alibaba Cloud, not yet profitable, had to feed DAMO Academy. At that rate, no business faced real survival pressure.

After the split:

  • Taobao and Tmall Group (e-commerce) — your cash-flow business must generate its own cash
  • Alibaba Cloud Intelligence — Your future, but prove you can make money
  • Cainiao — logistics network; an independent listing is no bad thing either
  • Local life services (Ele.me) — frankly, it has lost money for years
  • International digital commerce — still in investment phase, needs time

But "spin-off" is a double-edged sword.

The upside is that each business unit gains autonomy and accountability. The downside is thatSynergies may decline— Taobao, Alipay, Cainiao and Alibaba Cloud used to operate as one coordinated whole; after the split they each go their own way, and efficiency may actually decline.

Moreover,The split is not an end in itself; the goal is to sharpen competitiveness.If the split merely produces more financial statements without any substantive business improvement, then it is just hovering at the door of "doing the right thing" without truly "doing things right."

Q: How does this "dare to be last in the world" philosophy manifest at Alibaba?

"Being willing to be last in the world" does not mean doing nothing and waiting for others to go first. It means—Do not chase the hype, but strike late to win。

On AI, Alibaba has shown a willingness to "let others go first."

When Baidu said "All in AI" back in 2017, Alibaba did not follow with slogans. It quietly invested in Tongyi Qianwen, in the technology foundation of Alibaba Cloud, and in computing infrastructure. Now that AI has exploded, Alibaba Cloud, as China's largest public cloud, is a natural beneficiary.

That is the "late-mover advantage" — shouting first does not mean you can deliver; coming later but executing solidly still wins.

Conversely,"Being willing to come late to the market" cannot become an excuse for "doing nothing"Alibaba's failure in social, its late entry into short video, its vacillation in local services—these can no longer be explained by "daring to be last in the world"; this isStrategic drift。

The premise of "daring to be last in the world" is that you know clearly what you want to do and are confident you can do it better than others. On many fronts, Alibaba has neither thought things through nor done them better.

Q: So if Duan Yongping had RMB 10 million, would he buy Alibaba now?

That question cannot be answered with a simple "buy" or "don't buy." Let me break it down for you:

The case for buying Alibaba:

  • ✅ PE 15x, valuation at a historical low
  • ✅ Alibaba Cloud + AI is a genuine future growth engine
  • ✅ New management focuses on the core business and returns to users
  • ✅ Still RMB 56.9 billion of net cash on the books (even though interest-bearing debt rose sharply)
  • ✅ Alibaba's position in domestic e-commerce cannot be overturned overnight

Reasons not to buy / to stay on the sidelines:

  • ❌ Core e-commerce growth has stalled amid intense competition
  • ❌ Free cash flow turns negative, capex surges
  • ❌ Management has the right direction but has yet to prove results
  • ❌ Alibaba is not a business where you "get paid to sit still" — it demands a large time commitment to track
  • ❌ No sign of easing in competition from Pinduoduo and Douyin

My answer is:

If I had RMB 10 million, I would not put the bulk of it into Alibaba.

That does not fit my investment principles.I would rather buy a certain good business at a certain price than buy an uncertain business at a cheap price.。

With companies like Moutai and Apple, you can buy and not worry. Alibaba is different — you need to watch its GMV growth, market share, cloud business profitability and the results of its organizational changes every quarter. Too exhausting.

But if you know Alibaba very well and have your own independent judgment—At 15x PE, Alibaba is worth considering as a non-core position.Keep the position within 5-10%, no more than that.

Remember: the first principle of investing isDon't lose moneyThe second principle is to remember the first.

Q: So what is Alibaba's biggest risk right now?

The biggest risk isSustained cash burn。

Look at Alibaba's current profit structure—its core e-commerce (Taobao and Tmall Group) remains the profit source. But e-commerce profit is being steadily eroded by Pinduoduo and Douyin. If e-commerce profit growth continues to slow or even decline, while Alibaba Cloud, local services and international business remain slow to contribute sufficient profit, Alibaba's overall profitability will keep sliding.

FY2026 operating margin plunges from 14% to 5%, a very strong warning signal.

The second risk isCapital allocation misstepsAlibaba's investments over the past few years were highly scattered—it acquired many companies, and many of them underperformed. Intime, RT-Mart, Ele.me—these investments did not generate good returns. In fiscal year 2024, Alibaba recognized more than RMB 70 billion in asset impairment losses.

The third risk isCorporate cultureAlibaba used to be a company full of "jianghu spirit" — brotherly loyalty, an iron-army culture. But it later became a "KPI culture" — doing whatever it took to hit targets. That cultural shift is very dangerous. Once a corporate culture of "benfen" is lost, it is very hard to get back.

To sum up:Alibaba does not lack opportunities; rather, opportunity and risk coexist, and uncertainty is too high.. For me, too much uncertainty means I will choose to stay away, or bet on a reversal with a very small position.

📌 Final conclusion

Duan Yongping's one-sentence operational advice

Conclusion: stay on the sidelines; no rush to buy.


Alibaba's problems are structural, not cyclical. Pinduoduo's and Douyin's erosion of its e-commerce share is not temporary, Alibaba Cloud's profit inflection point has yet to arrive, and the effects of its organizational overhaul will take at least 2-3 quarters to verify.


A PE of 15x looks cheap, but cheap is not a reason to buy—A good business plus a good price is what mattersIf you lack a deep understanding of Alibaba's business model and buy simply because it "has fallen a lot" or "trades at a low PE", you will most likely walk into a value trap.


If you must buy:
→ Position size no more than of total capital5-8%
→ Be prepared to hold for 3-5 years
→ Track core data quarterly (GMV growth, cloud business margin, free cash flow)
→ If management cannot restore profit growth, cut losses in time


By contrast, I would rather put my money in higher-certainty names like Moutai, Apple and Tencent.Investing is not about who earns more, but about who lasts longer.Do the right thing, then do the thing right. For Alibaba today, the former looks promising; the latter has yet to show.

"If you aren't willing to own a stock for 10 years, don't even think about owning it for 10 minutes. For Alibaba, are you willing to hold it for 10 years? Once you think that question through, you'll have your answer."
