E-commerce pioneer down 49%: at just 15x PE and below book value on PB, is Alibaba a golden pit or a value trap?

In-depth research Value investing China concept stocks
Original · AI Berkshire · July 7, 2026

In 1999, Jack Ma founded Alibaba with the "Eighteen Arhats" in an apartment at Lakeside Garden in Hangzhou. Twenty-six years later, the company has grown from a B2B website into a trillion-dollar business empire spanning e-commerce, cloud computing, logistics and local services.

Yet today, in 2026, Alibaba's share price stands atUSD 98.40— Down from its 52-week high of USD 192.6749%, nearly halved. Market cap now just USD 225 billion, forward P/E below 15x, and price-to-book even just0.22x— It has fallen below book value.

A company with annual revenue above RMB 1 trillion and hundreds of billions in cash on its books is somehow trading at a "discount." Is this a golden pit created by market mispricing, or a value trap of deteriorating fundamentals?

Let's skip the mystique today and take Alibaba apart from the ground up, using data and business logic.

I. Alibaba's current position at a glance

Start with the hard numbers. Alibaba's core financial metrics for fiscal year 2026 (ending March 2026):

RevenueRMB 1.02 trillion
Year-on-year growth+2.74%
Net profitRMB 105.9 billion
Change in profit-18.2%
SG&A expense growth+47.7%
Free cash flowTurns negative

Revenue is still growing, but the pace has slowed—under 3% revenue growth signals a mature giant. Compare that with the 30%-40% high-growth era of five years ago: Alibaba is now too large to drive rapid growth through core e-commerce alone.

More concerning is the profit line: net profit fell 18.2% year on year, while selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses surged nearly 48%. This shows that Alibaba isSpending money to buy growth, and the cost is steep. Under fierce attack from Pinduoduo and Douyin, Alibaba has been forced to ramp up marketing spending to defend its turf—a war of attrition in which whoever buckles first loses market share.

One more detail deserves attention: Alibaba's free cash flow (FCF) turned negative in fiscal 2026. Cash flow is a company's lifeblood; when cash generation falters, even the largest businesses can be pushed onto the back foot. Alibaba still holds hundreds of billions in cash reserves, so there is no near-term liquidity risk, but the signal of negative FCF warrants close tracking by every investor.

II. The four major business segments: who is carrying the load, and who is dragging?

Alibaba's current business can be broken down into four major segments, which we analyze one by one.

1. Taobao and Tmall Group: the core business, but under attack on all sides

Taotian (Taobao + Tmall + Xianyu, etc.) remains Alibaba's core profit engine and the leader in Chinese e-commerce. By GMV, Alibaba still holds roughly45% share— But five years ago that figure was above 60%.

Two completely different rivals are taking its share:

Pinduoduo attacked upward from the lower-tier market, breaching Alibaba's defenses with extreme low prices and its "RMB 10 billion subsidy" program. More damaging still, Douyin e-commerce used its short-video-plus-livestream "interest-based e-commerce" model to push GMV past RMB 3 trillion in 2025, closing in on half of Alibaba's territory.

What is Taotian's response?"Low-price battle" and "return to Taobao"Alibaba proposed its "price power" strategy in 2023, trying to counter Pinduoduo with low prices; in 2024 it reshuffled Taobao Tmall's management, with Wu Yongming taking direct charge. But the results remain to be seen—after all, Alibaba did not lose on price, but on user mindshare.

When consumers think "cheap," their first reflex is Pinduoduo; when they think "good products," it is Douyin. But when they think of Taobao — "it has everything, but I have to search for it" — that positioning has grown increasingly awkward in the mobile internet era.

One-line take on Taobao Tmall: the base remains large and the moat is narrowing, but no one can truly disrupt it in the near term.

2. Alibaba Cloud: the biggest hope for a second growth curve

Alibaba Cloud is in China's public cloud marketAbsolute leader, with a market share of about 34%, nearly twice that of second-place Huawei Cloud (about 19%).

What does the cloud business mean for Alibaba? It representsThe "utilities" of the AI eraIn 2025, Alibaba Cloud launched the Qwen large-model series and advanced on two fronts: the government and enterprise market and the SME segment. According to IDC data, China's AI cloud services market grew more than 60% year on year in 2025, with Alibaba Cloud holding a dominant position. If OpenAI defined the US AI era, then Alibaba Cloud plus Qwen is defining China's AI infrastructure standard.

More importantly, the cloud business's margins far exceed those of e-commerce. As scale effects materialize, Alibaba Cloud's margins are improving, and it has posted profits for several consecutive quarters in fiscal year 2026. Amid the AI wave, Alibaba Cloud is Alibaba's most noteworthy asset—Many people buying Alibaba are really betting on Alibaba Cloud + AI. This is a far bigger market than e-commerce itself.

3. International e-commerce: burning cash to buy market share

Alibaba's international e-commerce businesses include Lazada (Southeast Asia), AliExpress (global), Trendyol (Turkey) and Daraz (South Asia). This is Alibaba's fastest-growing segment, with growth reaching double digits, but it is alsoThe most aggressive cash burnsector.

In Southeast Asia, Shopee is a fierce competitor; in Europe, SHEIN and Temu (PDD's overseas arm) squeeze from both sides. Alibaba's international e-commerce arm positions itself as "infrastructure for Chinese manufacturing going global," but with global trade friction heating up, that road is far from smooth. This is a protracted war that demands patience and heavy capital.

4. Cainiao Logistics and local services

Cainiao is the core of Alibaba's logistics system and the moat behind the Alibaba ecosystem's fulfillment capability. Cainiao has been spun off from Alibaba Group to operate independently, but remains a key part of the Alibaba ecosystem. In the race for next-day and same-day delivery, Cainiao's integrated warehousing-and-delivery capability is Alibaba's key weapon against JD Logistics.

In local life services (Ele.me, Amap, Fliggy), Ele.me is at a clear disadvantage in its competition with Meituan — Meituan's share of the food delivery market has exceeded 65%, while Ele.me hovers around 30%. Amap is Alibaba's key card in the mobility space, with over 700 million monthly active users, but its monetization efficiency still has substantial room for improvement. Fliggy faces pressure from both Ctrip and Meituan in the OTA (online travel) market.

III. The biggest risk: not Pinduoduo, but the narrative that "nothing was done right"

The risks facing Alibaba have been widely discussed. We pick the three that truly matter:

Risk 1: Free cash flow turns negative

This is the most alarming signal. Alibaba's free cash flow (FCF) turned negative in fiscal year 2026. When a mature giant begins to see net cash outflows, either its investments have become too aggressive or its core business is weakening. For value investors, free cash flow is the anchor for valuation—if the anchor is lost, the valuation logic must be re-examined.

Risk 2: the systemic discount on China concept stocks

Alibaba is listed in the US, but it is fundamentally a Chinese company. China-US relations, audit working papers, Hong Kong stock liquidity… these "political risks" are beyond Alibaba's control through its own operations. Even if fundamentals improve, the valuation ceiling for Chinese ADRs is inherently lower than for US companies of the same caliber. The same business could command double the valuation in the US market — this is the "discount curse" of Chinese ADRs.

Risk 3: Loss of direction after management reshuffle

Joseph Tsai as Chairman, Eddie Wu as CEO, with Jack Ma fully in the background. The new management team is more pragmatic and lower-profile, but the question is — under pressure from Pinduoduo and Douyin, how exactly will Alibaba fight back? Since 2024, Alibaba has restructured its organization at least three times, from "1+6+N" to re-consolidation... Behind all these moves is a sense of anxiety:Alibaba has yet to find its own "Douyin moment" or "Pinduoduo narrative". While rivals capture territory with new playbooks, Alibaba is still searching for direction in a cycle of "split and remerge, remerge and split."

IV. But why does it still warrant attention?

Having covered the risks, let us return to the essential question: is a company trading at just 15x PE and 0.22x PB actually cheap?

Frankly, Alibaba in 2026 looks a bit like Tencent in 2016—core business under challenge, new businesses still incubating, and the market pricing the company for "decline." But Tencent went on to carve out a second growth curve with WeChat Pay and Honor of Kings. What will Alibaba's second curve be?

Scenario one:Alibaba Cloud + AI is the biggest variableIf Qwen scales in the government and enterprise market, Alibaba Cloud's annual revenue growth could return above 20%, which would drive a multiple-level re-rating of its valuation. The market has not yet fully priced Alibaba Cloud's AI story.

Scenario two:Restructuring the core e-commerce business. If Taobao Tmall can find a differentiated path on "low price + content" — for example by strengthening Taobao Live and the content-driven transformation of Guangguang — it remains China's largest consumer gateway. A 45% market share cannot be lost overnight, and Alibaba's supply chain and merchant ecosystem remain competitive moats.

Scenario three:Changes in shareholder returnsAlibaba holds ample cash, and in fiscal 2025 it rolled out a USD 25 billion share buyback program. If management shifts its strategic focus from "expansion" to "returns," the 0.22x PB alone implies substantial room to trade at a discount to book value. The power of buybacks and share cancellation would be significantly amplified at such a valuation.

Core conclusion:

Alibaba is not a "good company" right now—it is an excellent company going through a midlife crisis. The essence of a midlife crisis is not aging, but no longer being the market's "favorite." The other side of a midlife crisis, however, is having accumulated a sufficiently deep moat and sufficiently thick reserves.

Buying a company with RMB 1 trillion in annual revenue that still ranks first in China's e-commerce and cloud computing sectors at 0.22x PB—those odds deserve serious consideration.

V. Closing remarks

Over the past two decades of China's internet sector, every giant has faced its darkest hour. Tencent fell to 11x PE after the 3Q battle in 2011; Baidu lost two-thirds of its market value in the O2O bloodbath in 2016; Meituan dropped below HKD 80 in 2022 amid antitrust pressure.

Alibaba today stands at the same crossroads. No one can guarantee it will find its way out—but history tells us that China's best internet companies often harbor their greatest turnaround opportunities when they are most deeply undervalued.

Down 49%, PE 15x, PB 0.22. This is not a recommendation to buy—it is a reminder to pay attention.
