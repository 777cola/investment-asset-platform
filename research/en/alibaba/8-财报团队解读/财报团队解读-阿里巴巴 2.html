1. ROE — the true temperature of profitability

Buffett values long-term, stable, high ROE above all. Alibaba's net profit attributable to shareholders fell 18.2% year on year in FY2026, dragging ROE down with it. Although revenue still exceeded RMB 1 trillion,The collapse in profit margins is the core issue.。

The surge in SG&A expenses directly caused the operating margin to plunge to 4.90%. For a "mature" tech giant, an operating margin of 4.90% isWarning signs。

ROE diagnosis: Fails the test. Profit has swung sharply over the past three years (FY2024: 79.7B, FY2025: 129.5B, FY2026: 105.9B), lacking predictability and failing to meet Buffett's "consistent earnings" standard.

2. Moat — wide or drying up?

Alibaba once had the widest moat in China: network effects, brand barriers, switching costs. But over the past three years the moat has beenEroded: Pinduoduo erodes the low-price market, Douyin bypasses traditional shelf e-commerce, and the cloud computing price war squeezes margins.

What would Buffett ask?"Will this company still be around in 10 years? Will its competitive advantage be stronger or weaker in 10 years?" The answer is "still around, but the advantage is weakening."

3. Free cash flow — the biggest red flag

Free cash flow was -RMB 49.85 billion, which is the situation Buffett finds most unacceptable. CapEx reached RMB 126.06 billion, far exceeding operating cash flow. This means that in FY2026, Alibaba isA cash incinerator, not a money printer。

4. Buffett's judgment framework

✅ He will like itBrand recognition persists, management is actively restructuring, and the balance sheet is solid.

❌ He will refuse: Negative free cash flow (the most fatal), unstable margins on a downward trend, an eroding moat, and massive capex suppressing shareholder returns.

Conclusion

A good company, but not a good investment at present—unless the price offers a huge margin of safety sufficient to cover transformation risk.

Duan Yongping's Close Reading: Business Models and "Benfen"

Duan YongpingPerspective: business model, "Benfen", corporate culture, long-termism

Business model diagnosis

Advantages: Alibaba is essentially a "commercial real estate" model—it provides merchants with infrastructure such as traffic, payments and logistics, and charges commissions and advertising fees. This is aAsset-light, high-marginAlibaba Cloud is a subscription-based, high-stickiness, high-quality model.

Disadvantages: SG&A surged—a signal of not "staying true to one's roots." Operating margin was only 4.90%. Duan Yongping would press: "Where did the money go? Was it necessary competitive investment, or is management frittering it away?"

One metric Duan Yongping values most: free cash flow. If a company cannot generate positive free cash flow over the long run, its business model has fundamental problems.

Corporate culture assessment

✅ Bonus points: The "customer first" value, the "1+6+N" split to refocus on the core business, and the return of founder culture (Joseph Tsai + Eddie Wu).

❌ Negative factor: Past over-expansion in community group buying and local services showed a lack of "benfen"; bloated organization and slow decision-making; lagging response to user needs.

Duan Yongping-style soul-searching question

The answer is likely no—not because Alibaba is not excellent, but because it is in the throes of transition.

Munger's close reading: incentives and that famous "misjudgment"

MungerPerspective: incentives, organizational structure, industry ecosystem, countercyclical positioning

Incentive mechanism: where is the problem?

Munger focused intensely on whether incentive structures distort behavior. Professional managers (the Zhang Yong era) pursued short-term growth metrics, with bonuses tied to revenue growth rather than ROIC, which led to overexpansion. The massive losses in community group buying and local life services were precisely the result of distorted incentives.

Munger would comment:"If you do something stupid because someone else is doing something stupid, you are not just stupid, you are doubly stupid."

Organizational structure: the "entropy increase" of large companies

Alibaba's business lines are sprawling—e-commerce, cloud, logistics, local services, entertainment, overseas—each demanding heavy capital. Munger would call this a textbook case of"Six-Fingered Demon of the Zither"The "1+6+N" split reform is a direction Munger would applaud—making each business unit account for itself and bear its own profits and losses.

A Review of Munger's Investment Practice

Time of purchase (2021-2022): Munger may have made a "price anchoring" error—seeing Alibaba fall from USD 330 to just over USD 100 and judging it cheap. He underestimated theSystemic risk。

When trimming the position (2023): Munger admitted he "got it wrong" — and that is his most admirable quality. He recognized this lay outside the core of his "circle of competence."

The "too difficult" basket

Munger would put Alibaba inThe "too difficult" basketLi — "This company has too many variables; I can't build enough certainty."

Munger's inversion"If Alibaba's share price fell to one-third of its current level, would I buy more?"
If the answer is "uncertain," then now is not the time for a heavy position.

Conclusion

Buying was a misjudgment; trimming was a timely correction. The stock remains in the "too hard" bucket.

The free cash flow and DCF paradox

Negative free cash flow invalidates any DCF model. Li Lu was forced to switch to an asset-value approach—itself a downgrade. In "Civilization, Modernization, Value Investing and China," Li Lu stresses:"The premise of value investing is the ability to make a relatively accurate assessment of a business."

Li Lu's "modernization" framework

Li Lu argues that China is moving from a phase of "quantitative growth" to one of "qualitative growth." Within this transition, Alibaba's role resembles that of "a Chinese Microsoft in the midst of transformation"—focusing on cloud computing and AI, cutting unprofitable businesses, and lifting margins.

Why did free cash flow turn negative?

Free cash flow = operating cash flow − capital expenditure

FY2026 operating cash flow is approximatelyRMB 76.14 billion(estimated), minus CapExRMB 126.06 billion, obtaining-RMB 49.85 billion。

This means the cash Alibaba generates each day from its core business is not enough to cover its investment in infrastructure.This is not a sustainable state.

Why did the operating margin decline?

The 4.90% operating margin was mainly dragged down by the following factors:

  1. SG&A expenses: Sharply increased sales and marketing spending to counter competition (Pinduoduo/Douyin)
  2. R&D investment: Rising R&D spending on AI large models and cloud computing infrastructure
  3. Overseas expansion: Overseas businesses such as Lazada and Trendyol are still burning cash
