    Business Analysis — Duan Yongping's Perspective

    "Good business + good price + good timing" | Focus on the essence of the business model and the moat

    Alibaba Business Analysis Report — Duan Yongping's Perspective


    I. Underlying business logic: what business is Alibaba really in?


    Alibaba is essentially a"E-commerce infrastructure + cloud computing + digital consumption"a conglomerate. Unlike JD.com and Pinduoduo, Alibaba is not just a retailer but the infrastructure builder of China's digital economy—Taobao/Tmall is the underlying platform for goods transactions, Alibaba Cloud the underlying platform for computing power, and Cainiao the underlying platform for logistics.


    The first question Duan Yongping would ask: is this business "benfen" (true to its fundamental nature)?


    Alibaba's mission is "to make it easy to do business anywhere," and in essence it is in the business ofConnectAndEmpower—Connecting merchants and consumers, empowering small and medium businesses to do business. This positioning itself is "Benfen".


    In recent years, however, Alibaba has strayed somewhat from its strategic core:

  • Blind expansion: from e-commerce to entertainment (Youku/Damai), to new retail (Freshippo/Yintai), to local life services (Ele.me/Koubei)—in many areas it never established a sustainable competitive advantage
  • Management turmoil: from Jack Ma's retirement to Daniel Zhang's handover to the return of Wu Yongming and Joseph Tsai — 3.0 changes at the top in 3 years
  • Repeated organizational restructuring: from the "middle platform strategy" to the "1+6+N" split and then to "AI-driven" — a sign that the strategic direction is not yet fully thought through

    A good price?A PB of 0.22 means the market thinks Alibaba's assets are worth only 22% of book value — extremely pessimistic. But given heavy investment impairments, goodwill risk and a deteriorating competitive landscape, the pessimism may be justified.

A PB of 0.22 is certainly tempting, but if the business itself runs into trouble, the value of its book assets needs to be discounted. On Alibaba, I lean toward putting it on the watchlist first — waiting for management to stabilize and the competitive landscape to become clearer before making a decision."

    Key metrics Buffett would watch


    1. Revenue growth of only 2.74%

    For a company trading at 15x PE, revenue growth of less than 3% is a red flag. Buffett favors companies with strong organic growth.


    2. Net profit fell 18.2%

    Net profit fell sharply even as revenue still grew — the surge in SG&A was the main cause. This reflects Alibaba being forced to ramp up sales spending under competitive pressure, a form of "defensive spending".


    3. Operating margin just 4.90%

    For a company whose operating margin once held steady above 20%, 4.90% is practically the edge of a cliff. If competition keeps worsening, Alibaba will face the grim prospect of "earning hard money" or even "earning nothing at all."


    4. PB 0.22 — what is the market saying?

    The market believes much of Alibaba's asset value is overstated (goodwill, investments, fixed assets); the market expects Alibaba's future earnings to keep deteriorating. Buffett would ask:"Is the market right?"



    II. Free cash flow — Buffett's "shareholder return engine"

    What would Buffett say?


    "Negative free cash flow means the company is burning shareholder value to sustain operations."


    Alibaba is already a mature company with annual revenue of ~RMB 1 trillion and revenue growth of just 2.74%. Negative FCF under these conditions can only mean:

  • CapEx too high (RMB 126 billion) — massive cloud AI infrastructure spending, but returns yet to materialize
  • Operating cash flow growth is insufficient to fund CapEx—competition has compressed operating profit

    • The Buffett-style question:"If Alibaba's FCF stays negative for five consecutive years, is it still worth its current price?" Net cash is only RMB 56.9 billion; if it burns RMB 50 billion a year, that cash lasts just over a year.

    2. PB 0.22—deeply undervalued or a value trap?

    Much of Alibaba's assets are goodwill (from acquisitions) and long-term investments (already heavily impaired), with genuine hard assets accounting for only a small share. In liquidation, the value of goodwill and investments would be subject to steep discounts.

    I. Industry landscape: the "three-kingdom drama" of Chinese e-commerce

    The first question Munger would ask: why is Alibaba's moat narrowing?


    Answer: because e-commerce's "infrastructure layer" and "application layer" have been separated.


    In the past, Alibaba controlled both the "infrastructure" of e-commerce (product search, credit system, payments, logistics) and the "application layer" (buyer-seller transactions). But the rise of mobile internet decoupled these two layers:

  • DouyinUse content recommendations to bypass Alibaba's product-search infrastructure
  • PinduoduoUse social group buying plus rock-bottom prices to bypass Alibaba's brand-merchant resources
  • WeChat Mini ProgramLet merchants reach consumers directly

    • Munger would say: when an industry "decouples," existing moats dry up faster.


    Alibaba Cloud — the undervalued second ace

Yet Alibaba's total market cap is only ~USD 225 billion. This implies:Alibaba Cloud may account for 36% of Alibaba's total market cap — the market is essentially giving away e-commerce, logistics, international and all its other businesses for free.

When your rivals can consistently profit in your high-margin territory while you can only defend your turf—that is not a good business.



    III. Inversion — Munger's "20 Questions"


    Question 1: Is Alibaba's biggest risk fully priced in by the market?

    The market has already priced in a large part of this (PB 0.22, PE 15x), but some risks may not yet be fully priced: uncertain returns on AI investment, Ant Group's IPO being nowhere in sight, and delisting risk for Chinese ADRs.


    Question 2: What is the market not pricing in? Undervalued assets

  • Alibaba Cloud— Valued at 5x PS, worth ~$82B
  • Ant Group— Even at a 50% discount, Alibaba's stake is worth ~USD 30-50 billion
  • International e-commerce— Southeast Asia + Middle East + Europe footprint may be underestimated

    Question 3: Will Alibaba disappear?

    Short term (3 years):No. Alibaba still has the strongest merchandise supply chain and the largest pool of brand resources.

    Medium term (5-10 years):Could be marginalized. E-commerce share could fall from 35% to below 20%.

    Long term (10 years+):If spun off, Alibaba Cloud could become a USD 100 billion-class company.

    V. Munger's final judgment


    "Alibaba is a complicated case. It is not a simple 'good business or bad business' judgment—it has both good businesses (Alibaba Cloud, the payments ecosystem) and a business that is deteriorating (e-commerce). When a company contains both businesses you should buy and businesses you should sell, the most rational approach is: don't buy the company, buy only the part you want.     II. In-depth analysis of top risks


    Risk 1: E-commerce competition deteriorates (🔴 High)

    Current situation:Alibaba's share fell from over 70% to 35-40%. Pinduoduo's edge in the low-price segment is all but unshakable, and Douyin continues to grow fast in content-driven e-commerce.

    Best case:The three parties have reached a "Nash equilibrium" — each sticking to the area where it excels.

    Worst case:Alibaba's share continues to slide to 20-25%, and its e-commerce business has become a "low-profit + low-growth" utility.


    Risk 2: SG&A out of control (🔴 High)

    In SFY2026, the surge in SG&A caused the operating margin to plunge from a historical 20%+ to 4.90%. If the increased spending is aimed at preventing churn among existing customers (Alibaba's current situation is closer to the latter), then this is "cutting flesh to patch a sore."


    Risk 3: Negative free cash flow (🔴 High)

    FCF = -RMB 49,850 million (CapEx RMB 126,063 million). Negative free cash flow at a mature company with ~RMB 1 trillion in annual revenue is a highly dangerous signal. Burning RMB 50 billion a year, its net cash of RMB 56.9 billion lasts just over a year.


    Risk 4: delisting risk for US-listed Chinese stocks (🟡 Medium)

    PCAOB audit inspections made progress in 2022-2023, but US-China relations face rising uncertainty in 2026. Delisting risk is not the same as company risk—but it can severely hit share price and liquidity.


    Risk 5: Management turmoil (🟡 Medium)

    Three core management changes in 3 years. The frequent leadership turnover shows Alibaba has yet to find a stable governance structure for the post-Jack Ma era.
