1. Company overview and business characteristics (business model)

Company overview

Alibaba Group, founded in 1999, is China's largest comprehensive internet company, with core businesses spanningE-commerce (Taobao and Tmall Group), international e-commerce (Lazada/AliExpress/Trendyol), cloud computing (Alibaba Cloud), local services (Ele.me/Amap/Fliggy), logistics (Cainiao), digital media and entertainment (Youku/Damai)Six major segments. FY2026 revenue RMB 1,023.7 billion (about USD 142.2 billion), 131,462 employees.

Business model breakdown: the seven-layer flywheel

  1. Transaction layer (e-commerce): A super trading platform with China retail GMV of about RMB 8 trillion, monetized mainly through commissions, advertising and technical service fees
  2. Payment layer: Ant Group (divested but still 33%-owned) provides payment infrastructure, creating ecosystem synergies
  3. Logistics layer: Cainiao Network, integrating Tongda-system express delivery plus proprietary logistics, handles over 50 billion parcels annually
  4. Computing layer: Alibaba Cloud, No. 1 in China by market share (~34%), providing computing power services to millions of customers
  5. Internationalization layer:Lazada (Southeast Asia) + AliExpress (global) + Trendyol (Turkey): three overseas growth engines
  6. Local life services layer: Ele.me + Amap + Fliggy to capture "near-field consumption" scenarios
  7. Entertainment and investment segments: Youku + Damai + strategic investments across sectors (from cloud computing to AI to retail)
"Alibaba is essentially a super ecosystem aggregator—it connects Chinese consumers, merchants, logistics and computing resources, then continuously optimizes the efficiency of these connections through data and algorithms. Its value lies not in any single business, but in the synergies of the entire system."
— Core insight

Four Masters' Perspectives

BuffettValue perspective
Alibaba's business is enormous and has a certain economic moat (brand, scale, platform effects), but it has two characteristics Buffett dislikes: ①Capital intensity is rising— FY2026 CapEx RMB 126.1 billion, FCF turns negative, a signal Buffett warns against; ②High business complexity—From e-commerce to cloud to logistics to entertainment to AI, the scope is too broad and exceeds Buffett's "circle of competence" preference. But a PB of just 0.22x (below book!) means the market is pricing it at almost liquidation value—which in Buffett's framework would trigger the "cigar butt" alarm.
MungerMultidisciplinary thinking
Notably,Daily Journal, Munger's company, actually bought Alibaba in 2021-2022, although he fully exited the position in 2023 (at a loss of about 40%), this itself was a practice of his "contrarian thinking." At the time, he was bullish on the long-term value of Alibaba Cloud and the resilience of China's consumer market. Buying Alibaba demonstrated Munger's belief in "being greedy when others are fearful," while selling demonstrated the discipline of "admitting mistakes and iterating." Key lesson: even Munger himself made a mistake on Alibaba—this is precisely the price that contrarian thinking demands.
Duan YongpingThe Benfen Perspective
Duan Yongping has publicly stated on multiple occasionsBearish on Alibaba, arguing it was "not minding its own business" — the core criticisms: ① Taotian's GMV growth has slowed yet it still pursues scale rather than user experience; ② Alibaba Cloud is waging a price war under competitive pressure, eroding long-term value; ③ management's attention is spread across a large number of non-core businesses (digital media and entertainment, strategic investments, etc.) and is not "doing the right things." Duan Yongping stresses: a good business matters most; a good price comes second. Alibaba's current low valuation may be a "value trap" — not because the company is worth this price, but because it may never return to its past growth trajectory.
Li LuThe Certainty Perspective
Li Lu's investment framework centers on "certainty"—identifying the most certain beneficiaries of China's modernization. Alibaba, as the infrastructure of Chinese e-commerce and cloud computing, theoretically offers such certainty. But he would also focus on: ①Worsening competitive landscape— Pinduoduo, Douyin and JD.com are eroding Alibaba's turf from different directions, and certainty is declining; ②Management changes— Founder Jack Ma steps back; the new Tsai/Wu pairing needs to prove itself; ③Declining return on capital—ROE fell from over 20% to around 10%, a classic signal of diminished certainty.

2. Moat depth

Five moat assessment

Moat typeScoreAssessment
Platform network effects8/10China's e-commerce GMV exceeds RMB 8 trillion, and the two-sided network effects between buyers and sellers are extremely strong. Yet the rise of Pinduoduo and Douyin proves that network effects are not unassailable—when rivals enter with more efficient matching, an incumbent's network can be "bypassed." Key indicator: Taotian's GMV growth has nearly stalled.
Brand and trust7/10Taobao/Tmall enjoy extremely high brand recognition, built on consumer trust accumulated over years. Yet the "counterfeit goods" label persists in the mid-to-low-end market, and Pinduoduo is eroding this foundation of trust with a "cheap plus genuine" combination.
Switching costs8/10For sellers: store design, accumulated data, follower base and the operations tooling stack create high switching costs. For consumers: payment binding, order history and the review system create moderate switching costs. For cloud customers: migration costs are extremely high (data, applications, API dependencies).
Scale advantage9/10China's largest data-center network, the most complete logistics integration system (Cainiao plus the Tongda carriers), and the largest merchant and product base. These scale advantages are capital-intensive—it would take competitors tens of billions of US dollars to catch up.
Data flywheel7/10Massive transaction data trains algorithms → more precise recommendations → higher conversion → more data. But this data flywheel is being challenged by Douyin's interest-based recommendation and Pinduoduo's social fission—neither model relies on Alibaba-style traditional e-commerce data.

Four Masters' Perspectives

BuffettMoat assessment
"A truly great business must have an enduring moat that protects excellent returns on invested capital." Alibaba's moat—scale, data, network effects—is real and deep. But Buffett would also note: FY2026 operating margin plunged from 14.14% to 4.90%, with SG&A surging 47.7%. The moat is thinning—if margins keep sliding, the moat's "depth" needs reassessment. Buffett's biggest concern: does Alibaba need to invest ever more capital to sustain the same level of revenue and profit? The answer is:Yes, CapEx surged from JPY 32.1 billion in FY2024 to JPY 126.1 billion in FY2026This is a clear warning signal.
MungerMoat assessment
"Invert, always invert—what factors would cause a moat company to lose its moat?" Munger would ask: ① Have regulatory crackdowns (antitrust fines, higher data-compliance costs) permanently weakened Alibaba's competitive advantage? ② Are Pinduoduo and Douyin creating a "parallel universe" e-commerce ecosystem, such that Alibaba's network effects no longer reach new users? ③ Will AI disrupt the underlying technology of e-commerce search and recommendation, replacing Alibaba's data advantage with a new paradigm? Munger once "missed" on Alibaba—which precisely shows that even a mind like his can overestimate the durability of a moat.
Duan YongpingMoat assessment
"A good moat is one others cannot breach even if they try, and one you yourself will not actively destroy." Duan Yongping believes Alibaba's biggest problem is not external competition, butSelf-destruction. The large entertainment segment burns billions every year without generating returns; its investment portfolio reaches RMB 656.7 billion but many investments yield low returns; Ele.me and Meituan keep burning cash in competition. None of this is "benfen"-style capital allocation. In Duan Yongping's view, if management itself does not respect the moat, the moat will eventually run dry.
Li LuMoat assessment
"The real moat is the ability to keep evolving." Alibaba has historically shown strong evolutionary capacity—from B2B to C2C to B2C to cloud computing to internationalization. But the pace of evolution has slowed over the past two years: Taotian's responses (the 1688 strategic upgrade, the price war) look reactive rather than proactive; cloud computing's competitive edge is being eroded by Huawei Cloud and Tencent Cloud. Li Lu would ask: does Alibaba still hold to its original mission of "making it easy to do business anywhere"? If mission-drivenness weakens, the foundation of the moat will wobble too.
"The most honest assessment of Alibaba's moat is this: it owns China's largest e-commerce infrastructure and the most complete ecosystem synergies, but the competitive landscape is deteriorating faster than the moat is being reinforced. The moat has gone from extremely wide (2020) to relatively wide (2026), with the trend pointing downward."
— Overall moat assessment

Comprehensive Analysis of Financial Position

The most notable deterioration signals (ranked by severity):

  1. ⚠️ FCF turned negative (-RMB 49.9 billion): This is the most dangerous signal. Negative FCF means the company is burning cash to sustain operations—a state absolutely intolerable within a value-investing framework (Buffett: FCF is everything). The core reason is CapEx surging from RMB 32.1 billion in FY2024 to RMB 126.1 billion in FY2026 (+293%). Can such aggressive capital spending truly generate future returns? Extreme vigilance is warranted.
  2. ⚠️ SG&A surged (+47.7% to RMB 278.1 billion)Alibaba is sharply increasing selling and administrative expenses. This may directly reflect competitive pressure—to fight for merchants and users, Alibaba has to spend more. If so, this will be structural rather than one-off—meaning margins could be suppressed over the long term.
  3. ⚠️ Sharp net cash burn (from RMB 400.3 billion to RMB 56.9 billion, -86%)Alibaba's net cash fell from RMB 400 billion to less than RMB 60 billion in two years. Although it still holds net cash of RMB 56.9 billion (still positive), the burn rate is troubling. Combined with interest-bearing debt rising from RMB 170.8 billion to RMB 260 billion, financial flexibility is weakening fast.
  4. ⚠️ Sharp cutback in buybacks (-91.2% → RMB 7.6 billion): Slashing buybacks when the share price sits at a 10-year low—likely because management "has no money to buy back" or "doesn't think the stock is cheap enough." Either explanation is bad news.
  5. ⚠️ Operating cash flow fell (-53.4% to RMB 76.2 billion): The decline in operating cash flow is more severe than the decline in net profit—indicating deteriorating earnings quality, with more profit coming from non-cash items or the consumption of working capital.

Positive factors:

  • Gross margin stable at ~40% — pricing power in the core business has not been materially lost
  • Book value per share keeps growing (+5.5% to ¥441)
  • PB just 0.22x — the market is pricing the stock below book value, which in itself signals extreme undervaluation
  • Dividends keep growing (FY2026 RMB 33.7 billion, +16% YoY) — modest in size, but the right direction
  • Cash plus short-term investments still total RMB 316.9 billion, so short-term liquidity is not a concern

BuffettFinancial assessment
"Ultimately, what we care about most is free cash flow—the ultimate yardstick." Alibaba's FY2026 FCF was -RMB 49.9 billion, which in Buffett's framework isFatal flawBuffett's preferred company trait: FCF/revenue >15%. FY2024 was 15.99% (passes), FY2025 fell to 7.78% (below standard), FY2026 turned negative (fails). The trend is deteriorating rapidly. SG&A surge + CapEx surge + FCF turning negative + sharp drop in net cash — with all four metrics deteriorating simultaneously, Buffett would never accept this. He would say: "Only when the tide goes out do you discover who's been swimming naked." By this standard, Alibaba's FY2026 financials amount toSwimming naked。
MungerFinancial assessment
"Cross-validate financial quality using multiple models." Munger would examine it from several angles: ①Human nature model—Slashing buybacks when the stock has fallen 91% is a classic human behavioral bias (panic → conservatism), not rational capital allocation; ②Economies of scale model— Has Alibaba's scale advantage truly translated into a cost advantage? FY2026 data shows: scale is growing (revenue +2.7%), but SG&A rose 47.7% — diseconomies of scale are setting in; ③Signal model— Cutting buybacks when the share price is low plus expanding CapEx are two contradictory signals: management both thinks the stock is not worth buying (no buybacks) and that the future is worth heavy investment (CapEx expansion). This is not logical.
Duan YongpingFinancial assessment
"Companies that borrow money are off-limits—because once you borrow, you lose the initiative." Alibaba is still in a net cash position (RMB 56.9 billion), but interest-bearing debt rose from RMB 170.8 billion to RMB 260 billion, an unsettling trend. What Duan Yongping focuses on most is: the company'sBreakeven pointis rising. Operating margin fell from 14% to 5%, meaning profit shrinks sharply on the same revenue. If market competition worsens further, could Alibaba slip into losses? Current operating profit is RMB 50.2 billion, while the EBITDA margin fell from 18.4% last year to 9.5% — the safety buffer is thinning.
Li LuFinancial assessment
"The best way to understand a company's finances is to look at how it performs at the trough." Alibaba is now in a clear trough — revenue growth near zero, FCF turning negative, SG&A surging. Li Lu would ask two questions: (1) Is this cyclical or structural? If cyclical (intensified competition driving a short-term increase in investment), it is tolerable; if structural (a permanent erosion of the business model's own competitive advantage), it requires re-evaluation. (2) Does the company have sufficient financial resources to ride out the trough? RMB 316.9 billion in cash plus RMB 656.7 billion in long-term investments — abundant resources, but the pace of net cash burn is too fast.

Certainty assessment framework

High-certainty components:A neutral view of Taotian's core e-commerce: even at low growth (0-3%), it still generates RMB 30-50 billion in operating profit each year. This is Alibaba's cash cow, with relatively high certainty.

Moderate-confidence portion:International e-commerce (fast growth but poor profitability; Lazada still unprofitable) + Cainiao logistics (good growth but unclear independence and IPO path).

Low-certainty portion:Alibaba Cloud (the price war on the front end caps profit upside) + local services (continued cash burn) + digital media and entertainment (strategic value in doubt).

The most uncertain part:Realizable value and timing of the portfolio. The RMB 656.7 billion of investments spans multiple industries, but the true value and liquidity of the many unlisted assets remain unknown.

Four Masters' perspectives

BuffettGrowth assessment
"Certainty matters more than growth." The core problem facing Alibaba:High-growth businesses do not make money (international e-commerce), money-making businesses do not grow (Taobao and Tmall), and the businesses that most need investment face brutal competition (cloud and local life services)This pattern of "good businesses that don't grow, and growing businesses that aren't good" is unwelcome in the Buffett framework. He would rather buy businesses with certain growth and rich profits at a fair price (such as Coca-Cola and Apple) than buy businesses with uncertain growth and deteriorating profits at a cheap price.
MungerGrowth assessment
"The best way to predict the future is to understand which factors will not change." What will not change in ten years: ① Chinese consumers will still need to shop online, but the channels will change; ② Chinese enterprises will still need cloud computing, but the vendors will change; ③ Cross-border trade will still need logistics, but the logistics methods will change. The challenge Alibaba faces is this: its position within each "unchanging need" is being eroded—in e-commerce, nibbled away by Pinduoduo/Douyin; in cloud, chased by Huawei/Tencent. Munger would press the question: can Alibaba still hold its "supplier of first choice" status in every important track? The answer is increasingly uncertain.
Duan YongpingGrowth assessment
"Good companies and good prices matter more than high growth." From a benfen perspective, Alibaba's core problem today is this: the company has not gotten the most important things right. What matters most for Taotian? User experience. Yet over the past few years, Taotian has not held a clear lead over Pinduoduo and Douyin on user experience. What matters most for Alibaba Cloud? Technology and cost-performance. But the price war has eroded its technology premium. Duan Yongping would say: don't think about growth first; think first about how to take the existing businessDo the right things, do them deeply, do them wellIf core e-commerce cannot make users "unable to do without you," how can internationalization and AI growth even be discussed?
Li LuGrowth assessment
"The process of China's modernization is the best growth story there is." Driven by three long-term trends—consumption upgrading among 400 million middle-class consumers, the digital transformation of Chinese enterprises, and the globalization of cross-border e-commerce—Alibaba sits in a highly certain "lane." But Li Lu also stresses:A good sector does not guarantee a winning companyCan Alibaba make the right strategic choice at every node? That depends on management's judgment and execution. Against the backdrop of current management turnover and strategic vacillation, that is not reassuring.

Four Masters' Perspectives

BuffettRisk assessment
"Risk comes from not knowing what you are doing." For most investors, Alibaba is a highly complex business — e-commerce + cloud + logistics + entertainment + fintech + investments... Few can truly understand all of it. Buffett would advise: if you cannot understand how each business makes money, do not buy. With Alibaba, most investors can probably only "truly understand" its e-commerce and cloud businesses (~55% of revenue), while the other 45% lies outside their circle of competence. In addition, negative FCF in Buffett's framework isHigh-risk signals— A company that cannot generate free cash flow rests on an extremely fragile valuation foundation.
MungerRisk assessment
"Avoid the obvious stupidities." Munger would spend just two minutes on this risk checklist, then say:"Worsening competitive landscape + regulatory risk + management uncertainty—all three at once is reason enough to 'pass'.". His Daily Journal actually held Alibaba and ultimately exited at a loss — a real-world case: even Munger struggled to win when playing against three uncertainties at once: regulation, competition and management. In Munger's framework, "what not to do" matters as much as "what to do".
Duan YongpingRisk assessment
"What you don't do matters more than what you do." Duan Yongping long ago gave his verdict on Alibaba: "Don't buy." His logic is simple and profound: (1) He cannot understand why the core business (Taotian) is being eroded by Pinduoduo and Douyin; (2) he cannot understand why management would do so many things that go against "benfen" (integrity and duty) — Greater Entertainment, a large volume of investments; (3) he cannot understand why buybacks would be cut when the share price is low. With these three "cannot understands" stacked together, the conclusion is "don't invest." Duan Yongping's famous line:"When you hesitate between a good company and a good price, it means the company isn't good enough."
Li LuRisk assessment
"The core of risk management is understanding the dangers and opportunities of the company you invest in, then determining whether your margin of safety is sufficient." Li Lu's risk-management framework emphasizes: ① The risk of permanent capital loss — will Alibaba go bankrupt? Valuation deep dive

What does a PB of 0.22x mean?

Alibaba's book value per share is RMB 441.23 (=~$61/ADR), while the stock trades at just $98.40. PB=0.22 means the market believes Alibaba'sExtreme discount on asset qualityBut this does not necessarily mean "value discovered"—it may be because:

Is a PE of 15.35x reasonable?

At a normal operating profit level (FY2025: RMB 140.9 billion, operating margin 14.14%), the PE is about 10-11x — very cheap. But at the FY2026 operating profit level (RMB 50.2 billion, operating margin 4.90%), the PE is about 30-35x — not cheap. So the core question is:Is the FY2026 profit deterioration one-off or structural?If the short-term increase in SG&A and CapEx caused it, it can be repaired in the future, and the current PE is actually in the 10-11x range (calculated on normalized profit); if structural competitive pressure caused it, the "true level" of the current PE is 15-20x — reasonable but not cheap.

Overall conclusion: would the four masters invest in Alibaba?

Warren Buffett: Probably not (30% probability)

Core obstacles: ① FCF turned negative — a "veto" item in Buffett's framework; ② operating margin plunged from 14% to 5% — competitiveness is deteriorating; ③ business complexity exceeds the circle of competence; ④ SG&A surge + CapEx surge + sharp drop in net cash — a triple drag. The only attraction is the extreme cheapness of 0.22x PB — but he would likely say: "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price." Alibaba is currently in a transition whose outcome — "wonderful or fair" — carries too much uncertainty.

Charlie Munger: Probably not (25% probability)

Munger has already "paid his tuition" on Alibaba (Daily Journal exited its position at a loss of about 40%). Given his wisdom of "not walking the old road," the odds of him investing in Alibaba again are slim. He would say: "What I learned from Alibaba is that even the best investment thesis can be wrong. It's not about whether you make mistakes, but what you learn from them." What Munger learned: ① Chinese ADRs carry regulatory risks he cannot control; ② the competitive model of Pinduoduo plus Douyin is beyond his circle of understanding; ③ management stability is more fragile than he expected. Were he to act again, he would need to see marked improvement on all three fronts.

Duan Yongping: Most likely not (15% probability)

Duan Yongping has publicly stated his bearish view on Alibaba several times. His core logic is unchanged: 1) the business model is deteriorating (intensifying competition, falling margins); 2) the corporate culture is not sufficiently "benfen" (overly broad business scope, inefficient investment portfolio); 3) the quality of management's decisions at key moments is questionable (scaled-back buybacks, strategic vacillation). None of the three conditions gives him reassurance. Even at a cheap price, he will not act — "don't buy what you don't understand and what you're not confident about" is his iron rule.
