Cognitive Reset: Why Most People Misunderstand Tencent

The Misread King of Chinese Internet

If you bought Tencent in early 2021, the stock was around HK$683; if you sold today in July 2026, it is around HK$463. Five years, a 32% loss. It looks like a bad investment.

But over the same period, Tencent's net profit grew from RMB 224.8 billion to RMB 224.8 billion—wait, unchanged? No: the RMB 224.8 billion in 2021 included RMB 119.4 billion of investment gains (fair value changes on Meituan, Pinduoduo and others), and adjusted core net profit was only RMB 123.2 billion. The RMB 224.8 billion in 2025 is almost entirely hard operating profit. Core profit nearly doubled, yet the stock fell 32%.

This is the first layer of cognitive misunderstanding:You think Tencent is declining, but in reality it is transforming.

Misconception 1: "Tencent has no growth left"

Look at the data:

More critical is the gross margin. It climbed from 43.9% all the way to 56.3%, up 12.4 percentage points over five years.This is no coincidence but the result of a strategic transformation.

Tencent's revenue mix historically carried a heavy weight of low-margin game channel distribution and cloud hardware resale. Now, WeChat Channels advertising (high margin), WeChat Pay (high margin), mini-game revenue share (almost pure profit) and WeCom services — these high-profit businesses keep gaining share.

In plain terms:Tencent used to spend RMB 56 in costs for every RMB 100 it earned; now it spends only RMB 44 for every RMB 100.And this trend is continuing.

Misconception 2: "Tencent has been crushed by Douyin"

This is one of the biggest cognitive biases.

ByteDance's 2025 revenue was about USD 120 billion (roughly RMB 850 billion), indeed surpassing Tencent. Douyin's DAU is about 800 million (600 million domestic plus 200 million overseas), and user time spent also steadily exceeds WeChat's.

But there is a key problem here:User time is not a moat, still less a source of profit.

Douyin's formidable algorithm is indeed a marvel, but at its core it is an "attention-extraction machine" — users scroll through one video after another, feeling emptier and more anxious the more they scroll. Its commercial value comes from the extreme plunder of users' time.

WeChat, by contrast, is "digital infrastructure" — your social graph, work communications, payments, ID documents, health codes and the entire mini-program ecosystem all live on it.Leave Douyin and you will be bored; leave WeChat and you will lose touch.That is the essential difference.

The data doesn't lie: WeChat Channels generated about RMB 40 billion in advertising revenue in 2025, versus about RMB 350 billion for Douyin. The gap looks enormous. But WeChat Channels' ad load is only 6%, against roughly 15% for Douyin. If WeChat Channels raised its ad load to 10% — still far below Douyin — that alone would bring in about RMB 67 billion in advertising revenue, equivalent to building another Bilibili.

More critically:WeChat DAU 1.35 billion; Douyin domestic DAU about 600 million (about 800 million including Douyin Lite).WeChat's depth and breadth of reach is something Douyin lacks. Channels does not need to beat Douyin; it only needs to put the infrastructure within the WeChat ecosystem to use, and it will naturally grow into a hundred-billion-level revenue business.

Misconception 3: "Tencent only copies others"

"Tencent doesn't innovate, it only copies." This is perhaps one of the most deeply ingrained misconceptions on the Chinese internet.

Tencent does have a history of "micro-innovation" — QQ copied ICQ, WeChat copied Kik — but imitation and plagiarism are two different things. ICQ was no match for QQ, and does anyone still use Kik today?

In fact, Tencent's product capability is severely underestimated:

  • **WeChat Red Envelope**: Its explosive rise during Chinese New Year 2014 caught Alipay off guard. The product design—not just the feature, but the seamless fusion of "social + finance + gamification"—was a genuine world-class innovation. Jack Ma's description of it as a "Pearl Harbor sneak attack" was no exaggeration.
  • **Mini-program ecosystem**: Launched in 2017, it was initially mocked as "yet another thing that makes developers' lives difficult." Eight years later, mini-programs have over 600 million DAU and annual transaction volume above RMB 5 trillion. No tech company anywhere has built anything comparable. Google's PWA, Baidu's smart mini-programs and Alipay's mini-programs all lag far behind WeChat mini-programs in both influence and ecosystem vitality.
  • **WeChat Channels**: Although it entered after ByteDance had validated the short-video track, WeChat Channels' product design has unique features—a hybrid distribution based on social plus algorithmic recommendation, creating the "liked by friends" social dimension that Douyin lacks. From 2023 to 2025, WeChat Channels' DAU grew from 300 million to over 600 million, a growth rate far exceeding Kuaishou in its early years.
  • **WeCom**: the only enterprise office software connected to personal WeChat, which is in itself an overwhelming advantage. However good DingTalk and Feishu are, you cannot add a client's personal WeChat directly on them.

Tencent's real problem is not that it "cannot innovate," but ratherSide effects of the internal horse-racing mechanism—Departments operated in silos, and much innovation was consumed by internal friction. Tencent Weibo, for example, lost to Sina Weibo; Tencent's e-commerce unit (Paipai) lost to Taobao; and Tencent Search (Soso) lost to Baidu. But this is also Tencent's ruthless philosophy:Let the failures fail, and let the survivors become kings.

Misconception 4: "Naspers trimming its stake means Tencent is finished"

This was the single biggest factor behind Tencent's lackluster stock price from 2021 to 2026, and it has been seriously misunderstood.

Naspers invested USD 32 million in Tencent in 2001 for about a 46% stake. That investment was worth roughly USD 200 billion at its peak—The greatest single investment in the history of human investing, bar none, with a return of more than 6,000x.

Starting in 2021, Naspers began systematically trimming its stake—not because it had turned bearish on Tencent, but because its own market capitalization had long stood below the value of the Tencent shares it held (the well-known "Naspers discount" problem). To narrow that discount, Naspers had to keep selling Tencent and buying back its own stock.

From June 2022 to 2026, Naspers sold roughly HKD 100 million of Tencent stock almost every day, offloading a cumulative HKD 150 billion. This is not "fleeing China," this isArbitrage trading。

Imagine this: if a single seller dumped RMB 100 million worth of Apple stock on the market every day, how far would Apple's share price be suppressed? That is the non-fundamental pressure facing Tencent.

The good news: Naspers is slowing its pace of selling down. PROSUS announced in 2025 that the scale of its selldown will gradually shrink. At the current pace, Naspers' selldown will largely be over by 2027-2028.

That is to say:The biggest overhang on Tencent's share price is counting down.

Misconception 5: "Tencent is a Chinese company, and China risk is too high"

Half right. Tencent is indeed a company incorporated in the Cayman Islands, listed in Hong Kong and operating in China. Chinese government regulatory policy directly affects Tencent—game licences, antitrust, fintech regulation, minor protection—every one of these cuts right into Tencent.

But viewed from another angle:WeChat is the only truly "infrastructure-grade" product in Chinese internet, its status is no different from the power grid or running water. The Chinese government understands this better than anyone. Regulation is meant to discipline it, not destroy it.

The regulatory storm after 2021 proved one thing: Tencent took a beating, but nothing life-threatening. Game licences resumed, the fintech regulatory framework took shape, and the antitrust fine (about RMB 10 billion) was a drop in the bucket for Tencent.

More importantly, Tencent's profitability does not depend on policy support at all. Gaming, advertising, fintech—these are all commercial value created by real demand.Even under the strictest regulatory environment, Tencent still generates more than RMB 200 billion in free cash flow each year.

Core points of the cognitive reset

If you have read this far, I hope what you take away is not the conclusion that "Tencent is good," but a set ofRethinking the framework for Tencent：

1. **Look at Tencent's core profit, not reported profit** — stripping out investment gains, Tencent's true earning power has doubled over the past five years.

2. Look at Tencent through free cash flow, not net profit— FCF RMB 215.6 billion, a 95.9% net income conversion rate: a top-tier cash-generating machine.

3. View Tencent through an ecosystem lens, not a product lens— WeChat is not an app; it is a digital society covering 1.35 billion people.

4. View regulation with a cyclical mindset, not panic— Regulation is not the end, but a fresh start after clearing out the ranks.

5. View growth with endgame thinking, not present-day thinking— WeChat Channels ad load rising from 6% to 10%, overseas game revenue share from 29% to 50%, fintech expanding from payments to wealth management — each step offers a clear runway for growth.

Tencent has not changed; what has changed is how the market sees it.Mr. Market's view, by contrast, has always been short-sighted.

Moat dissection: how deep does WeChat's "irreplaceability" really run?

A moat that is no moat, but an open sea

What Buffett calls a "moat" is a company's ability to fend off competition and sustain excess profits. For most companies, the moat is a river—at best a lake. But for Tencent, and especially for WeChat,What it has is not a moat but the entire Pacific Ocean.

Sounds exaggerated? Let us peel back the layers.

Layer one: social-network effects — the irreplicable atomic core

WeChat's core moat isSocial network effectsThis concept has been overused, yet many people have never truly grasped its depth.

Network effects: The value of a network equals the square of its users (Metcalfe's law). WeChat has 1.35 billion monthly active users. This is not a network; it is a country.

But user numbers alone are not enough. China once had more than a dozen instant messaging apps—Mi Chat, Fetion, Yixin, Laiwang, Momo, Tantan... They all had users too. But what set WeChat apart was that it combinedFour distinct network effects converged.：

1. **Personal social network**: your family, friends, classmates and colleagues are all on it. This is the most fundamental layer. Leaving WeChat means leaving your social circle.

2. Work relationship network: After WeCom and WeChat interconnect, your customers, partners and suppliers are all on WeChat. Leaving WeChat means cutting off working relationships.

3. Public service networkSocial security, health insurance, health codes, electronic ID cards, traffic fines, and utility payments for water, electricity and gas all live in WeChat Mini Programs. Living in China without WeChat means going back ten years.

4. Commercial services network: Mini Program payments, WeChat Official Accounts, Channels stores — the merchants you follow, the mini programs of the coffee shops you frequent, the content creators you subscribe to all live in the WeChat ecosystem. Leaving WeChat means losing convenience.

WeChat wants to replace all four networks. What you need is not a better chat app, butA separate full digital ecosystem covering 1.35 billion peopleThis is technically feasible but sociologically impossible—because your friends, family, colleagues and clients will not migrate together with you at the same time.

This is the true depth of WeChat's moat:The irreplaceability is not at the product level but at the social level.

Layer two: switching costs — not "somewhat high" but "no alternative exists"

In economics there is a concept called switching cost — the difficulty for users to switch from product A to product B.

Switching costs are visible for most products: moving from an iPhone to Android means readjusting to a different operating logic, migrating data and rebuying apps. But once you commit, two weeks is enough to adapt.

WeChat's switching costs areInvisible and embedded in interpersonal relationships：

  • **Social switching costs**: You would have to persuade every contact to move to the new app with you—an impossible task. In theory you could run both apps, but most people eventually return to WeChat, because that is where their social center of gravity lies.
  • **Identity-switching cost**: Your digital ID, social security card and health insurance card all live in WeChat. Switch to another app and you lose not only your social connections but also your digital identity.
  • **Switching costs across scenarios**: You are used to reading news, watching videos, ordering takeout, hailing rides, buying tickets, booking doctor appointments and paying bills inside WeChat—not because WeChat does these well, but because you are already there. Building the same habits again in another app carries an extremely high cost.
  • **Payment switching costs**: WeChat Pay links your bank cards, Lingqiantong, Licaitong, credit card repayments and automatic utility bill payments. Switch to Alipay? Many people use both. But if you use only Alipay, you cannot grab or send red packets on WeChat, or split bills in group chats.

Together, these switching costs give WeChat a retention rate of nearly 100%.If you have 1.35 billion monthly active users, you might lose fewer than 5 million users in 12 months—these people either changed their phone numbers or countries, or... passed away.

Tier three: the mini-program ecosystem — an invisible operating system

If WeChat's first two moats are defensive, Mini Programs areAggressive moat— It turned a social app into a mobile operating system.

The power of mini programs lies not in technology but inLowered the cost of reaching service users。

Before WeChat mini programs, a merchant that wanted to reach users had to spend hundreds of thousands of yuan building an app (and persuade users to download it), run an official account (text and images only), or build an H5 page (poor experience). With mini programs, any merchant can spend a few thousand yuan on a content vehicle whose experience approaches that of a native app; users scan a code or search to use it, and leave once they are done, with no download required.

As of 2025, WeChat Mini Program ecosystem data:

  • **Daily active users**: above 600 million
  • **Number of developers**: above 5 million
  • **Annual transaction volume**: above the consensus of RMB 5 trillion
  • **Sectors covered**: 200+
  • **Number of mini programs**: above 10 million

What do these numbers mean?WeChat Mini Programs have become China's largest digital commerce operating system.It connects consumers and merchants, public services and residents, content creators and readers.

Do you know what the most terrifying thing is?Competitors do not even know how to replicate the mini-program ecosystem.The foundation of mini programs is not technology but WeChat's 1.35 billion users—you cannot replicate that user base.

Layer four: WeChat Pay — financial infrastructure

WeChat Pay is Tencent's second moat, but it is also the reinforced concrete that fortifies the WeChat moat.

The core advantage of WeChat Pay is not technology (Alipay's technology is also strong), butScenario embedding：

  • You send red packets, transfer money and split bills in WeChat chats — payment serving social interaction
  • When you scan a QR code to pay at a small offline shop, that is payments serving daily life.
  • You buy group-buy vouchers, order food delivery and hail rides in WeChat groups — this is payments serving commerce
  • You buy things in livestream rooms on Channels, which is payment serving content.

Payment binds users to WeChat's economic relationship.Once you have established a "financial relationship" with a platform, the psychological cost of leaving it rises sharply.

Alipay still has not cracked social scenarios to this day—because its product logic is "finance + social," while WeChat's is "social + finance." The former makes people feel you are here to manage money and chat on the side; the latter makes people feel you are here to chat and pay on the side. These two psychological cues are worlds apart.

Layer five: Pony Ma's restraint — guardian of the moat

If the previous four layers are structural moats, then "restraint" is the barrier Tencent's management has added around them.

You might say: Tencent restrained? I don't see it.

Look at WeChat's ad load rate — 6%. Compare that with Douyin at 15%, Kuaishou at 12%, Weibo at 15% and Bilibili at 10%. As the app with the most user time and the largest user base in China, WeChat could earn an extra RMB 150 billion a year if it raised its ad load rate to 15%.

But it didn't. Why?

Because Zhang Xiaolong knew:Over-commercialization hurts user experience and ultimately erodes WeChat's moat.This is not a moral choice but a strategic one.

Think again: WeChat is the only mainstream app without a splash ad. Toutiao has one, Weibo has one, Taobao has one, Douyin has one, Bilibili has one, Xiaohongshu has one. Only WeChat does not. It is not a technical impossibility; it is a decision not to.

That is not the only case of restraint:

  • **Moments ads**: Since launch in 2015, the ad load has been kept at an extremely low level. A typical user may not see 1 ad in a day. In theory, raising the Moments ad load 3.0x poses no technical difficulty at all.
  • **WeChat Official Accounts**: Unlike Toutiao, they never adopted algorithmic recommendations (still relying on subscriptions plus "friends are reading"), which is commercially less efficient but preserves the tone of the content ecosystem.
  • **WeChat Channels**: Unlike Douyin, it does not force full-screen immersion; users can choose whether or not to watch livestream e-commerce.

Such restraint is almost against human nature in the capitalist world. Yet it is precisely this restraint that has kept WeChat's user satisfaction exceptionally high, sustaining its base of 1.35 billion users.

Can the moat be eroded?

No moat is permanent. Risks facing WeChat:

1. **Generational migration risk**: The next generation of young people may come to see WeChat as "the app adults use" and migrate to other social platforms. But this will not happen in the foreseeable future—because even if young people use Douyin, Xiaohongshu or Bilibili, they still need WeChat to stay in touch with parents, teachers and classmates.

2. Regulatory risk: If the government mandated that WeChat open up its social graph (similar to the EU requiring WhatsApp interoperability), the moat would be substantially weakened. But China currently has no such legislation.

3. AI disruption risk: If an AI-native social platform emerges, it would offer a completely different mode of interaction. But that remains a theoretical possibility; in reality it will not be presentable within 5-10 years.

4. User fatigue riskWeChat is indeed becoming increasingly bloated, piling on more and more features. But however much users complain, they still will not leave.

Overall,WeChat's moat is all but impossible to erode materially over the next 10 years.

Summary: Moats determine pricing power

The value of a moat ultimately shows in:Can a company sustain excess profits without competitive pressure?

Tencent's 2025 gross margin was 56.3% and net margin 29.9%. For comparison, among global social media giants: Meta's net margin is about 29% and Google's about 24%, roughly in line. But Meta faces pressure from TikTok and must invest heavily in AI, while Google faces disruption from AI search. Tencent's biggest advantage is—Its moat has virtually no challengers in China.

Alibaba tried social, Momo tried social, ByteDance tried social (Duoshan, Feiliao), Baidu tried social—all failed. Not because the products were bad, but because WeChat's moat runs too deep. You dig one meter with a shovel and find another hundred meters beneath it.

The value of a moat ultimately determines the floor of a company's valuation.WeChat's moat gives Tencent a safety cushion at 15x PE—even if every other business stops growing, the WeChat moat alone is worth the current market cap.

Gaming leader: an evolution from agency to global R&D

Tencent is China's largest gaming company—but that is only the start of the story

Many people still think of Tencent as "a gaming company that makes money from Honor of Kings and PUBG Mobile." That view is both right and wrong. Right, because gaming is indeed Tencent's largest revenue source, accounting for nearly half of total revenue. Wrong, because it badly underestimates the global ambition of Tencent Games and what the business will look like in 10 years.

In 2025, Tencent's gaming revenue reached about RMB 368.4 billion (part of value-added services, with games the dominant component), ranking first globally in gaming revenue — a position held for more than 10 consecutive years. Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo, Apple and Google all trail behind it.

But what is truly excitingThe question is not how much Tencent has earned in the past, but how much it can earn over the next decade.

Phase 1: The agency era (2003-2010)

Tencent's history in gaming goes back further than most people think. In 2003, Tencent launched the QQ Game Hall, starting out with board and card games. This was the most primitive stage—introducing lightweight games onto its own social platform to "keep users on QQ."

The real turning point came in 2008, when Tencent secured the publishing rights to CrossFire (CF) and Dungeon & Fighter (DNF). Tencent did not develop either game, but both achieved enormous success under its operation. CF peaked at 3 million concurrent players in 2010, while DNF has run for nearly 20 years and still contributes steady revenue today.

Two key takeaways from the agent era:

1. **Tencent learned to operate large-scale online games**—server architecture, anti-cheat, player-community operations and the cadence of version updates are all experience that takes time to build.

2. Tencent has built the strongest distribution channel— QQ pop-ups, Qzone and WeChat — these social channels give Tencent an overwhelming advantage in game distribution.

But the agency model has obvious flaws: you take the majority of the revenue share (usually 70%), but the developer can terminate the partnership or raise its share at any time. Tencent needs its own IP.

Phase 2: Investment + M&A (2011-2019)

2011 was a watershed year for Tencent's gaming strategy. That year Tencent established the Tencent Games brand and began systematically investing in game companies worldwide.

Core strategy:No controlling stake, no interference in operations, give them freedom, take a share of the profits.This strategy made Tencent the most sought-after strategic investor in the global gaming industry.

Tencent's investment list is staggering:

After the full acquisition in 2015, Riot launched VALORANT (2020, one of the world's most popular tactical shooters) and the Arcane animated series (a Netflix hit).

Clash of Clans has been running for over 10 years and still generates stable annual revenue of USD 1.5-2 billion.

The core value of an investment strategy: Tencent did not choose the "must hold 100% control, must be absorbed into the Tencent system" integration model; instead, it lets portfolio companies keep operating independently. This brings two benefits: first, it preserves the portfolio companies' innovative vitality (Riot, Epic and Supercell have not deteriorated because of Tencent's investment); second, it makes Tencent the "least disliked boss" among global game developers — compared with the heavy-handed integration by Microsoft, EA and Activision Blizzard, Tencent truly "puts up the money and keeps its mouth shut."

Phase 3: In-house development + global publishing (2020–present)

With agency experience (operational know-how) and investment footprint (access to talent), Tencent entered the third phase of gaming after 2020 —Global in-house development。

Landmark events and products:

1. **TiMi Studio Group**: Tencent's flagship studio, with overseas studios in Los Angeles, Seattle and Montreal, and has absorbed a large pool of 3A game development talent. Key titles: Honor of Kings (the world's highest-grossing mobile game, with annual revenue above USD 5 billion) and Call of Duty: Mobile (developed with Activision, with global downloads above 700 million).

2. Lightspeed & Quantum Studios: Flagship titles: PUBG Mobile (over 1 billion global downloads, over USD 3 billion in annual revenue) and Game for Peace (the domestic version). Lightspeed is also expanding aggressively overseas, having set up multiple AAA studios.

3. MoFang Studio: Representative title: Naruto Mobile. Also expanding into 3A.

4. Aurora Studios: Representative work: Moonlight Blade. Currently developing a top-tier open-world game.

Tencent's core in-house strategy:Global talent + Chinese efficiency + global markets. Hiring 3A veterans at high cost overseas (from Ubisoft, EA, Rockstar, CD Projekt) and pairing them with the development efficiency and management capability of Chinese teams to make games for the global market. This is a model never validated in the gaming industry — traditional 3A development has been Western-led, and Chinese companies face cultural barriers and a technology gap in making 3A games. But in mobile gaming, Tencent has already taken "Chinese efficiency + global publishing" to its limit.

Overseas markets: the next growth engine

In 2025, overseas revenue accounted for about 29% of Tencent Games' total. That figure is growing fast.

For comparison:

  • Share of overseas revenue in 2021: about 25%
  • Share of overseas revenue in 2023: about 29%
  • 2025 overseas revenue share: approximately 33% (estimated)

This growth may not look fast, but it is underpinned by several genuinely global products:

  • **PUBG Mobile**: cumulative global downloads exceed 1 billion; a top mobile game in India (before the ban), Southeast Asia, Latin America and the Middle East
  • **Call of Duty Mobile**: strong performance in Europe and US markets
  • **VALORANT** (Riot, published by Tencent): one of the world's most popular tactical shooter PC games, with 2025 revenue expected to exceed USD 2 billion
  • **League of Legends**: the benchmark of global esports, with annual revenue steady at around USD 2 billion
  • **Supercell games**: stable cash flow worldwide

Tencent wants to becomeNintendo + Activision Blizzard + EA—a combination of the world's top IP (Honor of Kings, PUBG Mobile, League of Legends), world-class R&D capabilities (TiMi, Lightspeed, Riot, Epic, Supercell), and a global publishing network (Tencent Games operates in more than 200 countries worldwide).

Risks facing Tencent's gaming business

1. **Game licence restrictions**: The Chinese government's restrictive issuance of game licences is a long-term policy. This means Tencent's domestic gaming revenue growth will be constrained on the supply side. But Tencent's advantage is that its existing game pipeline (games awaiting licences) far exceeds that of any competitor.

2. Protection of minors: China strictly limits minors' gaming time (3 hours per week), which has some impact on Tencent's Honor of Kings and Peacekeeper Elite. But the impact is fading—minors already accounted for a small share of revenue (about 1-2%).

3. Domestic competition: NetEase (Justice Online, Eggy Party, Fantasy Westward Journey) and miHoYo (Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail, Zenless Zone Zero) exert genuine competitive pressure on Tencent in the domestic market. miHoYo in particular has proven that a model built on content quality rather than channel dependence is viable in the mobile-game market.

4. US regulatory risk: A US-China tech decoupling could restrict Tencent's gaming business in the US. But Tencent runs its overseas operations through Riot (a US company) and Supercell (a Finnish company), and has already ring-fenced the legal structure against this risk.

5. 3A development riskTencent's 3A in-house projects require massive investment (a single project may exceed USD 500 million), but the commercial returns on 3A games are highly uncertain.

Summary: Gaming is Tencent's most certain growth engine

From a valuation perspective, the market assigns an extremely low implied valuation to Tencent's gaming business—if Tencent were not a gaming company but a "pure gaming company," its PE should be 20-25x (versus Activision Blizzard's acquisition price at 35x, EA at about 25x, Nintendo at about 20x). But Tencent's overall PE is currently only 15x, and its gaming business is likely the most severely undervalued segment.

Core investment thesis:

1. **The global games market is still growing** (at roughly 5-8% a year), and Chinese games going overseas have only just begun

2. Tencent owns the world's best gaming portfolio.(Riot, Epic, Supercell) + in-house development capability

3. Gaming is a cash-cow business, unlike cloud computing, it does not require continuous cash burn

4. Tencent is nearly invincible in the mobile gaming market, among the world's top 10 highest-grossing mobile games, Tencent-affiliated titles account for 4-5

Tencent Games' endgame is not to be a Chinese game company, but a global game company.When overseas revenue exceeds 50% of the total (within 5-10 years), the market will value it on an entirely different basis.

Fintech: The trillion-yuan ecosystem behind WeChat Pay

From red packets to a super financial platform

During the 2014 Spring Festival, the WeChat team built a feature treated internally as a "small experiment": WeChat Red Envelope. The design logic was simple: let users send electronic red envelopes in WeChat groups, with random amounts, as fun as grabbing physical red envelopes.

No one expected this small feature to upend China's financial landscape. On Chinese New Year's Eve 2014, more than 5 million people took part in WeChat Red Envelopes. At the 2015 Spring Festival Gala, WeChat partnered with CCTV on "shake-to-open red envelopes," and the traffic crashed the servers outright — a peak of 810 million shakes per minute, with more than 11 billion participant actions that night. Jack Ma called it a "Pearl Harbor sneak attack" internally, and the entire Alibaba group went into emergency response mode.

A decade later, WeChat Pay has become one of China's largest mobile payment platforms, forming a duopoly with Alipay. Tencent's fintech business (including WeChat Pay, Licaitong, Weilidai, WeBank and others) generated about RMB 240.6 billion in revenue in 2025 (32% of total revenue) and is still growing rapidly.

Duopoly: Alipay vs WeChat Pay

WeChat Pay's business model: every transaction is a money printer

How does WeChat Pay make money? Mainly from three areas:

1. Trading fees

WeChat charges merchants a 0.38%-0.6% fee on every WeChat Pay transaction. This is the most basic and most stable revenue source.

In 2025, WeChat Pay's annual transaction volume exceeded RMB 150 trillion (including C2C transfers, B2C consumption and C2B merchant payments). At an average "net fee rate" of 0.3% (after deducting bank channel costs), annual revenue is about RMB 45 billion.

This is a classic "money-printing" business.— Near-zero marginal cost: every additional transaction adds another slice of profit.

2. Reserve fund interest

All WeChat Pay "wallet balances" sit in central bank reserve accounts (centrally custodied since 2020), and the interest earned is split between Tencent and the banks. WeChat Pay's average daily balance exceeds RMB 200 billion, generating roughly RMB 10-15 billion a year in reserve interest income.

3. Derivative financial services

Payment is just an entry point. The financial services derived from WeChat Pay are the real profit center:

  • **Licaitong**: Similar to Alipay's Yu'ebao, users can deposit spare cash to earn returns. Licaitong's assets under management (AUM) exceed RMB 3 trillion, generating annual management fee revenue of about RMB 15–20 billion.
  • **Weilidai**: a credit-loan product under WeBank, with lending distributed through the WeChat entry point. WeBank's net profit exceeded RMB 20 billion in 2025, with Weilidai as the main source of profit.
  • **WeChat Fenfu**: a consumer credit product similar to Alipay's Huabei, in an early expansion phase with a potentially huge addressable market.
  • **Insurance**: Selling insurance products through the WeChat channel (WeSure), with commission income growing rapidly.

WeBank: Tencent's "hidden gold mine"

WeBank may be Tencent's most undervalued asset.

It was one of China's first private banks, approved in 2014 and launched in 2015. Unlike traditional banks with physical branches, WeBank operates entirely online, reaching users through WeChat and QQ.

WeBank's core data (2025):

  • **Total assets**: approximately RMB 550 billion
  • **Loan balance**: approximately RMB 400 billion
  • **User count**: over 400 million (past customers of Weilidai)
  • **Non-performing loan ratio**: about 0.9% (below the industry average of 1.5%)
  • **Net profit**: approximately RMB 25 billion
  • **ROE**: about 22% (far above traditional banks)

WeBank has no physical branches, no tellers and no ATMs—it is a purely digital lending machine. Its core assets are big-data risk control and WeChat's customer acquisition channel.

If WeBank were listed independently, at current city commercial bank valuations (0.6-0.8x PB), its market cap would be roughly RMB 300-400 billion. Given its tech attributes and high growth, at fintech company valuations (2-3x PB), its market cap could reach the trillion level.

Tencent holds a 30% stake in WeBank; valuing that stake on a 30% equity basis, the asset is worth roughly RMB 150–300 billion.

Future growth space for fintech

1. Wealth management (Licaitong)

In Chinese households' financial assets, deposits account for over 50%, and penetration of wealth-management products and funds is far below that of the US. As Chinese interest rates keep falling (the 10-year government bond yield has dropped below 2%), the "migration of deposits" is a certain ten-year trend.

Yu'ebao's AUM has surpassed RMB 3 trillion, but it still lags far behind Yu'ebao (AUM of over RMB 7 trillion), and against China's RMB 300 trillion in household financial assets, the headroom is even larger. For every RMB 1 trillion increase in AUM, Tencent earns roughly RMB 5 billion more in management fee revenue.

2. Consumer credit (WeChat Fenfu)

WeChat Fenfu benchmarks against Alipay's Huabei. At its peak, Huabei's loan balance exceeded RMB 300 billion, while WeChat Fenfu currently sits at only about RMB 50 billion. Given WeChat Pay's user base (800 million) and scenario coverage (offline QR code payments), Fenfu reaching RMB 300 billion is a high-probability event. At a 5% net margin, that implies annual profit contribution of about RMB 15 billion.

3. Insurance (WeSure)

China's insurance market penetration is far below developed countries, leaving substantial room for growth in health and life insurance. WeSure, as the insurance gateway within the WeChat ecosystem, currently writes about RMB 100 billion in premiums. Benchmarked against Ant Insurance (premiums above RMB 300 billion), WeSure has 3x growth headroom.

4. Enterprise payments (WeCom + Tencent Meeting)

B2B payments are the new battleground for fintech. WeCom's SaaS services, paid subscriptions to Tencent Meeting, and merchant acquiring through WeChat Enterprise Pay—these B2B financial services carry far higher margins than C2C payments.

Fintech risks

1. **Regulatory risk**: Beijing's regulation of fintech is tightening. Following Ant Group's restructuring, requirements such as the financial holding company licence, data compliance and the interest rate cap (4x LPR) all bear directly on Tencent.

2. Competitive riskAlipay will not stand by while WeChat Pay expands rapidly in credit and wealth management. Ant Group may have been restructured, but it remains China's most powerful fintech company.

3. Asset quality risk: If China's economy enters a deep recession, consumer credit NPL ratios could rise sharply, directly hitting the profitability of WeBank and WeChat Fenfu. For now, however, the risk is manageable — Tencent's customer base skews toward high-quality users, and its credit-granting strategy is relatively conservative.

Summary: An underpriced "lever"

Tencent's fintech business is essentially aMassive leverage— Leveraging WeChat's 1.35 billion user base and payment scenarios to unlock wealth management, credit, insurance and other high-margin businesses at extremely low marginal cost.

Unlike gaming and advertising, fintech growth does not depend on content investment or incremental user growth, but onGreater monetization depthAs long as online penetration in China's financial markets keeps rising, Tencent's fintech business will keep growing.

Fintech is valued at roughly 25-30% of Tencent's overall valuation, or about HKD 1.2-1.5 trillion. As Licaitong and micro-lending scale up, that weight will only grow.By 2030, fintech could become Tencent's largest profit source.

AI and cloud: Tencent's "second curve" or "homework it must catch up on"

AI and cloud: Tencent's "second curve" or "homework it must catch up on"

Has Tencent fallen behind in the AI era?

After ChatGPT burst onto the scene in late 2022, the hottest topic in China's tech circles was this: Baidu built ERNIE Bot, Alibaba built Tongyi Qianwen, ByteDance built Doubao, and even startups like Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Zhipu secured billions of dollars in funding.

And Tencent? It did not officially unveil its "Hunyuan large model" until September 2023. On social media, Tencent is seen as "a latecomer to the AI era."

Is that true? Yes and no.

Tencent's AI strategy: pragmatic, low-key, and striking late

Tencent's AI strategy follows the same playbook as its approach in other areas:Don't be the first, but be the best.

This strategy has worked repeatedly throughout Tencent's history:

  • **Social**: QQ was not the first instant messenger (ICQ was first), and WeChat was not the first mobile IM (Kik was first)
  • **Gaming**: Tencent was not the first company to make games, but it now makes money faster than anyone
  • **Video**: Tencent Video was not the first to enter long-form video, but it now ranks among the top two in the industry
  • **Short video**: Weixin Channels launched five years after Douyin, yet it has the fastest DAU growth

"Latecomer" is an unfair label for Tencent. In reality,Tencent's AI build-out began far earlier than the public realizes.

2016-2022: Building momentum

  • **2016**: Tencent founded AI Lab, led by former Microsoft researcher Zhang Tong, covering computer vision, speech recognition and natural language processing.
  • **2018**: Tencent publicly showcased its Go AI "FineArt," placing it among the world's top-tier Go AIs in elite competitions.
  • **2019**: Tencent launched "Tencent Miying" (medical imaging AI), gaining a measure of influence in medical AI.
  • **2020-2022**: AI Lab continued to place in multiple top international AI conference competitions, but the results remained largely at the research stage, with limited commercial application.

Frankly, Tencent's AI at this stage can be summed up in eight words:"Research is strong, commercialization is lacking."Like many leading tech companies, Tencent's AI Lab resembles an academic institution, producing numerous top-conference papers, but the share that actually converts into products and revenue is low.

2023-2025: acceleration phase

ChatGPT's arrival reset everyone's timetable. In September 2023, Tencent officially launched its Hunyuan large model and began embedding AI capabilities across its entire product line.

As of the end of 2025, the implementation of Tencent AI:

  • **Tencent Hunyuan large model**: now iterated to version 3.0, ranking in China's top tier for Chinese comprehension and multi-turn dialogue capability (on par with ERNIE 4.0, Tongyi Qianwen 2.5 and the Doubao large model).
  • **Advertising system**: The AI-optimized ad delivery system (ad recommendation plus automated creative generation) drove advertising revenue growth of more than 20% in 2025.
  • **Game AI**: AI NPCs and in-game intelligent combat systems have been deployed in multiple new games, boosting user retention and paid conversion.
  • **WeChat Search**: AI-enhanced WeChat Search delivers more precise answers across an information-retrieval scenario serving 1.35 billion users.
  • **Tencent Meeting**: AI summary (real-time meeting minutes) plus AI noise cancellation have become differentiating highlights.
  • **WeCom**: AI customer-service bots and intelligent workflows cut customer-service costs.
  • **Coding assistant**: Tencent Coding Copilot, an AI code-generation tool used internally, significantly boosts development efficiency.

Tencent's core AI strategy is not "build AI and sell it to customers" but "arm all of its own products with AI".

This closely mirrors Microsoft's AI strategy: rather than turning ChatGPT into a standalone money-making product (though Copilot does have a paid version), Microsoft embedded AI into every corner of Office 365, Azure, Bing and Windows. That is also why Microsoft has achieved the world's largest market capitalization in the AI era.

Tencent Cloud is the most overlooked part of the entire Tencent AI story.

From 2018 to 2022, Tencent Cloud went through a painful period of "burning cash for growth" — to catch up with Alibaba Cloud, Tencent undercut rivals on price and even sold cloud services at a loss. The result: revenue grew, and so did the losses.

Strategic shift: from "scale first" to "profit first"

At the end of 2022, Tencent Cloud began a strategic pullback. New CEO Dowson Tong (also head of Tencent Cloud) made it clear:"Cloud business must be profitable—no loss-making growth."

This transformation delivered notable results in 2023-2025:

  • Tencent Cloud has given up a lot of pure bandwidth resale and hardware procurement-on-behalf business with "extremely low margins and extremely poor customer stickiness"
  • Focus on **high-margin PaaS and SaaS businesses**: Tencent Meeting, WeCom, Tencent Docs, TRTC (real-time audio and video)
  • Scenarios where Tencent has a natural advantage: gaming cloud, social pan-entertainment, WeChat Mini Program cloud development, financial cloud

Results: Tencent Cloud's gross margin rose from under 10% in 2022 to over 20% in 2025. Although revenue growth slowed, losses narrowed sharply, and the business is expected to reach break-even in 2026.

Differentiated competition: WeChat Mini Program cloud development

Tencent Cloud holds one trump card that neither Alibaba nor Huawei has —WeChat Mini Program cloud development。

Over 8 million Mini Program developers inherently need cloud services—storage, databases, CDN, and AI capabilities. WeChat Mini Program Cloud Development (Tencent Cloud Base) offers out-of-the-box cloud services, enabling Mini Program developers to deploy applications quickly without building their own servers.

By 2025, Mini Program Cloud Development had served more than 3 million developers, with annual revenue above RMB 10 billion and growth of over 40%. This is a business with both high stickiness (once adopted, customers do not migrate away) and ample room for growth.

If Mini Program Cloud Development can achieve annual revenue of RMB 20-30 billion, that alone would make Tencent Cloud a "mid-sized cloud vendor."

Does Tencent really need to win?

One question that must be confronted:What if Tencent's AI and cloud business never catches up with Alibaba and ByteDance?

The answer may surprise many:It does not matter much.

Why?

Because Tencent's core profit engine isGaming + advertising + fintechrather than AI and cloud. What AI and cloud mean for Tencent:

1. **Defensive**: Without AI, the competitiveness of gaming and advertising would erode over the next 10 years (AI optimization is central to game experience and ad efficiency).

2. Enabling: AI has improved the efficiency and monetization of Tencent's existing businesses—this is "incremental," not "everything."

3. SelectiveTencent does not need to go head-to-head with Alibaba and ByteDance on general-purpose large models. It can focus on "scenario AI" — vertical AI models for gaming, social, payments and advertising, where the data and user base are things competitors cannot replicate.

Tencent does not need to lead the AI era; it only needs to be an adopter in the AI era.

Just as Tencent did not invent cloud computing technology back then, but found its place in the cloud market through WeChat Mini Program cloud development, the same logic applies to AI.

A few key judgments

1. **Hunyuan will not become a core revenue source for Tencent.** General-purpose large models struggle to monetize independently in China — Baidu's ERNIE has yet to generate meaningful revenue, and Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen is likewise "technology enablement" rather than "product monetization." Tencent's Hunyuan will most likely follow the same path — free and open, but embedded in the product ecosystem.

2. Tencent Cloud's value needs to be re-rated.Comparing market share only against Alibaba Cloud and Huawei Cloud, Tencent Cloud is indeed undervalued. But add the ecosystem value of WeChat Mini Program cloud development, and Tencent Cloud's business model is actually "smarter" than Alibaba Cloud's — it is not simply "selling compute" but "selling an ecosystem."

3. AI's incremental contribution to Tencent's existing businesses could reach RMB 50-100 billion per year within 3-5 years.This was achieved through three paths: higher advertising efficiency, paid conversion in gaming, and price increases in enterprise services.

4. Most noteworthy is Tencent's investment in AI talent and computing power.In 2025, Tencent's capital expenditure (Capex) rose sharply, mainly for building AI computing centers. On talent, Tencent recruited heavily overseas for AI research staff (especially in North America and Singapore). The results of these investments may take 2-3 years to fully materialize.

Summary: Second curve not here yet; defence is enough

If Tencent's AI and cloud business were an exam, its score would be roughly75 points—Not excellent, but a pass, with room to improve.

For Tencent investors, the more important question is not whether Tencent can become the king of AI, but rather:If Tencent does not pursue AI, will its moat be eroded?The answer is clearly yes. Without algorithmic recommendations, Channels' user growth and retention would suffer; without AI-optimized ad systems, monetization efficiency would lag ByteDance; without AI in gaming, player experience and spending would decline.

So Tencent must do AI. Not because it wants to win, but because it cannot afford to lose.Tools that preserve a competitive position are defense; only tools that genuinely create incremental growth qualify as a second curve.

For now, AI is more of a defensive tool for Tencent and has not yet become a true "second curve." But given that Tencent's historically "late-arriving follower" has always quietly had the last laugh, it may well use the same playbook in AI to force the market to re-rate it.

The Video Account War: Tencent vs Douyin's Ultimate Showdown

Video account war: Tencent vs Douyin's ultimate showdown

An Asymmetric War

Douyin launched in September 2016. By early 2017, daily active users (DAU) had passed one million. During Chinese New Year 2018, DAU surged from 30 million to 70 million. In 2020, DAU topped 600 million. This is the fastest-growing product in the history of China's internet, bar none.

ByteDance reshaped how content is consumed through algorithmic recommendation. Everyone's Douyin feed is different—the ultimate "thousand people, a thousand faces." This experience—"the algorithm knows what you want better than you do"—dwarfs traditional content distribution models (social distribution, subscription distribution).

Tencent's response in the short-video arena has been notoriously slow. It launched Weishi in 2013 and shut it down in 2017. It relaunched Weishi in 2018 and poured in heavy capital (reportedly RMB 3 billion in creator subsidies), but never managed to shake Douyin. Weishi peaked at roughly 50 million DAU, less than one-tenth of Douyin's.

Many people believe Tencent has already lost the short-video battle.

But the emergence of Channels changed the entire landscape.

Why can Weishi not beat Douyin, while Video Accounts can?

Weishi's failure is a textbook case: Tencent tried to take on ByteDance's Douyin head-on with its own product, using the same playbook, the same content and the same tactics. The result: with Douyin already having built a creator ecosystem and user mindshare, Weishi had no differentiated advantage whatsoever.

But WeChat Channels is completely different. It is not a standalone app; rather, it isShort-video feature embedded in WeChat. This means:

1. **Zero customer acquisition cost**: Weishi has to pay to acquire users (app downloads); Channels does not — WeChat's 1.35 billion users are naturally Channels users.

2. Social relationship chain: Douyin's recommendation logic relies purely on algorithms; what you see has nothing to do with your friends. WeChat Channels has a "Liked by friends" section, where videos your friends have liked are pushed to your homepage—This is the social distribution capability that Douyin can never replicate。

3. Boundless scenarios: WeChat chats allow direct forwarding of Channels content, Moments posts can carry Channels content, Official Account articles can embed Channels content, and Channels livestreams can be watched inside WeChat without redirects—this richness and reach of scenarios is something Douyin cannot match.

4. Creator incentives: Channels creators can funnel followers into WeChat groups, Official Accounts and Mini Programs — something impossible within the ByteDance ecosystem (Douyin's traffic is centralized, leaving creators with weak control over their followers). Channels gives creators the possibility of "private-domain monetization."

Livestream e-commerce: the promise of a trillion-yuan GMV

Beyond advertising revenue, the second-largest monetization engine for Channels is livestream e-commerce.

In 2025, WeChat Channels livestream e-commerce GMV (gross merchandise value) exceeded RMB 1 trillion. By comparison, Douyin e-commerce GMV was about RMB 3.5 trillion and Kuaishou e-commerce about RMB 1.2 trillion.

Unique advantages of Channels e-commerce:

1. **Private-domain attributes**: Douyin e-commerce is "public-domain e-commerce" — users are seeded while scrolling videos, make impulse purchases, and the relationship with the merchant ends there. WeChat Channels e-commerce can achieve a closed loop of "watch livestream → place order → join the merchant's WeChat group → become a private-domain customer." For categories with high ticket sizes and high repurchase rates (tea, jewelry, maternal and infant products, paid knowledge), WeChat Channels e-commerce is far more efficient than Douyin.

2. Basis of trustDouyin e-commerce's return rate runs as high as 30-40% (the cost of impulse buying), while WeChat Channels e-commerce's return rate is about 15-20%. This is because WeChat Channels' content recommendations incorporate social relationships (friends' likes), and purchases driven by friend recommendations reflect more rational decision-making and higher trust than algorithm-driven recommendations.

3. Audience differentiationDouyin's users skew younger (mostly 18-35), while WeChat Channels reaches a broader age range (the 35-60 group has strong spending power). High-net-worth middle-aged consumers may "scroll past nothing they want to buy" on Douyin, but they can find it on WeChat Channels. As a result, WeChat Channels e-commerce has a higher average order value (about RMB 200) than Douyin (about RMB 100).

4. InfrastructureWeChat Pay + WeCom: a one-stop "transactions + repeat purchases" solution that Douyin e-commerce cannot replicate.

Risks facing Channels

1. **User time spent far below Douyin**: WeChat Channels averages about 60 minutes of daily user time, versus about 120 minutes for Douyin. WeChat Channels' content recommendation algorithm still needs continuous refinement.

2. The creator ecosystem is immature: Top creators, especially in entertainment and drama, prefer to invest their effort in Douyin rather than WeChat Channels. Content quality on WeChat Channels is generally lower than on Douyin.

3. Live-streaming e-commerce supply chainsDouyin e-commerce's supply chain and logistics system is far more mature than that of WeChat Channels.

4. Zhang Xiaolong's restraint is a double-edged sword: It is this restraint that has preserved WeChat's user experience, but it is also this restraint that has made Channels' commercialization progress "slow."

Final thoughts on "Tencent vs Douyin"

ByteDance poses the greatest threat to Tencent in the history of China's internet. Not because ByteDance is bigger than Alibaba, but because ByteDance has genuinely eroded Tencent's foundation along the dimension of "user time spent."

The final competitive landscape, however, may not be "Douyin eats WeChat," but rather"Douyin and WeChat each occupy a distinct niche—one meets entertainment needs, the other meets daily-life needs."

For investors, the key point to understand is:WeChat does not need to "defeat" Douyin. Tencent does not need to "stop" ByteDance's growth either.Tencent's investment value lies in this: even with ByteDance's formidable rise, Tencent still holds a 1.35 billion-user social graph, a trillion-scale fintech ecosystem, and the world's largest gaming business — assets whose value will not diminish because ByteDance exists.

What WeChat Channels means for Tencent is not "a head-to-head showdown with Douyin", but rather"provides Tencent's advertising and e-commerce businesses with a reliable long-term growth engine."The engine began to gain traction in 2025 and will keep generating strong cash flow over the next 5-10 years.

WeChat Channels may be Tencent's most valuable new business over the next decade—not because it can beat Douyin, but because it can make Tencent's existing ecosystem more valuable.

The art of capital allocation: from "buy, buy, buy" to "sell, sell, sell + buybacks"

▾

The Art of Capital Allocation: From "Buy, Buy, Buy" to "Sell, Sell, Sell + Buybacks"

Where does Tencent's capital come from, and where does it go?

A company generating more than RMB 200 billion in annual free cash flow, with cash reserves that at their peak exceeded USD 200 billion—this is Tencent's capital strength.

How Tencent deploys this capital is one of the most important determinants of long-term shareholder returns (second only to business fundamentals).

Tencent's capital allocation history falls into three phases:

Phase 1 (2004-2017): acquisition spree

After Tencent listed in 2004, the core strategy of its capital allocation was"Enclosing land with walls"—Enter any seemingly valuable field quickly through investment and acquisitions.

At this stage Tencent invested in more than 600 companies, with total investment exceeding RMB 400 billion. Core investment logic:

1. **Business**: fills gaps Tencent cannot enter effectively on its own — e-commerce (JD.com, Pinduoduo), mobility (Didi), local services (Meituan), search (Sogou), video (Kuaishou/Bilibili), music (Spotify/Tencent Music), literature (China Literature), healthcare (WeDoctor)…

2. On strategy: Investing in a competitor's competitor—for example, investing in JD.com (Alibaba's rival), investing in Pinduoduo (the rival of both JD.com and Alibaba), investing in Meituan (the rival of Alibaba's local-services arm)—this "proxy war" strategy is highly astute.

3. On the ecosystem front: giving portfolio companies access to WeChat and QQ traffic, which in turn reinforces WeChat's ecosystem moat.

At this stage Tencent was hailed as "China's smartest investor"—its investment returns were astonishing:

  • **JD.com**: invested about USD 215 million in 2014 for roughly a 15% stake; distributed about HKD 100 billion to shareholders in 2021
  • **Meituan**: invested about USD 1 billion for roughly a 17% stake; received about HKD 150 billion in dividends in 2021
  • **PDD**: invested about USD 500 million, gradually reduced its stake after 2024, recovering over HKD 60 billion in total
  • **Kuaishou**: Pre-IPO investment of about USD 1 billion for roughly 20%, peak market cap of HKD 200 billion
  • **Epic Games**: invested USD 330 million for 40%, now worth over USD 10 billion
  • **Supercell**: acquired 84% for USD 8.6 billion; now contributes billions in profit each year
  • **Riot Games**: acquired 70% for USD 400 million (2011); now contributes billions in profit annually

Tencent's investment portfolio has been called "half of China's internet landscape", peaking above USD 200 billion in valuation.

Phase 2 (2018-2021): Regulatory storm and passive stake reduction

The internet antitrust regulation that began in late 2020 directly changed Tencent's capital allocation strategy. Key events:

1. **Antitrust fine**: In 2021, Tencent was fined about RMB 10 billion for failing to file concentrations of undertakings as required by law.

2. Spinning off portfolio companies: Regulators require Tencent not to use its capital advantage to "stifle competition." This means Tencent can no longer expand its footprint through investment the way it once did.

3. Passive dividend-driven reduction: In 2021-2022, Tencent was forced to reduce its stakes in JD.com and Meituan through "in-kind dividends"—distributing the shares it held directly to shareholders rather than selling them on the secondary market. This both accommodated regulatory requirements and sent a signal to investors—"we don't want to sell, but we have to."

At this stage, Tencent has shifted from being a "never-resting M&A machine" to a"A lion forced to slim down"。

Phase 3 (2022-2026): strategic focus + massive buybacks

Starting in 2022, Tencent's capital allocation underwent a fundamental shift:From "outward expansion" to "inward focus".

1. Sharply reduce investment

In 2021, Tencent's external investments exceeded RMB 150 billion. In 2023, that fell to about RMB 30 billion. In 2025, it dropped further to about RMB 20 billion. The investment scope has also become more focused—centered mainly on three areas: gaming (overseas studios), AI and cloud services—and no longer follows the "cast a wide net" approach that spanned e-commerce, mobility and local services.

The "Connecting Everything" strategy has ended; Tencent's core strategy is now "Refocusing".

2. Systematically reducing holdings in portfolio companies

Beyond the in-kind distributions of JD.com and Meituan shares, Tencent also reduced its stakes in the following from 2023 to 2025:

  • **Sea Limited**: trimmed its stake by about 90%, recovering roughly HKD 30 billion (Sea is one of Tencent's most important investments in Southeast Asia)
  • **Kuaishou**: stake cut by over 50%, recovering about HKD 40 billion
