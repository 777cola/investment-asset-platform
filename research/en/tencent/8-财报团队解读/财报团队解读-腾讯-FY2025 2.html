📝 Tencent FY2025 earnings review: from 43% to 56%, a four-year march in gross margin

Edited and polished | Publishable version | ~30% colloquial + 70% professional

Bottom line first.

Tencent's 2025 annual report, summed up in one word, is"Solid"。

Revenue RMB 751.8 billion (+13.9%), net profit RMB 224.8 billion (+15.9%). Gross margin 56.3% — note this figure: four years ago it was just 43.9%. From 44% to 56%, Tencent took four years to complete a "quality upgrade" of its business model.

Tencent's earnings are increasingly valuable.

1. Gross margin — the standout number

2021: 43.9% → 2022: 43.1% → 2023: 48.1% → 2024: 52.9% → 2025: 56.3%. A gain of 2–4 percentage points a year.

Three reasons:

  1. The Rise of Advertising— WeChat Channels turns into a money printer. Load rate is only 3% versus Douyin's 15%, leaving substantial headroom.
  2. Gaming gross margin improves— Rising in-house share: in-house gross margin runs 10-15 percentage points above agency.
  3. Low-margin business contraction— Tencent Cloud culls reseller projects, markedly improving gross margin.

"Companies that can consistently raise gross margins usually have pricing power; a company with pricing power rarely has a shallow moat."

II. Cash flow — real money earned

Free cash flow RMB 215.6 billion, up 10.2% year on year, equal to 96% of net profit. Net cash USD 13.2 billion (about RMB 96 billion), a very low debt-to-equity ratio (D/E 0.33) and interest coverage of 19x.

A company with over RMB 90 billion in net cash and more than RMB 200 billion in annual cash generation carries essentially zero financial risk.

III. Shareholder returns — increasingly generous

Dividend raised from ¥3.40 to ¥4.76 (+40%), with buybacks exceeding HKD 100 billion. Total shareholder return of about 3.5%, roughly in line with Apple (~4%) — generous for a company still growing.

Reviewing the capital allocation track record — trimming JD.com and Meituan at highs in 2021, buying back at lows from 2022 to 2025 —A textbook case of selling high and buying low.

IV. Risks — Watch Three Things

Tier one: regulation.Game licences, fintech — Tencent has adapted, and compliance costs are a barrier to entry for competitors.

Second layer: competition.ByteDance is the strongest competitor. But no one has yet been able to shake WeChat's social graph.

Tier three: macro.The growth slowdown is real, but Tencent is infrastructure and more resilient to cycles than the industry average.

V. Valuation — expensive or cheap?

PE 15x, a historical low. FCF yield 6%, more than double the government bond yield.

In plain terms:Neither expensive nor outrageously cheap—it falls into the category of "normal undervaluation that is reasonably low."

If you believe in 8-10% growth over the next five years, annualized returns will be 10-15%. If you don't, wait for a lower price.

The only variable is whether you can hold on—a 20% swing either way is normal volatility.
