1. Is Tencent a "good business"?

Business model assessment:★★★★☆

Tencent's business model, in one sentence:It is the infrastructure of the Chinese internet.。

WeChat plus QQ are what 1.3 billion people rely on every day. This is not a "product"; it is an ecosystem. You chat, pay, read news, play games, hail rides, order food delivery and buy insurance on it—it has become as essential as water and electricity.

The "moat" Duan Yongping values most

Moat 1: Network effects
WeChat's moat is not technology, it is people. 1.1 billion MAU, everyone around you is on it. This is the classic Metcalfe effect: the more people use it, the more valuable it becomes. A competitor wants to replicate it? Technically possible, but getting you and your friends to move at the same time costs almost infinitely much.

Moat 2: Switching costs
Your social connections, payment history, mini-program ecosystem, official account subscriptions—all of it lives in WeChat. Move away? That means starting life over. These are extremely high switching costs.

Moat 3: Ecosystem barrier
WeChat Pay, Mini Programs, Channels, Official Accounts and WeCom together form an operating-system-level ecosystem. It does not fight alone; it is a nation.

Certainty: high, but not perfect

Duan Yongping once said: "Certainty matters most."

Where does Tencent's certainty lie? Chinese people need social connection, and Chinese businesses need digitalization—these will not change. WeChat's stickiness has been proven countless times over the past 10 years.

But uncertainties remain:

  • Regulatory risk: Game licences, antitrust, data security—none of these are within Tencent's control
  • Growth has peakedUser ceiling reached (1.1 billion); future growth can only come from ARPU
  • International expansion stalls: Overseas expansion is difficult; the business still depends mainly on the Chinese market

So Tencent is a "good business," but not a "perfect business." Duan Yongping would rate it "excellent" — below Moutai (a perfect business), but better than 95% of listed companies.

II. Is Tencent "doing the right thing"?

Management assessment:★★★★★

What Duan Yongping values most is "right people" — people must"Do the right thing", then"Getting things right"。

Pony Ma Huateng: This is a classic example of someone who "does the right things." He is not aggressive, not restless, and does not chase hype. Tencent's biggest decisions — building WeChat (letting it cannibalize QQ), opening up the ecosystem (not doing everything in-house), and investing rather than acquiring — are all models of "doing the right things."

Pony trimmed some shares at the 2021 peak, but donated a large portion to charity. His compensation ranks among the lowest of the internet tycoons. He cultivates no personality cult, runs no PPT culture, and burns no cash on to-VC businesses. These are the hallmarks of the "right people".

Capital allocation capability:★★★★☆

Duan Yongping examines one question closely — does management deploy the money the company earns wisely?

  • Dividends + buybacks: Buybacks exceeded HKD 100 billion in 2024, with further increases in 2025. Combined buyback and dividend yield approaches 3-4%.
  • Investment: Tencent holds an investment portfolio of about RMB 800 billion. Over the past few years it has steadily trimmed stakes in Meituan, JD.com and Pinduoduo—shifting from "buying everything" to "focusing on the core plus returning capital to shareholders".
Duan Yongping might say"Tencent's management has improved a great deal on capital allocation. In the past, investments were too scattered; now they are focusing on the core business and stepping up buybacks, which is the right approach."

IV. If I were Duan Yongping, would I buy?

Answer: I would buy, but not as a large position

Buy rationale:

  • ✅ Good business: exceptionally deep network-effect moat, very high switching costs
  • ✅ Good management: Pony and team "do the right things", capital allocation is improving
  • ✅ Good price: 15x PE is reasonably low for a quality company growing 10% a year
  • ✅ Certainty: WeChat will not be displaced, and monetization headroom remains (Channels, Mini Programs, payments)

Reasons for not taking a heavy position:

  • ⚠️ Not Moutai: Tencent's business is more complex, competition is fiercer, and regulatory risk is greater
  • ⚠️ International political risk: US-China rivalry, Hong Kong stock liquidity, delisting risk for China concept stocks
  • ⚠️ Growth quality: driven by "deepening the existing base" rather than "expanding into new territory"; ARPU upside is capped
Duan Yongping's one-line operational advice:

"If you understand this business, buying Tencent at 15x PE and holding for 5 years will most likely not lose you money, and will most likely beat deposits and wealth-management products.
But to make big money, you need patience.

I would treat it as a position (10-15%), not expecting it to double, but believing it can grow steadily.
The core position stays in businesses like Moutai that require no monitoring at all.

Remember: buying Tencent is not about buying tomorrow's share price, it is about buying its value five years from now.
If you can't hold a stock for 5 years, don't hold it for 5 minutes.

5. What are the main risks? What would Duan Yongping worry about?

The three things Duan Yongping worries about most

Risk 1: Regulation — the biggest uncertainty
The kind of risk Duan Yongping dislikes most is "risk you can neither predict nor control." Regulation is exactly that.

Game license approvals could tighten at any time; data-security regulations could restrict WeChat monetization; antitrust action could force WeChat to open its ecosystem; fintech regulation could squeeze margins.

Duan Yongping said: "Regulatory risk is unique to Tencent. Moutai does not have this risk, and even Apple does not. This is the price one must bear for holding Tencent."
Risk 2: Management turnover / cultural erosion
Duan Yongping places great weight on corporate culture. He said: "A good corporate culture enables a company to weather crises; a bad one makes a company destroy itself."

Tencent's current culture is sound—pragmatic, low-key, user-oriented. But if one day Pony retires, the founding team disperses and professional managers take over, that would be dangerous. The risk looks small for now, but it bears watching.
Risk 3: Growth engine stalls
Tencent's growth over the past 20 years has been driven by two wheels: user growth (0 to 1.1 billion) and ARPU improvement. The first wheel has stalled.

Gaming is being eroded by new players such as miHoYo; ad load is already high; payment penetration is near its ceiling; cloud remains loss-making.

Duan Yongping said: "A growth slowdown is not a problem in itself. The problem is the price you pay. If you buy a company with slowing growth at 40x PE, that is a disaster. If you buy at 15x PE, the slowdown is already priced in."

Risk 1.5: Geopolitics

Hong Kong stock market liquidity, the ebb and flow of foreign capital, and the audit dispute over US-listed Chinese stocks — these are external risks Tencent cannot control.
