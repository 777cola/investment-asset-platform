Tencent Holdings (00700.HK):

Why might a "money printer" at 15x PE be the most undervalued core asset across A-shares, Hong Kong stocks and US stocks?

Author | AI Berkshire Research Team

Time | July 2026


I. Opening: A Tencent that leaves people somewhat "split"

Honestly, discussing Tencent is a rather schizophrenic affair.

On one hand, you open WeChat, check Moments the moment you wake up, scroll through Channels, grab a red packet in a group chat, and order takeout via a mini program—Tencent's products have seeped into almost every capillary of your life.

On the other hand, look at the share price: it has fallen nearly by half from its 2021 peak. The PE ratio is just above 15x — what does that mean? It is one of the lowest valuation ranges for Tencent over the past decade.

Why does the capital market price a platform that over a billion people rely on every day—a cash cow generating more than RMB 220 billion in real cash a year—so low?

Is Tencent failing, or is the market wrong?

In this article, we break down Tencent's financials, business, valuation, buybacks and stake reductions piece by piece. No emotional judgments, just data and logic.


II. First, the bottom line: how solid are Tencent's FY2025 results?

Bottom line first: Tencent's core business is very solid.

In FY2025, Tencent generated revenue ofRMB 751.8 billion, up 13.9% year on year. Net profitRMB 224.8 billion, up 15.9% year on year. Gross margin rose to56.3%—This number deserves a closer look. Gross margin has kept climbing, which shows Tencent's revenue mix is shifting away from low-margin game publishing and cloud toward high-margin advertising, value-added services and Channels e-commerce.

Breaking this down into the three core segments:

First, the WeChat ecosystem.WeChat's monthly active users (DAU) held steady at1.35 billionabout right. This is not a growth story; it is the literal embodiment of "impregnable."

WeChat's moat lies not in "having many features" but inThe non-transferability of social relationship chainsYour family, colleagues and clients are all on it; you cannot get everyone to switch to another app. This network effect is the most extreme form of the "moat" Buffett talks about.

Second, gaming.Tencent is already the world's largest company by game revenue. From Honor of Kings and Game for Peace to PUBG Mobile and VALORANT, Tencent holds the world's largest game IP portfolio. And it is not just a domestic leader: overseas revenue keeps rising as a share of the total, and the company is turning from "China's Tencent" into "the world's Tencent."

Third, advertising and Channels.This is Tencent's most "leveraged" segment right now and the core near-term catalyst.

WeChat Channels DAU has surpassed 500 million, but its ad load rate currently stands at only6%Around that level. For comparison: Douyin's ad load is 15% or even higher. Between 6% and 15%, there is nearly a 3x gap. Even if Channels' load rises only to 10%, the corresponding ad revenue increment would be in the tens of billions.

And Channels is closing the loop on livestream e-commerce. Watch a video, click a link, place an order — the full chain is taking shape. Everyone once thought Tencent could never get e-commerce right, but WeChat Channels may be the roundabout path that gets Tencent there.


III. Valuation: 15x PE — what are you afraid of?

Let us first run the numbers.

Based on FY2025 net profit of RMB 224.8 billion, the current market cap implies a PE of about 15.19x. Where does that sit in Tencent's own history?

Looking back at 2016-2020, Tencent's PE normally ranged between 30x and 50x. Even after the 2021 pullback from its highs, it stayed above 20x in 2022-2023.A 15x PE is in the extremely low range of the past decade.

So what is the market afraid of? I see several main areas:

First, macroeconomic pressure.The uncertainty surrounding the regulatory environment for China's internet sector as a whole, and the systematic underweight of Chinese assets among foreign investors, are indeed real background factors.

Second, Naspers continued to reduce its stake.This is what has suppressed Tencent's share price over the past two yearsThe most direct and the most sustainedone factor. To buy back its own shares, Naspers has been continuously selling Tencent stock, with daily selling pressure of several hundred million to over a billion, weighing on Tencent's share price like a stone. We will discuss this separately later.

Third, the narrative that "Tencent is getting old."Some say Tencent is no longer in a high-growth phase and looks more like a "utility stock" — steady but lacking imagination. That narrative has some merit, but if we break it down, the compound growth rates of Video Accounts, enterprise services (Tencent SaaS) and overseas gaming are not low.

But the key question is:If Tencent really becomes a "utility"—a company that steadily earns over RMB 200 billion a year, keeps buying back and cancelling shares, and pays out large dividends—is 15x PE expensive or cheap?

Let us do a simple arithmetic problem: a company earns RMB 220 billion in net profit each year. Buy it at 15x PE, and you are paying RMB 3.3 trillion for it. If it pays out every last cent of its annual profit to you (in reality Tencent retains some for reinvestment), your annualized return is roughly 6.7% (=1/15).

But the reality is far better than that. Tencent is still growing (net profit up over 15% year on year) and buys back large amounts of stock each year to shrink its share count, so intrinsic value per share compounds far faster than 6.7%.

If you accept that China's best internet assets should command a valuation premium of at least 20x PE (which is not excessive by global market standards), then Tencent's current share price offers roughlyMore than 30% potential upside。


IV. Interpreting the share buyback: Tencent's management is voting with real money

We need to take buybacks seriously.

In 2025, Tencent's full-year buyback amount exceededHKD 100 billionWhat scale is this? Total Hong Kong stock market buybacks in 2024 came to roughly HKD 120 billion, and Tencent alone accounted for nearly all of it.

More critically—Tencent's buyback is a genuine buyback, not a token gesture.

After the buyback, all these shares are cancelled. This means existing shareholders' ownership stakes rise passively, and earnings per share (EPS) is directly accreted. In 2024-2025, Tencent cancelled roughly 3-4% of total share capital through cumulative buybacks. A 3% reduction in share count per year, compounded over a decade, produces a striking effect.

Why did Tencent's management do this? Because they themselves believeThe stock price is severely undervalued.. When a company buys back its own shares with its own cash flow, it is the "most sincere vote of confidence" management can show.

Why has Berkshire Hathaway next door been able to compound so remarkably over decades? One core strategy:Persistent large-scale buybacks when the stock price is undervalued.

Tencent is taking the same path.


Five, some hard truths: the Naspers selldown—an "invisible ceiling" on Tencent's share price

Investing in Tencent, you cannot avoid Naspers.

A brief look at the history: in 2001, Naspers invested USD 32 million in Tencent for roughly a 46% stake. That investment became the most successful venture bet in the history of investing—worth more than USD 200 billion at its peak.

But after 2021, Naspers' parent Prosus began to run into problems of its own: its share price traded at a large discount to the market value of its Tencent stake. To solve this "discount problem", Prosus began steadily selling Tencent shares and using the proceeds to buy back its own stock.

So a brutal landscape emerged:

Prosus sells Tencent every day -> Tencent buys back stock every day -> Prosus sells more -> Tencent steps up buybacks again.

These two giants were locked in battle, leaving Tencent's share price with almost no room for a sustained rally over the past two years—a fixed block of sell orders was waiting every single day.

But this situation will not last forever.

Several key observations:

First, Prosus's discount is narrowing. Once the discount shrinks to a certain level, the incentive to keep trimming its stake will fade.

Second, Prosus's pace of reduction is capped. Naspers and Prosus could in theory reduce their stakes to the point where they no longer hold control, but that would still be a long process. And as a long-term shareholder, Prosus has no need at all to fully exit Tencent — after all, this is an asset that contributes billions of US dollars in dividends to them each year.

Third,Tencent management's exceptional capital allocation capability is in fact also hedging this negative impact.Naspers cashed out over HKD 400 billion, while Tencent bought back more than HKD 150 billion over the same period. You sell, I buy — management is telling the market through its actions: we understand this company's value better than you do.


Six, several key core issues

Q1: Can Tencent's growth continue?

On the revenue side, raising the ad load on Video Accounts (from 6% toward 15%) is a highly certain incremental driver. In gaming, overseas expansion is still early. Continued penetration of WeChat Pay, monetization of WeCom, and Tencent's SaaS offerings (such as the enterprise edition of Tencent Meeting and paid WeCom) are all potential incremental drivers.

On the profit side, the logic for continued gross margin improvement is clear. The share of high-margin advertising revenue is rising, while the low-margin cloud business is optimizing its margins. Management's thinking is clear: "No inefficient expansion through large-scale heavy investment—make every business profitable."

Q2: Will regulatory policy bring more bad news?

The current policy tone is already very clear: "support the healthy development of the platform economy" and "encourage the private economy to grow bigger and stronger." Compared with the 2021 regulatory storm, the broader environment is now completely different. Policy risk, of course, will always exist, but at the margin, the harshest moment has most likely passed.

Q3: Is Tencent still a "technology company"?

That is an interesting question. Some argue Tencent should be valued as a consumer stock, because its core profit comes from game skins, membership subscriptions and ad impressions. Seen that way, a consumer company at 15x PE is genuinely not expensive.

Some also call Tencent a composite of "China's Meta + parts of Snapchat + Nintendo of gaming." If you value that combination piece by piece, 15x does not look expensive by any measure.


Seven: Cross-sectional comparison: how cheap is Tencent against global tech stocks?

A quick comparison (all figures are rough estimates):

Tencent: PE ~15x, net profit growth ~15%

Meta（Facebook）: PE ~23x, growth ~20%

Google: PE ~22x, growth ~15%

Microsoft: PE ~33x, growth ~15%

Apple: PE ~30x, growth ~8%

Nintendo: PE ~20x, growth ~10%

Tencent's profit growth sits roughly in the middle of the tech giants, yet its PE valuation is among the lowest. On profit growth versus valuation (PEG), its value for money ranks at the top among global tech giants.

Of course, A-shares and Hong Kong stocks carry a certain "China discount" — that is an objective reality.But should the discount really be this deep? We believe the market has turned overly pessimistic.


Eight, conclusion: a risk-reward ratio worth serious consideration

Investing is not a black-and-white multiple-choice question.

The problems Tencent faces are real—pressure from Naspers' stake reduction, macroeconomic volatility, and weak market sentiment. These objectively exist and need no denial.

Conversely, however:

- A company with annual profit above RMB 220 billion and growth of over 15%

- A global social moat built on 1.35 billion daily active users

- PE at a ten-year historical low (15x)

- WeChat Channels ads offer a certain 3x incremental growth opportunity

- Management buys back and cancels shares with real cash every day

- The "forced selling" by top global capital will end one day

This is a classic "good company + reasonable price + clear catalyst" setup.

Our view on Tencent can be summed up in two sentences:

In the short term, selling pressure will continue to create volatility, and the share price may stay choppy. But over the medium to long term, Tencent at 15x PE is most likely undervalued.

A company with a moat, the ability to generate free cash flow consistently, honest and capable management, and one that buys back its own stock with its own money every day—such opportunities are actually rare in the capital markets.

If you believe Chinese internet has long-term promise, and that a social tool used daily by 1.35 billion people will not be replaced, then rather than chasing highs in a bubble, it is better to be a little more patient when valuations are low.
