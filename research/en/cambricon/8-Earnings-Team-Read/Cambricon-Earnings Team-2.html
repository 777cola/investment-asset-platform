Core view

Buffett looks at two tables: the moat (why customers don't leave) and cash flow (where the money earned actually sits). Cambricon passes the first and fails the second.

Moat: switching costs are real, though not as deep as pricing power

Switching suppliers forces customers to rewrite operators and retune performance. BANG and Neuware already support DeepSeek, Qwen, Hunyuan, GLM, Kimi and MiniMax. Switching costs exist, and they are the substance of the moat. But the moat is shallower than CUDA's: CUDA is fifteen years of developer habit, BANG a three-year compatibility list.

More important, this moat cannot stop "the customer wanting a second supplier." Buffett's favourite moat is "the customer doesn't want to leave"; Cambricon's is "the customer can't rely on just one." The former is pricing power; the latter an insurance policy. The premium on that policy is the 55% gross margin — it will not disappear, but it will not expand either.

Cash flow: the statement where profit looks good but cash looks bad

Net profit RMB 2.31 billion, operating cash flow RMB 311 million. Buffett would flip straight to the balance sheet: inventory RMB 8.25 billion, prepayments RMB 2.91 billion. His verdict: this is not profit, this is inventory.

The only semiconductor company he ever bought was TSMC — and he bought it and then sold it, on the grounds that "geopolitics is not a variable I can bear." Cambricon's geopolitical exposure is more extreme than TSMC's: it is itself on the entity list. For Buffett, that alone is close to a terminal veto.

Valuation: at 218x P/E, the market is already paying for future cash flow that has yet to materialise

He anchored on long-term Treasury yields his whole life. At 218x P/E, even if net profit grows from RMB 3.3 billion to RMB 10 billion, the valuation still hinges on the multiple not compressing. And multiples always compress.

By his math, net profit of RMB 15 billion in ten years on a 20x multiple implies a market cap of RMB 300 billion, 59% below today's level; justifying the current price would require RMB 36 billion of net profit in ten years at 20x. Not impossible, but it leaves no room for a single mistake in any year.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: There is a moat, but no water in it. Customers stay out of fear of supply disruption, not because switching is impossible; the profit is real, but the cash is not on the books. At a 218x P/E, you are paying a certain price for an uncertain decade. Do not buy.
