Core view

Munger never asks how much a stock can rise, only how it could die. Read the interim report in reverse and three numbers could kill it: inventory, customer concentration and the growth curve.

Inventory of RMB 8.25 billion: the one line item that can turn on you within a single fiscal year

Inventory stands at RMB 8.248 billion, equal to 45.32% of total assets and 60.84% of net assets, against write-down provisions of about RMB 767 million (8.5%). Chip inventory is unusual: it does not depreciate, it becomes obsolete. Siyuan 690 enters mass production in early 2026; if the 590 inventory cannot be sold through 2027, the provision will be 30%, not 8.5%.

In the extreme case, lifting the provision ratio from 8.5% to 25% means an additional RMB 1.49 billion of provisions, and full-year 2026 net profit drops straight from RMB 5.2 billion to RMB 3.7 billion, moving the P/E from 140x to 196x. One accounting line can rewrite a third of the valuation.

Customer concentration at 88.66%: the flip side of the moat is dependence

In 2025 the top five customers accounted for RMB 5.76 billion, or 88.66%, and in Q1 2025 a single large customer at one point accounted for 96.48%. Munger would read this as "it doesn't have customers, it has one customer."

The flip side of dependence is that bargaining power sits with the other side. The customer can shift orders to Huawei at any time, or demand price cuts in the next round of negotiations — a 55% gross margin is in fact fragile when faced with "one customer." If concentration does not fall, the moat is merely "no alternative for now."

Growth trajectory: three quarters, three speeds — the most dangerous signal

2025: +453%. 2026Q1: +159.56%. 2026Q2: +75.83%, up 7.84% QoQ. Munger would say the trendline broke, not demand — the narrative broke.

The market pays 218x P/E because it believes the doubling can continue. Q2's 7.84% already says it cannot. That is not the earnings' fault; it is the expectations' fault — and expectations' mistakes get paid for in price.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: The company need not put a foot wrong. Growth only has to slow a little, inventory write-downs only have to rise a little, and the market only has to rotate into another style, and the stock can fall 60%. All three are quite likely. Avoid.
