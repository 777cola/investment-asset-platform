Core view

This is a publish-ready version: no sector narratives, no grand macro bets, just three numbers — RMB 5.996 billion, RMB 2.311 billion and RMB 311 million.

I. First, the good news

On the evening of August 7, Cambricon reported its 2026 interim results: revenue of RMB 5.996 billion, up 108.13% year on year; net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 2.311 billion, up 122.61%; non-GAAP net profit of RMB 2.166 billion, up 137.30%; gross margin of 55.25%. Profit earned in the first half was already 1.12x the full-year 2025 figure (RMB 2.059 billion).

These results rank in the top tier of A-share semiconductors. The quality is more impressive still: non-GAAP profit accounts for 93.7% of net profit, with no asset sales or government subsidies propping up earnings; gross margin fell only 0.68 points year on year and rose 1.77 points quarter on quarter in Q2 — showing it earns money from its products, not from cutting prices to win orders.

2. Three more unsettling numbers

The first is 7.84%. Q2 revenue was RMB 3.111 billion, up 75.83% year on year — looks decent, but up only 7.84% quarter on quarter. Q1 growth was 159.56% year on year. Three quarters, three speeds — this is not "steady," this is "shifting gears." To hit the 2026 incentive target of RMB 13.5 billion, the company must deliver RMB 7.504 billion in the second half, with Q3 and Q4 averaging over 12% sequential growth.

The second is RMB 8.248 billion. That is period-end inventory, up RMB 3.304 billion from the start of the year, equal to 45.32% of total assets and 60.84% of net assets. Breaking it down: raw materials RMB 5.749 billion, outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion. These are wafers, HBM and work-in-progress chips that have not yet become revenue. The company has booked roughly RMB 767 million in write-down provisions (8.5% of the balance) — whether that is enough depends on how long the 590 inventory can still sell once the Siyuan 690 ramps.

The third is RMB 311 million. That is net operating cash flow for the first half, down 65.83% year on year and just 13.5% of net profit. Where did the money go? Inventory up RMB 3.304 billion, prepayments RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37%). In one sentence: the profit is real, the cash is still on its way.

III. Why the stock fell 6.33% on August 10

On the first trading day after the interim results, the stock closed at RMB 1,124, down 6.33%, wiping about RMB 48 billion off its market value. The market was not rejecting the results; it was repricing growth. On June 30 it had just become the STAR Market's first trillion-market-cap company, closing at RMB 1,595.55 and touching an intraday high of RMB 1,620; that evening the company issued its own risk warning, stating a trailing P/E of 368.97x against an industry average of 74.86x.

A company voluntarily telling the market "I am five times more expensive than my peers" is rare in A-shares. By August 17, the stock stood at RMB 1,156 for a market value of RMB 726.3 billion and a TTM P/E of 218x — still expensive, just less so than a month earlier.

IV. What is a 218x P/E buying?

Reverse the math and it is clearest: at the current price of RMB 726.3 billion, if the market only awards 80x, net profit needs to be RMB 9.08 billion; backing out at a 38.5% net margin requires revenue of RMB 23.6 billion. The company's assessment targets are RMB 13.5 billion for 2026 and RMB 27 billion for 2027—in other words, the current price roughly equals "full achievement of the 2027 assessment target + 80x P/E".

That is not impossible, but it is the optimistic scenario, not the base case. The neutral case, at 2027 revenue of RMB 17.5 billion, net profit of RMB 6.5 billion and 80x, gives a target price of RMB 830, 28% below the current price; the pessimistic case is RMB 400, 65% lower; the optimistic case is RMB 1,450, only 25% higher. Twenty percent upside, sixty percent downside—this arithmetic does not need to be precise.

V. So what should be done

Three actionable disciplines: ① Do not chase — no position at the current price; ② Two watch lines — the share price falls back below RMB 800, or Q3 single-quarter revenue grows ≥15% QoQ and operating cash flow/net profit returns above 0.5; ③ One veto line — if Q3 revenue grows less than 5% QoQ and inventory keeps rising, the shift is a demand problem, not a pacing problem, and the name is dropped outright.

Cambricon is the only scaled supplier in Chinese AI chips besides Huawei, and that position deserves respect. But respecting a company and buying it at this price are two different things. When a good company gets expensive, the best move is to put it on the watch list and do nothing.

One often-overlooked comparison to close: on the same 55% gross margin, Hygon posted RMB 9.099 billion in revenue and RMB 1.798 billion in net profit for the first half of 2026, with R&D spending of RMB 2.652 billion (29.15% of revenue); Cambricon posted RMB 5.996 billion in revenue and RMB 2.311 billion in net profit, with R&D spending of RMB 702 million (11.72%). Cambricon earns higher margins; Hygon invests more heavily. Both are good companies — the difference is that one bets on a single-point breakout and the other on steady dual-line execution — but neither is cheap.

Conclusion

Conclusion: avoid; wait for valuation to fall. The earnings are real, the scarcity is real, and so is the expense. Reopen discussion below RMB 800; upgrade to watch if Q3 rises more than 15% quarter on quarter.
