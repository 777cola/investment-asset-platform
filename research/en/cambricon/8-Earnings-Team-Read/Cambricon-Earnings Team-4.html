Core view

Li Lu's framework asks two questions of any company: where it sits in the arc of civilisation, and whether that position can translate into shareholder returns. Cambricon scores full marks on the first; the second remains unproven.

Compute is the electricity of this technology revolution

The US Entity List (2022/12/15) and TSMC's 7nm supply cutoff have squeezed domestic AI chip supply down to two lines: Huawei Ascend and Cambricon. SMIC's N+2 capacity is limited, and few design companies can secure an allocation. At this position, it is a scarce asset on a ten-year horizon.

The first-half numbers prove it is not a PowerPoint company: RMB 5.996 billion revenue, RMB 2.311 billion net profit, 55.25% gross margin, seven consecutive profitable quarters. In the history of Chinese semiconductors, going from losses to RMB 2 billion in annual profit in just two years is extremely fast.

Returns: industries can win without shareholders winning

Li Lu has studied many Chinese cases of "industry success, shareholder failure": panels, solar, LEDs. The common thread: industry scale grew large, but capacity races ate up returns on capital. Cambricon's current capex direction (RMB 3.953 billion private placement plus RMB 1.16 billion in additional equipment) is exactly such a capacity race.

ROIC is the test. 2026H1 net profit was RMB 2.311 billion, or RMB 4.6 billion annualised, against invested capital (net assets of RMB 13.555 billion plus a small amount of liabilities) of about RMB 16 billion, implying ROIC of around 29% (rough calculation)—a very good number, too good to be true, because it does not yet count the capacity that must be invested over the next two years.

Valuation: industrial value is priced in; shareholder value is not yet proven

At 75.6x price-to-sales, the market has already paid in full for "industry position." Li Lu would ask: how much of that price is for "China must have its own computing power"? About 70%. The remaining 30% is for growth.

His conclusion is usually restrained: a good company in a good position, but the price already discounts the next decade of industry dividends. Waiting for a moment when "the industry is still there and the price is reasonable" fits compounding better than chasing the high now.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: This is one of the most worthwhile companies to track long term in China's AI chip industry, but today it is an "industry stock," not a "shareholder stock." Wait for the price to reflect cash flow rather than narrative before discussing long-term ownership.
