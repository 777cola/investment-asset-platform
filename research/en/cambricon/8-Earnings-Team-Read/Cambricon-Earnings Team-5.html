Core view

Taken together, the four deep dives reveal far more consensus than disagreement: the business is real, the position is scarce, management is reliable, the cash has yet to come back, and the price is expensive. The one genuine dispute is how expensive it has to get before it counts as unbuyable.

Consensus one: no padding in the results

All four agree on the quality of these results. Revenue of RMB 5.996 billion, net profit of RMB 2.311 billion, non-recurring items at 93.7% of profit, gross margin of 55.25% — none of it is manufactured through accounting. Duan Yongping accepts that "the revenue is real," Buffett that "the moat has physical substance," Munger that "the numbers hold up when read backwards," and Li Lu that "the industry position has been delivered."

Consensus 2: Cash is the weak spot

Operating cash flow of RMB 311 million, net profit cash content of 0.135 and inventory of RMB 8.248 billion. The four frame it differently but land in the same place: profit has yet to convert into cash, so the "cash cow" valuation framework does not apply — only the "growth stock" framework does.

Disagreement: is 218x "too expensive" or "expensive for good reason"

Duan Yongping: too expensive; revisit below RMB 800. Buffett: too expensive, and the geopolitical variable is unbearable. Munger: too expensive; a 60% drop needs no reason. Li Lu: expensive, but the industry position merits long-term tracking; wait on price. None of the four says "buy"; the only disagreement is how long to wait.

My verdict: anchor to Duan Yongping's price line (RMB 800), the only executable one. Buffett's and Munger's rejection is qualitative; Li Lu's "long-term tracking" does not constitute a buy case. Avoid, wait for valuation to fall back — consistent with the working paper.

A third view sits outside the disagreement: what if the assessment target is in fact met?

Be honest: if revenue really hits RMB 27 billion in 2027 — the incentive assessment threshold — then at 80x the stock is worth above RMB 1,300, and today's price is cheap. But that requires consecutive QoQ growth above 20% from Q3 onward, in direct conflict with Q2's 7.84%.

The choice is not binary but tiered: buying at the current price is a lottery ticket on "full achievement of the assessment target," with odds of 1:2.6; waiting for Q3 data confirmation before buying improves the odds to 1:1 or better. Same company, different entry point, and the odds differ threefold.

This is also why four perspectives can hold at once: Duan Yongping and Buffett buy "certainty," Munger fears "uncertainty," and Li Lu bets on "ten years out." The same financials, three time scales, three conclusions — all pointing to "no action now." This is where value investing is most counterintuitive: even its biggest bulls may not buy now.

Conclusion

Synthesis: All four masters reject "buy now" with zero dissent, differing only on how to wait. The ruling adopts the strictest executable condition: reopen discussion below RMB 800; if Q3 rises more than 15% QoQ, upgrade to "watch". Rating: avoid, wait for valuation to pull back.
