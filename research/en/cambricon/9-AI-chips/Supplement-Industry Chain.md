## Upstream: two gates, each tighter than the last

The first gate is wafer foundry. After the December 2022 Entity List, TSMC halted supply of 7nm and below to mainland AI chip customers, and advanced nodes were largely ceded to SMIC N+2 (7nm-equivalent). **This is not a problem money can solve**—SMIC's advanced capacity is only so large, Huawei Ascend gets priority, and Cambricon sits further back in the queue. The RMB 2.914 billion prepayment in the first half (+291.37%) is essentially a "queue deposit."

The second gate is HBM. The global market is dominated by SK hynix, Samsung and Micron, while domestic CXMT's HBM3 has only just begun ramping; HBM will be in short supply with rising prices in 2026, and institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027. **The Siyuan 690 uses dual-die + HBM3: no HBM means no 690, and no 690 means no 2027 growth.**

Beyond these two gates lies advanced packaging. Dual-die Chiplet requires 2.5D packaging capacity, TSMC's CoWoS is essentially closed to mainland customers, and domestic JCET and Tongfu are still ramping. **These three hurdles stacked together are the root cause of "design companies not being in control."**

## Midstream: value distribution in design—NVIDIA takes 70%, Cambricon 55%

The same AI chip: Nvidia's gross margin exceeds 70%, Cambricon's is 55.25%, Hygon's 55.21%. Where does the gap come from? **Not manufacturing cost, but the pricing power of the software ecosystem.** Nvidia sells a bundle of "CUDA + chip + networking," and customers pay for migration costs; Cambricon sells "chips you can actually get," and customers pay for supply security.

These two kinds of money are different in nature. Ecosystem money can rise year after year; supply-security money disappears as supply loosens. **So the 55% gross margin is a "scarcity rent," not a "technology premium"**—it is highly sensitive to the supply landscape and, conversely, less sensitive to technology iteration.

Within the midstream there is another layer: Hygon runs a dual CPU + DCU line, with 2026H1 revenue of RMB 9.099 billion, net profit of RMB 1.798 billion, and R&D of RMB 2.652 billion (29.15% of revenue); Cambricon fights on a single line, with revenue of RMB 5.996 billion, net profit of RMB 2.311 billion, and R&D of RMB 702 million (11.72%). **Hygon trades higher R&D for a steadier base; Cambricon trades lower R&D for a higher profit margin**—two ways of living, both defensible.

## Downstream: three buyers, one playbook for squeezing prices

Only three types of buyers purchase cards: internet cloud providers (ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu), carriers and local intelligent computing centers, and industry customers. The first two account for the vast majority. **Cloud providers buy on "performance/price + supply security"; intelligent computing centers buy on "policy + delivery"**—neither cares about brand loyalty.

That is where the 88.66% customer concentration comes from—not for lack of wanting to diversify, but because there are only so many buyers to begin with. The more concentrated the customers, the stronger their bargaining power; Cambricon sustains 55% gross margin because "no third supplier can deliver for now," not because of negotiating leverage. **Once Ascend capacity opens up, or cloud providers' in-house chips mature, price cuts begin immediately.**

## Cycle position: capex at a high, supply shifting from scarcity to competition

Demand remains elevated: the four major North American cloud providers sharply raised 2026 capex, and domestic carriers and local intelligent-computing centers continue to tender; memory has entered a super-cycle (DRAM contract prices up 60%–70% year-to-date, NAND up over 100%), indicating that compute infrastructure is still expanding. **This is the industry foundation for Cambricon's 2026 revenue doubling.**

Supply is expanding rapidly: SMIC is expanding advanced capacity, CXMT's HBM is ramping, and new players such as Moore Threads are listing to raise funds and expand capacity. **The industry is moving from "quota scarcity" to "full competition"**—a process typically accompanied by margin compression and valuation de-rating, both occurring at once.

So the cycle position can be described as follows: **demand is in the mid-to-late stage of the cycle, supply is in the early-to-mid stage of expansion.** For design companies, this is the most uncomfortable combination—volumes are still rising, but prices are starting to loosen.

## Its position in the chain: in one sentence

**The scarce node on the design side, the weak customer on the manufacturing side.** Downstream, it is the customer's only "second choice" and holds pricing power; upstream, it is one name on SMIC's and the memory makers' waiting lists, with no bargaining power. This asymmetry dictates its high gross margin and poor cash flow.It also dictates its valuation logic: **the market pays for the "scarce node" (218x P/E), but cash flow exposes the "weak customer" side (cash conversion 0.135).** The two cannot coexist for long—either cash flow improves and proves it is genuinely strong, or the valuation falls back to a level cash flow can support.
