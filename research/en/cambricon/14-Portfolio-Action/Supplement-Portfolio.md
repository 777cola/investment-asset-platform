## Why 0% and not "try 1%"

Many will say: with a company this good, buy 1% to watch it. I do not accept this approach. A 1% position makes no money; it only makes you stare at the stock price every day. It also creates a psychological anchor—once you buy, you start looking for bullish reasons, which is called "position bias."

The more practical reason is the payoff. An expected return of −24% means every purchase at this level loses money over the long run; a 24% loss on a 1% position affects the portfolio by only 0.24%, but it consumes attention and judgment. At 218x P/E, attention is more expensive than money.

One more piece of portfolio-level arithmetic: assume a portfolio of RMB 1 million and a 5% cap (RMB 50,000). Under the neutral scenario (RMB 830), a 4% gain; the optimistic scenario (RMB 1,450), a 25% gain; and the pessimistic scenario (RMB 400), a 65% loss—the expected contribution of a 5% position is −1.2%. In other words, even if this position is right, it contributes only 1 point; if wrong, it loses 3 points. With risk-reward this asymmetric, the only correct action is not to do it.

## How was the RMB 800 threshold set?

This is not a guess. 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 6.5 billion × 80x = RMB 520 billion market cap ÷ 628.29 million shares = RMB 827. Setting 800 leaves a 3% buffer. At this price, the neutral case offers 4% upside, the optimistic case 81% upside, and the pessimistic case −50%, shifting the risk-reward from 1:2.6 to about 1:1.

Why not wait for RMB 700? Because waiting for RMB 700 means you believe even the neutral case will not materialize—that is the pricing of the "pessimistic case." If it does reach RMB 700, one thing must first be confirmed: has the valuation fallen, or has the thesis broken. If the latter, do not buy even at RMB 700.

## Triggers for adding to and trimming positions

Three conditions to add to the position (3% → 5%), all of which must be met simultaneously: ① Q3 and Q4 revenue up more than 15% QoQ (full-year RMB 13.5 billion achieved); ② operating cash flow/net profit back above 0.5; ③ gross margin no lower than 53%. Three conditions to cut the position (5% → 0), any one of which triggers: ① single-quarter gross margin falls below 50%; ② top-five customer concentration rises above 90% or a major customer shifts orders; ③ share price returns above RMB 1,400 (valuation overextended).Note there is no "stop-loss price" here. For a stock that swings 5% a day on average, a price stop-loss will only shake you out at the bottom. Use fundamentals for stop-loss, use price for take-profit—this is the only workable discipline for high-volatility growth stocks.

## Correlation with other holdings: it is not a diversification tool

Cambricon's earnings driver is "domestic AI compute capex," which moves highly in the same direction as the following assets: Hygon Information (same track, rough correlation coefficient 0.7–0.8), SMIC (upstream capacity, 0.6–0.7), and memory and optical modules (AI compute chain, 0.5–0.7). If your portfolio already holds AI compute chain positions, adding Cambricon amounts to adding leverage, not diversification.

Its correlation with consumer, dividend and utility assets is low or even negative, so in theory it can hedge — but that is "hedging low-volatility assets with high-volatility assets," putting the cart before the horse. The right approach: cap the AI compute chain as a whole at 10%–15% of the portfolio, with Cambricon only a small slice of that.

Correlation coefficients are rough estimates based on industry logic and historical price action, without rigorous regression; they serve only to judge position diversification and are not a quantitative basis.
