## Q: Is this a good business?

Answer: a good business, but not an easy one. Selling chips to cloud providers at a 55.25% gross margin and 38.54% net margin — few chip companies worldwide can hit those numbers. But the money it earns is not in its own pocket: net profit for the first half was RMB 2.311 billion, while cash flow was only RMB 311 million, with the rest sitting in inventory (RMB 8.248 billion) and prepayments (RMB 2.914 billion).

The test of a good business is that it makes money while you sleep.This business requires constant vigilance over capacity, customer acceptance and HBM prices — that is not making money while you sleep, that is making money on your feet. A business that makes money on its feet can still be worth owning, but not at a 218x P/E.

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

Answer: It will be there, but most likely not first. China will certainly have its own AI chip industry, and Cambricon is one of its core players — that I believe. But "being there" and "being first" are two different things. Huawei's Ascend has cloud, complete systems and policy resources, and prioritizes capacity for its own use; Cambricon's position is "second supplier" — customers keep you alive so they are not locked in by a single vendor, not because you are better.

Being second supplier has an upside: stable orders, no need to win the technology war. It also has a downside: someone else sets your ceiling. Customers give you 20%–30% share for bargaining leverage; they will not give you 70%. So a decade from now it will most likely still be around, with revenue possibly reaching several tens of billions of RMB, but it will not be the one defining the industry.

## Q: Can you understand it?

Answer: the business is understandable; the orders are not. What it does, who it sells to, and why it makes money can be explained in half an hour. But how much cash it will collect next quarter, I cannot calculate — Q1 2026 revenue was RMB 2.885 billion (+159.56% YoY), Q2 was RMB 3.111 billion (+75.83% YoY, +7.84% QoQ), three quarters, three different speeds. The top five customers account for 88.66%, and in Q1 2025 a single large customer at one point accounted for 96.48%. Under this structure, "forecasting" is just self-encouragement.

Where I cannot understand, I do not bet. This is not modesty; it is discipline. What you cannot understand, you cannot hold when it falls, and you cannot hold when it rises either.

## Q: At what price to buy?

Answer: below RMB 800, or wait for revenue to reach RMB 23.6 billion. First, where the RMB 800 comes from: my neutral case is 2027 revenue of RMB 17.5 billion and net profit of RMB 6.5 billion, at 80x, giving a market cap of RMB 520 billion, or RMB 830 per share; leaving some margin, I start looking below RMB 800.

Now the RMB 23.6 billion: at the current price of RMB 726.3 billion, if the market is only willing to pay 80x, net profit needs to be RMB 9.08 billion, which at a 38.5% net margin implies revenue of RMB 23.6 billion. The company's own assessment targets are RMB 13.5 billion for 2026 and RMB 27 billion for 2027—in other words, the current price already fully discounts the 2027 target. Others treat the target as an expectation; I treat it as a ceiling.

If either condition is met, I reopen the discussion. If neither is met, I watch. Watching costs nothing.

## Q: What is the biggest fear?

Answer: two things worry me. The first is inventory. Inventory of RMB 8.248 billion, of which raw materials are RMB 5.749 billion and outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion, against which roughly RMB 767 million of write-downs have been provided (8.5%). In chips, every product iteration discounts the prior generation's stock — Siyuan 690 goes into mass production in early 2026, so how much longer can 590 inventory sell? At a 10% provision, RMB 820 million would wipe out three-tenths of half a year's profit.

The second is customers switching orders. Once Huawei's Ascend 950 series frees up capacity, customers have no reason to keep a second supplier. This is not a technology question but a politics-and-capacity question, and I cannot call it. What I fear most is not the company deteriorating, but the valuation moving first while the company does not deteriorate. From June 30 to August 17, the stock fell 27.6% and the P/E went from 369x to 218x, and the company did nothing wrong.

## Q: So what now?

Answer: Wait. Don't chase, don't short, don't predict. Wait for one of two signals: the price falls back below RMB 800, or Q3 revenue returns to above 15% quarter-on-quarter with cash flow keeping pace with profit. Waiting is not a waste of time; waiting is the only free action in investing.

If one must offer a word of encouragement: this company is doing the right things — the product is real, the customers are real, the gross margin is real. It has simply been bought at too high a price. When a good company has been bought at too high a price, the best strategy is to put it on the watchlist and then do nothing.
