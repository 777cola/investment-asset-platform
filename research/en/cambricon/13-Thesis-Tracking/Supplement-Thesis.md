## Two things that hold: the gap is real, and so is the gross profit

The first point (the gap persists) needs almost no argument. As long as US controls are not relaxed and Ascend capacity is prioritized for internal use, customers must keep a non-Huawei path. In the first half, contract liabilities rose from RMB 610,000 to RMB 188 million—customers voted with prepayments; Q2 was still +75.83% year on year, showing demand has not collapsed.

The third point (gross margin) is actually more valuable. 55.25%, Q2 single-quarter 56.10%, up another 1.77 points sequentially—gross margin was still improving in the same quarter that growth shifted gears—this rules out "price cuts for volume." If it were sustaining growth by cutting prices, gross margin should have dropped 5 points long ago.

## The most critical one remains unverified: whether deliveries can keep pace

The second point is the core of the entire tracking table. The company has staked RMB 2.914 billion in prepayments and RMB 8.248 billion in inventory on capacity, with plans to deliver about 500,000 units in 2026 (media citation; the company has not issued formal guidance). If capacity comes online, Q3 revenue should grow more than 15% quarter-on-quarter; if it does not, Q2's 7.84% becomes the new normal.

The verification method is simple: watch Q3 single-quarter revenue. RMB 3.111 billion × 1.15 = RMB 3.58 billion is the passing line; below RMB 3.3 billion (up 5% quarter-on-quarter) is a fail. This number will be available in late October; no need to wait a year.

## Three that do not hold: cash, concentration, valuation

Criterion four (cash) is the hardest to fix. OCF/net profit of 0.135 needs to return to 0.5, meaning second-half operating cash flow must exceed RMB 2 billion—nearly impossible while inventory build continues. This criterion will most likely improve only in 2027, when capacity stops expanding.

Criterion five (customer concentration) must wait for the annual report. The 88.66% for 2025 is historical data; the 2026 interim report did not disclose it, so strictly speaking it is "unknown." I treat it as "not met" because there is no evidence of improvement. Criterion six (valuation) changes daily; at 218x now, it is still more than half way from offering a margin of safety.

## Falsification conditions: three red lines

① Q3 single-quarter revenue down more than 5% QoQ (below RMB 3.27 billion) with inventory still rising — this shows the shift is not a timing issue but a demand issue; walk away and stop waiting for RMB 800. ② Single-quarter gross margin falls below 50% — this shows the company has begun cutting prices to move volume, the moat is leaking, and the valuation framework needs to be rebuilt. ③ Top five customers rise above 90% of revenue, or public information emerges of a major customer shifting orders to Ascend — the second-supplier thesis is broken.

If any one of the three red lines triggers, move the company from the "watch list" to "no longer covered." The value of falsification conditions lies not in forecasting, but in deciding in advance "under what circumstances I admit I am wrong."

## Watch calendar for next steps

- December 2026: industry tenders and cloud vendors' 2027 capex guidance — determines whether the second assumption (a persistent gap) still holds next year.

- January 2027: annual earnings pre-announcement — whether the RMB 13.5 billion assessment line is met directly determines whether incentives unlock.
