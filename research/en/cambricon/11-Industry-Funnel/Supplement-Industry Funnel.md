## First-pass screen: 10 in, 5 out

Screen all domestic AI chip names: Huawei Ascend, Cambricon, Hygon, Kunlunxin, Moore Threads, MetaX, Enflame, Biren, Iluvatar CoreX and Nvidia (China sales). The first cut removes the "can't buy" names — Huawei Ascend, Kunlunxin, MetaX, Enflame and Biren are either unlisted or have no defined listing timetable, so they drop out immediately; Nvidia is subject to export controls, and A-share investors cannot directly own its China business.That leaves four: Cambricon, Hygon, Moore Threads and Iluvatar CoreX.

The second cut removes the "can't understand the financials" name: Iluvatar CoreX is unlisted and drops out. The third cut removes the "loss-making with no valuation anchor" name: Moore Threads posted H1 2026 revenue of RMB 1.736 billion, up 147.42% YoY, at a 57.0% gross margin, but net profit was still RMB -12 million; on 2025 revenue of RMB 1.506 billion the static P/S is roughly 178x — a "growth option" rather than a business that can be valued, so it stays in this round of comparison but is not selected. Two remain: Cambricon and Hygon.

## Second-layer comparison: Cambricon vs Hygon, two routes to survival

Hygon's strength lies in its base business: dual CPU and DCU lines, with 2026H1 revenue of RMB 9.099 billion, net profit of RMB 1.798 billion, gross margin of 55.21% and R&D spend of RMB 2.652 billion (29.15% of revenue). It is closer to "China's AMD" — steady, but a notch slower (+66.52% vs +108.13%).

Cambricon's strength is elasticity: a pure AI chip play, with gross margin of 55.25%, net margin of 38.54% (Hygon 22.69%) and revenue growth twice as fast. The cost is customer concentration of 88.66%, cash flow of RMB 311 million and inventory of RMB 8.248 billion.

Both look expensive on valuation: Cambricon at 218x TTM, Hygon at about 250x forward. In other words, the case for Cambricon rests not on cheapness but on being "purer and more elastic" — on the AI compute theme, purity is the source of the premium.

## Final pick: why this one and not Hygon

Three reasons. First, purity: Cambricon derives 100% of revenue from AI chips, so every additional yuan of AI capex gives it the greatest leverage, whereas half of Hygon's business is CPUs tied to IT localization, leaving it only half-exposed to AI. Second, margins: 38.54% versus 22.69% — on the same revenue, Cambricon earns 70% more. Third, the scarcity narrative: as the first STAR Market stock with a market cap of RMB 1 trillion and the only pure-play AI chip name, it draws better investor attention and liquidity.

The case against Hygon is equally clear: its R&D intensity is 2.5 times Cambricon's (29.15% vs 11.72%), implying greater pressure to catch up technologically; its trailing P/E of 250x is even richer than Cambricon's; and its DCU follows a "CUDA-like" path, which over the long run means competing head-on with Nvidia on ecosystem — a harder outcome to call. Hygon is "steady"; Cambricon is "sharp" — and this cycle's main theme calls for sharp.

## Final selection is not a buy: the valuation funnel rules it out

The industry funnel answers "which company to buy"; the valuation funnel answers "whether to buy now." Cambricon trades at 218x P/E (TTM), 75.6x P/S and 53.6x P/B. Our base case is RMB 830 (−28%), bear case RMB 400 (−65%) and bull case RMB 1,450 (+25%). Twenty percent upside against sixty percent downside — the odds point the wrong way.

The funnel therefore ends at: keep it on the list, zero in the position. If one must own a domestic AI chip name, the value-for-money ranking at the current price is "no buy > Hygon > Cambricon > Moore Threads" — no buy comes first, because all three are expensive and Cambricon's growth is decelerating most visibly (Q2 up 7.84% QoQ).

What would move it from "list" to "position"? One of two conditions: the share price falls below RMB 800 (77x 2027 base-case net profit), or Q3 revenue returns to above 15% QoQ growth with cash content back at 0.5. The first waits on the market, the second on the company — neither requires action now.
