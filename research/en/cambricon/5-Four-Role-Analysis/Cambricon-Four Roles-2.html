Core view

The income statement says "RMB 2.3 billion earned," the cash flow statement says "only RMB 300 million collected," and the balance sheet says "RMB 8.2 billion sitting in inventory." The three statements do not contradict each other; they describe the same thing: this company is trading cash for the future.

Income statement: the growth is real, and so is the leverage release

Revenue RMB 5.996 billion (+108.13%), gross profit RMB 3.313 billion (+105.62%), net profit RMB 2.311 billion (+122.61%), non-GAAP net profit RMB 2.166 billion (+137.30%). Net profit grew faster than revenue because the expense ratio fell: R&D spending dropped from 18.81% to 11.72% of revenue. This is operating leverage, not accounting manipulation.

Three quality checks: non-GAAP net profit is 93.7% of net profit (high); non-recurring gains and losses are only RMB 145 million (low); gross margin fell just 0.68 points year on year (stable). All three pass, showing profit came from selling cards, not from asset sales or subsidies.

Cash flow statement: the crux of the entire report

Operating cash flow RMB 311 million, down 65.83% YoY; cash conversion of net profit 0.135. Financing cash flow negative RMB 657 million (dividends and debt repayment). For a company with RMB 2.3 billion in net profit, free cash inflow of only RMB 300 million means, in Buffett's eyes, "it didn't make money."

Where the money went: inventories +RMB 3.304 billion, prepayments +RMB 2.17 billion (RMB 2.914 billion vs about RMB 745 million a year earlier, back-calculated from +291.37%). The two together total roughly RMB 5.47 billion, which more than covers the RMB 2 billion gap between net profit and cash flow — the excess funded by payables and contract liabilities. This is not bleeding cash; it is a transfusion to the supply chain.

Balance sheet: a fabless company's shift toward asset-heavy operations

Total assets RMB 18.199 billion (+35.43%), net assets attributable to shareholders RMB 13.555 billion (+14.53%). Inventory is 45.32% of total assets and 60.84% of net assets; the debt-to-asset ratio is about 25.5% (rough calculation), with almost no interest-bearing debt. No debt risk — that is the good news.

The bad news is asset quality. Of the RMB 8.248 billion of inventory, raw materials account for RMB 5.749 billion and outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion — these are not finished goods, but "materials not yet made into cards". Their value depends on two things: whether the wafers come back on schedule, and whether the cards sell on schedule. Any delay in either link, and impairment begins.

Return and Valuation: Is the 29% ROIC an Illusion or Real

First-half net profit was RMB 2.311 billion, or about RMB 4.6 billion annualized; invested capital was about RMB 16 billion (net assets of RMB 13.555 billion plus a small amount of debt), implying ROIC of about 29% (rough calculation). That figure is top-tier for a manufacturer. But it rests on two premises: gross margin holds at 55%, and capacity does not have to be funded with its own money.

Once it builds its own capacity or sharply raises equipment spending (it just raised equipment outlays by RMB 1.16 billion in July), ROIC will drift lower. Buffett would dismiss 29% as a cyclical peak and use 15%–20% for long-term assumptions; at 18% ROIC and a market cap of RMB 726.3 billion, it would need RMB 130 billion of invested capital each year to sustain that, which is impossible. This is where valuation and return on capital diverge.

Conclusion

Financial conclusion: Strong income statement, poor cash flow statement, clean but distorted balance sheet. ROIC is high, but rests on the assumption that capacity does not have to be funded with its own money. This is a good company, not a good price.
