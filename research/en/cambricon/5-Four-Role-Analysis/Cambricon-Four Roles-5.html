Team Lead summary: three disagreements, one ruling — Cambricon

Core view

Four reports completed independently, with highly convergent conclusions: the business is viable, the financials are strong, the industry is right, the risks are elevated, and the price is too expensive. Only three points of disagreement genuinely require adjudication.

Consensus: four points beyond dispute

1) The business works: selling accelerator cards to cloud providers and intelligent computing centers, 55% gross margin, 38% net margin, 2026H1 revenue of RMB 5.996 billion and net profit of RMB 2.311 billion. 2) The position is scarce: after the Entity List and TSMC's supply cutoff, only Ascend and Cambricon remain as scaled domestic suppliers of training chips. 3) Cash has not come back: operating cash flow of RMB 311 million, cash content of net profit of 0.135, inventory of RMB 8.248 billion. 4) The price is too expensive: 218x TTM P/E, 75.6x P/S.

Not one of the four reports says "you can buy now."That is unusual across four parallel roles — normally the business and financial angles clash; this time they agree, because the problem is not the business, it is the price.
Disagreement one: is the 7.84% QoQ in Q2 delivery cadence or peak demand

The business analyst (Duan Yongping lens) sees it as timing: prepayments +291.37%, inventory +RMB 3.304 billion, showing the company is locking up capacity, and revenue will return once capacity is in place; customers are still paying prepayments (contract liabilities RMB 188 million vs RMB 610,000 at the start of the year).

The risk analyst (Li Lu lens) sees it as a demand signal: if this were a capacity problem, the company should have explained the delivery schedule on the earnings call, but on August 12 Chen Tianshi "did not provide further explanation" when pressed on Q2 growth and hardware deliveries.No explanation is itself information.

Verdict: treat as "unconfirmed." Evidence is insufficient on both sides, so "it comes back once capacity is in place" cannot be the base case. The neutral scenario therefore uses 2027 revenue of RMB 17.5 billion (rather than the RMB 27 billion incentive line), applying a 65% discount to the timing assumption.
Divergence 2: Can the scarcity premium persist long term

An industry analyst (Munger lens) says no: the industry is moving from "quota scarcity" to "full competition." In five years, three players will survive and gross margins will revert to 45%. The 218x P/E prices the scarcity period, and that period is ending.

A business analyst says it can last longer: as long as US restrictions are not relaxed, someone will pay for the second-supplier position; the A-share market is also willing to pay a premium for "the only one" — the STAR Market's first trillion-market-cap stock, and that label itself carries capital value.

Verdict: the premium can exist, but it cannot serve as the valuation basis.This report values the stock at 80x (neutral) rather than 218x, which assumes the premium converges gradually. If the premium persists long term, actual returns will exceed the neutral case — that is upside for holders, not a reason to buy.
Divergence 3: Is the RMB 8.2 billion inventory an order backlog or a landmine?

The business analyst leans positive: RMB 5.749 billion of raw materials and RMB 2.537 billion of commissioned processing represent "stockpiling for orders"; contract liabilities rose from RMB 610,000 to RMB 188 million, indicating orders are coming in.

The financial analyst (Buffett lens) and the risk analyst lean negative: the RMB 188 million of advance receipts covers only 2.3% of RMB 8.248 billion of inventory, so the stockpiling is almost entirely self-funded; the RMB 767 million (8.5%) already provisioned is low, versus 10%–15% typically at peers.

Verdict: neutral to negative. Acknowledge that the stockpiling has order support (otherwise it would be expensed rather than booked as inventory), but the risk exposure exceeds the payoff: a 10% price decline means RMB 820 million, equal to 30% of half-year profit. This goes into m13's falsification conditions.
Ruling and enforcement

All three disagreements resolve in the same direction: the business earns a pass, the price vetoes. Across the four reports, the rating stays "Avoid, wait for valuation to fall," consistent with the working papers. Three execution points:

  • No position: at the current price of RMB 1,156, no tracking position—avoid the psychological trap of "just buying a little."
  • Price line RMB 800: corresponds to 77x 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 6.5 billion, at which point the risk-reward improves from 1:2.6 to about 1:1.
  • Data line: Q3 single-quarter revenue up ≥15% QoQ and operating cash flow/net profit ≥0.5—if both are met, upgrade to "Watch" without waiting for price.

A final methodological note: the greatest value of running four roles in parallel is not "four opinions," but forcing you to write "why I'm bullish" and "why I dare not buy" separately. For Cambricon, both statements hold—that is its true current state.

One more execution detail: no shorting. A stock at 218x P/E can be very expensive, but A-shares lack convenient shorting tools—STAR Market margin lending is costly with scarce borrowable supply; more importantly, the scarcity narrative can ignite at any time on new policy or orders, as the single-day +7.66% on June 30 showed. The right posture for avoiding is "not participating," not "betting the other way."

Conclusion

Team Lead conclusion: Four points of consensus, three areas of disagreement; every ruling points to "avoid, wait for valuation to pull back." Two triggers — the price line at RMB 800 and the data line of 15% QoQ in Q3 — either one reopens discussion. Rating is consistent with the underlying work.
