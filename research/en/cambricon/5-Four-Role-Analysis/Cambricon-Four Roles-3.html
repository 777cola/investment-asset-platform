Core view

The structure of China's AI chip industry in one sentence: supply is squeezed by controls into two tracks, while demand is monopolized by a handful of cloud providers. Design companies in the middle have neither production capacity nor the ultimate source of pricing power.

Supply side: the Entity List splits the industry in two

On December 15, 2022, the US BIS added 36 Chinese companies, including Cambricon and YMTC, to the Entity List. TSMC subsequently stopped supplying 7nm and below to mainland AI chip customers, and domestic advanced processes were essentially consolidated into SMIC N+2. The result is not that "China cannot make AI chips," but that "only a few companies can secure capacity."

Those with quotas: Huawei Ascend (capacity prioritized for internal use, taking the bulk), Cambricon (second), Hygon (CPU+DCU dual line), plus Kunlunxin, MetaX, Moore Threads and others. This is a "quota system" industry, not a "free competition" industry. Munger would say: under a quota system, the number two is usually comfortable, but the ceiling is set by the quota.

Demand side: three buyers decide an industry

There are only three types of card buyers: internet cloud providers (ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu), telecom operators (the big three plus regional intelligent computing centers), and industry customers (financial, energy, government). The first two account for the vast majority, and both are developing in-house chips or deeply tied to suppliers.

This means bargaining power sits with the buyer. Cloud providers can say, "I'll either use Ascend, build my own, or buy from you — but you have to give me a price." Cambricon sustains 55% gross margin not through negotiating leverage, but because "for now there is no third supplier." Once that premise changes, so does the margin.

Competitive landscape: Ascend is both a peer and a rule-setter

Huawei's Ascend 910C/950 series, CANN ecosystem, and full stack from cloud to complete systems—it is not "a competitor," it is a system. Customers buying Ascend are buying certainty: available capacity, service, and policy backing. Cambricon sells "the reassurance of a second supplier," and the scale of these two demands differs by an order of magnitude.

Hygon takes another path: dual lines of CPU + DCU, with revenue of RMB 9.099 billion in the first half of 2026 (+66.52%), net profit of RMB 1.798 billion (+49.69%), gross margin of 55.21%, and R&D investment of RMB 2.652 billion (29.15% of revenue). Its R&D intensity is 2.5 times that of Cambricon, while its net margin is only 60% of Cambricon's—this is the difference between "stability with an established base" and "high elasticity from a single product."

Cycle position: peak AI capex, mid-stage domestic substitution

Globally, the four major North American cloud providers sharply raised 2026 capex, and AI compute remains the main track; memory has entered a super-cycle, with DRAM contract prices up 60%–70% and NAND up over 100% since the start of the year, HBM in short supply, and institutions expecting the shortage to persist into 2027. Domestically, import substitution has moved from "pilots" to "volume procurement" — the industry backdrop for Cambricon's 2026 revenue doubling.

The risk lies in the cycle position. AI capex has grown at a high rate for three consecutive years, and in the second half of 2026 the market began questioning the return on investment; once North American cloud providers slow down, global AI chip valuations will be the first to de-rate, and domestic names will follow.Cambricon's problem is not that the industry is weak, but that it is so strong that everyone is expanding capacity.

Endgame: three survive, two make money

Munger-style reasoning: in five years, China's AI chip industry will most likely support three survivors — Huawei Ascend (ecosystem), one of Cambricon or Hygon (independent third party), and one focused purely on inference (one of Kunlunxin / Moore Threads / MetaX). Design companies will shift from "scarcity" to "full competition", with gross margins reverting from 55% toward 45%.

This is fatal to current valuations: the market assigns 218x on a "scarcity phase" basis, but the industry is moving into a "competition phase". When the cycle and the valuation point in opposite directions, that is the hardest money to make.

Conclusion

Industry view: Right sector, right position, wrong timing. The supply quota system gave it two years of excess profit; concentrated demand gave buyers two years of leverage to push prices down. The industry is moving from scarcity to competition, yet valuations still price in scarcity.
