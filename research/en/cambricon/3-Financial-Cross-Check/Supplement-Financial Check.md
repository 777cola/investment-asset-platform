## Six items that line up: the interim results themselves are not in dispute

Revenue, net profit, non-recurring-adjusted profit, R&D, operating cash flow, total assets and net assets — all six line items match digit for digit across the two sources. This is standard for A-share interim reports, because every media outlet copies the same filing. So "dual-source verification" here verifies not the numbers but whether the transcription is correct: confirming that RMB 5.996 billion is not 5.969 billion and that RMB 2.311 billion is not 2.131 billion — that step is enough.

A few derived metrics, pinned down: gross margin = 33.13/59.96 = 55.25%; net margin = 23.11/59.96 = 38.54%; non-recurring-adjusted profit is 93.7% of net profit, indicating high earnings quality, with non-recurring gains and losses of only RMB 145 million (mainly government subsidies and wealth-management income). Profit was not manufactured by selling assets — this matters more than the numbers themselves.

## The first mismatch: inventory 82.48 or 90.15

The broker note cites "inventory gross carrying amount of RMB 9.015 billion," while the company discloses "inventory net book value of RMB 8.248 billion" — a gap of RMB 767 million. This is not a transcription error — gross carrying amount is the original cost before write-down provisions; net book value is the amount after provisions. The RMB 767 million difference is the write-down provision the company has already taken against inventory, about 8.5% of the gross amount.

This detail is more interesting than the numbers themselves: it shows the company knows its RMB 8.2 billion of inventory carries obsolescence risk and has provided 8.5% against it. Is 8.5% enough? Consider its product cadence — the Siyuan 590 was the mainstay in 2024–2025, the 690 enters mass production in early 2026, and once the 690 ramps, 590 inventory will be marked down. If the transition comes faster than expected, the 8.5% provision is thin; if the 590 can still sell for two more years (which is indeed the case in government/enterprise and carrier markets), it is sufficient. This report uses RMB 8.248 billion throughout.

## The second mismatch: market cap 7,271 vs 7,263

The market snapshot gives RMB 727.1 billion; my own calculation gives RMB 726.3 billion, a difference of RMB 0.8 billion, or 0.11%. There are only two possible reasons: the share count used is not 628.29 million shares (possibly including unregistered restricted shares or a difference in scope), or the snapshot price differs slightly from the closing price. The 0.11% difference does not affect any judgment, but it must be stated clearly in 03, because all subsequent PE and PS figures derive from this number.

This report uses a unified basis: market cap = RMB 1,156.00 × 628.29 million shares = RMB 726.3 billion. Hence PE(TTM) = 7,263/33.32 = 217.9x; PS(TTM) = 7,263/96.12 = 75.6x; PB = 7,263/135.55 = 53.6x.

## The number most often gotten wrong: the P/E ratio has four versions

① 368.97x — the figure in the company's 30 June risk-warning announcement, with a denominator of "2025 annual report + 2026Q1 − 2025Q1" = RMB 2.717 billion and a share price of RMB 1,595.55; ② 217.9x — this report's basis, with the denominator switched to TTM RMB 3.332 billion (including 2026Q2) and a share price of RMB 1,156; ③ about 140x — using the 2026 incentive target of RMB 13.5 billion × a 38.5% net margin to roughly calculate net profit of RMB 5.2 billion; ④ about 112x — using 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 6.5 billion.

All four numbers are correct; what differs is the denominator and the timing. If the report writer mixes them, readers will think the share price fell 40% in a month while profit stayed flat — in fact the share price fell 27.6% and profit rose 22.7%, and the two directions together compressed the multiple from 369 to 218. That is why layer 03 has to be done: not to catch errors, but to give the later valuation a benchmark that does not contradict itself.
