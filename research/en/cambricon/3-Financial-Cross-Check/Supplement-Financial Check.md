## Six things line up: the interim results themselves are not in dispute

Revenue, net profit, non-recurring-adjusted profit, R&D, operating cash flow, total assets and net assets — all six line items match digit for digit across the two sources. That is standard for A-share interim reports, since every media outlet copies the same filing. So "dual-source verification" here verifies not the numbers but whether the transcription is correct: confirming that RMB 5.996 billion is not 5.969 billion and that RMB 2.311 billion is not 2.131 billion — that step is enough.

A few derived metrics, pinned down: gross margin = 33.13/59.96 = 55.25%; net margin = 23.11/59.96 = 38.54%; non-recurring-adjusted profit is 93.7% of net profit, pointing to high earnings quality, with non-recurring gains and losses of only RMB 145 million (mainly government subsidies and wealth-management income). Profit was not manufactured by selling assets — which matters more than the numbers themselves.

## The first mismatch: inventory 82.48 or 90.15

The broker note cites "inventory gross carrying amount of RMB 9.015 billion" against the company's disclosed "inventory net book value of RMB 8.248 billion" — a gap of RMB 767 million. This is no transcription error: gross carrying amount is original cost before write-down provisions; net book value is the amount after provisions. The RMB 767 million difference is the write-down provision already taken against inventory, about 8.5% of the gross amount.

The detail matters more than the numbers themselves: management knows its RMB 8.2 billion of inventory carries obsolescence risk and has provided 8.5% against it. Is 8.5% enough? Consider the product cadence — the Siyuan 590 was the mainstay in 2024–2025, the 690 enters mass production in early 2026, and once the 690 ramps, 590 inventory will be marked down. If the transition comes faster than expected, the 8.5% provision is thin; if the 590 can still sell for two more years (which is indeed the case in government/enterprise and carrier markets), it is sufficient. This report uses RMB 8.248 billion throughout.

## The second mismatch: market cap 7,271 vs 7,263

The market snapshot shows RMB 727.1 billion against my own calculation of RMB 726.3 billion — a difference of RMB 0.8 billion, or 0.11%. Only two explanations are possible: either the share count used is not 628.29 million shares (perhaps including unregistered restricted shares or reflecting a difference in scope), or the snapshot price differs slightly from the closing price. The 0.11% gap has no bearing on any judgment, but it must be flagged explicitly in 03, because all subsequent P/E and PS figures derive from this number.

This report uses a unified basis: market cap = RMB 1,156.00 × 628.29 million shares = RMB 726.3 billion. Hence P/E (TTM) = 7,263/33.32 = 217.9x; PS (TTM) = 7,263/96.12 = 75.6x; PB = 7,263/135.55 = 53.6x.

## The P/E ratio is the metric most often misquoted, and it comes in four versions

① 368.97x — per the company's 30 June risk-warning announcement, on a denominator of "2025 annual report + 2026Q1 − 2025Q1" = RMB 2.717 billion and a share price of RMB 1,595.55; ② 217.9x — the basis used in this report, with the denominator switched to TTM RMB 3.332 billion (including 2026Q2) and a share price of RMB 1,156; ③ about 140x — derived from the 2026 incentive target of RMB 13.5 billion × a 38.5% net margin, implying net profit of roughly RMB 5.2 billion; ④ about 112x — on 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 6.5 billion.

All four numbers are correct; only the denominator and the timing differ. Mix them up and readers will conclude the share price fell 40% in a month while profit stayed flat — in fact the share price fell 27.6% and profit rose 22.7%, and the two moves together compressed the multiple from 369 to 218. That is why layer 03 has to be done: not to catch errors, but to give the later valuation a benchmark that does not contradict itself.
