Core thesis

The 2026 interim report tells two stories: a clean income statement and a troubling cash flow statement. Revenue of RMB 5.996 billion (+108.13%), net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 2.311 billion (+122.61%), non-recurring-adjusted profit of RMB 2.166 billion (+137.30%) and gross margin of 55.25% — all four are hard numbers, none propped up by non-recurring gains and losses (non-recurring-adjusted profit is 93.7% of net profit).

The problems sit entirely on the asset side: inventory of RMB 8.248 billion (up RMB 3.304 billion from the start of the year, 45.32% of total assets and 60.84% of net assets) and prepayments of RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37%), against which operating cash flow is only RMB 311 million (−65.83%), giving a net profit cash content of 0.135. This is not fraud; it is the cost of paying upfront to lock in capacity — but it means the earnings quality of this report deserves a discount.

Layer one: revenue mix—one business, two customer types, three speeds across three quarters

Revenue comes from a single line: cloud AI chips and accelerator cards. Customers fall into two groups — internet cloud providers (LLM training and inference) and carriers/local intelligent computing centres (government and enterprise compute). Of the RMB 5.996 billion booked in the first half, no single business line could have carried growth on its own; it all came down to accelerator card shipments.

The pace is shifting gears. Full-year 2025: RMB 6.497 billion (+453.21%); 2026Q1: RMB 2.885 billion (+159.56% YoY); 2026Q2: RMB 3.111 billion (+75.83% YoY, only +7.84% QoQ). YoY growth fell from triple digits to seventy percent, and QoQ growth from a doubling to single digits — the same business running at three different speeds in three quarters.

Management did not explain the shift. On the August 12 earnings call, investors repeatedly pressed on Q2 growth and the hardware delivery cadence; Chen Tianshi offered no further detail. Financially, the most likely explanation is delivery timing: prepayments of RMB 2.914 billion and inventory of RMB 8.248 billion are both waiting on wafers and HBM to arrive, and revenue can only be recognised once capacity is in place. This is an upstream bottleneck, not vanishing demand.

Second layer: gross margin of 55.25% is the most solid line in this report

Gross margin 55.25%, down just 0.68pp YoY; Q2 alone 56.10%, up 1.77pp QoQ. Against 55.15% for full-year 2025, that is barely any movement over eighteen months. In a year when domestic chips broadly competed for share by cutting prices, this number shows it did not trade price for volume.

Why the resilience? Scarce supply. Customers do not want the cheapest card; they want a card they can actually get and that runs their models. The Entity List keeps competitors out, and SMIC's N+2 capacity allocation is limited, so supply and demand give it pricing power. That is also why I scored the moat 7 out of 10 on the six gates — not technological leadership, but a scarce position.

One caveat: a 55% gross margin is high for semiconductor design and will revert to 45%–50% over time. Two triggers could set off that reversion — Huawei Ascend freeing up capacity and starting a price war, or cloud providers developing in-house chips (ByteDance and Alibaba both are) to replace general-purpose purchases. Whichever comes first, the gross margin loosens first.

Tier 3: Costs and profit — operating leverage released, but only for two years

Net margin 38.54%, up 2.50 points YoY. This is not gross margin expansion (gross margin actually fell) but a lower expense ratio: R&D spend was RMB 702 million, 11.72% of revenue, versus 18.81% a year earlier and triple digits in 2024. Revenue grew faster than R&D, and profit followed — classic operating leverage.

But leverage has a ceiling. R&D in absolute terms is still rising (RMB 702 million versus RMB 542 million, +29.5%); it is simply being outpaced by revenue growth. To sustain a 38% net margin, revenue must keep growing above 50%; once growth drops to 20%, the R&D expense ratio will climb again and net margin will fall below 30%. That is the most direct financial consequence of a growth downshift.

Two other line items merit attention: non-recurring gains and losses were only RMB 145 million (net profit 23.11 minus non-recurring-adjusted 21.66), 6.3% of the total, mainly government subsidies and wealth-management income — a healthy proportion; and the income tax burden is light (preferential rates for high-tech and integrated-circuit enterprises), a technical reason net margin runs high rather than a sign of operating improvement.

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — profit sits in inventory, not in the accounts

Operating cash flow was RMB 311 million, down 65.83% YoY from RMB 911 million in the prior-year period, with net profit cash content of 0.135. The gap is easy to trace: inventory rose from RMB 4.944 billion to RMB 8.248 billion (+RMB 3.304 billion), of which raw materials accounted for RMB 5.749 billion and consigned processing materials RMB 2.537 billion; prepayments reached RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37%). The company converted cash into wafers, HBM and work-in-progress chips.

The balance sheet has been reshaped accordingly: total assets of RMB 18.199 billion (+35.43%), net assets attributable to shareholders of RMB 13.555 billion (+14.53%), inventory at 45.32% of total assets and 60.84% of net assets, and a debt-to-asset ratio of about 25% (our calculation). This is an asset-heavy Fabless balance sheet—no fabs are built in name, but in practice capacity deposits have been paid to the extreme.

On the positive side, contract liabilities surged from RMB 610,000 at the start of the year to RMB 188 million, indicating customers are paying advances and the orders are real. On the negative side, RMB 188 million covers only 2.3% of the RMB 8.248 billion of inventory, so the stockpiling is almost entirely self-funded. If customer acceptance slips by a quarter, this RMB 8.2 billion will sit on the statements for three more months—and the write-down risk will extend by three months with it.

Three things to watch most closely

① Inventory turnover and write-down provisions: book value of RMB 8.248 billion against a gross balance of RMB 9.015 billion, with roughly RMB 767 million (8.5%) already provided. SiYuan 690 enters mass production in early 2026, so how long the 590 inventory can still sell will determine whether this provision is sufficient. A 10% provision would be RMB 820 million — equivalent to wiping out 30% of half-year profit.

② Q3 growth: the 2026 incentive target is RMB 13.5 billion; first-half revenue reached RMB 5.996 billion (44.4%), leaving RMB 7.504 billion for the second half — Q3 and Q4 must average above +12% quarter-on-quarter. Q2 grew only 7.84%, making Q3 the most critical quarter in three years.

③ Top five customer concentration: 88.66% in 2025; not disclosed in the 2026 interim report. Until this number falls, a single major customer's procurement cadence will always dictate a quarter's results; only when it falls does the company shift from "supplier to one customer" to "supplier to the industry."

One accounting note: the RMB 8.248 billion inventory figure is book value, against a gross balance of RMB 9.015 billion; the RMB 767 million difference is the provision already taken (8.5%). That ratio is low for a semiconductor design company — peers typically run 10%–15%, since chips carry higher obsolescence risk than ordinary electronic components. If the 2026 annual report raises the provision to 12%, the additional ~RMB 320 million would consume exactly one quarter of net profit.

Conclusion

Earnings verdict: real results, but quality warrants a discount. Revenue doubled, net profit reached RMB 2.311 billion, gross margin 55.25% and non-recurring items 93.7% — all four hold up to scrutiny. But operating cash flow of RMB 311 million is just 13.5% of net profit, and RMB 8.248 billion of inventory is crimping the conversion of profit into cash. This is a report where orders come first and cash arrives later. If Q3 revenue keeps pace and inventory starts turning, it is the textbook shape of a growth stock; if Q3 rolls over, the RMB 8.2 billion of inventory becomes the second blade in the valuation cut. Rating unchanged: avoid, wait for below RMB 800 or Q3 sequential growth of 15%.
