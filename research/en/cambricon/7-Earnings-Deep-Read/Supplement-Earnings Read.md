## Layer one: revenue structure—one business, two customer types, three speeds across three quarters

Revenue comes from one line only: cloud AI chips and accelerator cards. Customers split into two groups: internet cloud providers (LLM training and inference) and carriers/local intelligent computing centers (government and enterprise compute). Of the RMB 5.996 billion in the first half, no single business line could carry growth on its own — it all came down to accelerator card shipments.

The pace is shifting gears. Full-year 2025: RMB 6.497 billion (+453.21%); Q1 2026: RMB 2.885 billion (+159.56% YoY); Q2 2026: RMB 3.111 billion (+75.83% YoY, only +7.84% QoQ). YoY growth fell from triple digits to seventy percent; QoQ growth fell from a doubling to single digits — the same business, three different speeds in three quarters.

The company did not explain the shift. On the August 12 earnings call, investors repeatedly pressed on Q2 growth and the hardware delivery cadence; Chen Tianshi offered no further detail. From a financial perspective, the most likely explanation is delivery timing: prepayments of RMB 2.914 billion and inventory of RMB 8.248 billion are both waiting on wafers and HBM to arrive, and revenue can only be recognized once capacity is in place. This is a bottleneck upstream, not vanishing demand.

## Second layer: gross profit — 55.25% is the most solid line in this report

Gross margin 55.25%, down only 0.68 points year on year; Q2 alone 56.10%, up 1.77 points quarter on quarter. Against 55.15% for full-year 2025, barely any movement over eighteen months. In a year when domestic chips broadly competed for share by cutting prices, this number shows it did not trade price for volume.

Why could it hold? Scarce supply. Customers do not want the cheapest card; they want a card they can actually get and that runs their models. The Entity List keeps competitors out, and SMIC's N+2 capacity allocation is limited, so supply and demand give it pricing power. That is also why I scored the moat 7 out of 10 on the six gates — not technological leadership, but a scarce position.

One caveat is needed: a 55% gross margin is high for semiconductor design and will revert to 45%–50% over time. Two triggers could set off that reversion — Huawei Ascend freeing up capacity and starting a price war, or cloud providers developing in-house chips (ByteDance and Alibaba both are) to replace general-purpose purchases. Whichever comes first, the gross margin loosens first.

## Tier 3: Costs and profit — operating leverage released, but only for two years

Net margin 38.54%, up 2.50 points year on year. Unpacked, this is not gross margin improvement (gross margin actually fell) but a lower expense ratio: R&D spend was RMB 702 million, 11.72% of revenue, versus 18.81% a year earlier and triple digits in 2024. Revenue grew faster than R&D, and profit followed — classic operating leverage.

But leverage has a ceiling. R&D in absolute terms is still rising (RMB 702 million versus RMB 542 million, +29.5%); it is simply being outpaced by revenue growth. To sustain a 38% net margin, revenue must keep growing above 50%; once growth drops to 20%, the R&D expense ratio will climb again and net margin will fall below 30%. That is the most direct financial consequence of a growth downshift.

Two other line items merit attention: non-recurring gains and losses were only RMB 145 million (net profit 23.11 minus non-recurring-adjusted 21.66), 6.3% of the total, mainly government subsidies and wealth-management income — a healthy proportion; and the income tax burden is light (preferential rates for high-tech and integrated-circuit enterprises), a technical reason net margin runs high rather than a sign of operating improvement.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — profit sits in the warehouse, not in the accounts

Operating cash flow RMB 311 million, down 65.83% YoY (RMB 911 million in the prior-year period), with a net profit cash content of 0.135. The destination of the gap is clear: inventory rose from RMB 4.944 billion to RMB 8.248 billion (+RMB 3.304 billion), of which raw materials were RMB 5.749 billion and consigned processing materials RMB 2.537 billion; prepayments were RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37%). The company turned cash into wafers, HBM and work-in-progress chips.

The balance sheet has consequently been reshaped: total assets RMB 18.199 billion (+35.43%), net assets attributable to parent RMB 13.555 billion (+14.53%), inventory at 45.32% of total assets and 60.84% of net assets, debt-to-asset ratio about 25% (rough calculation). This is an asset-heavy Fabless balance sheet—nominally no fabs are built, but in practice capacity deposits have been paid to the extreme.

The good side is that contract liabilities surged from RMB 610,000 at the start of the year to RMB 188 million, indicating customers are paying advances and the orders are real. The bad side is that RMB 188 million covers only 2.3% of the RMB 8.248 billion of inventory, so the money for stockpiling is almost entirely self-funded. If customer acceptance is delayed by a quarter, this RMB 8.2 billion will sit on the statements for three more months, and the write-down risk will extend by three months as well.

## Three things to watch most closely

① Inventory turnover and write-down provisions: book value of RMB 8.248 billion against a gross balance of RMB 9.015 billion, with roughly RMB 767 million (8.5%) already provided. SiYuan 690 enters mass production in early 2026; how long the 590 inventory can still sell determines whether this provision is sufficient. A 10% provision would be RMB 820 million — equivalent to wiping out 30% of half-year profit.

② Q3 growth: the 2026 incentive target is RMB 13.5 billion; first-half revenue reached RMB 5.996 billion (44.4%), leaving RMB 7.504 billion for the second half — Q3 and Q4 must average above +12% quarter-on-quarter. Q2 grew only 7.84%, making Q3 the most critical quarter in three years.

③ Top five customer concentration: 88.66% in 2025; not disclosed in the 2026 interim report. Until this number falls, a single major customer's procurement cadence will always dictate a quarter's results; only when it falls does the company shift from "supplier to one customer" to "supplier to the industry."

One accounting note: the RMB 8.248 billion inventory figure is book value, against a gross balance of RMB 9.015 billion; the RMB 767 million difference is the provision already taken (8.5%). That ratio is low for a semiconductor design company — peers typically run 10%–15%, since chips carry higher obsolescence risk than ordinary electronic components. If the 2026 annual report raises the provision to 12%, the additional ~RMB 320 million would consume exactly one quarter of net profit.
