## One man decides life or death: Chen Tianshi's three judgments

The first time was in 2016: an academic team from the Institute of Computing Technology at the Chinese Academy of Sciences left to start a company building general-purpose intelligent processors rather than custom IP for Huawei. At the time the choice looked like a losing one—in 2017 the NPU in the Kirin 970 used Cambricon IP, the most stable revenue stream; in 2019 Huawei switched to its in-house Da Vinci architecture and Cambricon's revenue fell off a cliff. **But it survived as an independent chip company rather than a department of someone else's.**

The second time was 2022–2024: added to the Entity List, TSMC halting 7nm supply, consecutive losses—and the company did not cut R&D. In 2025 revenue reached RMB 6.497 billion, +453% year on year, with its first full-year profit (net profit RMB 2.059 billion), proving those three years of investment were not wasted.

The third time is 2026: it is directing the entire RMB 3.953 billion private placement into a "chip platform for large models" (RMB 2.054 billion) and a "software platform" (RMB 1.452 billion), and in July raised equipment spending by RMB 1.16 billion while cutting labor spending by RMB 910 million. **This is a bet that "hardware costs more than people, and capacity is more urgent than hiring"**—a classic cycle-top decision whose rightness will only be known two years from now.

## Capital allocation: RMB 6.5 billion in financing, RMB 630 million in dividends

String together six years of capital actions: IPO in July 2020, issuing 40.10 million shares at RMB 64.39, raising RMB 2.582 billion (net RMB 2.533 billion); a targeted placement in 2025 raising RMB 3.953 billion; the two together total roughly RMB 6.5 billion, all invested in R&D and industrialization. Only one dividend: the 2025 annual report paid RMB 15 per 10 shares (tax inclusive), roughly RMB 630 million based on the 417 million shares outstanding at the time, a payout ratio of about 30%; alongside a 10-for-4.9 bonus share issue.

The combination is sensible: **a growth-stage company should not pay large dividends, but a first dividend is a signal**—cash on hand is sufficient and management is willing to treat minority shareholders as shareholders. Zero buybacks are understandable: with the stock at RMB 1,156 and a P/E of 218x, buybacks would be the worst capital allocation.

The real capital-allocation test comes in 2027: if the RMB 13.5 billion revenue target falls short and inventory requires write-downs, will it need to raise capital again? **Issuing equity at a 218x P/E benefits the company and hurts existing shareholders**; issuing at a 40x P/E does the opposite. This is what holders must think through in advance.

## Incentive plan: writing the "RMB 100 billion over three years" target into performance reviews

Restricted stock plan announced on July 28, 2026: 5 million shares granted, 0.80% of total share capital, grant price RMB 750/share, a roughly 33.5% discount to the July 28 close. Performance targets in three tiers: 2026 revenue of no less than RMB 13.5 billion; cumulative 2026–2027 of no less than RMB 40.5 billion; cumulative 2026–2028 of no less than RMB 100 billion.

Break the three tiers apart: RMB 13.5 billion in 2026, RMB 27 billion in 2027, RMB 59.5 billion in 2028. **This is not "growth," this is a "triple jump."** Against reality: RMB 5.996 billion in H1 2026, 44.4% completed, leaving RMB 7.504 billion for H2; Q2 grew only 7.84% QoQ. Hitting RMB 27 billion in 2027 means doubling again off the 2026 base.

How to assess it? **Right direction, aggressive pace.** Equity incentives should set a bar that is a stretch but reachable; this bar looks more like it is meant for the market. The upside is that it locks in the core team for three years (semiconductor design has extremely high talent turnover); the downside is that if year one is already a struggle, the next two years will force "channel-stuffing revenue recognition"—inventory of RMB 8.248 billion is already a warning signal.

## Integrity and governance: no blemishes, but only one voice

In the public record before the report date, there are no penalties for financial fraud, fund misappropriation or irregular guarantees; inclusion on the entity list is a geopolitical issue, not a corporate governance one. Two things, however, show it cares about the market: on 30 June, with the share price at RMB 1,595.55, it voluntarily issued a risk warning announcement stating its trailing P/E of 368.97x against an industry average of 74.86x; and Chen Tianshi personally attended the 12 August results briefing.

The blemish also surfaced at that briefing: investors repeatedly pressed on why Q2 revenue grew only 7.84% quarter on quarter, on the hardware delivery cadence, and on the relationship between prepayments and inventory, and **Chen Tianshi offered no further explanation**. That is not a violation, but for a company with a market cap of RMB 700 billion, sidestepping key questions is a communication misstep.

The governance structure is a classic "one-man founder company": Chen Tianshi holds 28.35%, and concurrently serves as chairman, general manager and core technical personnel, with a term running to November 2028 after the November 2025 re-election. There are no AB shares, no external professional manager as a check, and independent directors could not plausibly vote against the technology roadmap. **Efficient in good times, no course correction in bad.**

## Succession: not a problem, but a risk

Chen Tianshi is 41, so succession is not an issue in the near term. The real issue is that **the company's technology roadmap depends heavily on one person's judgment**: everything from the instruction set (BANG) to product definition (Siyuan 590 for integrated training and inference, 690 on a dual-die chiplet) is decided by him. Among the middle generation, Liu Shaoli (39, R&D) and Ye Haoyin (38, finance and capital) can already hold their own, but no one has been publicly designated as the "number two."

The other thread is Chen Yunji — Chen Tianshi's elder brother, a researcher at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and co-founder of Cambricon. His academic reputation is one source of the company's technology brand, but he does not take part in day-to-day operations. **If anything happened to Chen Tianshi, the company would not collapse immediately, but its product cadence would certainly be disrupted** — fatal for a company that lives on "next-generation chips shipping on schedule."

Score for management: 6/10 — judgment 9, execution 8, capital allocation 7, governance checks and balances 4, communication 5. **The deductions are not about ability; they are about structure.**
