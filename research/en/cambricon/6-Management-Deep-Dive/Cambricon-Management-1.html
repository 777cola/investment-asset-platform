Core view

Chen Tianshi is the company's hardest asset and its biggest single-point risk. Aged 41, with a PhD from the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, he holds a 28.35% stake on an annual salary of RMB 1.4642 million — a low-salary, high-equity structure that broadly aligns his interests with those of minority shareholders. On capital allocation he got three things right: the 2020 IPO raised RMB 2.582 billion, carrying the company through five years of losses; the 2025 private placement of RMB 3.953 billion went entirely into large-model chips and software; and in 2025 came the first dividend of RMB 15 per 10 shares (about RMB 630 million) plus a 4.9-share bonus issue.

The ruthless part is the July 2026 incentive plan: 5 million shares at a grant price of RMB 750, with performance thresholds of RMB 13.5 billion for 2026, RMB 40.5 billion cumulative for 2026–2027 and RMB 100 billion cumulative over three years. "RMB 100 billion in three years" is not a slogan but a commitment written into the assessment — back-solving from that line implies RMB 27 billion in 2027 and RMB 59.5 billion in 2028. The one governance concern: a 28.35% stake plus the chairman-and-GM role, with no one on the board who can say no to him.

One man decides life or death: Chen Tianshi's three judgments

The first inflection came in 2016, when an academic team from the Institute of Computing Technology at the Chinese Academy of Sciences left to found a company building general-purpose intelligent processors rather than custom IP for Huawei. At the time it looked like a losing bet: in 2017 the NPU in the Kirin 970 ran on Cambricon IP, its most stable revenue stream, but in 2019 Huawei switched to its in-house Da Vinci architecture and Cambricon's revenue fell off a cliff. Yet it survived as an independent chip company rather than a department of someone else's.

The second came in 2022–2024, when the company was added to the Entity List, TSMC halted 7nm supply and losses piled up—and still it did not cut R&D. Revenue reached RMB 6.497 billion in 2025, +453% YoY, with its first full-year profit (net profit RMB 2.059 billion), proving those three years of investment were not wasted.

The third is 2026: the company is directing the entire RMB 3.953 billion private placement into a "chip platform for large models" (RMB 2.054 billion) and a "software platform" (RMB 1.452 billion), and in July raised equipment spending by RMB 1.16 billion while cutting labor spending by RMB 910 million. This is a bet that "hardware costs more than people, and capacity is more urgent than hiring"—a classic cycle-top decision whose rightness will only be known two years from now.

Capital allocation: RMB 6.5 billion in financing, RMB 630 million in dividends

Six years of capital actions in sequence: the July 2020 IPO issued 40.10 million shares at RMB 64.39, raising RMB 2.582 billion (net RMB 2.533 billion); a 2025 targeted placement raised RMB 3.953 billion; together roughly RMB 6.5 billion, all deployed into R&D and industrialisation. There has been one dividend: the 2025 annual report paid RMB 15 per 10 shares (tax inclusive), about RMB 630 million on the 417 million shares outstanding at the time, a payout ratio of roughly 30%, alongside a 10-for-4.9 bonus issue.

The combination is sensible: a growth-stage company should not pay large dividends, but a first dividend is a signal—cash on hand is sufficient and management is willing to treat minority shareholders as shareholders. Zero buybacks are understandable: with the stock at RMB 1,156 and a P/E of 218x, buybacks would be the worst possible capital allocation.

The real capital-allocation test comes in 2027: if the RMB 13.5 billion revenue target falls short and inventory requires write-downs, will it need to raise capital again? Issuing equity at a 218x P/E benefits the company and hurts existing shareholders; issuing at a 40x P/E does the opposite. Holders must think this through in advance.

Incentive plan: embedding the "RMB 100 billion over three years" target in performance reviews

Restricted stock plan announced July 28, 2026: 5 million shares granted, 0.80% of total share capital, grant price RMB 750/share, a roughly 33.5% discount to the July 28 close. Performance targets come in three tiers: 2026 revenue of no less than RMB 13.5 billion; cumulative 2026–2027 of no less than RMB 40.5 billion; cumulative 2026–2028 of no less than RMB 100 billion.

Decompose the three tiers: RMB 13.5 billion in 2026, RMB 27 billion in 2027, RMB 59.5 billion in 2028. This is not "growth," this is a "triple jump." Against reality: RMB 5.996 billion in H1 2026, 44.4% completed, leaving RMB 7.504 billion for H2; Q2 grew only 7.84% QoQ. Hitting RMB 27 billion in 2027 means doubling again off the 2026 base.

How to assess it? Right direction, aggressive pace. Equity incentives should set a bar that is a stretch but reachable; this one looks built for the market rather than for management. The upside is that it locks in the core team for three years (talent turnover in semiconductor design is extremely high); the downside is that if year one is already a struggle, the next two years will force "channel-stuffing revenue recognition"—inventory of RMB 8.248 billion is already a warning signal.

Integrity and governance: no blemishes, but only one voice

The public record before the report date shows no penalties for financial fraud, fund misappropriation or irregular guarantees; inclusion on the entity list is a geopolitical issue, not a corporate governance one. Two things, however, show it cares about the market: on 30 June, with the share price at RMB 1,595.55, it voluntarily issued a risk warning announcement flagging its trailing P/E of 368.97x against an industry average of 74.86x; and Chen Tianshi personally attended the 12 August results briefing.

The blemish also surfaced at that briefing: investors repeatedly pressed on why Q2 revenue grew only 7.84% quarter on quarter, on the hardware delivery cadence, and on the relationship between prepayments and inventory, and Chen Tianshi offered no further explanation. That is not a violation, but for a company with a market cap of RMB 700 billion, sidestepping key questions is a communication misstep.

The governance structure is a classic "one-man founder company": Chen Tianshi holds 28.35% and concurrently serves as chairman, general manager and core technical personnel, with a term running to November 2028 following his re-election in November 2025. There are no AB shares, no external professional manager as a check, and independent directors could not plausibly vote against the technology roadmap. Efficient in good times, no course correction in bad.

Succession: not a problem, but a risk

Chen Tianshi is 41, so succession is not a near-term issue. The real issue is that the company's technology roadmap depends heavily on one person's judgment: everything from the instruction set (BANG) to product definition (Siyuan 590 for integrated training and inference, 690 on a dual-die chiplet) is his call. Among the middle generation, Liu Shaoli (39, R&D) and Ye Haoyin (38, finance and capital) can already hold their own, but no one has been publicly designated as the "number two."

The other thread is Chen Yunji — Chen Tianshi's elder brother, a researcher at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and co-founder of Cambricon. His academic reputation underpins the company's technology brand, but he does not take part in day-to-day operations. If anything happened to Chen Tianshi, the company would not collapse immediately, but its product cadence would certainly be disrupted — fatal for a company that lives on "next-generation chips shipping on schedule."

Score for management: 6/10 — judgment 9, execution 8, capital allocation 7, governance checks and balances 4, communication 5. The deductions are not about ability; they are about structure.

Conclusion

Management passes, but not with distinction. Chen Tianshi has made three judgment calls, two of which were validated (the independent chip route, and not cutting R&D through loss-making periods). Capital allocation has not run amok: the entire RMB 6.5 billion financing went into R&D, and the first dividend was RMB 630 million. The incentive plan locked in the team for three years. Marks are deducted on governance: a 28.35% stake plus the chairman and general manager roles in one person, with no checks and balances; at the results meeting he dodged questions on Q2 growth, and communication is not candid enough. The "RMB 100 billion in three years" assessment line is both confidence and pressure: it turns RMB 27 billion in 2027 into a commitment that must be met — the first thing holders should watch.
