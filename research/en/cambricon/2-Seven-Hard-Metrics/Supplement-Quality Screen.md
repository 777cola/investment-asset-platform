## Three passed: gross margin, net margin, clean balance sheet

Gross margin of 55.25% puts it in the top tier of A-share semiconductor design companies. Full-year 2025 gross profit of RMB 3.583 billion and gross margin of 55.15%, then RMB 3.313 billion and 55.25% in the first half of 2026 — **the gross margin barely moved over 18 months, showing it is not winning share by cutting prices**. It clears this test cleanly.

Net margin of 38.54%, up 2.5 points year on year, driven by scale effects on the cost side: R&D spending of RMB 702 million, or 11.72% of revenue, versus 18.81% a year earlier and a triple-digit percentage in 2024. **Revenue grew faster than costs, and profit followed** — a textbook release of operating leverage that can run for another two years.

Interest coverage is a free pass: fabless, with almost no interest-bearing debt, and liabilities on the books mainly payables and contract liabilities. A clean balance sheet is what most distinguishes it from the many semiconductor companies expanding capacity on high leverage.

## The first point, however: cash content 0.13 — this is the hardest one

Operating cash flow RMB 311 million, net profit RMB 2.311 billion, a ratio of 0.13 against a threshold of 0.7. Where did the gap go? Two pockets: inventory of RMB 8.248 billion (raw materials RMB 5.749 billion, outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion) and prepayments of RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37% YoY). **This is not financial fraud; it is the cost of locking in capacity early** — in a year when advanced process nodes and HBM are both queued, no prepayment means no goods.

But a cost is still a cost. Failing this metric means its income statement looks far better than its cash flow statement, means weak dividend capacity (first-ever dividend in 2025 of RMB 15 per 10 shares, about RMB 630 million in total, just 30% of net profit), and means that any delay in customer acceptance turns inventory straight into impairment. **When reading Cambricon's financials, look at cash flow first, profit second.**

## The second caveat: ten-year ROE is negative, because it has had only two good years

This framework requires a 10-year average ROE above 8%. Cambricon listed in 2020 and lost money every year from 2020 to 2024 (a loss of RMB 452 million in 2024), earning a profit only in 2025, of RMB 2.059 billion. The ten-year average is bound to be negative. **This is not a management problem but an industry-rhythm problem**: R&D spending on AI chips must come three to four years ahead of revenue, which waits for the ecosystem to mature.

So I read this as "insufficient track record" rather than "poor quality". But an insufficient track record is itself a risk: **we have only two years of data to prove that the 38% net margin is sustainable**, and net margins in the semiconductor industry have never held steady for three years. Failing this test means the valuation cannot carry a "mature company" premium.

## The third caveat: share count up 50.6%, half from capitalisation of reserves, half from real dilution

The 2025 annual report included a 10-for-4.9 bonus share issue, raising share capital from about 417 million shares to about 622 million shares. This is a bookkeeping exercise and does not dilute value. In July 2026, a further 5 million restricted shares were granted (0.80% of total share capital, grant price RMB 750), which is genuine dilution, but the proportion is small. Combined, +50.6%, formally below the threshold.

What really matters is not this 0.8%, but **the next round of refinancing**. The RMB 3.953 billion private placement (2025 targeted issuance to specific investors) has already been spent on the "chip platform for large models" (RMB 2.054 billion) and the "software platform" (RMB 1.452 billion), and on 24 July 2026 a further RMB 1.16 billion of equipment investment was added. **If capacity and tape-outs still require heavy spending in 2027, the probability of equity financing is not low** — issuing shares at a 218x P/E is good for the company and bad for existing shareholders.

## But is it fatal? No, but it defines its positioning.

Of the four tests it fails, three (FCF, cash content, share count) point to the same thing: **it is still in its investment phase, and the money it earns has to be ploughed straight back in**. That is normal for a growth-stage company; in a value-investing framework it warrants a discount. So the conclusion is not "this is a bad company" but "this company can only be priced as a growth stock right now, not as a cash cow".

Pricing it as a growth stock means: valuation rests on expectations, and when expectations shift, the multiple collapses. A 368.97x P/E on 30 June and 218x on 17 August — the same fundamentals, and the valuation fell 40% in a month and a half. **That is the concrete cost of "not being able to price it as a cash cow".**
