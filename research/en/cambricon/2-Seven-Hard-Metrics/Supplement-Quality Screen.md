# Cambricon（688256）— Quality Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/08/17
- Price：1156.0 CNY
- Market cap：Approximately RMB 727.1 billion

## Notes

The framework fixes seven metric thresholds: 10-year ROE>8%, 5-year cumulative FCF>0, interest coverage>2x, long-term gross margin>15%, OCF/net profit>0.7, long-term net margin>5%, and 5-year share count dilution<20%. Company figures are drawn from the 2026 interim report (revenue RMB 5.996 billion, net profit RMB 2.311 billion, R&D RMB 702 million, inventory RMB 8.248 billion, prepayments RMB 2.914 billion, OCF RMB 311 million, net assets RMB 13.555 billion), the 2025 annual report (revenue RMB 6.497 billion, net profit RMB 2.059 billion, net margin 31.7%, calculated as net profit/revenue), and the 2026 incentive draft (5 million shares, 0.80%). Estimates: ROE uses period-end net assets rather than the average, at 17.4% for 2025 and 18.2% for 2026H1 (half-year, not annualised); the dividend of RMB 15 per 10 shares equates to roughly RMB 630 million on 417 million shares.

## Three passed: gross margin, net margin, clean balance sheet

A 55.25% gross margin puts it in the top tier of A-share semiconductor design companies. Gross profit was RMB 3.583 billion at a 55.15% margin for full-year 2025, then RMB 3.313 billion at 55.25% in 1H26 — the margin barely moved over 18 months, evidence it is not buying share with price cuts. It clears this test cleanly.

Net margin of 38.54%, up 2.5 points YoY, reflects scale effects on the cost side: R&D of RMB 702 million, or 11.72% of revenue, versus 18.81% a year earlier and a triple-digit percentage in 2024. Revenue outgrew costs and profit followed — a textbook release of operating leverage with another two years of runway.

Interest coverage is a free pass: fabless, with almost no interest-bearing debt and liabilities consisting mainly of payables and contract liabilities. That clean balance sheet is what most distinguishes it from the many semiconductor companies expanding capacity on high leverage.

## Cash content of 0.13 is the hardest issue

Operating cash flow of RMB 311 million against net profit of RMB 2.311 billion gives a ratio of 0.13, well short of the 0.7 threshold. Where did the gap go? Two places: inventory of RMB 8.248 billion (raw materials RMB 5.749 billion, outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion) and prepayments of RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37% YoY). This is not financial fraud; it is the cost of locking in capacity early — in a year when advanced process nodes and HBM are both queued, no prepayment means no goods.

But a cost is still a cost. Failing this metric means the income statement looks far better than the cash flow statement, dividend capacity is weak (a first-ever dividend in 2025 of RMB 15 per 10 shares, about RMB 630 million in total, just 30% of net profit), and any delay in customer acceptance turns inventory straight into impairment. When reading Cambricon's financials, look at cash flow first, profit second.

## The second caveat: ten-year ROE is negative, as the company has had only two good years

The framework calls for a 10-year average ROE above 8%. Cambricon listed in 2020 and lost money every year from 2020 to 2024 (RMB 452 million loss in 2024), turning a profit only in 2025, of RMB 2.059 billion. The ten-year average is bound to be negative. This is not a management problem but an industry-rhythm problem: R&D spending on AI chips must come three to four years ahead of revenue, which waits for the ecosystem to mature.

We therefore read this as "insufficient track record" rather than "poor quality". But an insufficient track record is itself a risk: we have only two years of data to prove that the 38% net margin is sustainable, and net margins in the semiconductor industry have never held steady for three years. Fail this test and the valuation cannot carry a "mature company" premium.

## Third caveat: share count up 50.6%, half from capitalisation of reserves, half from genuine dilution

The 2025 annual report included a 10-for-4.9 bonus share issue, lifting share capital from about 417 million shares to about 622 million shares. This is a bookkeeping exercise with no dilution of value. A further 5 million restricted shares were granted in July 2026 (0.80% of total share capital, grant price RMB 750) — genuine dilution, but small. Combined, +50.6%, formally below the threshold.

What really matters is not this 0.8%, but the next round of refinancing. The RMB 3.953 billion private placement (the 2025 targeted issuance to specific investors) has already been spent on the "chip platform for large models" (RMB 2.054 billion) and the "software platform" (RMB 1.452 billion), with a further RMB 1.16 billion of equipment investment added on 24 July 2026. If capacity and tape-outs still require heavy spending in 2027, the probability of equity financing is not low — issuing shares at a 218x P/E is good for the company and bad for existing shareholders.

## But is it fatal? No, but it defines its positioning.

Three of the four failed tests — FCF, cash content and share count — point to the same conclusion: the company is still in its investment phase, and the cash it generates has to be ploughed straight back in. That is normal for a growth-stage company and warrants a discount in a value-investing framework. The conclusion is therefore not that this is a bad company, but that it can currently be priced only as a growth stock, not as a cash cow.

Pricing it as a growth stock means valuation rests on expectations, and when expectations shift, the multiple collapses. A 368.97x P/E on 30 June and 218x on 17 August — the same fundamentals, yet the valuation fell 40% in a month and a half. That is the concrete cost of not being able to price it as a cash cow.
