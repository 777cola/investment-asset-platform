## Start with an attribution: what fell was the multiple, not the earnings

Break it down with share price = P/E × EPS. On June 30: share price RMB 1,595.55, TTM net profit RMB 2.717 billion (2025 annual report 2.059 + 2026Q1 1.013 − 2025Q1 0.355), P/E 368.97x. On August 17: share price RMB 1,156.00, TTM net profit RMB 3.332 billion (including 2026Q2), P/E 217.9x.

P/E change −40.9%, EPS (TTM) change +22.6%, multiplied together −27.6% — **the share price decline exactly equals what remains after "multiple compression" eats "earnings growth"**. This decomposition is more reliable than any narrative: everything the company did over three months added points; every multiple the market assigned subtracted them.

So the question "why did it fall" has a precise answer: **not because results were poor, but because the starting point was too expensive.** The 369x P/E on June 30 required "doubling every quarter" to sustain; Q2 delivered 7.84% quarter-on-quarter, and the story broke.

## Three informative events

**The first is the June 30 risk warning announcement.** On the very evening its share price hit an all-time high and its market cap broke the trillion mark, the company stated in a filing: "rolling P/E 368.97x, P/B 77.88x, significantly above the industry level (74.86x/8.06x), exposing it to elevated valuation risk." This is rare candor for an A-share company — **the company knows better than the market just how expensive its price is.** **The second is the August 7 interim report.** Revenue of RMB 5.996 billion and net profit of RMB 2.311 billion are hard numbers; but on the same day the market had already begun calculating Q2 sequential growth of 7.84%, inventory of RMB 8.248 billion and cash flow of RMB 311 million. **Earnings delivery and a growth downshift arriving at once is the most awkward combination.** **The third is the August 12 earnings briefing.** Investors repeatedly pressed on Q2 growth, the hardware delivery cadence, and the relationship between prepayments and inventory; Chen Tianshi "gave no further explanation." **Silence is not an answer, but in capital markets silence gets read as "can't say."**

## Which are noise: four things to ignore

① **The 6/17 note on ByteDance's 120,000–160,000 580 chips**: the source was an unsigned meeting note; the company denied "large-volume supply to internet companies" that same day, and ByteDance has never publicly acknowledged a procurement relationship. Such numbers cannot be verified; their only function is to ignite the stock. **But it reveals something real: the market has anchored Cambricon's valuation to "ByteDance's procurement volume," and that itself is the risk.**

② **The 7/24 adjustment to the fundraising project structure**: equipment +RMB 1.16 billion, labor −RMB 910 million — a response to "more expensive hardware, more urgent capacity," and it does not change the revenue model. ③ **The 7/28 incentive plan**: 5 million shares, 0.80%, minimal dilution; the real information is in the performance targets (RMB 13.5 billion / 40.5 billion / 100 billion), not the grant price. ④ **The +5.76% on 8/17**: an oversold rebound with no new information.

Strip out the noise, and only two numbers from the past three months are worth remembering: **218x (the current valuation) and 7.84% (the current quarter-on-quarter growth rate)**. The first is the price; the second is the direction.

## Three things the event reveals

First, **the company cares about its share price, but cares more about not taking the blame.** The proactive risk warning on 6/30 and the unexplained Q2 on 8/12, taken together: management knows valuations are high and knows growth has a problem, but is unwilling to give guidance in public. For investors this means — **don't expect the company to make the judgment for you; you have to do the math yourself.**

Second, **event-driven volatility far exceeds fundamental change.** Over three months the fundamentals went from "doubling growth" to "doubling but shifting gears," a limited magnitude of change; the share price swung 27.6% and the P/E swung 40.9%. **At a 218x P/E, the leverage on any single news item is magnified tenfold.**

Third, **"RMB 1 trillion market cap" is a psychological threshold.** On 6/30, the day it broke RMB 1 trillion, turnover was RMB 25.487 billion, and the following two days wiped out RMB 140 billion. Turnover of that magnitude shows short-term money makes up a very high share of the holder base — **for a company with an unstable shareholder base, valuation mean-reversion happens earlier than fundamental mean-reversion.**
