⑤ Industry and competition: the runner-up in the quota system — Cambricon

Eight Long-Form Deep Dives · Part Five

Core view

China's AI chip industry is not a free market but a quota system: controls compress supply while a handful of cloud providers monopolise demand. Only by understanding this structure can one see where Cambricon's 55% gross margin comes from — and how it will disappear.

Supply: two lines, one semi

The Entity List and TSMC's supply cutoff have concentrated domestic advanced-process capacity at SMIC N+2. Quota holders are Huawei Ascend (prioritised for internal use), Cambricon (second), and Hygon (dual CPU + DCU lines), alongside players still ramping such as Kunlunxin, MetaX and Moore Threads. "One and a half" means Ascend holds one full line and Cambricon half a line, constrained by capacity.

The economics of a quota system are simple: quota holders earn excess profit, everyone else cannot produce at all. Cambricon's 55% gross margin is therefore essentially "quota rent"—not because its technology is 55% better than others', but because others cannot get the chips.

Demand: three buyers, all developing in-house

Four cloud vendors—ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu—absorb most of the compute, with the three major telecom operators and local intelligent-compute projects taking another share and industry customers remaining fragmented. The problem: both buyer groups are developing chips in-house or are deeply tied to a single supplier—ByteDance has its own team, Alibaba has T-Head, and the operators lean toward Ascend.

Cambricon's customer relationships are therefore "phase-specific": customers buy from it today only because in-house development is not yet mature and Ascend capacity is insufficient. Once either premise changes, purchase volumes will fall quickly. That is the second layer of risk beyond its 88.66% customer concentration.

Competition: Ascend is a system play, Hygon is steady, Moore Threads is an option
Huawei Ascend is less a competitor than a system — chips, CANN, cloud, complete machines and policy resources combined. What customers buy is certainty. Hygon Information is "China's AMD": 2026H1 revenue of RMB 9.099 billion, net profit of RMB 1.798 billion, gross margin of 55.21% and R&D at 29.15% of revenue — steady but slow. Moore Threads is an option: 2026H1 revenue of RMB 1.736 billion, up 147.42% YoY, net profit still −RMB 12 million, market cap RMB 268.8 billion.

Cambricon sits in the middle: more mature than Moore Threads (profitable, 3x the scale), purer than Hygon (100% AI chips) and weaker than Ascend (lagging across the board on capacity, ecosystem and resources). This position is comfortable, but the ceiling is not its to set.

Cycle: demand mid-to-late stage, supply early-to-mid stage

Demand: the four major North American cloud providers have sharply raised 2026 capex, domestic intelligent-computing tenders continue, and memory has entered a super cycle (DRAM up 60%–70%, NAND up over 100%). This is the industry backdrop behind Cambricon's doubling in 2026.

Supply: SMIC is expanding capacity, CXMT is ramping HBM, and new entrants are listing and raising capital to add capacity. The industry is shifting from "scarcity" to "competition"—a process typically accompanied by gross margin compression and de-rating. For investors, the biggest danger is not falling demand but "volumes still rising while prices start to loosen"—at that point the financials still look good, but the valuation has already collapsed first.

How the quotas are allocated: queue order for SMIC's 7nm

SMIC's advanced-process capacity (N+2, 7nm equivalent) is limited. The 2026 allocation roughly follows this pecking order: Huawei's Ascend takes the bulk, Cambricon gets a single-digit to 10% share, and the remaining dozen or so players split what is left. That order reflects not commercial negotiation but a combination of strategic priority, procurement scale and long-term cooperation.

For Cambricon the implications are twofold. First, its capacity ceiling is set by others: whatever its order book, it can ship only the volume its quota allows. Second, the quota can be squeezed—if the Ascend 950 series ramps while SMIC's capacity stays flat, Cambricon's share will be compressed.

This also explains the RMB 2.914 billion prepayment and RMB 8.248 billion inventory build—under a quota system, cash is the queueing chip. Whoever prepays more moves up in the next allocation round. That is both its tactic and its financial burden.

When does the cycle peak: three leading indicators

① Cloud providers' capex guidance: any cut to 2027 guidance from North America's four largest cloud providers would trigger a de-rating across global AI chip valuations first. ② HBM prices: the end of a price-hike cycle typically leads compute oversupply; institutions expect the shortage to persist into 2027, so any early easing is a warning sign.

③ Localization rate targets: if policy shifts from "must be domestic" to "cost-effectiveness first," quota rents shrink immediately. Of the three indicators, the first is fastest (quarterly earnings), the second most accurate (a direct reflection of supply and demand), and the third slowest but most lethal.

Conclusion

Industry view: Right sector, right position, wrong timing. The quota system delivered two years of excess profit, but also a ceiling set by others. The industry is shifting from scarcity to competition, yet a 218x P/E still prices in scarcity.
