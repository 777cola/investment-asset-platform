Core view

Cambricon's management boils down to one keyword: Chen Tianshi. Age 41, holding 28.35%, annual salary RMB 1.4642 million, three key judgments, two already validated.

Three judgments: independence, conviction, doubling down
In 2016 it chose to be an independent chip company rather than an IP supplier to Huawei. The most reliable revenue at the time was NPU licensing for the Kirin 970; when Huawei switched to its in-house Da Vinci architecture in 2019, Cambricon's revenue fell off a cliff — but precisely because it stayed independent, it is today a "second supplier" rather than a department inside someone else's system. Sanctioned in 2022–2024, it did not cut R&D through three consecutive years of losses. In 2025 revenue reached RMB 6.497 billion (+453%) and net profit RMB 2.059 billion, its first full-year turnaround, proving the investment was not wasted. In 2026 it is putting the entire RMB 3.953 billion private placement into large-model chips and software, and in July added another RMB 1.16 billion in equipment spending. This is doubling down at the top of the cycle; whether it is right will only be known two years from now.
Incentive: write the "RMB 100 billion over three years" target into contracts

Incentive draft dated July 28, 2026: 5 million shares (0.80% of total share capital), grant price RMB 750/share, targets RMB 13.5 billion for 2026, RMB 40.5 billion cumulative for 2026–2027, and RMB 100 billion cumulative over three years. Working backwards: RMB 13.5 billion in 2026, RMB 27 billion in 2027, RMB 59.5 billion in 2028.

This trajectory is aggressive and specific. The upside is that it locks in the core team for three years (semiconductor design talent turnover is extremely high); the downside is that if the first year proves difficult, the following two years will force "channel-stuffing revenue recognition" — the RMB 8.248 billion of inventory is the first signal to watch.

Governance: a board without checks and balances

Chen Tianshi holds 28.35%, and concurrently serves as chairman, general manager and core technical personnel, with his term running to November 2028 after the November 2025 board re-election. There are no AB shares and no external professional managers; independent directors cannot plausibly vote against the technology roadmap.This structure is highly efficient in good times and has no course-correction mechanism in bad times.

Communication also lost points: at the August 12 earnings briefing, investors pressed on the 7.84% Q2 quarter-on-quarter figure, the delivery cadence, and the relationship between inventory and prepayments, and Chen Tianshi "gave no further explanation."For a company with a market cap of RMB 70 billion, dodging key questions reads to the market as "can't say."

The arithmetic of low salary, high equity: is he in the same boat as you?

Chen Tianshi's annual salary is RMB 1.4642 million, and he holds 119.53 million shares (28.35%), worth about RMB 138.2 billion at RMB 1,156.His salary is only one ten-thousandth of his wealth.This structure determines that what he cares about is not his pay, but the share price and the company's long-term value.

From a shareholder's perspective this is a good thing—interests are aligned. But note one detail:his wealth is already highly concentrated in a single stock, so in theory he has an incentive to support the share price (for example through incentive targets or market-cap management), and also an incentive to reduce his stake at the right time. Public information as of the report date shows no record of stake reduction, which counts in his favor.

Another number: the 2026 incentive grant price is RMB 750, only 65% of the current price. If the share price falls below RMB 750, the incentive becomes "out of the money" and the core team's motivation will be diluted.This is another reason for the RMB 800 observation line—it is not just a valuation line, but also a lifeline for the incentive plan.

If he makes one mistake, what is it most likely to be?

The most likely mistake is not the technology roadmap but overbetting on capacity. In July 2026, adding RMB 1.16 billion in equipment and cutting labor costs by RMB 910 million was essentially "betting that capacity is more expensive than people." If demand falls short of expectations in 2027, this RMB 1.16 billion of equipment and RMB 8.2 billion of inventory will together turn into impairments.

The second most likely mistake is underinvestment in software. R&D was RMB 702 million, or 11.72% of revenue, and how much of that went to the software platform is not disclosed. Against Hygon's R&D at 29.15% of revenue, Cambricon's investment intensity is only about 40% of that. Ecosystem is the endgame moat; underinvestment will show up in three years.

Conclusion

Management verdict: judgment 9, execution 8, capital allocation 7, governance checks and balances 4, communication 5, overall 6/10. The right people, thin structure; the "RMB 100 billion in three years" target is both confidence and pressure.
