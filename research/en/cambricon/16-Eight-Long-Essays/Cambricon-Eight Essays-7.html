Core view

The risk is not "will it fall" but "which variables, once they move, break the thesis." Cambricon has six, but they are highly correlated—essentially four faces of the same risk.

Foundry and HBM: two physical gates

SMIC's N+2 allocation determines how much revenue it can generate in a year; the HBM allocation determines whether Siyuan 690 can ramp volume.Neither is controlled by the company, nor decided by money — the RMB 2.914 billion prepayment only buys a place in the queue, not a jump ahead of it.

If SMIC capacity is squeezed by Ascend, or HBM prices rise 30%, the consequences are twofold: a quarter of lost revenue and several points off gross margin.And that hit cannot be hedged by "selling more cards."

Customers and inventory: one from downstream, one from upstream

Customer concentration is 88.66%, with a single large customer once accounting for 96.48%. One customer's purchasing cadence is a quarter's financial statements. Q2 rose 7.84% QoQ, likely because one large customer deferred acceptance.

Inventory of RMB 8.248 billion, with RMB 767 million (8.5%) already provisioned. Peers typically provision 10%–15%, because chips carry higher obsolescence risk than ordinary components. Siyuan 690 enters mass production in early 2026; how long the 590 inventory can still sell determines whether the provision is sufficient. A 10% provision would be RMB 820 million, equivalent to wiping out 30% of half-year profit.

Valuation and governance: two amplifiers

A 218x P/E means any single risk materializing will amplify the drawdown. From June 30 to August 17, fundamentals did not change, yet the stock fell 27.6% and the P/E compressed from 369x to 218x—this is the amplifier effect of valuation.

Governance risk is a single point: Chen Tianshi holds three roles at once and owns 28.35%, with no checks and balances on the board. It is not that "he will do bad things," but that "when he misjudges, no one can stop him." For a company that survives by betting on a technology roadmap, this is a substantive risk.

Why this is the same risk

Foundry, HBM, customers, inventory — the four risks share one common thread: all point to a mismatch between capacity and orders. Capacity falls short → revenue recognition delayed → inventory builds up → impairment provisions → profit declines → valuation de-rates. A problem in any one link worsens the other four.

So "diversifying risk" does not work here. Either the whole chain runs smoothly (bull case RMB 1,450) or the whole chain seizes up (bear case RMB 400). This is a binary structure, not a continuous distribution.

Which risks are priced in, and which are not

At 218x P/E, the risks already priced in are: growth will slow (otherwise the multiple would not have compressed from 369x to 218x), competition will intensify (Ascend's existence is public information), and the valuation will be volatile (as demonstrated between 6/30 and 8/17).

The risks not priced in are: the specific size of inventory write-downs (whether an 8.5% provision is enough), whether customer concentration deteriorates (not disclosed in the 2026 interim report), the possibility of allocation being squeezed out (unquantifiable), and dilution from refinancing (if more capital is raised in 2027).

What these four have in common is that they are "unquantifiable but hugely consequential." The market discounts quantifiable risks and panics over unquantifiable ones. So the real selloff will not come from "growth slowing from 108% to 50%" but from "an announcement one day of a RMB 2 billion inventory write-down" — a day with no warning.

Tail risk: how bad could it get

Run the worst case: revenue falls to RMB 8 billion (customers shift orders away + quotas squeezed), gross margin 45%, net margin 20% → net profit RMB 1.6 billion; the market assigns 40x (cyclical-stock valuation) → market cap RMB 64 billion → share price RMB 102. That is −91%.

This scenario is low-probability (it requires customers, quotas and valuation to collapse at the same time), but it makes one thing clear: for a stock at 218x P/E, there is no such thing as "down by half means it has bottomed." Position discipline therefore matters more than stock selection — which is why m14 caps it at 5%.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: Six variables, four with high weight, fundamentally the same origin. This is not a "probability problem" but a "structural problem"—so it cannot be solved by diversification, only by price compensation. And the current price offers no compensation.
