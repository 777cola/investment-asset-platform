Core view

The question is not whether the stock falls, but which variables, once they move, break the thesis. Cambricon carries six such risks, though they are highly correlated—essentially four faces of the same risk.

Foundry and HBM: two physical gates

SMIC's N+2 allocation sets the ceiling on annual revenue, while HBM allocation determines whether Siyuan 690 can ramp volume.Neither is under the company's control, and neither can be bought — the RMB 2.914 billion prepayment secures a place in the queue, not priority over it.

If Ascend squeezes SMIC capacity, or HBM prices rise 30%, the hit is twofold: a quarter of revenue lost and several points off gross margin.Nor can that hit be hedged by "selling more cards."

Customers and inventory: one downstream, one upstream

Customer concentration is 88.66%, and one large customer has accounted for as much as 96.48%. A single customer's purchasing cadence can make or break a quarter's financials. Q2 rose 7.84% QoQ, likely because one large customer deferred acceptance.

Inventory stands at RMB 8.248 billion, of which RMB 767 million (8.5%) is already provisioned. Peers typically provision 10%–15%, as chips carry higher obsolescence risk than ordinary components. The Siyuan 690 enters mass production in early 2026, so how long the 590 inventory can still sell will determine whether the provision is sufficient. A 10% provision would be RMB 820 million, equivalent to wiping out 30% of half-year profit.

Valuation and governance: two amplifiers

At 218x P/E, any single risk materialising will amplify the drawdown. Fundamentals did not change between June 30 and August 17, yet the stock fell 27.6% and the P/E compressed from 369x to 218x—this is the amplifier effect of valuation.

Governance risk comes down to one point: Chen Tianshi holds three roles simultaneously and owns 28.35%, leaving the board with no checks and balances. The issue is not that "he will do bad things," but that "when he misjudges, no one can stop him." For a company whose survival rests on betting on a technology roadmap, this is a substantive risk.

Why this is the same risk

Foundry, HBM, customers and inventory are four risks that share one common thread: all point to a mismatch between capacity and orders. Capacity falls short → revenue recognition is delayed → inventory builds up → impairment provisions → profit declines → valuation de-rates. A problem in any one link worsens the other four.

So "diversifying risk" does not work here. Either the whole chain runs smoothly (bull case RMB 1,450) or the whole chain seizes up (bear case RMB 400). This is a binary structure, not a continuous distribution.

Which risks are priced in, and which are not

At 218x P/E, the market already discounts three risks: growth will slow (otherwise the multiple would not have compressed from 369x to 218x), competition will intensify (Ascend's existence is public information), and the valuation will be volatile (as demonstrated between 6/30 and 8/17).

What it does not discount: the specific size of inventory write-downs (whether an 8.5% provision is enough), whether customer concentration deteriorates (not disclosed in the 2026 interim report), the possibility of allocation being squeezed out (unquantifiable), and dilution from refinancing (if more capital is raised in 2027).

All four share one trait: unquantifiable but hugely consequential. The market discounts quantifiable risks and panics over unquantifiable ones. The real selloff, then, will not come from "growth slowing from 108% to 50%" but from "an announcement one day of a RMB 2 billion inventory write-down" — a day with no warning.

Tail risk: how bad could it get

Bear case: revenue falls to RMB 8 billion as customers shift orders away and quotas are squeezed, gross margin 45%, net margin 20% → net profit RMB 1.6 billion; at 40x (cyclical valuation) → market cap RMB 64 billion → share price RMB 102. That implies −91%.

This scenario is low-probability, requiring customers, quotas and valuation to collapse simultaneously, but it makes one thing clear: for a stock on 218x P/E, there is no such thing as "down by half means it has bottomed." Position discipline therefore matters more than stock selection — which is why m14 caps it at 5%.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: Six variables, four of them high-weight, all trace back to a single root cause. This is not a probability problem but a structural one: diversification cannot solve it, only price compensation can. The current price offers none.
