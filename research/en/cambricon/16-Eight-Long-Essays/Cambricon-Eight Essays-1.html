Core view

The biggest obstacle to researching Cambricon is not a lack of information, but three overly tidy narratives. Dismantle them, and what remains is the fact you can bet on.

Misconception 1: Domestic substitution = easy wins

"The more the US blockades, the more Cambricon benefits" has been the most popular line of the past two years. It is half true: after the December 2022 Entity List and TSMC's halt of 7nm supply, domestic training-chip supply indeed came down to just two lines, Huawei Ascend and Cambricon, and customers had to cultivate a second supplier. But "benefiting" is not the same as "winning without lifting a finger."

Winning without lifting a finger means making money without doing the work. Cambricon's first-half operating cash flow was only RMB 311 million against net profit of RMB 2.311 billion, with prepayments of RMB 2.914 billion and inventory of RMB 8.248 billion — it bought capacity back with cash; it did not sit there waiting for orders. Controls gave it a ticket, not a profit; the profit it sold card by card on its own.

More counterintuitive still: if China-US tensions ease and controls loosen, that is negative for the stock. Once Nvidia's specially tailored cards flood back in, customers have no reason to pay a 55% gross margin for a "second supplier." So the "blockade beneficiary" thesis is essentially a bet that geopolitics will not improve — a bet that does not belong on a value investor's checklist.

Misconception 2: Doubling revenue = a good company

H1 2026 revenue RMB 5.996 billion, +108.13% YoY; net profit RMB 2.311 billion, +122.61% YoY. The numbers look good, but the quarterly breakdown tells a different story: Q1 revenue RMB 2.885 billion (+159.56%), Q2 RMB 3.111 billion (+75.83%, only 7.84% QoQ).

Doubling revenue has two possible explanations: an explosion in demand, or a very low base. Full-year 2025 revenue was RMB 6.497 billion, +453% YoY, a jump off a low base of RMB 1.17 billion; the 2026 doubling comes on top of a RMB 6.5 billion base, so it carries more substance, but the Q2 QoQ figure already shows the "explosive phase" is over.

The standard for a good company is not "fast growth" but "sustainable growth that converts into cash."Cambricon has yet to prove the first; it fails the second (cash content 0.135). So it is "a company that is getting better," not yet "a good company."
Misconception 3: Scarcity means you can charge any price

The A-share market will indeed pay for "the only one." Cambricon is the first STAR Market stock to reach a market cap of RMB 1 trillion; on June 30 it traded intraday at RMB 1,620 and closed at RMB 1,595.55, for a market cap of RMB 100.25 billion. What the market is pricing is not valuation but a "scarcity premium."

But the premium has a ceiling. The current price of RMB 1,156 corresponds to 218x TTM P/E, 75.6x P/S and 53.6x P/B; working backwards, an 80x multiple requires net profit of RMB 9.08 billion and revenue of RMB 23.6 billion, while the company's 2026 assessment target is RMB 13.5 billion. In other words, the current price has already fully discounted the 2027 target.

Scarcity deserves respect, but what it protects is revenue, not valuation. From June 30 to August 17, scarcity did not change at all, yet the stock fell 27.6% and the P/E dropped from 369x to 218x. This proves that "scarcity" and "price" are two different things.

The fourth misconception: policy will always backstop the market

Many treat the "localization-rate requirement + local intelligent-computing tenders + the Big Fund" as a permanent safety net. Policy does provide support: operator and local intelligent-computing procurement explicitly mandates a share for domestic chips, and semiconductor industry funds have kept investing since 2021. But policy supports the "industry", not "any one company".

The competitive landscape is the evidence: among domestic AI chips that enjoy the same policy support, Hygon posted 2026H1 revenue of RMB 9.099 billion and net profit of RMB 1.798 billion; Moore Threads posted revenue of RMB 1.736 billion, up 147.42% YoY. Policy provides market space, not an exclusive license for Cambricon. Ascend took the largest share; Cambricon got the "second supplier" slot.

More concretely: local intelligent-computing center procurement in 2026 already shows a tilt toward "delivery and operations", no longer judging only on "is it domestic". When the procurement standard shifts from "domestic first" to "cost-performance first", the 55% gross margin comes under pressure. This is the first crack in the retreat of policy dividends.

So what is the right question to ask?

Strip away the three illusions, and the question becomes three answerable ones: can it reach RMB 27 billion in revenue by 2027 (the incentive threshold), will net margin still be 37% when it does, and what multiple will the market be willing to pay once it gets there? The first depends on capacity, the second on competition, the third on sentiment.

Of these three, the first can be half-verified in October, the second must wait until 2027, and the third can never be verified. So any decision to "buy now" must rest on the third question—which is exactly what this asset is: a sentiment-priced asset.

Finally: what does it actually change

Zoom out a little. Before 2016, China's AI chip sector was essentially a blank; in 2017 Cambricon's IP went into the Kirin 970; in 2020 it became the "first AI chip stock"; in 2022 it was sanctioned; in 2025 it turned profitable; in the first half of 2026 it earned RMB 2.311 billion. In ten years, it turned the question "can China make AI chips" into "can China make AI chips cheaply".

The significance of that shift matters far more than the share price. But investing is not about picking "the most meaningful company"; it is about judging "what this money will be worth in ten years". Its significance is already fully priced in at a market cap of RMB 726.3 billion; the remaining upside depends on cash flow, not sentiment.

Conclusion

Three lines on the cognitive reset: regulation grants the ticket, not the profit; fast growth does not make a good company; scarcity protects revenue, not valuation. Remember these three lines, and the seven pieces that follow will make sense.
