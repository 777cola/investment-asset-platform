④ Financial reality: profit sits in the warehouse — Cambricon

Eight in-depth reports · Part Four

Core view

One line sums up this interim report: the income statement booked RMB 2.3 billion, the cash flow statement collected just RMB 300 million, and the balance sheet shows the other RMB 2 billion sitting in inventory.

Income statement: clean growth

Revenue RMB 5.996 billion (+108.13%), gross profit RMB 3.313 billion (+105.62%), net profit RMB 2.311 billion (+122.61%), non-recurring net profit RMB 2.166 billion (+137.30%). Non-recurring profit is 93.7% of net profit, with non-recurring gains and losses of only RMB 145 million — the profit came from selling cards, not from asset sales or subsidies.

Net profit outgrew revenue on a lower expense ratio: R&D spending was RMB 702 million, 11.72% of revenue, versus 18.81% a year earlier. This is operating leverage, and real leverage — absolute R&D spending still rose (+29.5%), just outpaced by revenue growth.

Cash flow statement: RMB 311 million, down 65.83% YoY

Net profit RMB 2.311 billion, operating cash flow RMB 311 million, cash content of net profit 0.135 against Buffett's 0.7 threshold. The gap sits in inventories, up RMB 3.304 billion, and prepayments, up about RMB 2.17 billion. One sum became wafers in the warehouse, the other prepayments to suppliers.

Financing cash flow −RMB 657 million, reflecting the RMB 15 per 10 shares dividend paid out of the 2025 annual report. A company generating RMB 300 million of operating cash flow while paying RMB 630 million in dividends is effectively handing shareholders its balance-sheet cash — a fine gesture, but unsustainable.

Balance sheet: a fabless company's "asset-heavy shift"

Total assets RMB 18.199 billion (+35.43%), net assets attributable to shareholders RMB 13.555 billion (+14.53%), debt-to-asset ratio about 25.5% (rough calculation), with almost no interest-bearing debt. No debt risk — this is where it beats many semiconductor companies.

Asset quality, however, is deteriorating: inventory RMB 8.248 billion (45.32% of total assets, 60.84% of net assets), of which raw materials RMB 5.749 billion and outsourced processing RMB 2.537 billion; impairment provisions of RMB 767 million (8.5%). These are not finished goods but "materials not yet made into cards." A one-quarter delay in wafer arrivals leaves them sitting on the balance sheet three months longer, and each product iteration triggers another provision.

Three line items to watch
Inventory days (RMB 8.2 billion, equal to 8 months of revenue; anything above 10 months signals unsold stock); quarterly gross margin (55.25% is the moat’s thermometer; below 50% means the company has begun cutting prices to move volume); operating cash flow/net profit (0.135 is the ugliest number; only back at 0.5 does it count as “actually making money”).

All three will be readable in the Q3 report in late October. Until then, any judgment about this company is a guess.

Three questions on accounting quality: is it window-dressed?

Question one: Has R&D been capitalized? No. The full RMB 702 million of R&D spending ran through the income statement, equal to 11.72% of revenue — capitalization did not inflate profit; it depressed it. That is a positive: many chip companies turn losses into profits through capitalization.

Question two: How much comes from government subsidies? Non-recurring gains and losses were RMB 145 million (net profit 23.11 less non-GAAP 21.66), or 6.3% of net profit, mainly government subsidies and wealth-management income. The ratio is healthy — above 20% would warrant scrutiny for "subsidies propping up profit."

Question three: Any anomalies in receivables or inventory? Inventory is the anomaly: RMB 8.248 billion, 45.32% of total assets, against an 8.5% write-down provision (peers: 10%–15%). No anomaly in accounts receivable has been publicly disclosed as of the report date. Conclusion: a clean income statement, with hidden concerns on the asset side — "real profit plus uncertain assets."

When does the cash come back: three possibilities

First, capacity stops expanding: capex stabilises after 2027, inventory turnover accelerates and OCF/net profit returns to 0.6–0.8. This is the best case, and it requires the industry to reach a steady state. Second, prepayments convert to inventory and then to revenue: from 2026H2 to 2027H1 the RMB 2.914 billion of prepayments gradually arrives and is recognised as revenue, with cash flow improving on a lag.

Third, continued deterioration: if the company still has to stock up in 2027 to hit a revenue target of RMB 59.5 billion, inventory could exceed RMB 12 billion and cash flow stays permanently negative. The test is simple: watch whether inventory growth keeps outpacing revenue growth. In 2026H1 inventory rose 66.83% and revenue rose 108.13% — still healthy for now, but a reversal would be a warning sign.

A comparison: why Hygon's cash flow is better

Both are domestic computing-power chipmakers. Hygon posted 2026H1 revenue of RMB 9.099 billion, net profit of RMB 1.798 billion and a net margin of 22.69%, below Cambricon's. Half its business, however, is CPUs (the Xinchuang market, with stable collections) and half DCUs, giving it a more diversified customer base. More diversified customers = better cash flow; that is business common sense.

Cambricon's 0.135 reflects business structure, not financial capability: the more concentrated the customers, the more concentrated the acceptance, and the more volatile the cash flow. The most effective way to improve cash flow is therefore not chasing receivables but diversifying customers — something it has talked about for three years, yet the 2026 interim report still does not disclose customer concentration.

Conclusion

Financial conclusion: The profit is real; the cash is not (for now). This is not fraud but a deliberate choice to "pay first to lock in capacity". It nonetheless means valuation must rest on a growth-stock framework, not a cash-cow one.
