Investment Summary

Investment rating: Avoid (wait for a better entry point). 12-month target price RMB 830, implying 28% downside from RMB 1,156. Cambricon is the only scaled domestic AI chip supplier besides Huawei. H1 2026 revenue of RMB 5.996 billion (+108.13%), net profit of RMB 2.311 billion (+122.61%) and a 55.25% gross margin show fundamentals genuinely improving.

Three issues temper this. Q2 revenue grew just 7.84% QoQ, a clear downshift in growth. And a 218x P/E (TTM) and 75.6x P/S already discount the 2027 incentive assessment targets. Good company, genuinely scarce, expensive price — add to the watchlist and revisit below RMB 800, or if Q3 QoQ growth reaches ≥15%.

Chapter 1 Company Overview

Cambricon Technologies Corporation Limited (STAR Market: 688256) listed in July 2020, issuing 40.10 million shares at RMB 64.39 to raise RMB 2.58 billion. The company designs and sells cloud AI chips and accelerator cards, principally the MLU 590 (7nm, FP16 256 TOPS) and the MLU 690 (dual-die Chiplet with HBM3, mass production in early 2026). Actual controller Chen Tianshi holds 28.35% and serves as chairman and general manager.

Cambricon is the only pure-play AI chip name on the A-share market. Its market cap broke RMB 1 trillion intraday on 30 June 2026, the first STAR Market company to reach a trillion-yuan valuation; by 17 August it had closed at RMB 1,156.00, a market cap of RMB 726.3 billion and down 27.6% from its 52-week high of RMB 1,595.55.It posted its first full-year profit in 2025 (net profit RMB 2.06 billion), and net profit of RMB 2.31 billion in the first half of 2026 already exceeds the prior full year.

On share structure, total shares outstanding are 628.29 million following the 2025 annual report's 10-for-4.9 bonus share issue. Chen Tianshi holds 28.35%, and founder shareholders such as Zhongke Suanyuan have state-owned backgrounds. In July 2026 the company introduced 5 million restricted shares (0.80% of total shares, grant price RMB 750), its largest incentive plan since listing.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's AI chip industry runs on a quota system. On 15 December 2022 the US BIS added Cambricon and 36 other companies to the Entity List, TSMC halted 7nm-and-below supply to mainland AI chip customers, and advanced-process capacity was folded into SMIC's N+2. No more than five design companies can secure quotas, and no more than two — Huawei Ascend and Cambricon — can produce training cards at scale.

Demand is concentrated in internet cloud providers and carrier/local intelligent computing centres. In 2026 domestic substitution shifts from pilots to volume procurement. Memory has entered a super-cycle (DRAM contract prices up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%), and the HBM shortage is expected to persist into 2027. The industry is expanding, but so is supply: SMIC capacity expansion, CXMT's HBM ramp and new entrants raising capital via listings. The industry is moving from scarcity to competition, and gross margins will step down.

The underappreciated variable sits on the cost side. HBM's share of AI chip bill-of-materials cost keeps rising, and in 2026 supply will fall short of demand and prices will climb, directly squeezing design companies' gross margin. Hence the company's RMB 1.16 billion in one go for equipment and product trial production — not expansion, but a response to rising prices. The stronger the industry cycle, the more the upstream earns and the more the midstream struggles.

Chapter 3 Business model

Fabless model: design chips, engage foundries, sell cards. Revenue comes almost entirely from accelerator cards sold to internet cloud providers (LLM training and inference), telecom operators, local intelligent computing centres and industry customers. The top five customers accounted for 88.66% in 2025, and a single major customer once accounted for 96.48% in Q1 2025.

At the core of the profit model sits "quota rent": a 55.25% gross margin, above most domestic chip design companies, sourced not from a technology gap but from supply scarcity. The cost shows up in cash flow. To lock in wafers and HBM, prepayments reached RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37%) and inventory RMB 8.248 billion (45.32% of total assets) at end-June 2026, while operating cash flow was only RMB 311 million. This is a business with profit but no cash—inevitable during capacity shortage, a burden during capacity abundance.

Breaking down unit economics one layer further: assuming roughly 500,000 units delivered in 2026 (the planned volume cited by media), RMB 5.996 billion in half-year revenue implies about RMB 12,000 revenue per unit. That is clearly below market pricing for high-end training cards, indicating the revenue mix blends inference cards with training cards and cannot simply be extrapolated as "average price per card × shipments." This is also why the valuation does not use a "shipments × unit price" model.

One industry fact is easily overlooked: the "usability" of domestic AI chips has improved markedly over the past two years. In 2024, customers worried "it won't run"; in 2026, they worry "we can't get supply." That shift is good for the entire domestic chain but bad for valuation—because "it won't run" means scarcity persists, while "can't get supply" means scarcity ends as soon as capacity expands.

Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

1H26: revenue RMB 5.996 billion (+108.13%), gross profit RMB 3.313 billion (55.25%), net profit attributable to parent RMB 2.311 billion (+122.61%), non-recurring-adjusted net profit RMB 2.166 billion (+137.30%), R&D RMB 702 million (11.72% of revenue vs 18.81% a year earlier). Profit quality is high — non-recurring-adjusted profit accounts for 93.7% — and growth is revenue-driven rather than cost compression.

Balance sheet: total assets RMB 18.199 billion (+35.43%), net assets attributable to parent RMB 13.555 billion (+14.53%), inventory RMB 8.248 billion, prepayments RMB 2.914 billion, contract liabilities RMB 188 million (RMB 610,000 at the start of the year), debt-to-asset ratio about 25.5% (our rough calculation, with almost no interest-bearing debt). Cash flow: operating cash flow RMB 311 million (−65.83%), financing cash flow −RMB 657 million. Cash content of net profit is 0.135 — the biggest blemish in this report.

The peer comparison sharpens the picture: Hygon posted a 2026H1 net margin of 22.69% with R&D at 29.15% of revenue, versus Cambricon's 38.54% net margin and 11.72% R&D. Cambricon's margins are higher, but it parks more capital in inventory and prepayments (RMB 11.16 billion combined, 61% of total assets), whereas Hygon runs a lighter asset base and steadier cash flow. Each model carries a cost: the former offers more upside, the latter more certainty.

Chapter 5 Management

Chen Tianshi (41, PhD from the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences) founded the company in 2016 on three key judgments: build an independent chip company rather than supply IP; hold R&D through the 2022–2024 sanctions period; and in 2026 commit the entire RMB 3.953 billion private placement to large-model chips and software. The company turned profitable in 2025, validating the first two.

Capital allocation: two rounds of financing totalling about RMB 6.5 billion (IPO RMB 2.582 billion + private placement RMB 3.953 billion); in 2025, a first dividend of RMB 15 per 10 shares (about RMB 630 million) plus a 4.9-for-10 bonus share issue; in July 2026, an incentive plan of 5 million shares (0.80%, grant price RMB 750), with performance targets of RMB 13.5 billion for 2026, RMB 40.5 billion cumulative for 2026–2027 and RMB 100 billion cumulative over three years. The governance concern is the "one-man company" structure: a 28.35% stake, the chairman also serving as general manager, and a board lacking checks and balances.

Two details are worth recording. First, Chen Tianshi's compensation is only RMB 1.4642 million, while each of the three vice presidents earns above RMB 1.75 million — the founder's incentive lies not in salary but in equity (28.35%, about RMB 138.2 billion at the current price). Second, public information as of the report date shows no records of core shareholders reducing holdings — uncommon against a multi-fold share-price rise over the past year, and a governance plus.

Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three drivers. 1) Domestic substitution volumes: procurement by carriers and local intelligent computing centres, together with cloud providers' second-supplier strategy, underpins the revenue path from RMB 6.5 billion to RMB 17.5 billion in 2026–2027. 2) Product iteration: Siyuan 690 (dual-die, HBM3) enters mass production in early 2026 and drives 2027 growth; a next-generation microarchitecture and instruction set are in development. 3) Software ecosystem: already adapted to DeepSeek, Qwen, Hunyuan, GLM, Kimi and MiniMax, broadening the ecosystem and lowering customer migration costs.

The constraints are equally clear: capacity (SMIC N+2 allocation) and HBM (SK hynix/Samsung/CXMT allocation) set the ceiling. The company plans to deliver about 500,000 units in 2026 (per media reports), with actual volume dependent on yield and materials. In other words, the ceiling on growth sits on the supply side, not the demand side.

Product cadence is central to the growth drivers: Siyuan 590 is the mainstay in 2025–2026, while Siyuan 690 (dual-die, HBM3) enters mass production in early 2026 and begins volume delivery in the second half, with a next-generation microarchitecture and instruction set in development. On software, adaptation to DeepSeek, Qwen, Hunyuan, GLM, Kimi and MiniMax is complete and customer migration costs are falling—but this moat requires sustained investment, and the R&D expense ratio has already fallen from 18.81% to 11.72%.

Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Four competitor types. Huawei Ascend: systemic competition (chips + CANN + cloud + full systems + policy), with capacity prioritised for internal use; it sets the ceiling for Cambricon. Hygon: dual CPU + DCU lines, 2026H1 revenue RMB 9.099 billion, net profit RMB 1.798 billion, gross margin 55.21%, R&D at 29.15% of revenue — steady but slow. Moore Threads: 2026H1 revenue RMB 1.736 billion (+147.42%), net profit RMB −0.012 billion, market cap RMB 268.8 billion — a high-valuation option. NVIDIA: constrained by export controls, able to sell only specially provisioned models.

Cambricon's differentiation is "pure AI + second supplier": the only one of the three domestic players combining scale, profitability and purity. But the ceiling on the "second supplier" position is set by others — customers hand you 20%–30% share to gain bargaining leverage, not 70%.

The key competitive variable is not chip specs but ecosystem. Huawei's CANN is backed by system builders and cloud providers, NVIDIA's CUDA has fifteen years of developer accumulation, and Cambricon's Neuware has only just over three years of adaptation listings. Customers' real selection sequence runs: first, can I buy it (supply); second, will it run (ecosystem); last, what does it cost (price) — Cambricon is weak on the second hurdle and has no advantage on the third.

Chapter 8 Risk factors
① Growth deceleration: Q2 revenue rose 7.84% QoQ; Q3 QoQ growth below 5% would put the full-year RMB 13.5 billion target out of reach. ② Inventory impairment: inventory of RMB 8.248 billion carried at an 8.5% provision ratio (peers 10%–15%); a single product iteration would have to be absorbed through the P&L. ③ Customer concentration: top five at 88.66%, with a single major customer once accounting for 96.48%. ④ Supply chain: SMIC allocation and HBM supply sit outside the company's control. ⑤ Valuation: 218x P/E (TTM), 75.6x P/S; any one risk materializing would be amplified. ⑥ Geopolitics: looser controls are a negative (return of Nvidia's China-specific cards). ⑦ Governance: single-point dependence on a key figure. ⑧ Refinancing: continued capacity expansion in 2027 would require a high-valuation share issuance and dilute existing shareholders.

Stress test: on inventory of RMB 8.248 billion, lifting the provision ratio from 8.5% to 15% adds about RMB 540 million of provisions, equal to 11% of 2026E net profit (RMB 4.9 billion); lifting it to 25% adds RMB 1.36 billion, equal to 28%. If revenue simultaneously falls back to RMB 11 billion and gross margin drops to 50%, 2026E net profit would fall below RMB 3 billion and the 218x P/E would instantly become more than 240x.

A further point on share: in the domestic training card market, Huawei Ascend holds roughly more than half, Cambricon holds a single-digit to ~10% share, and Hygon, Kunlunxin, Moore Threads and others split the remainder. Cambricon's share was not won by taking it; customers allocated it proactively so as not to be locked in by a single vendor. That share is stable but does not grow, unless Ascend's production runs into problems.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026E: revenue RMB 13.0 billion (+100.1%), net margin 37.7%, net profit RMB 4.9 billion. This assumes RMB 5.996 billion in 1H plus RMB 7.0 billion in 2H (Q3 and Q4 averaging roughly +14% QoQ), slightly below the RMB 13.5 billion incentive threshold. 2027E: revenue RMB 17.5 billion (+34.6%), net profit RMB 6.5 billion — a "smooth but unspectacular" path, below the RMB 27.0 billion implied by the incentive target, given the downshift already evident in Q2. 2028E: revenue RMB 22.0 billion, net profit RMB 7.9 billion.

Sensitivity: every 10% difference in revenue moves net profit by about 12% (operating leverage); every 1 point difference in gross margin moves net profit by about 3.5%. If 2027 revenue does reach RMB 27.0 billion (the incentive threshold), net profit at the same net margin would be RMB 10.0 billion, and the neutral target price would need to be revised up to RMB 1,270 — the biggest upside risk in this report.

Quarterly path: 2026Q3 assumes revenue of RMB 3.4 billion (+9.3% QoQ) and Q4 RMB 3.6 billion (+5.9% QoQ), for RMB 13.0 billion for the full year; 2027 spreads RMB 17.5 billion across the four quarters, implying average QoQ growth of +7.7%. This assumption is no longer aggressive — it sits below the incentive assessment threshold and below consensus, yet remains compatible with the 7.84% in Q2.

Chapter 10 Valuation

Three methods cross-check each other. P/E method: 2027E net profit of RMB 6.5 billion × 80x = RMB 520 billion = RMB 830. P/S method: 2027E revenue of RMB 17.5 billion × 30x PS = RMB 525 billion = RMB 835 (cross-validation consistent). PEG method: 2027 revenue growth of 34.6%; at 1.5x PEG, assigning 52x, corresponds to RMB 338 billion = RMB 538; at 2x PEG, assigning 70x, corresponds to RMB 455 billion = RMB 724.

The three methods span RMB 538–835, with the P/E and P/S cross-validation near RMB 830 and the PEG method lower (RMB 538–724). We take RMB 830 as the 12-month target price—the most generous of the three methods. The current price of RMB 1,156 implies "full delivery of RMB 27 billion revenue in 2027 + 80x P/E," an optimistic scenario. Peers: Hygon Information at 250x dynamic P/E on August 10, Moore Threads at 178x static P/S—all expensive, pointing to an industry-wide bubble rather than a stock-specific problem, but a bubble does not change the odds.

Historical valuation range for reference: trailing P/E of 368.97x on June 30, 2026 (company disclosure basis), 217.9x on August 17, compressing 40.9% in a month and a half. Peer comparison: Hygon Information at 250.12x dynamic P/E on August 10, Moore Threads at roughly 178x static P/S on 2025 revenue. The entire sector sits in bubble territory; Cambricon is not the most expensive, but its growth shift is the most pronounced.

One further assumption must be stated: a 2027 net margin of 37.1%, slightly down from 37.7% in 2026, as gross margin falling from 55% to 52% (intensifying competition) is partly offset by a lower expense ratio. If gross margin holds at 55%, 2027E net profit would reach RMB 7.2 billion, lifting the target price from RMB 830 to RMB 920; if gross margin falls to 48%, net profit would be only RMB 5.6 billion, for a target price of RMB 715.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations
Rating: Avoid (wait for a better entry). 12-month target price RMB 830, −28% versus the current price. We would not build a position at RMB 1,156, nor would we short it (securities-lending costs on the STAR Market are high, and the scarcity narrative could reignite at any time).

Two triggers, either of which reopens the debate: ① Price: RMB 800 (77x 2027E net profit, improving the risk-reward to roughly 1:1); ② Data: Q3 single-quarter revenue up ≥15% QoQ and operating cash flow/net profit ≥0.5 (evidence that the shift is a matter of pace). Three falsification conditions: Q3 QoQ growth <5% with inventories still rising, single-quarter gross margin <50%, and top-five customer concentration above 90%.

Our view by investor type. Long-term capital: do not buy now; wait for below RMB 800 and for Q3 data to confirm capacity is in place. Trading capital: trade the volatility, but with strict discipline (no adding to positions, no holding overnight against the trend). Observers: put it on the watchlist; the Q3 report in late October is the first decision point, and there is no need to act early. Three types of investors, three approaches; the only common ground is "do not chase now."

All three approaches rest on one premise: "RMB 17.5 billion in revenue by 2027." If 2027 revenue reaches only RMB 14 billion (the Q2 shift continuing), at 80x and a 37% net margin the target price is just RMB 660—which is why growth, more than gross margin and more than the ecosystem, is the only variable that matters for this stock.

Conclusion

Conclusion: avoid; wait for a valuation pullback. Fundamentals have genuinely improved (revenue +108%, net profit +123%, gross margin 55.25%), but three things hold at once: growth is downshifting (Q2 QoQ 7.84%), cash has not returned (OCF RMB 311 million), and valuation is stretched (218x P/E (TTM)). 12-month target price RMB 830, bear case RMB 400, bull case RMB 1,450. Reopen discussion below RMB 800 or if Q3 QoQ ≥15%; remove from the list if Q3 QoQ <5% and inventory keeps rising.
