## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Cambricon Technologies Corporation Limited, STAR Market ticker 688256, listed in July 2020 (issued 40.10 million shares at RMB 64.39, raising RMB 2.58 billion). The company designs and sells cloud AI chips and accelerator cards; its main products are the MLU 590 (7nm, FP16 256 TOPS) and the MLU 690 (mass production in early 2026, dual-die Chiplet, with HBM3). Actual controller Chen Tianshi holds 28.35% and serves as chairman and general manager.

The company is the only pure-play AI chip name on the A-share market. On 30 June 2026 its market cap broke RMB 1 trillion intraday, making it the first STAR Market company to reach a trillion-yuan valuation; on 17 August it closed at RMB 1,156.00, a market cap of RMB 726.3 billion, down 27.6% from its 52-week high of RMB 1,595.55.**It posted its first full-year profit in 2025 (net profit RMB 2.06 billion), and net profit of RMB 2.31 billion in the first half of 2026 already exceeds the prior full year.**

On share structure, total shares outstanding are 628.29 million (after the 2025 annual report's 10-for-4.9 bonus share issue). Chen Tianshi holds 28.35%, and founder shareholders such as Zhongke Suanyuan have state-owned backgrounds. In July 2026 the company introduced 5 million restricted shares (0.80% of total shares, grant price RMB 750), its largest incentive plan since listing.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's AI chip industry runs on a quota system: on 15 December 2022, the US BIS added Cambricon and 36 other companies to the Entity List, TSMC halted supply of 7nm and below to mainland AI chip customers, and advanced process capacity was folded into SMIC's N+2. No more than five design companies can secure quotas, and no more than two (Huawei Ascend, Cambricon) can produce training cards at scale.

Demand is concentrated in internet cloud providers and carrier/local intelligent computing centers. In 2026, domestic substitution shifts from pilots to volume procurement. Memory has entered a super-cycle (DRAM contract prices up 60%–70%, NAND up more than 100%), and the HBM shortage is expected to persist into 2027. The industry is expanding, but so is supply (SMIC capacity expansion, CXMT HBM ramping, new entrants raising capital via listings). **The industry is moving from scarcity to competition, and gross margins will step down.**

The cost-side variable is underappreciated: HBM's share of AI chip bill-of-materials cost keeps rising, and in 2026 supply will fall short of demand and prices will climb, directly squeezing design companies' gross margin. That is why the company is adding RMB 1.16 billion in one go for equipment and product trial production — not expansion, but a response to rising prices. The stronger the industry cycle, the more the upstream earns and the more the midstream struggles.

## Chapter 3 Business model

Fabless model: design chips, engage foundries, sell cards. Revenue comes almost entirely from accelerator cards, with customers being internet cloud providers (LLM training/inference), telecom operators and local intelligent computing centers, and industry customers. The top five customers accounted for 88.66% in 2025, and a single major customer once accounted for 96.48% in Q1 2025.

The core of the profit model is "quota rent": gross margin of 55.25%, higher than most domestic chip design companies, sourced not from a technology gap but from supply scarcity. The cost shows up in cash flow: to lock in wafers and HBM, prepayments reached RMB 2.914 billion (+291.37%) and inventory RMB 8.248 billion (45.32% of total assets) at end-June 2026, while operating cash flow was only RMB 311 million. **This is a business with profit but no cash—inevitable during capacity shortage, a burden during capacity abundance.**

Breaking down unit economics one layer: assuming roughly 500,000 units delivered in 2026 (the planned volume cited by media), RMB 5.996 billion in half-year revenue implies about RMB 12,000 revenue per unit—this figure is clearly below market pricing for high-end training cards, indicating that the revenue mix blends inference cards with training cards and cannot be simply extrapolated as "average price per card × shipments." This is also why the valuation does not use a "shipments × unit price" model.

One industry fact easily overlooked: the "usability" of domestic AI chips has improved markedly over the past two years. **In 2024, customers worried "it won't run"; in 2026, they worry "we can't get supply."** This shift is good for the entire domestic chain but bad for valuation—because "it won't run" means scarcity persists, while "can't get supply" means scarcity ends as soon as capacity expands.

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

First half of 2026: revenue RMB 5.996 billion (+108.13%), gross profit RMB 3.313 billion (55.25%), net profit attributable to parent RMB 2.311 billion (+122.61%), non-recurring-adjusted net profit RMB 2.166 billion (+137.30%), R&D spending RMB 702 million (11.72% of revenue, versus 18.81% a year earlier). **Profit quality is high (non-recurring-adjusted profit accounts for 93.7%), and growth comes from revenue rather than cost compression.**

Asset side: total assets RMB 18.199 billion (+35.43%), net assets attributable to parent RMB 13.555 billion (+14.53%), inventory RMB 8.248 billion, prepayments RMB 2.914 billion, contract liabilities RMB 188 million (RMB 610,000 at the start of the year), debt-to-asset ratio about 25.5% (rough calculation, with almost no interest-bearing debt). Cash flow: operating cash flow RMB 311 million (−65.83%), financing cash flow −RMB 657 million. **Cash content of net profit is 0.135, the biggest blemish in this report.**

A comparison with peers makes the problem clearer: Hygon's 2026H1 net margin was 22.69% and R&D 29.15% of revenue, while Cambricon's net margin was 38.54% and R&D 11.72%. Cambricon's margins are higher, but it parks more money in inventory and prepayments (RMB 11.16 billion combined, 61% of total assets); Hygon's asset base is lighter and its cash flow steadier. Each model carries a cost: the former offers more upside, the latter more certainty.

## Chapter 5 Management

Chen Tianshi (41, PhD from the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences) founded the company in 2016 and made three key judgments: insist on building an independent chip company rather than an IP supplier; do not cut R&D during the sanctions period of 2022–2024; and in 2026 commit the entire RMB 3.953 billion private placement to large-model chips and software. The company turned profitable in 2025, validating the first two judgments.

Capital allocation: two rounds of financing totaling about RMB 6.5 billion (IPO RMB 2.582 billion + private placement RMB 3.953 billion); in 2025, the first dividend of RMB 15 per 10 shares (about RMB 630 million) plus a 4.9-for-10 bonus share issue; in July 2026, an incentive plan of 5 million shares (0.80%, grant price RMB 750), with performance targets of RMB 13.5 billion for 2026, RMB 40.5 billion cumulative for 2026–2027, and RMB 100 billion cumulative over three years. **The governance concern is the "one-man company" structure: a 28.35% stake, chairman and general manager, and a board lacking checks and balances.**

Two details are worth recording. First, Chen Tianshi's compensation is only RMB 1.4642 million, while the three vice presidents each earn above RMB 1.75 million — the founder's incentive lies not in salary but in equity (28.35%, about RMB 138.2 billion at the current price). Second, public information as of the report date shows no records of core shareholders reducing holdings, which is uncommon against a backdrop of a multi-fold share-price rise over the past year and is a governance plus.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three drivers. **1) Volume of domestic substitution:** domestic procurement by carriers and local intelligent computing centers, plus cloud providers' second-supplier strategy, underpins the revenue path from RMB 6.5 billion to RMB 17.5 billion in 2026–2027. **2) Product iteration:** Siyuan 690 (dual-die, HBM3) enters mass production in early 2026 and is the core of 2027 growth; a new-generation microarchitecture and instruction set are in development. **3) Software ecosystem:** already adapted to DeepSeek, Qwen, Hunyuan, GLM, Kimi and MiniMax; a broader ecosystem lowers customer migration costs.

The constraints are equally clear: capacity (SMIC N+2 allocation) and HBM (SK hynix/Samsung/CXMT allocation) set the ceiling. The company plans to deliver about 500,000 units in 2026 (per media reports), with actual volume dependent on yield and materials. **In other words, the ceiling on growth drivers sits on the supply side, not the demand side.**

Product cadence is the core of the growth drivers: Siyuan 590 is the mainstay in 2025–2026; Siyuan 690 (dual-die, HBM3) enters mass production in early 2026 and begins volume delivery in the second half, with a new-generation microarchitecture and instruction set in development. On software, adaptation to DeepSeek, Qwen, Hunyuan, GLM, Kimi and MiniMax is complete and customer migration costs are falling—but this moat requires sustained investment, and the R&D expense ratio has already fallen from 18.81% to 11.72%.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Four types of competitors. **Huawei Ascend:** systemic competition (chips + CANN + cloud + complete systems + policy), with capacity prioritized for internal use; it sets the ceiling for Cambricon. **Hygon:** dual CPU + DCU lines, 2026H1 revenue RMB 9.099 billion, net profit RMB 1.798 billion, gross margin 55.21%, R&D at 29.15% of revenue — steady but slow. **Moore Threads:** 2026H1 revenue RMB 1.736 billion (+147.42%), net profit RMB −0.012 billion, market cap RMB 268.8 billion — a high-valuation option. **NVIDIA:** constrained by export controls, able to sell only specially provisioned models.

Cambricon's differentiated positioning is "pure AI + second supplier": among the three domestic players, it is the only one combining scale, profitability and purity. **But the ceiling on the "second supplier" position is set by others** — customers give you 20%–30% share to gain bargaining leverage, not 70%.

The key competitive variable is not chip specs but ecosystem. Huawei's CANN has the backing of system builders and cloud providers, NVIDIA's CUDA has fifteen years of developer accumulation, and Cambricon's Neuware has only just over three years of adaptation listings. Customers' real selection sequence is: first, can I buy it (supply); second, will it run (ecosystem); last, how much does it cost (price) — Cambricon is weak on the second hurdle and has no advantage on the third.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

**① Growth deceleration:** Q2 revenue rose 7.84% QoQ; if Q3 QoQ growth falls below 5%, the full-year target of RMB 13.5 billion will be missed. **② Inventory impairment:** RMB 8.248 billion of inventory, provision ratio 8.5% (peers 10%–15%); a single product iteration would have to be paid for out of profit. **③ Customer concentration:** top five at 88.66%, a single major customer once accounted for 96.48%. **④ Supply chain:** SMIC allocation and HBM supply are not under the company's control. **⑤ Valuation:** 218x TTM P/E, 75.6x P/S; any one risk materializing would be amplified. **⑥ Geopolitics:** looser controls are a negative for it (return of Nvidia's China-specific cards). **⑦ Governance:** single-point dependence on a key figure. **⑧ Refinancing:** if capacity expansion continues in 2027, a high-valuation share issuance would dilute existing shareholders.Run a stress test: inventory of RMB 8.248 billion; raising the provision ratio from 8.5% to 15% adds about RMB 540 million of provisions, equal to 11% of 2026E net profit (RMB 4.9 billion); raising it to 25% adds RMB 1.36 billion, equal to 28%. If revenue simultaneously falls back to RMB 11 billion and gross margin drops to 50%, 2026E net profit would fall below RMB 3 billion, and the 218x P/E would instantly become more than 240x.

One more point on share: in the domestic training card market, Huawei Ascend holds roughly more than half, Cambricon holds a single-digit to ~10% share, and Hygon, Kunlunxin, Moore Threads and others split the remainder. **Cambricon's share was not won by taking it; customers allocated it proactively so as not to be locked in by a single vendor.** That share is stable but does not grow, unless Ascend's production runs into problems.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026E: revenue RMB 13.0 billion (+100.1%), net margin 37.7%, net profit RMB 4.9 billion. This is based on RMB 5.996 billion in the first half plus RMB 7.0 billion in the second half (Q3 and Q4 averaging roughly +14% QoQ), slightly below the incentive threshold of RMB 13.5 billion. **2027E: revenue RMB 17.5 billion (+34.6%), net profit RMB 6.5 billion** — a "smooth but unspectacular" path, below the RMB 27.0 billion implied by the incentive target, because Q2 already showed a downshift. 2028E: revenue RMB 22.0 billion, net profit RMB 7.9 billion.

Sensitivity: every 10% difference in revenue moves net profit by about 12% (operating leverage); every 1 point difference in gross margin moves net profit by about 3.5%. **If 2027 revenue does reach RMB 27.0 billion (the incentive threshold), net profit at the same net margin would be RMB 10.0 billion, and the neutral target price would need to be revised up to RMB 1,270** — the biggest upside risk in this report.

Quarterly path: 2026Q3 assumes revenue of RMB 3.4 billion (+9.3% QoQ), Q4 assumes RMB 3.6 billion (+5.9% QoQ), for RMB 13.0 billion for the full year; 2027 is spread over RMB 17.5 billion, implying average QoQ growth of +7.7% across the four quarters. This assumption is no longer aggressive — it is below the incentive assessment threshold and below the market consensus, but it is compatible with the 7.84% in Q2.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

Three methods cross-check each other. **P/E method:** 2027E net profit of RMB 6.5 billion × 80x = RMB 520 billion = RMB 830. **P/S method:** 2027E revenue of RMB 17.5 billion × 30x PS = RMB 525 billion = RMB 835 (cross-validation consistent). **PEG method:** 2027 revenue growth of 34.6%, at 1.5x PEG assigning 52x, corresponding to RMB 338 billion = RMB 538; at 2x PEG assigning 70x, corresponding to RMB 455 billion = RMB 724.

The three methods give a range of RMB 538–835; the PE and PS cross-validation sits near RMB 830, the PEG method lower (RMB 538–724). We take RMB 830 as the 12-month target price—the most generous of the three methods. **The current price of RMB 1,156 implies "full delivery of RMB 27 billion revenue in 2027 + 80x P/E,"** an optimistic scenario. Comparable companies: Hygon Information at 250x dynamic PE on August 10, Moore Threads at 178x static PS—peers are all expensive, indicating an industry-wide bubble rather than a stock-specific problem, but a bubble does not change the odds.

Historical valuation range for reference: trailing P/E of 368.97x on June 30, 2026 (company disclosure basis), 217.9x on August 17, compressing 40.9% in a month and a half. Peer comparison: Hygon Information at 250.12x dynamic P/E on August 10, Moore Threads at roughly 178x static P/S on 2025 revenue. **The entire sector sits in bubble territory; Cambricon is not the most expensive, but its growth shift is the most pronounced.**

Another assumption that must be stated: 2027 net margin of 37.1%, slightly down from 37.7% in 2026, as gross margin falling from 55% to 52% (intensifying competition) is partly offset by a lower expense ratio. **If gross margin holds at 55%, 2027E net profit would reach RMB 7.2 billion, lifting the target price from RMB 830 to RMB 920**; if gross margin falls to 48%, net profit would be only RMB 5.6 billion, for a target price of RMB 715.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

**Rating: Avoid (wait for valuation to fall). 12-month target price RMB 830, −28% versus the current price.** We do not recommend building a position at RMB 1,156, nor do we recommend shorting (high securities-lending costs on the STAR Market; the scarcity narrative could ignite at any time).Two triggers, either of which reopens the discussion: **① Price line RMB 800** (corresponding to 77x 2027E net profit, improving the risk-reward to about 1:1); **② Data line: Q3 single-quarter revenue up ≥15% QoQ and operating cash flow/net profit ≥0.5** (proving the shift is a matter of pace). Three falsification conditions: Q3 QoQ growth We address each investor type separately. Long-term capital: do not buy now; wait for below RMB 800 and for Q3 data to confirm capacity is in place. Trading capital: participate in the volatility, but with strict discipline (no adding to positions, no holding overnight against the trend). Observers: put it on the watchlist; the Q3 report in late October is the first decision point, and there is no need to act early. **Three types of investors, three approaches; the only common ground is "do not chase now."**

Note that all three methods rest on the premise of "RMB 17.5 billion in revenue by 2027." **If 2027 revenue reaches only RMB 14 billion (the Q2 shift continuing), at 80x and a 37% net margin, the target price is just RMB 660**—which is why "growth" is the only variable that matters for this stock, more than gross margin, more than the ecosystem.
