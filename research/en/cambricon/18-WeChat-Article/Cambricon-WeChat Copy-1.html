Title and lead-in
Title: Profit doubled, cash up only RMB 300 million: is trillion-cap Cambricon expensive?Subtitle: RMB 2.3 billion earned in 1H, only RMB 300 million more cash on the books; RMB 8.2 billion of inventory in the warehouse; a 218x P/E means you are buying 2028.Lead: Cambricon reported the best interim results in its history after the close on August 7. Three trading days later the stock had fallen 6.33%, wiping RMB 48 billion off its market value. Why does doubled profit trigger a selloff? The answer lies not in the income statement but in the cash flow statement.
Main text 1: First, the good news

1H revenue RMB 5.996 billion, up 2x YoY; net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 2.311 billion, +122.61% YoY; non-recurring net profit RMB 2.166 billion, +137.30% YoY; gross margin 55.25%. 1H profit alone already exceeds the full-year 2025 figure of RMB 2.059 billion.

On the A-share semiconductor screen, that scorecard sits in the top tier. The quality is more impressive still: non-recurring items account for 93.7% of net profit, with no asset sales and no subsidy padding, while gross margin fell just 0.68pp YoY and rose 1.77pp QoQ in Q2.It earns on product, not on price cuts to win orders.

Read this section alone and you would think this is a great company taking off. Hold on — look at the next three numbers.

Main text 2: Three unsettling numbers
First: 7.84%. Q2 revenue came in at RMB 3.111 billion, up 75.83% YoY — respectable on the face of it, but only 7.84% QoQ. Q1 growth was +159.56% YoY. Three quarters, three speeds: this is not "steady," it is "shifting gears." To reach the 2026 assessment line of RMB 13.5 billion, the company must deliver RMB 7.5 billion in the second half, with Q3 and Q4 averaging more than +12% QoQ. Second: RMB 8.248 billion. That is period-end inventory, up RMB 3.3 billion from the start of the year, equal to 45% of total assets and 61% of net assets. The breakdown: raw materials RMB 5.749 billion, outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion. These are wafers, HBM and work-in-progress chips that have yet to become revenue. The company has already recognised RMB 767 million in write-down provisions. Whether that is enough depends on how much longer older-model inventory can still sell once new-generation chips ramp. Third: RMB 311 million. That is first-half net operating cash flow, down 65.83% YoY and just 13.5% of net profit. Where did the money go? Inventory rose RMB 3.3 billion and prepayments rose RMB 2.1 billion. In one sentence: the profit is real, the cash is still on its way.
Main text 3: Why the stock fell on August 10

On the first trading day after the interim report, the stock closed at RMB 1,124, down 6.33%, wiping out about RMB 48 billion in market value. The market is not rejecting the results; it is repricing growth.

June 30 is more interesting. The stock surged intraday to RMB 1,620 and closed at RMB 1,595.55, giving a market value of RMB 1,002.5 billion and making it the first STAR Market company to reach a trillion-yuan market value. That evening the company issued a risk warning announcement, stating in black and white a trailing P/E of 368.97x and P/B of 77.88x, versus industry averages of 74.86x and 8.06x. A company voluntarily telling the market "I am five times more expensive than my peers" is rare in the A-share market.

By August 17, the stock was at RMB 1,156, market value RMB 726.3 billion, P/E 218x. Still expensive, just not as expensive as a month earlier.

Main text 4: What is a 218x P/E buying?

The clearest approach is to work backwards. At the current market cap of RMB 726.3 billion, an 80x multiple implies net profit of RMB 9.08 billion; at a 38.5% net margin, that requires revenue of RMB 23.6 billion. Yet the company's assessment targets are RMB 13.5 billion for 2026 and RMB 27 billion for 2027. In other words, the current price roughly equates to "full delivery on the 2027 assessment target + 80x P/E."

That is not impossible, but it is the optimistic scenario, not the base case. Our neutral case—2027 revenue of RMB 17.5 billion, net profit of RMB 6.5 billion and 80x—gives a target price of RMB 830, 28% below the current price; the pessimistic case is RMB 400, 65% lower; the optimistic case is RMB 1,450, just 25% higher. Twenty percent upside, sixty percent downside—the math doesn't need to be precise.

Body 5: Why some still say it can reach 1,300

There is another way to model it. If 2027 revenue actually reaches RMB 27 billion (the full incentive target), a 37% net margin gives RMB 10 billion of net profit; at 80x that implies a RMB 800 billion market cap, or above RMB 1,270 per share.

The math is right; the problem is probability. Q2 sequential growth was only 7.84%, and lifting revenue from RMB 13 billion to RMB 27 billion in a year and a half requires capacity, HBM and customers to all break right at once. Treating that as the base case means treating the company's KPI as your own margin of safety.

The most expensive phrase in investing is "if everything goes right." When everything goes right, no price looks expensive; the problem is that everything rarely does.

Body six: So what should be done

Three actionable disciplines:

① Don't chase. Don't build a position at the current price, and don't set up an "observation position" either—a 1% position won't make money; it will only make you watch the stock price every day and then start looking for reasons to be bullish.② Two observation lines.Reopen the discussion if either is met: the price falls back below RMB 800 (corresponding to 77x 2027 neutral net profit), or third-quarter single-quarter revenue grows ≥15% QoQ with operating cash flow/net profit back above 0.5.③ One veto line.If Q3 revenue grows less than 5% QoQ and inventory continues to rise, the shift is not a matter of pace but of demand—walk away and don't buy no matter how low the price goes.
Conclusion: Respect the company, respect the price

Cambricon is China's only scaled AI chip supplier outside Huawei. It has gone from a single office at the Chinese Academy of Sciences in 2016 to the first STAR Market stock to reach a RMB 1 trillion market cap in 2026, and from sanctions and three straight years of losses to RMB 2.3 billion of profit in half a year—a company that has genuinely delivered.

But respecting a company and buying it at this price are two different things. When a good company gets expensive, the best move is to put it on the watchlist and do nothing.

The earnings are real, the scarcity is real, and so is the valuation. Wait for revenue to catch up to the valuation, or for the price to fall below RMB 800, before discussing a purchase.
