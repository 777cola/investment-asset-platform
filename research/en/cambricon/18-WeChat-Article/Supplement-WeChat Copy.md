## Title and lead-in

Title: Profits doubled, cash only RMB 300 million: is trillion-cap Cambricon expensive?Subtitle: RMB 2.3 billion earned in the first half, only RMB 300 million more cash on the books; RMB 8.2 billion of inventory sitting in the warehouse; 218x P/E means buying 2028.Lead: On the evening of August 7, Cambricon delivered the best interim report in its history. Three trading days later, the stock had fallen 6.33%, wiping RMB 48 billion off its market value. Why does doubled profit bring a selloff? The answer is not in the income statement but in the cash flow statement.

## Main text 1: First, the good news

First-half revenue RMB 5.996 billion, doubled year on year; net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 2.311 billion, +122.61% year on year; non-recurring net profit RMB 2.166 billion, +137.30% year on year; gross margin 55.25%. Profit for the first half already exceeds all of 2025 (RMB 2.059 billion).

On the A-share semiconductor screen, this scorecard sits in the top tier. More impressive is the quality: non-recurring items account for 93.7% of net profit, with no asset sales and no subsidy padding; gross margin fell only 0.68 points year on year and rose 1.77 points quarter on quarter in Q2.That shows it earns on product, not on price cuts to win orders.

Read only this section and you would think this is a great company taking off. Hold on — look at the next three numbers.

## Main text 2: Three unsettling numbers

First: 7.84%. Q2 revenue was RMB 3.111 billion, up 75.83% year on year, which looks decent, but up only 7.84% quarter on quarter. Q1 growth was +159.56% year on year. Three quarters, three speeds—this is not "steady," this is "shifting gears." To hit the 2026 assessment line of RMB 13.5 billion, the company must deliver RMB 7.5 billion in the second half, with Q3 and Q4 averaging over +12% quarter on quarter. Second: RMB 8.248 billion. This is period-end inventory, up RMB 3.3 billion from the start of the year, accounting for 45% of total assets and 61% of net assets. Broken down: raw materials RMB 5.749 billion, outsourced processing materials RMB 2.537 billion. These are wafers, HBM and work-in-progress chips that have not yet become revenue. The company has already recognized RMB 767 million in write-down provisions. Whether that is enough depends on how much longer older-model inventory can still sell after new-generation chips ramp. Third: RMB 311 million. This is first-half net operating cash flow, down 65.83% year on year, only 13.5% of net profit. Where did the money go? Inventory rose RMB 3.3 billion, prepayments rose RMB 2.1 billion. In one sentence: the profit is real, the cash is still on its way.

## Main text 3: Why the stock fell on August 10

On the first trading day after the interim report, the stock closed at RMB 1,124, down 6.33%, wiping out about RMB 48 billion in market value. The market is not rejecting the results; it is repricing growth.

More interesting is June 30. That day it surged intraday to RMB 1,620 and closed at RMB 1,595.55, with a market value of RMB 1,002.5 billion, becoming the first STAR Market company to reach a trillion-yuan market value. That evening, the company itself issued a risk warning announcement, stating in black and white: trailing P/E of 368.97x and P/B of 77.88x, versus industry averages of 74.86x and 8.06x. A company voluntarily telling the market "I am five times more expensive than my peers" is rare in the A-share market.

By August 17, the stock was at RMB 1,156, market value RMB 726.3 billion, P/E 218x. Still expensive, just not as expensive as a month earlier.

## Main text 4: What is a 218x P/E buying?

The clearest way is to work backwards. At the current price of RMB 726.3 billion, if the market is willing to pay only 80x, net profit needs to be RMB 9.08 billion; backing out at a 38.5% net margin requires revenue of RMB 23.6 billion. Yet the company's assessment targets are RMB 13.5 billion for 2026 and RMB 27 billion for 2027. In other words, the current price roughly equals "full achievement of the 2027 assessment target + 80x P/E."

That is not impossible, but it is the optimistic scenario, not the base case. The neutral case, assuming 2027 revenue of RMB 17.5 billion, net profit of RMB 6.5 billion and 80x, gives a target price of RMB 830, 28% below the current price; the pessimistic case is RMB 400, 65% lower; the optimistic case is RMB 1,450, only 25% higher. Twenty percent upside, sixty percent downside—this math doesn't need to be precise.

## Body 5: Why some still say it can reach 1,300

Because there is another way to model it. If 2027 revenue actually reaches RMB 27 billion (the full incentive target), a 37% net margin gives RMB 10 billion of net profit; at 80x that is a RMB 800 billion market cap, or above RMB 1,270 per share.

The math is right; the problem is probability. Q2 sequential growth was only 7.84%, and lifting revenue from RMB 13 billion to RMB 27 billion in a year and a half requires capacity, HBM and customers to all break right at once. Treating that as the base case means treating the company's KPI as your own margin of safety.

The most expensive phrase in investing is "if everything goes right." When everything goes right, no price looks expensive; the problem is that everything rarely goes right.

## Body six: So what should be done

Three actionable disciplines:

① Don't chase. Don't build a position at the current price, and don't set up an "observation position" either—a 1% position won't make money; it will only make you watch the stock price every day and then start looking for reasons to be bullish.② Two observation lines.The price falls back below RMB 800 (corresponding to 77x 2027 neutral net profit), or third-quarter single-quarter revenue grows ≥15% quarter-on-quarter and operating cash flow/net profit returns above 0.5. If either is met, reopen the discussion.③ One veto line.If Q3 revenue grows less than 5% quarter-on-quarter and inventory continues to rise, it means the shift is not a matter of pace but of demand—walk away and don't buy no matter how low the price goes.

## Conclusion: Respect the company, respect the price

Cambricon is the only scaled AI chip supplier in China besides Huawei. From a single office at the Chinese Academy of Sciences in 2016 to the first STAR Market stock to reach a RMB 1 trillion market cap in 2026; from being sanctioned and losing money for three straight years to earning RMB 2.3 billion in half a year—this is a company that has genuinely delivered.

But respecting a company and buying it at this price are two different things. When a good company gets expensive, the best move is to put it on the watchlist and do nothing.

The earnings are real, the scarcity is real, and so is the valuation. Wait for revenue to catch up to the valuation, or for the price to fall below RMB 800, before discussing a purchase.(Data in this article comes from the company's 2026 interim report, 2025 annual report, 2026 equity incentive draft and public reports; estimated figures are marked in the text.
