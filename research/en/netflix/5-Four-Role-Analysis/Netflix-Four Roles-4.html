Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Netflix

What could make this investment lose money, how much would we lose, and how would we see it coming?

One line: five risks, two already unfolding, three not yet priced in

Li Lu's approach to risk: "first calculate how much you can lose, then calculate how much you can gain." Netflix trades at 65.85; the bear case of USD 45 implies a maximum drawdown of 32%.That is tolerable, because its balance sheet can withstand the extreme assumption of zero revenue growth.

Of the five risks I listed, two are already materializing: advertising estimate cuts and organizational turmoil. Three are not yet in the price: sports rights inflation, EU content regulation, and buybacks funded with leverage.The real danger is not what has already happened, but what has not yet been discussed.

Two already under way: advertising and organization

Advertising. Cut from RMB 3 billion to RMB 2.7 billion, a 10% reduction. Small in absolute terms at 5.3% of revenue, but the signal matters: if the "second growth curve" still fails to scale by year three, the market will reclassify the company from "advertising platform" to "subscription company" — and the valuation multiple gap is more than 5x. Half of this is already priced in: the de-rating from 45x to 18x is exactly that.

Organisation. On October 13, 6% layoffs, replacement of the content chief and departure of the film head. Three events in the same week signal this is not routine optimisation but structural restructuring. The danger lies in the lag: content takes 18 months to show up in the data. If the 2027 slate thins out, rising churn only becomes visible in 2028 — by which time the stock will have already fallen.

Both risks are "buyable" because neither affects current cash flow. Q3 operating profit of RMB 4.459 billion and FCF of RMB 2.884 billion are real. The risks affect future growth, not present profitability.

Three unpriced factors: sports, regulation, leverage

Sports rights inflation. Netflix is doubling down on the NFL and boxing in 2026. Rights sellers are the leagues, supply is monopolistic and prices are renegotiated every year. Sports lower churn but bring rigid costs and no long-term IP.If sports spending keeps rising while subscriber growth fails to keep pace, operating margin falls from 31.5% back to 28%–29% — the 2022 script.

EU content regulation. Europe is 31.9% of Netflix revenue (USD 4.107 billion in Q3), and the EU is tightening local-content quotas and investment obligations. Compliance costs will not be fatal, but they compress EMEA margins in the company's second-largest market.

Buybacks plus leverage. Net debt/operating profit is currently 0.44x, very safe. But with buybacks running at an annualized USD 19.2 billion against USD 12 billion of FCF, net debt rises by about USD 7 billion a year.Three years out that is USD 28 billion, leverage at 1.7x — and if a revenue decline hits then, buybacks must stop, and a halt in buybacks is itself a second blow to the share price.

How the USD 45 downside figure was derived

Bear case: 2027 revenue growth of 8%, advertising stuck at USD 2.7 billion, operating margin back down to 30%, EPS of USD 3.0, at 15x P/E = USD 45. This assumes three bad things happen at once: growth drops, advertising dies, margin falls.

All three have coincided once before — in 2022. That year Netflix posted its first net subscriber decline and the stock fell from USD 700 (pre-split) to USD 165, a 76% drop. So USD 45 is not alarmism; there is precedent.

This time is different in two ways. First, in 2022 Netflix had no advertising business, no sports, no buyback authorization; now it has all three — three extra buffers. Second, valuation started 2022 at 45x P/E; now it is 18x — the damage from a high-valuation collapse is far greater than that from earnings misses at a low valuation. So I put 25% probability on USD 45, not 50%.

Conclusion

Five risks: two are already materialising (advertising, organisation), three remain unpriced (sports, regulation, leverage). The 32% maximum drawdown requires three adverse events to coincide, and each of the three has a buffer. Conclusion: the risks are manageable, but two monitoring lines are mandatory — stop out if net debt/operating profit exceeds 1x, and trim if 2027 revenue guidance points to single-digit growth. These two matter more than any price stop-loss, because this company's risks are chronic rather than acute.
