View synthesis: four readings form one picture — Netflix

Four people, one earnings report, four entirely different focal points

The four readings do not conflict, because they examine four different quadrants.

Four people read the same earnings report, each with a different focus: Duan Yongping looks at customers (churn rate, growth by region), Buffett looks at cash (FCF, buybacks), Munger looks at accounting (margin quality, non-operating items), and Li Lu looks at position (stage of industry evolution). Put the four quadrants together and you get a complete picture.

Once assembled, the picture shows: the company's operating side (customers + cash) is fine, the accounting side (single-quarter margins + net profit growth) is inflated, and the strategic side (industry position) is shifting from growth to maturity. The conclusions at all three levels point to the same action: buy, but at a mature-company price.

Common conclusion at three levels

Operations: healthy. Double-digit growth across four regions, churn down year on year, operating margin genuinely up 2 percentage points (29.5% → 31.5%). No argument at this level.

Accounting: some padding, but not severe. The single-quarter 34.6% includes expense timing; net profit growth includes non-operating items. On the squeezed numbers (31.5%, FCF RMB 12 billion), valuation is still reasonable. The padding means "don't be too optimistic," not "don't buy."

Strategy: shifting gears. From growth to returns, from user acquisition to pricing plus advertising. A gear shift typically looks like "good financials, ugly growth numbers" — exactly the current situation. The market is watching the growth numbers, hence the low valuation; the financials prove the shift is working, hence worth buying.

Synthesis: one price, three actions, two red lines

Price: USD 65.85 corresponds to 18x 2026E PE, 21x normalized PE, and a 4.4% FCF yield. This price sits at the lower edge of the reasonable range for a "mature streaming company." Buy, but not a bargain.

Three moves: build one-third at the current price; add a second tranche below USD 55 (15x normalized PE); fill out the position near USD 45 (bear case). The three price levels correspond to three probabilities, not three judgments.

Two red lines: ① If 2027 revenue guidance points to single-digit growth, trim the position; ② if net debt/operating profit exceeds 1x, stop adding. Neither looks at the share price—only at the facts. This is "buy the thesis, not the price" made concrete.

Conclusion

The picture assembled from the four readings is clear: healthy operations, accounting with some padding, a strategic shift, and valuation reasonably low. The resulting action is to buy in tranches, with growth and leverage as the red lines. If you can remember only one sentence: what this company is selling now is "maturity," and what you are buying is "maturing faster than the market thinks."
