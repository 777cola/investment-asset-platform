Masterclass Close Read (Munger): Three Traps Hidden in Accounting Line Items — Netflix

Read the same earnings report from a different angle and three red flags emerge

The numbers all add up, but growth is masking three structural problems

Munger's approach to financial statements is to invert: don't ask what's good about the report, ask where it might be fake, where it has been prettified, where it is borrowing from the future. Reading Q3 this way, I found three things that don't add up.

First, the 6.4 percentage point margin jump includes an accounting timing effect. Second, net profit growth (44%) outpaces operating profit growth (37%), with the gap coming from non-operating items. Third, buybacks exceeded free cash flow. None of the three amounts to fraud, but all three suggest the impression that "this quarter was exceptionally good" needs a discount.

Pitfall 1: Part of the 34.6% margin reflects expenses pushed into Q4

Operating margin rose 6.4 percentage points in a year, a pace rarely seen at a mature company. The filing itself admits why: part of the profit came from "changes in the timing of expense recognition." In plain terms, costs that belonged in this quarter were pushed into the next.

The maneuver is legal and common, but it carries one feature: it is unsustainable and reverses the following quarter. Q4 guidance is the evidence: operating margin of 26.0%, 8.6 percentage points below Q3. Management attributes 1.2 points of the gap to severance costs, and part of the remaining 7.4 points to "deferred expenses coming back."

The Munger-style judgment: do not look at margin in a single quarter; look at the four-quarter moving average. Across 2026 the four quarters roughly run: Q1 high (including one-off gains), Q2 flat, Q3 high (expense timing), Q4 low, for 31.5% for the full year. That 31.5% is the real number; 34.6% is the made-up one.

Trap 2: net profit growth of 44% exceeds operating profit growth of 37% — what explains the gap?

The 7 percentage point gap between the two figures equates to roughly USD 200 million, and could stem from three sources: a lower tax rate, reduced interest expense, or non-operating gains. The earnings summary does not break this out, so rather than guess which item it is, I simply flag that the gap exists.

Why flag it? Because net income is the most frequently cited number in the market—and the one most easily distorted by non-operating items. 2026Q1 is a case in point: net income of RMB 5.28 billion, up 83% YoY, on revenue growth of just 16%. Value the company on net income and that quarter's P/E looks absurdly low—only for the next quarter to "blow up."

Munger would say: "EBITDA is bullshit, net income is half bullshit, cash flow is the real thing." Crude, but apt here—for Netflix's business, operating profit (RMB 4.459 billion) is a more reliable gauge of true earning power than net income (RMB 3.664 billion), and free cash flow (RMB 2.884 billion) is the most reliable of all.

Trap 3: Buybacks exceeding cash flow — confidence or overreach

Q3 buybacks were RMB 4.8 billion and FCF RMB 2.884 billion; the RMB 1.916 billion gap was funded with cash and debt. At 18x P/E the move is rational; as a "do it every year" policy it is dangerous.

Run the numbers three years out: net debt rises about RMB 7 billion a year, from RMB 7.1 billion to RMB 28 billion, and leverage from 0.44x to 1.7x. 1.7x is not high, but if revenue growth then falls to 5%, buybacks must stop, and a halt in buybacks is itself a second hit to the share price.

What Munger hated most was exactly this structure of "using leverage to amplify shareholder returns," because it turns a problem that was merely slow growth into the double problem of slow growth plus deleveraging. He would probably say: buybacks are good, but don't turn them into debt-financed dividends. I fully agree, and that is why I set "net debt/operating profit above 1x" as my trim line.

Conclusion

None of the three traps argues against buying the stock; each argues against believing the 34.6%. The true earnings level is a full-year operating margin of 31.5% and free cash flow of RMB 12 billion, not the single-quarter 34.6% and net profit +44%. Value it on the real numbers and 18x is a fair price; value it on the single-quarter numbers and you will wrongly conclude the stock is absurdly cheap.
