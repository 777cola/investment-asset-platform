Masterclass Close Read (Duan Yongping): customers first, line items second — Netflix

This quarter's results prove two things: pricing holds, and content keeps coming.

Duan Yongping reads financial reports in a different order from everyone else: first, whether customers are being lost; second, whether price increases are meeting resistance; and only last, profit. Read in that order, the best news in the Q3 report is not net profit +44%, but the YoY decline in churn.

Profit can be managed; customers will not manage it along with you. Revenue of RMB 12.874 billion and double-digit growth in all four regions show customers are still there; an operating margin of 34.6% shows price increases met no resistance. Together, these two matter more than any profit figure.

Look at customers first: all four regions are growing, so no single market is carrying the business

Revenue by region: US & Canada RMB 5.628 billion (+11%), EMEA RMB 4.107 billion (+11%), Latin America RMB 1.603 billion (+17%), Asia-Pacific RMB 1.537 billion (+12%). All four regions grew double digits — in streaming, only Netflix can do that. Disney's streaming growth comes mainly from India and Southeast Asia, with Europe close to stagnant; Paramount relies on bundling.

Latin America grew fastest at +17%, but only +12% in constant currency — FX absorbed half the growth. Asia-Pacific is the mirror image: reported +12%, constant currency +15%, so local-currency growth runs ahead. The gap between the two says one thing — Netflix sets prices in local currency, so FX swings hit reported figures directly, yet its pricing power is local.

India merits separate mention: viewing hours +24% and record net paid additions, driven by local content and the Flipkart bundle. That points to a "low ARPU + high growth" path in low-price markets, rather than pricing every market like the US.

Pricing: a 34.6% operating margin is quantifiable proof of pricing power

Operating margin rose from 28.2% to 34.6%, up 6.4 percentage points in a year. That gain did not come from cost cutting—the layoffs were only announced in October, with the impact landing in Q4. It came from charging more for the same content.

This is exactly what Duan Yongping values most: pricing power. His point, in essence, was that being able to raise prices without losing customers shows the product is worth it to customers. Netflix raised prices once in 2026, churn fell YoY, and operating margin rose 6.4 percentage points—linked together, these three numbers form a complete chain of evidence for pricing power.

But one boundary condition needs stating clearly: the 34.6% is inflated by a "change in expense recognition timing"; the full-year guidance of 31.5% is the real level. The 101 basis point improvement (29.5% → 31.5%) is what is sustainable. Read the sustainable part of the financials, or you will be fooled by the quarterly numbers every year.

Finally, content investment: the one line that makes me uneasy

Free cash flow was RMB 2.884 billion, up only 8.4% YoY and below revenue growth of 11.8%. Where did the gap go? Content cash spending accelerated. That is consistent with the direction of sports rights investment: NFL and boxing rights are rigid costs — once signed, they must be paid.

Duan Yongping's test is: is this money maintenance spending or growth spending? Maintenance spending is money that must be spent — without it, customers leave. Growth spending can be stopped — stopping it only means no growth. Sports rights currently sit between the two: they reduce churn (maintenance) but can also bring in new users (growth).

My reading: sports is a trade of profit for retention. Q4 operating margin guidance is 26.0%, 8.6 percentage points below Q3, of which 1.2 points are severance costs and the rest is mainly seasonal content and marketing. Whether this trade pays off depends on whether churn can fall further in 2027 — if it does, the money was well spent; if not, it was wasted.

Conclusion

In Duan Yongping's framework, this print ticks all three boxes — "customers haven't left, prices can rise, investment is increasing" — though the third warrants watching. His verdict would likely be: a good business at a fair price, just don't expect the growth it once delivered. In practice: buy at a fair 18–22x P/E, but not as a growth stock.
