Write the buy case as five testable hypotheses

The case for buying Netflix cannot rest on "it's cheap"; it has to rest on five testable propositions. Having written all five out, I find three already supported by the 2026 data and two that can only be verified in 2027. Hence the position must be built in three tranches — not tranches by price, but tranches by evidence.

The five propositions: pricing power, advertising as a second growth curve, margins holding at 32%, sustainable buybacks, and a content input-output ratio that does not deteriorate. The first two drive valuation upside; the last three underpin downside protection. Falsify any one and the position must change.

Assumption one: pricing power — already proven, in three numbers

Prices rose in 2026, churn fell YoY and the operating margin climbed from 29.5% to 31.5%. Read together, the three form a complete chain of evidence: price hike → customers stayed → margin expansion.

Why does this matter? It determines where revenue growth comes from. Growth driven by new subscriber acquisition requires continuous traffic purchases and marketing spend, which drags on margin; growth driven by price hikes is almost pure profit. Of Netflix's current 11.8% growth, price hikes and advertising are the main contributors, with membership count contributing very little.

The risk is the ceiling on price hikes. The US standard tier already sits close to cable TV pricing, and further increases will run into a psychological threshold. The metric to watch is churn in the 3 months after a price hike — a jump of more than 1 percentage point from the current level would signal the ceiling has been reached.

Assumption 2: Advertising as the second growth curve — the most uncertain one

USD 2.7 billion, or 5.3% of revenue. The test is clear: above USD 3.5 billion by 2027 and it qualifies as a "second curve"; stuck at USD 2.7 billion and it is merely a "side dish."

Why does this determine valuation elasticity? Advertising revenue commands a far higher multiple than subscription revenue. Subscriptions grow linearly, are predictable and have a clear ceiling; advertising is cyclical, highly elastic and has a high ceiling. The market assigns pure advertising companies 25–30x P/E and subscription companies 15–20x.

Expanding into 15 new countries in March 2027 is the first hard milestone. If advertising revenue grows more than 15% QoQ in the two quarters after expansion, the assumption holds; below 8%, it can basically be judged a failure. At that point, the base case must be cut to the bear case, and the target price lowered from 79 to around 60.

Assumption 3: margins hold at 32% — the toughest test for 2027

2026 guidance is 31.5%, and 2027 must hold 32%, implying a further 0.5pp gain. That sounds easy, but three forces are dragging it down: rising sports rights costs, restructuring costs after layoffs, and narrowing room for price increases.

Q4 guidance of 26.0% is a warning. Although it includes 1.2 points of severance costs, part of the remaining 7.4 point sequential decline (from 34.6%) is structural — concentrated content launches, high marketing spend, sports rights amortization. If Q4 2027 is still 26%, the full year cannot hold 32%.

This assumption carries the highest weight because it affects both profit and narrative. A 2 point margin decline cuts EPS by 8%, and the market will reclassify the company as a "content company with runaway costs," compressing the valuation multiple. A double hit. So if this breaks, it is not a matter of trimming the position — it is a full exit.

Assumptions 4 and 5: buybacks and content efficiency underpin downside protection

The buyback thesis holds, with limits. Q3 buybacks totalled RMB 4.8 billion against RMB 22.3 billion of remaining authorization, with net debt of RMB 7.1 billion (0.44x operating profit). The limit: buybacks ran at 1.66x FCF, the gap funded by cash and debt. Watch net debt/operating profit; stop adding above 1x.

The content-efficiency thesis also holds. FCF growth of 8.4% trails revenue growth of 11.8%, pointing to accelerating content cash spending that remains under control; FCF/net income of 93% sits in the healthy 85%–100% range. Watch this ratio; below 80% would signal content investment is starting to erode cash flow.

Both are downside protection, not upside drivers. They won't push the stock up, but they will keep it from falling — because a company buying back RMB 19 billion a year with RMB 12 billion of FCF and 0.44x net debt will find strong buyers at USD 45.

Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar

Three falsification triggers: 1) 2027 revenue guidance of single-digit growth; 2) 2027 operating margin below 30%; 3) net debt/operating profit above 1x. If any one of the three materialises, the thesis is void and the action is to trim or exit entirely — regardless of the share price at the time.

Watch calendar: mid-to-late January 2027 Q4 results (check whether revenue is ≥ RMB 13.3 billion, whether Q4 operating margin is ≥26.0%, and the first sentence of 2027 guidance); March 2027 advertising expansion into 15 countries goes live; April 2027 Q1 results (check advertising revenue QoQ and churn rate); every quarter check buyback amount and net debt.

This calendar is more useful than a target price. A target price is a guess; a calendar waits for facts. In the end, investing earns money from "waiting for facts to materialise," not from "guessing the price right."

Conclusion

Of the five assumptions, three hold (price increases, buybacks, content efficiency) and two await verification (advertising, margins). The three that hold are what prevents us from losing money; the two awaiting verification determine how much we make. That structure explains the phased approach: buy the "not losing" part first, then add once the "how much we make" part delivers.
