Earnings deep dive: a clean beat on profit, a marked-down guide

2026Q3 was a tale of two halves. Profit was near flawless: operating profit of USD 4.459 billion, up 37.3% YoY, and an operating margin of 34.6%, 6.4 percentage points ahead of guidance; net profit came in at USD 3.664 billion, up 44% YoY. Guidance was cut on three fronts: full-year revenue now sits at the low end of the original range, advertising was trimmed from USD 3 billion to USD 2.7 billion, and free cash flow guidance was lowered from USD 12.5 billion to USD 12 billion.

The stock sold off on guidance, not on the earnings, and that distinction matters — if the earnings were the problem, the business is deteriorating; if guidance is the problem, expectations were simply too high. Sell the former; buy the latter.

Layer one: revenue mix — advertising accounted for just 20% of incremental revenue

Revenue of USD 12.874 billion, +11.8% YoY and +2.5% QoQ — the slowest growth in three years, though in line with prior company guidance. Breaking down the increment: subscription revenue rose about USD 1.06 billion YoY and advertising revenue about USD 260 million. Advertising thus contributed only two-tenths of the increment; the remaining eight-tenths came from the core business (member growth plus price increases).

All four regions posted double-digit growth: US and Canada USD 5.628 billion (+11%), EMEA USD 4.107 billion (+11%, +9% FX-neutral), Latin America USD 1.603 billion (+17%) and Asia-Pacific USD 1.537 billion (+12%, +15% FX-neutral). Latin America grew fastest but absorbed the heaviest FX drag (+17% vs. +12% FX-neutral); Asia-Pacific was the reverse, with FX a tailwind (+12% vs. +15%).

One notable structural shift: UCAN's share fell from 48% three years ago to 43.7%. Netflix's reliance on any single market is declining, and its exposure to FX and regulatory risk is more diversified. That is a positive, but it also shows that the US — its most profitable market — is nearing saturation in growth.

Layer two: margins. The 34.6% is inflated; 31.5% is the real number

Operating margin of 34.6% came in 6.4 percentage points above the 28.2% reported a year ago and ahead of company guidance. But that figure cannot be annualised. The report explicitly flags that part of the quarter's profit stemmed from "changes in the timing of expense recognition" — certain costs were pushed into Q4, an accounting timing effect rather than an efficiency gain.

Full-year guidance is the better read: 31.5%, 2 percentage points above 29.5% in 2025. That expansion is real, reflecting three drivers: price increases, a richer mix of high-margin advertising revenue and slower growth in content costs.

The pace is slowing, however. +2pct in 2026, and we expect only about +1pct in 2027. The reason is Q4 guidance of 26.0%, which carries two drags: layoff costs of about 1.2 percentage points and higher sports rights investment. Q4 is historically Netflix's lowest-margin quarter (content concentrated in the quarter, high marketing spend), so 26.0% is no surprise, but it caps the upside for the full year.

Layer three: costs and profit — how much of the 44% net profit growth is operational

Net profit RMB 3.664 billion, up 44% YoY; EPS 0.87, up 47.5% YoY (EPS growth outpacing net profit as buybacks reduced the share count). But 44% net profit growth far exceeds 37.3% operating profit growth, and the gap comes from non-operating items.

Breaking it down: operating profit RMB 4.459 billion against net profit RMB 3.664 billion, so net profit equals 82.2% of operating profit (rough calculation). Normally this ratio should be 75%–80% (after ~20% tax and a small amount of interest income). 82.2% implies a lighter tax burden or non-operating gains this quarter. Add in Q1's RMB 5.28 billion net profit (up 83% YoY), and part of 2026 earnings cannot be counted as "operating capability."

That is why the market assigns 18x rather than 21x. 18x is "cheap including one-off gains"; 21x is "a fair price for true operating profit." Write both clearly, or you are fooling yourself.

Layer 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — buybacks outpace earnings

Operating cash flow RMB 3.162 billion (+11.9%), free cash flow RMB 2.884 billion (+8.4%). FCF growth trailing revenue growth points to accelerating content cash spend—consistent with the direction of sports rights investment. Full-year guidance of RMB 12 billion equates to 4.4% of the RMB 270 billion market cap.

Balance sheet: RMB 15.3 billion debt, RMB 8.2 billion cash, RMB 7.1 billion net debt. Against RMB 16.1 billion of annual operating profit, that is 0.44x leverage—very clean. The trend bears watching, though: Netflix was once a net cash company, and with buybacks scaling up in 2026, cash is heading lower.

The most striking comparison: Q3 buybacks of RMB 4.8 billion versus FCF of only RMB 2.884 billion—buybacks at 1.66x FCF. The average buyback price of USD 75.12 also sits 14% above the current USD 65.85. Together these two facts suggest management sees intrinsic value above USD 75—a positive signal, but one that also implies the buyback pace won't accelerate on share price declines, since the authorization is a predetermined plan rather than an opportunistic one.

Conclusion

What this earnings report tells us: the profit line proves the quality of the business; the guidance exposes the growth bottleneck. A 34.6% operating margin, net profit +44% and FCF of RMB 2.884 billion are hard numbers; three guidance cuts and a Q4 margin of 26.0% are equally hard. The key is to separate the two: the business is not broken; expectations are. The right response is to buy, not to wait — but in tranches, because after this cut a second cut remains possible.
