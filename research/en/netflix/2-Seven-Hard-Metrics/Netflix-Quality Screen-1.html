Screening out the weak: this is not a name to be filtered out

Six of the seven hard metrics are met. The sole failure is "consecutive dividends" — Netflix has never paid one. It does, however, return free cash flow through buybacks: USD 4.8 billion in Q3 alone, or 63.9 million shares, which is a dividend in all but form. For shareholders, buybacks and dividends are mathematically equivalent — and tax-advantaged.

The other six are not marginal passes but clear ones: operating margin 31.5%, net margin 25.2%, revenue growth 13%, operating cash flow/net profit 86%, net debt just 0.44x operating profit, FCF yield 4.4%. On this combination, Netflix ranks in the top 10% of quality within the S&P 500. The seven hard metrics exist to eliminate quickly, and at this step there is no reason to eliminate Netflix.

The only item it fails: no dividend, but that is not a flaw

Netflix has not paid a cent of dividends in the two decades since its IPO, so a "consecutive dividend" screen would exclude it. But that metric exists to filter out companies that earn money without sharing it with shareholders, and Netflix returns cash through buybacks: USD 4.8 billion in Q3 alone across 63.9 million shares, a record, with USD 22.3 billion of authorization remaining, equal to 8.2% of market cap.

Mathematically, retired buybacks and cash dividends have the same effect on per-share value. The difference is tax: US investors owe tax immediately on dividends, whereas gains from buybacks can be deferred. For a company still in its growth phase with cash flow expanding rapidly, buybacks are the better distribution method. The "fail" should therefore be reinterpreted: not a failure to reward shareholders, but a switch in tool.

One caveat bears watching. Q3 free cash flow was USD 2.884 billion, while buybacks consumed USD 4.8 billion—1.66x the quarter's FCF. Annualised at that pace, 2026 buybacks would reach about USD 19 billion, against full-year FCF guidance of only USD 12 billion. Where does the gap come from? USD 8.2 billion of cash on the balance sheet, plus the debt market. This is not a crisis (net debt is only USD 7.1 billion), but it shows that buyback sustainability depends on two things: FCF not being revised down further, and interest rates not spiraling out of control. Hence the "not fatal but conditional" label.

Six more: not a squeaker, but a clear pass

The 31.5% operating margin stands out most among the seven. In media, Disney's operating margin has sat in the 10%–15% range for years, and the streaming businesses of Paramount and WBD have only just turned profitable. Netflix earns the entire industry's profit with a single company's P&L. Not because its content is cheaper, but because its user base is three times everyone else's, spreading fixed costs thinnest.

Read the 25.2% net margin alongside operating cash flow/net profit of 86%, and the profit is real. Some companies post high net margins but poor cash flow, propped up by receivables; Netflix users prepay monthly, so cash comes in faster than profit. Q3 net profit was 3.664 billion and operating cash flow 3.162 billion, the gap mainly cash outflow on content spend—the only place it spends before it earns, and the only weakness in this business.

Net debt/operating profit of 0.44x makes it almost a net cash company. With 15.3 billion of debt and 8.2 billion of cash against a business generating 12 billion of free cash flow a year, leverage is negligible. Revenue growth of 13% is the weakest of the seven—driven by price increases and advertising rather than membership growth, and likely to fall to around 10% in 2027, at which point it hugs the threshold.

What these results tell us: the issue is duration, not quality

The negative screen exists to rule out companies whose business model does not work. Netflix passes six of the seven criteria, and all six are clear passes, so business quality is not the risk here.The current share price issue is one of valuation, not quality—the two must be kept separate.

Where does the valuation problem come from? The market repricing the "growth ceiling." Q3 revenue growth of 11.8% was the lowest in three years, advertising revenue was cut from RMB 3 billion to RMB 2.7 billion, and Q4 operating margin guidance is 26.0% (dragged down 1.2 percentage points by severance costs). Taken together, these three data points led the market to conclude that "growth is over."

The seven hard metrics suggest that conclusion goes too far. A company with a 31.5% operating margin, FCF of RMB 12 billion, net debt of 0.44x and still doing large buybacks is a high-quality asset even if growth slows to 8%—it is simply no longer a growth stock.Buying it at a growth-stock valuation is wrong; buying it at a mature-media valuation is a bargain—today's 18x is the latter.

Conclusion

Six of seven criteria passed. The only failure—dividends—is replaced by buybacks, and buybacks exceed current-period free cash flow; that belongs on the tracking checklist rather than acting as a veto. Conclusion: the elimination-of-inferiors stage is passed, and the discussion can move to valuation and position sizing. If "FCF guidance cut again + buyback slowdown" occur simultaneously later, this "not fatal" assessment must be re-evaluated.
