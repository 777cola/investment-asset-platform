Last three months: it wasn't earnings that fell, it was expectations

Six things happened at Netflix between late July and October 23. Only three truly affect fundamentals: the October 13 organizational reshuffle, the October 20 Q3 earnings and guidance, and the programmatic expansion of the ad tech stack within Q3.

The other three are noise: the single-day 1.79% drop, the "advertising is collapsing" media headlines, and the USD 75 average buyback price. What the noise has in common is that none of it changes any future cash flow—it only changes the day's sentiment.

Signal one: Q3 results combine "profit above consensus, guidance cut"

This is the most important development of the past three months, and the only one that changes the valuation. Profit was near-perfect: operating margin 34.6% (6.4 percentage points above guidance), net profit RMB 3.664 billion (+44%), and a record RMB 4.8 billion buyback. Guidance was cut on three items: revenue landed at the low end of the range, advertising from RMB 3 billion to RMB 2.7 billion, and FCF from RMB 12.5 billion to RMB 12 billion.

The market chose to believe the guidance, not the profit. That choice is not wrong—guidance is forward-looking, profit is backward-looking. But the nature of the cuts matters: revenue was trimmed only at the low end of the range (near the midpoint of 51,000–51,400), advertising by 10%, and FCF by 4%. Two of the three are minor adjustments; one (advertising) is a substantive cut, but it accounts for just 5.3% of revenue.

My read: this is expectation-management guidance trimming, not fundamental deterioration. The evidence: in the same earnings release, the company moved its 2027 advertising country-expansion plan from "in planning" to "15 countries live by March 2027"—if fundamentals were deteriorating, it would not make expansion commitments.

Signal two: the October 13 organizational reshuffle, with the cost falling in 2028

Cutting about 6% of staff, close to 1,000 people, the largest since 2022. In the same round, content chief Bela Bajaria was replaced and film head Dan Lin departed. Three events in the same week show this is not routine optimization.

Why call it a signal rather than noise? Because what it changes is "what content can be produced in the future," not "how much was spent this quarter." The content business has an 18-month lag: a change of people in October 2026 affects the 2027 slate, and only in 2028 will churn speak.

The market barely reacted to this (the same-day close was not obtained, but the stock was already at lows before earnings). That is precisely why it is worth noting — risk that is not priced is the real risk.

Signal 3: Catching up on the ad tech stack is the only good news

In Q3, the Netflix Ads Suite expanded programmatic buying, connecting to external demand-side platforms such as MercadoLibre and AdForm. The significance: Netflix has finally begun selling ads the industry-standard way.

Previously, its ads were sold mainly direct through its in-house sales team, which was inefficient and narrow in reach. Programmatic access means it can plug into advertisers' existing buying systems, no longer requiring advertisers to "set up a dedicated process for Netflix." That is the foundation for volume.

But it is also one reason for the ad guidance cut — in the early days of a new system, delivery capacity lags commitments. This is what the company meant by "some ad product features rolling out slower than planned." So this is both good news and bad news: a near-term drag on revenue recognition, a long-term opening of the ceiling.

Three noise items: why they should not affect judgment

Noise 1: The 1.79% drop on October 23. A single-day decline of this size is normal at this volatility (Netflix moves about 2% on an average day). Price alone is not an event unless it comes with new information. There was no new information that day.

Noise 2: The "advertising collapsed" headlines. RMB 2.7 billion versus RMB 3 billion, a 10% cut, equal to 5.3% of revenue. Framing this as "the second curve failed" is clickbait logic, not analytical logic. The real test is whether ad revenue can return above RMB 3.5 billion after the country expansion in March 2027.

Noise 3: The average buyback price of USD 75 is above the current price. Some read this as "management overpaid." That is wrong — buybacks are a long-term action, and the right yardstick is "purchase price below intrinsic value," not "below the market price three months later." If intrinsic value is USD 79 (base case), buying back stock at USD 75 is a good deal.

Conclusion

Six events in three months: three signals, three noise items. Signals share one trait—they change future cash flow. Noise shares another—it only changes same-day sentiment. String the signals together and a clear picture emerges: profit still growing, guidance being cut, organization shifting gears, advertising catching up. Together these four phrases point to one action: buy, not wait.
