## Signal one: Q3 results pair an earnings beat with a guidance cut

This is the most important development of the past three months and the only one that changes the valuation. Profit was near-perfect: operating margin of 34.6% (6.4pp above guidance), net profit of RMB 3.664 billion (+44%) and a record RMB 4.8 billion buyback. Guidance was cut on three items: revenue landed at the low end of the range, advertising from RMB 3 billion to RMB 2.7 billion, and FCF from RMB 12.5 billion to RMB 12 billion.

The market chose to believe the guidance, not the profit—and that choice is not wrong: guidance is forward-looking, profit is backward-looking. But the nature of the cuts matters: revenue was trimmed only at the low end of the range (near the midpoint of 51,000–51,400), advertising by 10% and FCF by 4%. Two of the three are minor adjustments; one (advertising) is a substantive cut, but it accounts for just 5.3% of revenue.

My read: this is expectation-management guidance trimming, not fundamental deterioration. The evidence: in the same earnings release, the company moved its 2027 advertising country-expansion plan from "in planning" to "15 countries live by March 2027"—if fundamentals were deteriorating, it would not make expansion commitments.

## Signal two: the 13 October reorganisation, with the cost landing in 2028

Netflix is cutting roughly 6% of staff — close to 1,000 people — the largest reduction since 2022. Content chief Bela Bajaria was replaced in the same round, and film head Dan Lin has departed. Three events in one week signal this is no routine optimisation.

Why treat it as signal rather than noise? Because what it changes is what content can be produced in the future, not how much was spent this quarter. Content carries an 18-month lag: a change of personnel in October 2026 feeds into the 2027 slate, and only in 2028 will churn show up in the numbers.

The market barely reacted (the same-day close was not obtained, but the stock was already at lows before earnings). That is exactly why it matters — risk that is not priced is the real risk.

## Signal 3: Ad tech catch-up is the one bright spot

In Q3, the Netflix Ads Suite widened programmatic buying, connecting to external demand-side platforms including MercadoLibre and AdForm. The significance: Netflix has finally begun selling ads the industry-standard way.

Previously its ads were sold mainly direct through the in-house sales team — inefficient, with narrow reach. Programmatic access lets Netflix plug into advertisers' existing buying systems, so advertisers no longer need to "set up a dedicated process for Netflix." That is the foundation for volume.

It is also one reason for the ad guidance cut: in the early days of a new system, delivery capacity lags commitments. That is what the company meant by "some ad product features rolling out slower than planned." So the news cuts both ways — a near-term drag on revenue recognition, a long-term opening of the ceiling.

## Three noise items: why they should not affect judgment

Noise 1: the 1.79% drop on October 23. A single-day move of that size is unremarkable at this volatility — Netflix moves about 2% on an average day. Price alone is not an event unless it comes with new information. There was none that day.

Noise 2: the "advertising collapsed" headlines. RMB 2.7 billion versus RMB 3 billion — a 10% cut, equal to 5.3% of revenue. Calling this "the second curve failed" is clickbait logic, not analysis. The real test is whether ad revenue can recover above RMB 3.5 billion after the country expansion in March 2027.

Noise 3: the average buyback price of USD 75 sits above the current price. Some read this as "management overpaid." That is wrong: buybacks are a long-term action, and the right yardstick is "purchase price below intrinsic value," not "below the market price three months later." If intrinsic value is USD 79 (base case), buying back stock at USD 75 is a good deal.
