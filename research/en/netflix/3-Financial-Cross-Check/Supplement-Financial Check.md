## Three measurement bases must be clarified first, otherwise the numbers are meaningless

First, the stock split. Netflix's board approved a 10-for-1 stock split on 2025/10/30, with a record date of 2025/11/10 and trading at post-split prices from the open on 2025/11/17. So every per-share figure in this report—the USD 65.85 share price, diluted EPS of USD 0.87, the 52-week high of USD 109.35, the USD 75 average buyback price—is on a post-split basis. For pre-split equivalent prices, multiply by 10: share price USD 658.5, 52-week high USD 1,093.5. The USD 0.59 EPS for 2025Q3 is the restated, comparable-basis figure from the financials, not the originally reported value. This is the easiest place to go wrong: much historical material still sits on a pre-split basis, and comparing the two produces the absurd conclusion that the stock "fell 90%".

Second, the one-off gain. 2026Q1 net income was USD 5.28 billion, up 83% YoY, while revenue grew only 16%. Profit growth at five times revenue growth can only come from non-operating items. The earnings summary does not break out the composition, so this report does not give a specific amount for this gain and instead runs two bases: 18x PE (including the one-off gain) and 21x PE (normalized, excluding it). Wherever valuation is involved, use the 21x tier; where the question is "what price is the market assigning", use 18x.

Third, how advertising revenue is recognized. The USD 2.7 billion is "revenue recognized for the full year", not "advertiser commitments" (bookings). In the ad industry these two numbers often differ by more than 20%, because commitments can be recognized across periods and can be cancelled. When the company cut guidance, it cited "some ad product features rolling out slower than planned", pointing to inventory delivery capacity, not a collapse in demand—this distinction determines whether the cut is an "execution problem" or a "demand problem"; the former is fixable, the latter is not.

## The one item with a deviation above 1%, and why it does not matter

EPS 0.87 versus 0.86, a 1.2% deviation — the only one of the eight items above the line. The reason is simple: net income of RMB 3.664 billion ÷ 4.211 billion diluted shares = USD 0.8700, so rounding to either 0.87 or 0.86 is defensible for the media. This is a printing-precision issue, not a data-quality issue. The approach is to use the reported figure of 0.87 and retain two decimal places in all EPS-derived calculations to avoid error accumulation.

A cross-check: RMB 3.664 billion net income ÷ USD 0.87 EPS = 4.211 billion diluted shares, 100 million shares more than the 4.11 billion total shares. This gap is the time-weighted effect of buybacks during Q3 — the 639,000 shares repurchased occurred at different points within the quarter, and diluted shares use a weighted average. The two figures reconcile, confirming the financial statements are internally consistent.

Conversely, if EPS were 0.86, implied shares would be 4.26 billion, 150 million above total shares — a deviation too large to explain. So 0.87 is the only self-consistent value. This kind of cross-check is more useful than dual-source comparison: it validates the internal logic of the financial statements, not whether two media outlets copied correctly.

## The finally adopted methodology

This report series uniformly adopts four conventions: share price and per-share data use the post-split basis; profit uses reported financial statement figures; valuation uses normalized PE; market cap uses USD 65.85 × 4.11 billion shares = USD 270.6 billion (written as "about 270 billion").

There is one figure I choose not to provide: total content spending. The earnings summary does not separately disclose cash content spending for 2026Q3; only the change in "content assets" can be used to back it out, and the error is too large. Whenever this report touches on content costs, it uses qualitative language throughout ("content spending is the largest cash outflow item") without giving a specific amount. Better to be one number short than to give a number I cannot verify myself.

Another is subscriber count. The company now discloses "audience reach" and revenue by region; the precise number of paid members has not been reported quarterly since 2026. So this report says "reaching nearly 1 billion viewers" rather than "300 million paid members." A comparison with inconsistent definitions is worse than no comparison at all.
