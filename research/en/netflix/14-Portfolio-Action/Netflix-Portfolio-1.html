Position in the portfolio: a "defensive growth" holding

Netflix is not the offensive holding here; it is "defensive growth", there to support cash flow when tech sells off broadly rather than to lead in a bull market. The position should therefore stay light, with 6% as the cap.

Tranche pacing: build 2% at the current price of 65.85, add to 4% below USD 55, and fill out to 6% near USD 45. The three tiers map to three kinds of evidence: buy "business quality" at the current price, "valuation safety" at 55, and "pessimism already priced" at 45.

Why the cap is 6%, not 10%

The single-name cap is usually 10%, but Netflix is sized at a 30% haircut to that. One reason alone explains it: management scores 6 out of 10. Three organisational changes in a year, with the content chief and film head both leaving at once — the cost of that only surfaces 18 months later. Risks you cannot see clearly must be covered by position size, not by judgment.

Correlation is a second reason. Netflix and the portfolio's tech names (especially ad-driven Meta and Alphabet) move together on the ad budget cycle, so holding both amplifies the same risk. With 6% in Netflix plus the existing tech positions, actual ad-cycle exposure may already exceed 15%.

Third, the payoff structure is "downside 32%, upside 20% (base case)." That profile does not suit a heavy position; it suits a medium position plus adding on dips. A heavy position requires upside far in excess of downside, and that is not the case here.

How is the add-position price level calculated?

USD 55 equates to 15x normalized P/E (55 ÷ 3.66). 15x marks the absolute floor of Netflix's P/E range since 2023, a level touched only briefly during the 2022 net subscriber decline. At USD 55, the market has already priced in growth slowing to 8%.

USD 45 corresponds to the bear case (EPS 3.0 × 15x). This price implies three things at once: advertising fails outright, margins roll over and growth slows to 8%. I put the probability at 25%, but if it gets there the payoff becomes 10% downside against 75% upside — worth going all in.

No mechanical averaging between the two levels. If the stock falls to 55 because earnings blow up (say, revenue below 13.3 billion), that is not an opportunity — it is evidence. The precondition for adding is fundamentals intact, not price reached.

Set a thesis stop, not a price stop

Do not use a price stop on this stock. Volatility is simply too high: down 29% year to date and 39.8% from its high, so any 15% price stop would be triggered repeatedly. A stop-loss exists to control losses, not to control volatility.

The right approach is a thesis stop: of the five assumptions written at purchase, if any one is disproven, trim the position. Single-digit revenue growth guidance for 2027, for example, would break the assumption that "price increases plus advertising sustain double-digit growth", warranting a cut to 2%; a 2027 operating margin below 30% would break "costs are controllable", warranting a full exit.

The cost of this method is slow reaction — earnings come four times a year and events a few times a year, so stops always lag. But it avoids the risk of being washed out by noise, and that risk does greater damage to long-term holding.

Calculate the three exposures separately to gauge correlation with other holdings

Advertising-cycle exposure. Moves with Meta and Alphabet: strong advertiser budgets lift all three, weak budgets drag all three. Advertising is just 5.3% of Netflix's business, so the exposure is small, but the direction is the same.

Content and IP-cycle exposure. More often inverse than aligned with Disney, since the two compete for the same users' subscription fees. Holding Disney alongside amounts to running a hedge, but neither side makes money.

Rate exposure. Negatively correlated. Netflix's valuation rests on discounted cash flow, so rising rates compress the multiple. Its debt is fixed-rate, locking in interest costs, so rising rates barely affect profit but hit valuation hard. If the portfolio already holds a lot of long-duration assets (e.g., high-multiple software stocks), adding Netflix increases rate sensitivity.

Conclusion

Positioning: cap 6%, initial position 2%, add to 4% at USD 55, fill to full at USD 45. No price stop; three thesis stop lines. The stock's role is a defensive cash-flow position, not an offensive one — if tech growth already accounts for more than 30% of your portfolio, Netflix adds limited marginal diversification and the position should be smaller.
