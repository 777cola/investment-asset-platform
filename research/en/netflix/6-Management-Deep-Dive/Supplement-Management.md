## Capital allocation record: management's strongest card

Lining up capital allocation since 2024 reveals a clear discipline: **no M&A, no dividend, buybacks only.** Abandoning the USD 82.7 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery in February 2026 is the most telling decision—had that deal gone through, Netflix would have taken on over USD 80 billion in debt and a clutch of declining cable TV channels.

Buyback intensity has stepped up year by year. Q3 alone saw USD 4.8 billion and 63.9 million shares at an average price of about USD 75 (rough calculation), a record; remaining authorization of USD 22.3 billion equals 8.2% of market cap. **Buying back at this scale on 18x PE is management publicly stating "we think we're cheap."**

One nit to pick: **Q3 buybacks were USD 4.8 billion, while quarterly FCF was only USD 2.884 billion.** The gap was covered with balance-sheet cash and debt. The result of doing this repeatedly is rising net debt—now USD 7.1 billion, 0.44x operating profit, still safe; in three years it could reach USD 28 billion. Capital allocation discipline has a gap here: **it treats buybacks as routine spending rather than an opportunistic move made only when cheap.**

## Organizational turmoil: three moves in a year, with the cost only surfacing after 18 months

On October 13, 2026, the two co-CEOs sent an internal letter announcing layoffs of about 6%, or close to 1,000 people, the largest since 2022. In the same round of changes, Chief Content Officer Bela Bajaria was replaced and film division head Dan Lin departed. **Content acquisition and film production are Netflix's two main arteries, and both changed hands within a year.**

Further back, the company abandoned the WBD acquisition in February 2026; before that, a 10-for-1 stock split in November 2025 (a neutral move in itself, but typically signals a company wants to broaden its shareholder base and stabilize its share price). Taken together, the three events form a chain: "growth deceleration → efficiency push → organizational change."

The problem is the lag in content. A series takes 12–18 months from greenlight to launch, so replacing the content chief means the 2027 slate will be affected, and a thinner slate will only show up in churn in 2028. **In other words, the cost of this October 2026 decision can only be verified in 2028.** This is the hardest kind of risk for investors to handle: not invisible, but visible and impossible to disprove in the short term.

## Integrity and governance: clean, but with one structural flaw

The integrity record shows no hard flaws: no financial restatements, no SEC investigations, no related-party scandals. In the 2022 net subscriber decline, management directly acknowledged in the earnings report that "our content supply had a problem" rather than blaming the macro environment—**this kind of candor is uncommon among US-listed companies.**

There is one flaw in the governance structure: **the dual-CEO arrangement.** Sarandos runs content, Peters runs product and technology, and both report jointly to the board. The upside is clear division of labor and fast decisions; the downside is that when the two disagree, there is no clear arbitration mechanism. In the October 2026 organizational reshuffle, outsiders cannot see who led it—**this kind of "unclear accountability" is not a problem in good times, but becomes one in bad times.**

Another detail: Reed Hastings remains in place as executive chairman. The founder's influence on company culture is positive (the Netflix culture deck is a Silicon Valley classic), but it also means the professional managers' decision space is compressed. **The succession question still has no answer in 2026—both co-CEOs are over 50, and the chairman is 65.**

## Score for management: 6/10 — where points were deducted, and how to add them back

A score of 6 does not mean a passing grade; it means "capability is fine, but execution is noisy." There are three positives: **capital allocation discipline (rejecting the RMB 82.7 billion acquisition), candid communication (owning up to mistakes in 2022), and strategic continuity (both the advertising and sports lines were set in motion three years ago).**

There are two negatives: **organizational churn (three rounds of action in one year) and unclear succession (dual CEO plus a founder still in place, with no clear succession path).** Neither affects current performance, but both affect certainty after 2028.

The path to a higher score is clear: hold the operating margin at 32% in 2027, showing the layoffs cut redundancy; and keep the 2027 slate from visibly thinning, showing the long-content leadership change did not damage capacity.** Achieve both, and the score can return to 8; fail, and it drops to 4 — at which point the rating must change accordingly.**
