Part Four: The Financial Truth — Profit Is Not the Most Important Line in the Three Statements — Netflix

Net profit of RMB 3.664 billion is an accounting figure; free cash flow of RMB 12 billion is the fact

Profit can be arranged; cash flow cannot

A financial report has three statements, but with Netflix you only need to watch two things: whether free cash flow is real, and whether buybacks are borrowing from the future. The income statement tells you whether the business is good, but not where the money is.

2026Q3 figures: revenue 12.874 billion, operating profit 4.459 billion, net income 3.664 billion, operating cash flow 3.162 billion, free cash flow 2.884 billion. From operating profit to free cash flow, it fell 1.575 billion along the way — that 1.575 billion is the cost of sustaining this business.

First: content accounting, the profit adjustment valve

Netflix capitalises content spending and amortises it over several years based on viewing. So "how much it spends buying content this year" and "how much cost it deducts this year" are two different things. The gap between the two is the room to adjust profit.

How do you tell whether it has been adjusted? Compare two numbers: content cash spending vs content amortisation. If cash spending runs above amortisation for an extended period, it is accelerating content purchases (profit is overstated); the reverse means it is building up inventory (profit is understated). At Netflix the two now roughly match, with FCF/net income at 93%, a healthy range.

Q3 offers a counterexample: net income of RMB 3.664 billion > operating cash flow of RMB 3.162 billion. This is a quarterly timing effect, not a trend. Verify it on a full-year basis — 2026 FCF of RMB 12 billion ÷ normalised net income of RMB 12.9 billion = 93%, no problem. Only if this ratio stays below 80% for two consecutive years is it a real problem.

Second point: buybacks, the most misread line item

Q3 buybacks: RMB 4.8 billion, 63.9 million shares, average price USD 75.12. The key number is the average price — 14% above the current share price, which some read as management overpaying.

That judgment is wrong. A buyback should be judged on "purchase price < intrinsic value," not "purchase price < market price three months later." On a base-case intrinsic value of USD 79, buying at USD 75 is a good deal.

Scale is the real concern: the buyback is 1.66x FCF, with the gap funded by cash and debt. Annualised at USD 19 billion against FCF of USD 12 billion, that adds roughly USD 7 billion of net debt a year. Net debt stands at USD 7.1 billion now (0.44x operating profit) and reaches USD 28 billion in three years (1.7x). That trajectory becomes a problem when rates rise — the one thing in this report that keeps me up at night.

The third point: one-off gains split the PE into two

2026Q1 net profit was USD 5.28 billion, up 83% YoY, on revenue growth of just 16%.Profit growing five times faster than revenue can only come from non-operating items.The earnings summary does not break out the composition, so this report does not quantify them.

We take a dual-caliber approach: 18x P/E on reported earnings including one-off gains, as the market commonly uses, and 21x P/E normalized to exclude them.Use 21x for valuation and 18x to judge "what price the market is paying."

The distinction matters in practice: at 18x the stock looks "absurdly cheap"; at 21x it looks "reasonably undervalued."The two conclusions imply two different position sizes—which is why the caliber must be stated clearly.Our conclusion (buy in tranches) rests on 21x, not 18x.

Fourth, break the guidance down by quarter and examine the 2027 base

Full-year RMB 51.1 billion less Q4 guidance of RMB 13.442 billion leaves RMB 37.658 billion for the first three quarters, with Q3 at 25.2% of the full year. A 1.5% shortfall in Q4 alone cuts USD 30 million from the full year — no impact on valuation, but it shifts the 2027 base.

Rolling forward from RMB 51.1 billion to 2027: 10% growth gives RMB 56.2 billion, a 32% operating margin gives RMB 18 billion of operating profit, and after tax and interest net income is about RMB 14 billion (rough calculation), or USD 3.6 EPS on 3.9 billion shares — exactly the neutral-case assumption.

The point of this exercise: the neutral case needs no upside surprise, only guidance delivered and buybacks continued. The market is currently pricing in "guidance will not be delivered." The gap between the two is the odds on this investment.

Conclusion

Reading the three statements together: profit is real (FCF/net profit 93%), buybacks are aggressive (1.66x FCF), and one-off gains need to be stripped out (18x vs 21x). On a true profitability basis, revenue is RMB 51.1 billion, operating margin 31.5%, FCF RMB 12 billion and net debt RMB 7.1 billion. On these numbers, 18x is a fair price.
