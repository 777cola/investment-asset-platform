Part Seven: Risk Checklist — Five Risks, Two Already Unfolding — Netflix

Turn "what to fear" into a scoreable table

The biggest risk is not what has already happened but what has yet to be priced in.

Five risks: advertising failure, loss of time spent, sports rights inflation, organisational turmoil, and buybacks funded with leverage.The first two are already playing out and partly priced in; the last three are not yet part of the market debate.

The aim of this list is not to leave you more fearful after reading it, but to map each risk to an observable indicator. Risks cannot be managed; indicators can.

Two already happening: advertising and time spent

Advertising budget cut from RMB 3 billion to RMB 2.7 billion, a 10% reduction. The absolute number is small at 5.3% of revenue, but the signal matters: if the "second growth curve" still fails to scale in year three, the market will reclassify the company from "advertising platform" to "subscription business"—and the valuation multiple gap between the two is over 5x. Half of this is already priced in: the de-rating from 45x to 18x is exactly that.

Watch-time share of 7.8% versus YouTube's 14%, and the gap is widening. This is structural and irreversible. The impact is not a revenue decline but a growth ceiling fixed at 8%–12%. The market prices this risk by refusing to value the stock as a growth company, and rightly so.

Both risks are already reflected in the 18x P/E. They are therefore not a reason to sell, only a reason not to expect a 30x P/E.

Three not yet priced in: sports, organization, leverage

Sports rights inflation. For rights such as the NFL and boxing, the leagues are the sellers, supply is monopolistic and prices are renegotiated annually. Sports content reduces churn, but it is a rigid cost and builds no long-term IP. If investment keeps rising while subscriber growth fails to keep pace, the operating margin will fall from 31.5% back to 28%–29% — that was the 2022 script.

Organizational turmoil. The October 2026 restructuring will take 18 months for its cost to show through. The market has barely reacted, because the impact is not in this quarter's earnings — it is in the 2028 churn rate.

Buybacks plus leverage. Net debt is currently RMB 7.1 billion, 0.44x operating profit — very safe. But with RMB 19 billion of annualized buybacks against RMB 12 billion of FCF, net debt rises by about RMB 7 billion a year, reaching RMB 28 billion and 1.7x in three years. If revenue declines hit then, buybacks must stop, and a halt to buybacks is itself a second blow to the share price.

Worst case: how we derive the USD 45 and what probability we attach to it

Bear case: 2027 revenue growth of 8%, advertising stuck at USD 2.7 billion, operating margin of 30%, EPS of USD 3.0, 15x P/E = USD 45. Three bad things happen at once: growth drops, advertising dies, margins fall.

The precedent is 2022, when membership fell on a net basis for the first time and the stock went from USD 700 (pre-split) to USD 165, a 76% decline. So USD 45 is not alarmism. Two things are different this time: in 2022 there was no advertising, no sports and no buyback authorization; now there are all three. And in 2022 the valuation started at 45x; now it is 18x — the damage from a high-valuation collapse is far greater than that from earnings misses at a low valuation.

I assign a 25% probability to the bear case, 55% to the base case and 20% to the bull case. Expected value is USD 76.1, +16% versus the current price. That expected value is not high, but it rests on the premise that «no good news is needed».

Correlation between risks: will they occur together

The five risks are not independent. If revenue growth falls to 8%, management will most likely raise content spending to rescue growth, and margins will fall in turn; with margins down, there is less money for buybacks, and the leverage problem follows. Three risks chain into one.

Conversely, if advertising succeeds, the chain never starts: advertising brings incremental revenue → growth holds → no need to raise content spending → margins hold → buybacks continue. So the five risks are really one risk: whether advertising succeeds.

This changes how to monitor the story. No need to watch five metrics at once; one is enough: quarter-over-quarter growth in advertising revenue. Above 12%, none of the other risks trigger; below 5%, the other risks appear in sequence. This is the single most memorable metric in the entire report.

Conclusion

Five risks: two are priced in (advertising, time spent) and three are not (sports, organisation, leverage). Priced-in risks are no reason to sell; unpriced risks are a reason not to hold a heavy position. Hence the 6% cap and the decision to build the position in tranches rather than all at once.
