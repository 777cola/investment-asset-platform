Part Eight: Decision Framework — Position Sizing, Entry Price and Exit Conditions — Netflix

Turn judgment into three executable one-pagers
Research date 2026/10/23  ·  Price $65.85 (-1.79%)  ·  Market cap About USD 270 billion  ·  PE(2026E) ~18x (including one-off gains, ~21x excluding them)

A framework earns its keep by being fixed in advance, not by explaining things after the fact.

The previous seven pieces were analysis; this one is execution. Analysis can be fuzzy; execution must be specific: how much to buy, at what price, under what conditions to sell, and how to verify.

The framework boils down to one line: buy the thesis, not the price. Build the position in three tranches, each tied to one type of evidence; set three lines for trimming, each tied to one thesis being disproven; and do not watch the tape throughout.

First sheet: position sizing — management score sets the cap

Position sizing is not guesswork; it is the output of "certainty × payoff." Netflix's certainty is moderate (management scores 6, duration risk unresolved) and its payoff is moderate (downside 32%, upside 20%–62%). Both are moderate, so the position should be moderate—6% is a reasonable level, not a conservative one.

Weight "cash flow certainty" more heavily and disregard growth, and you can go up to 8%; if tech already dominates your portfolio, cut it to 4%. 6% is the baseline, not dogma.

The tranching cadence maps to three types of evidence: buy "business quality" at the current price (already delivered), buy "valuation safety" at USD 55 (wait for the market to offer it), buy "pessimism already priced" at USD 45 (wait for panic). Each tranche carries an explicit "why buy now," rather than "buy because it fell."

Second sheet: sell discipline — three lines, not price-based

Trim on events: ①2027 revenue guidance of single-digit growth; ②2027 operating margin falling below 30%; ③net debt/operating profit exceeding 1x.

Why not a price stop-loss? Because volatility is too high: the stock is down 29% year-to-date and 39.8% from its high, so any 15% price stop would be triggered repeatedly. A stop-loss is there to cap losses, not to cap volatility.

Why these three? They map to the three core assumptions behind the purchase: growth holds up (revenue), costs stay controlled (margin), capital allocation stays disciplined (leverage). If any one breaks, the buy case no longer holds, regardless of the share price.

The third sheet: how to verify — one calendar beats a hundred models

Verification calendar: mid-to-late January 2027 Q4 results (revenue ≥ RMB 13.3 billion; Q4 operating margin ≥26.0%; the first line of 2027 guidance); March 2027 ads rollout to 15 countries; April 2027 Q1 results (advertising QoQ, churn); each quarter buyback and net debt.

This calendar is more useful than a target price. A target price is a guess; a calendar waits for facts. Investing ultimately pays for "waiting for facts to materialise," not for "guessing the price right."

One last reminder: don't add to your position the day before earnings. Netflix's single-quarter earnings volatility often exceeds 8%, and betting when information asymmetry is greatest is the worst possible timing. Scaling in exists precisely to spread "bet timing" across different information states.

Three common mistakes: the errors buyers most often make

Mistake one: valuing it on a "streaming disruptor" framework. That leads to buying at 45x and refusing to buy at 18x. The right framework is "mature content cash-flow asset" — 18–22x.

Mistake two: using price stops. With annual volatility above 40%, a 15% stop gets triggered repeatedly and the end result is being "shaken out at the bottom." The right approach is event-based stops: three triggers, and at most one can fire in a year.

Mistake three: treating an "advertising cut" as "the business deteriorating." RMB 2.7 billion is just 5.3% of revenue; it affects valuation, not profit. Confusing valuation issues with quality issues is the most common cognitive error on this stock.

Conclusion

The framework has three pages: how much to buy (6%, in three tranches), under what conditions to sell (three event lines), and how to verify (four time nodes). Everything is fixed in advance; afterwards only one thing is done — a check against the plan. This is the complete execution version of "buy, in tranches."
