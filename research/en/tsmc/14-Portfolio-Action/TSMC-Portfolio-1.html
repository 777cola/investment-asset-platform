I. Why the cap is 12%

Two constraints bind. The first is concentration: TSMC's correlation with Nvidia and ASML is 0.7–0.9, so three 10% positions are effectively a 30% bet on a single macro variable (AI capex). The second is geopolitics: to cap single-region tail risk, no single holding should exceed 15%.

12% sits at the intersection of the two. Below 8%, the contribution is too small to matter (at 30% annualised volatility, an 8% position contributes 2.4% of portfolio volatility, just enough to register); above 12%, a single-day black swan event would impair the principal structure.

II. Three price levels for adding to positions

First tranche at RMB 2,000 (19x 2026, 16x 2027), building to 6%. At this level the market begins to discount cyclical risk and risk-reward improves from 1:3 to 1:1.3.

Second tranche at RMB 1,800 (14x 2027), building to 10% — the lower edge of the 2015–2025 P/E midpoint, a level historically seen only in downturns. Third tranche below RMB 1,600 (13x 2027), building to the 12% cap; this tranche requires confirmation that gross margin has already fallen to around 58%, otherwise it risks catching a falling knife.

III. Trimming and stop-losses: let the thesis, not the price, drive the decision

TSMC is ill-suited to price-based stop-losses: its trading range is inherently wide (52-week high of 2,510; low not disclosed, though historical annual amplitude often exceeds 40%), and a price stop would shake you out of the position. A thesis-based stop is more reliable — quarterly gross margin falling below 60% (confirming a structural downward shift), or monthly revenue turning negative MoM for two consecutive months while capex continues to be raised (a precursor to overcapacity).

The probability of both conditions being met at once is not high, but once they are, the valuation centre will shift down by more than 20%, and selling at that point is more rational than holding on stubbornly. The point of discipline is not to make money every time, but to lose less when you are wrong.

IV. Rebalancing pace

Rebalance quarterly, in sync with the earnings call. Rule: if the position exceeds the upper limit by 2 percentage points (e.g., rises to 14%), trim it back to 12%; if it falls 2 percentage points below the lower limit (e.g., drops to 6%) and the thesis is intact, top it up to 8%. No monthly fine-tuning, to avoid trading costs and emotional interference.

Dividend treatment: RMB 24 per share in 2026, a 1.0% dividend yield; reinvest rather than withdraw. TSMC's compounding comes mainly from capital appreciation; dividends are just a rounding error.

V. Stress test: what if AI capex is halved

Assume the four major North American cloud providers cut capex 30% YoY in 2027, TSMC's revenue growth slows to -5%, gross margin returns to 53% and EPS comes in at about RMB 78. At 16x — a typical downcycle multiple — the stock is worth RMB 1,248, 48% below the current price. This is a stress test, not a forecast.

In that scenario a 12% position would cut portfolio returns by 5.8pp. Is that tolerable? It depends on what else is in the portfolio: if you also hold Nvidia and ASML, the correlation across the three would amplify total losses to 12%–15%. This is why AI exposure must be sized on a combined basis, not by looking at the position size of a single name.

Conclusion: the 12% cap is the cap for "can withstand a halving." If your tolerance for volatility is below 20%, the cap should drop to 8%.

Conclusion

Conclusion: core position 8%–12%; no adds or trims at the current price.Add in three tranches at RMB 2,000 / 1,800 / 1,600; trim on thesis, not on price. Given the high correlation with Nvidia and ASML, AI exposure must be aggregated — otherwise the book looks diversified while it is in fact concentrated.
