## I. Why the cap is 12%

Two constraints. The first is concentration: TSMC's correlation with Nvidia and ASML is 0.7–0.9. If each of the three is 10% of the portfolio, that is effectively 30% bet on the same macro variable (AI capex). The second is geopolitics: for single-region tail risk, no single holding should exceed 15%.

12% is the intersection of these two constraints. Below 8%, the contribution to the portfolio is insufficient (for a name with 30% annualized volatility, an 8% position contributes 2.4% of portfolio volatility, just enough to register); above 12%, a single-day black swan event would damage the principal structure.

## II. Three price levels for adding to positions

First tranche at RMB 2,000 (19x 2026, 16x 2027), build to 6%. At this price the market starts discounting cyclical risk, and the risk-reward improves from 1:3 to 1:1.3.

Second tranche at RMB 1,800 (14x 2027), build to 10%. This is the lower edge of the 2015–2025 PE midpoint, seen historically only in downturns. Third tranche below RMB 1,600 (13x 2027), build to the 12% cap — this tranche requires confirmation that gross margin has already fallen to around 58%, otherwise it risks catching a falling knife.

## III. Trimming and stop-losses: use the thesis, not the price

A company like TSMC is not suited to price-based stop-losses — its trading range is inherently wide (52-week high of 2,510, low not given, but historical annual amplitude often exceeds 40%), and a price stop would shake you out. A thesis-based stop is more reliable: quarterly gross margin falling below 60% (confirming a structural downward shift), or monthly revenue turning negative month-on-month for two consecutive months while capex continues to be raised (a precursor to overcapacity).

The probability of both conditions being met at once is not high, but once they are, the valuation center will shift down by more than 20%, and selling at that point is more rational than holding on stubbornly. **The point of discipline is not to make money every time, but to lose less when you are wrong.**

## IV. Rebalancing pace

Rebalance quarterly, in sync with the earnings call. Rule: if the position exceeds the upper limit by 2 percentage points (e.g., rises to 14%), trim it back to 12%; if it falls 2 percentage points below the lower limit (e.g., drops to 6%) and the thesis is intact, top it up to 8%. No monthly fine-tuning, to avoid trading costs and emotional interference.

Dividend treatment: RMB 24 per share in 2026, a 1.0% dividend yield; reinvest rather than withdraw. TSMC's compounding comes mainly from capital appreciation; dividends are just a rounding error.

## V. Stress test: what if AI capex is halved

Assume the four major North American cloud providers cut capex 30% year on year in 2027, TSMC's revenue growth slows to -5%, gross margin returns to 53%, and EPS comes in at about RMB 78. At 16x (a typical multiple for a downcycle), the stock price is RMB 1,248, 48% below the current price. This is the result of a stress test, not a forecast.

In this scenario, a 12% position would drag the portfolio down by 5.8 percentage points. Is that tolerable? It depends on how the portfolio's other assets perform—if you also hold Nvidia and ASML, the correlation among the three would amplify total losses to 12%–15%. **This is why AI exposure must be calculated on a combined basis rather than by looking only at the position size of a single name.**

Conclusion: the 12% cap is the cap for "can withstand a halving." If your tolerance for volatility is below 20%, the cap should drop to 8%.
