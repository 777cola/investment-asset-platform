Investment summary

TSMC's 2026Q2 revenue reached TWD 1.27 trillion (+36.0% YoY) and net profit TWD 706.56 billion (+77.4% YoY), with EPS of TWD 27.25 and a 67.7% gross margin — all four at record highs. August revenue came in at TWD 514.806 billion, +53.3% YoY, topping TWD 500 billion for the first time and setting a record for the fourth consecutive month. AI demand shows no sign of slowing. But the 67.7% gross margin is an all-time high for the foundry industry, and Q3 guidance has already stepped down to 65%–67%. At the current price of TWD 2,410, the stock trades at 27.9x P/E (TTM) and 22.7x 2026E P/E; our 12-month target price of TWD 2,625 (21x 2027E EPS of TWD 125) implies 9% upside. Rating: Hold.

Chapter 1 Company Overview

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (2330.TW), founded in 1987, is the world's largest wafer foundry, operating two businesses: wafer manufacturing (foundry by process node) and advanced packaging (CoWoS, SoIC, InFO). 2026Q2 revenue was TWD 1,270.38 billion and net profit after tax TWD 706.56 billion, with a market cap of about TWD 62.5 trillion (about USD 1.95 trillion).

Capacity footprint spans Taiwan (Hsinchu, Taichung and Tainan, including the main 2nm site), Arizona in the US, Kumamoto in Japan, Dresden in Germany (production starts 2027) and Nanjing in China. Headcount is about 80,000. Chairman and CEO is C.C. Wei.

Revenue mix (2026Q2): HPC 66%, smartphones 22%, IoT 5%, automotive 4%, digital consumer 1%. Process mix: 7nm and below accounted for 77% (5nm 33%, 3nm 30%, 7nm 11%, 2nm 3%).

Ownership and governance: there is no controlling shareholder. Taiwan's Executive Yuan National Development Fund is the largest shareholder at about 6%, foreign investors hold about 70%, and Taiwanese domestic institutions and value funds hold the rest. The nine-seat board includes independent directors. Institutional rather than individual ownership underpins the stability of this structure, and the 2024 chairman transition did not raise governance concerns. Of about 80,000 employees, roughly 10,000 work in R&D, with R&D spending at 6%–7% of revenue.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

The global semiconductor market is roughly USD 700–800 billion, of which foundry accounts for about 25%. Foundry is a highly concentrated industry: TSMC holds about 70%, Samsung Foundry about 8%, UMC about 6%, SMIC about 5%, GlobalFoundries about 5%, and Intel Foundry under 3%.

That concentration reflects economies of scale and the learning curve: larger output lowers cost, lower cost makes pricing more competitive, and this positive feedback loop, sustained over a decade, creates a chasm. Advanced nodes (below 7nm) sit almost entirely in TSMC's hands, while mature nodes are a price war.

Cycle position: this upcycle began in the second half of 2023 and enters its third year in 2026. Both capex and gross margin are at all-time highs—a typical late-upcycle characteristic. On historical rhythm, the second half of 2026 to the first half of 2027 is the risk window.

Demand breakdown: AI servers are the only strong incremental driver in 2026, accounting for more than 40% of global semiconductor demand growth (institutional estimate). Phones and PCs are recovering only modestly, while automotive and industrial control are restocking after destocking. Semiconductor cyclicality is therefore being driven by a single AI pole: the upside is high growth, the downside is high concentration—once AI capex turns, no other demand can pick up the slack.

The supply-side constraints must be stated clearly: capacity expansion at advanced nodes is limited by three things—EUV lithography tool lead times (18 months), cleanroom construction cycles (24 months), and the training of process engineers (over 5 years). Even if demand suddenly surges, supply cannot catch up within two years. This is the structural reason gross margin can reach 67.7% in 2026, and also the source of risk from concentrated capacity release after 2028.

Chapter 3 Business Model

Revenue model: build capacity, ramp yield, then charge per wafer, with prices rising at each node. A 2nm wafer is reportedly quoted at about USD 30,000, 30%–50% above 3nm. Customers do not haggle because there is no substitute.

Cost structure: equipment depreciation accounts for 40%–50% (a 2nm fab costs USD 20 billion and up, depreciated over five years), materials 10%–15% (wafers, photoresist, specialty gases), and labour plus electricity about 10% combined. The business loses money below 80% utilisation; at full load gross margin can reach 67%.

Four legs of the moat: technology leadership (one to two generations ahead), switching costs (12–18 months to re-tape-out), scale (USD 60–64 billion in annual capex) and customer trust (it does not compete with its customers). The crack is geopolitics: overseas expansion dilutes gross margin by 3–4 percentage points.

Customer relationships are part of the model: TSMC engages at the chip definition stage, co-developing with customers, charging development fees and tuning process parameters to their requirements. That gives it the upper hand in pricing negotiations for the next node — customers have already committed design resources to the process, so switching costs arise before tape-out.

Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

Profitability: 2026Q2 gross margin 67.7% (+9.1pct YoY), operating margin c.57%, net margin 55.6%, ROE c.33%. All three gross margin drivers — pricing (5–6pct), full utilisation (2–3pct) and product mix (1–2pct) — peak simultaneously in 2026.

Cash flow: Q2 operating cash flow c.RMB 950 billion (rough calculation), capex USD 15.7 billion (c.RMB 495 billion), free cash flow c.RMB 455 billion and still positive. Full-year capex USD 60–64 billion, c.45% of revenue — the heaviest investment cycle in history.

Balance sheet: cash c.RMB 3.5 trillion, interest-bearing debt c.RMB 1 trillion, net cash RMB 2.5 trillion. Interest coverage above 100x. Contract liabilities (customer prepayments) are rising, a leading indicator of demand intensity. Financial risk negligible.

Cost side: R&D expense estimated at c.TWD 250 billion in 2026 (6%–7% of revenue), G&A c.RMB 120 billion, selling expense minimal (concentrated customer base, no promotion needed). Falling expense ratios reflect revenue dilution rather than absolute cost savings — a dynamic that reverses immediately once revenue growth slows, one of the concerns for 2027.

One easily overlooked metric: return on invested capital (ROIC). With 2026 NOPAT of c.TWD 2.3 trillion and invested capital (net debt + equity) of c.TWD 7 trillion, ROIC is c.33%, far above the weighted average cost of capital (estimated 8%–9%). Every yuan of new capital invested therefore creates value, and expanding capacity is the right call — the core test of whether an asset-heavy company should keep investing.

Chapter 5 Management

C.C. Wei (born 1952), Chairman and President, joined in 1998, became President in 2018 and took over as Chairman in 2024. His communication style is "never promise what you cannot deliver": over the past eight years, earnings-call guidance has been met more than 90% of the time.

Dual COOs: Y.J. Mii (R&D, led 7/5/3/2nm) and Y.P. Chyn (manufacturing and global capacity), both promoted in 2024, alongside CFO Wendell Huang. The arrangement is transitional, part of the succession plan.

Capital allocation: 5nm in 2019–2021, 3nm and CoWoS in 2022–2024, raised to USD 60–64 billion in 2026 — each time half a step ahead of demand. No large acquisitions; dividends raised year after year (RMB 24 in 2026, +33%); share count grew within 0.5% a year on average over five years.

Incentives and constraints: director and executive compensation is less than 0.1% of net profit; incentive intensity rests on industry reputation and long-term shareholding. This is a double-edged sword — no foolish moves for short-term bonuses, but also hard to lock in core process executives with equity. In 2021–2022 some executives were indeed poached by competitors, and the company subsequently increased the size of restricted stock grants.

Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Driver 1: AI compute capex. HPC is 66% of revenue, up 20% QoQ, with customer order visibility extending to 2027. The four major North American cloud providers sharply raised 2026 capex, a direct source of TSMC's revenue growth.

Driver 2: ASP uplift from process upgrades. 2nm was 3% of revenue in Q2, ramps rapidly in the second half and is on track for a double-digit percentage in 2027; A16 (1.6nm) enters risk production in the second half of 2026. Each generation lifts ASP by more than 30%.

Driver 3: Advanced packaging. CoWoS is the second bottleneck for AI chips; capacity keeps doubling yet remains in short supply, with the market estimating gross margins above 50%. TSMC does not disclose this business separately, but it is a key source of gross-margin mix improvement.

Quantifying the three drivers: of the roughly TWD 400 billion in 2026 revenue growth, AI-related (HPC) contributes about 70%, ASP uplift from process upgrades about 20%, and advanced packaging about 10% (estimate). The 2027 incremental structure will be similar, but the ASP uplift contribution will expand on the 2nm ramp, and the packaging contribution will rise on CoWoS capacity expansion.

Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Samsung Foundry: the only rival not lagging on node, with 2nm in volume production, but yield is estimated 20 percentage points behind, the foundry business is loss-making, and the structural obstacle of competing with customers remains unresolved.

Intel Foundry: 18A ramps in 2026 with US government backing, but it lacks customer trust and scale, and the foundry business is still loss-making. The threat comes after 2028.

SMIC: locked at the 7nm-class by equipment controls and serving China's domestic demand at a gross margin of around 22%, it does not compete in the same market as TSMC. UMC and GlobalFoundries have exited advanced nodes and compete on price in mature nodes.

One more underappreciated competitive front: customer in-house design. Apple, Nvidia, Google and Amazon are all evaluating shifting some mature-node orders to Samsung or Intel — not to save money, but for negotiating leverage. This does not affect advanced-node share, but it does affect the utilization buffer: once demand turns down, customers flee faster than anyone, and utilization will fall more steeply than in past cycles.

Chapter 8 Risk factors

Geopolitical risk: over 90% of advanced capacity sits in Taiwan, with an annualised 2%–5% probability and extreme impact. This is the only risk that could wipe out the investment entirely, and no amount of research can eliminate it.

Cyclical risk: if AI capex stalls in 2027, revenue growth slows to single digits, gross margin reverts to 55%–58% and EPS falls 15%, implying a 29% share-price decline (bear case: RMB 1,710).

Cost risk: overseas fabs dilute gross margin by 3–4pp (company guidance), with roughly USD 13 billion a year of incremental depreciation in 2027–2028. Customer concentration: the top two customers account for more than 40% of revenue.

The transmission paths need to be spelled out. Geopolitical event → capacity disruption → customer reallocation (irreversible) → long-term share loss. Cyclical downturn → lower utilisation → gross margin compression → lower valuation centre. The difference between the two: the former is a one-time wipeout, the latter a de-rating. For holders, the former can only be defended against through position sizing; the latter can be pre-empted by trimming on observable indicators.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026E: revenue of about TWD 14.5 trillion (+38% YoY, rough estimate), gross margin 65%–66%, EPS of about TWD 106 (TWD 49.33 in 1H plus about TWD 57 in 2H).

2027E, three scenarios: bear case EPS TWD 95 (AI capex growth slows to 10%, gross margin 55%), base case TWD 125 (revenue +18%, gross margin 60%), bull case TWD 140 (demand extends into 2028, 2nm fully loaded).

Key assumptions: 2027 capex stays above USD 60 billion, 2nm reaches a double-digit percentage of revenue, overseas fabs dilute margin by no more than 4 percentage points. If any one falls short, EPS is cut 10%–15%.

Sensitivity: on the 2027 base case (EPS TWD 125), every 5-percentage-point change in revenue growth moves EPS by about TWD 8; every 1-percentage-point change in gross margin moves EPS by about TWD 4.5. Gross margin therefore matters more than revenue growth — which is why this report keeps stressing gross margin rather than revenue.

Quarterly path: 2026Q3 revenue, extrapolated from the July–August run rate, is about TWD 1.5 trillion (+18% QoQ, rough estimate), with gross margin of 66%; in Q4, a deeper 2nm ramp may pull gross margin to 64%–65%, though revenue should keep hitting new highs. Full-year EPS of TWD 106 breaks down as Q1 22.08, Q2 27.25, Q3 about 29 and Q4 about 28 (the last two quarters are estimates).

Chapter 10 Valuation

Method: 2027E EPS as the denominator, applying an 18–24x P/E. The range reflects TSMC's P/E centring on 18x from 2015–2025 before re-rating to 22–27x after 2024 on the AI narrative; the asset-heavy model caps the upper bound, while technology leadership underpins the lower bound.

Target price: base case TWD 2,625 (TWD 125 × 21x), bear case TWD 1,710 (TWD 95 × 18x), bull case TWD 3,360 (TWD 140 × 24x). Against the current TWD 2,410, that implies +9% in the base case, -29% in the bear case and +39% in the bull case.

Expected value: weighting the scenarios at 30%/45%/25% gives an expected price of TWD 2,548, just 5.7% above the current price. The risk/reward does not support opening a new position.

Cross-check: on EV/EBITDA, 2026E EBITDA of about TWD 10 trillion against enterprise value of about TWD 60 trillion (market cap TWD 62.5 trillion less net cash TWD 2.5 trillion) implies 6x — mid-range versus the 5–8x we see as reasonable for manufacturers. On dividend discount, a 1.0% yield cannot support the valuation, implying the market is paying for growth, not dividends. Both lenses point to the same conclusion: not cheap, but not a bubble.

On the historical valuation range: 12–18x P/E in 2016–2019 (single-digit growth), 15–25x in 2020–2023 (5G and the chip shortage), 22–30x in 2024–2026 (AI). Each step up came with a narrative upgrade, and each retreated as the narrative faded. We are now in the upper band of the third cycle, and mean reversion to 18–20x is the reasonable assumption.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Rating: Hold. Existing positions stay; those with no position should wait for a pullback. Rationale: business quality scores 9–10 (top tier), but a 22.7x forward P/E already prices in 2027 growth, leaving only 9% upside in the base case.

Entry: build the first tranche at 2,000 (19x 2026 EPS), the second at 1,800, and fill to the cap at 1,600. Exit: thesis invalidated — quarterly gross margin falls below 60%, or monthly revenue turns negative month-on-month for two consecutive months while capex is raised again.

Position: core holding 8%–12%; calculate AI exposure combined with NVIDIA and ASML to avoid looking diversified while actually being concentrated.

Advice by investor type: long-term allocators (five years or more) can hold within an 8% position and ignore short-term volatility; swing traders should wait for below 2,000, because the risk-reward at the current price is unattractive; conservative investors (who cannot tolerate a 30% drawdown) should not allocate, because annualized volatility exceeds 30%.The same company gets different answers from different money.

Finally, a watch list in chronological order: mid-October Q3 earnings call (actual gross margin, Q4 guidance, preliminary 2027 capex framework) → around November 10, October revenue → January 2027, final full-year capex → April 2027 Q1 earnings call (2nm share of revenue). If any two of these four checkpoints turn unfavorable, cut the position from 10% to 6%.

Conclusion

Investment view: Hold, 12-month target price RMB 2,625 (+9%). Top-tier business quality, but the cycle is in the late upswing and valuation sits at the upper end of the forward range. Existing holders should stay put; those without a position should wait for below RMB 2,000.
