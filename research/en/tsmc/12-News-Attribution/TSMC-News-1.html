Core call: earnings calls and monthly revenue are signals; the rest is sentiment

Seven events between mid-June and September 11 merit a mention: the shareholder meeting's tone-setting, the Q2 earnings call, the additional Arizona investment, July revenue, August revenue and the September debate over AI capex. Only two genuinely change the view: the earnings call (gross margin guidance reversal) and August revenue (demand has not decelerated). The other five alter no assumptions.

1. Signal one: gross margin guidance at the earnings call

The July 16 earnings call was the most important event in three months. Three metrics hit record highs — revenue, gross margin and net profit — yet management guided Q3 gross margin to 65%–67%, a midpoint 1.7 percentage points below Q2, and gave the reason explicitly: the 2nm ramp dilutes margin by 3–4 percentage points, with overseas fabs a further 2–3 percentage points.

This is a signal, not noise: it is the first time in the current cycle that revenue guidance points up while gross margin guidance points down. In foundry, the profit inflection typically leads the revenue inflection by one to two quarters, and this combination is the textbook shape of that inflection.

The market rallied on the day because everyone looked only at revenue and the capex raise. That is precisely the nature of this kind of signal: it does not get priced on the day, it gets priced two quarters later.

II. Signal two: August revenue of RMB 514.8 billion

August revenue, reported on September 9, came in at RMB 514.806 billion, up 53.3% YoY — the first month above RMB 500 billion and a record for the fourth consecutive month. The value of this data point is that it disproves the "AI demand has peaked" narrative: if customers were cutting orders, monthly revenue would react first.

Note the gap between the YoY and MoM figures. The 53.3% YoY gain looks impressive only because the prior-year base was low (roughly RMB 335.8 billion in August 2025, back-calculated from the growth rate). The sequential trend matters more: four consecutive record highs from May through August show demand is still accelerating rather than leaning on the base effect.

Watch September and October. If September holds above RMB 500 billion, Q3 revenue will significantly exceed guidance; a fallback to around RMB 470 billion would suggest August revenue included some pull-forward of orders.

III. Noise item one: Arizona adds USD 100 billion

The news drew wide coverage but barely moves the needle on our view: the market has long known TSMC would step up US investment, with customer demand and policy requirements setting the direction two years ago. The added amount only turns "what to do" into "how much has been done."

Its real significance lies in long-term cost. Management itself said overseas fabs dilute gross margin by 3–4 percentage points, and depreciation on the USD 100 billion investment will concentrate from 2028 onward.This is a chronic condition, not something that flares up on a single day, so it should not be treated as a trading signal.

IV. Noise 2: Tariffs and the AI bubble debate

The mid-August semiconductor trade-measure discussions and early-September AI capex debate were both headline-driven volatility and did nothing to fundamentals. The test is simple: did either change customers' ordering behaviour? No. August revenue still hitting a record high is the best answer.

On September 11 the share price was RMB 2,410, down 1.63% and just -4.0% from the 52-week high of RMB 2,510. That level signals hesitation, not panic. Hesitation is not a signal; it is background noise.

V. Filter out the noise — what remains

Across three months of news flow, only two items can move the valuation: gross margin guidance (July 16) and monthly revenue validation (September 9). The other five — shareholder meeting tone, additional investment, July revenue, tariff discussion and the bubble debate — were absorbed by the market within a week of occurring and have no bearing on the 12-month target price.

Filtering noise is straightforward: ask whether the news changes cash flow assumptions. Price, volume and cost structure change cash flow; sentiment, expectations and policy statements do not. On that standard, 90% of the news over three months can be skipped.

The next key date is the mid-October Q3 earnings call. Three numbers there — actual Q3 gross margin, Q4 guidance and the preliminary 2027 capex framework — will determine whether this cycle is a soft landing or a hard turn.

Conclusion

Conclusion: two signals, five noise items. The signals are the gross margin guidance shift and August revenue confirming demand; the noise is shareholder meeting framing, additional investment, July revenue, tariff discussion and bubble debate. The next key date is the mid-October Q3 earnings call, where the actual gross margin and 2027 capex guidance will determine whether this cycle sees a "soft landing" or a "hard turn".
