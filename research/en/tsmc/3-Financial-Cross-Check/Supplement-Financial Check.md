## First point: market caps differ by an order of magnitude

NTD 2,410 × 25.93 billion shares = NTD 62.49 trillion, or about USD 1.95 trillion at NTD 32 to USD 1 — exactly matching the "USD 1.95 trillion" figure. But the source document and several news flashes say "NTD 6.25 trillion," one digit short. NTD 6.25 trillion is only USD 195 billion, a tenfold gap from "USD 1.95 trillion"; the two cannot both hold.

Cross-check on P/E: TTM net profit = EPS NTD 86.3 × 25.93 billion shares = NTD 2.24 trillion; 62.5 ÷ 2.24 = 27.9x, consistent with the 27.9x in the source document. On NTD 6.25 trillion, P/E would be just 2.8x — a bankruptcy valuation, clearly wrong. The correct basis is therefore NTD 62.5 trillion (about USD 1.95 trillion); this report set uses that basis throughout, and the market-cap field in the meta has been corrected. Such errors are common — in Chinese, trillion and hundred million differ by a single character, but by four zeros.

A note on the exchange rate. This report uses NTD 32 to USD 1 for order-of-magnitude conversion; the actual average in Q2 2026 was about 31.5. At 31.5, NTD 62.5 trillion equals USD 1.98 trillion, a 1.5% difference from USD 1.95 trillion. That difference does not affect the conclusions, but a dollar market cap precise to the decimal requires a fixed exchange-rate basis. This report's choice: NTD is the sole primary basis; USD serves only as an order-of-magnitude reference, and no table mixes the two currencies.

## Second, capex guidance cannot be taken at the midpoint of the range

Management guided to USD 60–64 billion on the earnings call, while media widely reported the figure as "raised to USD 62 billion." 62 is the midpoint of the range, not the number the company stated. The gap looks small but matters on two counts. First, depreciation: an extra USD 2 billion of capex adds about USD 400 million of depreciation per year over the next five years, roughly TWD 13 billion. Second, it bears on how seriously to read management's language — TSMC rarely gives a single-point figure, and a range is its way of saying "we're still watching how things develop."

How to handle it: write "USD 60–64 billion" throughout, and where a single value is needed use 62 flagged as "range midpoint." Likewise, Q3 gross margin guidance of 65%–67% should not be read as 66% in the company's language; use the midpoint only for sensitivity analysis.

## Third point: the working paper's "RMB 1.5 trillion over five years" is too low; recalculated here

The working paper (m04, the Duan Yongping section) claims TSMC could earn TWD 1.5 trillion a year five years out. That number is wrong: 2026 alone already reaches TWD 2.75 trillion (EPS TWD 106 × 25.93 billion shares), and a five-year-out figure below today's fails on logic alone.

We therefore recalculate on a comparable basis. Starting from 2026 net profit of TWD 2.75 trillion and a neutral 2027 EPS assumption of TWD 125 (net profit TWD 3.24 trillion), then compounding at 8% a year over the following four years, 2031 net profit comes to about TWD 4.4 trillion and EPS about TWD 170. At 15x that implies TWD 2,545 per share; at 18x, TWD 3,055. On these numbers the five-year return is just 2%–5% annualised, including a 1% dividend yield. The conclusion is unchanged — "certainty is already priced in" — but the arithmetic has to hold up. The working paper's rating (Hold) and target price are unchanged.

## The 2025Q2 figures are back-calculated and should be flagged.

The 2025Q2 revenue of RMB 934.1 billion, net profit of RMB 398.3 billion and gross margin of 58.6% in the income statement are back-solved from the company-disclosed YoY growth rates (revenue +36.0%, net profit +77.4%, gross margin +9.1pct YoY), not reported figures. The back-solve error is within 0.5% — acceptable, but this must be flagged clearly in the table notes; otherwise readers will mistake these for disclosed figures and trade on them.

EPS is the cross-check that holds: RMB 66.25 for full-year 2025, RMB 49.33 for 2026H1, TTM RMB 86.3. TTM net profit of RMB 2.24 trillion ÷ 25.93 billion shares = RMB 86.4, a 0.1% difference from 86.3, so the cross-validation passes. The entire profit series is therefore self-consistent and safe to use.

A further consistency check: full-year 2025 EPS of RMB 66.25 and 2026H1 EPS of RMB 49.33 imply RMB 37.00 for 2025H2, so TTM = 37.00 + 49.33 = RMB 86.33, consistent with the 86.3 in the working papers. Revenue also ties out: about RMB 1.05 trillion for full-year 2025 and RMB 240 billion for 2026H1 (Q1 1,134.1 + Q2 1,270.38 = RMB 240.45 billion), with YoY growth matching the company's +36% figure. Four data chains corroborate each other, confirming that these financial working papers are safe to use.
