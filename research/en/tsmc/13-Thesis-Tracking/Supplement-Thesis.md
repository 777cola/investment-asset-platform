## 1. Point one: AI demand is still accelerating

This is the foundation of the thesis, and the evidence chain has three layers: TSMC's own monthly revenue (RMB 514.806 billion in August, +53.3% YoY), customer order behaviour (HPC +20% QoQ, capacity booked through 2027) and downstream capex (the four major North American cloud providers sharply raising 2026 budgets). The three layers corroborate each other; this is not a single data point.

The falsification test is equally clear: monthly revenue turning negative on a sequential basis for two consecutive months. Note this is QoQ, not YoY. YoY is distorted by base effects, whereas QoQ reflects the true change in demand. In October 2022, QoQ turned negative first while YoY was still growing, and the stock had already peaked.

## 2. Third takeaway: gross margin is the sole metric deteriorating

Q2's 67.7% was a historical peak. Management's own Q3 guidance is 65%–67%, midpoint 66% — a pullback, not a collapse. Two reasons, both active choices: the 2nm ramp sacrifices near-term yield, and overseas fabs trade higher costs for supply-chain security.

The key question is whether gross margin can return to 65% in 2027. If it can, pricing power remains intact and 67.7% was merely cyclical; if it cannot, or even falls below 60%, this is a structural step-down and the valuation centre must fall from 22x to 18x, implying a share price near RMB 1,900. This is the single number to watch most closely in this thesis.

## III. Article 4: Conversion rate of capital expenditure

Capex of USD 60–64 billion in 2026 implies revenue of roughly USD 130 billion on our rough calculation, a capex/revenue ratio of nearly 50% — high by historical standards, versus about 47% in 2022 and about 35% in 2019.

A high ratio is not a problem in itself, provided revenue keeps pace. The test: if 2027 revenue growth falls below 15% while capex stays above USD 60 billion, capacity has been overbuilt, and 2028 will bring falling utilization and gross margin compression.So the 2027 capex guidance matters more than 2026 revenue.

## IV. Falsification Conditions and Monitoring Calendar

Three falsification conditions, any one of which triggers a review: first, quarterly gross margin falls below 60%; second, monthly revenue turns negative MoM for two consecutive months while capex is raised further over the same period; third, yields at the Arizona fab lag the Taiwan fabs materially (two or more generations behind), leaving overseas capacity unable to serve its "insurance" function.

Watch calendar: monthly revenue around the 10th of each month; the Q3 earnings call in mid-October (actual gross margin plus preliminary 2027 capex guidance); final 2027 capex in January 2027; whether 2nm's full-year share reaches double digits as a percentage in April 2027.

## V. The payoff structure of the thesis

Thesis holds (base case): 2027 EPS of RMB 125, 21x, RMB 2,625, +9%. Thesis disproved (bear case): EPS of RMB 95, 18x, RMB 1,710, -29%. That is 9% upside against 29% downside — a 1:3 risk-reward.

The structure carries one message: a thesis that has not broken is not a reason to add. The case for holding is "stay at the table," not "double down." Wait for the bear case to be priced in — below RMB 2,000 — before adding; there the risk-reward improves beyond 1:1.3.

Add a time dimension: the thesis holds roughly through mid-2027. Three things land at once by then — whether 2nm is fully loaded (which decides whether gross margin can return to 65%), the 2027 capex decision (which sets depreciation pressure) and the growth rate of AI capex (which drives revenue). If two of the three turn adverse by mid-2027, the thesis needs rewriting, not patching.
