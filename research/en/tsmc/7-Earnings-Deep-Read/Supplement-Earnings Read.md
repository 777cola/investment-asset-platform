## Layer one: revenue mix — growth is now fully tied to AI

Q2 revenue was USD 40.2 billion (about TWD 1.27 trillion at an exchange rate of 31.5), up 36% YoY. By platform, HPC accounted for 66%, up 20% QoQ, and smartphones for 22%, down 4% QoQ. This is a structural reversal: three years ago phones were half the business; now they are a supporting act.

The process-node mix tells the same story: 7nm and below combined accounted for 77%, of which 5nm was 33%, 3nm 30% and 7nm 11%, while 2nm, just ramping, accounted for 3%. The higher the share of advanced nodes, the higher the unit price and the higher the gross margin—the structural basis for the 67.7% gross margin.

One detail stands out: 2nm's first full quarter was only 3%, leaving ample room to ramp in the second half. That both supports Q3/Q4 revenue and explains the gross-margin dilution.

On regions and customers: North American customers accounted for about 70% of revenue, Chinese customers just over 10% (advanced-node business there has been essentially zeroed out by export controls), and the rest came from Asia-Pacific and Europe. Customer concentration is high: the top two customers together account for more than 40% (estimated). That is a risk, but also pricing power—neither of the two largest customers dares to switch orders, because there is no capacity elsewhere with equivalent yields.

An easily overlooked detail: automotive electronics accounted for only 4%, but grew 15% QoQ, making it the second-fastest-growing platform. Automotive chip qualification cycles run as long as two years, and once a supplier is in the supply chain it is hard to replace, so the value of this business is "stability" rather than "growth." It hedges the cyclical swings of smartphones.

One more detail on process economics: 2nm yields fewer good dies per wafer than 3nm (because dies are larger and the process is harder), but the unit price is 30%–50% higher, so revenue per wafer is higher. This is the transmission mechanism behind "a higher share of advanced nodes leads to a higher gross margin," and one reason revenue can keep growing in 2027.

## Layer two: gross margin — what 67.7% actually means

Historical foundry gross margins: UMC 30%–35%, GlobalFoundries 25%, SMIC 20%–25%, Samsung Foundry an estimated 30%–40%. TSMC at 67.7% is more than double the industry average. The read-across: absolute pricing power over every wafer.

Decomposing the gross margin change: of the +9.1pct YoY, roughly 5–6pct came from pricing and product mix (a higher 3nm share), 2–3pct from fixed-cost dilution on full utilisation, and 1–2pct from FX and cost improvements (estimated). All three legs are cyclical; none is permanent.

That makes the Q3 guidance of 65%–67% more significant than it looks: not "seasonal fluctuation" but the first leg (pricing) starting to exhaust, with the second and third legs offset by the 2nm ramp.

The peer comparison makes it clearer: UMC about 30%, GlobalFoundries about 25%, SMIC about 22%, Samsung Foundry an estimated 30%–40%. TSMC's 67.7% is two to three times peers. That gap did not form in a year—in 2019 TSMC stood at 46% and UMC at 15%, a 31 percentage point gap; by 2026 it widens to more than 35 percentage points. In an upcycle, the leader's gross margin elasticity is more than double everyone else's—the leverage conferred by "advanced-node share."

## Tier 3: Costs and profit — operating margin around 57%

A 67.7% gross margin less roughly 10.7pp of opex yields an operating margin of about 57%. Within opex, R&D accounts for 6%–7% (2026 R&D spending estimated at roughly TWD 250 billion) and administrative expenses about 3%. R&D intensity sits in the upper-middle range of the semiconductor industry, and in absolute terms ranks top three globally.

Net margin 55.6%, up 13.0pct YoY. That jump exceeds the +9.1pct rise in gross margin, and the gap has two sources: operating expense ratio diluted by revenue, and interest income growing with cash scale.Both are "tailwinds of a good year" and will not repeat every year.

One more tax detail. TSMC's effective tax rate has long been 13%–15%, below the nominal 20%, thanks to R&D investment credits under the Statute for Industrial Innovation and overseas income taxation rules. That advantage is not permanent—Taiwan's tax incentives are revised every few years, and if credits shrink after 2026, each 1 percentage point rise in the tax rate reduces EPS by about TWD 1.2.This is not the main variable, but leave some buffer when modeling 2027 EPS.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — nothing to fault

Q2 capex was USD 15.7 billion (about TWD 495 billion), or 39% of revenue. Operating cash flow came to roughly TWD 950 billion and free cash flow to about TWD 455 billion, still positive. The implication: even in the heaviest investment cycle in its history, TSMC earns enough to fund its own spending.

Balance sheet: cash of about TWD 3.5 trillion against interest-bearing debt of about TWD 1 trillion, for net cash of TWD 2.5 trillion. Accounts payable and contract liabilities (customer prepayments) are important non-interest-bearing funding sources. No financial risk, and no line items that need explaining.

The one concern is future depreciation: 2026 capex of USD 60–64 billion, depreciated over five years and concentrated from 2027, adds about USD 13 billion (about TWD 410 billion) of depreciation a year, equivalent to 15% of 2026 net profit. This is a structural drag on gross margin in 2027–2028.

A depreciation sensitivity: 2026 capex of USD 60–64 billion, depreciated over five years and fully amortised from 2027, adds about USD 12–13 billion (about TWD 380–410 billion) of depreciation a year. On a 2026 revenue base of TWD 14.5 trillion, that equals a 2.6–2.8pp rise in the operating expense ratio. If revenue growth falls below 10%, depreciation dilution turns into a gross margin killer—the number that most needs to be worked out in advance for 2027.

## The three things to watch most closely

First, gross margin trajectory. The Q3 midpoint is 66%; a Q4 print below 63% would signal that 2nm dilution and overseas fab costs are weighing more heavily than guidance implies, in which case 2027 EPS estimates would need to be cut by more than 10%.

Second, 2027 capex. Further upward revisions signal strong demand; a halt to upward revisions may mean customers are tapping the brakes. This is the most sensitive leading indicator.

Third, month-over-month revenue. August's RMB 514.806 billion is a record high; the next test is whether September–October can hold above RMB 500 billion. Whether AI orders are real shows up in the month-over-month figures, not the year-over-year ones.
