Masterclass Close Read: Munger — TSMC

Think in reverse — how could this company lose you money?

Core view

Munger has one method and one only: don't study how to win, study how to lose. So how could TSMC lose people money? Not through technology being overtaken — that takes a decade — and not through management missteps, since this team is steady. The risk is buying at 27.9x P/E when gross margin is 67.7%, then watching gross margin revert to 55%.

The first counterexample: semiconductors in 2022

In 2022 TSMC's revenue was still hitting new highs, gross margin stood at 59.6% and the market was paying 15x P/E. Half a year later inventory correction set in: Q1 2023 revenue fell 18% QoQ, gross margin dropped to 45% and the stock fell 45% from its high. At the time no one saw a decline coming, because every data point looked good.

Munger would ask: what signals were visible before that decline? Two: capex hitting new highs, and gross margin hitting new highs. In 2026, both signals have appeared simultaneously again.

Second cautionary tale: Intel's 10nm

Intel ran the same playbook in 2015–2019: capacity fully loaded, gross margin above 60%, record capex. The outcome was a five-year slip on 10nm, and one process misstep consumed an era of leadership. Munger's point stands: technological leadership is not a permanent asset; it is a position that must be re-won every year.

TSMC's lead today is wider than Intel's was then (two generations vs half a generation), and its customer relationships are stronger. Even so, the risk that leadership erodes must be priced into the valuation.

What advice would he give?

This is not a sell call; it is "do not add". Munger never advised selling a good company, only objected to buying at the wrong price. His view: if you already hold it, hold it, collect the 1% dividend and wait for the next cycle; if you do not, do not start here.

He would add a variant of something he said often: "Invert: if you need to bet on AI capex not stalling at 22x P/E, that is not investing, that is a wager."

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: no adding to the position. The prettier the earnings report at a cycle peak, the more cautious you should be. Wait until gross margin falls back to around 58% and the market starts calling it a "cyclical stock" — that is when it belongs in his portfolio.
