Core view

Li Lu would not look at a single quarter. He would ask what long-term trend this earnings report represents. The answer: compute is turning into infrastructure, and the builders of that infrastructure are capturing excess profit. Q2 net profit of RMB 706.56 billion, up 77.4% YoY, is not one quarter's luck — it is a structure taking shape.

Where does he rank TSMC

In his framework, every general-purpose technology revolution in history throws up a "toll booth": the railway companies of the railway age, the power grids of the electricity age, the cloud providers of the internet age. The defining trait of a toll booth is that whatever cargo passes over it, the toll must be paid.

TSMC is the toll booth of the compute age. Whether what runs on top is training or inference, whether it is Nvidia's GPUs, Google's TPUs or Amazon's Trainium, everything ultimately has to be taped out in its fabs. The more diversified the customer base (HPC 66%, smartphones 22%, automotive 4%), the more secure the toll booth's position.

The long-term variables he sees

One detail stands out: 2nm accounted for just 3% in its first full quarter, while 7nm and below already made up 77%. Node iteration is still advancing and customers are still paying for smaller transistors. As long as that cycle persists, TSMC retains pricing power.

Agentic AI is the other variable. Inference demand will bring CPUs back into the data center, and the CPU players — Intel, AMD, the ARM camp, the RISC-V camp — are almost all TSMC customers. Its customer base is broadening beyond Nvidia. This is where it is stronger than in 2023, and the core reason for holding long term.

How will he handle geopolitics?

He neither ignores geopolitics nor tries to forecast it. His framework treats geopolitical risk as a permanent discount factor: relocating capacity overseas costs more than USD 100 billion and dilutes gross margin by 3–4 percentage points. That is a certain cost, and it can be deducted directly from the valuation. On the conflict itself he makes no assumptions—because assumptions would serve no purpose.

His conclusion: This is an asset you can hold on a ten-year horizon, but position size must be controlled, because you cannot price tail risk.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: civilisation-level infrastructure that merits long-term ownership. Geopolitics is a tail risk that cannot be priced; manage it through position sizing rather than valuation. On a ten-year view the current price is not expensive; on a three-year view it is not cheap.
