Financial reality: how the 67.7% gross margin is calculated — TSMC

Three numbers, two layers of leverage, one unsustainable premise
Research date 2026/09/11  ·  Price NT$2,410 (-1.63%)  ·  Market cap Approximately TWD 62.5 trillion (about USD 1.95 trillion)  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 27.9x

Core view

The 67.7% gross margin is no accounting trick: it is the product of three real variables — price increases, full utilisation and product mix. All three peak at once in 2026, so the margin has peaked too. Understand this, and you understand everything this earnings report means.

I. Three sources of gross margin

First, pricing. A 2nm wafer is reportedly quoted at about USD 30,000, 30%–50% above 3nm, while 3nm pricing is flat in 2026. The increase adds roughly 5–6 percentage points to gross margin (estimate).

Second, utilisation. Capacity utilisation rises from about 85% in 2025 to above 95% in 2026, diluting fixed costs (depreciation, labour, facilities) and contributing 2–3 percentage points.

Third, product mix. 3nm accounts for 30% of revenue and 5nm for 33%; the higher share of high-margin products contributes 1–2 percentage points. Together the three add 8–11 percentage points, consistent with the +9.1pct YoY.

II. Why is net profit growth twice revenue growth?

Revenue +36%, net profit +77.4%. The gap is operating leverage: the fixed cost base (depreciation, labour) does not scale with revenue, so each additional RMB 1 of revenue drops almost entirely to gross profit.

In detail: gross margin rose from 58.6% to 67.7% (+9.1pct) and the opex ratio fell from about 12% to about 10.7% on revenue dilution, together lifting the operating margin by about 10 percentage points. Interest income also grew with the cash base, taking net margin from 42.6% to 55.6%.

These are the financial hallmarks of a cycle top: every lever points up at once. On the way down they reverse in unison—which is why cyclical stocks swing far more than revenue.

III. Cash flow: real money earned, but spent just as fast

Q2 operating cash flow was roughly RMB 950 billion, 1.3x net profit. The gap is depreciation—about RMB 200 billion with no cash outflow—a standard feature of asset-heavy companies, and it shows book profit is conservative.

Capex was USD 15.7 billion (about TWD 495 billion), half of operating cash flow. Free cash flow was about RMB 455 billion, still positive. Even in the heaviest investment cycle in history, it earns enough to cover its own spending and needs no financing.

The 2026 dividend is NTD 24 per share, totaling about RMB 620 billion, or 136% of free cash flow (rough annualised Q2 calculation). A payout ratio of 23% looks low, but is reasonable for a company expanding capacity on a large scale.

IV. Three details of the balance sheet

First, contract liabilities — customer prepayments of capacity deposits — are growing. That is a leading indicator of demand intensity and a source of interest-free capital: customers will pay upfront to lock in capacity.

Second, interest-bearing debt is about TWD 1 trillion against roughly TWD 3.5 trillion of cash, implying net cash of TWD 2.5 trillion. Interest income contributes only 1–2 percentage points of net profit (estimated) and will decline as rates fall.

Third, depreciation lives. TSMC depreciates advanced-node equipment over shorter lives than some peers, which makes current profit more conservative and sets up a depreciation roll-off tailwind. This is accounting conservatism, not financial engineering.

V. An Unsustainable Premise

A 67.7% gross margin rests on two premises: capacity stays fully loaded, and customers accept price increases. Both hold at once in 2026, but neither is permanent.

Management's own Q3 guidance already points to 65%–67%. Two drags explain the gap: the 2nm ramp dilutes margin by 3–4pct, and overseas fabs dilute it by a further 2–3pct. Both persist through 2027–2028.

So do not use 67.7% in valuation; use 58%–60%, the mid-cycle level. At a 58% gross margin and flat revenue, EPS falls from RMB 106 to about RMB 85 — the "normalized earnings" basis, and the source of the RMB 1,800 fair value.

One further observation: gross margin peaks tend to coincide with falling receivables turnover days and rising contract liabilities — customers paying up front. Both metrics improved in 2026Q2, confirming demand is real. Historically, however, these two metrics turn one quarter ahead of gross margin declines. So watch contract liabilities, not gross margin, for the earlier signal.

Conclusion

The financial reality: this is a genuinely strong report, but strong at the top of the cycle. All three drivers of gross margin have peaked, while two dilutive factors have only just begun to bite. Valuing the stock off these numbers requires normalisation, otherwise the cycle peak gets mistaken for the norm.
