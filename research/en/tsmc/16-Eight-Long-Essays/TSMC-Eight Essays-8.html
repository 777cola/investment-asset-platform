Core view

The previous seven pieces covered "what this is." This one covers "how to do it." There are only four rules: a position cap, three tiers of buy prices, two falsification conditions, and a quarterly review cadence. Write them down, and emotion won't carry you away.

1. Position: core holding, cap 12%

TSMC is the portfolio's core AI manufacturing position, sized at 8%–12%. The rationale: with a high beta (roughly 0.9 correlation with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index) and heavy overlap with Nvidia (0.8–0.9), an oversized position would amount to betting the entire portfolio on AI capex.

The 12% cap reflects two constraints: concentration (AI exposure measured jointly with Nvidia and ASML) and geopolitics (single-region tail risk capped at 15%). Below 8%, the contribution to the portfolio is insufficient.

For existing holders: at a cost below RMB 1,800, hold 8%–10% and do not take profits; above RMB 2,200, hold 8% and do not add.

2. Buy-in prices: three tiers, in batches

First tranche at RMB 2,000 (19x 2026, 16x 2027), building to 6%. At that level the market begins to discount cyclical risk and the payoff ratio improves from 1:3 to 1:1.3.

Second tranche at RMB 1,800 (14x 2027), building to 10% — the low end of the 2015–2025 P/E range, historically seen only in downturns. Third tranche below RMB 1,600, building to the 12% cap; this one requires confirmation that gross margin has already fallen to around 58%, otherwise you risk catching a falling knife.

At the current price of RMB 2,410, none of the three tranches triggers. The action now is to wait.

One further discipline: do not fill all three tranches at once. Deploy only one-third of the target position per tranche, with at least one quarter between them. Cyclical stocks rarely bottom in a single move — in the 2022 cycle the stock fell from RMB 688 to RMB 370, with three rallies along the way that looked like the bottom. The point of scaling in is not to buy cheaper, but to buy in a way you are less likely to regret.

III. When to Admit We Are Wrong

Do not use price stops. TSMC's annualised volatility exceeds 30%, and price stops will shake you out. Use thesis stops instead: first, quarterly gross margin falls below 60%, confirming a structural step-down; second, monthly revenue turns negative MoM for two consecutive months while capex continues to be raised over the same period, a precursor to overcapacity; third, yields at the Arizona fab lag Taiwan fabs by more than two generations, meaning overseas capacity has failed.

If any one triggers, cut to 4% or exit entirely. If two conditions are met simultaneously, the thesis is broken, not volatility.

The point of discipline is not to make money every time, but to lose less when wrong.

IV. Monitoring cadence: monthly, quarterly, annual

Monthly: watch consolidated revenue around the 10th and focus on the MoM change, not the YoY. Quarterly: three things at the earnings call — the gap between actual gross margin and guidance, preliminary 2027 capex guidance, and the 2nm share of revenue. Annual: whether overseas fab P&L is disclosed separately, and whether there is progress on succession planning.

No need to watch the share price every day. TSMC's disclosure is among the most transparent in the market, with new data every month, which makes over-trading easy. Cut your observation frequency to monthly and decision quality will improve.

V. One-Sentence Executive Version

Hold, 8%–12%. No adds or trims at the current price. Build to 6% at 2,000, to 10% at 1,800 and to 12% at 1,600. Cut to 4% if gross margin falls below 60% and monthly revenue turns negative sequentially. Review quarterly; check monthly revenue sequentially each month.

That is all. No need to call the AI peak or judge the Taiwan Strait situation; you only need to act when price offers the opportunity and admit error when the thesis breaks.

The hardest part of investing has never been analysis; it is waiting. Everything in this TSMC report ultimately comes down to that one word.

One more easily overlooked discipline: do not make decisions on the earnings-call day. TSMC's earnings call is usually in the afternoon, and the stock reacts sharply after hours and the next day, when emotions are most likely to spiral. Delay the decision by 48 hours and replace the on-the-spot reflex with a calmer judgment; over the long run this saves a lot of money.

Conclusion

Decision framework: Hold, cap at 12%, three buy levels at RMB 2,000/1,800/1,600, two falsification conditions, quarterly review. Once the rules are written down, there is no need to agonise daily.
