Cognitive reset: your impression of TSMC may be stuck five years in the past — TSMC

Five years ago it was "Apple's contract manufacturer"; now it is "the bottleneck in AI compute"
Research date 2026/09/11  ·  Price NT$2,410 (-1.63%)  ·  Market cap Approximately TWD 62.5 trillion (about USD 1.95 trillion)  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 27.9x

Core view

Most people still think of TSMC as "the Taiwanese factory that makes chips for Apple"—a stable, cyclical manufacturer with modest growth. That view was right in 2021; it is wrong in 2026. It has become the only physical bottleneck in the AI supply chain, and the market prices a bottleneck nothing like it prices a foundry.

I. Five outdated impressions

First impression: TSMC lives off Apple. In fact, smartphones accounted for only 22% of revenue in 2026Q2, while HPC (high-performance computing) accounted for 66%, and smartphones were still down 4% quarter on quarter. Apple remains the largest single customer, but it is no longer the growth engine.

Second impression: foundry is a price-war industry. In fact, mature nodes are a price war, while advanced nodes are a seller's market — 2nm wafers are reportedly quoted at about USD 30,000 each, 30%–50% higher than 3nm, and customers do not haggle because there is nowhere else to make them.

Third impression: semiconductors are strongly cyclical, and a 40% gross margin counts as good. In fact, gross margin was 67.7% in 2026Q2, 21 percentage points higher than 46% in 2019. Fourth impression: TSMC is large but slow-growing. In fact, 2026Q2 revenue rose 36% year on year and net profit 77%. Fifth impression: geopolitical risk makes it cheap. In fact, it sits just 4% below its 52-week high.

2. In which year did the change begin?

The turning point was 2023. That year the industry was destocking, TSMC's revenue fell and capex was cut to just over USD 30 billion, and the market widely believed "the golden age of semiconductors is over." At the same time, OpenAI's ChatGPT began consuming massive compute, and Nvidia's orders started to queue up.

In 2024 TSMC pulled capex back above USD 30 billion, in 2025 CoWoS became the world's tightest capacity, and in 2026 capex was raised to USD 60–64 billion. In three years it went from "victim of the cycle" to "beneficiary of the cycle," because the 3nm and advanced packaging it invested in five years early caught the AI wave exactly.

What this shift means for valuation: the market began pricing it as "compute infrastructure" rather than "wafer foundry." The former is benchmarked against cloud providers and equipment leaders at 20–30x PE; the latter against manufacturers at 10–15x PE. This is the fundamental reason its valuation has re-rated upward, and also where it is most fragile now—if the AI narrative fades, the valuation will revert to the manufacturing framework.

Three, the same mistake everyone is making

Confusing "certainty" with "cheap." TSMC's certainty is indeed high—two generations of technology leadership, customers lining up, order visibility through 2027. But high certainty does not mean high returns, because certainty gets priced in.

The current price (27.9x TTM, 22.7x 2026E) already discounts "continued growth in 2027." Buying at this price, you are not earning money from "it will grow"—you are earning money from "it will grow faster than the market expects." The latter is far harder.

The cost of this mistake is not a loss—it is low returns. Buying at 2,410, five-year annualized returns may be only 5%–10%, and you would have to endure 30% volatility along the way. That drains people more than losing money does.

IV. Re-establishing three coordinates

First coordinate: is it manufacturing or infrastructure? The answer is half and half. The manufacturing side (yield, depreciation, capacity) sets the floor; the infrastructure side (bottlenecks, pricing power) sets the ceiling. Valuation must weight both, not one or the other.

Second coordinate: where are we in the cycle? Capex at a record high (USD 60–64 billion), gross margin at a record high (67.7%), and gross margin guidance beginning to decline—two of these three are top-of-cycle signals. The cycle is in its late upswing.

Third coordinate: who is setting the price? Foreign investors hold about 70%, and the main buyers are passive funds and AI-themed funds. This means its share price is more sensitive to the "AI narrative" than to "quarterly earnings." Only by understanding this can one understand why it still hit a new high after news of declining gross margin guidance.

V. Conclusions after the cognitive reset

After the reset, TSMC is no longer a "steady manufacturing blue chip" but a "high-certainty cyclical growth stock." That shift in label creates three differences in how to position.

First, the "buy at a low valuation, hold for the long term" framework no longer applies, because its valuation center moves with the cycle. Second, gross margin must be watched rather than revenue, because the profit inflection leads the revenue inflection. Third, position size must be controlled, because its correlation with Nvidia and ASML is 0.7–0.9, so a portfolio that looks diversified may be highly concentrated.

In one sentence: the good-company label is unchanged, but the inference that "good company = good investment" does not hold at this price.

Conclusion

The conclusion of the cognitive reset: it is the only physical bottleneck in the AI era, and also the most expensive certainty. The point of studying it is not "whether it is good" but "how much odds this price offers." The answer is +9% in the neutral case and -29% in the bear case — not worth it.
