Risk list: five risks, one that can really kill you — TSMC

Ranked by "probability × impact", geopolitics is the only factor beyond our control
Research date 2026/09/11  ·  Price NT$2,410 (-1.63%)  ·  Market cap Approximately TWD 62.5 trillion (about USD 1.95 trillion)  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 27.9x

Core view

TSMC's risk list is long, but ranked by probability and impact the conclusion is clear: only geopolitics is truly fatal, the cycle is the most likely to materialise, and the remaining three are chronic conditions. Chronic conditions compress the valuation without destroying the business; the acute risk destroys the business but has low probability.

I. Geopolitics: the only risk that goes to zero

More than 90% of TSMC's advanced-process capacity sits in Taiwan. Management cannot fix this: it is a historical industrial cluster—the science parks in Hsinchu, Taichung and Tainan plus over a thousand surrounding suppliers, an ecosystem three decades in the making.

Probability: 2%–5% per year (subjective estimate, referencing Taiwan Strait shipping insurance and changes in foreign shareholding). Impact: extremely high—capacity goes to zero and the global AI supply chain halts.

How to handle it: don't forecast, just price it. Research cannot eliminate this risk; only position sizing can—a 12% cap on any single holding, or demanding a valuation discount. The current 22.7x P/E embeds a discount for overseas expansion dilution, but not for a geopolitical event.

II. Cycle: The Most Likely Scenario

The 2026 boom rests on AI capex. The four major North American cloud providers sharply raised 2026 capex, but the market has begun to question the returns—no one has given a clear answer on when the money spent on compute will be recouped.

Probability: 30% before 2027 (subjective estimate). Impact: EPS -15%, P/E de-rating of 3–5x, share price -29% (pessimistic scenario RMB 1,710).

Observable indicators: quarterly capex guidance from the four major North American cloud providers, TSMC's MoM revenue, CoWoS capacity utilisation. When these three indicators turn, the cycle has peaked. This is not a black swan but a gray rhino—visible.

III. Overseas fab costs: the most certain headwind

Arizona is in mass production, Kumamoto Fab 1 started production in 2024 with Fab 2 under construction, and Dresden starts production in 2027. On the company's own numbers, the overseas fabs initially dilute gross margin by 2–3 percentage points a year and by 3–4 percentage points over the long term.

Probability: 100% (already happening). Impact: moderate; gross margin shifts structurally lower and ROE falls from 33% to around 28%.

This is the cost of buying insurance, not an operating misstep.But it means the 67.7% gross margin will never come back.

IV. Customer concentration and technology substitution: two slow-moving variables

Customer concentration: Apple and Nvidia, the top two customers, together generate more than 40% of revenue, so any product-cycle swing at either is amplified. Samsung and Intel are both chasing these orders; neither will win the main volume near term, but both will keep pressing on price.

Technology substitution: if optical computing or compute-in-memory eventually bypasses advanced logic nodes, the foundry's position in the value chain would decline. Low probability within five years, but extremely high impact. TSMC is itself investing in silicon photonics and advanced packaging as a hedge.

Both are slow variables. They do not change the three-year view, but they do affect what P/E multiple to assign.

V. Risk ranking and mitigation

Ranking: cycle (medium probability, high impact) > overseas fab costs (high probability, medium impact) > customer concentration (medium-low probability, medium impact) > geopolitics (low probability, extremely high impact) > technology substitution (low probability, extremely high impact).

Response: for the cycle, use monitoring indicators (monthly revenue MoM, capex guidance); for overseas fabs, apply a valuation discount (normalized gross margin 58%); for customer concentration, track order attribution; for geopolitics and technology substitution, use position caps.

No single risk warrants selling now, but every one of them argues against adding at RMB 2,410. That is the practical use of a risk list—it does not tell you what to buy; it tells you when not to buy.

A final note on how to use probabilities. These percentages are not precise forecasts; they are a ranking tool. Knowing "cycle 30%, geopolitics 3%" is far more useful than knowing "there are many risks"—because it tells you the near-term threat is the cycle and the long-term threat is geopolitics, and the two require completely different responses: one monitoring indicators, the other a position cap.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: of the five risks, one is genuinely lethal (geopolitics), one is the most likely to materialise (cycle) and three are chronic. Only one action follows: position discipline — do not add at highs.
