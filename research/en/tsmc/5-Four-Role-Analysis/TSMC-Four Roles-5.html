Team Lead summary — TSMC

Points of divergence and adjudication across the four reports

Core view

Four independent reports agree completely on business quality (top-tier score) but split on price: the commercial and industry perspectives say "wait for a pullback," while the financial and risk perspectives say "hold at the current price but do not add." Verdict: hold; add only below RMB 2,000. The disagreement stems not from facts but from time horizon.

1. First point of contention: what is the calculated five-year return?

From Duan Yongping's perspective, the five-year annualized return is 2%–5% (neutral scenario with a 15x exit multiple); from Buffett's perspective, buying at 22.7x and holding long term earns "EPS growth + dividends," or 8%–10% annualized (assuming an 18–20x exit). The difference lies in the exit multiple.

Verdict: A 15x exit is more conservative and better reflects the long-term midpoint for an asset-heavy cyclical stock. TSMC's PE midpoint from 2015–2025 was roughly 18x, and 22.7x sits at the upper end of its historical range. Duan Yongping's approach is therefore closer to reality; Buffett's optimism rests on "the market granting a long-term growth premium," a phenomenon that only emerged after 2020.

II. Second Point of Divergence: Is the Gross Margin Decline a Risk or an Opportunity

The Munger lens reads the Q3 gross margin guidance pullback (66% midpoint, -1.7pct QoQ) as the first signal of a cyclical peak; the financial lens reads it as normal dilution from the 2nm ramp, a case of "spending money to buy the future."

Verdict: both are right, but on different time horizons. Near term (2–3 quarters) it is dilution, and management gave explicit reasons (2nm 3–4pct, overseas fabs 2–3pct); medium term (2027–2028) hinges on whether gross margin can recover after the dilution—if gross margin returns above 65% once 2nm is fully loaded, pricing power remains; if it cannot, the shift is structural.

One layer deeper: the gross margin decline is not linear. 2nm dilution will be most pronounced from H2 2026 to H1 2027 (the ramp period), while overseas fab dilution intensifies year by year (Arizona Fab 2, Kumamoto Fab 2 and Dresden coming online in succession). With the two overlapping, 2027 is the trough for gross margin, after which full 2nm loading and depreciation absorption could drive a 2–3 percentage point recovery. So the "below 60%" falsification threshold has a decent probability of being hit in some quarter of 2027—we need to decide in advance how to act then.

III. Disagreement point three: should geopolitical risk be discounted

The risk lens says apply a discount; the business lens says do not abandon a good company over an unpredictable risk. This is the oldest disagreement in value investing.

Verdict: no discount, but cap the position. The reason is that a discount cannot be quantified (you do not know how much to discount), whereas a position can be. Holding TSMC within a 15% single-position cap is more honest than stuffing a 10% geopolitical discount into the valuation model.

IV. The Consensus: This Is the Best AI Picks-and-Shovels Play

The four reports do not disagree on one fact: August 2026 revenue was RMB 514.806 billion, up 53.3% year on year, a fourth consecutive record month, with no sign that AI demand is slowing. Q2 net profit was RMB 706.56 billion, up 77.4% year on year, with EPS of RMB 27.25 — the best quarter in history.

The disagreement is only over "what this good news is worth." The market's answer is 27.9x TTM and 22.7x forward — not cheap, but not crazy either. This is exactly the typical scenario for a "Hold" rating: no reason to sell, and no reason to chase.

V. Execution checklist

1. Positions already held: no action; review gross margin and capex quarterly.

2. For those with no position: set two entry points — first tranche at RMB 2,000 (19x 2026) scaled in, second tranche at RMB 1,800 (17x).

3. The only condition that triggers trimming: 2027 capex raised again + monthly revenue turning negative month-on-month for two consecutive months.

4. Conditions that trigger a review: quarterly gross margin falling below 60%, or Arizona fab yield significantly lagging Taiwan fabs.

One addition: if the Q3 earnings call gives preliminary 2027 capex guidance of above USD 65 billion while Q3 actual gross margin comes in below 65% (the low end of guidance), that is the combination of "accelerating investment + falling returns" — a signal to trim, not to add. Conversely, if 2027 capex holds at USD 60 billion and gross margin holds at 66%, the 2nm ramp is going more smoothly than expected; maintain the existing position and consider adding modestly near RMB 2,100. Both branches must be decided on the day of the earnings call, not deferred to quarter-end.

Conclusion

Verdict: Hold — all five tests passed, margin of safety barely adequate. The four differ on time horizon, not on facts. Execution replaces forecasting with position size plus price levels: add in tranches below RMB 2,000; revisit the thesis if gross margin falls below 60%.
