## Headline and lead

Alternative headline 1: The world's best manufacturer — buy now and you might make just 2% over five years

Alternative headline 2: 67.7% gross margin — and still I would not chase TSMC

Lead: TSMC has just posted its best quarter ever: revenue up 36%, net profit up 77%, gross margin a record 67.7%. Then the company said it itself — gross margin falls next quarter. That line matters more than the whole report.

## Part 1: Lay out the numbers first (about 350 words)

TSMC's 2026Q2 revenue reached TWD 1.27 trillion, up 36% YoY, with net profit after tax of TWD 706.56 billion, up 77% YoY, and earnings per share of TWD 27.25. Gross margin came in at 67.7%, 9 percentage points above the prior-year period. August revenue was TWD 514.806 billion, up 53.3% YoY, breaking TWD 500 billion for the first time and setting a record high for the fourth consecutive month.

By any industry standard these numbers are monstrous. Net profit growth running at twice revenue growth is operating leverage at work: with capacity fully loaded, incremental revenue flows almost entirely to the bottom line.

The mix is more telling still. High-performance computing accounted for 66% of revenue, up 20% QoQ, while smartphones made up just 22% and are still shrinking. TSMC's growth is now fully tied to AI servers and no longer hinges on whether consumers upgrade their phones.

## Part Two: Why can it charge so much (about 350 words)

A 2nm wafer is reportedly quoted at about USD 30,000 — ten times the price of China's mature nodes. Why don't customers bargain? Because there is nowhere else to make it.

Designing an AI chip costs USD 500 million, and each tape-out USD 30 million. Fail the first time and the loss isn't money — it's 6 to 9 months. In the AI market, nine months late means losing a generation. Customers aren't buying silicon wafers; they're buying "launch on schedule."

That is the essence of TSMC's business: turn uncertainty into certainty, then charge a fee. Its moat has four legs — a one-to-two-generation technology lead, 12 to 18 months of rework for customers who switch fabs, a USD 60 billion annual capex threshold, and the trust of "I don't make chips, I only build them for you." Only one of the four is leaking: capacity is too concentrated in Taiwan.

## Part Three: Where is the turning point (approx. 350 words)

The company's own guidance for the third quarter is a gross margin of 65% to 67%, with a midpoint of 66%, 1.7 percentage points lower than the second quarter. The reasons are clearly stated: the new 2-nanometer process is ramping, diluting margin by 3 to 4 percentage points; new overseas fabs carry high costs, diluting it by a further 2 to 3 percentage points.

Neither reason is bad: the company is trading short-term margin for long-term capacity. But the market needs to get used to one thing: revenue will keep hitting new highs, while profit growth will fall first. In the foundry industry's history, profit peaks always come one to two quarters before revenue peaks.

At the same time, full-year capital expenditure was raised from USD 52 billion to 56 billion to USD 60 billion to 64 billion. Customers are pushing for it, which is good near term. But depreciation will concentrate in 2027 to 2028, adding USD 13 billion a year, and that is the source of gross margin pressure next year and the year after.

## Part Four: Crunching the Numbers (approx. 350 words)

At TWD 2,410, the stock trades on 2026 EPS of roughly TWD 106, or 22.7x. Expensive? Depends on the comparison.

Base case: 2027 EPS of TWD 125 at 21x gives TWD 2,625, 9% upside. Bear case: AI capex stalls, 2027 EPS only TWD 95 at 18x gives TWD 1,710, 29% downside. Bull case: demand extends into 2028, EPS TWD 140 at 24x gives TWD 3,360, 39% upside.

9% upside against 29% downside. That risk/reward does not justify a new position.

Nor should you sell. The bear case requires AI capex to hit an abrupt brake, and no order data supports that today — August revenue is still hitting new highs and customers are still prepaying for capacity. The answer: hold what you have, and wait for below TWD 2,000 to add.

One more number worth remembering: the dividend yield is only 1.0%. While you wait for the stock to rise, you get almost no cash return. That is a stress test for buy-and-hold investors — can you hold for three years on conviction alone, without the dividend?

## Closing: in plain terms (about 150 words)

A good company is not the same as a good price. TSMC is the most certain AI business in the world, but that certainty is already in the price — at 22x forward P/E, the market has already paid you for 2027 growth in advance.

At 2,410, TSMC is a hold, not a chase. If you want to buy, wait for below 2,000 — or wait for a genuinely bad headline.In the stock market, patience is not a virtue; it is the only free lunch.

One last point that may not be popular: anyone who bought TSMC over the past two years made money, which breeds the illusion that "buying at any time is right." But those who bought at 688 in 2022 waited a year and a half just to break even.Good companies can also keep you underwater for a long time; the difference is only that they eventually come back.
