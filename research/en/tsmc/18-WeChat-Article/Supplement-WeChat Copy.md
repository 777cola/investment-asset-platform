## Headline and lead

**Lead:** TSMC just delivered its best quarterly report ever — revenue up 36%, net profit up 77%, gross margin at a record 67.7%. Then it said itself: gross margin will fall next quarter. That sentence matters more than the entire report.

## Part 1: Lay out the numbers first (about 350 words)

In the second quarter of 2026, TSMC revenue reached TWD 1.27 trillion, up 36% year on year; net profit after tax was TWD 706.56 billion, up 77% year on year; earnings per share were TWD 27.25. Gross margin was 67.7%, 9 percentage points higher than the same period last year. August revenue was TWD 514.806 billion, up 53.3% year on year, breaking TWD 500 billion for the first time and hitting a record high for the fourth consecutive month.

By any industry standard, these numbers are monstrous. Net profit growth is twice revenue growth — that is operating leverage: when capacity is fully loaded, the incremental revenue flows almost entirely to profit.

More notable is the mix: high-performance computing accounted for 66% of revenue, up 20% quarter on quarter, while smartphones accounted for only 22% and are still declining. In other words, TSMC's growth is now fully tied to AI servers and no longer depends on whether consumers upgrade their phones.

## Part Two: Why can it charge so much (about 350 words)

A 2nm wafer is reportedly quoted at about USD 30,000, ten times that of China's mature nodes. Why don't customers bargain? Because there is nowhere else to make it.

An AI chip costs USD 500 million to design and USD 30 million per tape-out. Fail the first time and what you lose isn't money — it's 6 to 9 months. In the AI market, 9 months late means losing a generation. So customers aren't buying silicon wafers; they're buying "launch on schedule."

That is the essence of TSMC's business: turn uncertainty into certainty, then charge a fee. Its moat has four legs — a one-to-two-generation technology lead, 12 to 18 months of rework for customers who switch fabs, a USD 60 billion annual capex threshold, and the trust of "I don't make chips, I only build them for you." Of the four legs, only one is leaking: capacity is too concentrated in Taiwan.

## Part Three: Where is the turning point (approx. 350 words)

The company's own guidance for the third quarter is a gross margin of 65% to 67%, with a midpoint of 66%, 1.7 percentage points lower than the second quarter. The reasons are clearly stated: the new 2-nanometer process is ramping, diluting margin by 3 to 4 percentage points; new overseas fabs carry high costs, diluting it by a further 2 to 3 percentage points.

Neither reason is bad: the company is trading short-term margin for long-term capacity. But the market needs to get used to one thing: **revenue will keep hitting new highs, while profit growth will fall first.** In the foundry industry's history, profit peaks always come one to two quarters before revenue peaks.

At the same time, full-year capital expenditure was raised from USD 52 billion to 56 billion to USD 60 billion to 64 billion. Customers are pushing for it, which is good near term. But depreciation will concentrate in 2027 to 2028, adding USD 13 billion a year, and that is the source of gross margin pressure next year and the year after.

## Part Four: Crunching the Numbers (approx. 350 words)

The stock trades at TWD 2,410 now, with 2026 EPS of roughly TWD 106, or 22.7x. Is that expensive? Depends what you compare it to.

Base case: 2027 EPS of TWD 125, 21x, TWD 2,625, up 9%. Bear case: AI capex stalls, 2027 EPS only TWD 95, 18x, TWD 1,710, down 29%. Bull case: demand extends into 2028, EPS TWD 140 at 24x, TWD 3,360, up 39%.

Up 9%, down 29%. That risk/reward does not justify a new position.

But nor should you sell. The bear case requires AI capex to hit an abrupt brake, and no order data supports that today — August revenue is still hitting new highs and customers are still prepaying for capacity. So the answer is: hold what you have, and wait for below TWD 2,000 to add.

One more number worth remembering: the dividend yield is only 1.0%. That means while you wait for the stock to rise, you get almost no cash return. That is a stress test for buy-and-hold investors — can you hold for three years on conviction alone, without the dividend?

## Closing: in plain terms (about 150 words)

A good company is not the same as a good price. TSMC is the world's most certain AI business, but that certainty is already priced in — 22x forward PE means the market has already paid you for 2027 growth in advance.

TSMC at 2,410 is a hold, not a chase. If you want to buy, wait for below 2,000, or wait for a genuinely bad headline.**In the stock market, patience is not a virtue — it is the only free lunch.**

One last point that may not be popular: anyone who bought TSMC over the past two years made money, which breeds the illusion that "buying at any time is right." But those who bought at 688 in 2022 waited a year and a half just to break even.**Good companies can also keep you underwater for a long time; the difference is only that they eventually come back.**
