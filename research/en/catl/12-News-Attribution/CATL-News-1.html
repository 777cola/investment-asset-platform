Core thesis

Seven things happened to CATL between July and September, and the stock fell from its February high of RMB 458.60 to a 52-week low of RMB 291.99 on 28 September. Three of the seven are real signals: the consumption tax taking effect, Li Auto's capital injection into Sunwoda, and the step-down in Q2 growth; two are sentiment noise: the media narrative of "fleeing CATL" and the self-reinforcing effect of the new low itself.

The attribution is clear: the market is not selling the earnings, it is selling the certainty of share. Earnings kept beating, certainty kept falling.

Li Auto's capital injection into Sunwoda turns "de-CATL-ization" into a template

RMB 2.65 billion and 11.17% look modest, but their significance is outsized: automakers are no longer simply "adding another supplier" — they are becoming direct shareholders in suppliers and locking costs into equity. Li Auto's new L8 switching entirely to Sunwoda is the result of this shift, not the cause.

Why is this the most important point? Because it is replicable. Any automaker that runs the numbers — RMB 50 billion and five years to build its own battery plant, versus a few billion and two years to take a stake in a second-tier player — will follow. This is a transfer of supply-chain power, not a one-off procurement decision.

The scale does not warrant panic: Li Auto and Xiaomi together account for 13.6% of domestic installations. They can compress CATL's gross margin, but they cannot crush its share. Once the paradigm takes hold, however, it will spread to 25%–30% of domestic installations in 2027–2028.

The overestimated one: consumption tax

Effective September 1 at 2%, rising to 4% in September 2027. The market reads this as "profit about to halve". The actual magnitude: first-half battery revenue of RMB 245.386 billion, or RMB 490.8 billion annualised; 2% is roughly RMB 9.8 billion, equal to 10% of the RMB 95.2 billion of net profit expected for 2026, while 4% is roughly RMB 19.6 billion, or 20% (before pass-through).

The real issue is not the tax rate but structural unfairness. BYD uses its batteries in-house, so the tax falls at the vehicle-assembly stage and the impact is diluted; CATL sells cells externally, so the tax lands directly on it. The same policy imposes unequal burdens on the two.

Can it be passed through? Domestically most likely not, as tier-2 players are undercutting for orders; overseas it can be, as European customers accept a premium. So the actual impact is 5%–10%, not 20%. This is "genuinely bad news", not "fatally bad news".

The third genuine signal: growth steps down

Q1 net profit +48.5%, Q2 +36.5% — a 12-point drop in a single quarter. This is not a base effect: Q2 revenue was still accelerating (+56.9%). Accelerating revenue with decelerating profit means price concessions are widening.

The market's reaction has been the most rational: it simply reclassified the valuation from "growth stock" to "manufacturer." The 14x P/E is not the product of a selloff; it is the product of a de-rating.

What counts as noise: the new low itself, and the "flight" narrative

The September 15 piece "Stock Hits New Low for the Year: 'Flight' from CATL" carried almost no information — it described the price, not the company. The 52-week low on September 28, with the stock down just 0.51% that day on no pickup in volume, shows that selling was persistent, not panicked: institutions were trimming slowly rather than rushing for the exits.

This kind of noise is self-reinforcing: the stock falls, the media reports, more holders have doubts, and it keeps falling. It does not change the fundamentals, but it does change the price you pay. For anyone building a position in tranches, noise is a gift, not a risk.

The transmission chain from event to valuation

Put these seven events together and the transmission chain reads as follows: growth steps down (7/24) → valuation de-rates from growth stock to manufacturer (multiple 25x→14x) → de-CATLization narrative takes shape (9/18) → consumption tax confirms the cost (9/1) → share price hits a 52-week low (9/28). Each step gives the next a reason to happen — that is "narrative self-reinforcement."

Breaking the chain takes only one counterexample: an automaker switching a second supplier back to the primary, or growth returning above 40% in a quarter. No one announces this in advance; you can only wait for the quarterly report. The right stance now is "hold and watch," not "add and average down."

One more contrarian indicator: who is buying

It hit a 52-week low on September 28, down just 0.51% on the day with no pickup in volume—this is "sustained de-risking," not "panic selling." Panic selling typically brings heavy volume and a long bearish candle; a slow bleed instead points to institutions trimming positions in line with their models (a lower valuation centre), not a rejection of the company.

The real bottom signal often shows up on the day when "earnings beat consensus but the stock stops falling."That hasn't happened yet, so for now buy only the first tranche.

Conclusion

Attribution: 70% of this selloff is a valuation de-rating (growth stepping down, post-Ningde-era paradigm, consumption tax), 30% a sentiment-driven stampede. All three genuine signals are already in the RMB 291.99 price; the two noise items will fade once the quarterly report lands. What is genuinely unpriced is the solid-state battery 2027 timeline and full absorption of the consumption tax — both of which must wait until 2027.
