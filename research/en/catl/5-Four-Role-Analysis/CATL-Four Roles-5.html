TeamLead summary: disagreements and rulings across the four perspectives — CATL

Four seats, three buy votes, one conditional: the split turns solely on "the nature of growth"

Core view

Four independent lenses point to the same conclusion: a good business, real profits, a structure that is loosening, and a price that has already discounted most of the bad news. Three of the four seats rate it a buy (business, financials, risk); the industry seat says "buy, but compress the valuation." The only genuine disagreement is whether the 36.5% net profit growth is temporary or a trend.

Divergence one: is slower growth temporary or structural?

The commercial desk calls it temporary: Q2 growth of 36.5% still outpaced the industry, with volumes +60%, pointing to price concessions rather than weak demand. The finance desk calls it a trend: net margin fell from 17.0% to 15.6% and unit net profit from RMB 104 to RMB 94, and neither reading is a one-off. The industry desk sides with finance, citing second-tier capacity already in place. The risk desk declines to take a position, asking only that "we survive under both scenarios."

Ruling: treat it as a trend. Cut 2027 net profit to RMB 110 billion from the consensus RMB 116.5 billion, the implied assumption in the neutral scenario, but leave the target price range unchanged — 15x times RMB 110 billion divided by 4.627 billion shares = RMB 357, a 6% gap from RMB 378 that does not change the conclusion. The real difference is position sizing: if the slowdown is a trend, we can only hold the first tranche.

Divergence 2: Can consumption tax be passed on?

The commercial desk argues that half can be passed on: CATL's 29.97% gross margin in Europe points to pricing power, and domestically it simply chooses not to use it. The finance desk argues it cannot be passed on: second-tier players are undercutting for orders, and whoever raises prices first loses share. The risk desk offered a compromise: passable overseas, not passable domestically.

Ruling: treat it as "fully absorbed domestically, partially passed on overseas," with a full-year impact of about RMB 9.8 billion pre-tax, or 7%–10% of net profit. That magnitude does not change our Buy conclusion, but it consumes half of 2027 profit growth (2027 consensus growth of 22.4%, about 12% after deducting the consumption tax impact).

Disagreement 3: How large a discount should solid-state batteries carry?

The sector seat pushed for a 20% technology-generation discount; the finance seat called the discount premature, arguing scale-up is unlikely before 2028; the commercial seat framed the issue not as a discount question but as one of "no heavy position in what we don't understand."

Ruling: no separate discount in the valuation; express it through a position cap instead — CATL's portfolio weight may not exceed half of core assets. Rationale: technology-generation risk cannot be precisely priced, so it can only be controlled through exposure.

Unified conclusion: Buy (in tranches), three gates

Four seats converge on a single execution plan: an initial position of no more than one-third of planned exposure in the RMB 291–300 range; a second tranche if Q3 single-quarter net profit grows ≥25% YoY and domestic share holds ≥44%; stop loss if power battery gross margin falls below 19%. Target price RMB 378 base case and RMB 562 bull case, with RMB 190 bear case as the floor for stress testing.

Three seats make the same point: do not buy on "long-term bullish on electrification." That rationale held in 2021 but no longer suffices in 2026—the industry is still growing, but CATL no longer monopolizes the growth. The only valid reason to buy is that share and unit profitability are verifiable, and the price already reflects the bad news.

One more point: why none of the four seats carries a "Sell"

None of the four seats recommends Sell — not out of any affection for the company, but because the case for selling does not hold. A Sell typically requires one of three conditions: stretched valuation (the stock trades at 14x, so no), deteriorating fundamentals (gross margin 20.63%, still above 19%), or a better opportunity elsewhere (no superior pure-play battery name among peers). None of the three applies, so the harshest view is merely "buy a little less."

Conclusion

Verdict: Buy (staged), consistent with the working papers. Four seats, three votes to buy and one conditional. Disagreement centred on three points — the nature of growth, excise-tax pass-through and the solid-state discount — all of which were converted into position sizing and gating conditions rather than a change in rating. The key execution line: the first tranche buys the 14x valuation, the second buys the Q3 report data and the third buys the share-price trend; for now, only the first tranche is warranted.
