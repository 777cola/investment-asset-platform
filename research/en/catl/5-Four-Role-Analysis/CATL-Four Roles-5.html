TeamLead summary: disagreements and rulings across the four perspectives — CATL

Four seats, three buy votes, one conditional vote — the split is only over "the nature of growth"
Research date 2026/09/28  ·  Price ¥291.99 (-0.51%)  ·  Market cap Approximately RMB 1.35 trillion (A+H combined 4.627 billion shares)  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 14x (2026 net profit consensus of RMB 95.2 billion)

Core view

Four independent lenses converge on one conclusion: a good business, real profits, a structure that is loosening, and a price that has already discounted most of the bad news. Three of the four seats call it a buy (business, financials, risk), while the industry seat says "buy but compress the valuation." The only real disagreement is whether the 36.5% net profit growth is temporary or a trend.

Divergence one: is the growth step-down temporary or a trend?

The commercial desk sees it as temporary — Q2 growth of 36.5% remains above the industry, with volumes +60%, indicating price concessions rather than weak demand. The finance desk sees it as a trend — net margin fell from 17.0% to 15.6% and unit net profit from RMB 104 to RMB 94, and these two readings are not one-off. The industry desk sides with finance, citing second-tier capacity already in place. The risk desk declines to take a position and asks only that "we survive under both scenarios."

Ruling: treat it as a trend. Cut 2027 net profit from the consensus of RMB 116.5 billion to RMB 110 billion as the implied assumption in the neutral scenario, but leave the target price range unchanged — because 15x times RMB 110 billion divided by 4.627 billion shares = RMB 357, a 6% gap from RMB 378 that does not change the conclusion. The real difference is position sizing: if the slowdown is a trend, we can only hold the first tranche.

Divergence 2: Can consumption tax be passed on?

The commercial desk believes half can be passed on—CATL has a 29.97% gross margin in Europe, showing it has pricing power; domestically it simply chooses not to use it. The finance desk believes it cannot be passed on—second-tier players are undercutting for orders, and whoever raises prices first loses share. The risk desk offered a compromise: passable overseas, not passable domestically.

Ruling: treat it as "fully absorbed domestically, partially passed on overseas," with a full-year impact of about RMB 9.8 billion pre-tax, or 7%–10% of net profit. This magnitude will not change the Buy conclusion, but it will consume half of 2027 profit growth (2027 consensus growth of 22.4%, about 12% after deducting the consumption tax impact).

Disagreement 3: How much of a discount should solid-state batteries carry?

The sector seat asked for a 20% technology-generation discount; the finance seat argued the discount is premature (scale-up unlikely before 2028); the commercial seat argued this is not a discount question but a "no heavy position in what we don't understand" question.

Ruling: do not break out a separate discount in the valuation; express it through a position cap instead — CATL's portfolio weight may not exceed half of core assets. Rationale: technology-generation risk cannot be precisely priced, so it can only be controlled through exposure.

Unified conclusion: Buy (in tranches), three gates

Four seats converge on one execution plan: initial position no more than one-third of planned exposure (RMB 291–300 range); add a second tranche if Q3 single-quarter net profit grows ≥25% YoY and domestic share holds ≥44%; stop loss if power battery gross margin falls below 19%. Target price: RMB 378 base case, RMB 562 bull case, with RMB 190 bear case as the floor for stress testing.

The same point from three seats: do not use "long-term bullish on electrification" as a reason to buy. That rationale held in 2021 but no longer suffices in 2026—the industry is still growing, but CATL no longer monopolizes the growth. The only valid reason to buy is that "share and unit profitability are verifiable, and the price already reflects the bad news."

One more point: why none of the four seats carries a "Sell"

None of the four seats recommends Sell, not because they "like the company" but because "the case for selling does not hold." A Sell usually requires one of three conditions: excessive valuation (it is at 14x now, so no), deteriorating fundamentals (gross margin 20.63%, still above 19%), or a better opportunity found (no better pure battery play among peers). None of the three holds, so the worst opinion is only "buy a little less."

Conclusion

Verdict: Buy (staged), consistent with the working papers. Four seats, three votes to buy, one conditional; disagreement centred on three points — the nature of growth, excise-tax pass-through, and the solid-state discount — all converted into position sizing and gating conditions rather than a change in rating. The single most important line on execution: the first tranche buys the 14x valuation, the second tranche buys the Q3 report data, the third tranche buys the share trend — for now, only the first tranche is warranted.
