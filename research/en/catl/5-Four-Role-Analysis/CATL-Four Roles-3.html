Is the industry structure improving or deteriorating: share, second-tier players, technology routes

Core view

Munger asked only one question about an industry: is competition in this industry decreasing or increasing. For power batteries the answer is "increasing" — in 2026 second-tier manufacturers are trading joint-venture plants for design wins, automakers are actively cultivating second suppliers, and the industry is shifting from "one leader, many strong players" toward "two leaders, many strong players." This is not a demand problem; it is a structural one.

The structure is deteriorating: three forces squeeze at once

The first force is automakers' demand for bargaining power. Li Auto injected RMB 2.65 billion into Sunwoda Power for an 11.17% stake; Xiaomi's "Longjia Battery" uses cells from CALB and Sunwoda; AITO brought in CALB and Gotion. These are not price negotiations but a shift in supply-chain power — automakers are moving from "choosing suppliers" to "cultivating suppliers."

The second force is second-tier players' capacity coming online. In the past, second-tier players could not win orders because of poor yield and delivery; by 2026 this problem is largely solved, and what remains is only brand trust. Technology diffuses faster than expected.

The third force is the technology roadmap. Solid-state batteries: semi-solid-state small-scale installation in 2026, all-solid-state small-batch trial production in 2027 — CATL does not lead this round. If solid-state reaches mass production early in 2027, the existing liquid-electrolyte capacity advantage will be discounted.

Together, these three forces shift the industry structure from "winner takes all" to "the winner's share slowly erodes." This is the type of change Munger disliked most: not collapse, but continuous seepage.

But some things have not changed: demand is still growing above 50%

In the first half of 2026, CATL's revenue rose 54.8% and volumes 60%, showing that industry volume is still expanding rapidly. The global electrification rate is still climbing, and energy storage is the new increment (CATL's energy storage revenue +87.5%). The industry is growing; only the division of the pie is changing.

This determines the investment math: share fell from 40.2% to 35%, but if industry volume rises 40%, absolute volume still grows. "De-CATLization" hits valuation, not revenue. What would truly hit revenue is industry growth stalling—that requires the electrification rate to near saturation, which is not a 2026 event.

Cycle position: mid-to-late stage of capacity expansion

CATL has 764GWh under construction, and second-tier players are also expanding capacity; global battery capacity will be in clear oversupply in 2027–2028. The direct consequence of oversupply is that price wars become the norm, and gross margin sliding further from 20.63% is the high-probability path. This resembles the PV module situation in 2019–2020, except that batteries carry safety and integration barriers, so the decline will be slower.

A Munger-style judgment: this is an industry with good demand and even better supply, and even the best company can only earn manufacturing-level margins. Valuation therefore cannot carry growth-stock multiples, and position sizing cannot carry core-asset weight.

Conclusion

Sector rating: B. Demand is growing; the structure is deteriorating. CATL still sits in the top tier, but "top" no longer means "exclusive." Investment implication: you can earn from industry growth, not from share gains; anchor valuation to manufacturing (12–18x), not to growth stocks (25x and above).
