Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — CATL

Core view

Li Lu's risk framework ignores volatility and focuses on "permanent capital loss." CATL faces five paths to permanent loss: a structural disadvantage on consumption tax, de-CATLization shifting from isolated cases to a paradigm, a generational technology lag in solid-state batteries, political risk on overseas capacity, and overcapacity pushing gross margin down a long-term step. The first two are already unfolding, the third is a 2027 issue, and the last two are slow variables.

The first point is the most certain: consumption tax is a structural disadvantage, not a one-off shock

From September 1, 2026, a 2% consumption tax will be reinstated on lithium-ion batteries, rising to 4% in September 2027. CATL mainly sells cells externally, so the tax burden falls directly on the company; BYD uses its batteries in-house, so the tax lands at the vehicle-assembly stage, a far smaller impact. The same policy imposes different burdens on the two companies — this is structural.

Sizing it: first-half battery revenue was RMB 245.386 billion, or RMB 490.8 billion annualized; 2% is RMB 9.8 billion, equal to 10% of the RMB 95.2 billion expected net profit for 2026; at 4% it is RMB 19.6 billion, about 20%. How much can be passed through is the key — if half can be shifted to automakers via price increases, the actual impact halves; if none can be passed through (likely, as second-tier players are undercutting for orders), it is a full 10%–20%.

The second point hurts most: de-Ningdeification is a paradigm, not an isolated case

After Li Auto took an 11.17% stake in Sunwoda Power, "build a joint-venture plant in exchange for a design win" became a replicable template. The automakers' logic is hard-nosed: rather than negotiate prices with CATL every year, become a shareholder and lock costs into equity. Once this model is proven, other automakers will follow.

Scale estimate: Li Auto and Xiaomi together account for 13.6% of domestic installations. If within three years the second tier spreads from these two to 30% of domestic installations, CATL's domestic share falls from 46.7% to 35%–38%, implying an 8%–12% revenue decline (rough calculation). This is not a collapse; it turns a "single dominant player" into "one leader and one strong challenger."

The third is optionality: the generational risk of solid-state batteries

CATL is the strongest in the world on the liquid lithium-ion route, but the solid-state battery race is a different track. Semi-solid-state batteries begin small-scale installation in 2026, and all-solid-state enters small-batch trial production in 2027 — CATL is not the fastest. If solid-state reaches scale in 2028, depreciation on the existing 500GWh-class liquid production lines will become a burden.

This risk is characterized by "uncertain timing, certain magnitude." It will not show up in the 2026 financials, but it will weigh on valuation — the market is unwilling to pay a high price for capacity that could be wiped out by a technology transition. This is part of the reason for the 14x PE.

The fourth and fifth points are slow variables: overseas politics and overcapacity

Overseas politics: the US Department of Defense's 1260H list in early 2025 — the company has already filed suit; European clients' boards will raise this issue. The response is to move capacity to customers' doorstep (Hungary's initial 40GWh already booked, Spain joint venture with Stellantis, Indonesia starting mass production by year-end). Localization hedges list risk, but drags down overseas gross margins — this is trading profit for market.

Overcapacity: CATL has 764GWh under construction, and second-tier players are also expanding; global capacity will be markedly oversupplied in 2027–2028. The direct consequence of overcapacity is that price wars become the norm, and a further 2–4 point decline in power battery gross margin from 20.63% is highly likely. This will not push the company into losses, but it will turn it into "a normal manufacturing company."

Conclusion

Risk rating: manageable but not light. Two of the five are already unfolding (consumption tax, de-Ningdeification), one is a 2027 technology option (solid-state), and two are slow variables (politics, overcapacity). None will push the company into loss within a year, but together they are enough to keep the valuation capped at 12–15x. Investment implication: this is not "low risk, high return" but "medium risk, slightly positive odds." Position sizing must leave room for risk.
