Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — CATL

What makes this a good business: who pays, why they pay, and how long they keep paying

Core view

In one sentence: CATL turns lithium, nickel, iron and graphite into battery cells and sells them to automakers and grid operators, using scale to drive unit costs to the lowest in the world, then leveraging its 40% share to turn procurement discounts and the pace of technology iteration into a second layer of cost advantage. It earns manufacturing money, not brand money; the unit economics are calculable, and the ceiling is visible.

Who pays: the automakers foot the bill, but bargaining power is shifting toward them

The customer structure is clear: major global automakers (Tesla, BMW, Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz, Li Auto, Xiaomi, AITO, NIO) plus energy-storage integrators and grid operators. First-half sales of 440GWh and revenue of RMB 276.9 billion imply an average of RMB 629 million per GWh (rough calculation). The payers are highly concentrated — both a blessing and a curse: large customers bring certainty, but also tender-based price comparison.

Why do they pay? Because the battery accounts for 30%–40% of vehicle cost and is the primary determinant of range, safety and charging speed. Automakers dare not use unvalidated cells; a new product's validation cycle runs two years. This is exactly what Duan Yongping values most — "can customers switch away?" They can, but it takes two years, fresh safety certification and exposure to recall risk.

But in 2026 that premise is loosening. Li Auto's new L8 switches entirely to Sunwoda, and Li Auto is also injecting RMB 2.65 billion into Sunwoda Power for an 11.17% stake; Xiaomi's "Longjia Battery" cells come from CALB and Sunwoda; AITO has brought in CALB and Gotion. The essence of these moves is not "dropping CATL" but "denying CATL exclusivity." Automakers want pricing power, not to replace the best supplier. So share will slip, but it will not collapse.

Why pay longer: one and a half of three criteria met

Can it raise prices? No. Power battery gross margin was 20.63%, down 1.78 points year on year; energy storage fell 1.56 points. CATL can tell automakers "I won't do this price," but it dare not say that to the whole market—second-tier manufacturers are waiting to take the orders. Its cost advantage guarantees "earning more than second-tier players," but cannot guarantee "earning more than last year."

Can customers switch away? Not in the short term. Two years of validation + safety liability + production line retrofits—switching costs are real. But in the long run, automakers are amortizing this cost through "joint-venture plants + dual sourcing"—this is the moat being bypassed, not breached.

Does it need to keep investing? Yes. 764GWh under construction, roughly RMB 230–310 billion in capex at RMB 3–4 billion/GWh. This is the hard flaw: a good business is one where "this year's earnings can be distributed"; CATL is one where "this year's earnings must become next year's capacity."

One and a half out of three pass; the conclusion is "a good business, but not a Moutai-kind of business." The buying point for such companies is when the industry is at its most pessimistic and the company's share is still intact—which is exactly now.

At what price to buy: 12x 2027 is the starting point, not the end point

Duan Yongping's method is crude: normalized profit times a "cheap enough" multiple. The 2027 consensus net profit is RMB 116.5 billion on 4.627 billion shares, or RMB 25.18 per share; the current price of RMB 291.99 corresponds to 11.6x. If 15x is deemed fair value for a manufacturer, the current price is at a 23% discount.

He does not buy all at once. His habit is to wait until bad news has run its course, and in September 2026 the bad news was still coming: the consumption tax took effect on September 1, a report on relocating to Ninghua came in mid-September, and a 52-week low was hit on September 28. His move would be to buy a little first, then add once the Q3 report confirms growth is no longer stepping down. That is the origin of "phased buying" in the working notes.

Conclusion

Business rating: B+. Understandable, calculable, number one in share, but weak pricing power and capital-intensive. The buy trigger is "peak industry pessimism + share still intact + valuation at the low end of the manufacturing range"; the RMB 291.99 price in September 2026 met half of all three — pessimism sufficient, share still eroding, valuation low enough. So buy, but only the first tranche.
