Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — CATL

ROE, cash, capex: is this company a money machine
Research date 2026/09/28  ·  Price ¥291.99 (-0.51%)  ·  Market cap Approximately RMB 1.35 trillion (A+H combined 4.627 billion shares)  ·  PE(2026E) Approximately 14x (2026 net profit consensus of RMB 95.2 billion)

Core view

Buffett's three questions: is ROE high enough, is profit backed by cash, and must all the money earned be plowed back in. CATL passes the first two (ten-year average ROE of 22%–25%, net cash ratio around 1.9x) but fails the third — it is a "money-making machine," but also a "money-swallowing machine."

Quality of ROE: high, but not built on leverage

A ten-year average ROE of 22%–25% ranks at the top of heavy-asset manufacturing. More important is the source: it does not lift ROE by adding leverage—interest-bearing debt is under 10% of liabilities, and the 60%–65% debt-to-asset ratio consists mainly of accounts payable and contract liabilities (funds owed to upstream suppliers, advances from downstream customers). This is "doing business with other people's money," not "borrowing against one's own assets."

A DuPont breakdown (rough calculation for 2026H1): net margin 15.6% × asset turnover of about 0.5x × equity multiplier of about 2.7x ≈ 21%. All three factors are healthy: margins are not thin, turnover is not slow, leverage is not high. This is a structure Buffett would nod at.

The only problem is the trend. Net margin has fallen from 17.0% to 15.6%; if it drops to 13% in two years, ROE will follow to around 18%—still a good company, but no longer "first-rate." The speed of ROE decline deserves more attention than the absolute level of ROE.

Cash: earned, but not retained

Operating cash flow/net profit has averaged about 1.9x over the past three years, confirming profits are real cash. The battery business has an advantage in that customers are automakers, bad debt is low, and deposits can be collected upfront; the downside is long payment cycles and the need to front material costs. Overall, its profit quality ranks in the top 10% of manufacturing.

But free cash flow can only "barely get by." It was negative during the heaviest capacity expansion in 2021–2022, and has been covered by operating cash flow since 2023. The reason lies in 764GWh under construction—at the industry intensity of RMB 300–400 million/GWh, that is a bill of RMB 230–310 billion. Buffett would ask: once this capacity is built, how much money is needed to maintain it? Battery production lines have a technology iteration cycle of about 3–5 years, meaning this batch of capacity will need to be retrofitted for next-generation processes before it is even fully depreciated.

This is why he does not value growth stocks: for a company that must constantly convert profit into capacity, its "owner earnings" are far below net profit. By his measure, CATL's owner earnings may be only 60–70% of net profit—which directly turns a 14x PE into over 20x.

Capital allocation: the money is being spent on the right things, but too fast

Both major fundraisings (the RMB 45 billion private placement in 2022 and the roughly HKD 35.6 billion H-share offering in 2025) went entirely into capacity — not into buying into sectors, ecosystems or traffic. For a manufacturing company, this is the least error-prone way to spend. When the share price hit a new low in September 2026, the company announced a RMB 20–40 billion buyback and an interim dividend of RMB 6.53 billion — also a clear value signal.

But spending correctly is not the same as spending well. The test is the return on the new capacity: if each GWh costs RMB 300–400 million to build, generates RMB 600 million of revenue and RMB 100 million of net profit a year (rough calculation), the payback period is 3–4 years and IRR above 20% — that return is worth investing in, provided the output can be sold once built. A capacity utilization rate of 94.86% shows it can be sold now; whether it can be sold when 764GWh comes on stream in two years is another question.

Conclusion

Financial rating: A-. ROE is high and clean, earnings are backed by real cash, capital allocation avoids mistakes, and shareholder returns are improving. Marks are deducted for capital intensity and owner earnings—it is a good machine, but it needs constant refueling. On Buffett's terms, applying 15x normalized profit implies RMB 378 for 2027; at the current price of RMB 291.99, the stock is "reasonably undervalued," not a "free lunch."
