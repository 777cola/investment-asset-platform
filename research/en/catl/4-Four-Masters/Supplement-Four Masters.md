## Duan Yongping: A good business, but not a Moutai-kind business

Duan Yongping judges a good business by just three things: whether it can raise prices, whether customers can switch away, and whether it must keep investing capital. CATL passes one and a half of the three — **a customer switching suppliers must redo two years of validation, so switching costs are real**; but it cannot raise prices, as evidenced by its power-battery gross margin of 20.63%, down 1.78 points year on year; and it must keep investing capital, with 764GWh of capacity under construction at RMB 300–400 million/GWh amounting to RMB 230–310 billion.

How would he price it? Duan Yongping bought Apple, Moutai and Tencent — in every case buying "predictable cash flow that does not depend on reinvestment to defend its position." CATL fails the last test. So he would most likely assign it a "manufacturing price" — at 15x normalized profit, 2027 earnings of RMB 116.5 billion imply RMB 378. **The current price of RMB 291.99 is 12x 2027 earnings, already inside the range where he would start looking.**

But he would not buy all at once. Duan Yongping's habit is to "wait until the bad news is all out," and CATL's bad news is still coming — the consumption tax taking effect on September 1, mid-September reports of automakers moving away from CATL, and a 52-week low on September 28. **His move would be: buy a little first, then add once Q3 data confirms growth is no longer stepping down.** That is exactly the "tranche" discipline in the memo.

## Buffett: A 40% share is a real moat, but technological shifts will breach it

Buffett looks at two things first: ROE and return on capital. CATL's ten-year average ROE is 22%–25%, with a net margin of 15.6% — excellent for an asset-heavy manufacturer. Overseas gross margin is 29.97%, 8.8 points higher than domestic, showing it has pricing power in Europe — **the same cell earns nearly half again as much gross profit when sold in Europe**, and that is where it most resembles a "good business."

What would make him frown is the technology roadmap. Batteries are the fastest-iterating component in vehicle technology: semi-solid-state cells begin installation in 2026, with small-batch trial production of all-solid-state in 2027, and CATL is not leading this round. Buffett's stance on technological change has always been avoidance — he buys companies that will still be selling the same thing in ten years, whereas the cells CATL sells in ten years may be a generation removed in chemistry.

So his valuation would cap at within 15x, treating CATL as a cost leader in manufacturing rather than a growth stock. **By that standard, RMB 291.99 is a fair price, not a cheap one.** That also explains why he would agree to buy but not take a heavy position: the 40% share gives him confidence, and technological uncertainty sets his position ceiling.

## Munger: What 36% has already priced in, and what has not

Munger's first instinct is to turn the question around: what does the market already know? From RMB 458.60 to RMB 291.99, the combined A+H wipeout is roughly RMB 770 billion (rough calculation: RMB 166.61 × 4.627 billion shares). **That decline has already priced in "growth falling from 48% to 36%", "second-tier players grabbing orders" and "a 2% consumption tax".**

He would list three things not yet fully priced: ① the consumption tax rising to 4% in September 2027 — annualizing first-half battery revenue of RMB 490.8 billion, the tax burden doubles from RMB 9.8 billion to RMB 19.6 billion, **equivalent to cutting 2027 expected net profit by more than 15%** (before any pass-through); ② after Li Auto's 11.17% stake in Sunwoda Power, "joint-venture plants in exchange for design wins" becomes the industry paradigm, with second-tier makers trading equity for orders, structurally diverting CATL's share; ③ if solid-state batteries reach mass production early in 2027, depreciation on 500GWh-class liquid-electrolyte capacity becomes a burden.

If just two of the three happen, 14x becomes 20x — but earnings will be lower by then too. The Munger-style conclusion: **"This is not a bet that requires intelligence, it is a bet that requires patience."** The odds are sufficient, but don't expect payoff within a year.

## Li Lu: In a civilization-scale trend, it has secured the best position

In Li Lu's framework, electrification and energy storage represent a civilization-level energy transition, not an industrial cycle. Within this trend, only one company globally has achieved three things at once: 40.2% power battery share, +87.5% energy storage revenue, and 29.97% overseas gross margin. **And this leadership was earned through engineering capability and cost, not subsidies.** That determines it still has room to survive in an environment of rising trade barriers — its three bases in Hungary, Spain and Indonesia are moving capacity to customers' doorstep.

But he would press on the endgame: will batteries become a business like PV modules — technology diffusion, overcapacity, margins to zero? For now, no, because batteries involve vehicle safety and integration, and customers will not push prices down indefinitely; power battery gross margin is still 20.63%, energy storage 23.96%, both far above modules. **The trend is moving in that direction, it just has not arrived yet.**

Li Lu's judgment would be: worth owning, but buy during "industry consolidation", not when "share is just starting to be eroded". The compromise is to scale in — **buy part of the "correct endgame" at today's price, and let subsequent data determine the rest of the position.**

## Risk-reward: 29% upside, 35% downside, but the distribution is asymmetric

Neutral RMB 378, bear case RMB 190, bull case RMB 562; weighted at 40/40/20, the expected value is about RMB 370, 27% above the current price. **That expected value is unremarkable, but the shape of the distribution is favorable**: the bear case requires "share falls to 33% + full absorption of the consumption tax" to happen at the same time, a low probability; the bull case requires only "overseas progress + energy storage doubling," both of which were already happening in the first half of 2026.

What is really holding back the valuation is certainty, not headroom. The market is willing to pay 30x for certainty and 12x for uncertainty—CATL is currently classified in the latter. To change that classification, all that is needed is one quarterly report showing "growth no longer declining." **That is why the core of this report is not a valuation model but a single observation point: the Q3 report in October.**

## Divergence: Duan Yongping and Li Lu part ways on the same point

The disagreement among the four is not about valuation but about "when counts as a buying point." Duan Yongping wants "bad news fully out"; Li Lu wants "industry shakeout." These two conditions differ widely in timing—bad news fully out may take a quarter, industry shakeout may take three years.

This disagreement can be resolved through position structure without picking sides: **use Duan Yongping's timing to buy the first tranche (now), and use Li Lu's standard to decide the last tranche (at shakeout).** If three years later the industry truly clears out and CATL is still No. 1, the valuation then might be 25x, but the profit base will also be double today's—returns come from profit, not from multiples.

The only risk is the middle stretch: de-CATLization continues, the consumption tax rises to 4%, and solid-state batteries lag—if all three happen at once, the stock could grind around RMB 200 for two years. **The point of tranching is to keep that grind from forcing you to sell at the bottom.**
