## Tier-one revenue mix: power is the base, energy storage the accelerator, materials the profit patch

Power batteries generated RMB 192.125 billion, 69.4% of revenue and still the core base, but growth of 46.0% now trails total revenue growth of 54.8%. Energy storage generated RMB 53.261 billion, 19.2% of revenue, up 87.5% — the only segment to outpace the total. Battery materials and recycling generated RMB 31.531 billion, 11.4% of revenue, at a gross margin of 27.04%, above both core businesses; this "patch" contributed roughly RMB 8.5 billion of gross profit, 12.9% of consolidated gross profit.

The geographic split offers a counterintuitive read: overseas RMB 87.129 billion, +42.4% YoY; domestic RMB 189.788 billion, +60.9% YoY (derived). Domestic grew faster but earns less — domestic gross margin of about 21.2% versus 29.97% overseas. Growth in the first half of 2026 therefore came mainly from volume driven by the domestic price war, not from overseas premium.

On volume: about 440GWh in the first half, +60% YoY, above revenue growth of 54.8%, implying falling unit prices (revenue per GWh down from about RMB 660 million last year to RMB 629 million, on our rough calculation). Rising volume, falling price — that is the underlying fact of this earnings report.

## Second-layer gross profit: power batteries front costs for customers

Power battery gross margin of 20.63%, down 1.78 points YoY, is the single most important line in this report. On revenue of RMB 192.125 billion, that 1.78 points equals RMB 3.42 billion of forgone gross profit, close to 8% of H1 net profit. Energy storage gross margin of 23.96%, down 1.56 points, shows the price war has spread from vehicles to the grid side.

Why can't the gross margin hold? Two mechanisms. First, prices of raw materials such as lithium carbonate have rebounded, pressuring costs; second, once automakers bring in second and third suppliers, tenders become price comparisons, and CATL's quotes must follow the second tier. Its cost advantage guarantees it earns more than the second tier, but cannot guarantee it earns more than last year.

The only structural improvement comes from overseas: overseas gross margin is 29.97%, 8.8 points above domestic. A rough sensitivity check: every 10-point rise in the overseas share lifts blended gross margin by about 0.9 points. This is the most certain profit source over the next three years, and the entire rationale for the three bases in Hungary (initial 40GWh already booked), Indonesia and Spain.

## The third layer, costs and profit: R&D intensity is declining

R&D spending was RMB 11.376 billion, up only 12.7% YoY against revenue growth of 54.8%, pushing the R&D expense ratio down from 5.64% to 4.11%. There are two readings: the optimistic one is scale dilution and improving R&D efficiency; the pessimistic one is belt-tightening at the very moment when solid-state batteries most warrant heavy spending.

Given that its solid-state progress is not leading, I lean half toward the latter and half toward the former. Absolute R&D spending is still growing, but not fast enough to keep pace with revenue — not a good signal on the eve of a technology transition. The key metric to watch is whether the R&D expense ratio can return above 4.5% in the second half of 2026.

Net margin was 15.6%, down 1.4pp YoY. The quarterly picture is clearer: 16.1% in Q1 and 15.3% in Q2. The decline in net margin was smaller than that in gross margin (1.07pct), indicating that expenses and other income (government subsidies, investment gains) provided some support — this component is not operating in nature and should not be treated as the norm.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — earnings are real, but cash is tight

The interim report summary does not disclose a full cash flow statement. Extrapolating from the 2023–2025 operating cash flow/net profit range of 1.8–2.1x, H1 operating cash flow should be RMB 78–90 billion (rough estimate). Cash collection in the battery business is not bad — the customers are automakers; payment terms are long but bad debt is low.

Investment is the tight side: 764GWh under construction, which at an intensity of RMB 300–400 million/GWh implies roughly RMB 230–310 billion of capex. That is why five-year cumulative free cash flow only barely clears the threshold, and why it still needs to raise funds in Hong Kong in 2026. It is not short of money; it has staked everything on capacity.

On balance-sheet structure, the debt-to-assets ratio has long stood at 60%–65%, but the bulk is accounts payable and contract liabilities (operating float from suppliers and customers); interest-bearing debt is under 10% of the total, with interest coverage above 20x. The implication of this structure: it has exceptionally strong float capacity with upstream suppliers (another manifestation of scale advantage), and it holds advance receipts from downstream customers (automakers must pay deposits to lock in capacity). There is no near-term solvency risk.

## The three most common mistakes when reading this earnings report

Error one: reading energy storage's share as 25%. The correct figures are 19.2% of total revenue and 21.7% of battery revenue. A 5-point gap overstates energy storage's contribution to gross profit, and with it the speed at which the "second curve takes over." Energy storage gross profit was RMB 12.76 billion, 19.3% of consolidated gross profit—that is its true weight.

Error two: treating 525GWh as full-year capacity. It is effective capacity for the first half (498÷94.86%), or roughly 1,050GWh annualized. Misreading it yields a "189% capacity utilization rate," and also counts the 764GWh under construction as 1.45x capacity (actually 0.73x), doubling the estimated capex pressure.

Error three: taking 42% as "strong growth." Break it down: net profit growth of 42.0% Put the three numbers together and the truth of this report emerges: scale is still expanding, unit profitability is contracting.

Conversely, the report also holds an overlooked good number: overseas gross margin of 29.97%. Look only at the 23.93% total gross margin and you would take CATL for an ordinary manufacturer; only by separating the two do you see it runs a different business in Europe.

## One addition: two numbers outside the financial statements

Two figures absent from the statements but material to the judgment: revenue per GWh of RMB 629 million (about RMB 660 million last year) and gross profit per GWh of RMB 151 million (662.66÷440, rough calculation). Unit price fell about 5% while unit gross profit fell further — the price cut was not "passing on cost reductions" but "ceding profit".

The other is per capita: CATL's global workforce is on the order of 100,000, implying first-half revenue per employee of about RMB 2.77 million and profit per employee of about RMB 430,000 (rough calculation). For a manufacturer this is very high efficiency, and it explains how the company held net margin above 15% while cutting prices — it relies on automation and line cycle times, not on squeezing labour.
