## I. First, what I did

CATL closed at RMB 291.99 on September 28, down 0.51% to a 52-week low. The stock is down 36.3% from RMB 458.60 in February, and across A+H shares RMB 770 billion of market value has been wiped out.

I bought my first tranche at this level, one-third of my planned position. Not much, because I know I don't know where the bottom is.

Before buying, I went through the interim report three times. The conclusion is simple: not a single quarter of this company's fundamentals has been bad; what's bad is expectations.

## II. How good was the interim report

1H revenue RMB 276.92 billion, +54.8% YoY; net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 43.28 billion, +42.0% YoY — equivalent to RMB 240 million earned per day.

Power battery revenue RMB 192.13 billion, +46%; energy storage revenue RMB 53.26 billion, +87.5%, growing at twice the pace of power batteries. Overseas revenue RMB 87.13 billion, +42.4%, with gross margin of 29.97%, 8.8pp above domestic.

Global power battery share 40.2%; domestic passenger vehicle installation share 46.7%; NCM domestic market share above 75%. First-half output 498GWh with capacity utilisation of 94.86% — lines are running full, not idle.

Yet the stock price is hitting new lows — what I mean by "fundamentals intact, expectations broken."

## III. What Is the Market Actually Selling?

Two points.

First, de-CATL-ization. Li Auto's new L8 lineup sources entirely from Sunwoda, and Li Auto has injected RMB 2.65 billion into Sunwoda Power for an 11.17% stake. Xiaomi's "Longjia Battery" cells come from CALB and Sunwoda; AITO has brought in CALB and Gotion.

That looks alarming, but read the moves for what they are: not "dropping CATL" but "not letting CATL be the sole supplier." Automakers want pricing power, not to replace their best supplier. Li Auto and Xiaomi together account for only 13.6% of domestic installations — enough to squeeze CATL's gross margin, not to crush its share.

Second, consumption tax. From September 1, 2026, lithium-ion batteries will again be subject to a 2% consumption tax, rising to 4% in September 2027. CATL mainly sells cells externally, so the tax hits it directly; BYD uses its batteries in-house, so the tax falls at the vehicle level, with a far smaller impact. The same policy carries unequal burdens — a genuine structural disadvantage.

Sizing it: first-half battery revenue was RMB 245.386 billion, or RMB 490.8 billion annualized; 2% is RMB 9.8 billion, equal to 10% of expected 2026 net profit, and at 4% it doubles. Pass through half and the actual impact is 5%–10%. This is real bad news, not fatal bad news.

## IV. One number the market is not watching

Overseas gross margin 29.97%.

In plain terms, the same battery cell earns nearly half again as much gross profit in Europe as it does at home: domestic gross margin is roughly 21.2% versus 29.97% overseas.

That is the real gap between CATL and second-tier makers. Second-tier players can fight price wars in China, but they cannot get into Europe's automotive-grade supply chain. The Hungary plant starts production this year, with its initial 40GWh already fully booked; Indonesia begins mass production by year-end; the Stellantis joint venture in Spain is advancing.

Rough sensitivity: every 10 percentage point rise in the overseas share lifts blended gross margin by 0.9 percentage points. Moving from the current 31.5% to 45% would offset a full year of margin erosion from the domestic price war. This is the only certain source of profit over the next three years.

## V. 14x — expensive or cheap?

RMB 291.99, market cap RMB 1.35 trillion. 2026E net profit RMB 95.2 billion, P/E 14.2x; 2027E RMB 116.5 billion, P/E 11.6x.

Many investors point to the 100x P/E of 2021 and argue the stock is "at a 75% discount now, far too cheap." That reference frame is wrong. In 2021 the market treated it as a growth stock; now it treats it as a manufacturer. This is not a discount, it is a different ruler.

On the manufacturing ruler, 15x normalized profit implies RMB 378 for 2027, 29% upside to the current price. Bear case RMB 190, 35% downside. Bull case RMB 562, 92% upside. Odds of 2.6 to 1, skewed positive, but it requires patience.

One number to watch: in the first half it earned RMB 98 per 1kWh sold. Q1 was RMB 104, Q2 was RMB 94. The speed at which this line declines determines whether 14x is cheap or fair. Q2 is already at 94, one step from my RMB 90 warning line.

## Six: How I buy and how I sell

The first third of the position has already been bought.

When the Q3 report lands in late October, watch two numbers: whether single-quarter net profit holds 25% YoY, and whether domestic passenger-vehicle share holds 44%. If both hold, add the second tranche. If share falls below 40%, or power-battery gross margin drops below 19%, cut half the position—these two conditions are the foundation of the thesis; if the foundation cracks, walk away, regardless of the share price at the time.

If it rises above RMB 450 (18x 2027), halve the position. A manufacturer should not command 18x.

One honest note to close: this is not a bottom-fishing story. CATL will not return to 40x; it will be a 15–20x manufacturing leader going forward. What you earn from owning it is profit growth, not multiple re-rating. Be clear on that before deciding whether to buy.

## Seven, where I could be wrong

Three scenarios.

First, solid-state batteries reach mass production early. If an all-solid-state vehicle is announced in 2027, CATL's 500GWh of liquid-electrolyte capacity faces impairment. This is not a profit issue; it is an asset issue.

Second, in-house battery production by automakers becomes the norm. BYD has already proven the model works. If Li Auto, Xiaomi and Geely all follow, CATL's customer pool shrinks permanently.

Third, CATL absorbs the full 4% consumption tax, cutting net profit by 15%–20% and pushing the multiple from 14x to 17x.

None of these three can be verified in advance, so position sizing is the only lever I control. Earn more when right, lose less when wrong—that is the entire point of scaling in.
