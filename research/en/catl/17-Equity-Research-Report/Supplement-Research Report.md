## Chapter 1 Company Overview: A Global Battery Leader Spun Off from ATL

CATL was founded in 2011, spun out of consumer-battery maker ATL to build the power-battery business, listed on ChiNext in June 2018 and completed a secondary listing on HKEX in 2025. Three main segments: power battery systems (2026H1 revenue RMB 192.13 billion, 69.4%), energy-storage battery systems (RMB 53.26 billion, 19.2%), and battery materials and recycling (RMB 31.53 billion, 11.4%).

Scale: first-half output 498GWh, effective capacity 525GWh, utilization 94.86%, capacity under construction 764GWh. Global power-battery installation share 40.2% (SNE, January–May 2026), domestic passenger-car installation share 46.7%, and over 75% of the domestic NCM market. **It is the only battery maker globally with a share above 40%; second-place BYD holds about 16%.**

Shareholder structure: 4.627 billion A+H shares in total, market cap RMB 1.35 trillion. Founder Zeng Yuqun holds roughly 23% indirectly through Ruiting Investment and other vehicles and is the actual controller. **In September 2026 the company announced a RMB 20–40 billion buyback plan, and the interim report set a dividend of RMB 14.11 per 10 shares.**

Capacity footprint: five domestic bases in Ningde, Fujian; Liyang, Jiangsu; Xining, Qinghai; Yibin, Sichuan; and Zhaoqing, Guangdong; overseas in Thuringia, Germany (in production), Debrecen, Hungary (starting production in 2026, with the initial 40GWh already booked), Indonesia (mass production by year-end) and Spain (a joint venture with Stellantis). **Building plants at customers' doorsteps is its core strategy for managing tariff and entity-list risk.**

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis: Demand Still Growing, Structure Deteriorating

Demand side: the global electrification rate is still rising, and energy storage is the incremental growth driver (CATL's energy storage revenue +87.5%, twice the growth rate of power batteries). In the first half of 2026, CATL's sales volume was about 440GWh, +60% year on year, indicating that total industry volume is still expanding rapidly.

Supply side: CATL has 764GWh under construction, and second-tier players are expanding in parallel; global battery capacity will be significantly oversupplied in 2027–2028. **The direct consequence of oversupply is that price wars become the norm** — power battery gross margin of 20.63% (-1.78pct) and energy storage gross margin of 23.96% (-1.56pct) already reflect this.

Structure side: the industry is shifting from "one leader, many strong players" to "two leaders, many strong players." Automakers are actively cultivating second suppliers (Li Auto injected capital into Sunwoda for an 11.17% stake, Xiaomi's Longjia battery uses dual suppliers, AITO brought in CALB and Gotion), and second-tier players are trading equity for orders. **This is the most important industry change in 2026, affecting valuations more than any technological breakthrough.**

The technology roadmap timeline deserves separate mention: lithium iron phosphate remains the mainstream in China (low cost, good safety), ternary lithium holds an advantage in high-end and overseas markets, semi-solid-state batteries begin small-scale installation in 2026, and all-solid-state enters small-batch trial production in 2027, with scale-up not expected until 2028–2030. **This means liquid lithium-ion battery capacity has at least five more years of validity, but valuations have already begun discounting for the "next generation."**

## Chapter 3 Business model: selling battery cells, making money from scale

The revenue model is simple: battery cell unit price × sales volume + energy storage systems + external materials sales. Unit economics for the first half: revenue of RMB 629 million per GWh, net profit of approximately RMB 98 million per GWh (RMB 98/kWh, rough calculation). **The three core variables for profit are: sales volume, unit price, and unit cost.**

Cost structure: materials (cathode, anode, separator, electrolyte) account for about 60%–70%, manufacturing expenses 15%–20%, labor around 5%. CATL's advantage lies in self-supply of materials (cathode, recycling) and manufacturing efficiency (line cycle time, yield rate). **These two factors make its unit cost 10%–15% lower than second-tier players (industry estimate).**

Customer structure: major global automakers + energy storage integrators + power grids. Top five customers account for a high share, but concentration is improving (more overseas customers). **The essence of the business model is a loop of "trading scale for cost, trading cost for share, trading share for scale"**—once share declines, the loop runs in reverse.

On pricing mechanism, there is an easily overlooked detail: CATL signs "metal price linkage" clauses with most automakers—when lithium carbonate rises, cell prices rise accordingly; when lithium falls, prices fall accordingly. **This mechanism means it does not have to absorb the full impact when raw materials surge, but it also means it cannot capture the spread when raw materials decline.** The real variable for gross margin is therefore not lithium price, but processing fees and product mix.

The real source of customer stickiness is not contracts, but "co-development." CATL engineers are stationed at automakers year-round doing envelope design, thermal management, and BMS integration. Once this work becomes embedded in a vehicle platform, switching suppliers means redoing the entire vehicle validation process. **So what it sells is not a standard part, but "half a custom part"—this is the technical reason its 20% gross margin holds.**

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis: The Quality of Growth Is Declining

Income statement: revenue +54.8%, net profit +42.0%, **profit lagging revenue by 13 points**. Broken down, Q1 net profit +48.5%, Q2 +36.5% — growth stepping down; gross margin 23.93% (-1.07pct), net margin 15.6% (-1.4pct). R&D RMB 11.376 billion, +12.7%, R&D expense ratio falling from 5.64% to 4.11%.

Cash flow: operating cash flow/net profit historically 1.8—2.1x, high earnings quality; but capex is heavy (764GWh under construction, corresponding to RMB 230—310 billion on a rough estimate), and five-year cumulative free cash flow is only "thinly positive".

Balance sheet: debt-to-assets 60%—65%, interest-bearing debt ratio below 10%, the bulk being accounts payable and contract liabilities. **This is the scale advantage of "holding up both ends", and also the first place to deteriorate when share slips.**

One accounting-basis difference to flag: summing segment gross profit (power 39.64 + energy storage 12.76 + materials 8.53) gives RMB 60.93 billion, while the consolidated gross margin implies RMB 66.27 billion — a gap of RMB 5.34 billion (8.1%). This is the difference from inter-segment eliminations and unallocated items, not an error. **This report uses the segment basis when comparing structure and the consolidated basis when sizing the total; the two do not overlap.**

## Chapter 5 Management: Founder still in place, succession a blank

Zeng Yuqun (born 1968) remains chairman, personally overseeing technology roadmaps and major clients. He bet on NMC lithium in 2016 and on energy storage in 2021, both two years before consensus formed. **This is the company's most valuable intangible asset and also its most concentrated risk.**

Capital allocation: a RMB 45 billion private placement in 2022 and about HKD 35.6 billion in H shares in 2025, all directed to capacity, with no indiscriminate M&A; a RMB 20–40 billion buyback in September 2026 plus an interim dividend of RMB 6.53 billion (rough calculation). **The money is spent on the right things, but spent too fast.**

Governance: eight years since listing with no financial fraud and no record of major-shareholder fund appropriation. The risk lies in geopolitics—the U.S. Department of Defense 1260H list in early 2025, which the company has challenged in court. **There is no public information on succession arrangements, a discount factor for long-term holders.**

Organizationally, CATL operates as "business units + project teams," with power, energy storage, materials and overseas operations each accounted for independently, but technology-roadmap decisions concentrated at the chairman level. **This structure is highly efficient during expansion (fast decisions, resources that can be mobilized), and concentrates risk during technology transitions (one wrong judgment and the whole company follows).** Zeng Yuqun himself has publicly stressed "a strong gambling instinct" many times; that phrase explains his two early bets, and also explains where the risk lies.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers: Three Legs, Only One Accelerating

The first leg is power-battery share (40.2% global, 46.7% domestic) — this leg is retreating, as second-tier players grab orders and automakers dual-source. The second leg is energy storage (+87.5%) — this leg is growing, but gross margin is also falling (-1.56pct). The third leg is overseas (RMB 87.13 billion, gross margin 29.97%) — **this is the only leg that is both long and profitable.**

Scale estimate: overseas share rising from 31.5% to 41.5% lifts blended gross margin by about 0.9pct, corresponding to +6% net profit (rough calculation). This is the most certain profit source over the next three years. **The three bases — Hungary (initial 40GWh already booked), Indonesia (mass production by year-end) and Spain (JV with Stellantis) — are the entire hope.**

The fourth potential leg is materials and recycling (RMB 31.53 billion, gross margin 27.04%) — highest gross margin but smallest scale, unable to change the big picture near term.

Two more "option-like" businesses remain unpriced: first, battery swapping (Chocolate swap blocks + battery bank, turning batteries from a sale into a lease and changing the revenue model); second, battery recycling (Brunp Recycling, effectively an urban mine that appreciates with lithium prices). **Both are still small, but both can grow without additional capex — this is what sets it apart from a pure manufacturer.**

## Chapter 7 Competitive landscape: five rivals, only one structural

BYD (about 16%): vertically integrated, lowest cost, wins customers directly in the RMB 100,000–200,000 vehicle market, and is the only competitor capable of overturning the premise of whether batteries should be outsourced.**CALB (about 5%): price plus rapid response, vying for the primary mid-range supply slot. Sunwoda: joint ventures in exchange for design wins (Li Auto holds 11.17%). Gotion (about 3%): Huawei Jujing battery chain. LG Energy Solution (about 10%): North American localization.**

The key to competition is not technology but the distribution of supply-chain power. Automakers are shifting from selecting suppliers to cultivating suppliers, and this shift turns CATL's 40% share from a monopoly into leadership.**Leadership can be sustained for a long time; only a monopoly can extract excess profit.**

An often overlooked gap is yield and utilization: CATL's capacity utilization was 94.86% in the first half, versus 60%–80% for second-tier players generally.**On the same production line, a 94% versus 70% utilization rate means a unit fixed-cost gap of more than 25%.**This is why CATL still makes money when outsiders see it as having cut prices to the point of no profit, and why second-tier players win orders but fail to make money.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors: five in total, two already materializing

① Consumption tax: 2% from 2026/09/01, rising to 4% in 2027/09. Annualized on first-half battery revenue, 2% equals roughly RMB 9.8 billion per year, equivalent to 10% of expected 2026 net profit; **BYD uses its batteries in-house, so the tax falls at the vehicle-assembly stage — a structural disadvantage.** ② De-CATLization: Li Auto and Xiaomi account for 13.6% of domestic installations; if the model spreads, it would affect 25%–30% of installations in 2027–2028.

③ Solid-state batteries: small-scale semi-solid-state vehicle fitment in 2026, small-batch trial production of all-solid-state in 2027; CATL does not lead here, and its 500GWh-class liquid-electrolyte capacity faces long-term substitution. ④ Overseas politics: the 1260H list and European localization requirements. ⑤ Overcapacity: gross margin may fall another 2–4pct in 2027–2028. **None of these would push the company into loss within a year, but together they are enough to cap the valuation at 12–15x.**

Three secondary risks also belong here: ① Lithium prices — CATL lacks sufficient owned mines, so a sharp rise in lithium carbonate prices would compress gross margin (partly hedged by metal-linked pricing clauses); ② FX — overseas revenue of RMB 87.1 billion, with EUR and USD swings affecting translation; ③ Customer concentration — the top five customers account for a high share, so a design win change at a single major customer would hit quarterly revenue directly. **None is fatal, but all three would amplify quarterly volatility.**

## Chapter 9 Earnings forecast: can the RMB 95.2 billion target be met in 2026

The consensus forecasts 2026 net profit of RMB 95.2 billion (Huachuang, 2026/07), implying RMB 51.9 billion in the second half, up 20% HoH from the first half.**First-half HoH growth was only +8.7% (Q2 225.46 vs Q1 207.38)**, so this target requires Q3 and Q4 single-quarter net profit to exceed RMB 25 billion.

This report's neutral case: RMB 93 billion for 2026 (2.3% below consensus) and RMB 110 billion for 2027 (5.6% below consensus), on consumption tax and price war. **The optimistic case returns to consensus; the pessimistic case puts 2027 at RMB 80 billion.**

Sensitivity: every RMB 5/kWh decline in unit net profit cuts full-year net profit by about RMB 2.2 billion (rough calculation); every 1pct decline in power battery gross margin cuts full-year net profit by about RMB 3.8 billion (rough calculation on annualized power revenue of RMB 380 billion).

Quarterly breakdown: Q1 net profit RMB 20.738 billion, Q2 RMB 22.546 billion. Neutral case assumes Q3 RMB 24 billion (about 20% YoY growth) and Q4 RMB 25.7 billion, for RMB 93 billion for the full year.**The key to this assumption is that HoH growth stops accelerating—if Q3 turns down HoH, even RMB 90 billion for the full year becomes difficult.**For 2027, revenue +25% and net margin 15.2% (a further 0.4pct decline from 2026) yields RMB 110 billion.

Running the reverse calculation: if the 4% consumption tax is fully absorbed and power battery gross margin falls another 1.5pct, 2027 net profit drops from RMB 116.5 billion to around RMB 95 billion, or 14.2x the current price—**in other words, the market's current price already embeds a combination of "consumption tax doubling + another gross margin decline." This is the source of the margin of safety for buying.**

## Chapter 10 Valuation: 14x is reasonably low for a manufacturer

Method: 2027 net profit × manufacturing-sector multiple range (11–20x) ÷ 4.627 billion shares. Base case RMB 116.5 billion × 15 = RMB 378 (+29%); bear case RMB 80 billion × 11 = RMB 190 (-35%); bull case RMB 130 billion × 20 = RMB 562 (+92%).

The reference frame needs stating clearly: CATL's five-year valuation center is 25–35x, but that is the yardstick for a "growth stock"; by 2026 the market has switched to the "manufacturing" yardstick (net margin 15.6%, gross margin 23.93%, capital-intensive). **Using the wrong yardstick yields the wrong conclusion that "14x is cheap" — the correct statement is "14x is low within the manufacturing range."**

Dividends and buybacks: interim dividend RMB 6.53 billion (payout ratio 15.1%) + buybacks RMB 20–40 billion (1.5%–3.0% of market cap), for a total shareholder yield of about 2%.

Cross-check one: historical PE range. CATL's PE (TTM) fluctuated between 15–40x in 2023–2026, with a median of about 24x; the current 14.2x (2026E) already sits below the bottom of that range. **But the historical range includes the "growth-stock premium" period, so applying it directly would overstate fair value.**

Cross-check two: dividend + buyback yield of about 2%, below the 10-year government bond yield, shows this is not a name whose valuation is supported by payouts — owning it works only through profit growth. **Both checks point to the same conclusion: 14x is "reasonably low for a manufacturer," not "deeply undervalued."**

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendation: Buy (in tranches), Three Gates

Rating: Buy (staged). Initial position 2% (of the equity portfolio); add to 4% if Q3 single-quarter net profit grows ≥25% YoY and gross margin ≥20%; add to 6% if domestic share ≥44% and overseas revenue share ≥33%; cap at 8%.

Stop-loss on fundamentals: cut the position by half if power battery gross margin falls below 19% or domestic share falls below 40%; exit entirely if both occur.**Price reference levels: halve above RMB 450 (18x 2027E); add below RMB 230 (9x) if fundamentals remain intact.**

Time horizon: the reconciliation period for this investment is three quarters (Q3 2026, the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027), not three months.**The return here comes from profit growth, not from valuation re-rating.**

Role in the portfolio: manufacturing-cycle exposure, highly correlated with new energy vehicle ETFs and weakly correlated with the AI compute chain.**If the portfolio already holds BYD or a new energy vehicle ETF, CATL exposure must be aggregated and kept to no more than 10% combined.**Its role is to provide cash flow that is not synchronized with AI, not to provide high beta.
