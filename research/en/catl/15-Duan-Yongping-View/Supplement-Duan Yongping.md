# CATL（300750.SZ）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/09/28
- Price：291.99 CNY
- Market cap：RMB 1.35 trillion market cap (4.627 billion A+H shares)

## Notes

This is an inference built on Duan Yongping's public methodology ("understand it, wait, buy in tranches", "don't touch what you don't understand", "three criteria for a business"), not a statement by him. Data are drawn from the 2026 interim and Q1 reports: power battery gross margin 20.63%, energy storage 23.96%, 764GWh under construction, unit net profit of about RMB 98/kWh. Valuation: RMB 116.5 billion ÷ 4.627 billion shares = RMB 25.18/share, ×15 = RMB 378; 291.99÷25.18 = 11.6x.

## Q1: Is this a good business?

Good, but not the best kind. Three tests decide it: can it raise prices, can customers switch away, and must it keep investing.

Raise prices? No. Power battery gross margin was 20.63%, down 1.78 points YoY; energy storage fell 1.56 points. The real gap is here — Moutai's gross margin is 91%, and it raises prices whenever it wants; when CATL raises prices, customers go ask the second-tier suppliers.

Can customers switch? Not in the short term. Automakers need two years of validation to change suppliers, redo safety certification and bear recall risk. But in 2026 Li Auto, Xiaomi and AITO are all adding second suppliers, which shows they are willing to pay that cost to "switch away".

Must it keep investing? Yes. 764GWh under construction, at RMB 300–400 million/GWh that is RMB 230–310 billion. The money it earns turns into next year's capacity, so this is not a business that makes money while sitting still.

It passes one and a half of the three. So my verdict: a good business, but not a Moutai-kind of business. Good businesses have entry points too, but the valuation cannot be high.

## Question 2: Will it still be around in ten years?

Most likely it survives — but not necessarily as number one. The battery industry is not going away: electrification is irreversible and energy storage has barely started. With a 40.2% share, the world's lowest cost base and three overseas plants, the probability of CATL being fully displaced within a decade is very low.

Surviving and staying number one, however, are two different things. Through 2026 automakers actively cultivated second suppliers, and Li Auto even took a stake in Sunwoda. On that path, ten years out the market could split three ways: CATL, BYD and one overseas player.

So the question is not whether CATL dies but whether it can still earn excess profit. The answer goes from "yes" to "a little", and the valuation from 30x to 15x. That is what is playing out now.

## Question three: Can I understand it?

The business is straightforward: it makes battery cells and sells them to automakers and grid operators, with revenue split three ways (power, storage, materials) and unit economics that can be calculated — 440GWh in the first half, net profit of RMB 43.284 billion, or RMB 98/kWh. No black box, no complex financial engineering, no related-party transactions I cannot understand.

The one thing I do not understand is when solid-state batteries will reach mass production, and whose route is right. I will not pretend otherwise — and precisely because I do not understand it, I cannot take a heavy position. Duan Yongping's principle is "don't touch what you don't understand"; I have revised it to "for the parts you don't understand, cap the position size," since I do understand the core business (liquid lithium batteries).

## Question 4: At what price to buy

My method is crude: normalised profit × a sufficiently cheap multiple. 2027 consensus net profit is RMB 116.5 billion on 4.627 billion shares, or RMB 25.18 per share. A 15x manufacturing multiple gives RMB 378; at the current RMB 291.99 the stock trades at 11.6x, a 23% discount.

Is that discount enough? It depends on the comparison. For a manufacturing company steadily earning RMB 100 billion with no growth, 11.6x would be fair, not cheap. This one is still growing (+22% expected in 2027), so 11.6x looks on the low side.

But I will not buy all at once. The reason is simple: bad news is still coming out. The September 1 consumption tax, mid-September reports of the Ningde exit, and a 52-week low on September 28. My habit is to wait until the bad news is fully out; only half of it is out now.

So: buy one-third first, then wait for the Q3 report. If Q3 growth holds at 25%, I will buy more.

## Question five: What do you fear most?

Two risks dominate the bear case. The first is early mass production of solid-state batteries: if all-solid-state vehicles are announced in 2027, depreciation on CATL's 500GWh of liquid-electrolyte capacity becomes a burden. This is not a profit issue but an asset issue—those production lines on the books will need impairment.

The second is in-house battery production by automakers becoming the norm. BYD has already proven the model works (vehicle-battery integration at the lowest cost). If Li Auto, Xiaomi and Geely all follow with in-house production, CATL's customer pool shrinks permanently. This is not a share issue but a market-definition issue.

Consumption tax, de-CATLization and share-price declines do not belong in the realm of "fear"—they are already in the price. What is feared are the risks that do not show up in the financials first but rewrite valuation in one stroke.

## Question 6: If you could buy only one battery stock

CATL is the only option — not because it is the best, but because it is the only pure battery play. BYD's batteries are captive, so buying BYD means buying the entire vehicle business; second-tier makers offer neither an overseas premium nor a margin of safety; LG's gross margin is just 10%–15%.

But "the only one to buy" does not mean "buy the full position now." My approach: take a 2% position to hold a seat, convert research into holdings, then test it against data every quarter — add if right, exit if wrong. Investing is not a bet on a single judgment; it is the continuous revision of judgment against facts.
