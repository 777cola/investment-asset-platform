Core view

Risk is not about listing as many items as possible; it is about tiering them. Two fatal ones (technology transition, change in market definition), two major ones (consumption tax, share loss), three tolerable ones (lithium price, exchange rate, customer concentration), and a pile of noise (new stock-price lows, media narratives).

Only risks at the major level or above require position changes; the rest only need to be recorded.

Fatal: two paths to permanent investment loss

The first is early mass production of solid-state batteries. If CATL's 500GWh-class liquid-electrolyte lines face a technology route switch in 2027, depreciation and retrofitting become real losses. This is not a profit issue, it is an asset issue — the lines on the books will need impairment.

The second is the trend of automakers building their own batteries. BYD has already proven this path works (vehicle-battery integration, lowest cost). If two of the top five customers build in-house, CATL's customer pool shrinks permanently — this is not a share issue, it is a market-definition issue.

Neither is in the 2026 financials, but both are in the 2026 headlines (semi-solid-state batteries in vehicles, automakers developing their own cells). So they are not "black swans" but "gray rhinos" — visible, you just don't know when they arrive.

Critical: two already underway and require quarterly reconciliation

Consumption tax: the 2% rate took effect (2026/09/01) and rises to 4% in September 2027. Annualizing first-half battery revenue, 2% equates to about RMB 9.8 billion, or 10% of expected 2026 net profit; at 4% it is about RMB 19.6 billion, or 20%. Half can be passed through, leaving an actual impact of 5%–10%.

Share: 46.7% of domestic passenger-car installations, with Li Auto and Xiaomi together accounting for 13.6%; all three developments (Li Auto switching to Sunwoda, Xiaomi dual-sourcing, AITO adding a second supplier) land in the second half of 2026. The thesis line is 44%, leaving only 2.7 points of headroom.

What these two have in common is that they are observable and reconcilable. So the response is not forecasting but setting triggers: cut the position if share falls below 44%, stop out if gross margin falls below 19%.

Tolerable vs. noise: no position change needed, but log it

Lithium price: CATL lacks sufficient captive mines, so a sharp rise in lithium carbonate would squeeze gross margin; however, metal price-linked clauses partially hedge this. FX: overseas revenue of RMB 87.1 billion; EUR and USD swings affect translation. Customer concentration: top five customers account for a high share; a single design-win change would hit quarterly revenue.

Noise level: stock at a 52-week low, media writing "fleeing CATL," retail sentiment. None of this changes any fundamental number; it only changes the price you pay. For anyone building a position in tranches, noise is a gift.

The point of tiering is this: do not let noise consume your decision bandwidth. Of the bad news an investor reads every day, 90% is noise; the times you actually need to act may come only once or twice a year.

Under what circumstances must you sell: three hard-coded rules

First, power-battery gross margin falls below 19% (now 20.63%). This signals that "trading price for volume" has spiraled out of control and the cost advantage no longer converts into profit.

Second, domestic passenger-vehicle share falls below 40% (now 46.7%). This signals that the "one dominant player" position is eroding and the valuation center will shift down permanently.

Third, a second-tier player announces mass production and vehicle installation of all-solid-state batteries and wins a designated supply contract from a mainstream automaker. This signals the start of a generational technology shift and liquid-electrolyte capacity will need to be revalued.

Outside these three, do not sell. Including if the share price falls to RMB 200 — a falling price is not a reason to sell; a broken thesis is.

The overlooked fourth major risk: refinancing

The 764GWh under construction implies RMB 230–310 billion of capex (rough estimate), while cumulative five-year free cash flow is only a "thin pass." The gap can only be filled by refinancing.

The company already issued H shares once in 2025 (about HKD 35.6 billion). Another round, priced at a 5%–8% discount to the current market cap, would dilute about 3%–5%. A single round is not fatal, but if it issues two years in a row, cumulative dilution exceeds 10%, and hard metric No. 7 shifts from "collateral damage" to "real damage."

Monitoring indicators: the annual report's capex plan, Hong Kong exchange filings, and whether major shareholders subscribe. If management is willing to put in its own money, it believes the price is cheap; if only external institutions subscribe, it is plugging the gap.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: two fatal (technology transition, market definition), two major (consumption tax, share), the rest tolerable or noise. Only major and above require position changes, and all are already written as observable gates. Investment implication: the biggest risk in this investment is not volatility but the technology roadmap five years out — so buy only three years, not ten.
