Zeng Yuqun's Decision-Making Style, Capital Allocation and That Blank Space

Core view

Zeng Yuqun once said he has "a strong gambling instinct." But look at his two bets (ternary lithium in 2016, energy storage in 2021): both wagered on the intersection of "technology route + customer demand," and both came two years before industry consensus formed. This is no gambler — it is an engineer placing his bets early.

His real weakness is not judgment; it is having "no backup."

Decision model: first look at what the customer wants, then at whether the technology can deliver it

The 2016 bet on NCM batteries was not driven by the logic that "NCM is more advanced," but by "subsidies tiered by range, automakers needing range, and NCM raising energy density." He bet on the intersection of policy and customers, not on chemistry.

The 2021 bet on energy storage likewise was not driven by "storage being bigger," but by "when power batteries were in short supply, storage customers were willing to pay cash and did not press for lower prices." As a result, energy storage revenue reached RMB 53.261 billion in the first half of 2026, up 87.5%, making it the only segment that is accelerating.

The strength of this model is its high hit rate (right both times); the weakness is that it depends on one person's judgment, and the judgment cycle runs as long as five years—one mistake costs five years.

On incentives, the company has repeatedly rolled out restricted stock and employee stock ownership plans covering R&D and manufacturing key staff, with assessment metrics typically including revenue and net profit growth. This mechanism ensures "execution" but does not solve "succession"—incentives retain people, not strategic judgment. This is the point that most needs to be distinguished when evaluating management: organizational capability is an A, strategic continuity only a B.

Capital allocation: all the money went into capacity, and that was the right call

The RMB 45 billion private placement in 2022 and the roughly HKD 35.6 billion H-share offering in 2025 went entirely into capacity (Fuding, Zhaoqing, Hungary) — no buying of hot themes, ecosystems or traffic. For a manufacturing company, this is the least error-prone way to spend money.

The September 2026 move is more telling: with the stock at a 52-week low, it announced a RMB 20–40 billion buyback plus an interim dividend of RMB 6.53 billion. The payout ratio is only 15.1% — hardly generous, but the direction is right: it is telling the market that 14x is not a price it recognizes.

The demerit is spending too fast: 764GWh under construction implies RMB 230–310 billion, and free cash flow stays tight for the next two years. If it refinances in 2027, dilution turns from collateral damage into real damage.

Organization and succession: highest efficiency, most concentrated risk

The organization runs as "business units + project teams," with power, energy storage, materials and overseas operations accounted for independently, and technology-route decisions centralized with the chairman. This structure is highly efficient during expansion and concentrates risk during technology transitions.

Succession is a blank: Huang Shilin stepped back from day-to-day management in 2022, Wu Kai is chief scientist rather than an operating successor, and Zhou Jia runs daily operations but not technology routes. Zeng Yuqun is 58 and still personally manages technology routes — not a good signal.

Investment implication: no "founder premium." This is one reason the report caps valuation at 11–20x rather than above 25x. A ten-year holder must treat "who comes after Zeng Yuqun" as a real discount item.

One-line verdict: execution A, sustainability B

If management is split into "can they get things done" and "can they keep getting things done," the answers are A and B. Getting things done depends on judgment plus organizational efficiency; keeping it going depends on succession plus technology generations. The former is proven; the latter has no answer.

That is why its valuation can only be "the ceiling for a manufacturing leader," not "the floor for a great company." A great company can rely on people; a manufacturing leader must rely on institutions.

Where the R&D money goes

R&D in the first half was RMB 11.376 billion, up 12.7% year on year, with the expense ratio falling from 5.64% to 4.11%. The absolute amount is growing while the intensity is declining—a contradictory combination on the eve of a technology transition.

The money goes mainly to three areas: material systems (high-compaction lithium iron phosphate, high-nickel NCM), structural innovation (Qilin, Shenxing, 587Ah large cells), and manufacturing processes (line takt time and yield). All three are "improvements", not a "generational shift"—solid-state batteries are a separate track and require a different investment structure.

A pointer for investors: the R&D expense ratio is the simplest indicator of whether the company is preparing for the next generation. If it is still around 4% in 2027, that means it has chosen to "push liquid-state to the limit" rather than "race to be first with solid-state". That is a strategic choice, not a capability problem.

Conclusion

Management 8/10: full marks on execution, a clean governance record, restrained capital allocation — all deductions come down to "one person". Two early bets prove judgment; buybacks and dividends point the right way; cash goes only into capacity. The gaps are succession and generational technology transition. Conclusion: can be trusted for three years, not ten.
