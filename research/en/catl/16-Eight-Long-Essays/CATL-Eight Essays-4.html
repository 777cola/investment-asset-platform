Revenue is growing, cash flow is tightening and the balance sheet is soaking up capital

Core view

All three statements tell the same story: CATL is trading scale for growth, float for cash, and the future for the present. The income statement looks the strongest (+54.8%/+42.0%), the cash flow statement the tightest (free cash flow razor-thin), and the balance sheet best explains its position (float taken at both ends).

Income statement: growth is real, quality is deteriorating

Revenue +54.8%, net profit +42.0%. Volume rose 60% while unit price fell 4.7% (from roughly RMB 660 million per GWh to RMB 629 million, on our rough calculation). Volume up, price down — that is the underlying fact of this earnings report.

Gross margin 23.93% (-1.07pct), net margin 15.6% (-1.4pct). Power batteries 20.63% (-1.78pct), energy storage 23.96% (-1.56pct). Both core businesses declined simultaneously, pointing to a price war rather than raw materials.

R&D RMB 11.376 billion, +12.7%, with the expense ratio down from 5.64% to 4.11%. At the very point when solid-state batteries most need investment, R&D intensity is instead declining — this is the part of the report I am least comfortable with.

One more cash flow detail: interim dividend RMB 6.53 billion, 15.1% of net profit; buyback RMB 20–40 billion, 1.5%–3.0% of market cap; combined shareholder return of about 2% — below the 10-year government bond yield, indicating this is not a stock whose valuation is supported by dividends. Buying it can only rely on profit growth; this must be accepted in advance, otherwise you will lose patience during two years of sideways trading.

Cash flow statement: earns it, but can't keep it

Operating cash flow/net profit has historically ranged 1.8–2.1x, confirming earnings are real cash. Capex, however, is heavy, and cumulative free cash flow over five years is only "thinly positive." The 2021–2022 expansion period was negative, and only recovered in 2023.

764GWh under construction corresponds to RMB 230–310 billion (rough estimate), and this money must come from subsequent operating cash flow plus possible refinancing. So the 2025 Hong Kong IPO is not "raising money for the sake of it" — it is "filling the gap."

From an owner earnings perspective: after deducting maintenance capex (about 40%, per industry experience), true owner earnings are about 70%–75% of net profit. A 14x P/E translates to 19–20x owner earnings.

Balance sheet: funding from both ends is both a strength and a vulnerability

Debt-to-asset ratio 60%–65%, interest-bearing debt below 10%. The bulk is accounts payable (owed to suppliers) and contract liabilities (prepayments from automakers). This is "doing business with other people's money" — the cash-form expression of scale advantage.

But the mechanism runs in reverse too: if share slips, suppliers demand shorter payment terms and automakers cut prepayments, and its ability to hold others' funds declines in tandem. Cash flow deteriorates before the income statement does — the hidden risk to watch most closely in holding CATL.

The three statements in one sentence: the income statement tells you how much it earns now, the cash flow statement tells you how much cash it needs, and the balance sheet tells you how hard its position is. They do not contradict each other; they describe the same thing.

Three most commonly misread figures

First: 525GWh. That is effective capacity for 1H, derived as 498÷94.86%, not annual capacity; annualised it is roughly 1,050GWh. Misread it and your error in sizing the 764GWh under construction against the base doubles.

Second: energy storage share. RMB 53.261 billion is 19.2% of total revenue and 21.7% of battery revenue, not 25%. A 5-point gap will lead you to overestimate how fast the "second curve takes over."

Third: 65.3%. That is the ratio of gross profit converted into net profit, which looks high; but after deducting maintenance capex, owner earnings are only 70%–75% of net profit, so a 14x P/E translates into 19–20x owner earnings.

Three explanations for the gross margin decline, only one of which the data support

Explanation 1: Rising raw material costs. If valid, gross margins should fall in sync across all manufacturers and recover next quarter as lithium prices decline. But energy-storage gross margin is also falling (-1.56pct), and energy storage is less sensitive to lithium prices than power batteries; a synchronized decline shows raw materials are not the cause.

Explanation 2: Product mix shift. If valid, the share of low-priced products should rise and unit price should fall. Unit price fell from about RMB 660 million/GWh to RMB 629 million/GWh (rough calculation, -4.7%); this holds only halfway.

Explanation 3: Price war. If valid, three things should appear simultaneously: volume up, price down, gross margin down, and unit net profit down—all three readings match: sales volume +60%, unit price -4.7%, unit net profit RMB 104 to 94.

Conclusion: mainly a price war, with mix shift secondary and raw materials not the main cause. This judgment determines the modeling approach: do not assume gross margin mean-reverts; assume it drifts slowly downward, offset by overseas mix.

Conclusion

Financial reality: growth is real, cash is tight, the balance sheet is solid. The +42% net profit came alongside a -1.07pct gross margin, a -4.7% unit price and a 4.11% R&D expense ratio. The balance sheet is healthy (interest-bearing debt ratio <10%), but free cash flow is thin — not a financial risk, but a feature of the business model.To judge whether this report is "getting better" or "getting worse", watch one number: whether the R&D expense ratio can return above 4.5% — that would signal the company is still paying for next-generation technology rather than cashing in profit early.
