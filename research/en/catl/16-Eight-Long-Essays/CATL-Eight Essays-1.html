Part One · Cognitive Reset: First, Shed the "Battery King" Label — CATL

Three Misused Words: Monopoly, Growth, Tech

Core view

The "Battery King" label dates from 2021, when CATL traded at 100x P/E, held over 30% share and the market believed batteries were the next TSMC. Five years on, share has reached 40.2% and the valuation has de-rated to 14x. The market isn't crazy — the label has simply expired.

To understand this company anew, three assumptions must be dismantled first: it is a leader, not a monopoly; a cyclical growth stock, not a growth stock; and an engineering and manufacturing company, not a tech company.

The first word: monopoly — 40% is leadership, not monopoly

A 40.2% global share looks like a monopoly. But monopoly is not defined by high share; it is defined by customers having no alternative. CATL has alternatives: CALB, Sunwoda, Gotion and EVE all make automotive-grade cells. A 5%–10% price gap is enough to move customers.

Three events in 2026 prove the point: Li Auto's new L8 switched entirely to Sunwoda; Li Auto injected RMB 2.65 billion into Sunwoda Power for an 11.17% stake; and Xiaomi's "Dragon Armor Battery" cells come from CALB and Sunwoda. Were CATL a monopolist, none of these three could have happened.

The correct framing: CATL leads on cost and yield by roughly 10%–15%, a lead that can last a long time but does not confer pricing power. A leader earns "more than peers"; a monopolist earns "customers have no choice." The valuation gap between the two is twofold.

Compare 2021 with 2026: 2021 net profit was about RMB 15.9 billion and peak market cap about RMB 1.6 trillion, or 100x; 2026 expected net profit is RMB 95.2 billion and market cap RMB 1.35 trillion, or 14x. In five years profit rose fivefold while market cap fell — this is not earnings being sold off, it is the multiple being sold off. There is only one reason: the market no longer believes this profit can be extrapolated linearly for ten years.

Growth is still there, but the curve has bent

H1 2026 revenue +54.8%, net profit +42.0% — still looks like high growth. Split the two quarters, however: Q1 net profit +48.5%, Q2 +36.5%, a 12-point drop in a single quarter, while revenue kept accelerating (Q2 +56.9%). Revenue accelerating as profit decelerates means the company is trading price for volume.

This is not "a growth stock losing speed" but "a growth stock becoming a cyclical growth stock." The difference lies in the valuation method: growth stocks are valued on PEG and addressable market; cyclical growth stocks on normalized profit and where capacity sits in the cycle. Use the wrong method and you conclude "14x is cheap."

Use the right one: 2027 net profit of RMB 116.5 billion, at 15x, implies RMB 378. The current price of RMB 291.99 is a 23% discount, not a 70% discount. That difference determines whether you buy a third of a position or a full one.

Technology is the third pillar: the moat here is engineering execution, not patents

R&D spending came to RMB 11.376 billion, up 12.7% YoY, with the R&D expense ratio falling from 5.64% to 4.11%. A "technology company" does not cut R&D intensity on the eve of a technology transition.

Its real moat lies in its production lines: first-half output of 498GWh and a capacity utilisation rate of 94.86%, versus 60%–80% for second-tier players. On the same line, the gap in unit fixed cost between a 94% and a 70% utilisation rate is over 25%. This is engineering capability, not a patent moat; it can be replicated, but it takes five years and RMB 50 billion.

The cognitive reset, in short: value it as "China's best manufacturing company," not as "China's TSMC." The former deserves 15x, the latter 30x. The market has already made this switch; investors need to keep up rather than wait for a "valuation re-rating."

After the reset, only two real questions remain

Strip away the labels and only two questions matter: can unit profit hold at RMB 90/kWh, and can the overseas share rise above 35%. The first determines profit; the second determines gross margin.

The rest—whether share is 40% or 46%, whether it ranks first or second globally, whether it is the "battery king"—is narrative. Narrative determines valuation; numbers determine value. As an investor, buy the company with the best numbers when the narrative is at its worst, not the story with mediocre numbers when the narrative is at its best.

Conclusion

Post-reset, CATL is a manufacturing leader: it leads without monopolising, it grows but has decelerated, and its edge rests on engineering rather than patents. That identity supports a fair valuation of 12–18x, implying RMB 300–450 in 2027. At RMB 291.99 the stock sits below the lower bound, so buy; but because it is not a monopoly, buy only one-third.
