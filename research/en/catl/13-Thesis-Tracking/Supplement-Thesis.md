## Assumption 1: share — headroom of only 2.7 points

Domestic passenger-car installation share is 46.7%, and the thesis requires holding 44%. **The buffer is 2.7 percentage points, equivalent to one quarter of volatility.** Li Auto's new L8 switching entirely to Sunwoda, Xiaomi's Longjia battery dual-sourcing, and AITO bringing in CALB and Gotion will show up in installation data between the second half of 2026 and the first half of 2027.

Do the math: Li Auto and Xiaomi together account for 13.6% of domestic installations. If they cut CATL's share from 100% to 60%, the impact is about 5.4 points; if second-tier suppliers pick up another 10% at other automakers, the cumulative impact is 7–8 points, and CATL's domestic share falls to around 39%. **That breaks the 44% line.**

So the question is not whether this assumption breaks, but how fast. **Q4 2026 installation data is the first read.**

## Scenario 2: Unit profit — already RMB 94 in Q2, just a step away from RMB 90

H1 unit net profit was about RMB 98/kWh, Q1 104 and Q2 94. **At this pace, Q3 could fall below RMB 90.** This is the tightest of the five assumptions, because it directly determines whether 14x PE is cheap or fair.

Sensitivity: every RMB 5 drop in unit net profit cuts full-year net profit by about RMB 2.2 billion (440GWh x 2 halves, rough calculation), equal to -2.3% of 2026E net profit. **A RMB 20 drop cuts net profit 9%, turning 14x into 15.4x — still acceptable; a RMB 40 drop cuts net profit 18%, and the valuation is no longer cheap.**

## Assumption 3: Overseas — 3.5 points short, but the direction is right

Overseas accounts for 31.5% versus the 35% required in the paper. Hungary starts production in 2026 with the initial 40GWh already booked, Indonesia begins mass production by year-end, and the Spain joint venture is progressing. **If all three bases ramp on schedule, overseas share reaching 40% in 2027 is possible.**

The risk lies in ramp speed: overseas plants typically run lower yields and labor efficiency than domestic ones, so initial gross margin may fall below 29.97%. **The thing to watch is not "start of production" but "gross margin in the second quarter after start of production."**

## Assumption 4 and 5: consumption tax and solid-state — one is priced in, one is not

The 2% consumption tax took effect on September 1, with an impact of 5%–10%, and the market has already priced it in. The real variable is the 4% in September 2027—**that would push the impact from 10% to 20%, and it has nothing to do with the cost gap versus second-tier players (second-tier players pay it too).** That means industry-wide price increases, which may actually benefit the leader (smaller players cannot hold on).

Solid-state batteries are the only variable that has not been priced in. Semi-solid-state batteries will be installed in vehicles on a small scale in 2026, and all-solid-state batteries will enter small-batch trial production in 2027; CATL is not ahead.**If a second-tier player announces mass-produced all-solid-state batteries installed in vehicles in 2027, CATL's liquid-battery capacity will be revalued.** This cannot be verified in advance; we can only track installation data and customer design wins.

## Watch calendar (2026/10–2027/04)

**Around October 20: Q3 results.** Watch three numbers — single-quarter net profit year on year (threshold 25%), power battery gross margin (threshold 19%), unit net profit (threshold RMB 90). **January 2027:** 2026 annual report and full-year installed share, to verify 44%. **April 2027:** Q1 report and Hungary plant ramp-up data, to verify the 35% overseas share.

Three dates, six numbers — a complete reconciliation chain. **If any one breaks, cut the position. No debating the narrative.**

## Add one point: what does "the thesis hasn't broken but the stock is falling" mean

The most common confusion in investing is "I got the thesis right, so why am I still losing money." The CATL case is this: the thesis (share, unit profit, overseas) still holds, but the valuation center is drifting down — **the market has cut its fair multiple from 25x to 15x, and that adjustment has nothing to do with the thesis; it is about the disappearance of the certainty premium.**

The response is not to revise the thesis but to revise the purchase price. The thesis decides whether to buy; valuation decides at what price to buy. **Buying a manufacturing leader with an intact thesis at 14x, even if the multiple never expands again, still earns 20% a year from profit growth.**
