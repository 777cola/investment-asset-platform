Core view

Every number is falling except one: overseas revenue of RMB 87.13 billion and a gross margin of 29.97%. The same battery cells earn nearly half again as much gross profit in Europe as they do at home. This is CATL's most certain profit source over the next three years, and the part the market ignores at 14x.

Why overseas operations earn 8.8 points more

Three reasons. First, European automakers do not procure on lowest price; they buy on supply security, carbon-footprint compliance and localisation, and will pay a premium. Second, overseas product mix skews toward high-nickel NCM and large cells, commanding higher unit prices and gross margins. Third, local European competition is weak — LG Energy Solution's capacity ramp-up has stumbled, and no second European manufacturer can deliver at scale.

That premium will not last forever, but it will last until local European capacity is built. CATL's response is to build plants in Europe (Hungary starts production in 2026, with the initial 40GWh already booked; Spain is a joint venture with Stellantis), using localisation to lock in the premium — hedging tariff and list risks while protecting gross margin.

Sensitivity analysis: every 10-point rise in overseas share lifts gross margin by 0.9 points

Rough calculation: overseas gross margin 29.97%, domestic 21.2% — an 8.8-point gap. Overseas share rising from 31.5% to 41.5% lifts blended gross margin by about 0.88 points. At 50%, the lift is 1.6 points — enough to offset a full year of domestic price-war erosion.

This is why the Hungary, Indonesia and Spain bases are the core valuation variable, not a nice-to-have. Capacity ramp progress deserves closer attention than domestic share. A 2-point loss of domestic share cuts revenue 5%; a 10-point gain in overseas share lifts gross margin 0.9 points and net profit 6% (rough calculation). The two are of comparable magnitude.

Endgame: one of the few Chinese manufacturers that can command a premium

Li Lu is not concerned with next quarter, but with whether this company will still sit high in the value chain a decade from now. The answer: if it can extend that 8.8-point premium from "a handful of European customers" to "localized production capacity worldwide," it is no longer a PV module maker but a components giant like Bosch—modest margins, but stable, with pricing power and hard to replace.

Conclusion

The most valuable number in this interim report is not the RMB 43.284 billion net profit, but 29.97%. Net profit is the past; the gross margin structure is the future. Every step up in the overseas share from 31.5% counteracts domestic price cuts.
