View synthesis: where the four converge and diverge — CATL

Put the four close readings together and only three judgments remain

Core view

Four close readings point to the same structural problem: volumes are rising, prices are falling, and overseas markets are the lifeline. The disagreement is over "when prices stop falling" — Duan Yongping looks at unit profitability, Buffett at owner earnings, Munger at competitive structure, and Li Lu at overseas structure. The intersection is three actionable judgments.

Intersection one: gross margin will not recover—do not build it into the model

All four reach a rare consensus: power batteries at 20.63% and energy storage at 23.96%, both declining, and the decline is structural (second suppliers, price comparison, overcapacity), not cyclical. The model should assume gross margin continues to drift lower rather than mean-revert.

The only offset is the overseas mix: overseas at 29.97%, 31.5% of the total, with every 10-point gain adding 0.9 points. So the right model is "gross margin = domestic decline + overseas uplift", a race between two lines.

Intersection 2: Growth now comes from "volume" rather than "price", and the ceiling on volume lies in market share

Revenue +54.8%, volume +60%, ASP RMB 629 million/GWh (about RMB 660 million last year) — all growth came from volume. The ceiling on volume is share multiplied by total industry size. Industry size is still growing (global electrification + energy storage), share is falling (46.7% domestic), and the two offset each other.

So the answer to "can revenue still grow +50%" is not in CATL's hands — it is in the hands of industry growth and second-tier capacity.

Intersection 3: 14x is "reasonably low for manufacturing," not a "mispricing"

None of the four see this as an unjustified selloff. An unjustified selloff means the market has ignored obvious good news, but here the good news (29.97% overseas) is known to the market; it simply will not pay a premium for it, because overseas accounts for only 31.5% and carries political risk. 14x means "reasonably priced with slightly positive odds," not "free money."

There is only one point of disagreement: Duan Yongping and Li Lu apply different standards for "when to buy" (bad news fully out vs. industry consolidation complete). This can be resolved by scaling in; there is no need to pick a side.

Finally: the one number from this interim report worth remembering

Not the RMB 43.284 billion net profit, nor the +54.8% revenue growth, but 29.97% — the overseas gross margin. It shows that while this company is being pounded by price wars at home, it can still command a premium in another market.

Four close reads came full circle and all landed on this number: Duan Yongping sees "there is still room to raise prices," Buffett sees "one segment of the moat is still intact," Munger sees "competition has not yet spread globally," and Li Lu sees "in the endgame it may not be a module maker." One number supports four judgments — that is what a good financial report looks like.

Conclusion

Synthesis: Buy (in tranches). The model assumes no gross margin recovery; growth comes from volume, profit from overseas. All three intersection points can be reconciled in the quarterly report: whether gross margin holds at 19%, whether unit price stops falling, and whether the overseas share rises. These three are far more useful than a grand narrative like "electrification rate."
