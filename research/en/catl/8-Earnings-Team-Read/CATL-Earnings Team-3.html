Core view

The market worries about Power's share; I worry more about energy storage's gross margin. Energy storage revenue rose 87.5% with a gross margin of 23.96%, down 1.56 points year on year — a segment growing 87% still cutting prices to win orders shows competition has spread to the "second curve."

Contrarian thinking: if energy storage were the lifeline, it should not be cutting prices

The core bull case for CATL is "defend share in power batteries, storage takes the baton." But storage revenue rose 87.5% while gross margin fell 1.56 points, meaning that 87.5% was bought with price cuts. That is not a baton pass; it is another price war.

Munger would say: "Show me a fast-growing industry with falling gross margins, and I will tell you what it looks like in ten years." Solar, LED and displays have all been down this road. Storage has lower barriers than power batteries (no vehicle safety certification required), and its customers are system integrators and grid operators, who compare prices more directly, so the price war arrives faster.

Competitive structure: all three legs are being stepped on

The power battery business is being eroded by automakers' second-source suppliers (Li Auto, Xiaomi, AITO); energy storage is being benchmarked on price by integrators; materials are being diverted by upstream lithium salt producers and recyclers. All three legs under pressure at once — a first in CATL's history.

But it has one leg others lack: an overseas gross margin of 29.97%. That leg is valuable because European customers will pay a premium for "certainty" — on-time delivery, stable yields, local capacity (the initial 40GWh in Hungary is already booked). This is the only battlefield where CATL faces no price war.

Conclusion: good company, bad industry

A Munger-style judgment: CATL is an excellent company on an increasingly poor track. The excellence shows in cost, scale and overseas footprint; the poor track shows in all three legs cutting prices. The correct valuation for this combination is the "manufacturing ceiling", not the "growth-stock floor".

Conclusion

The gross-margin decline in energy storage is the most overlooked signal in this interim report: even a segment growing 87% has to cut prices, which shows the price war is not confined to EV batteries but spans the entire battery industry. Investment implication: do not count on a gross-margin recovery; count only on share gains and an improved overseas mix.
