# CATL（300750.SZ）— Quality Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/09/28
- Price：291.99 CNY
- Market cap：Approximately RMB 1.35 trillion (A+H combined 4.627 billion shares)

## Notes

The metrics follow the seven deterioration filters used across this series: (1) 10-year average ROE > 8%; (2) cumulative free cash flow over 5 years > 0; (3) EBIT/interest > 2x; (4) long-term gross margin > 15%; (5) operating cash flow/net profit > 0.7; (6) long-term net margin > 5%; (7) 5-year share count expansion < 20%. CATL data: 2026H1 gross margin 23.93%, net margin 15.6% (interim report); share count 4.627 billion shares (A+H, working-paper basis). ROE of 22%–25% and OCF/net profit of 1.9x are estimates for the 2016–2025 range and were not verified year by year; the bonus share conversions and two equity financings within the share count changes are public information, with ratios roughly calculated.

## Three markers of comfort: ROE, gross margin, net margin

These three are the dividing lines in manufacturing. CATL has cleared all of them and still has headroom. A ten-year average ROE of 22%–25% against an 8% threshold means it has long earned shareholders a return three times the opportunity cost; a long-term gross margin of 23.93% against a 15% threshold shows it is not an assembler surviving on thin margins and high volume; a net margin of 15.6% against a 5% threshold is three times the bar.

Note that the trend is weakening: net margin fell from 17.0% a year earlier to 15.6%, and gross margin from about 25.0% to 23.93%. Clearing the bar is not the problem; the direction is ugly. At the current pace of decline of 1–2 points a year, three years out gross margin stays above 20% and net margin drops to around 13% — still above the thresholds, but the 14x PE then corresponds to lower profit.

## Two that just cleared the line: free cash flow and interest coverage

Item 2 is the only one of the seven that makes CATL sweat. 2021–2022 were its two most aggressive years of capacity expansion, with capex in the hundreds of billions annually and negative free cash flow; operating cash flow picked up after 2023 (net profit above RMB 40 billion, net cash ratio near 2x), and only then did the cumulative figure turn positive. The conclusion is "passes, but thinly" — it has not left itself a large cash pile it can distribute at any time.

The reason lies in capacity: 764GWh under construction, close to 70% of existing annualized capacity (525GWh in the first half, or roughly 1,050GWh annualized). At the industry investment intensity of RMB 300–400 million/GWh, this pipeline implies RMB 230–310 billion of capex. Item 3's interest coverage is high precisely because most of the money is its own and it borrows little — it expands capacity with equity and operating cash flow, not leverage. That is a strength, and also why Item 2 only passes thinly.

## The one that did not pass: a bonus share conversion is not dilution

Item 7 does not pass on a literal reading: share count rose from about 2.4 billion to 4.627 billion over five years. But broken down, a capital reserve capitalization in 2023 nearly doubled the share count, with shareholders' holdings rising proportionally—equity was not diluted by a single cent. Only two rounds involved genuine financing dilution: the RMB 45 billion private placement in 2022 (about 110 million shares) and the 2025 H-share IPO (about 140 million shares, proceeds for the Hungary plant), together just over 5% of the current share count.

So this item is "a fail in name, a pass in substance." Its value in the report is as a reminder: reading the numbers alone would screen out a shareholder-friendly company; negative-screening metrics must look at composition, not just the change. The question ahead is whether it raises capital again—764GWh of capacity under construction is a large number, and another Hong Kong offering would make dilution no longer collateral damage.

## But is it fatal? Not fatal, but keep an eye on refinancing

None of the seven is at the "business model is broken" level, however. Gross margin, net margin and ROE all clear the thresholds with room to spare; cash flow is thin but positive, and interest coverage is under no pressure at all. Two things genuinely need watching: first, where the money for the 764GWh of capacity under construction will come from (another equity raise would turn item 7 from "collateral damage" into "real damage"); second, the pace of the gross margin decline (below 19%, item 4 still passes, but the business's pricing power has changed in nature).
