Cognitive reset: you are not buying an automaker, you are buying a capacity option — Tesla

Reclassifying Tesla is the first step to understanding it
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)

Core view

Treat it as a carmaker and RMB 1.48 trillion is absurd; treat it as an AI infrastructure company and RMB 1.48 trillion is "possibly cheap." The entire debate lies in the gap between those two classifications.

1. Error category one: treating it as a growth stock

From 2020 to 2022 Tesla was a textbook growth stock: deliveries doubled, gross margin reached 25% and market cap climbed from USD 100 billion to USD 1 trillion. Back then, both P/S and PEG worked as valuation frameworks.

The 2026 data no longer supports that classification. Deliveries were 1.636 million units in 2025, down 8.6% YoY, and 838,000 units in 1H26, up 16% YoY — back in double digits, but hardly explosive. Gross margin fell from 25% to 16.8% and net margin was 3.9%. For a company growing 16% at a 3.9% net margin, the growth-stock framework no longer fits.

More critically, that growth has to be bought with ever-rising capex — RMB 8.5 billion in 2025 and over RMB 25 billion in 2026. This is a classic capital-intensive recovery, not asset-light growth.

2. Error type two: mistaking deliveries for profit

Most people look at Tesla and see only deliveries — 480,126 units, +25%, the highest for the period on record. But deliveries cap revenue, not profit.

Do the math: Q2 2026 deliveries of 480,100 vehicles, revenue of 28.236 billion, net profit of 1.114 billion — a net profit of USD 2,320 per vehicle. Q2 2025 deliveries of 384,100 vehicles, net profit of 1.172 billion — a net profit of USD 3,051 per vehicle.

It sold 100,000 more vehicles and total profit fell 0.058 billion. That is the mathematical proof of "revenue up, profit down" — every extra vehicle sold earns less. The record deliveries are a negative contribution on the income statement, because they were bought with price cuts.

III. Getting the classification right: three asset types under one roof
Category 1: Mature manufacturing (autos). Gross margin 16.9%, operating margin 1.4%, and an optimistic 2027 net profit of USD 1.8 billion. At 25x, that is USD 450 billion. This is the entire source of certainty. Category 2: Growth-stage energy business (energy storage). 13.5GWh/quarter, +41% YoY, with California 40GWh + Shanghai 40GWh + Texas planned 50GWh. Segment gross margin 20.4%, a figure struck after one-off provisions. 30x and USD 80 billion is a reasonable range. Category 3: Options (Robotaxi, Optimus, FSD). No standalone revenue, no unit economics, no clear timeline. Their value today equals "whatever the market believes they are worth," USD 910 billion.

Each category demands a different valuation method: P/E for manufacturing, P/E or EV/EBITDA for energy, probability weighting for options. Lumping them together under a single multiple is the root of the current mispricing.

IV. Three conclusions after the cognitive reset
Conclusion 1: The company is certainly worth RMB 570 billion; whether it is worth RMB 1.48 trillion is uncertain. Uncertainty is not an asset, but the market has already booked it as one. Conclusion 2: What decides whether this investment works is not vehicle sales but whether Robotaxi and Optimus can generate USD 10 billion-level revenue before 2028. Delivery volumes are the wrong metric to watch. Conclusion 3: This is not a stock that can be "held long term without looking." Its valuation rests on assumptions, and those assumptions need annual reconciliation. If you cannot reconcile them annually, you should not hold it.
Five, what this classification means for you

Accept the "three asset classes" framework and the next step is obvious: you do not need to forecast whether Tesla can succeed; you only need to judge how much of the current price is paying for possibility. The answer: 61% of market cap is paying for it.

The metrics to track change accordingly. Stop watching monthly deliveries; watch instead whether FSD revenue is broken out separately, whether Robotaxi trip volume is disclosed, and whether Optimus ships.If two of these three metrics appear, "possibility" is starting to become "fact," and at that point USD 1.48 trillion may not look expensive.

Conversely, if none of the three appears by 2027, the USD 910 billion option depreciates at 30% a year and the stock grinds down toward the USD 144 level implied by USD 570 billion.This is not alarmism; it is math.

Conclusion

What the cognitive reset means: translate "Tesla is impressive" into "which parts are worth how much." Do that and you find the impressive parts are indeed impressive, but they account for only 39%; the remaining 61% is "possibly impressive." Paying full price for possibility is the biggest risk in this investment.
