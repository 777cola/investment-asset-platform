Financial reality: linking the three statements reveals a story of "mortgaging the present against the future" — Tesla

The income statement is deteriorating, cash flow is bleeding, but the balance sheet remains healthy

Core view

Any single statement misleads: the income statement says "fine" (net profit down only 5%), the cash flow statement says "danger" (FCF -RMB 1.09 billion), the balance sheet says "no problem" (RMB 43.5 billion cash). Only by reading the three statements together does the truth emerge.

1. Income statement: net profit fell only 5% thanks to RMB 590 million in non-operating income

Operating profit USD 398 million (-57%) → plus other income USD 590 million (bitcoin fair value changes, FX) → plus/minus tax → net profit attributable to shareholders USD 1.114 billion (-5%). This chain shows that the "resilience" in net profit comes from items unrelated to operations.

Excluding this item, operating net profit is roughly USD 500–600 million, a year-on-year decline of more than 50%. Non-GAAP net profit USD 1.153 billion, -17% year on year, is closer to reality than GAAP because it excludes some one-off items.

So the correct order for reading this earnings report is: first look at operating profit (USD 398 million), then operating margin (1.4%), and only then net profit (USD 1.114 billion). Reverse the order and you will be fooled by the "only down 5%".

II. Cash flow statement: cash generation is fine, investment intensity is out of control

Operating cash flow RMB 4.697 billion, +85% YoY, driven mainly by delivery growth and inventory drawdown (production 451,800 units, deliveries 480,100 units, net drawdown of 28,400 vehicles). This shows the core business still generates cash.

Capex RMB 5.789 billion, +142% YoY, free cash flow -RMB 1.092 billion. This is the first quarterly cash burn since early 2024. Full-year guidance exceeds USD 25 billion (USD 8.5 billion in 2025), with borrowing capacity of up to USD 30 billion being arranged.

Reading the three numbers together: cash generation 4.7 billion, investment 5.8 billion, gap 1.1 billion. The gap is small, but the trend is poor—Q1 was still +1.44 billion, and it flipped in a single quarter.

III. Balance sheet: a shield now, possibly a liability later

Cash and short-term investments of RMB 43.52 billion, minimal interest-bearing debt, liabilities dominated by accounts payable and deferred revenue, debt-to-asset ratio around 40%.This is its hardest advantage over China's new EV makers: it does not need to survive on financing.

But if free cash flow stays negative through 2026–2027, a large chunk of the RMB 43.5 billion cash will be consumed and the RMB 30 billion borrowing facility will be drawn.At that point the "net cash company" label is gone, and the valuation framework shifts from "growth stock" to "heavy-asset manufacturer."

How damaging is that shift?The same RMB 4 billion net profit is worth RMB 100 billion at 25x and RMB 60 billion at 15x.A change in the multiple is more lethal than a change in profit.

IV. Three accounting line items to watch
1. Other income. RMB 590 million this quarter, including Bitcoin and FX. This line item is highly volatile; in 2025, fair-value changes on Bitcoin alone halved net profit. Treat it as a "random item," not as profit. 2. Share-based compensation. Tied to the 2025 compensation plan; the amount rises as the share price rises. Higher share price, higher expense, lower profit — a self-offsetting loop. 3. Warranty accruals. RMB 240 million in the Energy segment this quarter, cutting gross margin from 39.5% to 20.4%. Once is an accident; twice in a row is a product problem. In Q3, we must check whether this item is still there.
Section 5: Three quarters ahead: two scenarios
Scenario A (40% probability): Q3 automotive gross margin holds at 16.5%, energy storage gross margin returns to 25% (no further warranty accruals), capex falls QoQ, and free cash flow returns to around 0.0. In this case, the market will price it as "the investment phase is nearing its end," and the share price will find support.Scenario B (60% probability): Q3 gross margin falls below 16%, energy storage gross margin remains near 20%, capex continues to rise, free cash flow is negative for a second consecutive quarter, and the company draws on its borrowing facility. In this case, the market will price in "asset-heavy transformation," valuation multiples will be revised down, and the share price will fall back below USD 300.

The dividing line between the two scenarios is the same number: 16% automotive gross margin. It determines whether the company is in an "investment phase" or a "cash-burn phase," and whether USD 374 is reasonable or overvalued.

VI. Like-for-like comparison with 2025Q2: every line tells a story

Revenue 22,496 → 28,236 (+25.5%); gross profit 3,878 → 4,751 (+22.5%); operating profit 923 → 398 (-56.9%); net profit attributable to parent 1,172 → 1,114 (-4.9%); operating cash flow 2,540 → 4,697 (+84.9%); capex 2,394 → 5,789 (+141.8%); free cash flow 146 → -1,092.

Of the six lines, only two are good: revenue and operating cash flow. The other four—gross profit growing slower than revenue, operating profit halved, net profit down slightly, free cash flow turning negative—all say the same thing: scale is expanding, efficiency is falling.

Capex especially: +141.8%, from RMB 2.394 billion to RMB 5.789 billion. This is not "investing a bit more," it is "more than doubling." Annual investment intensity jumping from RMB 8.5 billion to RMB 25 billion amounts to restructuring the company from "manufacturing" into "infrastructure."

Conclusion

Financial reality: The company has no financial crisis, but its financial model is changing. It is shifting from "self-funded spending out of its own earnings" to "debt-fueled expansion," and from "asset-light, high-margin" to "asset-heavy, mid-margin." This shift will not show up in the share price immediately, but it will gradually change the multiple the market is willing to pay—and the multiple is the main variable in valuation.
