## Assumption 1: Auto gross margin bottoms out — under observation, don't jump to conclusions

The 16.9% automotive gross margin includes USD 146 million in credits (about 0.7 percentage points); excluding them it is 16.2%. Last year the same period ex-credits was about 15.2%. **Year over year it improved, showing cost reduction is working; but sequentially it fell from 21.1% in Q1 (including tariff refunds), an ugly trend.**

What matters is not the absolute level but whether "cost reduction can outrun price cuts." **In 2026Q2 per-vehicle manufacturing cost is falling and ASP is also falling, the two nearly offsetting each other — that is why gross margin is stuck near 16%.** If Q3 breaks below 16%, price cuts have outrun cost reduction and the thesis fails.

## Hypothesis 2 and 3: energy storage and FSD—one falls short, one is invisible

The problem with energy storage is "volume up, price down": 13.5GWh, up 41% year on year, with revenue up only 12.6% and unit price down about 20%; segment gross margin was 20.4%, halved from 39.5% a year earlier, of which RMB 240 million was a supplier battery warranty provision.**Add back the one-off and gross margin is roughly 28% — the thesis hasn't broken, it was just obscured by a one-time item.**

The problem with FSD is that it's "invisible": services revenue was RMB 4.581 billion, up 50% year on year, and the company says FSD subscription growth is in there but won't give the amount.**A segment contributing 16% of revenue and growing 50%, with no segment data at all — that's rare among large US-listed companies.**

Taken together:**energy storage is "visible good, obscured by a one-off"; FSD is "invisible good, taken on faith."**The former can be verified in the next earnings report; the latter can only wait for the company to disclose it.

## Assumption 4: Robotaxi — initially holds, but scale is a black box

The progress is real: Dallas and Houston launched in April 2026, both driverless from day one; expansion to the entire Austin metro area in June; Miami, Orlando and Tampa added in July, covering seven major US metro areas in total; the Cybercab is already in production at the Texas factory, with employee pilot operations starting in July.

But compare with Waymo: 10 cities, 500,000 paid rides per week, a fleet of about 3,000 vehicles, and a year-end target of 1 million rides per week. **Tesla discloses no ride volume, no fleet size and no average daily revenue per vehicle—all three key metrics are missing.**

So the status is "preliminarily established": **the technology route works, but the business model is unproven.** Whether this can become "established" depends on whether the company is willing to give the numbers in its Q3 earnings report.

## Assumption 5: capex self-funding — disproven, and this is the most important change

2026Q2 operating cash flow was RMB 4.697 billion, capex RMB 5.789 billion, free cash flow negative RMB 1.092 billion. Full-year guidance exceeds USD 25 billion (USD 8.5 billion in 2025), alongside preparations for a borrowing facility of up to USD 30 billion. **Put the three numbers together and only one conclusion holds: starting this year, Tesla will rely on external funding to expand.**

This is not a financial crisis—it still holds RMB 43.5 billion in cash, with a debt ratio of about 40%. **It is a change in the valuation logic: from a self-funding growth stock to an asset-heavy company that needs continuous financing.** The former can command high multiples; the latter only manufacturing multiples.

So the falsification of this assumption matters more than the other four combined. **It does not change whether the company succeeds; it changes how much the market is willing to pay for that success.**

## Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar

**Falsification conditions for the overall thesis (any one triggers removal from the watchlist):**- Automotive gross margin below 15% for two consecutive quarters;
- FSD operations restricted in major markets, or a major regulatory penalty;
- Musk steps down as CEO or substantively exits day-to-day management;
- Energy storage segment gross margin below 15% for two consecutive quarters (still low after excluding one-offs).

**Watch calendar:** Early each month, China wholesale/insurance registration data (BYD, Xiaomi, Tesla China sales); early October, Q3 delivery announcement; late October, Q3 earnings (focus on automotive gross margin, whether FSD is broken out separately, FCF); each quarter, new Robotaxi cities and whether safety drivers are removed; energy storage orders and Megapack delivery cadence.**Next decision point: Q3 earnings.** If automotive gross margin holds at 16.5%, FCF turns positive quarter on quarter, and FSD is still not broken out separately — maintain watch; if gross margin falls below 16% and FCF is negative for a second consecutive quarter — downgrade to "avoid".
