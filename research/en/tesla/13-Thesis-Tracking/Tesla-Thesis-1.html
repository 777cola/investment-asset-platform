Core call: the thesis is intact, but it has not advanced enough to justify buying

Five assumptions must hold to justify buying Tesla. We check them one by one: auto gross margin bottoming out (under observation), energy storage becoming a second growth curve (not met), FSD monetisable (unverified), Robotaxi driverless across multiple cities (preliminarily holds), capex self-funding (disproven).

The fifth is the most consequential, and it has shifted from an "assumption" to a "counter-proof." Free cash flow of -USD 1.092 billion, full-year capex above USD 25 billion and a USD 30 billion borrowing facility being arranged — together these point to a company that has entered a phase of growth funded by external financing. For a stock trading at 389x P/E, this is the last change that should be appearing.

This report's assumptions draw on Tesla's 2026Q2 financial statements and public disclosures. Status definitions: Confirmed = metric achieved; Preliminary = direction correct but scale unverified; Under observation = neither deteriorated nor confirmed; Not met = metric clearly missed; Falsified = opposite to the assumed direction.
Assumption 1: Auto gross margin bottoms — under review, no premature call

The 16.9% automotive gross margin includes USD 146 million of credits (about 0.7pp); ex-credits it is 16.2%, versus roughly 15.2% ex-credits a year ago. YoY it improved, evidence that cost reduction is working; sequentially, however, it fell from 21.1% in Q1 (including tariff refunds) — an ugly trend.

The absolute level matters less than whether "cost reduction can outrun price cuts." In 2026Q2 per-vehicle manufacturing cost is falling and ASP is falling too, the two nearly offsetting each other — which is why gross margin is stuck near 16%. If Q3 breaks below 16%, price cuts have outrun cost reduction and the thesis fails.

Hypothesis 2 and 3: energy storage and FSD—one falls short, one is invisible

Energy storage has a volume-up, price-down problem: 13.5GWh, up 41% YoY, but revenue up only 12.6% and unit price down about 20%. Segment gross margin was 20.4%, halved from 39.5% a year earlier, including a RMB 240 million supplier battery warranty provision.Add back the one-off and gross margin is roughly 28% — the thesis hasn't broken, it was just obscured by a one-time item.

FSD has a visibility problem: services revenue was RMB 4.581 billion, up 50% YoY, and the company says FSD subscription growth is in there but won't give the amount.A segment contributing 16% of revenue and growing 50%, with no segment data at all — that's rare among large US-listed companies.

Taken together:energy storage is visibly good but obscured by a one-off; FSD is invisibly good and taken on faith.The former can be verified in the next earnings report; the latter can only wait for the company to disclose it.

Assumption 4: Robotaxi — holds for now, but scale remains a black box

Progress is real: Tesla entered Dallas and Houston in April 2026, both driverless from day one; expanded to the entire Austin metro area in June; added Miami, Orlando and Tampa in July, bringing the total to seven major US metro areas; and Cybercab has started production at the Texas plant, with employee trial operations beginning in July.

The contrast with Waymo is stark: 10 cities, 500,000 paid rides per week, a fleet of about 3,000 vehicles, and a year-end target of 1 million rides per week.Tesla discloses no ride volume, fleet size or average daily revenue per vehicle — all three key metrics are missing.

The status is therefore "preliminarily established": the technology route works, but the business model is not yet validated. Whether it becomes fully "established" depends on whether the company is willing to give numbers in its Q3 earnings report.

Assumption 5: capex self-funding — disproven, and this is the most important change

2026Q2 operating cash flow was RMB 4.697 billion, capex RMB 5.789 billion and free cash flow negative RMB 1.092 billion. Full-year guidance exceeds USD 25 billion (USD 8.5 billion in 2025), alongside preparations for a borrowing facility of up to USD 30 billion. Put the three numbers together and only one conclusion holds: starting this year, Tesla will rely on external funding to expand.

This is not a financial crisis—it still holds RMB 43.5 billion in cash, with a debt ratio of about 40%. It is a change in the valuation logic: from a self-funding growth stock to an asset-heavy company that needs continuous financing. The former can command high multiples; the latter only manufacturing multiples.

So the falsification of this assumption matters more than the other four combined. It does not change whether the company succeeds; it changes how much the market is willing to pay for that success.

Falsification conditions and monitoring calendar
Falsification conditions for the overall thesis (any one triggers removal from the watchlist):
  • Automotive gross margin below 15% for two consecutive quarters;
  • FSD operations restricted in major markets, or a major regulatory penalty;
  • Musk steps down as CEO or substantively exits day-to-day management;
  • Energy storage segment gross margin below 15% for two consecutive quarters (still low after excluding one-offs).
Watch calendar: Early each month: China wholesale/insurance registration data (BYD, Xiaomi, Tesla China sales). Early October: Q3 delivery announcement. Late October: Q3 earnings (focus on automotive gross margin, whether FSD is broken out separately, FCF). Each quarter: new Robotaxi cities and whether safety drivers are removed; energy storage orders and Megapack delivery cadence.Next decision point: Q3 earnings. If automotive gross margin holds at 16.5%, FCF turns positive quarter on quarter and FSD is still not broken out separately, maintain watch; if gross margin falls below 16% and FCF is negative for a second consecutive quarter, downgrade to "avoid".

Conclusion

Conclusion: the thesis survives, but its quality is deteriorating. Of the five hypotheses, two hold (gross margin, still under observation, counts as half), one is disproven and two remain unverified. The most critical change: "self-funding capex" has been disproven, pushing the company from a "growth stock" toward a "heavy-asset manufacturer" valuation framework. Action: stay on watch, no position; Q3 earnings are the next decision point, with focus on gross margin and free cash flow, not deliveries.
