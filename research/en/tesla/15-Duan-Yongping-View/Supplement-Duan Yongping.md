## Q: Is this a good business?

Answer: Half good, half grind. **Selling cars is a tough business** — 16.8% gross margin, 1.4% operating margin, a production line costs several billion dollars, model changes require new tooling, and prices fall every year. The world's best automakers earn long-run net margins of just 8%. **Energy storage is a good business** — long-term contracts, predictable cash flow, clear scale effects — but gross margin is only 20.4% because the cells are purchased.

Robots and transport capacity are not yet businesses, only possibilities. **Possibilities can be very big things, but possibilities cannot be counted as profit.**

So I often say, don't touch a business you don't understand. Not because it's bad, but because I can't put a price on it. Anything I can't price can go to the moon and it's none of my concern.

## Q: Will Tesla still be around in ten years?

Answer: Yes, most likely still there, and possibly bigger. **But whether it will be number one, I am not sure.**

A decade ago, who could have imagined BYD selling 4 million vehicles a year, or Xiaomi building a car in three years? **In the auto industry, a technology lead is worth only one product cycle.** Tesla's 4680 cells, gigacasting and end-to-end autonomous driving could be standard equipment three years from now.

When I bought Apple, I asked: will people still use the iPhone in ten years? The answer was yes. When I bought Moutai, I asked: will people still drink Moutai in ten years? The answer was yes. **The question for Tesla is: will people still buy Tesla cars in ten years? I cannot answer that — because there are too many substitutes.**

## Q: Can you understand it?

Answer: I understand the car business; I do not understand the valuation. **These are two separate matters.**

On the car side, I can do the math: 48.0 vehicles, RMB 28.2 billion revenue, RMB 4.75 billion gross profit, RMB 400 million operating profit. **Every line is in the financials.** I can also do the math on energy storage: 13.5GWh, RMB 3.14 billion revenue, 20.4% gross margin.

But of the RMB 1.48 trillion market cap, RMB 910 billion has no corresponding revenue. **Robotaxi discloses no trip volumes, FSD discloses no dollar amounts, Optimus has not shipped.** You want me to believe these three together are worth RMB 910 billion — that is not investing, that is faith.

I have nothing against faith; I have believed in things myself. **But faith should not be practiced under the name of margin of safety.**

## Q: At what price would you buy?

A: Below USD 300 is negotiable; below USD 250 I would look seriously. **This is not a guess — it is calculated.**

The auto business sells 1.9 million vehicles in 2027 at USD 950 net profit per vehicle, at 25x, worth USD 450 billion; energy storage USD 80 billion; net cash USD 40 billion. **The three together total USD 570 billion, or USD 144 per share.** The rest is optionality.

At USD 300, the optionality component is 55%, meaning the market is willing to pay half the price for "possibility" — I accept that, because what I am buying is "half certainty, half possibility." **At USD 374, optionality is 61% — possibility costs more than certainty, and that I do not accept.**

Some will ask, what if it rises to USD 600? **Then I do not buy. Missing out is no disgrace; buying wrong is.**

## Q: What are you most afraid of?

Answer: My biggest fear is that it becomes "the right company, the wrong stock." **Companies getting better while shareholder returns get worse—I have seen that far too many times.**

Railroads were right, aviation was right, solar was right, but the long-term returns for the vast majority of participants were close to zero. **Because all the money they earned was plowed back into capacity.** Tesla's 2026 capital expenditure of USD 25 billion and free cash flow of -USD 1.09 billion mark the beginning of this pattern.

The second fear is the person. **This company relies too heavily on one man.** He is genuinely a genius, but geniuses have their own lives, their own interests, their own pile of companies. What you are buying is his attention, and his attention is finite.

The third fear is "the story keeps getting bigger." Today it is Robotaxi, tomorrow it is Optimus, the day after it is Mars. **The bigger the story, the more evidence it requires; the less evidence there is, the more expensive the price.**

## Q: Growth is this fast—why aren't you tempted?

A: Because I ran the numbers, and the growth was bought.**Revenue +26%, net profit -5%, free cash flow -RMB 1.09 billion — put those three lines together and that says it all.**

At my age, I am not easily moved by "growth rates."**Growth can be bought; profit cannot.**If a company has to spend two times the capex to buy one time the revenue growth, that growth is worth nothing to shareholders.

Of course, I could be wrong.**If Optimus really works out, if Robotaxi really scales, then looking back ten years from now, there will be no difference between USD 300 and USD 374.**But investing is not about buying "possible"; it is about buying "certain." I can only earn the money I understand.
