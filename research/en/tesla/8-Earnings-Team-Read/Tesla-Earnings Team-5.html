Synthesis of Views — Tesla

The four's consensus and disagreement
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)

Core view

Four readers, four angles on the same earnings report, and consensus outweighs disagreement. The consensus: profits are deteriorating and cash is bleeding. The disagreement: whether this is a temporary investment phase or a permanent structural problem.
Three consensus views
Consensus one: revenue growth is real, and so is the profit deterioration. All four acknowledge that 480,126 deliveries and RMB 28.236 billion in revenue are hard results; all four also acknowledge that a 1.4% operating margin and -17% Non-GAAP net profit are hard problems. None of them treats this as a "one-off factor," because the expenses are structural. Consensus two: capex is the core variable in this earnings report. USD 25 billion, free cash flow of -1.09 billion and a 30 billion borrowing facility — what Buffett sees is negative owner earnings, what Munger sees is incentive-driven resource transfer, what Li Lu sees is the early stage of a capital cycle, and what Duan Yongping sees is "money I don't understand." The four said the same thing in four languages: the risk on this money is extremely high. Consensus three: the disappearance of credit revenue is irreversible. 146 million versus 439 million — the policy has already changed (subsidies expired + penalties eliminated), and this line will not come back. This means the "hidden subsidy" to gross margin has permanently vanished, and all future gross margins must be compared against a lower baseline.
Two disagreements
Disagreement 1: Is the 16.8% gross margin a floor or a midpoint? Duan Yongping argues the underlying gross margin (excluding credits) is still improving YoY, evidence that cost reduction is working, and that the floor sits around 16%. Buffett argues the price war has not cleared, Chinese manufacturers are still adding capacity, and gross margin will keep falling. Verdict: watch Q3. If automotive gross margin falls below 16%, Buffett's judgment holds.Disagreement 2: Is USD 25 billion of capex foresight or loss of control? Li Lu leans toward "foresight" — only Tesla can invest simultaneously in compute, chips, vehicles and energy storage, a distinctive capital allocation capability. Munger leans toward "loss of control" — the incentive structure dictates that management will inevitably put money into robots rather than cars. Verdict: both are right; the difference is the time scale. Within three years it looks like loss of control; after ten years it may look like foresight.
One-line synthesis

If you keep only one line, make it this: "With a record-revenue earnings report, Tesla proved it is turning into an asset-heavy company, while the market still prices it as an asset-light one."

That line explains every numerical contradiction: revenue up, profit down, cash flow negative — because it is building new factories; valuation high, P/E above 300x — because the market still applies software-company multiples. The mismatch between the two sides is the current USD 374 price.

Conclusion

Synthesis: All four masters agree the company is fine, the financials are problematic and the price even more so. Their disagreement bears only on when to buy, not on whether to refrain from buying now. On the watchlist: gross margin bottoming, capex peaking and FSD revenue broken out separately.
