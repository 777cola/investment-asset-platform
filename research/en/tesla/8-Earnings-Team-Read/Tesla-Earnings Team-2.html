Earnings Deep Dive (Buffett) — Tesla

Owner earnings are negative. That is enough.

Core view

Buffett looks at just one number: owner earnings, or operating cash flow less maintenance capex. This quarter that is RMB 4.697 billion minus at least RMB 3 billion of maintenance spending and RMB 2.8 billion of growth spending, leaving negative RMB 1.09 billion.
Owner earnings: negative this quarter

Buffett values companies on "owner earnings" — operating cash flow less maintenance capex. Tesla generated RMB 4.697 billion of operating cash flow this quarter, which sounds healthy, but capex was RMB 5.789 billion: maintenance spend (equipment upgrades, molds, production line upkeep) conservatively RMB 3 billion, growth spend (AI compute, Cybercab, Optimus) about RMB 2.8 billion. Owner earnings = 4.697 − 3 = RMB 1.697 billion; free cash flow = −RMB 1.092 billion.

The gap between the two: owner earnings assume growth spending can stop, free cash flow assumes it cannot. At Tesla it cannot — stopping means abandoning Robotaxi and Optimus, and that USD 910 billion option instantly goes to zero. Cash genuinely available for distribution is therefore negative.

When a company cannot freely distribute cash, its value rests solely on "free cash flow turning positive in some future year." And "some future year" is the hardest thing to value.

Moat test: can it raise prices?

I judge a moat by one standard only: can it raise prices without losing customers. See's Candies can, Coca-Cola can, Apple can. Tesla cut prices in every major market in 2026; Q2 deliveries of 480,000 units set a record, but average selling price kept falling.

It has real assets: 70,000 superchargers, a vertically integrated manufacturing system, fleet data from millions of vehicles, and 200MW-class training compute. These cannot be bought with money; they are built over time. But assets are not a moat—a moat means "competitors cannot take your profit," not "competitors cannot catch your technology."

Chinese manufacturers have caught up. BYD uses vertical integration to push costs below Tesla's, Xiaomi diverts demand in the 200,000–300,000 price band with its ecosystem and advanced driver assistance, and Huawei makes advanced driver assistance standard. A three-year technology lead supports only one product cycle in the auto industry.

Why I would take a look at energy storage

Energy is the only business in this earnings report that suits my taste: 13.5GWh, up 41% YoY, revenue of RMB 3.139 billion, long-term contracts, predictable cash flow, obvious scale effects, with utilities and data centers as customers. This is a business I can understand.

Gross margin was only 20.4% this quarter, however, versus 39.5% a year earlier, hit by a USD 240 million warranty provision for battery suppliers and the disappearance of tariff benefits. Warranty provisions are routine in manufacturing — they show its control over the supply chain is not as strong as imagined; the cells are bought in.

If Tesla ever spins off energy storage into a separate listing, I would look at it seriously. Bundled inside the vehicle business, it is obscured by AI spending and the price war, like selling a good plot of land tied together with a saline-alkali plot.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: A great company, but not a great investment. Owner earnings are negative, the price war is eroding the moat, and the valuation has no traditional anchor—any one of the three would be enough for me to pass. I will keep it on my radar and wait for the price to return to a range where I can "run the numbers".
