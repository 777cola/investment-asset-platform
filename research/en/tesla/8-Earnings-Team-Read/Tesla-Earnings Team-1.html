Earnings Deep Dive (Duan Yongping) — Tesla

First ask where the money comes from, then ask where it goes
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)

Core view

The most valuable line in this earnings report is "services and other +50% YoY." It shows subscription revenue is starting to reach scale. The most dangerous line is "capex +142% YoY." It shows the money earned has all been plowed back in.
Where the money comes from: four pockets, only one of them new

RMB 28.236 billion in revenue — I generally don't look at the total, I look at the structure. Automotive RMB 20.516 billion, services RMB 4.581 billion, energy RMB 3.139 billion, credits RMB 146 million. Automotive is the old business, energy the middle one, services the new one, credits the dead one.

Services, up 50% year on year to a record high, is the line I care about most. It houses supercharging, insurance, used cars and FSD subscriptions — what these four share is that they need no new factories, and one sale can collect money many times over. If this segment can keep growing above 40%, in three years it will be Tesla's most profitable business.

But the company won't tell you how much it earns. On RMB 4.581 billion of revenue, what's the gross margin? What share is FSD subscriptions? Not a single number. This is what I mean by "can't figure it out" — not can't figure out the technology, can't figure out the accounts.

Where the money went: USD 25 billion buys something three years out

Capex of RMB 5.789 billion, +142% YoY, exceeding RMB 25 billion for the full year, three times the RMB 8.5 billion in 2025. Where is the money going? AI compute (Cortex 1/2 above 200MW), the Cybercab line, the first-generation Optimus mass-production line, the Austin semiconductor plant, and energy storage capacity in three locations. Not a single dollar is invested in "selling more cars next year."

From the shareholder's perspective, this is a big bet: trading certain automotive cash flow for uncertain robot and transport-capacity revenue. From the company's perspective, it is the only choice—because the automotive business carries a gross margin of just 16.9%, and further capacity investment will not earn excess profit.

My view is simple: I don't invest in what I can't understand. I'm not saying he is necessarily wrong—it's that I can't value what he's betting on. That's also why I didn't buy Amazon back then. It has risen a lot since, and I have no regrets—because that wasn't money I could earn.

One detail: the points are gone, and gross margin's cover is blown

Regulatory credits fell from 439 million to 146 million. This revenue carries zero cost and flows straight into gross profit. Last year it contributed about 2 points to gross margin; this year only 0.5 points. Strip out the credits, and Tesla's true gross margin in 2026Q2 is around 16.2%, versus around 15.2% in 2025Q2.

Interestingly, the true gross margin still rose year on year. That shows carmaking efficiency itself is improving — 4680 cells, gigacasting and supply-chain localization are genuinely cutting costs. But the savings were given back to consumers through price cuts and consumed by AI investment.

That is the essence of this business: it can bring costs down, but it cannot hold the line on price. Able to cut costs but not raise prices is manufacturing; able to raise prices but not cut costs is a brand; only doing both makes a good business.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style conclusion: A good car, a tough business, an expensive price. Service revenue +50% is the only exciting number in this report, but it is too small and too opaque to support RMB 1.48 trillion. I would put it on the "buy once I understand it" list, not the "buy now" list.
