Investment conclusion: a good company at an expensive price—wait for a pullback, or wait for evidence

Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in Q2 2026 (+25%) on revenue of USD 28.236 billion (+26%), both quarterly records, yet operating margin fell to 1.4%, net profit attributable to shareholders came in at USD 1.114 billion (-5%) and free cash flow was -USD 1.092 billion. The company is shifting from a car-selling manufacturer to an infrastructure business selling compute, transport capacity and robots — at the cost of capex jumping from USD 8.5 billion to USD 25 billion.

Sum-of-the-parts: autos USD 450 billion + energy storage USD 80 billion + net cash USD 40 billion = USD 570 billion, or USD 144 per share; the remaining USD 910 billion (USD 230 per share) is option value on Robotaxi, Optimus and FSD. The current price of USD 374.01 already discounts most of the probability of success; 12-month target price USD 380, rating "Neutral/Watch", with a recommendation to build the position below USD 300.

Chapter 1 Company Overview

Tesla (TSLA.NASDAQ) was founded in 2003, listed in 2010 and is headquartered in Austin, Texas. Its business spans four segments: automotive (Model 3/Y, Cybertruck, Semi, Cybercab); energy generation and storage (Megapack, Powerwall); services and other (Supercharging, insurance, used vehicles, FSD subscription); and Robotaxi and Optimus, both still in the investment phase.

2026Q2 revenue was USD 28.236 billion, of which automotive accounted for USD 20.516 billion (72.7%), services USD 4.581 billion (16.2%) and energy USD 3.139 billion (11.1%). Global capacity: vehicle assembly across four sites—Shanghai, Fremont, Berlin and Texas; batteries in Texas at over 40GWh of 4680 cells and 7GWh of LFP in Nevada; energy storage at 40GWh in California, 40GWh in Shanghai and a planned 50GWh in Texas.

At the 2026/07/22 close of USD 374.01, market cap is roughly USD 1.48 trillion. The 52-week high is USD 489.88, leaving the stock -23.7% below that high and -17% year to date.It is the world's most valuable automaker and the one with the most unusual valuation approach.

On ownership, Musk holds about 13%, institutional investors led by Vanguard and BlackRock hold about 13% combined, and the remainder sits with retail investors and index funds. The company pays no dividend and has repurchased very little stock historically, so shareholder returns depend entirely on the share price—the structural reason it is so sensitive to narrative.

The business boundary deserves explanation: Tesla is neither a company that "only makes cars" nor one that "has already built AI." It uses vehicle assembly as its cash-flow entry point and channels profit into four new tracks—energy, compute, robotics and transport capacity.The key to understanding it is to value these four separately, not to assign a single total.

Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

The global EV industry enters 2026 with stock-level competition: China's H1 new-energy passenger vehicle growth slows to single digits, with BYD sales at 1.809 million units, -15.7% YoY; Europe, hit by tariffs and weak demand, saw Tesla sales of 238,700 units in 2025, -27% YoY; the US cancelled the USD 7,500 federal subsidy on September 30, 2025.

Autonomous driving enters its first year of commercialisation: Waymo does 500,000 paid rides per week across 10 cities, targeting 1 million by year-end; Tesla covers 7 major US metro areas, with Dallas and Houston operating without safety drivers from April 2026. Energy storage remains mid-growth, with global installations growing 30–40%.

Three segments, three cycles — the industry as a whole is not "high growth" but "structural divergence."Upside lies in autonomous driving and energy storage; downside lies in the vehicle price war.

On a TAM basis: 2030 global new-energy passenger vehicle sales are forecast at 40–50 million units; at USD 30,000 per vehicle and a 15% gross margin, the vehicle segment's gross profit pool is about USD 200 billion; global energy storage installations could reach 1TWh; at USD 23 million revenue per GWh and 20% gross margin, the gross profit pool is about USD 46 billion; if Robotaxi replaces 10% of urban travel, annual revenue would exceed USD 500 billion.

The size of the three pools determines the valuation imagination: vehicles are "big and thin," storage is "mid and steady," mobility is "big and illusory."The premium the market gives Tesla essentially prices the "big and illusory" pool as if it were "big and real."

Chapter 3 Business Model

Four revenue pools, highly vertically integrated on the cost side. Automotive gross margin 16.9%, energy 20.4%; services are not disclosed separately but are described as "profitability improving." The strategic direction is clear: shift the revenue mix from one-time vehicle sales to recurring fees (FSD subscriptions, supercharging, insurance, Robotaxi capacity).

The price is capex. 2026 guidance exceeds USD 25 billion, directed at AI compute (Cortex 1/2, over 200MW), Cybercab production lines, the first-generation Optimus mass-production line, the Austin semiconductor plant and energy storage capacity. This marks a shift from an asset-light to an asset-heavy model and sits at the root of the current valuation disagreement.

By customer structure, automotive is B2C (direct sales, no dealers), energy storage is B2B (utilities, data centers, commercial and industrial customers), and supercharging is shifting from B2C to B2B2C as the network opens to other brands. Each structure carries entirely different cash-flow characteristics: B2C is fast, B2B is slow but steady, and the open network sits between the two.

Software revenue recognition also merits attention: FSD one-time purchases are recognized over time and subscriptions monthly, with both currently lumped into "services and other." Even if FSD grows rapidly, investors cannot see its standalone trajectory in the financials—a hard disclosure deficiency, not an accounting issue.

Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

2026Q2: revenue RMB 28.236 billion (+25.5%), gross profit RMB 4.751 billion (+22.5%), operating profit RMB 398 million (-56.9%), net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 1.114 billion (-4.9%), Non-GAAP net profit RMB 1.153 billion (-17.2%), operating cash flow RMB 4.697 billion (+84.9%), capex RMB 5.789 billion (+141.8%), free cash flow -RMB 1.092 billion.

Three drivers of the profit decline: lower average vehicle selling price, regulatory credits falling from RMB 439 million to RMB 146 million, and operating expenses +47% (AI and robotics investment, share-based compensation).Cash flow turned negative for one reason only: capex doubled.

The balance sheet remains healthy: cash and short-term investments of RMB 43.524 billion, little interest-bearing debt, debt ratio around 40%. But with full-year capex of RMB 25 billion, the net cash position will most likely be exhausted this year, and the company has already arranged a borrowing facility of up to USD 30 billion.

Gross margin over the last five quarters makes the trend clearer: 2025Q2 17.2%, Q3 18.0%, Q4 20.1%, 2026Q1 21.1%, 2026Q2 16.8%.The rebound in the first three quarters relied on tariff refunds and credits; with those one-offs gone in Q2, the underlying level stands exposed at 16.8%.

Working capital is the hidden contributor in this report: output of 451,800 vehicles against deliveries of 480,100, a net inventory drawdown of 28,400 vehicles, releasing roughly USD 1.5–2.0 billion of cash.In other words, without this destocking, operating cash flow would not have been RMB 4.697 billion but perhaps just over RMB 3 billion.That contribution is not sustainable.

Chapter 5 Management and Governance

Musk is CEO, owns about 13%, draws no salary and takes all compensation through equity incentives. On 6 November 2025, shareholders approved his ten-year compensation plan with more than 75% support, a maximum consideration of about USD 1 trillion and 12 market-cap and operating milestones (up to USD 8.5 trillion).

Governance risk centres on three points: key-person dependence (no COO, no clear successor), related-party transactions (procurement and talent flows with xAI/SpaceX) and historical controversies (the 2018 SEC settlement, large share sales in 2022).Management capability scores 9; governance scores 4.

The dilution from the compensation plan needs to be calculated clearly: a maximum of 423.7 million shares, unlocked across 12 tranches, against roughly 3.5 billion shares currently outstanding, implies potential dilution of about 12%.This is not a one-off expense but equity compensation recognised gradually each year—which also explains why the "equity compensation" line within operating expenses swells as the share price rises.

The board's independence has long been questioned: Chair Denholm, Musk's brother Kimbal and media executive Murdoch are seen as too close to the CEO, and ISS and Glass Lewis have repeatedly recommended against key proposals.Where "one person equals a company," the board's real checks and balances are limited—this is the source of the governance discount.

Chapter 6 Growth Drivers
Driver 1: Deliveries return to growth. 838,000 vehicles in 1H26, up about 16% YoY; Q2 alone reached 480,100 units, a record for the period, with low-priced models and emerging markets the sources of incremental volume. Driver 2: Energy storage scales up. 13.5GWh per quarter, up 41% YoY, with planned capacity across three sites totalling 130GWh. Driver 3: FSD and Robotaxi. The supervised version of FSD entered China and 9 other countries in May 2026; Robotaxi covers 7 major metropolitan areas, and Cybercab has entered production. Driver 4: Optimus. The first-generation mass-production line at Fremont is under construction, with production planned to start by year-end.

Of the four drivers, the first two are visible in the financials; the latter two are only progress bars. The valuation prices in success on all four.

By region: in 2026Q1 the Shanghai plant delivered 213,000 vehicles, up 23.5% YoY, accounting for nearly 60% of global volume, with China still the largest production base and export hub; North America relies on Cybertruck, Semi and low-priced models to sustain volume; Europe, hit by tariffs, saw sales of 238,700 vehicles in 2025, down 27% YoY, and localization at the Berlin plant is the only solution.

From a product-cycle perspective, 2026 is a year of "holding volume with old models while new categories begin": Model 3/Y is sustained through refreshes and price cuts, Cybercab starts production in Texas with employee trial operations from July, and Semi remains in small-batch production. The real incremental volume must wait for the low-priced model and Cybercab mass production in 2027.

Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

Vehicles: BYD (1.809 million units in 2026H1, 789,000 exports), Xiaomi (group market cap of about HKD 700 billion, vehicle volume ramping) and Li Auto (Q1 gross margin 7.9%) are splitting customers across price bands, while Toyota still leads on global sales and cash flow.

Autonomous driving: Waymo leads in driverless operations (10 cities, 500,000 orders per week), while Huawei has made smart driving a standard feature in China.Tesla's edge is cost and generalization capability; its weakness is its safety record and regulatory trust.

Energy storage: CATL, Sungrow and Fluence undercut utility-scale storage pricing by 20–30%, while Tesla defends the high end through system integration and Autobidder software.

A three-way comparison of gross margin, growth and valuation is more intuitive: Tesla 16.8% / +26% / 389x, BYD about 18% / -15.7% / 30x, Xiaomi Auto about 20% / high growth / 17.5x (group), Li Auto 7.9% / declining / loss-making, Toyota about 20% / slight growth / 10x.Tesla is the only one combining the highest growth, the most expensive valuation and a mid-range gross margin.

That combination shows the market is not paying for current profitability but for the right to define the future category.If Robotaxi and Optimus succeed, today's gross margin is irrelevant; if they do not, Tesla is an ordinary automaker on a 16.8% gross margin, 26% growth and a 389x valuation—a combination that holds up under no valuation framework.

Chapter 8 Risk Disclosures
Logic-level risks (these would destroy the investment thesis): ① Tighter FSD/Robotaxi regulation restricts driverless operations; ② Musk steps back from day-to-day management and product cadence stalls. Either would put more than 50% of the valuation at risk. Price-level risks: ③ A renewed price war pushes gross margin below 15%; ④ Rising financing costs make the USD 25 billion capex unsustainable; ⑤ The pure-vision approach hits a ceiling at L4; ⑥ Further de-rating (the stock is already down 17% year to date). Other: Tariffs and geopolitics (the Shanghai plant accounts for nearly 60% of deliveries), concentrated battery supply, and FSD-related litigation and recalls.

Sensitivity: every 1 percentage point of gross margin decline, on annualised revenue of USD 110 billion, cuts gross profit by about USD 1.1 billion and net profit after tax by about USD 850 million — equivalent to 20% of 2026E net profit (USD 4.2 billion). That is why gross margin is the single most sensitive variable for this company.

Capex is the second: if 20% of the full-year USD 25 billion is overspent, free cash flow falls by a further USD 5 billion, at which point the USD 30 billion borrowing facility may need to be drawn in 2027. For shareholders, financing is not the disaster; dilution is — and the extent of dilution depends on the share price: the lower the share price, the more dilution for the same amount raised.

Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast
Key assumptions: 1.75 million vehicles delivered in 2026 (838,000 in H1 + 912,000 in H2) and 1.90 million in 2027; automotive gross margin of 17.0% in 2026 and 17.5% in 2027, as cost cuts and scale offset regulatory credit revenue falling to zero; energy storage revenue of USD 12 billion in 2026 at a 22% gross margin; services revenue growth of 25–30%. Forecast results: 2026E revenue of USD 110 billion (+16%), net profit attributable to shareholders of USD 4.2 billion, EPS of USD 1.20, free cash flow of -USD 3 billion; 2027E revenue of USD 122 billion, net profit of USD 5.5 billion, EPS of USD 1.55, free cash flow of USD 1 billion, turning positive once capex peaks.

Taken together, the model points to moderate revenue growth, a slow profit recovery and cash flow that is negative before turning positive. This is not a high-growth model; it is an asset-heavy recovery model.

The forecast breaks into four drivers: volume (1.75 million vehicles in 2026, 1.90 million in 2027), price (revenue per vehicle drifting down from USD 42,700 to USD 41,000), gross margin (17.0% → 17.5%, on cost cuts and a low base after regulatory credits fall to zero) and expenses (operating expense ratio falling from 15.4% to 13%, assuming AI investment peaks in 2027).

Expenses are the most uncertain of the four. If Optimus mass production slips and AI compute spending keeps ramping, the expense ratio could stay above 15% and 2027 net profit would fall from USD 5.5 billion to USD 3.5 billion, implying EPS of USD 0.90 — at which point the USD 374 price is 415x P/E. This is the biggest downside risk to our forecast.

Chapter 10 Valuation

SOTP: auto RMB 450 billion (2027E net profit RMB 1.81 billion × 25x), energy storage RMB 80 billion, net cash RMB 40 billion, for a total of RMB 570 billion, or USD 144 per share. At a current market cap of RMB 1.48 trillion, the market is implying RMB 910 billion of option value, or USD 230 per share.

We price that option on a probability-weighted basis: bear case 0 (Robotaxi stuck in pilot), base case RMB 600 billion (10 cities, revenue disclosed separately), bull case RMB 1.6 trillion (20 cities plus Optimus shipments), with weights of 25/50/25, giving RMB 700 billion. Total RMB 1,270 billion, or USD 321 per share.

At USD 374, the market price sits 17% above this base-case valuation, implying the market assigns a bull-case weight above 25%. That is not wrong, it is a choice—but we should not make that choice on the market's behalf. 12-month target price USD 380 (slightly above base case, reflecting option value from FSD rollout in China).

On historical valuation: Tesla's P/S ranged 10–25x in 2020–2021, fell to 8x in 2024 and is about 14x now (RMB 1.48 trillion ÷ RMB 103.6 billion TTM revenue). At P/S 14x and P/E 389x, the market is pricing sharp margin expansion, not sharp revenue growth.

Cross-check against comparables: Apple P/S about 8x, P/E 30x; Nvidia P/S about 25x, P/E 45x; BYD P/S about 0.9x, P/E 30x. Tesla's P/S sits between Apple and Nvidia, yet its P/E is more than ten times either—it trades on "hardware-company revenue plus software-company valuation," the most dangerous kind of mismatch.

Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations
Rating: Neutral/Watch. 12-month target price USD 380 (+2%). Rationale: the company is top-tier on quality (moat 33/50, management capability 9 points), but the current price already fully reflects the optimistic scenario, and downside (pessimistic USD 250, -33%) far exceeds upside (+2%). Action recommendation: do not chase the rally or build a position; build 1–2% below USD 300 and add to 3–5% below USD 250; if FSD/Robotaxi revenue is reported separately and exceeds USD 5 billion annualized, build 2–3% at a higher price. Existing holders (cost >USD 400) should trim on rebounds. Key tracking indicators: quarterly automotive gross margin (threshold 16%), free cash flow (threshold 0), whether FSD is reported separately, and the number of cities with driverless Robotaxi service and order volume.

Catalyst calendar: early October Q3 delivery announcement (watch whether deliveries hold above 45.0 thousand units); late October Q3 earnings (watch automotive gross margin, FCF and whether FSD is reported separately); start of each month China wholesale and insurance registration data; unscheduled new Robotaxi cities and Cybercab production progress, Optimus line commissioning.

Rating definitions: Buy = expected return over the next 12 months >20%; Watch = expected return -10% to +20%, waiting for a better price or evidence; Avoid = expected return <-10%. The current neutral scenario is +2%, pessimistic -33%, probability-weighted +4.9%, placing it in the "Watch" range and near the lower bound.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Neutral/Watch, target price USD 380. Tesla has the deepest technical moat and the richest set of options along this supply chain — that is not in dispute; the dispute is price. 61% of its USD 1.48 trillion market cap sits in businesses not yet generating revenue. Buy below USD 300 (implying options at 55% of value), or wait for FSD revenue to be reported separately. Until then, watching is the optimal strategy.
