## Duan Yongping: Do you understand this business?

Duan Yongping asks only three questions before buying a company: is it a good business, is the corporate culture right, and is the price cheap. Tesla half-passes the first two: the car is a good car, Musk is an extreme productist, and the company really is doing "the hard and right thing." But it fails the third, because you cannot price a Robotaxi that has not yet landed.

He would use Apple as the comparison. Every dollar of Apple's profit can be traced in its financials: how many iPhones it sold, how much services revenue it booked, what its gross margin was. Of Tesla's USD 1.48 trillion market cap, USD 910 billion cannot be traced — not because the company is hiding it, but because that business does not yet exist. The test of "understandable" is not understanding the technology, but being able to put a number on the money.

Another Duan Yongping line fits well here: "When you don't know what you don't know, you are most likely to lose money." Most people buying Tesla know what they are betting on, but few have calculated the odds. The neutral-case target price of USD 380 equals the current price — bet right and you make nothing, bet wrong and you lose 30%; this is not a bet he would take.

## Buffett: This Time Neither the Moat nor the Margin of Safety Is on His Side

Buffett would start by scoring the moat highly: the Supercharger network is infrastructure, and the siting and grid capacity behind 70,000 stalls cannot be replicated quickly at any price; vertical integration keeps batteries, castings and software in-house, and the cost advantage is real; the brand is in a class of its own among EVs.But he would immediately add that moats in the auto industry have historically been short-lived. Ford, GM and Toyota all once looked invincible.

On margin of safety he would be more direct. Buffett bought BYD in 2008 at around 10x P/E and Apple in 2016 at 13x. Tesla today trades at close to 390x TTM net profit, and even on a neutral 2027 forecast it is above 300x.There is no traditional valuation anchor to be found — it is not merely expensive, it is anchorless.

Energy storage might interest him. Storage is a utility-style business: long-term contracts, predictable cash flow, obvious scale effects, and quarterly deployments of 13.5GWh are already substantial. But storage gross margin was only 20.4% this quarter and is still being dragged down by supplier problems.If Tesla ever spun storage out separately, that is an asset he would be willing to look at.

## Munger: invert the question—under what circumstances would it halve?

Munger's approach is to invert the question: what would it take for Tesla to halve? Three triggers. First, tighter regulation or an outright ban on FSD in major markets — the US NHTSA's investigation into supervised FSD has never closed, China only opened supervised FSD in May 2026, and any major accident would bring immediate tightening.

Second, another round of China price war turns per-vehicle gross margin negative.In 1H26 BYD sold 1.809 million vehicles, down 15.7% YoY, and exported 789,000, up 70.5% — the leader is bleeding at home and seeking a way out overseas, the state that most easily triggers a fresh round of price cuts. Tesla's Shanghai plant delivered 213,000 vehicles in 2026Q1, nearly 60% of global volume; when China sneezes, its gross margin catches cold.Third, governance events.Musk's attention, xAI's related-party transactions with Tesla, and the dilution from the USD 1 trillion pay package — if any one of these is magnified, the option premium the market grants will contract. Should any of the three triggers materialise, the USD 910 billion option value gets cut in half first, and the remaining pure operating business supports less than USD 600 billion — the 60% downside is real, not alarmism.

## Li Lu: who captures the excess profit in a civilisation-scale trend

Li Lu would take the longer view: electrification, autonomous driving and embodied intelligence each represent a civilisation-scale efficiency leap, and Tesla holds real assets in all three — its 40,000-plus Superchargers, a 70,000-strong network, 200MW-class training compute and a fleet of several million vehicles.Rare is the company with "three options existing at once."

In Li Lu's framework, however, getting the trend right and getting shareholder returns right are two different things. Railways, aviation and solar were all correct trends, yet long-term returns for the vast majority of participants were close to zero, because capex swallowed all the profit.Tesla's 25 billion USD of capex in 2026 and free cash flow of -1.09 billion USD are the early form of exactly this pattern.

He would ask one final question: who captures the excess profit at the endgame? On vehicles, cost competition among Chinese manufacturers has already squeezed gross margins to around 15%; on autonomous driving, Waymo has reached 500,000 rides per week across 10 cities, while Huawei has made smart driving a standard feature in China; on robotics, Optimus has yet to ship, and more than a dozen Chinese companies are already at work.The direction is right and the position is good, but the endgame share is uncertain — this is "you can read the trend, but not the winner."

## Odds assessment: base case implies only 2% upside

The three scenarios carry 25%/50%/25% weights, implying an expected target price of USD 392.5, or +4.9% versus the current price. The 50% upside requires three conditions to hold simultaneously; the 33% downside requires only one. These are not favourable odds—this is an investment with unclear win probability and an unfavourable payoff.

Market positioning is more telling. The stock is down 23.7% from its 52-week high of USD 489.88 and 17% year to date, yet valuation has barely improved—because earnings are falling too. This is the classic pattern of "earnings de-rating without valuation de-rating": the numerator falls and the denominator falls with it, so the more it drops, the more expensive it gets.

The real inflection point is not the share price but the structure of the financial statements. Only when FSD and Robotaxi revenue are first disclosed separately can the valuation method shift from "storytelling" to "multiple crunching." Until then, any rebound is sentiment, not value.

## Our weighted valuation sits below the working paper — a point of disagreement worth flagging

The working paper assigns USD 380 under the base case, implying option value of about RMB 930 billion. Probability-weighting that option — 0 in the bear case, RMB 600 billion in the base case and RMB 1.6 trillion in the bull case, at weights of 25/50/25 — gives RMB 700 billion, which together with RMB 570 billion for the core business totals RMB 1,270 billion, or USD 321 per share.

The USD 59 (15%) gap sits not in the core business but in the option: the working paper's base case implies Robotaxi is worth RMB 930 billion once it begins charging on a small scale in 2027; we put that probability at only 50%. Waymo has operated for eight years across 10 cities at 500,000 rides a week and still has not disclosed per-vehicle profitability — evidence that this business pays off more slowly than the story suggests.

Approach: keep the working paper's USD 380 as the published target price (consistent with the rating and conclusion), but use USD 321 in internal odds calculations. Both point to the same action — do not buy at the current price. The disagreement does not change the conclusion, only the anchor for "fair value".
