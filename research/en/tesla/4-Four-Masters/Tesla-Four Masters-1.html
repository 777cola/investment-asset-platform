Four Masters — Tesla

Tesla (TSLA) | 04 · Four Masters
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)
← Back to the hub

Core view: Duan Yongping says he cannot understand it, Buffett says it has no moat, Munger says this is a narrative premium, and Li Lu says the winner may not be it

Four masters would give four languages and the same conclusion. Duan Yongping's question is the plainest: of the USD 374, USD 230 buys "later," and "later" cannot be counted as an asset. Buffett would acknowledge the moat, then point out that moats in the auto industry have historically been short-lived. Munger would ask in reverse: under what conditions does it halve? The answer is three triggers, any one of which could fire.

Li Lu takes the longest view: electrification, autonomous driving and embodied intelligence are all civilization-scale leaps, and Tesla has real assets in all three. But a correct trend does not equal correct shareholder returns—railroads, aviation and solar were all correct trends, and the long-term returns for the vast majority of participants were close to zero. The four disagree on "what it is worth" and agree on "it should not be bought now."

Duan Yongping: Do you understand this business?

Duan Yongping asks only three questions before buying a company: is this a good business, is the corporate culture right, and is the price cheap. Tesla passes the first two halfway: the car is a good car, Musk is an extreme productist, and the company really is doing "the hard and right thing." But it fails the third, because you cannot price a Robotaxi that has not yet landed.

He would use Apple as the comparison. Every dollar of Apple's profit can be traced in its financials: how many iPhones it sold, how much services revenue it booked, what its gross margin was. Of Tesla's USD 1.48 trillion market cap, USD 910 billion cannot be traced — not because the company is hiding it, but because that business does not yet exist. The test of "understandable" is not understanding the technology, but being able to put a number on the money.

Duan Yongping has another line that fits well here: "When you don't know what you don't know, you are most likely to lose money." Most people buying Tesla know what they are betting on, but few have calculated the odds. The neutral-case target price of USD 380 equals the current price — bet right and you make nothing, bet wrong and you lose 30%; this is not a bet he would take.

Buffett: This Time Neither the Moat nor the Margin of Safety Is on His Side

Buffett would first give the moat a high score: the Supercharger network is infrastructure, and the locations and grid capacity of 70,000 stalls cannot be replicated quickly with money; vertical integration keeps batteries, castings and software in-house, and the cost advantage is real; the brand is in a class of its own among EVs.But he would immediately add: moats in the auto industry have historically been short. Ford, GM and Toyota all once looked invincible.

The margin-of-safety point is more direct. Buffett bought BYD in 2008 at around 10x PE and Apple in 2016 at 13x. Today Tesla trades at close to 390x TTM net profit, and even on a neutral 2027 forecast it is above 300x.He cannot find a single traditional valuation anchor — it is not a little expensive, it is anchorless.

He might be interested in energy storage. Storage is a utility-style business: long-term contracts, predictable cash flow, obvious scale effects, and quarterly deployments of 13.5GWh are already substantial. But the storage segment's gross margin was only 20.4% this quarter and is still being dragged down by supplier problems.If Tesla ever spun storage out separately, that would be an asset he would be willing to look at.

Munger: Invert the question—under what circumstances would it halve?

Munger's approach is to invert the question: under what circumstances would Tesla halve? Three triggers. First, FSD faces tighter regulation or a ban in major markets — the US NHTSA's investigation into supervised FSD has never stopped, China only opened supervised FSD in May 2026, and any major accident would bring immediate tightening.

Second, another round of China price war turns per-vehicle gross margin negative.In the first half of 2026, BYD sold 1.809 million vehicles, down 15.7% year on year, and exported 789,000, up 70.5% — the leader is bleeding at home and seeking a way out overseas, a state that most easily triggers a new round of price cuts. Tesla's Shanghai plant delivered 213,000 vehicles in the first quarter, nearly 60% of global volume; when China sneezes, its gross margin catches cold.Third, governance events.Musk's attention, xAI's related-party transactions with Tesla, and the dilution from the USD 1 trillion pay package — if any one of these is magnified, the option premium the market grants will contract. If any of the three triggers occurs, the USD 910 billion option value gets cut in half first, and the remaining pure operating business supports less than USD 600 billion — the 60% downside is real, not alarmism.
Li Lu: In a civilization-scale trend, who captures the excess profit

Li Lu would take a longer view: electrification, autonomous driving and embodied intelligence are all civilization-scale efficiency leaps, and Tesla holds real assets in all three — beyond its 40,000-plus Superchargers, a 70,000-strong network, 200MW-class training compute, and a fleet of several million vehicles.This is a rare company with "three options existing at once."

But in Li Lu's framework, getting the trend right and getting shareholder returns right are two different things. Railways, aviation and solar were all correct trends, yet the long-term returns for the vast majority of participants were close to zero, because capital expenditure swallowed all the profit.Tesla's 25 billion USD of capex in 2026 and free cash flow of -1.09 billion USD are the early form of exactly this pattern.

He would ask one final question: who captures the excess profit at the endgame? On the vehicle side, cost competition among Chinese manufacturers has already squeezed gross margins to around 15%; on the autonomous driving side, Waymo has reached 500,000 rides per week across 10 cities, while Huawei has made smart driving a standard feature in China; on the robotics side, Optimus has yet to ship, and more than a dozen Chinese companies are already at work.The direction is right and the position is good, but the endgame share is uncertain — this is "you can read the trend, but not the winner."

Odds assessment: base case implies only 2% upside

The three scenarios are weighted 25%/50%/25%, giving an expected target price of USD 392.5, or +4.9% versus the current price. The 50% upside requires three conditions to hold simultaneously; the 33% downside requires only one. This is not a bet with favorable odds; it is an investment with unclear win probability and unfavorable payoff.

More notable is the market positioning. The stock has fallen 23.7% from its 52-week high of USD 489.88 and is down 17% year to date, yet valuation has barely improved—because earnings are falling too. This is the classic pattern of "earnings de-rating without valuation de-rating": the numerator falls and the denominator falls with it, so the more it drops, the more expensive it gets.

The real inflection point is not the share price but the financial statement structure. Only when FSD and Robotaxi revenue are first disclosed separately can the valuation method shift from "storytelling" to "multiple crunching." Until then, any rebound is sentiment, not value.

Disagreement note: our weighted valuation is lower than the working paper

The working paper assigns USD 380 under the base case, implying option value of about RMB 930 billion. We reweight by probability: 0 in the bear case, RMB 600 billion in the base case, RMB 1.6 trillion in the bull case, with weights of 25/50/25, giving RMB 700 billion, which together with RMB 570 billion for the core business totals RMB 1,270 billion, or USD 321 per share.

The USD 59 (15%) gap does not come from the core business but from the option: the working paper's base case implies that Robotaxi is worth RMB 930 billion once it starts charging on a small scale in 2027; we think that probability is only 50%. Waymo has run for eight years across 10 cities at 500,000 rides a week and still has not disclosed per-vehicle profitability, showing that this business pays off more slowly than the story suggests.

Approach: keep the working paper's USD 380 as the published target price (consistent with the rating and conclusion), but use USD 321 in internal odds calculations. Both point to the same action — do not buy at the current price. This disagreement does not change the conclusion, only the anchor for "fair value".

Conclusion

Conclusion: four people, four languages, one action — don't buy. Duan Yongping finds it impossible to model, Buffett finds it lacks a moat, Munger fears a halving, and Li Lu worries the winner won't be it. These four are not four independent doubts but four facets of the same thing: 61% of this company's value rests on assumptions about the future, and those assumptions cannot be verified. Rating maintained at "Watch, do not chase"; only a share price back below USD 300, or FSD revenue disclosed separately, would bring it into discussion range.
