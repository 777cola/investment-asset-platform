Earnings Read — Tesla

Tesla (TSLA) | 07 · Earnings Read
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)

Core call: record revenue, but two of the three profit tiers have collapsed

2026Q2 revenue was USD 28.236 billion, up 25.5% year on year, the fastest growth since 2023, with TTM revenue exceeding 100 billion for the first time. But operating profit was only 398 million, an operating margin of 1.4% (4.1% in the year-earlier period), net profit attributable to shareholders was 1.114 billion, down 5% year on year, and Non-GAAP net profit was 1.153 billion, down 17% year on year.

Breaking it down, the problem is not gross profit (16.8%, down only 0.4 points) but what sits below it: operating expenses were 4.353 billion, up 47% year on year, with AI, Optimus, Robotaxi and share-based compensation together consuming 92% of gross profit. Capital expenditure was 5.789 billion and free cash flow was -1.092 billion, the first quarterly cash burn since early 2024.

Tier 1 Revenue mix: vehicles still dominate, services are the surprise

Of the RMB 28.236 billion, automotive accounted for RMB 20.516 billion (72.7%), services RMB 4.581 billion (16.2%) and energy RMB 3.139 billion (11.1%). Automotive's share fell from 74% in Q2 2025 to 72.7% — not because cars sold poorly, but because services grew too fast.

Services rose 50.4% year on year to a record high, the only segment in this report with both volume and price gains. The company attributes this to higher supercharging usage, insurance and FSD subscription revenue. This business matters more strategically than financially: it is the only evidence that Tesla is shifting from selling hardware to collecting subscription fees. Unfortunately, the company discloses neither its gross margin nor the specific FSD amount.

Energy grew only 12.6% year on year, but deployments rose 41% — unit prices fell by a fifth. This is a direct read on the global energy-storage price war, with Chinese manufacturers quoting 20–30% below Megapack. Volume rising, prices falling, margins shrinking — the classic mid-life of a growth stock.

Second layer, gross profit and expenses: gross profit held, expenses collapsed

Gross profit was RMB 4.751 billion, gross margin 16.8%, down only 0.4 percentage points year on year. That is better than the market expected — the market had worried the record deliveries were bought with steep price cuts. The answer: the price cuts did happen, but falling per-vehicle manufacturing cost (4680 batteries, single-piece casting, supply-chain localisation) offset most of them.

The real problem is expenses. Operating expenses were RMB 4.353 billion, up 47% year on year, equal to 92% of gross profit (76% a year earlier). The company cites four reasons: AI R&D, spending on Optimus and Robotaxi, share-based compensation tied to the 2025 pay package, and G&A. Of these four, the first three will keep rising.

The result: operating profit fell from RMB 923 million to RMB 398 million, and operating margin from 4.1% to 1.4%. A company with RMB 28.2 billion of revenue earning just RMB 400 million from its core business — that ratio would be dangerous in any industry.

Tier 3 Profit and one-offs: half of the RMB 1.114 billion net profit is not from operations

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 1.114 billion, down only 5% year on year, looking far milder than the -57% in operating profit. The gap came from USD 590 million in other income—which includes fair value changes on bitcoin holdings and foreign exchange gains, having nothing to do with selling cars.

Non-GAAP net profit was RMB 1.153 billion, down 17% year on year—a more honest number: it excludes some one-off items but does not exclude bitcoin or FX. Non-GAAP EPS was USD 0.33 versus the consensus of USD 0.51–0.53, below the consensus by 35%—the direct cause of the 4% after-hours share price drop this quarter.

There is another hidden one: regulatory credits fell from RMB 439 million to RMB 146 million. This revenue carries zero cost and flows straight into gross profit; last year it contributed about 2 percentage points to gross margin, this year only 0.5.In other words, even if expenses do not rise, gross margin will fall 1.5 points because the credits disappear.

Tier 4 Cash flow and balance sheet: generated RMB 4.7 billion in operating cash, deployed RMB 5.8 billion

Operating cash flow of RMB 4.697 billion, up 85% year on year, was the healthiest number this quarter, driven mainly by delivery growth and inventory drawdown — production of 451,800 vehicles (45.18 万辆) against deliveries of 480,100 (48.01 万辆), a net drawdown of 28,400 vehicles (2.84 万辆) in inventory, releasing working capital.

Capex of RMB 5.789 billion, up 142% year on year, left free cash flow at negative RMB 1.092 billion.Management guides full-year capex above USD 25 billion (USD 8.5 billion in 2025) and has lined up borrowing capacity of up to USD 30 billion. With RMB 43.524 billion of cash on hand, there is no near-term solvency pressure, but the "net cash company" label will most likely have to go this year.

The balance sheet structure remains healthy: little interest-bearing debt, with liabilities mainly accounts payable and deferred revenue.That means it can absorb one to two years of negative free cash flow, but not three.Before 2028, Robotaxi or Optimus must start contributing cash flow, otherwise the company will need equity financing — and that is the real dilution.

One more easily overlooked detail:the cash contribution from inventory reduction was roughly USD 1.5–2 billion.Production of 451,800 vehicles (45.18 万辆) against deliveries of 480,100 (48.01 万辆), a net drawdown of 28,400 vehicles (2.84 万辆) — this destocking flattered operating cash flow considerably. If Q3 returns to the normal pattern of production slightly above deliveries, that cash contribution disappears,and operating cash flow could fall back to around USD 3 billion.

Difference from market expectations: revenue well above consensus, profit a big miss

Consensus revenue was USD 25.7–26.4 billion (depending on the institution), actual USD 28.236 billion, above the consensus by 7–10%; deliveries were expected at about 406,000 units, actual 480,100, above by 74,000 units. On any automaker, these two numbers would be a blowout win.

But consensus Non-GAAP EPS was USD 0.51–0.53, actual USD 0.33, a 35% miss; gross margin was expected at 19.3%, actual 16.8%, 2.5 percentage points short. The revenue beat and the profit miss arriving together show that "selling more" was bought with concessions.

The stock fell about 4% after hours, the market's immediate pricing of this combination: it will pay for growth, but it will not pay for "buying growth with profit." Whether this reaction extends beyond the report date depends on whether management can give a timetable for "costs peaking" on the call—and as of this writing, the company has not.

The three things to watch most closely
1. Can automotive gross margin hold 16%. Q2 was 16.9% (automotive basis), of which regulatory credit contributed 0.7 points. With credits at zero, the true gross margin is around 16.2%. One more point of decline pushes it into the psychological zone of "losing money on every car sold," at which point all room for price cuts disappears. 2. Growth in operating expenses. Q2 was +47% year on year, with AI and Optimus investment rigid and share-based compensation moving with the stock price. If the Q3 expense ratio stays above 15% (expenses/revenue), there is no prospect of improvement in the income statement, and full-year Non-GAAP net income will fall below USD 3 billion. 3. Matching capex with financing. USD 25 billion for the full year, free cash flow of -1.09 billion, and a USD 30 billion borrowing facility. Put these three numbers together and a bond issue or equity placement before 2027 is highly likely. A bond issue is mild; an equity placement is brutal—the latter directly changes per-share value.

Conclusion

Conclusion: this is a three-in-one earnings report — growth-stock revenue, manufacturing-sector profit, startup cash flow. Revenue growth of 26% proves demand is still there; a gross margin of 16.8% proves the price war is containable; but an operating margin of 1.4% and free cash flow of -RMB 1.09 billion prove it is trading profit for the future. Implication for investors: the story is still advancing, but the bill for that advance is starting to arrive at shareholders' door. The thing to watch is not revenue but whether Q3 auto gross margin holds 16%, and the financing arrangements behind full-year capex of RMB 25 billion.
