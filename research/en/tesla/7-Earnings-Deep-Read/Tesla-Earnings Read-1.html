Earnings Read — Tesla

Tesla (TSLA) | Earnings Read

Core call: record revenue, but two of the three profit tiers have collapsed

2026Q2 revenue came in at USD 28.236 billion, up 25.5% YoY — the fastest growth since 2023 — with TTM revenue topping 100 billion for the first time. Operating profit, however, was just 398 million, a 1.4% operating margin (4.1% a year earlier); net profit attributable to shareholders fell 5% YoY to 1.114 billion and Non-GAAP net profit fell 17% YoY to 1.153 billion.

The pressure sits below the gross line rather than in it: gross margin was 16.8%, down only 0.4pp, but operating expenses rose 47% YoY to 4.353 billion, with AI, Optimus, Robotaxi and share-based compensation together absorbing 92% of gross profit. Capex was 5.789 billion and free cash flow -1.092 billion — the first quarterly cash burn since early 2024.

Tier 1 revenue mix: vehicles still dominate, services surprise

Automotive contributed RMB 20.516 billion of the RMB 28.236 billion total (72.7%), services RMB 4.581 billion (16.2%) and energy RMB 3.139 billion (11.1%). Automotive's share slipped from 74% in Q2 2025 to 72.7% — not on weak car sales, but because services grew so fast.

Services rose 50.4% YoY to a record high, the only segment in this report posting gains in both volume and price. Management attributes this to higher supercharging usage and to insurance and FSD subscription revenue. Strategically this business matters more than it does financially: it is the only evidence that Tesla is shifting from selling hardware to collecting subscription fees. The company discloses neither its gross margin nor the specific FSD amount.

Energy grew just 12.6% YoY, yet deployments rose 41% — unit prices fell by a fifth. That is a direct read on the global energy-storage price war, with Chinese manufacturers quoting 20–30% below Megapack. Volume up, prices down, margins compressing — the classic mid-life of a growth stock.

Second layer, gross profit and expenses: gross profit held while expenses collapsed

Gross profit was RMB 4.751 billion at a 16.8% gross margin, down just 0.4pp YoY — better than the market feared, given concerns that record deliveries had been bought with steep price cuts. The answer: the cuts were real, but falling per-vehicle manufacturing cost (4680 batteries, single-piece casting, supply-chain localisation) offset most of them.

The real problem is expenses. Operating expenses reached RMB 4.353 billion, up 47% YoY and equal to 92% of gross profit (76% a year earlier). Management cites four drivers: AI R&D, spending on Optimus and Robotaxi, share-based compensation tied to the 2025 pay package, and G&A. Of these, the first three will keep rising.

As a result, operating profit fell from RMB 923 million to RMB 398 million and operating margin from 4.1% to 1.4%. RMB 28.2 billion of revenue generating just RMB 400 million from the core business — a ratio that would be dangerous in any industry.

Tier 3 profit and one-offs: half of the RMB 1.114 billion net profit is non-operating

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 1.114 billion, down just 5% YoY—far milder than the -57% decline in operating profit. The gap reflects USD 590 million of other income, including fair value changes on bitcoin holdings and FX gains, neither of which has anything to do with selling cars.

Non-GAAP net profit was RMB 1.153 billion, down 17% YoY—a more honest number: it strips out some one-off items but not bitcoin or FX. Non-GAAP EPS came in at USD 0.33 against consensus of USD 0.51–0.53, a 35% miss that directly triggered the 4% after-hours share price drop this quarter.

There is another hidden drag: regulatory credits fell from RMB 439 million to RMB 146 million. This revenue carries zero cost and flows straight into gross profit; last year it contributed about 2pp to gross margin, this year only 0.5pp. In other words, even with expenses flat, gross margin falls 1.5pp simply because the credits disappear.

Tier 4 Cash flow and balance sheet: operating cash generation of RMB 4.7 billion, capex deployment of RMB 5.8 billion

Operating cash flow of RMB 4.697 billion, up 85% YoY, was the healthiest number this quarter, driven mainly by delivery growth and inventory drawdown: production of 451,800 vehicles against deliveries of 480,100, a net drawdown of 28,400 vehicles that released working capital.

Capex of RMB 5.789 billion, up 142% YoY, left free cash flow at negative RMB 1.092 billion.Management guides full-year capex above USD 25 billion (USD 8.5 billion in 2025) and has lined up borrowing capacity of up to USD 30 billion. With RMB 43.524 billion of cash on hand, there is no near-term solvency pressure, but the "net cash company" label will most likely have to go this year.

The balance sheet structure remains healthy: little interest-bearing debt, with liabilities mainly accounts payable and deferred revenue.That means it can absorb one to two years of negative free cash flow, but not three.Before 2028, Robotaxi or Optimus must start contributing cash flow, otherwise the company will need equity financing — and that is the real dilution.

One more easily overlooked detail:the cash contribution from inventory reduction was roughly USD 1.5–2 billion.Production of 451,800 vehicles against deliveries of 480,100, a net drawdown of 28,400 vehicles — this destocking flattered operating cash flow considerably. If Q3 returns to the normal pattern of production slightly above deliveries, that cash contribution disappears,and operating cash flow could fall back to around USD 3 billion.

Revenue well above consensus, profit a big miss versus market expectations

Consensus revenue was USD 25.7–26.4 billion depending on the institution; actual USD 28.236 billion, a 7–10% beat. Deliveries were expected at about 406,000 units against 480,100 actual, a 74,000-unit beat. For any automaker, those two numbers would be a blowout.

But consensus Non-GAAP EPS was USD 0.51–0.53 against USD 0.33 actual, a 35% miss, and gross margin came in at 16.8% versus 19.3% expected, 2.5 percentage points short. The revenue beat and the profit miss arriving together show that "selling more" was bought with concessions.

The stock fell about 4% after hours as the market priced that combination on the spot: it will pay for growth, but not for "buying growth with profit." Whether the reaction extends beyond the report date depends on whether management can give a timetable for "costs peaking" on the call—and as of this writing, it has not.

The three things to watch most closely
1. Can automotive gross margin hold 16%. Q2 came in at 16.9% on an automotive basis, 0.7 points of which was regulatory credit. Strip credits to zero and the underlying margin is closer to 16.2%; one more point of decline takes it into the psychological "loss on every car sold" zone, at which point all room for price cuts disappears. 2. Opex growth. Q2 opex rose 47% YoY, with AI and Optimus spend rigid and share-based compensation tracking the stock price. If the Q3 expense ratio (opex/revenue) stays above 15%, there is no prospect of income statement improvement and full-year Non-GAAP net income falls below USD 3 billion. 3. Funding the capex. USD 25 billion for the full year, free cash flow of -1.09 billion and a USD 30 billion borrowing facility. Put those three numbers together and a bond issue or equity placement before 2027 looks highly likely. A bond issue is mild; an equity placement is brutal—the latter directly changes per-share value.

Conclusion

Conclusion: this is a three-in-one earnings report — growth-stock revenue, manufacturing-sector profit, startup cash flow. Revenue growth of 26% proves demand is still there, a gross margin of 16.8% proves the price war is containable, but an operating margin of 1.4% and free cash flow of -RMB 1.09 billion prove the company is trading profit for the future. Implication for investors: the story is still advancing, but the bill for that advance is starting to arrive at shareholders' door. The thing to watch is not revenue but whether Q3 auto gross margin holds 16%, and the financing arrangements behind full-year capex of RMB 25 billion.
