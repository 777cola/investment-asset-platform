Six-Gate Screen — Tesla

Tesla (TSLA) | Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)

Core call: good company, bad price, and the story is pricing the cash flow

Six gates run, 33 points. The first three pass: the business is understandable, the moat is real, the brand is in a class of its own. It stalls at the fifth gate—of USD 374, USD 230 buys a future that has not happened yet. The auto business—1.9 million units in 2027, USD 950 net profit per vehicle at 25x P/E—is worth USD 450 billion; energy storage USD 80 billion; net cash USD 40 billion. The remaining USD 910 billion rests entirely on options on Robotaxi, Optimus and FSD.

Second-quarter deliveries of 480,126 units and revenue of USD 28.24 billion were both record highs, yet the operating margin was only 1.4% and free cash flow -USD 1.09 billion. Growth is bought with spending; profit is eaten away by AI investment and the collapse in regulatory credits. This is not a company that can be pinned down with a DCF, nor one that can protect itself with a margin of safety.

Gate 1 Circle of Competence: 8/10 — the car is quantifiable, capacity is not

Tesla's revenue comes from four pockets: car sales USD 20.52 billion, energy storage USD 3.14 billion, services USD 4.58 billion and regulatory credits USD 150 million. The first three break down cleanly: 480,126 vehicles at USD 20.52 billion, or USD 42,700 per vehicle; 13.5GWh of storage at USD 3.14 billion, about USD 23 million per GWh. There is no black box at this layer.

The deduction comes at the fourth layer: Robotaxi unit economics. That requires cost per mile, fleet utilisation, the operating radius regulators allow and insurance costs. To date the company discloses only "coverage of seven major US metropolitan areas", with no volume or average daily revenue per vehicle. You can understand how it builds cars; you cannot understand how it makes money from capacity.

Duan Yongping's test is "will this business still exist in ten years, and will it still be in it." The car will most likely still exist; Tesla may not be first. The capacity business will certainly still exist in ten years, but the winner may be Waymo. So 8 points, not full marks.

Stage 2 Good business: 6/10, the money machine is thinning

Autos is a heavy-asset business. 2026Q2 gross margin came in at 16.8% (16.9% for autos), down 0.4pp from 2025Q2; more tellingly, operating margin fell from 4.1% to 1.4%, with net margin at 3.9%.Selling more, earning less — the textbook read on a price war.

Capex exposes the true quality of the business: USD 5.79 billion in the quarter, +142% YoY, with full-year guidance above USD 25 billion, nearly triple 2025's full-year USD 8.5 billion. Operating cash flow of USD 4.70 billion against USD 5.79 billion invested left free cash flow at -USD 1.09 billion —the first quarterly cash burn since early 2024.

Energy storage was meant to be the second leg: 13.5GWh, +41% YoY, but segment gross margin fell from 39.5% to 20.4% on a USD 240 million battery supplier warranty provision and the loss of tariff benefits. The mark of a good business is pricing power without volume loss; Tesla cut prices in every major market in 2026.

Gate three, moat: 8/10 — four genuine moats, two of them thinning

Brand: the default EV choice in North America, but BYD and Xiaomi are eroding its China share, with 2025 China sales of 626,000 units, down 4.8% YoY.Supercharging network: about 70,000 connectors globally; opening it to other brands turned an exclusive asset into fee-charging infrastructure—steadier cash flow, a shallower moat.

Vertical integration is real: more than 40GWh of 4680 cells in Texas, 7GWh of LFP in Nevada, 10GWh of cathode, 30GWh of lithium refining, and per-vehicle manufacturing costs a notch below traditional automakers. The FSD data loop is real too: fleet size plus combined Cortex 1/2 training compute of over 200MW, a scale no other automaker globally possesses.

But the test of a moat is whether you can charge full price. FSD at USD 8,000 found no buyers; only the USD 99/month subscription moved it. Robotaxi is running in Austin, Dallas and Houston, while Waymo hit 500,000 rides per week across 10 cities over the same period.The moat is still there; the definition of its width has changed.

Level 4 Management: 6/10 — capable, but attention is elsewhere

Musk's product judgement and engineering ability are beyond doubt: the Model 3/Y platform, single-piece die-casting, 4680 cells, end-to-end autonomous driving — every step has put him two to three years ahead of peers. On 6 November 2025, shareholders approved his ten-year compensation plan with over 75% support and a maximum payout of close to USD 1 trillion, tied to market cap and operating milestones — the plan forcibly aligns management and shareholder interests for ten years.

Two marks against him. First, attention: xAI, SpaceX and politics take turns occupying his time; on the 2026Q2 earnings call he discussed the Cybercab line, Optimus volume production and a self-built wafer fab, and spent only ten minutes on the auto business. Second, capital allocation: tearing out the Model S/X line at Fremont to build the first-generation Optimus volume line swaps certain cash flow for an uncertain option.

From a shareholder's perspective, neither move is necessarily wrong — if Optimus succeeds, today's choice will be written up as foresight. But both are decisions where winning is called brilliance and losing cannot be pinned on anyone, and neither fits a margin-of-safety framework for valuation.

Gate 5 Margin of safety: 2/10; USD 230 of the USD 374 is option value

Sum-of-the-parts: the auto business, at 1.9 million vehicles in 2027 and USD 950 net profit per vehicle, is worth USD 1.81 billion; at 25x that is USD 450 billion. Energy storage, on USD 12 billion of 2026 revenue at a 20% net margin and 30x, adds USD 80 billion, and net cash is USD 40 billion. The three together total USD 570 billion, or USD 144 per share.

A USD 1.48 trillion market cap less that USD 570 billion leaves USD 910 billion — 61% of the total, or USD 230 per share. That USD 230 buys three things: Robotaxi at ten times Waymo's scale by 2030, Optimus at a million units a year, and FSD penetration above half. If all three land, USD 230 is not expensive; if one fails, it goes to zero.

Margin of safety means "you don't lose big even if you're wrong." Here the situation is reversed: you avoid a loss only if you're right, and if you're wrong the stock halves. Hence 2 points, not 3 and not 1 — the auto business is valuable in its own right, it just cannot support this price.

Level six Discipline: 3/10, the buy case is a timetable

The bull case is remarkably uniform: Cybercab enters production in Texas in 2026, with employee pilot operations from July; Robotaxi expands from 3 cities to 7 metro areas; and the first-generation Optimus mass-production line breaks ground at Fremont, with output starting at year-end. Every one of these milestones is "later this year," and not one comes with a revenue figure.

That is not to say the company is lying. Tesla has historically turned many "impossibles" into reality—the Shanghai plant went from groundbreaking to production in 10 months, and the company climbed out of Model 3 production hell. The problem is that investor discipline cannot rest on "it has done it before"; it has to rest on "what has it done this time."

The verifiable metrics are in fact clear: whether FSD revenue is broken out separately, whether Robotaxi removes safety drivers and discloses trip volumes, and whether energy-storage gross margin returns to 25%. None of the three has appeared, so the position should be zero.

Veto items and items to be verified
Two veto items: margin of safety and discipline. The first is a price issue—an operating business worth RMB 570 billion against a market cap of RMB 1.48 trillion. The second is a method issue—using businesses that have yet to materialise to justify the current price. Both point to the same action: do not buy. Four items to verify:
  • The number of cities where Robotaxi operates without safety drivers, and weekly trips. The earnings report cites only "seven major metropolitan areas," while Waymo over the same period covers 10 cities at 50.0 thousand trips per week—that gap is the anchor for valuation.
  • Whether FSD revenue is separately disclosed in the financial statements. As long as it stays buried in the RMB 4.58 billion of "services and other," the value of the option cannot be verified.
  • Whether the energy storage segment's gross margin can return above 25%. The 20.4% includes a one-time warranty provision, but also a permanent factor: the disappearance of tariff benefits.
  • Financing arrangements under USD 25 billion of capital expenditure. The company has already lined up a borrowing facility of up to USD 30 billion—a shift from self-funding to external financing, and a signal that the valuation framework is changing.

The first item determines "whether the story starts to deliver"; the fourth determines "whether problems will arise before delivery."

Conclusion

Conclusion: Watch, do not chase the rally. 33/60, four gates passed, two vetoed. Fundamentals are sound: record deliveries, energy storage +41%, FSD subscriptions growing, and the cost advantage of vertical integration intact. The issue is entirely price. Options account for 61% of the USD 1.48 trillion market cap, yet they still have no separate revenue line in the 2026 interim report. Two triggers; revisit once either is met: the stock falls below USD 300 (options share drops to 55%), or FSD/Robotaxi revenue is disclosed as a separate line item with annualized revenue above USD 5 billion.
