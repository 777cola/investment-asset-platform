Core call: the numbers are credible; the only thing to watch is that "gross margin" has three definitions

Eight key data points were cross-checked against two sources; seven deviations were within 1%. The largest was 2025Q2 revenue (company: USD 22,496 million vs. 22,396 back-calculated from the working papers, a 0.45% deviation), caused by the working papers back-calculating from year-on-year growth and rounding. All figures use the company's disclosed original values.

What genuinely needs clarifying is gross margin. The working papers state 16.9%, while the company discloses "total gross margin 16.8%, automotive gross margin 16.9%"—both figures are correct, just on different bases. This report standardizes: total gross margin uses 16.8%, automotive gross margin uses 16.9%, and any appearance of 16.9% must be noted as the automotive basis. In a year of thin profits, such details directly affect conclusions and cannot be left ambiguous.

Two deviations need explanation; the other six can be considered fully in line

Item 1: 2025Q2 revenue. The 22,396 in the working paper was back-solved by dividing 2026Q2 revenue by (1+26.1%); the company's original figure is 22,496. The 0.45% difference comes from rounding in the growth rate itself—the company reported +25.5%, while the working paper used the media figure of +26.1%. This item does not affect any conclusion, but it shows that "back-solved values" and "disclosed values" must be labeled separately.

Item 2: gross margin. This is the only item that looks contradictory but is not. Total company gross margin was 16.8% (gross profit RMB 4.751 billion ÷ revenue RMB 28.236 billion = 16.83%), and automotive gross margin was 16.9%. Automotive gross margin exceeds total gross margin because the energy storage segment was dragged down to 20.4% this quarter by a USD 240 million warranty provision, while services gross margin, though improving, remains below automotive.

How to handle it: for any discussion of "overall profitability," use 16.8%; for any discussion of "whether selling cars makes money," use 16.9%. The 16.9% in the working paper falls into the latter category—not wrong, just labeled too broadly.

Why gross margin is easy to get wrong this quarter: three one-off factors overlap

The first is regulatory credits. The USD 146 million in credit revenue carries almost zero cost and flows straight into gross profit, equivalent to a 0.5 percentage point contribution to total gross margin. The year-earlier figure was USD 439 million, contributing about 2 percentage points. For the same metric, the 1.5 percentage point gap between the two years stems from policy, not operations.

The second is energy storage warranties. A USD 240 million provision hit the energy segment's cost, compressing segment gross margin from 39.5% to 20.4%. The charge is a legacy liability from supplier cell issues and is one-off in nature, but it genuinely occurred and cannot be excluded.

The third is tariffs. The 21.1% gross margin in 2026Q1 included a favorable reversal of tariff costs from the prior quarter; Q2 had no such benefit, which amplified the sequential decline. Together, the three factors explain most of the 4.3 percentage point drop from 21.1% to 16.8%. To read this company's gross margin, at minimum split it into two lines—excluding credits and excluding one-offs—or every quarter will come as a shock.

Final adopted basis and calculation rules
For quarterly data, always use the company's reported figures (USD million); for year-on-year changes, use the percentages disclosed by the company; TTM is calculated independently. TTM revenue of USD 103.619 billion = 2025Q3 28.095 + 2025Q4 24.901 + 2026Q1 22.387 + 2026Q2 28.236; TTM net profit attributable to shareholders of USD 3.804 billion = 13.73 + 8.40 + 4.77 + 11.14. Based on a market cap of USD 1.48 trillion, PE(TTM) is about 389x — this figure serves only as an order-of-magnitude reference, because the denominator is too thin and any one-off item can move it by 20%.

A note on the market cap basis: the USD 1.48 trillion is the total market cap back-solved from the USD 374.01 closing price and about 3.96 billion shares, including unexercised equity incentives. The share count back-solved from diluted EPS of USD 0.32 is about 3.48 billion shares, a 12% difference between the two — this is the difference between the "market cap basis" and the "EPS basis"; all valuations in this report use the market cap basis to avoid per-share figures conflicting with one another.

All derived figures are labeled with their source method: revenue per vehicle of USD 42,700 = 20.516 billion ÷ 480,100 vehicles; company-wide net profit per vehicle of USD 2,320 = 1.114 billion ÷ 480,100 vehicles; automotive business net profit per vehicle of USD 950 is a rough calculation after excluding contributions from energy storage and services, used for valuation assumptions and not a disclosed figure.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the data is usable, but the basis of presentation must be stated clearly. Seven of the eight checks show deviations below 1%; the only one exceeding that is the back-solved value in the working papers (0.45%), which has been replaced throughout the main text with the company's original figures. The three gross margin bases (total, automotive, and excluding one-offs) are used separately, and this is the only presentational difference between this report and the working papers; it does not affect the 33/60 score or the "Watch, do not chase the rally" rating.
