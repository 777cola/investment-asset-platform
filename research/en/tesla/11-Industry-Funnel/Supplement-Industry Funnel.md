## How the funnel screens: three filter layers, each stricter than the last

The first layer is business attributes: the company must sit in the "EV + autonomous driving" chain, which rules out pure battery, pure chip and pure mobility-platform names. Of the six, Waymo is stuck at "cannot buy it," and the other five advance to the second layer.

The second layer is elimination metrics: gross margin, growth direction and earnings quality. Li Auto is out on a 7.9% gross margin, BYD is out as growth turns negative, Xiaomi is out because its auto business share is too small, and Toyota is out for lagging in electrification. Five leave one.

The third layer is endgame position: who can hold manufacturing, data, energy and robotics at the same time. Only Tesla clears this layer, because it has all four. But beyond the third layer sits another we added ourselves—the price layer. Three layers screen out good companies; the fourth decides whether to buy. On this layer, it fails.

## Rough screening: why four companies were eliminated first

BYD: H1 2026 sales of 1.809 million units, -15.7% YoY, the steepest decline among Chinese automakers that have disclosed figures; exports of 789,000 units, +70.5% YoY, propped up the total. Its cost advantage remains, but the growth story has shifted from domestic to overseas, and the valuation must be recalculated as a cyclical stock. Xiaomi: Group P/E 17.5x, market cap HKD 700 billion; the auto business is scaling, but its share is still small, with profit coming mainly from phones and IoT. Buying it means buying a "phone company + auto option" combination, not a pure autonomous-driving play. Li Auto: 2026Q1 gross margin 7.9%, net loss RMB 2.3 billion, product mix in transition (new L9/L8 delivered in May and June). An automaker whose gross margin falls below 8% lacks the fundamentals of a "first-tier company" and is out. Toyota: Global annual sales of 10 million units, 20% gross margin, P/E 10x — a model student of manufacturing. But its electrification transition is slow, autonomous driving lags, and software capability is weak; buying it means buying "cash flow + dividends," not "autonomous driving." Waymo: Ahead in both technology and operations, but unlisted, so ordinary investors cannot buy it. The only way to hold it indirectly is to buy Alphabet, and Waymo accounts for less than 5% of Alphabet's market cap.

## Final pick: why Tesla

Of the six, only Tesla has all four: manufacturing scale (838,000 vehicles in six months), autonomous-driving data (a fleet of several million cars plus 200MW-class training compute), an energy-storage network (13.5GWh per quarter, capacity across three locations) and a robotics option (the first-generation Optimus mass-production line under construction).

That four-way combination is unique. BYD has manufacturing but no software; Waymo has software but no manufacturing; Xiaomi has an ecosystem but no transport capacity; Toyota has cash flow but no electrification. If you could bet on only one company for the endgame of "EV + autonomous driving + robotics," the answer is Tesla, with no second option.

But the last cell of the funnel must be honest: "Why this company" answers "which company is best," not "which stock can be bought." The former is about competitiveness, the latter about price. We pick this company; we do not buy this stock.

## Why not buy now: three numbers say it all

First number: 389x. A P/E (TTM) of about 389x — 13x BYD and 39x Toyota. This is not "a little expensive"; it is pricing the stock on the valuation framework of an entirely different species. Second number: -USD 1.09 billion. Free cash flow of -USD 1.092 billion, negative for the first time in two years, with full-year capex above USD 25 billion. The company is trading cash for the future — and that cash belongs to shareholders. Third number: 61%. Of the USD 1.48 trillion market cap, 61% is options, equivalent to USD 230 per share. That ratio means a 30% drop only halves the option value; the operating business does not change at all.Put the three numbers together and the conclusion is clear: right company, wrong price. First on the watchlist, last on the buy list.
