## Why is the current price 0 rather than "buy a little and watch"

Many people habitually "buy 1% first to watch it," but a 1% position does not help research and hurts the portfolio. **Its function is to make you pick a side psychologically, after which every piece of analysis unconsciously finds reasons for the holding.**

More important is the payoff: base-case target price USD 380, 2% upside; bear case USD 250, 33% downside. **A 1% position at these odds has an expected contribution of -0.3%, but a high emotional cost.** Not buying instead preserves independence of judgment.

Real observation needs no position. Check deliveries monthly, earnings quarterly, track the number of Robotaxi cities — none of this requires holding the stock. **A position expresses conviction, not interest.**

## Specific price levels for adding to positions, trimming positions and stop-losses

**Add position:** Build the initial position only below USD 300, in two tranches—buy half at USD 300 and the other half at USD 280. **Do not chase rebounds, and do not add on news that "FSD has opened another city."** The only case for breaking the price condition is if FSD revenue is reported separately and annualizes above USD 5 billion. **Trim:** If already holding with a cost above USD 400, trim to below 1% on a rebound above USD 420. **Stop-loss:** There is no stop-loss for non-holders; for holders, halve the position on a break below USD 320 (corresponding to a market cap of USD 1.27 trillion), and exit fully on a break below USD 280. **The stop-loss is not about price, it is about logic—USD 320 corresponds to "options at 50% of the cap table," and USD 280 corresponds to "the market starting to price the business on fundamentals."** **Veto:** If Q3 auto gross margin falls below 16% and FCF is negative for a second consecutive quarter, do not build a position at any price. **That would mean the business model is deteriorating faster than the valuation is being marked down.**

## Correlation: not a diversification tool, but a leverage tool

Tesla's correlation with the Nasdaq 100 has long held above 0.6, and its correlation with the AI compute chain (Nvidia, AMD, TSMC) rose above 0.5 in 2026—**because its valuation logic has shifted to an "AI option" rather than "car sales."**

It is negatively correlated with bonds and negatively correlated with the dollar (high overseas revenue share, FX moves affect profit). **This means: if your portfolio already holds more than 20% tech growth stocks, adding Tesla will not diversify risk—it will only amplify drawdowns.**

Alternatives: **for autonomous-driving exposure, buy Alphabet (Waymo sits inside it); for EV-cycle exposure, buy CATL (strongest bargaining power in the battery segment); for AI compute exposure, buy Nvidia.** All three can partially replace one layer of Tesla's logic, and all have valuation anchors.

## Conflict check against other holdings

**Versus BYD/CATL:** Negatively correlated on price wars—when Tesla cuts prices, BYD's gross margin comes under pressure; the two should not be held as heavy positions simultaneously, otherwise you are betting on two directions of the same supply chain. **Versus Nvidia:** Positively correlated on the AI narrative. If Nvidia already accounts for 8% of the portfolio, adding Tesla at 3% means putting 11% on the single bet that "AI continues to beat expectations." **Recommendation: combined weight no more than 10%.** **Versus cash:** Tesla is a "long-duration asset"; its valuation compresses hardest when rates rise. **In a hiking cycle, its decline will exceed the Nasdaq's.** This is also why it should not be bought now—the rate path for the second half of 2026 remains uncertain.
