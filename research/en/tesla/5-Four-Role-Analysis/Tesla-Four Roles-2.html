Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Tesla

What lies beneath the income statement: half of net profit does not come from selling cars
Research date 2026/07/22  ·  Price $374.01 (-1.30%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 1.48 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) — (Margins too thin; PE no longer meaningful)

Core view

Operating profit was RMB 398 million and net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 1.114 billion, with the RMB 716 million gap coming from other income and tax items. Half of this quarter's net profit was not earned from operations — the single most important takeaway from the filings.
Income statement: from gross profit to net profit, two of the three steps have collapsed

Gross profit of RMB 4.751 billion, gross margin 16.8%, down only 0.4 percentage points year on year — looks acceptable. But the two steps below it collapsed hard: operating expenses of RMB 4.353 billion, up 47% year on year, consumed 92% of gross profit; operating profit fell to RMB 398 million, an operating margin of 1.4% versus 4.1% a year earlier.

Why did operating expenses rise 47%? The company gave four reasons: AI R&D; personnel and compute investment in Optimus and Robotaxi; equity incentives tied to the 2025 CEO compensation plan; and general and administrative expenses. The first three are not one-off items and will climb alongside capital expenditure.

Net profit of RMB 1.114 billion fell only 5% thanks to USD 590 million of other income — including fair value changes on bitcoin holdings and foreign exchange gains. This is money unrelated to operations, random in direction and unpredictable. Non-GAAP net profit of RMB 1.153 billion, down 17% year on year, better reflects the real trend than GAAP: selling more cars, earning less money.

Cash flow: generated RMB 4.7 billion, deployed RMB 5.8 billion

Operating cash flow of RMB 4.697 billion, up 85% year on year, is the prettiest number this quarter, driven mainly by higher deliveries and lower inventory — output of 451,800 vehicles against deliveries of 480,100 absorbed 28,400 units of inventory and released working capital.

But capex of RMB 5.789 billion, up 142% year on year, pushed free cash flow to negative RMB 1.092 billion.This is the first quarterly cash burn since early 2024. The company also guided full-year capex above USD 25 billion (USD 8.5 billion for full-year 2025) and arranged a borrowing facility of up to USD 30 billion.

Buffett's reaction to this passage would be blunt: a company that must keep raising capital to sustain growth is not growing for shareholders.The RMB 43.5 billion of cash on the books can last two years, but after that either free cash flow turns positive or the equity gets diluted.

Balance sheet and returns: the net cash is real, the ROIC is not

The balance sheet carries no obvious risk: cash and short-term investments of RMB 43.524 billion, a small debt load, liabilities mainly accounts payable and deferred revenue, and a debt-to-asset ratio of about 40%.This is Tesla's hardest advantage over China's new EV makers — it does not need financing to survive.

But return on capital looks ugly. Annualized operating profit of about USD 1.6 billion against invested capital (equity of about 73 billion plus interest-bearing debt) in the 75 billion range, ROIC of about 2%, far below the cost of capital. In other words, every dollar the company invests creates less value than the interest it costs — the value creation comes from valuation, not operations.

That is also why it must tell a new story: when ROIC sits below the cost of capital for a long time, the only way out is to put capital into new businesses with higher returns. AI compute, Robotaxi and Optimus are all products of that logic. The problem is that the returns on those new businesses are all negative today.

Three accounting details worth watching separately
1. Warranty accrual. The energy segment booked USD 240 million in battery supplier warranty accruals this quarter, compressing segment gross margin from 39.5% to 20.4%. One-off or recurring? If it shows up again next quarter, it is a systemic problem. 2. Equity compensation. SBC tied to the compensation plan is booked in operating expenses and rises as the share price rises — the higher the stock, the higher the expense, the lower the profit. This is a reflexive accounting structure that few notice. 3. Regulatory credits. USD 146 million, versus USD 439 million a year earlier. This revenue carries zero cost and flows straight into gross profit, an invisible subsidy to gross margin over the past two years. With it at zero, Tesla's "true gross margin" is half a point lower than what the statements show.

Conclusion

Financial assessment: The income statement is deteriorating, cash flow is bleeding, and the balance sheet remains healthy. In three phrases: selling more, earning less, investing harder. By Buffett's standard, this is not a company that can be valued on "owner earnings" — its free cash flow is negative and capex is rigid. On rating, this offers no case to buy; watch only.
