Core view

Li Lu would read this earnings report through a "capital-intensive industry" lens. Revenue RMB 3.661 billion, capex RMB 9.441 billion (cash basis), free cash flow negative RMB 7.466 billion — every 1 yuan of revenue requires 2.58 yuan of equipment investment. That is not an operational misstep but an industry trait: compute is an asset-heavy business, and asset-heavy cash flow always lags the income statement.

Railways, aviation, panels: the same playbook

Every capital-intensive industry in history has followed the same path: demand explodes, heavy borrowing funds capacity expansion, overcapacity sets in, a price war breaks out, weaker players are washed out, and only the surviving oligopolists gain pricing power. Panels took 15 years, aviation has yet to complete the cycle after 30 years, and cloud computing took 10 years.

CoreWeave now sits in the "middle of capacity expansion," not yet at the washout stage. The biggest danger at this stage is not demand but whether its balance sheet can hold out until the washout day—RMB 13.4 billion matures in 2027, while the industry washout could take 3–5 years.

Who profits from this capex

Break down where this RMB 9.441 billion of capex went: the vast majority went to Nvidia (GPUs), to power equipment suppliers (transformers, gas turbines) and to engineering contractors. Gross margins in these segments run 30%–70%, against a GAAP operating margin of 5.7% at CoreWeave.

Li Lu's conclusion would be clear: in an industry expanding capex at high speed, the shovel sellers make money while the shovel buyers front the cash. Read the same set of financials and Nvidia sees demand, CoreWeave sees cost. To make money in the AI trend, stand on the side collecting the cash.

A ten-year view of free cash flow

Assume everything goes right: 2027 revenue of RMB 25 billion, EBITDA of RMB 13 billion, capex down to 50% of revenue (RMB 12.5 billion) and free cash flow of about RMB 500 million — cumulative capex of over USD 100 billion over ten years, in exchange for free cash flow in the hundreds of millions. Such is the fate of capital-intensive industries: the larger the scale, the more it resembles a utility.

In Li Lu's framework, such companies are worth buying only when three conditions are met simultaneously: the capex cycle ends, an oligopoly takes shape and debt is cleared out. None of the three holds now. It is not a bad company; it is the wrong timing plus the wrong capital structure.

Conclusion

A Li Lu-style conclusion: the industry is worth owning; this company is not. AI compute is civilisation-level infrastructure and demand visibility is exceptionally high. But in capital-intensive industries the excess profit is usually consumed by capex itself, and what survives accrues to the oligopolists. CoreWeave today is mid-expansion, highly levered and without pricing power — three conditions that together amplify industry risk and sell it on to shareholders. The way to participate is upstream (Nvidia, HBM) or at the application end with real cash flow — not through the channel player carrying the heaviest leverage.
