Core view

Buffett would spend ten minutes on the income statement and the rest on the balance sheet. Total debt RMB 46.873 billion at an average cost of 7.9%, of which RMB 41.6 billion is with recourse and RMB 13.4 billion matures within two years; cash stands at RMB 5.5 billion. Then he would look at interest: RMB 846 million, 2.2x adjusted operating profit. After that, the RMB 132.5 billion order book no longer matters.

Interest coverage ratio: what does 0.24x mean?

GAAP operating profit of RMB 207 million ÷ net interest of RMB 846 million = 0.24x. Berkshire typically requires 5x or more from its subsidiaries. 0.24x does not mean "a bit tight" — it means current-period operating profit cannot even cover a quarter of interest. What fills the RMB 639 million gap? New borrowings and customer prepayments.

Q4 guidance pushes interest to RMB 1.05–1.15 billion, while adjusted operating profit guidance does not double in tandem. This means that even if Q4 operating profit reaches RMB 500 million, interest is still more than twice that. Buffett would call this structure "buying a machine that will become obsolete with borrowed money, then hoping it runs faster than the interest."

With recourse vs without recourse: what RMB 41.6 billion means

Of the RMB 46.873 billion in debt, RMB 41.6 billion is recourse—creditors can pursue the parent company directly, not just the project company. For shareholders, the implication is that this is not "the debt of a particular data center" but "the debt of the entire company." Any single project problem transmits to the consolidated level.

In his energy and railroad businesses, Buffett uses "non-recourse at the project level + long-term fixed-price contracts," matching debt to asset life. Here it is the reverse: the assets are GPUs depreciated over 6 years, the debt is short-term paper maturing within 2 years. Maturity mismatch is the common prelude to every financial crisis.

Cash of RMB 5.5 billion against RMB 13.4 billion maturing leaves a shortfall of RMB 7.9 billion

Two numbers sit side by side: roughly RMB 5.5 billion of cash on hand against about RMB 13.4 billion maturing over the next two years. Even if all free cash flow were halted and not reinvested (impossible), the gap would not close. The only way out is refinancing, and the pricing power over refinancing sits with the bond market—in mid-November, its unsecured bonds yielded about 15%.

What does 15% mean? Refinancing RMB 13.4 billion at a 15% cost would imply annual interest of RMB 2 billion, more than a third of full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA. This is the death spiral of "borrowing new to repay old" under high rates: the more it borrows, the more expensive it gets; the more expensive it gets, the more it borrows.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: do not invest, and no valuation is needed. Interest coverage of 0.24x, cash to maturing debt of 0.41x, recourse debt of RMB 41.6 billion — any one of these alone would be enough to reject it; all three together show this is not a "temporary difficulty." His stated logic on companies like this: "If you have to rely on others to keep lending you money just to survive, you are not running a business, you are running a financing."
