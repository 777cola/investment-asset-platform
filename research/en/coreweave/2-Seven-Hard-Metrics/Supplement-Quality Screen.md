## The one that got through is precisely the one least deserving of trust

Gross margin of roughly 70% is absurdly high for a hardware-intensive business. The reason: it sells "compute hours", so the cost side is limited to electricity, data centre rent and O&M, while GPU purchases flow through depreciation rather than cost of goods. Shifting capex out of cost and into depreciation flatters gross margin by construction — a generic accounting effect in asset-heavy industries, not a competitive advantage.

To judge whether the business actually makes money, look at what is left after depreciation: a GAAP operating margin of 5.7%, and a net margin of -19.4% after interest. A "pass" on this item therefore only means it is not losing money on direct costs; it does not mean it is profitable.

## The six points all point to the same problem: surviving on financing

Line up the three cash-flow metrics and the picture becomes clear. Operating cash flow was RMB 1.975 billion—healthy at face value—but RMB 2.127 billion of it was customer prepayments. Prepayments exceed total operating cash flow, so excluding them, day-to-day operations were net cash outflows. Capex was RMB 9.441 billion, leaving free cash flow at negative RMB 7.466 billion. What fills the gap? RMB 4.2 billion in convertible bonds, RMB 2.6 billion in delayed-draw loans, 35 million shares in a follow-on offering, and roughly RMB 9 billion of prepayments over the next three months.

Interest coverage of 0.24x and net debt/EBITDA of 10x measure the same thing: the absolute scale of debt has outstripped the business's ability to generate cash. Q4 interest guidance is USD 1.05–1.15 billion against adjusted operating profit of USD 379 million this quarter—even doubling margins would not cover the interest.

## But is it fatal: not fatal, but "uninvestable"

Each item, taken alone, has a defence: negative ROE because the company is still in its expansion phase; negative FCF because capex is front-loaded; dilution because it had to seize the window. Those defences held up within the 2024–2025 growth-stock narrative.

Failing all seven at once, however, does not signal "temporarily ugly" — it signals that this business's cash-flow structure is inherently misaligned with shareholder interests. Every dollar it earns must be ploughed straight back into equipment, and it borrows two more on top; what shareholders receive is accounting growth and an ever-thickening debt instrument. For a framework whose first principle is "don't lose money," this is not a question of valuation being high or low — the target is disqualified.
