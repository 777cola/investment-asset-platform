## Why not short it

Three reasons. First, borrow cost and squeeze risk: in a name with this much volatility and attention, any guidance raise or major new order can trigger a 20%–30% rebound, and shorts cannot absorb the drawdown. Second, time is not on the short side: its debt matures in 2027 and default is possible rather than underway, so shorts would pay a year of borrow fees waiting for an uncertain date.

Third, and most important: not investing is already a complete decision. Being bearish does not require shorting to prove the point; avoidance itself is how the risk gets priced. Shorting turns "I judge it will fall" into "I need it to fall by a certain date" — the latter requires forecasting timing, the former only requires judging value.

## Price levels for trimming and adding positions

Trim: any rebound to USD 55–60 (1.3–1.4x the USD 43 neutral valuation) is a trimming window — not on technical levels, but because risk-reward deteriorates further at this price. Add: no add level — not because the price isn't low enough, but because the triggers are structural (debt duration, FCF path, contract terms), not price-driven.

Stop-loss: none. Counterintuitive, but meaningless for anyone at a 0% position; for existing holders, USD 39.51 is -71.4% from the high, and a stop-loss should have triggered above USD 100 long ago. Debating stop-losses now uses trading discipline to mask the original buying mistake.

## Correlation with other holdings

If the portfolio already holds Nvidia, TSMC, SK hynix and Microsoft, CoreWeave's correlation with those names is extremely high — all are driven by AI capex — but the risk profile is entirely different: the upstream names offer "demand + pricing power", while CoreWeave offers "demand + leverage". On the same event (a slowdown in AI capex), the upstream names fall 20% and CoreWeave falls 60%.

Its only role in the portfolio, therefore, can be a "high-beta satellite position", never a core holding. And with a negative expected value, even a satellite position is unnecessary. To express the view that "AI compute continues to grow", use upstream names or hyperscalers instead — the payoff structure is far better: upside participation, with cash flow providing a floor on the downside.
