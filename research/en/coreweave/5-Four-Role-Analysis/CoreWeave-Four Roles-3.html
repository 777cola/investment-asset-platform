Core view

Demand in this industry is real; the profits are not. In 2026, the four major North American cloud providers will spend a combined several hundred billion US dollars on capex, and Nvidia's FY2027Q3 single-quarter revenue reached USD 115.8 billion, up 103% YoY. Yet the neocloud layer—CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN, Crusoe—all run negative free cash flow, funding expansion on debt and prepayments. Munger would ask: in an industry where everyone is burning cash to grab territory, whose pocket does the money end up in?

Profit distribution across the industry chain

Profit distribution across this arms race is highly uneven. Upstream captures the vast majority: NVIDIA's FY2027Q3 revenue reached USD 115.8 billion, up 103% YoY, with gross margin still above 70%; contract prices for the three HBM players (SK hynix, Samsung, Micron) surged in 2026, with capacity booked through 2027. Midstream neoclouds collect a "toll," while downstream AI labs gain "capability" but pay in cash.

Munger's verdict would be blunt: in a capital-intensive industry with homogeneous products, where demand explodes but supply can explode just as readily, excess profit accrues to the standard-setter, not the middleman. So it was with railways (rail and locomotive makers made the money), so it was with aviation (Boeing and Airbus made the money), and so it is with cloud computing (NVIDIA makes the money).

Cycle position: shifting from "chip shortage" to "power and data-centre shortage"

The 2026 bottleneck has shifted from GPUs to power and grid interconnection. Of CoreWeave's 4.6GW of contracted power, only 1.7GW is operational; the remaining 2.9GW is held up by transformers, gas turbines and interconnection approvals. A bottleneck shift means a shift in bargaining power: whoever controls power, land and interconnection permits holds pricing power, and "being able to get cards" is no longer a scarce capability.

That is bad news for neoclouds, because hyperscalers can outbid them for power resources. Microsoft spends 45.2 billion in a single quarter and about 170 billion for the full year; the premium it is willing to pay for 1GW of interconnection capacity is far higher than a company with a market cap of 22 billion can afford.

Circular financing: where this industry most resembles 2000

Nvidia invests in its customers, those customers buy its chips, and Nvidia then backstops idle capacity — a chain that drew repeated scrutiny in 2026. The issue is not fraud but that it discounts the "authenticity" of demand: where part of the buyer's money originates from the seller, how much of reported revenue growth reflects end demand and how much is capital circulation?

On November 17, the SEC's investigation into Nvidia's accounting treatment and Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO brought the issue to the fore. The direct read-across for CoreWeave: once its largest supplier and shareholder (Nvidia) pulls back support, both supply priority and potential backstops disappear. As Munger would put it, in such structures the weakest link breaks first under stress — and neoclouds are that weakest link.

How would Munger describe this phase?

"When everyone in an industry borrows money to buy the same equipment that becomes obsolete, sells it to a handful of customers, and that equipment gets a new generation every year—this is not a growth industry, this is a race, and whoever starts doing the math halfway through the race pays the bill." That line describes the neocloud space in November 2026.

He did not say to avoid the entire industry; he said to avoid the one with "the heaviest leverage and the thinnest moat." Within the same trend, upstream players gain pricing power, downstream players gain capability, and middlemen gain contracts and debt—a structure that has recurred over the past hundred years.

Conclusion

Sector view: real demand, cycle peak, middlemen most at risk. Total AI compute demand will keep growing, but as of November 2026 the industry sits at a triple inflection point: supply is catching up, the bottleneck is shifting to power, and circular financing is under scrutiny. At this stage profit concentrates in hyperscalers that control power resources and enjoy a cost-of-capital advantage, while neoclouds' scarcity pricing gets arbitraged away. Munger-style conclusion: this industry is worth researching, but never participate through the most heavily levered company in it.
