Team Lead summary — CoreWeave

Divergence points and verdicts across four independent perspectives

Core view

Four independent roles reached a highly consistent conclusion: do not buy. They diverged only on the "why" and on "what would change our mind." The business perspective frames it as a business-model problem (the spread gets arbitraged away), the financial perspective as a capital-structure problem (interest eats the profit), the industry perspective as a cycle-position problem (the bottleneck shifts to power and the middleman loses leverage), and the risk perspective as an odds problem (risk 70% to make 9%). Verdict: avoid, with no buy price set.

Disagreement one: is the problem cyclical or structural?

On the commercial lens, the case is "cyclical": scarcity pricing will fade, but so long as AI demand keeps growing the company can survive on scale, and conditions may improve once the industry consolidates. On the industry-and-risk lens, the case is "structural": a cost of capital of 7.9% versus 4.85%, customer concentration of 54% and product homogeneity — none of these reverses because the cycle recovers.

Verdict: treat it as structural. The debt maturity profile decides it — RMB 13.4 billion falls due over the next two years, and the company cannot wait for the day of "industry consolidation." A cyclical call needs time; a structural problem needs only one bad quarter.

Disagreement two: what the RMB 132.5 billion order book is really worth

The business lens treats backlog as real demand and values it accordingly; the financial lens treats backlog as not equal to cash flow, since most contracts specify maximum commitment amounts; the risk lens applies a straight 50% haircut. These three approaches produce valuations that differ by more than 2x.

Ruling: apply a 60% haircut and use it only in the optimistic scenario. The basis is contract structure (commitments, not take-or-pay) plus customers' in-house capability (Microsoft's Maia 300 is scheduled for mass production in 2027). This haircut is also why the neutral scenario assigns only USD 43.

Disagreement 3: Is there such a thing as "fallen enough"

The risk perspective raised a counterpoint worth recording: the stock is -71.4% from its high and has fallen below its USD 40 IPO price, and its USD 22 billion market cap corresponds to 4.6GW of contracted power, which at USD 5.5 billion per GW is already below new-build cost. On assets alone, that looks cheap.

Verdict: does not hold. The assets were bought with debt — EV of USD 83.5 billion against 4.6GW is USD 18.1 billion per GW, far above new-build cost. The cheap market cap is an illusion; enterprise value is the price I pay. This is the most important correction from this deliberation: always compare EV, not market cap, against assets.

Final ruling and execution

Avoid. No buy price, and not in the "buyable" zone of the watchlist. The call is not a view that the stock will fall; it reflects equity pricing that hinges on the credit market rather than on operations. Three triggers would prompt reassessment: ① free cash flow narrows for two consecutive quarters and management guides to a quarter for turning positive; ② unsecured bond yields fall below 10% and a new issue succeeds; ③ at least one major customer contract switches to minimum-payment terms.

Portfolio level: 0% position. If already held, treat any rebound as an opportunity to trim rather than waiting for a fundamental change—the direction of that change is likely to be adverse. To retain AI compute exposure, substitute Nvidia, TSMC and HBM: they capture pricing power, not leverage, in the same trend.

Conclusion

Team Lead ruling: avoid (0% position). All four perspectives agree on "do not buy"; they diverge only on attribution and reversibility, and the ruling defaults to the more conservative structural reading. In one line: the demand is real, the equity is not — this is an option on the credit window, not a business that can be valued. Reassessment requires simultaneous improvement on three fronts: the free cash flow path, debt duration and customer contract quality.
