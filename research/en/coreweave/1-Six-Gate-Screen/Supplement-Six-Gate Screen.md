## Gate one: the lease is legible; the depreciation is not

A score of 6 — not because the model is hard to understand, but precisely because it is too easy to understand, which invites underestimating the risk. The business model in three sentences: sign 5-year contracts with AI labs, use those contracts to borrow from banks and bond markets, buy Nvidia cards, rent power, build data centers, then collect rent monthly. Anyone can grasp this layer.

The real dividing line is the second-layer judgment: whether the economic life of an H100 or GB300 is 3 years or 6 years. The company depreciates over 6 years. If reality is 3–4 years — Nvidia ships a new generation every year, and Hopper rents will fall once Rubin launches — then current operating profit on the books is fictitious, and future catch-up depreciation will blow through the income statement. The useful-life assumption in the 10-Q matters more than any USD 20 billion order announcement. I cannot answer this layer, so the stock only barely passes.

## The second hurdle: 62% of EBITDA is an accounting illusion

Management leaned repeatedly on adjusted EBITDA of USD 2.256 billion and a 62% margin. Yet GAAP operating margin over the same period was just 5.7% (USD 207 million / USD 3.661 billion), and depreciation accounts for the entire 57-percentage-point gap. Below the operating line, USD 846 million of net interest swallowed the USD 207 million of operating profit and left a USD 639 million shortfall, driving a net loss of USD 709 million.

This is not a case of "things will be fine once scale arrives."Greater scale means more cards to buy, more debt to carry and more interest to pay. Q4 interest guidance of USD 1.05–1.15 billion is 2.8–3x this quarter's adjusted operating profit. When every extra USD 1 of revenue requires USD 2 of debt and USD 0.2 of interest, operating leverage breaks down at the interest line.

## Round three: the biggest customer becomes the biggest rival

Compute is a commodity. For identical GB300 racks, customers gravitate to wherever power is cheapest, PUE is lowest and energisation is fastest. CoreWeave's first-mover advantage — securing cards 9 months and power 6 months ahead of rivals — is a time window, not a structural moat.

The hardest evidence comes from the buyers themselves. On 18 November, Nadella said publicly that "selling to only one or two customers is not a business". Microsoft spent USD 45.2 billion on capex this quarter and roughly USD 170 billion for the full year, even as it was reported to be reviewing more than USD 80 billion of external AI compute commitments and to have cancelled a USD 3.22 billion Nscale order.Buyers are shifting spend from "renting" back to "building", and the spread that gets squeezed is precisely the middleman's.

## Stage four: execution A, capital structure D

Management's delivery is genuinely strong: 900MW of contracted power added in the quarter, 200MW brought online, and 1.7GW of the 4.6GW contracted already running — the fastest pace among global neoclouds. A 25% price increase in July and a further 10% at quarter-end show management is willing to raise prices with customers, and that this is a seller's market.

Yet in the same quarter the company pushed a USD 4.2 billion convertible bond and a USD 2.6 billion delayed-draw term loan, lined up a 35 million share placement, and staked its funding for the next three months on roughly USD 9 billion of customer prepayments. This is not "seizing the opportunity" — it is opening every financing channel to maximum at once. For a company with a USD 22 billion market cap, that financing density makes its dependence on the credit window survival-level. Score 5; the deductions are all in the capital structure.

## Stage Five: Still not cheap after a 71% drop

Capital structure: market cap USD 22 billion, total debt USD 46.87 billion (USD 41.6 billion recourse), enterprise value of about USD 83.5 billion including lease liabilities, and net debt of about USD 61 billion. In other words, equity is only 26% of enterprise value — a thin layer sitting on top. A 10% drop in enterprise value takes equity down 38%.

The price signal is more direct still: yields on the 9.625% unsecured notes issued in June had risen to around 15% by mid-November, entering distressed territory, while AAA issuers like Microsoft were funding at under 5% over the same period. For the same company, the bond market is pricing default probability while the stock market is still pricing the RMB 132.5 billion order book. When the two markets disagree, the bond market is usually right.

## Level six: the buy case is a narrative, not a premise

The only reason to buy CoreWeave now is a belief that AI compute demand will keep outpacing supply, so its orders will keep converting, rents won't fall and financing will always be available. None of these four can be verified from the financials; all are faith in the future.

Verifiable preconditions should look like this: free cash flow turns positive in some quarter of 2027, debt duration extends from 2 years to more than 5 years, and at least one major customer converts its "maximum commitment" into a "minimum payment guarantee." Buying before these three things happen is essentially betting on the credit market, not investing in a business.

## Veto items and items to be verified

Dealbreaker: capital structure. Debt of RMB 46.87 billion at an average cost of 7.9%, with about RMB 13.4 billion maturing within two years, against quarterly free cash flow of -RMB 7.466 billion and roughly RMB 5.5 billion of cash on hand. This is not growth-stock volatility; it is a model that cannot operate without continuous refinancing. If the credit window shuts, RMB 132.5 billion of orders will not convert into a single yuan of cash.

Items to verify (in order of importance):
- Whether management has given a quarterly path for 2027 capex guidance and the point at which free cash flow turns positive
- Whether customer prepayments can be sustained: RMB 2.127 billion this quarter versus guidance of about RMB 9 billion for Q4, the cheapest and most fragile source of funding
- Whether the assumed GPU depreciation life has been cut (if so, reported profit disappears immediately)
- The final conclusion of Microsoft's USD 80 billion review of external compute, and the impact of Maia 300 volume production in 2027 on demand for external GPU cloud
- Whether secondary-market yields on unsecured debt fall back below 10% (a leading indicator that the credit window is reopening)
