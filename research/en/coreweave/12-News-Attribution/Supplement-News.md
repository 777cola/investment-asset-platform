## The nature of this selloff: a de-rating, not an earnings hit

Line up three months of operating news against the share price and a contradiction emerges: the company raised guidance twice in the period (August and November), revenue beat the top end of guidance for three consecutive quarters, and orders climbed from RMB 104 billion to RMB 132.5 billion — results kept beating expectations, yet the stock fell 71%.

The market was not rejecting the results; it was rejecting the valuation method. In 2025 it priced the stock on "orders × multiple"; by November 2026 it had switched to "cash flow − debt". The same company, measured with a different ruler, commands a 3x difference in price. That is not sentiment fluctuation but a switch in pricing framework — and such switches are usually irreversible.

## The three most important things, in order

First, the Q3 cash flow. Free cash flow came in at -RMB 7.466 billion, with the shortfall widening every quarter (Q1 -4.71 billion, Q2 -5.74 billion, Q3 -7.47 billion), and RMB 2.127 billion of the operating cash flow is customer prepayments—once the market sees this clearly, "growth" will need redefining. The line-by-line breakdown in the November 18 10-Q put this detail in front of everyone.

Second, the November 17 bond. A 15% yield on unsecured debt means the credit market is already pricing default. Equities can hold up on narrative; bonds cannot—bond investors earn fixed income and cannot afford to lose, so their reading of the same financial report is usually closer to the truth.

Third, Microsoft's statement on November 18. Nadella's line that "selling to only one or two customers isn't a business," plus the USD 80 billion external compute review and the cancellation of the USD 3.22 billion Nscale order, tells the market: the biggest buyer is pulling outsourcing back in-house, and the middleman's spread is about to be erased.

## Which are noise

Noise 1: Raised guidance. Demand was never the market's concern — the 132.5 billion order book settled that. The guidance raise offers no support for the stock: the problem is cash, not revenue.

Noise 2: The "AI bubble" narrative behind the 12% one-day plunge. November 20 was a systemic selloff (Nasdaq -5.28%, Nvidia -14%), and attributing it to company fundamentals is wrong. Nor should anyone conclude the drop is over and done with — the systemic selloff only accelerated the de-rating; the direction was set long ago.

Noise 3: Share-price correlation with Neocloud peers. Nebius and IREN rise and fall with it, misleading investors into seeing a "sector-wide opportunity." In reality their leverage and customers differ; moving in lockstep is a liquidity phenomenon, not synchronized fundamentals.

## Three independent lines of evidence point to the same conclusion

Connect the three signals: a widening cash-flow gap (operations) → a 15% bond yield (financing) → Microsoft cancelling orders (demand). Each piece of evidence stands on its own, yet all three point to the same conclusion: in November 2026, this company's business model lost both its "cheap money" and its "irreplaceable position" at the same time.

That is also why it fell harder than Nvidia on November 20 (-12% vs -14%, though Nvidia was pulling back from a high while this stock kept falling from a -70% level): the same bad news, landing on the most leveraged equity, produces the greatest elasticity. In three months, not one piece of good news has changed this logic, and neither can a guidance raise.
