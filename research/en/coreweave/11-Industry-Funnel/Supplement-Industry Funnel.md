## Notes

Our funnel works in three layers. The first applies three hard thresholds: FCF turning positive within two years, interest coverage of at least 3x, and no single large customer at 30% or more of revenue. The second assesses business-model sustainability. The final selection tests for pricing power and a cost-of-capital advantage. Market cap and P/E are rough estimates at mid-November magnitudes and were not checked against each company's filings.

## First-pass screen: three hard thresholds

Threshold one: free cash flow is positive, or can turn positive within two years. CoreWeave burned RMB 7.466 billion in a single quarter, with the deficit widening each quarter; Nebius and IREN are likewise negative. Threshold two: interest coverage of at least 3x. CoreWeave sits at 0.24x. Threshold three: customer concentration below 30%. CoreWeave's top two customers account for 54%.

All three neoclouds fail all three thresholds. This is a call on the financial statements, not on the industry—in the same quarter all three tripped three lines at once: burning cash, borrowing expensive money and depending on a handful of customers. Microsoft, Oracle and Nvidia each clear at least two.

## Layer two: why the entire neocloud layer was eliminated

All three neoclouds run the same template: borrow to buy GPUs → build data centers → sign long-term contracts → borrow again to buy GPUs. They differ only in leverage multiples and customer mix. The template works during a GPU shortage, when scarcity pricing covers the cost of capital, and fails once supply catches up, bringing a price war and rigid interest costs.

The evidence in November 2026: all three drew down more than 50% at the same time, with financing costs rising in tandem. A single company's problem would not hit all three; all three together points to a problem with the model. This step eliminates the entire layer and is the most important judgment in this funnel.

## Final picks: NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Oracle under review

NVIDIA: the benchmark for AI compute, with gross margin above 70% and FY27Q3 revenue of RMB 115.8 billion, up 103%. Risks centre on the questioned circular financing and customers' in-house chips; the November 17 SEC investigation and the CEO change are major uncertainties, though its pricing power does not rest on any single customer.

Microsoft: both the largest buyer and the strongest self-builder, with a cost of capital of 4.85% (CoreWeave 7.9%) and a P/E of about 26x. Its AI exposure sits inside cloud and Office cash flow, giving it substantial downside protection. Oracle goes on the watchlist rather than into the portfolio: OCI order growth is extremely high, but leverage is rising and OpenAI accounts for a large share; its risk structure resembles CoreWeave's, only with a far stronger balance sheet.

## Why not CoreWeave: three questions it cannot answer

First, your cost of money is 3 percentage points above Microsoft's — how do you expect to win in the same market? For the same kilowatt-hour and the same GPU, Microsoft's cost of capital is 4.85% against your 7.9%. In a thin-margin industry, those 3 percentage points are the line between life and death. Second, when Microsoft pulls back its USD 80 billion of outsourced capacity, who will use your 4.6GW of power? Third, RMB 13.4 billion matures in 2027 — what do you plan to repay it with?

It has an answer to all three — "we deliver faster," "customer demand is strong," "we will refinance." But every one of those answers rests on external conditions; none rests on its own cash flow. That is why it cannot make the final cut: a company whose survival depends on external conditions staying favourable does not belong on a list of names worth holding for ten years.

## If only one metric were used to screen this chain

Just one: the interest coverage ratio. It captures three things at once: profitability, debt scale and financing cost. Nvidia and Microsoft clear 10x with ease, Oracle sits at 3–5x, CoreWeave at 0.24x, and both Nebius and IREN below 2x. A single metric splits this chain into two groups, and the grouping lines up closely with share-price performance in November 2026.

The second most important metric is capex/operating cash flow. Below 1 means "self-funding"; above 2 means "living on external transfusions". CoreWeave is at 4.8, close to the most aggressive phases of Amazon and Microsoft back then—but those two had cloud cash flow as a backstop, and this one does not.
