## First-pass screen: three hard thresholds

**Threshold one: whether free cash flow is positive or can turn positive within two years.** CoreWeave posted negative RMB 7.466 billion in a single quarter, widening each quarter; Nebius and IREN are likewise negative. **Threshold two: whether interest coverage is ≥ 3x.** CoreWeave stands at 0.24x. **Threshold three: whether customer concentration is below 30%.** CoreWeave's top two customers account for 54%.

All three neoclouds fail all three thresholds. This is not an industry call but a financial-statement call—**in the same quarter they all tripped three lines at once: burning cash, borrowing expensive money, and relying on a handful of customers**. Microsoft, Oracle and Nvidia each clear at least two.

## Layer two: why the entire neocloud layer was eliminated

Viewed together, the three neoclouds run the same template: **borrow to buy GPUs → build data centers → sign long-term contracts → borrow again to buy GPUs**. They differ only in leverage multiples and customer mix. The template works during a GPU shortage (scarcity pricing covers the cost of capital) and fails once supply catches up (price war plus rigid interest costs).

The evidence in November 2026: **all three drew down more than 50% at the same time, with financing costs rising in tandem**. A single company's problem would not hit all three; all three together points to a problem with the model. This step eliminates the entire layer, and is the most important judgment in this funnel.

## Final picks: NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Oracle under review

**NVIDIA**: the standard-setter for AI compute, gross margin 70%+, FY27Q3 revenue RMB 115.8 billion, +103%. Risks are the questioned circular financing and customers' in-house chips; the November 17 SEC investigation and CEO change are major uncertainties, but its pricing power does not depend on any single customer.

**Microsoft**: both the largest buyer and the strongest self-builder, cost of capital 4.85% (CoreWeave 7.9%), PE about 26x. Its AI exposure is wrapped in cloud and Office cash flow, giving it thick downside protection. **Oracle** goes on the watchlist rather than into the portfolio: OCI order growth is extremely high, but leverage is rising and OpenAI accounts for a large share; its risk structure resembles CoreWeave's, only with a far thicker balance sheet.

## Why not CoreWeave: three questions it cannot answer

**First, your money costs 3 percentage points more than Microsoft's — how do you expect to win in the same market?** For the same kilowatt-hour of power and the same GPU, Microsoft's cost of capital is 4.85% versus its 7.9%. In a thin-margin industry, those 3 percentage points are the line between life and death. **Second, when Microsoft pulls back its USD 80 billion of outsourced capacity, who will use your 4.6GW of power?** **Third, RMB 13.4 billion matures in 2027 — what do you plan to repay it with?**

It has answers to all three questions — "we deliver faster," "customer demand is strong," "we will refinance." **But all three answers point to external conditions; none points to its own cash flow.** That is why it cannot make the final cut: a company that needs external conditions to stay favorable in order to survive does not belong on a list of names worth holding for ten years.

## If only one metric were used to screen this chain

Just one: I use the **interest coverage ratio**. It captures three things at once: the profitability of the business, the scale of debt, and the cost of financing. Nvidia and Microsoft clear 10x with ease, Oracle sits at 3–5x, CoreWeave at 0.24x, and both Nebius and IREN below 2x. **A single metric splits this chain into two groups, and the grouping lines up closely with share-price performance in November 2026.**

The second most important metric is capex/operating cash flow. **Below 1 means "self-funding"; above 2 means "living on external transfusions"**. CoreWeave is at 4.8, close to the most aggressive phases of Amazon and Microsoft back then—but those two had cloud cash flow as a backstop, and this one does not.
