Core view

Read the three statements together once; that is worth more than ten research reports. Income statement: revenue RMB 3.661 billion, net loss RMB 709 million. Cash flow statement: operating +RMB 1.975 billion, of which RMB 2.127 billion is prepayments; investing -RMB 9.441 billion. Financing: bonds, convertibles and equity placements all net inflows. All three tell the same story: revenue is rising, cash is leaking out, and borrowing is plugging the gap.

Income statement: three levels of profit, each an order of magnitude apart

First-layer adjusted EBITDA of RMB 2.256 billion (62%) excludes depreciation, interest and share-based compensation. Second-layer adjusted operating profit of RMB 379 million (10.4%) excludes share-based compensation of about RMB 172 million, back-solved from 379−207. Third-layer GAAP operating profit of RMB 207 million (5.7%) bears the full depreciation charge. Below that, net interest of RMB 846 million widens the net loss to RMB 709 million.

The gap between the three layers tells the whole story: depreciation sits between 62% and 5.7%; interest sits between 5.7% and -19.4%. For the past two years the market valued the business at the 62% layer; it is now starting to price it at the -19.4% layer.

Cash flow statement: advance payments disguise financing as operating

Operating cash flow of RMB 1.975 billion looks healthy. But the notes show customer prepayments rose RMB 2.127 billion. Prepayments are cash customers pay upfront for future services; accounting books them as deferred revenue (a liability) and the cash flow statement as an operating inflow. In effect the company is recording financing as operating — strictly speaking compliant, but the reader has to strip it out.

Strip it out and day-to-day operations are a net outflow. Investing absorbed RMB 9.441 billion of cash (RMB 14.5 billion on an accrual basis), leaving free cash flow at -RMB 7.466 billion. Q1 -4.711, Q2 -5.743, Q3 -7.466 — the gap widened 58% across three quarters. This is no "investment phase"; an investment phase should see the gap narrow as scale effects kick in. This one is widening.

Depreciation sensitivity: a single assumption underpins all profit

Back-of-envelope: 4.6GW at USD 30 million/MW implies equipment capex of roughly USD 138 billion. A 6-year depreciation life gives annual depreciation of USD 23 billion; at 4 years it is USD 34.5 billion, USD 11.5 billion more. Full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA is roughly USD 6–7 billion. In other words, shortening the depreciation life by two years generates extra depreciation equal to 1.6x full-year EBITDA.

That is not to say the company will make the change, only that its income statement is far more sensitive to accounting assumptions than to operating improvements. When profit depends mainly on which number management picks, the stock should not command a growth valuation.

Balance sheet: equity accounts for just 26% of enterprise value

Total debt of RMB 46.87 billion (RMB 41.6 billion recourse) at an average cost of 7.9%, with RMB 13.4 billion maturing within two years, against cash of RMB 5.5 billion; including lease liabilities, enterprise value is about RMB 83.5 billion versus a market cap of RMB 22 billion. Equity is 26% of enterprise value — every 1% move in enterprise value moves equity 3.8%.

That leverage multiple explains two things: why it can double on the way up (from 40 to 138 in 2025), and why it can be cut in half twice over on the way down (138 to 39.5). The same machine, amplified in both directions.

An overlooked line item: equity incentives

The RMB 172 million gap between adjusted operating profit of RMB 379 million and GAAP operating profit of RMB 207 million is mainly share-based compensation. It costs no cash but genuinely dilutes shareholders: roughly RMB 700 million of share-based compensation a year against a market cap of RMB 22 billion implies 3% annual dilution.

Add the 35 million share issuance plan (about 6.3%), 3% annual dilution from share-based compensation and potential crisis financing, and the share count could expand by more than 30% over the next three years. Hence the valuation uses 560–620 million shares rather than the current 556 million: dilution is not a risk, it is part of the plan.

The line most easily misread

The line in the 10-Q stating "customer prepayments increased RMB 2.127 billion" is the most misread item in the entire filing. It turned operating cash flow positive, but it reflects an increase in liabilities, not the generation of profit. When reading financial statements, make this a habit: first check whether "operating activities" in the cash flow statement contains a large change in deferred revenue, and if so, strip it out before drawing conclusions.

A simple test for whether "prepayments count as operating cash flow": check whether the same amount can be sustained next quarter. If Q4 prepayments fall from RMB 2.127 billion to RMB 500 million while capex does not decline, the gap will be exposed for all to see. That is also why the line in the Q4 guidance about "roughly USD 9 billion of prepayments over the next three months" deserves to be flagged separately for reconciliation.

Conclusion

Financial reality: profit rests on assumptions, cash on prepayments, survival on refinancing. Three-tier profit fell from 62% to -19.4%, with depreciation and interest in between; operating cash flow is propped up by RMB 2.127 billion of prepayments; debt of RMB 46.873 billion against cash of RMB 5.5 billion. Any breakdown in an assumption or a channel and the statements collapse. Investment implication: do not value it on P/E or P/S; use "EV/EBITDA less net debt" and apply a haircut of more than 40% to EBITDA.
