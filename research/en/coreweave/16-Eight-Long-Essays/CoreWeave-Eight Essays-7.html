Risk checklist: five ways to die and one transmission path — CoreWeave

Which comes first, and how will it transmit

Core view

The risk here is not that "AI demand rolls over" — that probability is very low. What would actually kill the equity is four things: the refinancing window closing, customers pulling outsourcing back in-house, depreciation lives forced lower, and heavy equity dilution — plus one exogenous shock, "the circular financing chain breaking." These five risks reinforce one another, and every transmission path ends the same way: the equity is diluted or wiped out.

Transmission path: from bonds to equities

The chain runs: bond yields rise (now about 15%) → new issuance becomes more expensive or impossible → RMB 13.4 billion maturing cannot be rolled over → forced equity financing → equity dilution of 30%–60% → value per share falls. Every link is verifiable from public data; no forecasting required.

The first step is the key node. Bonds lead because creditors earn fixed income and cannot absorb losses; they read the same financial statements faster and more harshly than anyone else. On November 17 its unsecured bonds entered distressed territory — the signal that the chain had started. The share price's subsequent catch-up decline was a lagged reaction.

Five ways to die, ranked by probability

① Equity dilution (high probability, 60%): A 35 million-share offering is already on file, with equity incentives adding roughly 3% dilution a year.② Clients pull back outsourcing (medium-high, 40%): Microsoft is reviewing USD 80 billion of external compute, has cancelled the USD 3.22 billion Nscale order, and Nadella has publicly questioned the outsourcing model.③ Refinancing failure (medium, 35%): USD 13.4 billion matures in 2027 against USD 5.5 billion of cash.

④ Depreciation cut (medium, 30%): Nvidia refreshes its lineup annually; cut the 6-year assumption to 4 years and GAAP operating profit turns negative immediately.⑤ Circular financing breaks down (medium, 25%): With Nvidia under SEC investigation and a CEO change, the credibility of supply priority and backstop commitments weakens.Four of the five risks relate to the capital structure, and they reinforce one another.

The overlooked risk: contract quality

Meta's USD 2.1 billion and OpenAI's USD 650 million are both "maximum commitment amounts," not take-or-pay. If actual customer usage falls below the commitment, the company does not collect the full amount, yet racks, power and debt have already been provisioned at full capacity. The claimed 36x annual revenue coverage of the USD 132.5 billion backlog is illusory—real coverage depends on the fulfillment rate.

The 10-Q's limited disclosure on "minimum payment terms" is itself a risk. When you cannot verify the quality of a number, you should discount it at the worst case; this report applies a 60% haircut to the backlog in its optimistic scenario for exactly this reason.

Exogenous risk: a turn in the AI capex cycle

If capex growth at the four major North American cloud providers slows from +40% to +10% in 2027, neocloud rents and valuations will de-rate in tandem. CoreWeave's beta is 3–4x the sector (equity is only 26% of enterprise value), so a 20% sector decline translates into a 60%–80% decline for the stock.

This is not a "possibility" but a "certainty that will happen on some day" — every capex cycle turns. The only question is whether the company's debt maturity schedule will already have been rolled over when the turn comes. Under the current maturity structure, 2027 is its most vulnerable year.

The right way to use a risk checklist

A risk list is not there to scare you; it is there to rank. Of the five risks, only refinancing is irreversible; the other four can be mitigated through operational improvement. Monitoring should therefore centre on bond prices, not on quarterly revenue beating consensus.

The ranking: near term (within 6 months), equity dilution is the most likely; medium term (12–18 months), rising refinancing costs; long term (beyond 24 months), a business-model re-rating. Each timeline demands a different response, but for an investor at 0% position, all three point the same way: continue not to participate.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: unacceptable. Four of the five risks are capital-structure related and reinforce one another through a clear, traceable transmission path (bonds → refinancing → dilution). The two leading indicators to watch most closely: the unsecured bond yield (currently about 15%; only a fall back below 10% counts as relief) and quarterly customer prepayments (RMB 2.127 billion this quarter, Q4 guidance about RMB 9 billion). The first determines whether the company survives; the second, whether it survives like a normal company.
