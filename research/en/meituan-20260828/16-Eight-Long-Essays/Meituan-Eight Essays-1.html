I. Cognitive Reset: What You Think You Are Buying — Meituan

Ignore consensus and take a fresh look at this company.
Research date 2026/08/28  ·  Price HK$77.50 (+0.00%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 478.6 billion (6.175 billion shares)  ·  PE — (net loss of RMB 4.67 billion in H1 2026; PE distorted)

Core view

Most people now see Meituan as "a casualty of the food-delivery war" — share lost, profits gone, pounded by Alibaba and JD.com. That impression is not wrong, but it misses three things: it earned RMB 35.8 billion in 2024; it now holds RMB 168.3 billion in cash and short-term investments; what it gave up were unprofitable low-price orders. The point of this cognitive reset is not to talk the stock up but to swap the "victim narrative" for a "cost-structure narrative" — because the former can only conclude "wait for it to get better," while the latter lets you calculate "what it is worth."

Consensus one: Meituan lost share, so it lost

That claim has two flaws. First, share depends on the metric: Goldman Sachs' 51% in March 2026 is on an order-volume basis, while the company's "over 60%" in its 2025 annual report is on a GTV basis. Order volume falling fast while dollar value falls slowly means it is losing low-price orders below RMB 15.

Second, losing share is not the same as losing the business. Core local commerce revenue rose 10.1% YoY in Q2 2026 and operating profit reached RMB 5.67 billion, up 52.3% YoY. If the share loss were truly a "rout," revenue could not still be up 10%. The reality is that it deliberately gave up unprofitable orders and held on to the profitable ones.

That distinction drives the valuation: a rout implies HKD 60, a deliberate trade-off implies HKD 110.

Consensus view 2: food delivery is a tough business and does not deserve a multiple

That statement held true in 2025 and was wrong in 2024. Within the same business line, core local commerce operating margin was 20.9% in 2024, group operating profit was RMB 45.1 billion and net profit RMB 35.8 billion.

A business that earns a 20% operating margin, needs almost no capex and collects cash before it pays out is not a grind. Its only grind: it does not set its own profit. If rivals are willing to lose money, it loses too; when rivals stop, it earns.

The correct framing, then: this is a "good business + bad industry" combination. A good business deserves a multiple; a bad industry demands a discount. A complete judgment must price in both.

Consensus view 3: it is burning cash and is dangerous

The numbers speak for themselves: cash and equivalents of RMB 104.7 billion plus short-term wealth management products of RMB 63.6 billion as of June 30, 2026, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion; net operating cash inflow of RMB 9.7 billion in Q2 2026, 4.5x net profit.

That is not what a "cash-burning" company looks like. A cash-burning company is one where cash declines quarter after quarter, operating cash flow is negative, and it survives on financing. Meituan did go through exactly that in 2025 (operating cash flow of -RMB 13.815 billion, net financing inflow of RMB 21.243 billion), but by Q2 2026 it had turned around.

Its current state is "can afford to fight, but does not want to fight anymore."The distinction matters: a company that cannot afford to fight is forced to accept any terms; a company that can afford to fight but does not want to can wait for its rival to retreat first.

The new question after the reset

Set the three consensus views aside and the remaining questions sharpen: if this is a deliberate trade-off, why did it still lose RMB 4.67 billion in the first half of 2026? If it is a good business, why a 2.6% margin? If it can afford to fight, why buy back only HKD 200 million?

All three point to the same variable: the slope of the recovery. A deliberate trade-off can defend revenue but not margin, because margin depends on the industry's subsidy level, and the industry's subsidy level depends on rivals' patience.

The end point of the cognitive reset is therefore not "buy" but "know what you are buying": you are buying the event of rivals' patience running out, not the process of Meituan's operating improvement. The former is an option, the latter is compounding; an option warrants a small position, compounding can take a heavy one.

One last overlooked fact: it holds a portfolio of equity stakes

One further item sits off Meituan's balance sheet: equity investments. The most prominent disclosed holding is its stake in Unitree Robotics, where it ranks among the largest external shareholders (disclosed in the Q1 2026 report). The asset generates no operating profit, but could be worth a great deal amid the robotics boom.

We do not attempt to value it—the stake size and fair value cannot be confirmed, and "possibly very valuable" cannot go into a target price.But it is a free option: if Unitree lists, Meituan books a non-operating gain; if Unitree goes to zero, the impact on Meituan is negligible.

This section is a reminder: the company's value is not confined to the income statement. RMB 168.3 billion in cash, equity investments and an AI tool used by 3.4 million merchants are all things "not in the P/E."That is also why, when valuing on "normalised P/E + net cash," we prefer a range to a single point.

Conclusion

The upshot of that cognitive reset: from "a victim of the food-delivery war" to "a good business trapped in a bad industry." The shift changes your position—a victim can only wait, whereas a trapped company can be priced.
