IV. Financial reality: from RMB -23.4 billion to RMB 2.69 billion — Meituan

In a year and a half, what has changed and what has not
Research date 2026/08/28  ·  Price HK$77.50 (+0.00%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 478.6 billion (6.175 billion shares)  ·  PE — (net loss of RMB 4.67 billion in H1 2026; PE distorted)

Core view

A net loss of RMB 23.36 billion in 2025, then net profit of RMB 2.16 billion in Q2 2026. In eighteen months the same company went from "ICU" to "discharged," with revenue moving only from RMB 364.85 billion (full-year 2025) to RMB 195.68 billion (H1 2026). The swing sits entirely on the cost side: subsidies pulled back, incentives cut, loss ratio narrowed. One thing has not changed: its margin is still decided by its rivals.

2025: a gap of RMB 62.1 billion

Revenue RMB 364.85 billion, +8.1%; operating loss RMB 17 billion; net loss RMB 23.355 billion. Versus 2024: operating profit RMB 45.1 billion; net profit RMB 35.8 billion.

Revenue grew 8.1%, yet profit fell by RMB 62.1 billion. Where did that RMB 62.1 billion go? Three places: user incentives (subsidies), rider subsidies, and upfront investment in overseas expansion. The largest piece is user incentives — starting in 2025Q2, Taobao Shangou entered the market and the industry shifted to "whoever subsidizes more wins the orders," forcing Meituan to follow.

The most important number here is not the size of the loss but the way it lost money: operating cash flow of -RMB 13.815 billion and net financing inflow of RMB 21.243 billion. It survived the year on financing — the first time it has genuinely needed external capital.

First half of 2026: Q1 carries the entire half

1H revenue RMB 195.68 billion, +10.1%; operating loss RMB 3.78 billion; net loss RMB 4.67 billion. By quarter: Q1 revenue RMB 91.04 billion, operating loss RMB 6.47 billion, net loss RMB 6.83 billion; Q2 revenue RMB 104.64 billion, operating profit RMB 2.69 billion, net profit RMB 2.16 billion.

The RMB 6.47 billion Q1 loss more than consumed the entire RMB 2.69 billion Q2 profit, leaving a RMB 3.78 billion shortfall. That fully explains the loss at the half-year mark: Q2 was not weak; Q1 was simply too poor.

So where did Q1 go wrong? Revenue grew only 5.6%, the slowest of the year, hit by the shifted Chinese New Year timing and the fiercest stretch of the subsidy war, while expenses stayed elevated. Q2 revenue growth recovered to 14.4% and the expense ratio fell 0.6pp, with profit following. That elasticity shows profit is extremely sensitive to revenue growth and the expense ratio — the hallmark of a thin-margin business.

Cash: the real good news

Q2 operating cash flow was RMB 9.7 billion against net profit of RMB 2.155 billion, a multiple of 4.5. Cash and equivalents on the books stood at RMB 104.7 billion, plus RMB 63.6 billion of short-term wealth management products, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion.

Why is cash flow so much better than profit? The business model is "collect first, pay later": users pay on order, merchants settle at T+1 or T+7, riders are settled weekly.The platform always holds a pool of float, and the larger the order volume, the bigger that pool.

But float is a double-edged sword: when order volume declines it flows back out, faster than profit declines. 2025 is the example — operating cash flow of -RMB 13.815 billion, uglier than the net loss of RMB 23.355 billion.So for this company, order volume matters more than profit, and cash flow matters more than order volume.

Capital allocation: has the money, but won't spend it

RMB 168.3 billion of cash and short-term investments, no dividend, and HKD 200 million of buybacks in June 2026 (0.04% of market cap). Taken together, these three numbers are the most incongruous passage in this earnings report.

Management's explanation is that "value is severely undervalued, and we plan to buy back shares." But HKD 200 million of action does not match a "severely undervalued" judgment. Three explanations are possible: retaining the money to fight the next battle (Brazil, AI), believing the stock is not yet cheap enough, or a governance structure that leaves minority shareholders without a voice.

The implication for investors is direct: do not factor the "buyback" into valuation. Any material scaling up of buybacks (more than HKD 2 billion in a half-year) would be a welcome surprise; until then, this cash is worth only a "safety cushion," not "shareholder returns."

One table shows where the money went

Read the 2025 annual report alongside the 2026 interim report and the cash flow tells the story: operating cash flow of -RMB 13.82 billion in 2025, financing of +RMB 21.24 billion, a net increase of roughly RMB 7.4 billion; in 1H26 operating cash flow turned positive (Q2 alone +RMB 9.7 billion).

In other words, 2025 was borrowing to fight; 2026 is generating its own blood. That inflection matters more than the swing to profit, because it determines whether the company still has ammunition.

How much ammunition is left? Cash and short-term investments stood at RMB 168.3 billion at end-June, which at 2025's burn rate (RMB 13.8 billion of cash flow lost in a year) lasts ten years; at the worst pace of 2025Q1 (quarterly operating cash outflow potentially in the tens of billions), only four or five quarters. That is why the +RMB 9.7 billion in 2026Q2 is so critical—it pushes back the risk of running out of runway.

The overlooked line: RMB 26 billion in R&D

R&D spend reached RMB 26 billion in full-year 2025, up 23% YoY and equal to 7.1% of revenue. Set that against a RMB 23.355 billion loss and the takeaway is clear: a company fighting a war still put RMB 26 billion into R&D.

Where the money went: the LongCat model series, the consumer-facing "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan", the merchant-facing AI operations assistant (used by 3.4 million merchants), drones (70 routes, 780,000 orders), autonomous delivery vehicles and dispatch algorithms.

To judge whether that money is well spent, ask one question: is it replacing costs, or adding features? Drones and autonomous vehicles replace rider costs (labour, and rising over the long term); AI dispatch replaces operations staff—both are investments. AI assistants add user experience, which is an expense. On investment mix, the former accounts for the larger share, so the money is not being wasted.

Conclusion

Financial reality: the losses are real, and so is the swing to profit; cash flow runs ahead of profit, and cash exceeds cash flow; the only thing that is not real is the "buyback commitment." To read this company, focus on three numbers: quarterly operating profit, operating cash flow and buyback amount.
