VI. Management: good with numbers, less so with spending — Meituan

Wang Xing's three trade-offs, and one sum left unspent
Research date 2026/08/28  ·  Price HK$77.50 (+0.00%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 478.6 billion (6.175 billion shares)  ·  PE — (net loss of RMB 4.67 billion in H1 2026; PE distorted)

Core view

Wang Xing has made three key trade-offs at Meituan: the 2015 merger with Dianping (to stop burning cash), the 2021 retreat from community group buying (to stop the losses), and the 2025 pushback against involution (to protect UE rather than volume). All three were right, and all three were contractions chosen while others were expanding. That kind of judgment is rare. Yet in June 2026, with the stock "severely undervalued," the same man bought back only HKD 200 million — good at doing the math, not so good at spending the money.

Trade-off one: the 2015 merger with Dianping

Meituan merged with Dianping in October 2015, when the two were burning cash fighting each other in in-store services and losing several billion yuan a year between them. After the merger, in-store services became Meituan's cash cow: much of the 20.9% operating margin at core local commerce in 2024 came from in-store.

The judgment in this deal: Wang Xing saw that "if we keep fighting, both of us die," not "if I hold on a bit longer, I win." Most founders cannot do this — they would rather trust their own execution.

A decade on, this was the most valuable decision in Meituan's history, worth more than any product innovation.

Trade-off two: cutting community group buying in 2021

Community group buying (Meituan Select) was a battleground for the entire industry in 2020–2021, and Meituan invested heavily. It began pulling back in the second half of 2021 and had largely exited by 2023.

The cut was painful: the warehousing, group leaders and supply chain built up in the early phase were almost entirely sunk. Without it, however, losses in 2022–2023 would have been larger—other players (Chengxin Youxuan, Shihuituan) had already dropped out over the same period.

The trade-off exposed a trait in Wang Xing: he is willing to admit mistakes, but he admits them slowly.Community group buying ran for two years before Meituan retreated, and that slowness surfaced again in the 2025 subsidy war—the anti-involution stance was only stated publicly after RMB 17 billion in losses.

Trade-off 3: Anti-involution in 2025

At the peak of the 2025 subsidy war, Meituan chose to defend unit economics rather than volume. The cost: share fell from 65% to 51%, and the company lost RMB 17 billion for the year.

Only in Q2 2026 did that choice prove right. Core local commerce operating profit reached RMB 5.67 billion, up 52.3% YoY, driven by lower user incentives and lower per-order delivery costs. Had Meituan joined the volume war then, losses would now be larger and the recovery slower.

The cost, however, is real: the 14 percentage points of share lost will not come back. This was a trade of market share for profit quality—reasonably priced, but not free.

Unspent cash: HKD 200 million buyback

A USD 2 billion buyback was approved in June 2024, but only about USD 600 million had been executed by November that year. Shareholders reauthorised the programme at the June 2026 meeting, and on June 29–30 the company repurchased 2.9248 million shares for about HKD 200 million at HKD 66.5–69.

That HKD 200 million equals 0.04% of the HKD 478.6 billion market cap, against RMB 168.3 billion of cash and short-term investments on the balance sheet over the same period.This is a token buyback: it proves the company is buying, not that it believes the stock is cheap.

Why no dividend and no aggressive buyback? Either the cash is being reserved to fight in Brazil and fund AI, or the governance structure dictates it — Wang Xing controls 45.3% of voting rights through A/B shares, leaving minority shareholders no lever to push.Either way the conclusion is the same: strip shareholder returns out of the valuation model.

A counterexample: why he does not fight a volume war

The hardest decision of 2025 deserves its own post-mortem.

At the time, Alibaba and JD.com were both buying order volume with subsidies, and consensus was "share first": whoever held the highest share had the network effect and would win long term. By that logic, Meituan should have followed at full force, even at the cost of wider losses.

Wang Xing did not follow. He publicly declared "resolute opposition to involution" and chose to defend unit economics. The result: share fell from 65% to 51%, a full-year loss of RMB 17 billion, and the stock dropped from HKD 106 to HKD 64.

That decision only proved correct a year later: Q2 2026 core local commerce operating profit of RMB 5.67 billion, +52.3%, driven by "reduced user incentive spending." Had Meituan joined the fight then, losses today would be larger, and share would still fall once subsidies stopped — because users bought with subsidies are price-sensitive by nature.

This is a classic "long-termism" decision, but it has one precondition: the company must have enough cash to last until the day it is proven right. The RMB 168.3 billion in cash is that precondition.

The one thing he has never proven

Wang Xing has proven three things: he can shrink (exiting community group buying), he can make trade-offs (anti-involution), and he can show restraint (no Token factory).

What he has not proven: he can spend. Meituan holds RMB 168.3 billion in cash, pays no dividend, has bought back HKD 200 million of stock and has committed USD 1 billion over 5 years to overseas. Taken together, these four data points do not add up to a clear capital allocation framework.

Compare: Tencent pays dividends, buys back stock (2026 buyback in the tens of HKD billion) and distributes JD and Meituan shares to shareholders; Alibaba raises equity to fund AI; Pinduoduo pays no dividend but keeps cash in its most profitable core business. Meituan's problem is not a reluctance to spend—it is that the money has nowhere to go. The core is recovering, new businesses are burning cash, AI is absorbing investment and overseas is a trial run; each needs money, and none yet shows a return.

This is the typical state of a growth-stage company, not a value stock. Its valuation should therefore sit between the two—15 to 20 times, not 10 times or 30 times.

Conclusion

Management verdict: Judgment 9 (all three trade-offs were right), execution 8 (stable organisation, no outside hires), capital allocation 6 (sits on cash, buyback too small), transferability 5 (Wang Xing is 47, but judgment cannot be handed over).

Overall 7. Investor takeaway: you can trust him not to act recklessly, but do not expect him to put shareholders first.
