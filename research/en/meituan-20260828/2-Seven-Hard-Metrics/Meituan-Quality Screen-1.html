Core thesis

Only two and a half of the seven hard metrics pass: gross margin 33.5%, interest-bearing debt at net cash, revenue growth +10.1%. The rest — ROE, net margin, operating cash flow, free cash flow — all fail. But this is not the picture of an inferior company; it is the picture of a company that has just come out of a war. In 2024 operating profit was RMB 45.1 billion and net profit RMB 35.8 billion; a year later these became -RMB 17 billion and -RMB 23.355 billion, on revenue down only 8 points. Negative screening is not about finding perfect companies but about excluding structurally poor businesses: Meituan fails on reversible war damage, not because the business itself does not make money. The conclusion: keep it in the pool, wait for the income statement to repair itself, and do not use the seven failures as an excuse to avoid it.

Which lines fail: all four on the profit side, one on the cash side

ROE, net margin, operating cash flow and free cash flow all fail, and for the same reason: the 2025 subsidy war punched straight through the income statement. Revenue for 2025 was RMB 364.85 billion, up 8.1% YoY, against an operating loss of RMB 17 billion and a net loss of RMB 23.355 billion; operating cash flow was -RMB 13.815 billion, versus +RMB 57.147 billion in 2024. A company that earned RMB 35.8 billion a year earlier and now posts negative ROE and negative cash flow — that swing cannot reflect a change in business quality, only the cost of fighting a war.

The direction of cash flow has, notably, already turned: net operating cash inflow in Q2 2026 was RMB 9.7 billion, 4.5x quarterly net profit of RMB 2.155 billion. Meituan collects before it pays out — users pay upfront, merchants settle later, riders are paid weekly — so as long as order volume holds, operating cash flow naturally leads profit. As of Q2 2026, then, the "operating cash flow fails" criterion no longer holds in practice; only the annual measure has yet to flip.

Why the gross margin only rates a "barely passing" grade

A 33.5% gross margin is not high for an internet platform—Pinduoduo runs at 57.3%, Alibaba e-commerce around 40%, Meituan at just 33.5%—because Meituan books rider costs in cost of sales: it does not sell traffic; it sells "getting the meal into your hands," and more than 60% of that cost is hard cost.

"Barely" comes down to two things. On trend, the cost-of-sales ratio fell just 0.6pp, from 67.1% in 2025Q2 to 66.5%, driven by grocery retail improvement and operating leverage rather than structural price increases. On the floor, gross margin has further to fall if subsidies restart and low-margin self-operated categories (Xiaoxiang, Kuailehou) keep gaining mix. Full-year 2025 is the case in point: revenue grew 8.1%, but gross margin compression was enough to push Core Local Commerce straight into a loss.

But is it fatal: reversible, but it takes time

Only one test determines whether this is fatal: are these failing metrics structural or cyclical? JD.com's first-party retail is the structural case — gross margin is inherently 8%–15% and will never clear the 30% line. Meituan today is the cyclical case. The difference: Meituan's core local commerce operating margin was 20.9% in 2024 and its net margin 10.6% (358 ÷ 3,375); it once cleared both lines with ease.

The cost is time. It still posted a net loss of RMB 4.67 billion in the first half of 2026 and will most likely lose RMB 5–7 billion for the full year (rough math: H1 -RMB 4.67 billion; Q3 weakens sequentially on rider subsidies and heavier in-store investment; Q4 is the peak investment quarter). Annual ROE, net margin and free cash flow therefore will not turn positive until 2027 at the earliest. Failing these metrics will not be fatal, but it leaves the valuation without an anchor until 2027 — which is precisely why this can only be a small position.

What survives the screen: a turnaround candidate with verifiable evidence

The seven hard metrics screen out 90% of companies. Meituan stays on the list not on all seven but on three: revenue is still growing (+10.1%), the balance sheet is still net cash (RMB 168.3 billion in cash and short-term investments), and gross margin has not collapsed (33.5%). Those three ensure it survives until the war ends—a point more important than many realise; in the 2025 battle, the players who faltered first were in fact the smaller-share ones.

So it enters the watchlist on a different basis from the others: not by waiting for it to get cheap, but by waiting for it to become a company that "passes five of seven." The trigger is specific—if in the first half of 2027 we see "core local commerce operating margin back above 12% + annual operating cash flow turning positive + net profit turning positive," at least four of the seven flip automatically. The real use of negative screening on Meituan is as a health check sheet, measured once a quarter.

One more point: why not fall back on PB or PS

Loss-making companies are typically valued on P/B or P/S, and neither fits Meituan. P/B fails because most of its core assets — the rider network, merchant relationships, dispatch algorithm — sit off the balance sheet, so book net assets do not reflect the business. P/S fails because revenue includes low-margin, high-turnover self-operated merchandise sales, which inflates revenue and depresses P/S, making the stock look cheap when it is not.

The only workable method is "normalized P/E + net cash", which is also the approach used in the working papers. The cost is that it rests on an assumption rather than a fact — hence a margin of safety of only 6, not 8.

Conclusion

Two and a half of the seven pass (gross margin, interest-bearing debt, revenue growth); four fail, concentrated in profit and cash flow. Not fatal, because the same asset base earned RMB 35.8 billion in 2024 — the problem is the price war, not the business structure. Nor is it good news: the company will still post a full-year loss, and the four annual metrics will not turn positive until 2027 at the earliest.

How to use it: put it on the "to be repaired" list and measure it against this table every quarter; only when five or more of the seven pass and the price is still below 15x normalized profit is it time to build a heavy position. For now it is a small position to test the thesis, not a pass on quality.
