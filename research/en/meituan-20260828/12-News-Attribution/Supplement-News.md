## Signal one: Q1 results "lost less than expected"

The June 1 quarterly report showed revenue of RMB 91.04 billion, +5.6%, an operating loss of RMB 6.47 billion and an adjusted loss of RMB 4.97 billion. The market had expected a loss of RMB 9.01 billion—the actual loss was only 70% of expectations, the first "better than expected" of the year.

Hong Kong stocks rose about 5% the next day, showing the market understood: losses are narrowing, and narrowing on revenue growth of just 5.6%, driven by expense control. This is the first numerical validation of "anti-involution."

But note one detail: the Q1 operating loss of RMB 6.47 billion narrowed 59.8% from RMB 16.07 billion in Q4 2025. The narrowing was substantial, but the absolute figure remains a negative RMB 6-plus billion. What the market priced in at the time was "no longer deteriorating," not "starting to improve."

## Signal two: the "RMB 2.69 billion" in the Q2 report

After the close on August 28, Q2 results came out: revenue RMB 104.64 billion, +14.4%; operating profit RMB 2.69 billion, versus consensus for a loss of RMB 846 million. From an expected loss of RMB 850 million to an actual profit of RMB 2.69 billion — a swing of RMB 3.5 billion, the largest consensus surprise since 2025.

But the stock closed flat on the day (HKD 77.50, 0% change), because the results were released after the close and the trading session had not yet priced them in. Pricing of these results must wait for the next trading day — which is why the current level (HKD 77.5) is still a level where information is not fully priced.

Core local commerce RMB 5.67 billion, +52.3%, margin 7.9%; new initiatives lost RMB 1.74 billion, loss ratio 5.3% — three metrics improved simultaneously, and that is the part the market will re-rate, not the RMB 2.69 billion itself.

## Noise: three things that were over-interpreted

First, Wang Xing "didn't sell a single share." That is a fact and a statement, but it changes no cash flow. The founder holds 9.3% and 45.3% of voting rights, so there was never any motive to sell — selling one share would send the market a signal. Treating this line as a reason to buy mistakes governance structure for earnings.

Second, the HKD 200 million buyback. 2.9248 million shares, price HKD 66.5–69, 0.04% of market cap. The scale looks more like a "statement buyback" — it proves the company is buying, not that the company thinks the stock is cheap. The real signal is the second half: if cumulative buybacks exceed HKD 2 billion, that is conviction.

Third, the dispute over share metrics. "Meituan 51%," "Meituan 60%," "Taobao Shangou 45.7%" — three numbers from three bases (orders, GTV, revenue), which the media often mix. When you see a share news item, the first question is "which basis," the second is "who calculated it."

## Attribution: what drove the gains over these three months

52-week range HKD 64.25–106.30; now HKD 77.50, -27.1% from the high. From the late-June low, the rebound is about 20%.

Of that 20%, earnings contributed little (the Q1 report only showed a smaller loss, and the Q2 report has yet to be priced in after hours); the rally is mainly about expectations of a "subsidy retreat": reports that Alibaba halved its investment, JD.com pivoting to talk compliance, and regulators framing "anti-involution". In other words, the market is buying "the war is over", not "Meituan is getting stronger".

That distinction matters. If "the war is over" is the driver, then once the war restarts, the entire gain will be given back; if "Meituan is getting stronger" is the driver, the valuation center will shift up. For now only the former can be confirmed, not the latter — which is why we are buying only 2%.

## One piece of information that has not been priced in

The August 28 earnings were released after the close; the stock closed that day at HKD 77.50, up 0.0%. This means the market has not yet priced in "operating profit of RMB 2.69 billion, above the consensus of RMB 3.5 billion."

Compare with the Q1 report on June 1: a loss of RMB 6.47 billion (consensus loss of RMB 9.01 billion), and the stock rose 5% the next day. That was merely "a smaller loss than expected"; this is "a bigger profit than expected" — a different order of magnitude.

So the current level offers an information-gap window. But note: an information gap is worth one day, not one month. If the stock opens up more than 8% on the next trading day, the window closes; if it opens flat or lower, the market is worried about Q3 guidance (management explicitly said costs will rise), which is instead an opportunity.

## Three-month share price structure

Splitting the share-price move from end-May to end-August into three phases shows what the market was trading.

Phase one (early June): a rebound off the lows as the Q1 loss narrowed above the consensus. What rallied here was "no longer deteriorating".

Phase two (late June–July): the stock moved sideways in the HKD 65–70 range while the company bought back shares at HKD 66.5–69. This was "bottoming", with volume shrinking, indicating selling pressure was exhausted.

Phase three (August): the stock rose on Alibaba's earnings and reports of fading industry subsidies, reaching HKD 77.50 on August 28. What rallied here was the expectation that "the war is over".

The three phases together produced a gain of about 20%, but none of them was earnings-driven — the real pricing of earnings is still to come after August 28.
