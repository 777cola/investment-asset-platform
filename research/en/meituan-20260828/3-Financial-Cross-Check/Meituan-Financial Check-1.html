Core thesis

Eight key data points were cross-checked against two sources. Six deviations are within 0.05% (rounding differences between announcements and media). Two deviations exceed 1%, but neither is a data error — they reflect different definitions: First, food delivery share — Goldman Sachs' 51% is on an order-volume basis, while the company's "over 60%" is on a GTV basis; second, net cash — the announced RMB 168.3 billion is gross cash and short-term investments, while the RMB 120 billion used in the working papers is net of interest-bearing debt and restricted funds. Without clear explanation of these two points, the valuation and competitive assessment in the entire report would go astray. Final definitions adopted: financial data use the company's announced original RMB figures; valuation uses net cash of RMB 120 billion; market share is presented on both bases.

Deviation one: 51% and 60% refer to two different things

This is where the entire report is most prone to error. Goldman Sachs' March 2026 research note estimates Meituan's share at about 51% on a daily average order volume basis; Meituan's 2025 annual report, on a GTV (gross transaction value) basis, says it "steadily maintained a market share above 60%." The two figures differ by more than 9 percentage points, yet both are correct.

The reason is structural: Meituan holds an absolute advantage in full-service meals above RMB 30, chain brands and high-ticket orders, and cedes the most ground on low-price orders below RMB 15. The subsidy war targets low-price orders, so by order volume it lost badly; by value it held its ground. This explains another seemingly contradictory phenomenon: share fell 14 percentage points while core local commerce revenue still grew 10.1% year on year — because what it lost were unprofitable orders.

How to handle it: use order-volume share (51%) in the competition chapter and GTV share (60%+) in the profitability chapter, never mix the two anywhere, and never average them. Averaging is the most common mistake, producing a number that is right on neither volume nor value.

Deviation 2: RMB 168.3 billion in cash and RMB 120 billion in net cash

Reported basis: as of June 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents of RMB 104.7 billion and short-term wealth management investments of RMB 63.6 billion, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion. The valuation in the working papers uses "net cash of approximately RMB 120 billion." The gap of about RMB 48.3 billion comes from three items: interest-bearing debt (Meituan has historically issued USD bonds and convertible bonds), restricted funds (merchant deposits, rider security deposits, etc.), and the fact that part of the short-term wealth management is actually operating capital rather than free capital.

Why valuation must use the net figure: calculating EV on a gross basis treats money that does not belong to shareholders as a safety cushion. The gross figure of RMB 168.3 billion looks like 38% of market cap, but if RMB 48 billion of that is interest-bearing debt and restricted funds, the true cushion is only 27%. On the net figure of RMB 120 billion, enterprise value is RMB 320.3 billion, and 2027 normalized profit of RMB 25 billion implies 12.8x; on a gross basis the result would be 10.4x — a 2.4x difference. That is the power of the basis.

Treatment: the balance sheet section states RMB 168.3 billion and lists the components; the valuation section uses RMB 120 billion and notes that it is an estimate. This RMB 120 billion is not a reported figure but an assumption open to challenge, so it is flagged up front.

Final basis adopted and calculation checklist

Financials: all figures use the company's reported RMB original values; media retellings are used only for cross-checking. Quarterly data follow the announcements; Q1 single-quarter figures (revenue RMB 91.039 billion, operating loss RMB 6.470 billion, loss for the period RMB 6.827 billion) come from the Q1 report dated June 1, 2026, and are not back-solved as "half-year minus Q2" — the two reconcile, but the announcement is firmer.

Valuation: share price HKD 77.50, total shares 6.175 billion, market cap HKD 478.56 billion; exchange rate HKD 1 = RMB 0.92; net cash RMB 120 billion (estimated). Two versions of 2027 normalized profit: RMB 25 billion (conservative) and RMB 29 billion (assuming in-store margin recovery).

List of derived items, all flagged: 2025Q2 gross margin 32.9% (back-solved from +0.6pct YoY), core local commerce product sales of about RMB 5.15 billion (segment revenue minus delivery and merchant services, back-solved), annualized cost of new rider occupational injury coverage of about RMB 2.5 billion (rough calculation at 65–75 million orders per day and RMB 0.1 per order), full-year 2026 loss of RMB 5–7 billion (H1 actual plus H2 judgment). None of these four appears in company disclosures; readers can overturn and recompute all of them.

Basis calibration: source hierarchy of the three figures

The figures in this report fall into three tiers, each with a very different level of reliability. The first tier is company announcements (revenue, operating profit, cash flow, cash balance), which can be used directly. The second tier is non-financial metrics disclosed by the company (6 million riders, 8 million merchants, 2,800 counties and districts, GTV share above 60%), which can be used but must be labelled "company-reported". The third tier is third-party estimates (Goldman Sachs' 51% order-volume share, net cash of RMB 120 billion, Taobao Shangou's RMB 100 billion loss over five quarters), which can only serve as reference and must not enter the model.

All estimates in this report (gross-margin back-solving, reverse calculation of product sales, annualised RMB 2.5 billion in new occupational injury costs for riders, full-year 2026 loss of RMB 5–7 billion) are labelled "rough estimate/back-solved", and readers can refute them one by one. This is not false modesty but method: laying out falsifiable assumptions is more useful than offering a precise but false number.

Conclusion

Eight checks, six match, and the two deviations each have a clear accounting-basis explanation. The conclusion is that the data is credible, but watch out for three traps: the unit-volume/value distinction in share, the gross/net distinction in net cash, and the single-quarter/half-year distinction in basis.

Readers need only remember one thing: when you see 51%, think "unit volume"; when you see 60%, think "value"; when you see RMB 168.3 billion, think "gross"; when you see RMB 120 billion, think "valuation assumption". None of these four numbers is wrong; mixing them up makes them all wrong.
