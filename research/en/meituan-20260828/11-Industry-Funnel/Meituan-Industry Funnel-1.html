Core thesis

The funnel runs in three layers: Chinese internet platforms (a dozen or so) → those with local-services/instant fulfillment capability (5) → a final pick of 1. The elimination rationale is hard-edged: JD's food-delivery share fell from a peak of 31% to 7%, with new businesses losing RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter; Pinduoduo has no fulfillment network and does not do this business; Douyin lacks fulfillment capability and can only capture in-store; Alibaba is qualified, but lost roughly RMB 100 billion in instant retail over five quarters while generating only RMB 33–35 billion in incremental revenue — that money clearly should go to AI. That leaves only Meituan — the only company in this industry that made real money before the subsidy war, with a 20.9% core local commerce operating margin in 2024. The answer to "why this one" is not that it is the strongest, but that it understands this business's cost structure best.

First-pass screen: who runs the same business

Only five companies market-wide can offer "instant delivery within three kilometers": Meituan, Alibaba (Taobao Shangou + Ele.me + Freshippo), JD.com (JD Food Delivery + Dada), Douyin (in-store only, no delivery fulfillment) and Pinduoduo (which does not do it at all).

That layer alone eliminates two of them. Pinduoduo is the most profitable company in e-commerce, with a gross margin of 57.3% and ROE around 30%, but it has no riders, no merchant ground team and no willingness to build either — it earns money from "goods finding people," not from "people finding goods, delivered in thirty minutes"; the two capabilities are not interchangeable. Douyin has traffic, content and in-store, but no fulfillment network; its in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion in 2025, taking the fattest profit pool from Meituan, but it cannot take food delivery.

Second-layer screen: why JD.com drops out

JD.com is the only case of "getting everything right yet still unable to win." It had Dada's delivery capacity, a quality-first brand perception and a compliance edge from its July 17 announcement of 100% full-time rider labour contracts plus social insurance and housing fund contributions—the direction was right, yet its share fell from a peak of 31% to 7% and new businesses lost about RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter.

Its exit from the game is structural: JD.com has no merchant supply. Meituan's 8 million merchants were built over a decade of ground-level outreach; JD.com would have to start negotiating from zero, and the merchants it signs already have steady orders on Meituan, leaving them no reason to switch platforms.Food delivery is a business where "merchant density determines user experience," not one where "logistics capability determines experience"—JD.com applied its strength in the wrong place.

On valuation, JD.com is cheaper (single-digit P/E, cash at nearly 90% of market cap), but cheapness alone is not a reason to own it. Buying a company that spent two years on the wrong battlefield and lost about RMB 40 billion, betting it will "return to its core retail business," is a different story.

Third-round final pick: Alibaba is the only rival, but its capital has better places to go

Alibaba is the only company with the wherewithal to fight Meituan to the end: 800 million users on its main site, Ele.me's delivery capacity base, Hema's fresh-grocery supply chain, and e-commerce profits to fund subsidies. That combination is precisely why Taobao Shangou reached over 40% order-volume share within a year.

Yet it has also given its own exit signal. Media estimates put Taobao Shangou's cumulative losses at about RMB 100 billion over five quarters; after stripping out existing Ele.me and Hema revenue, incremental revenue was only RMB 33–35 billion; in August 2026 it raised HKD 80 billion in a placement, all directed to AI infrastructure; and according to 36Kr, investment in Taobao Shangou will be halved in the new fiscal year. When a company puts RMB 80 billion into A and halves its investment in B at the same time, it has already set its priorities.

So Alibaba is not "unable to win" — it "ran the numbers." The implication for Meituan: its most dangerous rival has voluntarily downgraded this war. This is the most important industry change of August 2026, and the premise on which this investment rests.

Why Meituan: three irreplaceable strengths

First, it is the only company in this industry that has ever made real money. In 2024, core local commerce operating margin was 20.9%, group operating profit was RMB 45.1 billion and net profit was RMB 35.8 billion. Profitability cannot be faked—it proves this cost structure holds up in steady state.

Second, it defended share on "value share." Order volume share of 51% and GTV share of above 60% show it lost low-ticket orders and held high-ticket ones. That determines its unit economics are better than rivals': on the same order, Meituan earns on a RMB 30 average ticket while rivals earn on a RMB 15 ticket.

Third, its valuation has a floor. Market cap of HKD 478.6 billion, net cash of RMB 120 billion and normalized profit of RMB 25–29 billion in 2027 correspond to 15–18x, or 11–13x ex-cash. This price does not require it to become great, only to stop getting worse.

Taken together, it is the only name in this funnel that can "win, stay cheap and be understood." Others either win but are expensive (Alibaba) or are cheap but lose (JD.com).

One flaw in the funnel

This funnel carries a methodological flaw that must be stated up front: it can only choose among companies already listed and already in the same business—it cannot choose "the best business model."

If the goal is "the best business," the first layer of the funnel should not be Chinese internet platforms but "all-market high-ROE companies"—and on that screen Meituan would not even make the candidate list, given its negative ROE.

The funnel is therefore correctly used as follows: first decide "I want exposure to local life services," then select companies within that industry. It answers "who to buy in this industry," not "whether to invest in this industry"—the latter is an asset allocation question, addressed in m14.

Conflating the two leads to the conclusion "Meituan is inferior to Moutai"—correct, but useless, because whoever buys Meituan was never looking for Moutai.

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: Five went in, one came out. The one eliminated was not a bad company but a mismatched one—JD.com lost on merchants, Pinduoduo on capability, Douyin on fulfilment, Alibaba on priority.

Meituan won for a simple reason: it is the only company that has turned "within three kilometres, in thirty minutes" into a business. Buying it means buying this cost structure coming back into force once subsidies recede, not buying a bigger story.
