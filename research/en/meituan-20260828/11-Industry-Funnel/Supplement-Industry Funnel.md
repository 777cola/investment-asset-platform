## First-pass screen: who runs the same business

Across the whole market, only five companies can offer "instant delivery within three kilometers": Meituan, Alibaba (Taobao Shangou + Ele.me + Freshippo), JD.com (JD Food Delivery + Dada), Douyin (in-store, no delivery fulfillment), and Pinduoduo (does not do it).

This layer alone eliminates two. Pinduoduo is the most profitable company in e-commerce, with a gross margin of 57.3% and ROE around 30%, but it has no riders, no merchant ground team, and no willingness to do this — **it earns money from "goods finding people," not from "people finding goods, delivered in thirty minutes"; the two capabilities are not interchangeable.** Douyin has traffic, content, and in-store, but no fulfillment network; its in-store GTV exceeded RMB 800 billion in 2025, taking the fattest profit pool from Meituan, but it cannot take food delivery.

## Second-layer screen: why JD.com drops out

JD.com is the only case of "getting everything right yet still unable to win." It has Dada's delivery capacity, a quality-first brand perception, and a compliance edge from its July 17 announcement of 100% full-time rider labor contracts plus social insurance and housing fund contributions—**the direction was right, but its share fell from a peak of 31% to 7%, and new businesses lost about RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter.**

The reason it is out of the game is structural: JD.com has no merchant supply. Meituan's 8 million merchants were built over a decade of ground-level outreach; JD.com would have to start negotiating from zero, and merchants it signs already have steady orders on Meituan, giving them no reason to switch platforms.**Food delivery is a business where "merchant density determines user experience," not one where "logistics capability determines experience"—JD.com applied its strength in the wrong place.**

On valuation, JD.com is cheaper (single-digit PE, cash at nearly 90% of market cap), but cheapness is not a reason to pick it. Buying a company that spent two years on the wrong battlefield and lost about RMB 40 billion, betting it will "return to its core retail business," is a different story.

## Third-round final pick: Alibaba is the only rival, but its capital has better places to go

Alibaba is the only company with the wherewithal to fight Meituan to the end: 800 million users on its main site, Ele.me's delivery capacity base, Hema's fresh-grocery supply chain, and e-commerce profits to fund subsidies. Taobao Shangou reached over 40% order-volume share in a year precisely because of this combination.

But it has given its own exit signal. According to media estimates, Taobao Shangou lost about RMB 100 billion cumulatively over five quarters; after stripping out existing Ele.me and Hema revenue, incremental revenue was only RMB 33–35 billion; in August 2026 it raised HKD 80 billion in a placement, all directed to AI infrastructure; and according to 36Kr, investment in Taobao Shangou will be halved in the new fiscal year. **When a company puts RMB 80 billion into A and halves its investment in B at the same time, it has already set its priorities.**

So Alibaba is not "unable to win" — it "ran the numbers." The implication for Meituan: its most dangerous rival has voluntarily downgraded this war. This is the most important industry change of August 2026, and the premise on which this investment rests.

## Why Meituan: three irreplaceable strengths

First, it is the only company in this industry that has ever made real money. In 2024, core local commerce operating margin was 20.9%, group operating profit was RMB 45.1 billion, and net profit was RMB 35.8 billion. **Profitability cannot be faked—it proves this cost structure holds up in steady state.**

Second, it defended share on "value share." Order volume share of 51% and GTV share of above 60% show it lost low-ticket orders and held high-ticket ones. This determines that its unit economics are better than rivals'—on the same order, Meituan earns on a RMB 30 average ticket while rivals earn on a RMB 15 ticket.

Third, its valuation has a floor. Market cap of HKD 478.6 billion, net cash of RMB 120 billion, and normalized profit of RMB 25–29 billion in 2027 correspond to 15–18x, or 11–13x ex-cash. **This price does not require it to become great, only to stop getting worse.**

Taken together: it is the only name in this funnel that can "win, stay cheap, and be understood." Others either win but are expensive (Alibaba) or are cheap but lose (JD.com).

## One flaw in the funnel

This funnel has a methodological flaw that must be stated: **it can only select among companies that are already listed and in the same business—it cannot select "the best business model."**

If the goal is "the best business," the first layer of the funnel should not be Chinese internet platforms but rather "all-market high-ROE companies"—in which case Meituan would not even make the candidate list (negative ROE).

So the correct use of this funnel is: **first decide "I want to invest in the local life services industry," then select companies within that industry.** It answers "who to buy in this industry," not "whether to invest in this industry." The latter is an asset allocation question, answered in m14.

Conflating these two questions leads to the conclusion "Meituan is inferior to Moutai"—correct, but useless, because whoever buys Meituan was never looking for Moutai.
