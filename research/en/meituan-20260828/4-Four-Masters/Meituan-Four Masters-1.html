Core view

The four reach a rare consensus: it is a good business, the company is the strongest player, but the price is only "reasonable", not "cheap". Duan Yongping asks whether users still open it — the answer is yes; after subsidies receded, order mix and frequency are both improving. Buffett asks whether the moat requires annual spending to maintain — it does, and in 2025 it consumed a RMB 62.1 billion profit gap. Munger flips the question to what would halve it again — all three paths remain open, but rivals' wallets are not unlimited either. Li Lu takes the ten-year view — instant retail is an efficiency dividend unique to China; the question is whether the endgame is one dominant player or three dividing the spoils. The four conclude: you can buy, but only in a small position, because you are betting on when rivals stop — and that you cannot control.

Duan Yongping: Do users still open it?

When Duan Yongping looks at a company, he does not look at profit first; he looks at "why users still use it." Meituan's answer is clear: delivery within three kilometers in thirty minutes, across 2,800 counties and cities nationwide — today only Meituan does this most reliably. The harder evidence lies in user behavior: in 2026Q2, Shanguong old-user frequency rose, order structure and user structure improved, and delivery service revenue returned to positive growth at +13.1% year on year — the subsidies receded, but users did not leave. This shows that some of the share bought over the past two years has genuinely stayed.

He would also credit management's trade-off during the fiercest subsidy period: not fighting an order-volume war, and defending unit economics. This fits "doing the right thing" — doing so while knowing share would be lost requires a bit of integrity. But he would certainly add: a good business also needs a good price. HKD 77.5 does not correspond to a deep discount; it corresponds to "fair."

He would probably end with one question: if the stock fell to HKD 50 tomorrow, would you want to buy more or would you be more afraid? If the answer is you would want to buy more, then you can start buying a little now; if the answer is fear, it means you do not yet understand this business, and you should not buy at HKD 77.5 either.

Buffett: Does a moat need annual spending to maintain?

Buffett would like the three-sided network effect: more merchants bring more users; more users bring more riders; more riders bring faster delivery. This is a classic virtuous cycle, and once built, marginal costs decline. In Q2 2026, core local commerce operating profit was RMB 5.67 billion, up 52.3% year on year — this curve at work.

But he would immediately spot a problem: this moat requires several tens of billions of yuan a year to maintain. In 2025, sales and marketing expenses rose sharply with the subsidy war, and group operating profit swung from +RMB 45.1 billion in 2024 to -RMB 17 billion, a gap of RMB 62.1 billion. See's Candies does not need this — it can raise prices every year, whereas Meituan's pricing power sits with consumers, competitors and regulators.

So he would apply a lower multiple: 15–20x, not the 30x of a growth stock. On 2027 normalized profit of RMB 25–29 billion plus net cash, 20x implies about HKD 110. The current HKD 77.5 sits at the low end of that range — a "can start buying" level, not a "should go heavy" level.

He would also look at one more thing: whether management is willing to buy its own stock when it is cheap. The buyback was approved in June 2026, and on June 29–30 the company spent HKD 200 million to buy 2.92 million shares. The direction is right, but against a HKD 478.6 billion market cap the number is too small. On this item, he would raise a question mark.

Munger: What would make it halve again?

Munger's habit was to invert the question: for HKD 77.5 to fall to HKD 45, what has to happen? Three paths, none of them low-probability.

First, Alibaba treats instant retail as a strategic-level investment and fights for another two years, with industry monthly subsidies returning to the 2025 peak. The evidence is already there: Taobao Shangou entered in 2025 and within a year pushed order volume above 40%, relying on main-app traffic plus subsidies, and the math works — losses in food delivery can be covered by e-commerce advertising.

Second, regulators come down hard on rider costs and algorithmic pricing, permanently lifting per-order costs by more than RMB 1. This path is already underway: from 1 July 2026, the new occupational injury insurance for riders covers the whole country, with premiums fully borne by the platforms; in December 2025 the State Administration for Market Regulation issued the recommended national standard "Basic Requirements for Food Delivery Platform Service Management", listing rider rights protection as a core requirement. At 70 million orders a day and RMB 1 more per order, that is roughly RMB 25 billion a year — more than normalized profit in 2027.

Third, Keeta repeats the Didi-style cash burn in Brazil, where USD 1 billion over five years is only the entry ticket.

But Munger would also look at the other side: Alibaba has just raised HKD 80 billion, all of it going to AI infrastructure rather than more food delivery subsidies; Taobao Shangou's investment is reportedly halved in the new fiscal year. The opponent's wallet is not unlimited either. That is why an inflection point may hold — not because Meituan got stronger, but because the opponent has done the math and concluded it does not pay.

Li Lu: China's unique efficiency dividend, and its endgame problem

Li Lu would place it on a longer time scale. Delivering within three kilometers in thirty minutes depends on three things: high-density cities, low-cost and flexible labor, and mature mobile payment and map infrastructure. Only China has all three at once. So instant retail is not a fake demand propped up by subsidies; it is a structural efficiency dividend — which is why its penetration in China is far higher than in Europe and the US.

Can the same model be exported? Keeta gives a preliminary answer: Hong Kong turned UE positive in Q4 2025, Saudi Arabia turned profitable in July 2026 in only 22 months, and Saudi Arabia already accounts for about 25% of Keeta's daily orders. Reaching profitability in 22 months matters more than the money it makes — it proves the dispatch system and ground-game playbook can be replicated across cultures.

His doubt lies in the endgame: after all three stop subsidizing and return to rational competition, will the industry be "one dominant player taking excess profit" or "three players splitting average profit"? Meituan is worth HKD 200 in the former case and only HKD 80 in the latter. Buying now is essentially a bet on the former. And his experience says most Chinese industries end in the second outcome, unless the winner has a moat beyond network effects — Meituan does: fulfillment density.

Odds assessment: 1:1.8 — worth buying, not worth a heavy position

Lay out the probabilities of the three scenarios: bear 30% (HKD 60, -23%), base 50% (HKD 110, +42%), bull 20% (HKD 150, +94%). Expected return = 0.3×(-23%) + 0.5×(+42%) + 0.2×(+94%) = +32.9%; downside 23%, upside 42% (base case), odds of about 1:1.8.

What do those odds mean? They mean the bet is worth taking, but not worth taking big. At 1:1.8, a 2%–3% position is reasonable; to go above 8%, the odds need to reach 1:3, which implies a share price near HKD 65.

Here is the point of disagreement: one camp values the company on its 2026 losses and finds it unreasonably expensive; the other values it on normalized 2027 profit and finds it unreasonably cheap. Neither camp is wrong — the only difference is whether "normalized" actually materializes. And the Q3 results will give the first answer to that.

The shared blind spot of the four

All four missed one thing: Meituan's "unallocated items" loss is widening. About -RMB 1.24 billion in 2026Q2 and -RMB 3.56 billion in the first half, widening 57.2% year on year, mainly on AI investment. This sits in no segment and in no one's analytical framework.

The implication: Meituan's income statement contains a "black box" that consumes roughly RMB 7 billion a year, with no visible return in the near term. If AI investment keeps escalating (R&D was already +22.5% in 2026Q2), the black box grows. All four modeled "how much the core business can earn"; none modeled "how much headquarters and AI will spend" — the most easily overlooked gap in this consensus.

Adding this back, the neutral-case assumption of RMB 25 billion in profit is in fact optimistic: if unallocated items still lose more than RMB 5 billion in 2027, core local commerce must earn an extra RMB 5 billion to offset it, implying an operating margin of 14%, not 12.5%.

Conclusion

Four-person panel: Duan Yongping looks at the business, Buffett looks at the moat but compresses the multiple, Munger looks at risk, Li Lu looks at long-term space—not one of the four says "don't buy," and not one says "heavy position." This is fully consistent with the working paper's "Buy, small position in tranches"; no rating change needed.

Operationally only two variables remain: price and evidence. At HKD 77.5 the odds are 1:1.8, size 2%–3%; if it falls to HKD 65 the odds become 1:3, add to 5%–6%. On evidence, watch whether Q3 core local commerce operating profit holds at RMB 2 billion. Add if either appears; if neither appears, wait.
