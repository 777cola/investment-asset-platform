## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Meituan was founded by Wang Xing in 2010, merged with Dianping in 2015 and listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in September 2018 (IPO price HKD 69). As of August 28, 2026, the shares traded at HKD 77.50 on total share capital of 6.175 billion shares, for a market cap of HKD 478.6 billion — down 27.1% from the 52-week high of HKD 106.30 and above the 52-week low of HKD 64.25.

Two segments: Core local commerce (food delivery, Meituan Instashopping, in-store hotel and travel; 2026Q2 revenue RMB 71.53 billion, 68.4% of total) and New initiatives (Xiaoxiang Supermarket, Happy Monkey, Kuailv, overseas Keeta; revenue RMB 33.11 billion, 31.6%). A separate unallocated line covers mainly AI investment and headquarters costs.

Scale: about 6 million riders with orders and about 8 million active merchants, covering 2,800 counties and districts; 2025 revenue was RMB 364.85 billion with a net loss of RMB 23.355 billion. Meituan operates the world's largest on-demand delivery network and ranks first in China's local life services, with GTV share above 60%.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

Local life services splits into two parts: in-store (group buying, travel and hospitality) and at-home (food delivery, instant retail). Restaurant sector growth falls to around 5% in 2026; in-store stays flat on offline consumption, while at-home keeps expanding—instant retail moves from "emergency" to "daily", with categories widening from pharmaceuticals and alcohol to supermarket daily goods.

Industry structure was disrupted in 2025: JD.com entered food delivery in February, Taobao Shangou launched in April, and the three players entered a subsidy war. The result is simultaneous losses for all three: Meituan posted an operating loss of RMB 17 billion in 2025, Alibaba lost about RMB 100 billion on instant retail over five quarters, and JD.com's new businesses lost about RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter.

2026 brings a turning point: Alibaba's August placement of HKD 80 billion directed at AI infrastructure, with Taobao Shangou's new-fiscal-year investment reported to be halved; JD.com pivots to full-time rider compliance; regulators issued the national standard "Basic Requirements for Food Delivery Platform Service Management" in December 2025, setting the tone of "anti-involution". The industry shifts from "grabbing share" back to "doing the math".

## Chapter 3 Business model

Three revenue lines: delivery services (RMB 26.78 billion in 2026Q2, +13.1%), comprising delivery fees charged to users and fulfilment service fees charged to merchants; merchant services (RMB 39.60 billion, +4.0%), comprising commissions and advertising; and goods sales (about RMB 5.15 billion, +78.9%), self-operated pharmaceuticals and liquor. New initiatives of RMB 33.11 billion are grocery retail plus overseas.

Cost structure: cost of sales ratio 66.5% (rider costs the largest component), marketing expense ratio 23.6%, R&D expense ratio 7.3%. Gross profit on a RMB 30 food-delivery order is RMB 1–2; fixed costs are spread across massive order volume.

The cash-flow structure is this business's hidden advantage: users pay first, merchants are settled later, riders are paid weekly, and the platform holds the float. Operating cash flow was +RMB 9.7 billion in 2026Q2, 4.5x net profit. But float cuts both ways—it reverses into an outflow when order volume declines, as in 2025, when operating cash flow was -RMB 13.815 billion.

By segment: Core Local Commerce 2026Q2 revenue RMB 71.53 billion, operating profit RMB 5.67 billion, margin 7.9% (FY24: 20.9%; 2025: -RMB 6.9 billion). New Initiatives Q2 loss RMB 1.74 billion, loss ratio narrowing from 7.1% to 5.3%. Unallocated items Q2 loss of about RMB 1.24 billion, up 57.2% YoY on AI investment.

Balance sheet: as of June 30, 2026, cash and equivalents RMB 104.7 billion plus short-term wealth management products RMB 63.6 billion, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion; net cash of about RMB 120 billion after deducting interest-bearing debt and restricted funds (estimate).Net cash position, no debt risk.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Four drivers, ranked by certainty.

First, instant retail (highest certainty). In Q2 2026 merchandise sales rose 78.0%, frequency among existing Shanguo users increased, and Xiaoxiang Supermarket expanded from 39 cities to 68. The category mix widened from emergency items to daily necessities, and rising frequency dilutes fulfilment cost.

Second, margin recovery (largest upside). Core local commerce margin recovers from 7.9% to 12%–13%, lifting profit from RMB 37.5 billion to RMB 45 billion, on subsidy retreat plus operating leverage; no new growth is required.

Third, overseas (optionality). Keeta turned UE-positive in Hong Kong in Q4 2025 and profitable in Saudi Arabia in July 2026 (22 months), has covered five Middle East countries and is already operating in Brazil. This driver determines the bull case.

Fourth, AI cost reduction (long term). R&D was RMB 7.67 billion in Q2 2026, +22.5%; drones fly 70 routes and 780,000 orders, alongside autonomous vehicles and AI dispatch. Near term this is expense; long term it may replace rider cost.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

By order volume (Goldman Sachs, March 2026, as of February 2026): Meituan about 51%, Taobao Shangou about 42%, JD.com about 7%. By GTV (company 2025 annual reports): Meituan above 60%. The two measures differ by more than 9 percentage points, reflecting Meituan's defence of high-ticket orders and the loss of low-price ones.

The three pursue different strategies: Meituan defends unit economics and avoids an order-volume war; Taobao Shangou trades main-app traffic plus subsidies for share, but halves investment in the new fiscal year; JD.com entered via quality food delivery, saw share fall back to 7%, and pivoted to a compliance card. The fourth force is Douyin, which does not handle delivery fulfillment and focuses on in-store — in 2025 in-store GTV grew nearly 60%, exceeding RMB 800 billion.

Endgame view: steady-state share of 6:3:1 (GTV), industry average operating margin of 10%–15%, versus 20.9% in 2024. This is the core reason for a 20x rather than 30x multiple.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

Five main risks, ranked by "probability × impact".

Rider costs step up again (40% probability): new occupational injury insurance covers the whole country from 2026/7/1, with the platform bearing the full premium, and JD's full-time riders already receive the five social insurances and one housing fund. A RMB 0.5–1 increase in per-order cost within three years equates to RMB 10–25 billion annualized.

In-store eroded by Douyin (35%): in-store is the fattest profit pool, and Douyin is carving into it with low commissions plus content traffic. Merchant services revenue growing only 4.0% in 2026Q2 is already a signal.

Subsidy war restarts (30%): Alibaba's instant retail losses widen again, or JD ups the ante. If it restarts, 2027 profit falls from RMB 25 billion to RMB 10 billion.

Keeta overseas expansion out of control (30%): Brazil quarterly loss exceeds RMB 2 billion, with additional investment.

Share keeps sliding (20%): order volume share falls below 45%.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

Method: sum-of-the-parts profit plus net cash. 2027 net profit of RMB 25 billion × 20x = RMB 500 billion, plus net cash of RMB 120 billion = RMB 620 billion; converted to HKD 673.9 billion and divided by 6.175 billion shares gives HKD 109, rounded to HKD 110.

Bear case: net profit of RMB 10 billion × 22x + RMB 120 billion = RMB 340 billion, or HKD 369.6 billion → HKD 60. Bull case: RMB 35 billion × 21x + RMB 120 billion = RMB 855 billion, or HKD 929.3 billion → HKD 150.

Sensitivity: each 1 percentage point change in core local commerce margin moves 2027 net profit by about RMB 2.5 billion, and at 20x that moves the target price by about HKD 9; each 1x change in the multiple moves the target price by about HKD 4.4 (RMB 25 billion × 1x ÷ 0.92 ÷ 6.175). Margin has twice the impact of the multiple—which is why this report devotes the most space to whether margins can recover.

## Chapter 13 Differences from Market Consensus

Sell-side views on Meituan diverge on two points: 2027 profit and the multiple to assign. Consensus looks for roughly RMB 28–32 billion of net profit in 2027 and a target price of HKD 120–140; my RMB 25 billion and HKD 110 sit one notch below.

Where is the gap? Core local commerce margin. The market broadly assumes a return to 15% in 2027 (from 20.9% in 2024); I assume 12.5%. The 2.5pp difference maps to about RMB 7.5 billion of profit and about HKD 13 of target price.

I am conservative for three reasons. In-store faces Douyin competition, making a return to the 2024 margin difficult. Rider costs face three step-ups (pension, new occupational injury insurance and possibly full social insurance), weighing on margins. Merchant services revenue grows only 4.0%, implying commission rates cannot rise. All three are structural constraints, not cyclical swings.

If the market is right (margin back to 15%), the target price is HKD 128; if I am right (12.5%), HKD 110. The difference does not affect the Buy call, only how much to buy.

## Chapter 15 Valuation Sensitivity

Two variables drive the target price: the core local commerce operating margin and the multiple the market is willing to pay. A matrix is more useful than a single point.

Our assumptions: 2027 core local commerce revenue of RMB 300 billion, new initiatives -RMB 3 billion, unallocated -RMB 5 billion, a 15% tax rate, net cash of RMB 120 billion and 6.175 billion shares.

- 8% margin, 15x: operating profit RMB 16 billion, net profit about RMB 13.6 billion, target price about HKD 57;

- 10% margin, 18x: operating profit RMB 22 billion, net profit about RMB 18.7 billion, target price about HKD 80;

- 12.5% margin, 20x: operating profit RMB 29.5 billion, net profit about RMB 25 billion, target price about HKD 110 (neutral);

- 15% margin, 22x: operating profit RMB 37 billion, net profit about RMB 31.5 billion, target price about HKD 143.

The second row matters most: if margin only recovers to 10% and the market only pays 18x, the target price is HKD 80 — essentially where it is now. In other words, HKD 77.5 already prices in a "moderate recovery to 10%"; the payoff comes from "recovery above 12.5%", not from "recovery itself".

That also explains why the RMB 2 billion quarterly earnings floor matters so much: it corresponds to an annualized margin of roughly 10% — below that, even a "moderate recovery" does not hold.

## Chapter 18 Guide to Using the Report

This report serves three types of readers, each with a different use for it.

The first is those preparing to buy. Read Chapters 11, 12 and 16: at what price to buy, how much to buy, and what to watch in the next earnings report. Return to the earlier chapters when the stock price fluctuates—if it falls to HKD 65, reread the risk warnings in Chapter 8, not the conclusion.

The second is those who already hold. Read Chapters 13, 14 and 15: your cost basis does not matter; what matters is where consensus differs from our view. If your assumptions are more optimistic than this report (15% margin), watch the in-store data; if more pessimistic, watch rider costs.

The third is those who do not plan to buy. Read Chapters 5, 7 and 9: what kind of company this is, what its moat is worth, and how its profit will trend. After reading, you may agree that not buying is right—because the return on this investment comes mainly from multiple re-rating, not from the compounding of the business itself.Not buying a company that needs its competitors to make mistakes to make money is a reasonable bias.
