Core thesis

An "option-like" setup: 23% downside (HKD 60 bear case) against 42% upside (HKD 110 base case), a 1:1.8 payoff, with a 2%–3% position sized to buy the possibility of "margin recovery." Position discipline matters more than stock selection here, because the volatility comes from competitors' moves rather than from the company itself. Three price levels: build 2% at HKD 77.5, add to 4%–5% at HKD 65, then after the Q3 results decide whether to add to 5% or cut to 1%.

Why 2%, not 5%

Run the numbers. A 2% position with base case upside of 42% adds 0.84% to the portfolio; bear case downside of 23% costs 0.46%. Positive expectancy, low impact: right, and you make 0.84%; wrong, and you lose 0.46% — and you will know the answer within three months.

At a 5% position: right, +2.1%; wrong, -1.15%. Returns double, but so does the contribution to portfolio volatility, and this stock's volatility sits far above the portfolio average (52-week range 64.25–106.30, a 65% swing).

My principle: cap positions where the uncertainty is external (competitor behaviour) at 3%; positions where the uncertainty is internal (execution) can run to 5%–8%. Meituan falls in the former camp — its profit is not its own to decide.

Three price levels: 77.5, 65, 55

HKD 77.5 is the "information price": earnings beat consensus but landed after the close, so the market has yet to price them. Buying here is a bet on a next-day re-rating.

HKD 65 is the "value price": about 12.8x 2027 normalized profit and about 9x ex-cash, which skews risk-reward to 1:3. This level also brackets the June 2026 buyback range (HKD 66.5–69) and the 52-week low (HKD 64.25) — the company itself has endorsed this price.

HKD 55 is the "thesis-invalidation price": the bear case (2027 net profit of RMB 10 billion) at a 10% discount. Here the market has stopped believing the recovery story, or new bad news has surfaced. Not a level to add; a level to reassess.

Which holdings does it clash with in the portfolio?

For a portfolio holding both Alibaba and JD.com, Meituan is a natural hedge: as subsidy intensity rises, Meituan's profit falls, Alibaba gains instant retail share (albeit with wider losses), and JD.com's new-business losses widen. All three react to the same variable—subsidy intensity—but in different directions, and the magnitude is significant for each.

Correlation with Tencent is moderate (both are Hong Kong-listed platforms carrying Hang Seng Tech beta, with a correlation coefficient of about 0.6–0.7); with Pinduoduo it is low (different track, different market); with Kuaishou it is moderate (both are affected by Douyin competition, but one is in-store services and the other advertising).

Portfolio-level implication: Meituan's 2% cannot simply be added to the Alibaba and JD.com positions to read as "8% China internet," because the three are negatively correlated on the same battlefield. If a combined figure is required, keeping total exposure across Meituan + Alibaba + JD.com within 12% is safer.

Rebalancing discipline: sell because the thesis has changed, not because the price has risen

The most common mistake with this position is "taking profit at 30%." If Q3 confirms the thesis and 2027 profit really returns to RMB 25 billion, HKD 110 is only the neutral target, with the optimistic HKD 150 still above it. The reason to sell should be "the thesis is disproven" or "valuation is fully realized," not "unrealized gains."

The corresponding discipline: at HKD 110 (neutral target), cut half; at HKD 150 (optimistic target), exit fully. At HKD 65, add; at HKD 55, reassess rather than mechanically adding.

Time stop: if the March 2027 annual report shows a full-year 2026 loss exceeding RMB 7 billion, cut to 1% even if the stock has not fallen—because that means the recovery slope is one year slower than assumed, and time has a cost.

Why not hedge

Some will ask: Meituan is negatively correlated with Alibaba and JD.com in the same battlefield, so why not pair them as a hedge?

Because the hedge does not work. The three stocks do not react symmetrically to "subsidy intensity": when subsidies rise, Meituan falls the most (small profit base), Alibaba falls less (cushioned by e-commerce profits) and JD.com falls the least (new businesses are a small share). Hedging Meituan with Alibaba is like hedging a small boat with a big ship — the direction is right, but the ratio can never be tuned correctly.

The more practical approach is to control total exposure: Meituan at 2%, with Alibaba and JD.com combined not exceeding 12%. Manage "China internet local life" as an asset class, not as three independent stocks.

Simpler still: do not buy Alibaba and JD.com. If you already own Meituan, there is no need to buy its rivals — that just buys the same risk twice.

Conclusion

Portfolio recommendation: initiate a 2% position, add to 4%–5% at HKD 65, let Q3 earnings decide the outcome, halve at HKD 110, exit fully at HKD 150. This is a trade with a defined timetable, not a long-term core holding.

One final reminder: do not buy it on margin. This stock's volatility comes from competitors' actions, and competitors' actions cannot be predicted—a 2% position can absorb a -30% move; a levered 2% position cannot.
