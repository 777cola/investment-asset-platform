Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — Meituan

What is this business really selling, and why can't users do without it?

Core view

Meituan does not sell traffic; it sells the certainty of "within three kilometers, within thirty minutes." It slices riders' time into minutes and sells them, earning the spread on fulfillment efficiency. In good times this business earns a 20.9% operating margin (core local commerce in 2024); in bad times it can lose as much as -2.6% (2025). The difference is not the business itself, but whether anyone is willing to lose money to take its orders.

Why do users still open it?

There is only one answer to this question: certainty. When you want a bowl of noodles at ten at night, only one platform nationwide can guarantee delivery in thirty minutes, with full coverage across 2,800 counties and cities. This is not a pricing question; it is a capability question. Subsidies can get someone to place one order, but not the next one—2026Q2 data proves this: as subsidies receded, order frequency among existing Shanguo users actually rose, and delivery service revenue returned to positive growth of +13.1%.

The essence of the business is "trading fulfillment density for time value." The higher the density, the lower the per-order cost, the faster the delivery, the better the user experience, the more orders—a virtuous cycle. But this cycle only holds within one's own city and one's own trade area; three kilometers out, it resets to zero. That is why Meituan must build county by county—grueling work that others are unwilling to do.

Evidence for and against a good business

Evidence: in 2024, core local commerce operating margin was 20.9%, group operating profit was RMB 45.1 billion, and net profit was RMB 35.8 billion; in Q2 2026, quarterly operating profit turned positive at RMB 2.69 billion, with core local commerce at RMB 5.67 billion, up 52.3% year on year. A business that needs little capital expenditure, collects cash before it pays out, and still earns a 20% operating margin is a good business.

Counter-evidence: in 2025, the group posted an operating loss of RMB 17 billion and a net loss of RMB 23.355 billion, even as revenue grew 8.1%. The same business line went from +RMB 45.1 billion to -RMB 17 billion in just four quarters. This shows pricing power does not sit with the company — if rivals are willing to lose money, it must lose money too, or cede share.

So the verdict on this business: a good business, but without pricing power. It earns the money of efficiency, not the money of monopoly. Efficiency can be accumulated; monopoly cannot be counted on.

Benfen: Is management doing the right thing?

Duan Yongping talks about "benfen" (doing one's duty). For Meituan it comes down to two things: execute fulfillment well and don't shortchange users; don't do things that damage the ecosystem for the sake of short-term numbers.

The first is done: rider pension insurance subsidies now cover the whole country, the new occupational injury insurance rolled out nationwide on July 1, 2026 with the platform bearing the full premium, and transparent kitchens plus the "Xingmou" food-safety large model went live. These are costs, not publicity. Spending on fulfillment and compliance is benfen.

The second is only partly done: in the 2025 subsidy war Meituan chose to "oppose involution" and protect unit economics, and that trade-off was right; but the 2021 "pick one of two" practice drew a RMB 3.442 billion fine, which was a real failure of benfen. The people aren't bad, but they will take shortcuts under competitive pressure—remember that.

At what price to buy

A good business does not equal a good price. HKD 77.5 corresponds to 15–18x 2027 normalized profit, or 11–13x after deducting net cash. This is a "fair price," not a "cheap price." Duan Yongping's test: if this were the only price at which you could buy a company for ten years, would you? Yes, but only a little.

The genuinely cheap price sits near HKD 65 — the level of the June 2026 buyback (HKD 66.5–69), which is also where the 52-week low of HKD 64.25 lies. The company itself bought at that level, which shows someone recognizes that price.

Conclusion

The business is good, management is basically honest, and the price is fair. All three boxes are ticked, but none of them is "excellent." That is why the rating is "Buy, build a small position in tranches" — not because I am bearish, but because a good asset has not yet reached a bargain-basement price.
