Core view

All four agree the business is a good business; they diverge sharply on what it is worth. Four points of disagreement: whether the stake is 51% or 60%, whether net cash is RMB 120 billion or RMB 168.3 billion, whether 2027 normalized profit is RMB 25 billion or RMB 35 billion, and whether Q3 is a peak-season illusion. After ruling on each point, the conclusion lands on the working paper's "Buy, build a small position in tranches"; no rating change needed.

The disagreements all stem from the four independent analyses in this report (the four pages of m05), and the Team Lead made the rulings, with the rationale set out in the body.
Disagreement one: 51% versus 60% — neither is wrong

Business analysis (Duan Yongping) judges by "will users still open it," leaning toward a GTV share above 60%; industry research (Munger) judges by competitive intensity, leaning toward the 51% order-volume threshold. Verdict: use both, but by scenario.

The reason is the business mix: Meituan holds an absolute advantage in full-service meals above RMB 30 and in chain brands, and loses share in low-price orders below RMB 15. The subsidy war targets low-price orders, so order volume falls faster than dollar value. This explains a key phenomenon: share fell 14 percentage points, yet core local commerce revenue still grew 10.1%. Looking only at order volume yields "the business is collapsing"; looking only at GTV yields "not a scratch." Both are wrong.

Divergence 2: normalized profit of RMB 25 billion or RMB 35 billion

This is the most valuable of the four debates, and it directly determines whether the target price is HKD 110 or HKD 150.

The RMB 25 billion calculation: core local commerce revenue of about RMB 300 billion (RMB 260.8 billion in 2025, up 8% year on year) at a 12.5% operating margin gives RMB 37.5 billion; new initiatives lose RMB 3 billion; unallocated items (AI, headquarters) lose RMB 5 billion; total operating profit of RMB 29.5 billion, at a 15% effective tax rate, gives RMB 25 billion.

The RMB 35 billion calculation: core local commerce margin returns to 15% (RMB 37.5 billion to RMB 45 billion), new initiatives break even, AI losses narrow to RMB 3 billion, giving about RMB 35 billion after tax.

Verdict: take the neutral RMB 25 billion; the RMB 35 billion belongs only in the bull case. Three reasons: in-store faces Douyin competition and margins will struggle to return to 2024 levels; rider costs are capped by three step-ups; merchant services revenue grows only 4.0%, so commission rates cannot rise. Choosing RMB 25 billion is not conservatism, it is respect for these three constraints.

Disagreement 3: Does Q3 extend the peak season or weaken sequentially?

The financial analysis (Buffett) sees Q2 operating cash flow of +RMB 9.7 billion and a 0.6 pp decline in the marketing expense ratio, and leans toward "the improvement can continue"; the risk assessment (Li Lu) sees management guidance and leans toward "Q3 weakening sequentially."

The verdict sides with Li Lu, and the reason is not judgment but three statements the company itself made: (1) Q3 per-order delivery cost will be higher than in Q2 (summer marketing, high-temperature rider subsidies, and nationwide contributions to the new work-injury insurance from July 1); (2) in-store business will step up investment in Q3 and Q4, and its operating margin will be lower than in Q2; (3) industry subsidy levels remain above 2024 and will need several quarters to normalize.

All three were stated explicitly by management at the August 28 earnings call, not my inference. So in the base case I put Q3 core local commerce operating profit in the RMB 3.0–4.5 billion range (Q2 was RMB 5.67 billion), rather than continuing to rise. That is also why the RMB 2.0 billion line is set so low — it is the floor for "confirming the peak-season illusion," not a forecast.

Final call: Buy, initiate at 2%

Two of the four (Duan Yongping, Munger) think it is a buy now; two (Buffett, Li Lu) think it is worth waiting for a lower price. As Team Lead, my ruling is: buy now, but with only 2% — a position that satisfies both camps.

The logic: if the inflection point holds, a 2% position captures the 42% gain, contributing 0.84% to the portfolio; if it does not hold, a 2% loss is -23%, a -0.46% impact on the portfolio. This is an asymmetric bet: win and make 0.84%, lose and give up 0.46%, and Q3 will settle the answer.

One more point: the most fragile part of this summary conclusion

The entire thesis rests on one assumption: industry subsidies will normalize in 2027. If that fails, all the valuation, position sizing and add conditions above are void — not marked down, void.

So I kept an asymmetry in the conclusion: a 2% position, an add line at HKD 65, a trim line at RMB 2 billion. All three numbers exist to keep the cost of being wrong manageable. If someone ever asks me how this investment is most likely to lose money, the answer is not "Meituan loses to Alibaba" — it is "the subsidies never stopped, and I kept waiting."

Conclusion

Summary: The four reports do not overturn the base case; they explain what "small position" in the base case actually means. All disagreements sit in the assumptions (share-count basis, normalized profit, Q3 trajectory); none is a factual disagreement — the data is reliable, the uncertainty lies in judgment.

Execution: initiate at 2%, add at HKD 65, verify in Q3. The answer comes within three months; this is an investment validated by quarterly results, not one held on conviction.
