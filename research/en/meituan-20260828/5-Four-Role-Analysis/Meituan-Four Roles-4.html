Core view

The biggest risk is not whether Meituan loses, but whether this war continues in another form. Meituan will not be killed — it holds 51% of order volume, more than 60% of GTV and RMB 168.3 billion in cash; but it could be beaten into a company whose margin never returns to 15%. That is the worst case: the stock does not collapse, but it does not rise for a decade.

The worst outcome is not a loss, but no growth

In Li Lu's framework, the most dangerous company is not one that goes bankrupt but one that "looks fine yet never earns excess returns." Meituan may become the latter: around 50% share, 8%–10% margins, RMB 20 billion of annual profit and a HKD 480 billion market cap — precisely today's price.

In other words, without margin recovery the current price is fair: no loss, no gain. The real risk is opportunity cost — that same money could have bought a company growing 15% a year at 20% ROE.

The essence of this investment, then, is a 2%–3% position buying an option on margin recovery. The premium is cheap (23% downside), but do not treat it as a core holding.

Regulation: not a black swan, but a gray rhino

Rider cost is the only quantifiable long-term risk. The path is clear: occupational injury protection (nationwide by 2026/7/1), then pension insurance subsidies (already nationwide in 2025), then full five social insurances and one housing fund (JD.com has announced full coverage for full-time riders). Each step converts "flexible employment" into "standard employment" and lifts per-order cost by a notch.

Under the most aggressive assumption, if all full-time riders are enrolled in the five social insurances and one housing fund within three years, per-order cost rises RMB 0.5–1, adding RMB 10–25 billion to Meituan's annualised cost. That equals 40%–100% of normalised profit in 2027. This is no black swan; it is a gray rhino, visible and slow-moving but certain to arrive.

The only hedge is to compress the valuation multiple below 20x so that rising costs are absorbed by the price. That is also why I do not accept a 30x valuation.

Going global: the one variable that could turn a "small position" into a "wrong position"

Keeta's story now reads well: Hong Kong UE turned positive in Q4 2025, Saudi Arabia reached profitability in July 2026 after just 22 months, and Saudi Arabia accounts for roughly 25% of Keeta's daily orders.Brazil is another matter.The agreement signed in May 2025 committed USD 1 billion over 5 years against Didi's 99Food—Didi's home turf after eight years in Latin America.

Li Lu would caution:Chinese companies rarely fail overseas because they cannot compete, but because they underestimate the "time cost of localization."Saudi Arabia reached profitability in 22 months on high ticket sizes and weak competition; Brazil offers low ticket sizes, fierce subsidies and complex regulation. If Brazil's quarterly loss exceeds RMB 2 billion in 2027, the "repair" thesis for this investment gets pushed back two years.

What if the subsidies never end

Of the five risks, I assigned only a 30% probability to "subsidy restart." But there is a more extreme scenario I did not factor in: subsidies neither restart nor stop — they persist at a low level where no one makes money and no one exits.

In that state, industry margins stay at 5%–8% long term, Meituan earns RMB 10–15 billion a year and its market cap sits at HKD 300–400 billion. That implies a share price of HKD 45–60, 23%–42% below the current HKD 77.5. This, not "widening losses," is the real source of the bear case: "perpetual thin profit."

In Li Lu's framework, this is a "good company in a bad industry" — the company is fine, but the industry's return on capital is insufficient. His usual approach: don't buy unless the price is low enough that you make money even if the industry never improves. By that standard, Meituan's buy point is below HKD 60, not HKD 77.5.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: It won't die, but it may not rise. Of the five risks, two (rider costs, subsidy restart) are medium-to-high probability, two (share loss, Keeta losing control) are medium probability, and one (in-store) is structural but slow-moving.

The response is not to avoid buying, but to buy less, buy cheap, and set clear falsification conditions. The RMB 2 billion line for Q3 core local commerce operating profit is the stop-loss line for this investment.
