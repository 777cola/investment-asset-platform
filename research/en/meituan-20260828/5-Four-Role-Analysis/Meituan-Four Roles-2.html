Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Meituan

The income statement has been shot through; the balance sheet has not collapsed

Core view

Read Meituan's earnings across two statements: the income statement is the casualty list, the balance sheet is the ammunition stock.Q2 2026 revenue was RMB 104.64 billion, operating profit RMB 2.69 billion, a 2.6% margin—not the margin of a good company; but RMB 168.3 billion in cash and short-term investments and quarterly operating cash flow of +RMB 9.7 billion—that is the balance sheet of a company that can keep fighting for a long time.

Income statement: RMB 2.69 billion saved

Breaking down the RMB 2.69 billion: core local commerce earned RMB 5.67 billion, new initiatives lost RMB 1.74 billion, and unallocated items (AI, headquarters, etc.) lost another RMB 1 billion-plus, leaving RMB 2.69 billion. Margin was 2.6%, versus 13.4% for full-year 2024.

Where did the improvement come from? Marketing expense ratio fell 0.6 percentage points year on year (about RMB 600 million), cost of sales ratio fell 0.6 percentage points (about RMB 600 million), and operating leverage from resumed growth in delivery service revenue.Not one of these is a price increase, and not one is a commission hike. There is a ceiling on cost savings, and that is my biggest concern for Q3 and Q4: management has already guided to higher per-order delivery costs and heavier investment in in-store.

Cash flow: this company will not die

In Q2 2026, net operating cash inflow was RMB 9.7 billion, 4.5x the quarterly net profit of RMB 2.155 billion. The reason is simple: users pay first, merchants settle later, riders are paid weekly — the platform always holds a float. As long as order volume holds, operating cash flow leads profit.

2025 was the exception: full-year operating cash flow was -RMB 13.815 billion (2024: +RMB 57.147 billion), because subsidies were paid in real cash, and with the income statement posting a loss of RMB 23.355 billion, cash flow fell in step. This shows the subsidy war has shifted from "marketing expense" to "cash outflow" — cash on book fell from its 2024 year-end high to RMB 168.3 billion in June 2026 (cash + short-term investments). It can keep fighting, but not indefinitely.

The three questions Buffett would ask

First, when does ROE turn positive? Negative in 2025, still loss-making through 2026 (roughly -RMB 5 to 7 billion), and 2027 at the earliest. Until this clears, the valuation has no anchor.

Second, is capex heavy? No. Meituan does not need to build warehouses like JD.com or buy GPUs like Alibaba; its main assets are riders (off balance sheet) and its dispatch system. This is a good asset-light, high-turnover structure, and it is the confidence behind its willingness to fight a price war.

Third, will management waste the cash? This is the only thing that gives me pause. RMB 168.3 billion in cash, zero dividend, only HKD 200 million spent on buybacks, USD 1 billion committed to overseas over 5 years—the money is in hand, but there is no clear path to returning it to shareholders. To Buffett, this warrants more caution than the losses.

On valuation itself, he never quite worked it out either

In the financial analysis I gave target prices under three scenarios, but one issue remains unresolved: should net cash be counted as RMB 120 billion or RMB 168.3 billion? The two figures differ by RMB 48.3 billion, which divided by 6.175 billion shares is HKD 7.8 per share—equal to 7% of the target price.

My approach is to take the conservative RMB 120 billion, on the grounds that interest-bearing debt and restricted funds must be deducted first. But this approach has a side effect: it makes "enterprise value" appear lower, and thus makes the valuation multiple appear more expensive. If true net cash is RMB 150 billion, the neutral target price should be raised from HKD 110 to HKD 118.

So the credible range for this financial analysis is HKD 110±8. That error is not small, but it does not affect the conclusion—because HKD 77.5 still has 42% upside to HKD 110, and an error of ±HKD 8 cannot change "buyable."

One more point: why I do not give "net cash discount" a higher score

Net cash of RMB 120 billion is 27% of market cap, which looks like a thick safety cushion. But I do not give it more credit, because the "deployability" of this money is questionable: part of it is merchant deposits and rider security funds, which are operating liabilities in nature; the other part is reserved for overseas expansion (USD 1 billion over 5 years in Brazil).

The money that can truly be returned to shareholders may be only half. At RMB 60 billion, that is 13.6% of market cap, and this is the real reason the stock "cannot fall further." 13.6% means the share price bottoms after another 14% decline—but only if the market believes the money is still there.

Conclusion

Financial takeaway: The income statement is recovering, but only thinly (2.6%); the balance sheet is solid (net cash in the region of RMB 120 billion), and cash flow has turned positive. This company will not die from this fight; the question is whether it can return to an operating margin above 12% within three years.
