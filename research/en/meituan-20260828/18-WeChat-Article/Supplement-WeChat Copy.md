## Title and lead-in

Title: Meituan earned RMB 2.69 billion — but this is not money won back

Subtitle: Q2 beat the RMB 3.5 billion consensus — why I only dare buy 2%

Meituan reported Q2 2026 results on the evening of August 28. Revenue came in at RMB 104.64 billion, +14.4% YoY, with operating profit of RMB 2.69 billion — a swing back into the black. Consensus had looked for a RMB 850 million loss.

It is the best report since the food-delivery war began in 2025.

Yet my first move after reading it was not to add to my position but to cut my buy plan from 5% back to 2%. The reason is below.

## I. First, the good news: three areas improved at once

First, revenue is back. RMB 104.64 billion, +14.4%, with delivery services contributing RMB 26.78 billion, +13.1%, returning to positive growth. The announcement cites "prudent investment in incentives" — in plain terms, fewer subsidies, while order volume and ticket size held up.

Second, the core business is profitable. Core local commerce revenue was RMB 71.53 billion, +10.1%, operating profit RMB 5.67 billion, +52.3% YoY, margin 7.9%, up 2.2 percentage points YoY. This segment is Meituan's entire source of profit.

Third, new initiatives lost less. Losses were RMB 1.74 billion, with the loss ratio narrowing from 7.1% to 5.3%. Xiaoxiang Supermarket covers 68 cities (39 cities at end-2025), Kuailehou has 40 stores, and Keeta is already profitable in Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia.

One easily overlooked figure: net operating cash inflow in the second quarter was RMB 9.7 billion, 4.5x net profit. For full-year 2025 this figure was -RMB 13.8 billion.

A swing to positive cash flow is harder to fake than a swing to positive profit.

## Less charitably, the RMB 2.69 billion was squeezed out of cost savings

Profit margin 2.6%, versus 13.4% for full-year 2024.

In other words, it has recovered to only one-fifth of the level of two years ago.

Where did the savings come from? Cost of sales ratio down 0.6pp (about RMB 630 million) and marketing expense ratio down 0.6pp (about RMB 630 million). No price increases, no commission hikes—just spending less.

And there are limits to spending less.

Management was explicit on three points at the earnings call: per-order delivery costs in Q3 will be higher than in Q2 (summer marketing, high-temperature rider subsidies, and nationwide coverage of the "new occupational injury" insurance for riders from July 1, with the platform bearing the full premium); investment in the in-store business will increase in Q3 and Q4, with the operating margin lower than in Q2; industry subsidy levels remain higher than in 2024 and will take several quarters to normalize.

In other words, RMB 2.69 billion is very likely one of the peaks for the next four quarters.

This isn't me talking it down—the company said so itself.

## III. The single most critical data point: 4.0%

One line in the revenue breakdown stands out: merchant services revenue of RMB 39.60 billion, up just 4.0% YoY.

Commissions plus advertising, this segment is the thermometer of the platform's bargaining power over merchants.

What does 4.0% mean? That eight million merchants did not get bigger — they were merely retained. Two reasons: Douyin is using low commissions to poach chain brands and influencer shops, and merchants themselves are cutting costs, trimming ad budgets harder than commissions.

Food delivery's moat is fulfilment density; in-store services' moat is merchant relationships. The first still holds; the second is loosening.

And in-store services is Meituan's fattest profit pool.

That is more worrying than delivery share falling from 65% to 51% — what is being lost is unprofitable orders, while what is loosening is the most profitable relationship.

## IV. So what is it worth

HKD 77.5, market cap HKD 478.6 billion (6.175 billion shares). Cash and short-term wealth management products on the balance sheet total RMB 168.3 billion; net of interest-bearing debt and restricted funds, net cash is about RMB 120 billion.

2026 losses leave no P/E to speak of. On normalized 2027 net profit of RMB 25 billion the stock trades at 17.6x, or 12.8x net of cash; on RMB 29 billion, 15.2x and 11x.

Three scenarios:

- Bear: subsidies run another two years, 2027 net profit RMB 10 billion, target HKD 60, -23%;

- Base: subsidies normalize, core local commerce margin back to 12%–13%, net profit RMB 25 billion, target HKD 110, +42%;

- Bull: share holds above 55%, Keeta contributes profit, net profit RMB 35 billion, target HKD 150, +94%.

Odds 1:1.8. Enough to buy, not enough to size up.

## V. My approach

Buy 2% at HKD 77.5.

Add to 5% at HKD 65, a level backed by the company's own June buyback (HKD 66.5–69) and the 52-week low of HKD 64.25. That takes the payoff ratio to 1:3.

Cut half at HKD 110. Exit fully at HKD 150.

Hold steady as long as three lines hold: Q3 core local commerce operating profit no lower than RMB 2 billion; industry subsidies stop rising; merchant services revenue growth does not turn negative.

If any one breaks, cut below 1%.

This is not a "long-term bullish" story; it is an investment validated by quarterly reports. October will settle it — no need to wait three years.

## Six, a comparison: how it differs from JD

Both fought in food delivery. JD.com lost about RMB 40 billion over two years, with share falling from 31% to 7%. Meituan fought for a year, lost RMB 17 billion and saw share fall from 65% to 51%, then earned it all back in the second quarter.

The difference is not how much money was spent, but whether there are merchants. Meituan has 8 million merchants; JD.com had to negotiate from zero. Without merchants, users acquired through subsidies open the app and find "nothing I want to eat," so they don't come back.

That is why I say food delivery is a "merchant density business," not a "logistics business." Whoever gets this wrong will burn money where there is no compounding.
