## People: all four positions are held by veterans with over ten years of tenure

Meituan's power structure is simple: **Wang Xing sets direction, Mu Rongjun runs the organization, Chen Shaohui runs the money, and Wang Puzhong runs the battles.** Three of the four are veterans from 2010–2015; there are no parachuted star executives and no visible succession race. This is rare among Chinese platform companies—most turn to "professional management" after reaching a USD 50 billion market cap. Meituan has not.

Wang Xing's control rests on a dual-class share structure: 515,869,783 Class A shares, representing 45.30% of Class A voting rights. Mu Rongjun is the other WVR beneficiary, and together the two hold an effective veto over shareholder meeting resolutions. Their economic interest is only about 9.3%, but their control is close to half. **The upside is that long-termism is protected—no one can force him to cut long-term investment for a single quarter's profit; the downside is that minority shareholders have no checks on him.**

At the shareholder communication meeting on June 25, 2026, Wang Xing said he "has not sold a single share since the company was founded and has no plan to sell," and Chen Shaohui said "the company's value is currently severely undervalued." Said at a share price of HKD 64, such words are worth more than at HKD 200.

## Money: right strategy, stingy buybacks, zero dividends

Start with what is right. The June 2023 acquisition of Light Year for RMB 2.065 billion brought in Wang Huiwen's team and compute, at a price equivalent to a seed-round valuation; two years later, the LongCat model series and the two AI assistants "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan" are the product of that money. **R&D in 2026Q2 was RMB 7.67 billion, 7.3% of revenue; at the height of the subsidy war it did not cut R&D, nor did it go build a "Token factory" — that restraint is correct.**

Now what is not right. A USD 2 billion buyback was approved in June 2024, but by November of that year only about USD 600 million had been executed; after reauthorization in June 2026, it bought 2.92 million shares for HKD 200 million over two days, 0.04% of market cap. Over the same period the company held RMB 168.3 billion in cash and short-term investments.**A net-cash company whose share price is -27% from its 52-week high, with management itself saying it is "severely undervalued," spent only HKD 200 million on buybacks — this is not a capability problem, it is a willingness problem.**

The dividend is zero; eight years listed and never paid one. For a growth company that is not wrong, but in a profit-recovery phase, buybacks are the only capital-allocation tool that can grow per-share value. This item is why the management score falls from 9 to 7.

## Integrity and governance: pushed to change three times

Meituan has been "pushed into change" three times in its history. None were voluntary, but each was carried through.

The first was the October 2021 antitrust penalty: a fine of RMB 3.442 billion, refund of RMB 1.289 billion in exclusive-cooperation deposits, and a finding that "choose one of two" restricted competition. The platform thereafter stopped requiring merchant exclusivity and made its commission structure transparent — it lost merchant lock-in in the short term but cleared the "choose one of two" regulatory landmine over the long term.

The second was rider rights. Pilots for occupational injury protection began in 2022; 2025 brought nationwide coverage of rider pension subsidies (the industry's first scheme covering all rider types); and on July 1, 2026, "new occupational injury" insurance rolled out nationwide with premiums fully borne by the platform. **This is real cost, and also a real compliance asset** — after the State Administration for Market Regulation issued the national standard "Basic Requirements for Service Management of Food Delivery Platforms" in December 2025, whoever finishes first takes fewer penalties.

The third was the 2025 "anti-involution" campaign. Regulatory interviews, the national standard, and the Central Economic Work Conference's framing of "win-win between platform companies and workers" — Meituan publicly stated firm opposition to involution, and profit turned positive in 2026Q2. **The common thread across all three: the company never moves first, but never digs in either.** The implication for investors: policy risk is manageable, but do not expect it to act ahead of regulators.

## Succession: no shortage of candidates, but no "next Wang Xing"

Wang Xing is 47; talk of succession is premature. But the organizational structure is already prepared: the 2024 restructuring handed core local commerce to Wang Puzhong, freeing Wang Xing to focus on AI and overseas; Mu Rongjun oversees organization and HR, Chen Shaohui oversees capital. **This is a three-way structure of "someone running the business, someone managing the money, someone setting direction" — healthier than at many companies.**

The real succession risk is not people but judgment. Every key decision at Meituan over the past decade — the 2015 merger with Dianping, the 2018 IPO, the 2021 retreat from community group buying, the 2025 anti-involution push — was Wang Xing's call. These decisions share one trait: **choosing to contract while everyone else was expanding.** That counterintuitive judgment cannot be institutionalized, nor handed over.

So the score for management is 7: 9 for the current lineup, 6 for capital allocation, 5 for transferability.

## A detail in the shareholding structure

One easily overlooked detail in Meituan's A/B share structure: **Wang Xing holds only about 9.3% of economic interest but 45.30% of voting power; Mu Rongjun holds about 2.0% and is also a WVR beneficiary.** Together they hold a de facto veto over ordinary resolutions at the shareholders' meeting.

The upside of this structure is strategic stability — no barbarians at the gate, no short-term performance pressure. The downside is that **minority shareholders have no say on three issues: dividends, buybacks, and major capital expenditure (Brazil, for example).**

The HKD 200 million buyback in June 2026 is a case in point: management called the stock "severely undervalued," yet the action amounted to just 0.04% of market cap. Minority shareholders cannot propose, cannot veto, and cannot demand an explanation. This must go into the governance discount — of the 7 points I give management, 1 is deducted here.
