## Who makes money along the chain: three profit pools, each thinner than the last

The first layer is the brand. A hot pot chain runs a 60% gross margin, but after rent, labour and platform commissions, net margin is only 8%–10%. The brand earns the premium consumers pay for recognising the label, and it must pay rent and commissions for that premium.

The second layer is the platform. Meituan's core local commerce revenue reached RMB 71.53 billion in 2026Q2, with operating profit of RMB 5.67 billion and a margin of 7.9%; in 2024 that figure was 20.9%. The platform earns money from "matching" — distributing demand to the most suitable merchants and collecting commissions and advertising. This is the most profitable link in the chain because it is asset-light, replicable and has near-zero marginal cost.

The third layer is fulfilment. Rider costs account for more than 70% of per-order delivery cost — a hard cost with no room to fall beyond economies of scale. From July 1, 2026, the new occupational injury insurance covers the whole country with premiums fully borne by platforms, effectively adding another layer to this segment. Meituan must run fulfilment itself because that is its moat; but it therefore can never earn the margins of a pure platform.

## Meituan is the only company to have built out all three segments

In one sentence: Meituan is the only company that simultaneously owns a demand gateway (an app with 500 million users), a fulfillment network (6 million riders across 2,800 counties and districts) and merchant digitalisation (POS, supply chain, Kuailv). Alibaba has the gateway and Ele.me's capacity but a weak merchant side; Douyin has the gateway and content but no fulfillment at all; JD has capacity and a quality mindset but is building its merchant side from zero.

The advantage of this position is two-way lock-in: merchants cannot do without its orders, and users cannot do without its speed. The downside is that Meituan must bear the full cost of fulfillment itself, so any rise in labour costs hits profit directly — a pure platform can pass it on; Meituan cannot.

There is also a hidden benefit: Meituan sees data on both "eating" and "buying." Food delivery knows what you want to eat today; Shanguo knows when the shampoo at home runs out. Douyin and JD do not fully possess this data asset, and it underpins Meituan's confidence to build the AI assistants "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan."

## Cycle position: stock efficiency, not incremental upside

Three cycles are overlapping and pulling in different directions.

The first layer is the restaurant market. In 2026 restaurant revenue growth slows to around 5%, store counts are shrinking and average ticket size is falling. This layer is heading down, irreversibly so—demographics and consumption habits decide it.

The second layer is food delivery penetration. Tier-1 cities are already saturated; growth sits in county-level markets and among the middle-aged and older population, where average ticket size is low and fulfillment costs are high. This layer is close to its ceiling.

The third layer is instant retail (flash shopping). It has moved from "emergency" to "everyday," with categories widening from pharmaceuticals and liquor into supermarkets and 3C. This layer is still expanding and drives Meituan's 25% new-business revenue growth.

Taken together, Meituan's growth over the next three years will come mainly from instant retail and overseas, not food delivery. Food delivery is the cash cow and the moat; flash shopping and Keeta are the growth drivers. The valuation implication: food delivery cannot support a growth-stock multiple, only a stable-cash-flow multiple of 15 to 20x.

## Upstream bargaining power, tested: who gets squeezed in the chain

"Pricing power" is an empty phrase; two numbers can measure it.

The first is restaurant merchants' net margin. Take a small restaurant with a RMB 40 average ticket and 100 orders a day: after food costs (35%), rent (15%), labour (20%) and platform commission plus promotion (15%–20%), it nets only 5%–8%. The 15%–20% the platform takes is almost equal to the merchant's entire net profit. That is why merchants are so sensitive to commission, and why Douyin can poach them on lower commission.

The second is Meituan's merchant services revenue growth: just +4.0% in Q2 2026. A platform with pricing power should be growing that line above 10% — merchants' business improves, the platform collects more. At 4.0%, the platform dare not raise prices, and merchants are unwilling to spend more on advertising.

Put the two together and the conclusion is clear: Meituan has pricing power over upstream merchants (it can charge 15%–20%), but has already pushed it to the limit — raise it further and merchants leave. This is the classic state of "a moat with no headroom."

## Overseas: can this supply chain be relocated?

The local-services chain rests on one premise: high-density cities, low-cost flexible labour and mature payments and maps. All three are readily available in China; elsewhere they must be rebuilt from scratch.

Keeta's experiment yields three data points. Hong Kong turned UE positive in Q4 2025 on high density, high ticket size and short delivery distance. Saudi Arabia reached profitability in July 2026 in just 22 months, helped by high ticket size, weak competition and ample foreign labour. Brazil has launched but disclosed no data, and on the three variables it scores badly: low ticket size, Didi's home turf and a complex tax regime.

Connect the three points and you get a sloped line: the higher the ticket size, the weaker the competition and the cheaper the labour, the faster the replication. By that rule Brazil is the hardest case, holding no advantage on any of the three. My expectation is therefore no profit within three years, with controlled losses counting as success.

If this chain can truly be transplanted to Brazil, Meituan's valuation framework must be rewritten: from "Chinese local-services platform" (15–20x) to "emerging-market instant-delivery platform" (25–30x). That is the source of the HKD 150 bull case and the largest option value in this investment.
