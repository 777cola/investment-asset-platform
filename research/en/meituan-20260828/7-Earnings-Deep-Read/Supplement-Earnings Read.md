## Layer one: revenue mix — flat advertising is the most telling line

Revenue RMB 104.64 billion, +14.4% YoY, the fastest growth since Q2 2025. But broken down, the three lines diverge sharply.

**Delivery services RMB 26.78 billion, +13.1%**, back to positive growth, mainly because incentives were deployed more cautiously — not a surge in order volume, but unit prices normalizing after subsidy cuts. **Merchant services RMB 39.60 billion, up only 4.0%** — this is commission plus advertising, the thermometer of the platform's bargaining power. 4.0% means merchants are not getting bigger, only being retained; commissions cannot be raised, and ads are not selling. **Goods sales about RMB 5.15 billion, +78.9%** — self-operated categories such as pharmaceuticals and liquor; the growth rate looks good but the base is small and gross margin low.

New initiatives at RMB 33.11 billion, +25%, are another matter: grocery retail (Xiaoxiang Supermarket in 68 cities, Happy Monkey in 40 stores) and Keeta's overseas expansion. **This is the lowest-quality revenue — self-operated grocery is an asset-heavy business, where RMB 1 of revenue corresponds to only a few jiao of gross profit.**

## Second layer: gross profit and expenses — the RMB 1.2 billion saved

Cost of sales RMB 69.548 billion, +13% YoY, 66.5% of revenue, down 0.6 pp YoY; gross margin 33.5%. The company attributes this to operating leverage and improved grocery retail operations — in plain terms: **order density rose, delivery cost per order fell; Xiaoxiang Supermarket's loss ratio narrowed.** Both are real, but neither is a price increase.

On the expense side, selling and marketing RMB 24.723 billion, +12%, 23.6% of revenue, down 0.6 pp YoY; R&D RMB 7.670 billion, +22.5%, 7.3% of revenue, up 0.5 pp YoY.

Calculating how much the 0.6 pp marketing expense ratio saved: 1,046.43 × 0.6% ≈ **RMB 630 million**. The 0.6 pp on cost of sales ratio ≈ **RMB 630 million**. Combined, roughly RMB 1.26 billion, against group operating profit of RMB 2.69 billion for the quarter — **in other words, nearly half of this quarter's profit came from these two 0.6 pp shifts, the other half from delivery revenue recovery and narrower overseas losses.**

## Tier three: segment profit — the core business makes money, new businesses narrow losses, AI burns cash

Core local commerce: revenue RMB 71.53 billion, operating profit RMB 5.67 billion, margin 7.9%, up 2.2 percentage points year on year, profit +52.3% year on year. This is the only good news in the entire report, and the quality is not bad — **it comes from lower user incentive spending and lower delivery cost per order, not from higher commission rates**. Against the 20.9% margin in 2024, only a little more than a third has been recovered.

New initiatives: revenue RMB 33.11 billion, loss RMB 1.74 billion, loss ratio 5.3% (7.1% in the year-earlier period). Xiaoxiang Supermarket in 68 cities, 40 Happy Monkey stores, Keeta profitable in Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia — **this segment has gone from a bottomless pit to a controllable loss, the second pillar of the valuation recovery.**

Unallocated items: Q2 loss of about RMB 1.24 billion (H1 -RMB 3.56 billion), widened 57.2% year on year, mainly on AI investment. A substantial part of the RMB 7.67 billion in R&D went to the LongCat model, unmanned delivery and the two AI assistants "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan". **Whether the money is well spent will not be clear within three years, but it is certainly weighing on current profit.**

## Tier 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — evidence it will not die

Q2 net operating cash inflow was RMB 9.7 billion, 4.5x the net profit of RMB 2.155 billion. Full-year 2025 was negative RMB 13.815 billion, with net financing inflow of RMB 21.243 billion—**in other words, in 2025 the company plugged its cash gap by borrowing and raising capital.** This inflection matters: 2025 was genuine cash burn; from Q2 2026 the company generates its own cash.

Balance sheet: cash and equivalents of RMB 104.7 billion plus RMB 63.6 billion in short-term wealth-management products, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion. Net of interest-bearing debt and restricted funds, net cash is roughly RMB 120 billion (estimate), equal to 27% of market cap. **This money is why Meituan can hold out until rivals exit first, and it is the valuation's margin of safety.**

But do not overstate it: part of the RMB 168.3 billion is merchant deposits and rider security funds, not free capital; and the company spent only HKD 200 million on buybacks in June 2026, suggesting the "deployability" of this cash is lower than the headline figure implies.

## Layer five: view the four quarters together

Single-quarter data can mislead; only four quarters together give a clear picture: 2025Q4 operating loss of RMB 16.07 billion, 2026Q1 loss of RMB 6.47 billion, 2026Q2 profit of RMB 2.69 billion. **Two quarters narrowed the gap by RMB 13.4 billion — an extremely steep slope.**

The steep slope shows two things. First, the losses stem mainly from subsidies; pull the subsidies and profit returns immediately — good news, because it shows the cost structure is intact. Second, this "switch-like" profit shows it is unstable — add subsidies back and a single quarter could swing back to a RMB 10 billion loss.

Spread across four quarters: the trailing 12 months (2025Q3–2026Q2) show a cumulative operating loss of about RMB 19.8 billion (2025Q3 was not disclosed separately; back-calculated at roughly -RMB 14.6 billion by subtracting Q1+Q2+Q4 from the full-year -RMB 17 billion — a rough estimate). **This is the reference point for the "true earnings level" — not RMB 2.69 billion, not -RMB 4.67 billion, but "nearly RMB 20 billion lost over the past year, just turning positive in the latest quarter."**

## The three things to watch most closely

First, **Q3 core local commerce operating profit**. This is the only number that can disprove the "peak-season illusion"; we will know by the end of October. RMB 2 billion is the floor; RMB 4 billion is the add-to-position line.

Second, **per-order delivery cost**. Management said Q3 would be higher than Q2 but did not say by how much. If per-order cost rises by more than RMB 0.3, that is RMB 7.5 billion annualized (roughly based on 70 million orders per day), equivalent to 30% of normalized 2027 profit. **This number matters far more than "share," because it directly determines whether margins can recover.**

Third, **merchant services revenue growth**. Q2 was only +4.0%; if it turns negative, the merchant ecosystem is deteriorating—a more fundamental problem than subsidies, because subsidies can stop, but a broken ecosystem takes years to repair.
