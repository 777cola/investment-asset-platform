## Layer one: revenue mix — flat advertising is the most telling line

Revenue of RMB 104.64 billion, +14.4% YoY, the fastest growth since Q2 2025. Beneath the headline, however, the three lines diverge sharply.

Delivery services RMB 26.78 billion, +13.1%, back to positive growth, mainly because incentives were deployed more cautiously — not a surge in order volume, but unit prices normalising after subsidy cuts. Merchant services RMB 39.60 billion, up only 4.0% — commission plus advertising, the thermometer of the platform's bargaining power. 4.0% means merchants are not getting bigger, only being retained: commissions cannot be raised, and ads are not selling. Goods sales about RMB 5.15 billion, +78.9% — self-operated categories such as pharmaceuticals and liquor; the growth rate looks good but the base is small and gross margin low.

New initiatives at RMB 33.11 billion, +25%, are another matter: grocery retail (Xiaoxiang Supermarket in 68 cities, Happy Monkey in 40 stores) and Keeta's overseas expansion. This is the lowest-quality revenue — self-operated grocery is an asset-heavy business, where RMB 1 of revenue corresponds to only a few jiao of gross profit.

## Second layer: gross profit and expenses — the RMB 1.2 billion saved

Cost of sales RMB 69.548 billion, +13% YoY and 66.5% of revenue, down 0.6 pp YoY; gross margin 33.5%. Management attributes this to operating leverage and improved grocery retail operations — in plain terms, order density rose and delivery cost per order fell, while Xiaoxiang Supermarket's loss ratio narrowed. Both are real, but neither reflects a price increase.

On the expense side, selling and marketing came in at RMB 24.723 billion, +12% and 23.6% of revenue, down 0.6 pp YoY; R&D was RMB 7.670 billion, +22.5% and 7.3% of revenue, up 0.5 pp YoY.

The 0.6 pp saving on the marketing expense ratio works out to 1,046.43 × 0.6% ≈ RMB 630 million, and the 0.6 pp on the cost of sales ratio to roughly RMB 630 million — about RMB 1.26 billion combined, against group operating profit of RMB 2.69 billion for the quarter. In other words, nearly half of this quarter's profit came from these two 0.6 pp shifts, with the other half from delivery revenue recovery and narrower overseas losses.

## Tier three: segment profit — core business profitable, new business losses narrowing, AI burning cash

Core local commerce: revenue RMB 71.53 billion, operating profit RMB 5.67 billion, margin 7.9%, up 2.2 percentage points YoY, profit +52.3% YoY. This is the only good news in the entire report, and the quality is not bad — it comes from lower user incentive spending and lower delivery cost per order, not from higher commission rates. Against the 20.9% margin in 2024, only a little more than a third has been recovered.

New initiatives: revenue RMB 33.11 billion, loss RMB 1.74 billion, loss ratio 5.3% (7.1% in the year-earlier period). Xiaoxiang Supermarket in 68 cities, 40 Happy Monkey stores, Keeta profitable in Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia — this segment has gone from a bottomless pit to a controllable loss, the second pillar of the valuation recovery.

Unallocated items: Q2 loss of about RMB 1.24 billion (H1 -RMB 3.56 billion), widened 57.2% YoY, mainly on AI investment. A substantial part of the RMB 7.67 billion in R&D went to the LongCat model, unmanned delivery and the two AI assistants "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan". Whether the money is well spent will not be clear within three years, but it is certainly weighing on current profit.

## Tier 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — evidence it will not die

Q2 net operating cash inflow was RMB 9.7 billion, 4.5x the RMB 2.155 billion net profit. Full-year 2025 was negative RMB 13.815 billion, offset by RMB 21.243 billion of net financing inflow—in other words, in 2025 the company plugged its cash gap by borrowing and raising capital. The inflection matters: 2025 was genuine cash burn; from Q2 2026 the company generates its own cash.

Balance sheet: RMB 104.7 billion of cash and equivalents plus RMB 63.6 billion of short-term wealth-management products, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion. Net of interest-bearing debt and restricted funds, net cash is roughly RMB 120 billion (estimate), or 27% of market cap. This is what lets Meituan hold out until rivals exit first, and it is the valuation's margin of safety.

But do not overstate it: part of the RMB 168.3 billion is merchant deposits and rider security funds rather than free capital, and buybacks totalled only HKD 200 million in June 2026—suggesting the deployability of this cash is lower than the headline figure implies.

## Layer five: view the four quarters together

Single-quarter data can mislead; only four quarters together give a clear picture: 2025Q4 operating loss of RMB 16.07 billion, 2026Q1 loss of RMB 6.47 billion, 2026Q2 profit of RMB 2.69 billion. Two quarters narrowed the gap by RMB 13.4 billion — an extremely steep slope.

That slope tells us two things. First, the losses stem mainly from subsidies: pull the subsidies and profit returns immediately — good news, because it shows the cost structure is intact. Second, profit that behaves like a switch is inherently unstable — add subsidies back and a single quarter could swing back to a RMB 10 billion loss.

Spread across four quarters, the trailing 12 months (2025Q3–2026Q2) show a cumulative operating loss of about RMB 19.8 billion (2025Q3 was not disclosed separately; back-calculated at roughly -RMB 14.6 billion by subtracting Q1+Q2+Q4 from the full-year -RMB 17 billion — a rough estimate). This is the reference point for the "true earnings level" — not RMB 2.69 billion, not -RMB 4.67 billion, but "nearly RMB 20 billion lost over the past year, just turning positive in the latest quarter."

## The three things to watch most closely

First, Q3 core local commerce operating profit. This is the only number that can disprove the "peak-season illusion", and we will know by the end of October. RMB 2 billion is the floor; RMB 4 billion is the add-to-position line.

Second, per-order delivery cost. Management guided Q3 above Q2 but gave no magnitude. A rise of more than RMB 0.3 per order implies RMB 7.5 billion annualised (roughly on 70 million orders per day), equivalent to 30% of normalised 2027 profit. This matters far more than "share", because it directly determines whether margins can recover.

Third, merchant services revenue growth. Q2 was only +4.0%; a turn negative would signal a deteriorating merchant ecosystem—a more fundamental problem than subsidies, since subsidies can be stopped, but a broken ecosystem takes years to repair.
