Core thesis

The buy case rests on five testable assumptions. Status today: one holds (share defended at 51%), one partially holds (subsidy retreat) and three remain unverified (margin recovery, new businesses breaking even, overseas replication). This is not a position with "sufficient evidence" but one where "evidence is starting to appear." Size it small, therefore, and fix the verification checkpoints in advance—the Q3 report at the end of October is the watershed, not 2027.

The one that held: share was defended

This is the most counterintuitive point. In 2025 the consensus was that Meituan would lose half its share, yet it held 51% of order volume and over 60% of GTV.

Why? Because what it defended was high-ticket orders: full-service meals above RMB 30, chain brands, office-district lunches. These orders are time-sensitive and price-insensitive, and subsidies cannot move them. And the low-price orders that subsidies captured were never profitable in the first place — losing them costs little.

Verdict: holds. But with a caveat — this is "defending the floor," not "reclaiming the ceiling." That 65% figure most likely will not come back.

Three unverified items: three "ifs" for 2027

First, core local commerce margin back to 12%. Q2 was 7.9%, and the return to 12% hinges on three conditions holding simultaneously: subsidies stop rising, in-store margins hold, and rider costs stay contained. Management has already flagged higher in-store investment and rising rider costs in Q3 — both are headwinds.

Second, new initiatives break even in 2027. The Q2 loss ratio was 5.3%, a trajectory on which breaking even in 2027 is achievable. But Xiaoxiang Supermarket is expanding from 39 cities to 68 and Happy Monkey is opening 40 stores, and loss ratios typically fluctuate during expansion.

Third, replicating Keeta. Saudi Arabia reaching profitability in 22 months is strong evidence, but Brazil is the rival's home turf. My view: Brazil cannot be profitable in 2027; containing losses would count as success. If Brazil's quarterly loss exceeds RMB 2 billion in 2027, the RMB 25 billion profit in the base case gets cut by 20%.

Falsification conditions: three triggers; cut the position if any one is hit

First, Q3 core local commerce operating profit below RMB 2 billion. This is a hard constraint: Q2 came in at RMB 5.67 billion and management guided only to a "sequential decline," not a drop below RMB 2 billion. A breach would show the peak-season effect was the entire cause.

Second, Alibaba steps up subsidies again. Watch for Alibaba's quarterly instant retail loss widening again, or Taobao Shangou investment no longer being halved. This is an external variable I cannot control, but I can see it.

Third, merchant services revenue growth turns negative. Q2 was only +4.0%; a turn negative would signal the merchant ecosystem is deteriorating — a more fundamental problem than subsidies.

If any one of the three triggers, cut to below 1%; if two trigger simultaneously, exit the position.No "wait and see" option — waiting is the most expensive cost of this investment.

Watch calendar: four points in time

Late October/early November 2026: Meituan Q3 results. Three numbers to watch: core local commerce operating profit (≥RMB 2 billion), merchant services growth (must not turn negative) and new business loss ratio (≤5.3%). This is the key event.

November 2026: Alibaba quarterly results. Watch instant retail revenue growth and the loss amount to judge whether subsidies are genuinely receding.

November 2026 (after Double 11): Watch Meituan's investment intensity in in-store services and Douyin's in-store GTV growth.

March 2027: 2026 annual report. Watch the full-year loss (expected -RMB 5 billion to -RMB 7 billion), cumulative buybacks and management guidance for 2027. If the 2026 annual report shows a full-year loss of less than RMB 5 billion and buybacks exceeding HKD 2 billion, the thesis moves from "partially valid" to "valid."

If Q3 comes in better than expected

My base case is Q3 core local commerce operating profit of RMB 3.0–4.5 billion, versus RMB 5.67 billion in Q2. A print above RMB 4.5 billion would signal three things at once: subsidies are retreating faster than expected, rider cost inflation is being absorbed, and in-store investment is less aggressive than feared.

In that case, the 2027 assumption of RMB 25 billion can be raised to RMB 29 billion, and the neutral target price rises from HKD 110 to HKD 128. More importantly, "certainty" rises from 50% to 70%, allowing the position to go from 2% to 5%.

But watch for one trap: Q3 is peak season, so good data may just be seasonal. The test is not the absolute number but the YoY comparison — if Q3 core local commerce operating profit grows more than 30% YoY, that is real improvement, because last year's Q3 was the height of the subsidy war (a low base).

Conclusion

Of the five assumptions, one holds, two hold partially, three remain unverified and one does not hold. That structure caps the position at 2% — not for want of conviction, but because the evidence is not yet in.

The next three months are critical: October brings Q3 profit, November clarity on whether Alibaba truly exits, and March the buyback. If all three pass, raise to 5%; if two fail, exit.
