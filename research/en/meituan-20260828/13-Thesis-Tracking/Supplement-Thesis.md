## The one that held: share was defended

This is the most counterintuitive point. In 2025 everyone was saying "Meituan will lose half its share," yet it held on to 51% of order volume and over 60% of GTV.

Why did it hold? Because what it defended was high-ticket orders. Full-service meals above RMB 30, chain brands, office-district lunches — these orders are time-sensitive and price-insensitive, and subsidies cannot move them. And the low-price orders that subsidies captured were never profitable in the first place — losing them costs little.

Verdict: holds. But with a caveat — this is "defending the floor," not "reclaiming the ceiling." That 65% figure most likely will not come back.

## Three unverified items: three "ifs" for 2027

First, core local commerce margin returns to 12%. Q2 was 7.9%; getting back to 12% requires three conditions to hold at once: subsidies stop rising, in-store margins do not collapse, and rider costs stay contained. Management has already flagged higher in-store investment and rising rider costs in Q3 — both are headwinds.

Second, new initiatives break even in 2027. The Q2 loss ratio was 5.3%; at that trajectory, breaking even in 2027 is possible. But Xiaoxiang Supermarket is expanding (39 cities to 68) and Happy Monkey is opening stores (40), and loss ratios typically fluctuate during expansion.

Third, replicating Keeta. Saudi Arabia reaching profitability in 22 months is strong evidence, but Brazil is the rival's home turf. My view: Brazil cannot be profitable in 2027; keeping losses contained would count as success. If Brazil's quarterly loss exceeds RMB 2 billion in 2027, the RMB 25 billion profit in the base case gets cut by 20%.

## Falsification conditions: three triggers; cut the position if any one is hit

First, Q3 core local commerce operating profit below RMB 2 billion. This line is a hard constraint, because Q2 was RMB 5.67 billion and management only guided to a "sequential decline," not a drop below RMB 2 billion. A breach would show the peak-season effect was the entire cause.

Second, Alibaba steps up subsidies again. Metrics to watch: Alibaba's quarterly instant retail loss widens again, or Taobao Shangou investment is no longer halved. This is an external variable I cannot control, but I can see it.

Third, merchant services revenue growth turns negative. Q2 was only +4.0%; a turn negative would signal the merchant ecosystem is deteriorating — a more fundamental problem than subsidies.

If any one of the three triggers, cut to below 1%; if two trigger simultaneously, exit the position.No "wait and see" option — waiting is the most expensive cost of this investment.

## Watch calendar: four points in time

Late October/early November 2026: Meituan Q3 results. Watch three numbers: core local commerce operating profit (≥RMB 2 billion), merchant services growth (not turning negative), new business loss ratio (≤5.3%). This is the most important one.

November 2026: Alibaba quarterly results. Watch instant retail revenue growth and loss amount to judge whether subsidies are truly receding.

November 2026 (after Double 11): Watch Meituan's investment intensity in in-store services and Douyin's in-store GTV growth.

March 2027: 2026 annual report. Watch full-year loss magnitude (expected -RMB 5 billion to -RMB 7 billion), cumulative buyback amount, and management guidance for 2027. If the 2026 annual report shows a full-year loss of less than RMB 5 billion and buybacks exceeding HKD 2 billion, this thesis moves from "partially valid" to "valid."

## If Q3 comes in better than expected

My base case is Q3 core local commerce operating profit of RMB 3.0–4.5 billion (Q2 was RMB 5.67 billion). If the actual figure exceeds RMB 4.5 billion, three things are true at once: subsidies are retreating faster than expected, rider cost inflation is being absorbed, and in-store investment is less aggressive than feared.

In that case, the 2027 assumption of RMB 25 billion can be raised to RMB 29 billion, and the neutral target price rises from HKD 110 to HKD 128. More importantly, "certainty" rises from 50% to 70%, allowing the position to go from 2% to 5%.

But watch for one trap: Q3 is peak season, so good data may just be seasonal. The test is not the absolute number but the year-on-year comparison — if Q3 core local commerce operating profit grows more than 30% YoY, that is real improvement, because last year's Q3 was the height of the subsidy war (a low base).
