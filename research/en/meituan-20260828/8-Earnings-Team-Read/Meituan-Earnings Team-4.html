Core view

A single quarterly report cannot capture a decade, but it can signal direction. Three long-term threads run through this report: instant retail is widening its categories (product sales +78.9%), overseas operations have turned profitable (Keeta Saudi Arabia in 22 months), and AI investment is reaching real-world use cases (3.4 million merchants using the AI assistant). None of the three is a source of profit this quarter, but all three are sources of profit three years out.

Instant retail: from emergency to everyday

Goods sales reached RMB 5.15 billion, +78.9%, the fastest-growing line. The absolute amount is modest, but the direction matters: instant shopping is widening beyond emergency categories such as "medicine and alcohol" into everyday goods such as "supermarket and daily necessities."

The significance of this shift lies in frequency. Emergency items are bought three times a year; daily necessities three times a month. As frequency rises, per-order fulfillment costs can be diluted and instant retail shifts from a "subsidy business" to an "efficiency business."

What Li Lu would focus on is penetration: China's instant retail penetration is still low, but fulfillment costs are also high. Where these two curves cross determines the ceiling of this industry. His judgment is usually: in China, the efficiency dividend is released faster than most people think, because urban density and labor supply are both unique.

Going global: what 22 months of profitability means

Keeta reached profitability in Saudi Arabia in July 2026, 22 months after launch; Hong Kong UE turned positive in 2026Q4; Saudi Arabia already accounts for about 25% of Keeta's daily orders. The Middle East footprint now spans Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait and the UAE, and Brazil is already live.

The 22-month mark matters far more than the profit it generated. It proves that Meituan's dispatch system, ground-game playbook and rider management system can be replicated across cultures — something many Chinese companies going overseas cannot do: they can export products, but not organisations.

Li Lu would also caution: Saudi Arabia is a market with high average order value, weak competition and cheap labour; Brazil is not. In Brazil the rival is Didi's 99Food, with low average order value, fierce subsidies and a complex tax regime. USD 1 billion over 5 years is the ticket, not the ceiling. If Brazil can replicate Saudi Arabia, Meituan's story shifts from "Chinese platform" to "global instant delivery," and its valuation framework must be rewritten; if it cannot, this is just another case of "paying tuition for going overseas."

AI: This Time It's Not a Story

R&D came in at RMB 7.67 billion, +22.5%, while the loss on unallocated items widened 57.2% YoY. Together, the two numbers show AI spending is real money being spent, not money spent at a launch event.

Where it goes: the LongCat model series; two user-facing AI assistants, "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan" (with over 100 million users planned to spend via "Xiaotuan" during Spring Festival); 3.4 million merchants on the AI operations assistant; drones across 70 routes with 780,000 cumulative orders; and autonomous vehicles.

What Li Lu would care about: is this spending replacing labour costs, or adding features? If the former, it shows up as gross margin expansion in three to five years; if the latter, it is marketing expense. On public information, unmanned delivery and AI dispatch fall in the former category, AI assistants in the latter. Wang Xing saying "we won't be a token factory" shows he knows where the difference lies.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: this quarterly report carries more long-term information than short-term information. Near term, it is RMB 2.69 billion and 2.6%; longer term, it is three things advancing at once: category expansion, overseas validation and AI deployment. He would buy a little at HKD 77.5, because what he is buying is a decade of "China efficiency dividend + overseas replication", not next quarter's profit.
