Look at users first, then at profit

Core view

The most valuable line in this earnings report is not the RMB 2.69 billion operating profit, but delivery service revenue up 13.1% year on year. Profit can be manufactured by cutting costs; delivery revenue only comes from users actually placing orders.

One line of data shows users did not leave

During the 2025 subsidy war, everyone was asking one question: of the share Meituan lost, was it low-price orders or users? This earnings report answers it.

Delivery service revenue reached RMB 26.78 billion, up 13.1% year on year, returning to positive growth. The announcement attributes this to "prudent investment in incentives" — translated: less subsidy, yet order volume and average order value held. If users had truly left, revenue would have fallen once subsidies were pulled back, not risen.

Clearer evidence comes from new initiatives: Shanguo's existing-user frequency rose, and both order mix and user mix improved. Rising frequency among high-frequency users is the hardest data to fake — it means users come for the habit, not the discount.

But one line makes me uneasy.

Merchant services revenue was RMB 39.60 billion, up just 4.0%. This segment is commissions plus advertising, a direct reflection of the platform's bargaining power over merchants.

What does 4.0% mean? It means 8 million merchants did not get bigger; they were merely retained. Commissions cannot be raised — regulators are watching, merchants are cutting costs, and Douyin is poaching them with low commissions; advertising is not selling either — merchants themselves are cutting marketing budgets.

The platform's business is essentially "help merchants make more money, then take a share." If merchants do not make money, the platform cannot take a share. This line of data is more truthful than any management statement: the ecosystem is holding steady, but it is not improving.

Where the money went

R&D RMB 7.67 billion, up 22.5% YoY, 7.3% of revenue. R&D was not cut at the height of the subsidy war — that deserves a mention.

Where did the money go? The LongCat model series, two AI assistants "Xiaomei" and "Xiaotuan", drones across 70 routes and 780,000 orders, autonomous delivery vehicles, and an AI business assistant for merchants (used by over 3.4 million merchants).These investments share one thing: they all target "higher fulfillment efficiency" or "lower merchant costs", not "telling an AI story".

Duan Yongping's standard is "do the right things, and do things right". Spending on fulfillment and merchant tools is the right thing; whether it succeeds is a matter of capability, not of duty. This one passes.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style conclusion: users are still there, habits are still there, and the money was spent in the right places; but merchants are not making more money, and that is the current ceiling for this business. After reading this earnings report, he would be willing to buy a little, but not much—because a 2.6% profit margin shows that this business has not yet returned to a "highly profitable" state.
