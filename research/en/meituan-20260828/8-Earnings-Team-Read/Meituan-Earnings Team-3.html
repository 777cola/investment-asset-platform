Earnings Deep Dive (Munger) — Meituan

How did this RMB 4.67 billion loss come about?
Research date 2026/08/28  ·  Price HK$77.50 (+0.00%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 478.6 billion (6.175 billion shares)  ·  PE — (net loss of RMB 4.67 billion in H1 2026; PE distorted)

Core view

A net loss of RMB 4.67 billion in the first half, all of it in Q1 (-RMB 6.83 billion). Q2 earned RMB 2.16 billion, pulling the half-year back to -RMB 4.67 billion. Same industry, same quarter, same company, and profit swings RMB 9 billion within three months—Munger would call this business "too unpredictable."

Source of losses: subsidies, not operations

First quarter versus second quarter: revenue of RMB 91.04 billion against RMB 104.64 billion (up 14.9% QoQ), and operating profit of -RMB 6.47 billion against +RMB 2.69 billion — a swing of RMB 9.16 billion.

Revenue grew just 15%, yet profit swung RMB 9.16 billion: the swing came from expenses, not revenue. In the first quarter the money went out on user incentives and rider subsidies; in the second it came back. That is the whole story.

This is the kind of business Munger hated most: the income statement is dictated by competitors' moves, not by its own operations. He cannot know what it earns next quarter, because that depends on how much Alibaba wants to lose.

So why is it still worth watching: rivals are bleeding too

The question is the reverse: if this business is so bad, why are the three giants still fighting? The answer: when they started, each thought it could win; halfway through, each found it could not afford to lose.

Consider two sets of numbers. Alibaba: Taobao Shangou lost roughly RMB 100 billion cumulatively over five quarters, while incremental revenue after stripping out the existing business was only RMB 33–35 billion. JD.com: new businesses lost about RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter, and its share fell from a peak of 31% to 7%. Neither set of numbers works, so both are pulling back — Alibaba has halved its investment for the new fiscal year, and JD.com has pivoted to talking up "full-time rider compliance."

Munger would say: irrational competition does not end because someone is right; it ends because someone runs out of money. All three still have money, but all three have started doing the math. That is the real meaning of the inflection point — not that Meituan won, but that this war can no longer be fought.

The most underappreciated section of the earnings report: new businesses

New business revenue came in at RMB 33.11 billion, +25%, with a loss of RMB 1.74 billion and the loss ratio narrowing from 7.1% to 5.3%. The market reads this segment as a "loss-narrowing story"; I think it matters more than that.

Three things sit inside it: Xiaoxiang Supermarket covers 68 cities (39 cities at end-2025, doubling in half a year), 40 Happy Monkey stores, and Keeta turning profitable in Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia. The first is scale, the second is a format experiment, the third is overseas validation.

Munger would ask: which of these three can contribute profit within three years? The answer is the third. Fresh-grocery self-operation (Xiaoxiang, Happy Monkey) is an asset-heavy business, and no one in China has made real money at it; Keeta Saudi Arabia reaching profitability in 22 months is the one that most resembles a "replicable business." If the new business's valuation ever comes to rest on Saudi Arabia and Brazil, the company's nature changes — from a Chinese local-services platform to an emerging-market delivery platform. That is another story, and another multiple.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: This is a business with "unpredictable profits," but its current price already reflects that flaw. Quarterly profit of RMB 2.69 billion and a 2.6% margin show it is still far from a "good business"; but rivals' retrenchment shows "the worst of the competition" is over. He would buy, but would demand a discount—and that discount is the gap between 15x and 25x.
