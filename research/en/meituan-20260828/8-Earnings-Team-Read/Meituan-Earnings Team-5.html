Core view

Four people reading the same financial report reach conclusions that overlap by 70%: the inflection is real, earnings quality is average, the cash flow turnaround is the hardest evidence, and capital allocation is conservative. Disagreement centers on two points: how many quarters this improvement can last, and what multiple to assign.

Consensus 1: The inflection is real, but there is only one quarter of evidence

All four acknowledge that 2026Q2 marks a turning point: operating profit swung from RMB -6.47 billion (Q1) to RMB +2.69 billion, core local commerce from RMB -2.03 billion to RMB +5.67 billion, and the new-business loss ratio fell from 7.1% to 5.3%. Three directions improving at once is unlikely to be a coincidence.

But all four also caution: this is one quarter of evidence, and it is the peak season. The real question is Q3—the summer peak overlaps with rider subsidies, nationwide contributions to the new occupational injury insurance, and heavier investment in in-store services, and management itself has guided to rising costs. If Q3 still holds above RMB 2 billion, the inflection point stands; if not, Q2 was a peak-season illusion.

Consensus 2: Positive cash flow matters more than positive profit

Q2 operating cash flow was +RMB 9.7 billion, 4.5x net profit; for full-year 2025 it was -RMB 13.815 billion. The significance of this reversal: it proves the improvement is not accounting treatment but real cash.

Profit can be managed — recognize a little less impairment, a little more other income; cash flow is hard to manage. So when looking at this company, I look at operating cash flow first, then operating profit. If some day profit looks good but cash flow deteriorates, that is a danger signal.

Disagreement: 12x or 20x — the gap lies in the "endgame"

Munger assigns 12–15x because he believes competitors will forever cap margins; Li Lu is willing to pay over 20x because he believes overseas expansion can rewrite the business's nature. This 8x gap is not a financial disagreement; it is a disagreement in worldview.

My verdict: take the midpoint at 20x (corresponding to HKD 110), because overseas expansion has not yet contributed profit and cannot be valued in advance; but we also cannot assign 12x, because 12x is the price of "never repairing," and there are now three improvement signals.

In other words: 20x is the price of "repair succeeds," 12x is the price of "repair fails," and the current HKD 77.5 sits below the midpoint between the two. That is why you can buy, but not with a heavy position.

The Price of Disagreement: What Does 8x Equal?

Munger would pay 12–15x; Li Lu is willing to pay over 20x. What is that 8x gap worth? On 2027 net profit of RMB 25 billion, 12x gives HKD 48 (about HKD 67 including net cash), and 20x gives HKD 81 (about HKD 100 including net cash). In other words, the spread between the two worldviews is HKD 33, equal to 43% of the current share price.

That number shows: the return on this Meituan investment comes mainly from the "multiple assumption," not the "profit assumption." Profit rising from RMB 25 billion to RMB 35 billion is only 40% more; the multiple rising from 12x to 20x is 67% more.

So buying Meituan is essentially buying "how many times the market is willing to pay," not "how much money it can earn." That is also why it can only be a small position — the multiple depends on sentiment and narrative, not on the financials.

Conclusion

Synthesis: All four agree on the facts and disagree on the multiple; the disagreement over the multiple stems from "terminal-state assumptions," not "current-period data." This means the marginal return from further work on quarterly reports is already low—what really matters is industry structure (whether Alibaba truly exits) and overseas expansion (whether Brazil can replicate Saudi Arabia).

Action: buy 2% at HKD 77.5; HKD 110 is the neutral target, HKD 60 the bear-case floor. No action in between; wait for Q3.
