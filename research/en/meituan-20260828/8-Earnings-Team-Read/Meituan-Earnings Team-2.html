Core view

If you look at only one statement, make it the balance sheet. RMB 168.3 billion in cash and short-term investments, quarterly operating cash flow of +RMB 9.7 billion and no major capex—this company will not die from this fight. The 2.6% on the income statement is war carnage, not a symptom of underlying ill health.

Income statement: where did RMB 2.69 billion come from?

Revenue RMB 104.64 billion, cost of sales RMB 69.55 billion, gross profit RMB 35.10 billion (33.5%). After deducting marketing of RMB 24.72 billion and R&D of RMB 7.67 billion and adding other items, operating profit comes to RMB 2.69 billion.

Gross margin rose 0.6 percentage points YoY and the marketing expense ratio fell 0.6 percentage points, together worth about RMB 1.26 billion—the main driver of the swing from "near break-even" to "RMB 2.69 billion." In other words, this quarter's profit was saved, not earned.

Can saved profit persist? We think not: management itself flags higher per-order delivery cost in Q3 (summer subsidies plus nationwide contributions to the new work-injury insurance) and stepped-up investment in in-store.Profit made by pinching pennies has a shelf life.

Cash flow: the float business

Q2 net operating cash inflow was RMB 9.7 billion against net profit of RMB 2.155 billion — cash flow ran at 4.5x profit. The structure is familiar: users pay upfront, merchants settle later and riders are paid weekly, so the platform permanently holds a pool of interest-free float.

The appeal of the float model is that cash flow leads profit as long as order volume holds. The risk is that once order volume declines, the float drains out faster than profit falls. 2025 is the case in point — full-year operating cash flow of -RMB 13.815 billion, plugged by RMB 21.243 billion of net financing inflow.

What I watch, then, is not profit but the gap between order volume and operating cash flow. While that gap keeps widening (cash flow > profit), the business is still running normally; once it turns negative, the ecosystem is contracting.

Capital allocation: the only area where I mark it down

Book cash and short-term investments of RMB 168.3 billion, no dividend, and only HKD 200 million of buybacks in June 2026 (2.9248 million shares at HKD 66.5–69).

That combination is odd. A company that calls itself "severely undervalued", with the share price 27% below its 52-week high and RMB 168.3 billion of cash on the books, bought back only HKD 200 million—not a lack of money, but a reluctance to spend it.

Three explanations come to mind. First, the cash is being kept for the next battle (Brazil, AI). Second, management does not think the current price is cheap enough. Third, the governance structure dictates it: Wang Xing controls 45.3% of voting rights through A/B shares, leaving minority shareholders with no means to push for buybacks. The first is the most likely, and also the most unsettling.

Conclusion

Buffett-style verdict: balance sheet scores 8 (net cash, asset-light, strong cash flow), income statement scores 4 (2.6% margin, profits from cost savings), capital allocation scores 5 (has the cash but does not buy back stock).On that combined read, this is a name you can hold but cannot overweight—unless buyback intensity proves management genuinely cares about per-share value.
