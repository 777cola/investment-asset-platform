Six-Gate Screen — Meituan

Core thesis

Six gates 43/60: circle of competence 9, good business 7, moat 8, management 7, margin of safety 6, discipline 6 — five gates passed, margin of safety barely. All the deductions come down to one thing — how much of the margin the subsidy war destroyed, and how much can come back. Q2 2026 revenue RMB 104.64 billion, +14.4% YoY; operating profit RMB 2.69 billion, turning positive; core local commerce operating profit RMB 5.67 billion, +52.3% YoY — the first evidence of an inflection. But the first half still posted a net loss of RMB 4.67 billion, and food-delivery order share fell from about 65% to about 51% in a year, proving the moat can be filled in with capital. On price, the HKD 478.6 billion market cap is about 15x 2027 normalized profit and about 12x net of net cash, a payoff ratio of 1:1.8. Conclusion: buyable, but only in small tranches, because management itself guided Q3 to be worse than Q2.

Gate 1: Circle of competence (9/10, pass)

Meituan's revenue has only two legs: Core Local Commerce at RMB 71.53 billion and New Initiatives at RMB 33.11 billion, totaling RMB 104.64 billion in 2026Q2. Core Local Commerce splits into three blocks — delivery services RMB 26.78 billion (+13.1%), merchant services RMB 39.60 billion (+4.0%), and product sales of about RMB 5.15 billion (derived by subtracting the first two from segment revenue, +78.9% YoY, mainly self-operated categories such as pharmaceuticals and liquor). New Initiatives comprises grocery retail (Xiaoxiang Supermarket covering 68 cities, 40 Happy Monkey stores, Kuailv) plus overseas Keeta.Reading the financials requires watching only three lines: delivery services revenue, merchant services revenue, and Core Local Commerce operating profit.

Understanding it is not the same as forecasting it. Revenue can be forecast: order volume × average order value, merchant count × commission rate, ad load — all three variables have historical ranges. Profit cannot be forecast: it depends on how much management and rivals are each willing to burn in a given quarter. Full-year 2025 revenue was RMB 364.85 billion, up only 8.1%, yet operating profit swung from +RMB 45.1 billion in 2024 to -RMB 17.0 billion,a mere 8-point gap in revenue and a RMB 62.1 billion gap in profit — that is what this business looks like now.The 1.0-point deduction comes here.

Gate two: good business (7/10, pass)

First, how good it looks at its best: 2024 core local commerce operating margin 20.9%, operating profit RMB 45.1 billion (group basis) — a business with 33% gross margin, almost no capex, and cash collected before it is paid out. That is one of the best business models in Chinese internet. Now how bad it looks at its worst: 2025 core local commerce posted an operating loss of RMB 6.9 billion, and 2026Q1 lost another RMB 2.03 billion. The same business line went from +20.9% to -2.6% within two years.

The essence is "trading fulfillment density for time value": delivery within three kilometers in thirty minutes, with per-order cost falling as order density rises — real economies of scale. But this curve has two ceilings. First, density is geographic — density in Beijing's Chaoyang district cannot dilute the cost of a county town in Hebei; economies of scale hold only segment by segment across 2,800 counties and districts. Second, the largest cost item is riders, a labor cost that can only rise, never fall; from July 1, 2026, nationwide coverage of the "new occupational injury" insurance for riders, with premiums fully borne by the platform, is an irreversible step up in cost.

What the 7 means: in steady state it is a good business, but half its income statement will always be decided by others — rivals' subsidies, regulatory costs, rider supply.

Gate 3: Moat (8/10, pass)

Three things competitors cannot build in the short term: the fulfillment network (about 6 million riders with active orders, 2,800 counties and districts, a minute-level dispatch system), merchant supply (about 8 million active merchants, especially small and mid-sized restaurants with dine-in), and user mindshare ("order delivery on Meituan," with almost no substitute for high-ticket full meals). These three reinforce each other: more merchants leads to more users leads to more riders leads to faster delivery leads to merchants becoming more dependent.

But the 2025 battle proved one thing: this moat keeps out new entrants, but not giants willing to lose money. Based on Goldman Sachs' March 2026 order-volume estimates, Meituan's share fell from about 65% in early 2025 to about 51% in February 2026; the "GTV share above 60%" that the company emphasized in its 2025 annual report is on a value basis. Both numbers are correct and describe different things: Meituan held high-ticket orders and lost low-price orders. The moat remains, but it has narrowed, and for the first time it has been quantitatively proven purchasable.

8 points rather than 6: because subsidies buy share, not density. Taobao Shangou's 45% order share still carries higher fulfillment costs than Meituan, which is already visible in the two companies' UE trends in 2026Q2.

Gate 4: Management (7/10, pass)

This test is not about capability; it is about trade-offs. At the height of the subsidy war in 2025, Wang Xing publicly declared "resolute opposition to involution," choosing to protect unit economics rather than order volume — the result was a 14-percentage-point loss of share and a full-year operating loss of RMB 17 billion. In the short term it looks like a loss; in the long term it means the money went where it can be recovered: Q2 2026 core local commerce operating profit was RMB 5.67 billion, up 52.3% year on year, driven by lower user incentive spending and lower per-order delivery costs — not by price increases, not by higher commission rates.

Capital allocation cuts both ways. The good: AI spending is restrained, with Q2 2026 R&D of RMB 7.67 billion, or 7.3% of revenue; Wang Xing explicitly said the company will not "build a token factory" and will not join the compute arms race. A USD 2 billion buyback plan was approved in June 2024, and after shareholder re-authorization in June 2026 the company began small-scale repurchases in the HKD 66.5–69 range (2.9248 million shares over June 29–30, about HKD 200 million), with CFO Chen Shaohui saying publicly in June that "the company's value is severely undervalued." The bad: Keeta's overseas expansion is a new capital drain, with a signed five-year USD 1 billion investment agreement in Brazil — the one variable that could spiral out of control.

7 points: knows how to do the math, can endure, does not spend recklessly, but no "trade profit for the future" decision has yet been fully validated. The buyback is also on the small side — HKD 200 million against a HKD 478.6 billion market cap amounts to nothing.

Gate 5: Margin of safety (6/10, barely passes)

The arithmetic first. HKD 77.50 × 6.175 billion shares = HKD 478.56 billion, which at HKD 1 ≈ RMB 0.92 converts to RMB 440.3 billion. As of 30 June 2026, cash and equivalents on the books were RMB 104.7 billion and short-term wealth management products RMB 63.6 billion, for a total of RMB 168.3 billion; after deducting interest-bearing debt and restricted funds, the working paper uses net cash of about RMB 120 billion (a media-basis estimate, not a disclosed figure). Enterprise value is about RMB 320.3 billion.

The denominator has two versions. Conservative case: 2027 net profit of RMB 25.0 billion (core local commerce operating profit RMB 37.5 billion, new businesses -RMB 3.0 billion, AI and other unallocated -RMB 5.0 billion, rough after-tax calculation), implying 17.6x market cap and 12.8x EV. Slightly more optimistic case: normalized profit of RMB 29.0 billion, implying 15.2x and 11.0x EV. The working paper's "about 15x, about 12x ex-net cash" is simply the top and bottom of these two versions — not a contradiction, but a range.

There is only one reason for a 6 rather than an 8: the word "normalized" is an assumption, not a fact.First-half 2026 was still a net loss of RMB 4.67 billion, and the full year will most likely remain loss-making (roughly -RMB 5.0 billion to -RMB 7.0 billion). On the current year's loss, the stock has no PE; on 2027 assumptions, it is cheap. A full year of evidence sits between the two.

Gate 6: Discipline (6/10, pass)

The discipline test is not about whether the company is good, but whether my buy thesis can be falsified within an acceptable timeframe. It can. All three are quantifiable: (1) 2026Q3 core local commerce operating profit no less than RMB 2 billion (Q2 was RMB 5.67 billion; management has already flagged higher per-order delivery costs and heavier investment in in-store for Q3); (2) industry subsidy levels decline quarter on quarter — management itself said on the earnings call that they "remain above 2024"; (3) merchant services revenue growth does not turn negative (Q2 was only +4.0%).

The answer to the first will be visible by the end of October — no need to wait three years. That is the most valuable thing about this investment — most "distressed reversals" require faith; this one requires a quarterly report.

A score of 6 because the probability of the premises holding is not high: Q3 is peak season but also a peak in investment, and the RMB 2 billion line itself sits at the low end of management guidance. I set that line so I admit I am wrong when I am wrong, not so I can feel comfortable.

Veto items and items to be verified

Veto items: none. No single item is enough to veto the thesis. But one hard constraint comes first: Q3 core local commerce operating profit falling below RMB 2 billion would show that the Q2 improvement was mainly a peak-season effect, and the rating drops immediately from "Buy" to "Watch" without waiting for the next earnings report.

To verify 1: the sequential direction of subsidies. Management explicitly said industry subsidies remain above 2024 levels and need "several quarters" to normalize. Watch two external signals: Alibaba's Taobao Shangou investment for the new fiscal year was reportedly halved, per 36Kr, and JD.com announced on July 17 that 100% of its full-time riders would sign labor contracts with full social insurance contributions (rising costs will force it to pull back).

To verify 2: in-store margins. In-store hotel and travel is the fattest profit pool; management has already flagged heavier investment in Q3 and Q4, with operating margin below Q2. If Q3 in-store margin falls more than 3 percentage points, full-year core local commerce profit will need to be revised down.

To verify 3: new rider costs. From July 1, "new occupational injury" insurance covers the whole country with premiums fully borne by the platform, and Q3 is the first earnings report to fully reflect it. At 65–75 million orders per day and RMB 0.1 per order, that is roughly RMB 2.5 billion annualized, about one-tenth of normalized 2027 profit — not fatal, but it will eat part of the recovery.

To verify 4: Keeta Brazil. USD 1 billion over 5 years is a commitment, not a cap. Saudi Arabia reaching profitability in 22 months proves the model can be replicated; Brazil is home turf for rival DiDi's 99Food, and this is the only variable that could turn a "small position" into a "wrong position."

Conclusion

Six gates 43/60, pass, but the pass is a strained one: the first three gates rest on assets and position, the last three on assumptions about the future. The buy case comes down to one sentence — normalized profit of RMB 25–29 billion in 2027, 11–13x after net cash, and the inflection point already showed its first evidence in Q2 2026.

Action: build a 2% position at HKD 77.5, add to 4% near HKD 65 (a 1:3 payoff), add to 5% if Q3 core local commerce operating profit comes in at ≥RMB 2 billion. The reverse conditions are equally clear: Q3 below RMB 2 billion, or industry subsidies trending back up — cut to under 1% or exit entirely.
