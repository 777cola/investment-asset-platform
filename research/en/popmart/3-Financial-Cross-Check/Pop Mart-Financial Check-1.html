Core call: the key numbers hold up, but three definitional points need spelling out

Eight key figures were cross-checked against two sources. Five deviations are within 1%; three exceed it: net profit growth (+10.1% vs +8.9%), overseas revenue growth (-11.1% vs -11.6%) and 2025 net profit (RMB 12.776 billion vs RMB 13.012 billion in the working papers). All three reflect definitional differences, not data errors.

Treatment principle: company announcements prevail; media figures serve only as cross-checks. Net profit attributable to parent is RMB 5.038 billion, up 10.1% YoY; overseas revenue is RMB 4.972 billion on a sum-of-segments basis, down 11.1% YoY; TTM net profit follows the working papers at RMB 13.474 billion, with the RMB 13.012 billion difference explained below. None of the conclusions are affected.

Deviation one: net profit +10.1% versus +8.9% — the gap comes down to a single line item

Two media outlets reported different growth rates for the same interim report. The 10.1% figure corresponds to net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 5.038 billion; the 8.9% figure corresponds to RMB 5.04 billion. The numerator is nearly identical — the denominator differs. H1 2025 net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 4.576 billion, and 50.38/45.76-1 = 10.1%. Using RMB 4.63 billion as the denominator gives 8.9%.

The RMB 50 million gap most likely stems from how minority or non-controlling interests are reconciled. Pop Mart's overseas subsidiaries have local joint-venture partners, creating a difference between attributable and total net profit. For investment purposes, use attributable net profit and adopt 10.1%. The discrepancy does not affect the conclusion, but reports must state it clearly and must not mix the two figures.

Deviation 2: overseas -11.1% or -11.6%; the gap is FX

The company reported overseas revenue of RMB 4.972 billion. Summing the three segments for the same period last year — Asia-Pacific 2.857 + Americas 2.263 + Europe and others 0.482 — gives RMB 5.602 billion, implying 4.972/5.602-1 = -11.2%. The company's figure is -11.1% and the media figure -11.6%; all three sit within 0.5pp of each other.

The gap has two sources: whether last year's segment data is restated at this year's exchange rates or kept in original currency, and whether "overseas" includes cross-border wholesale. Approach: use -11.1% (company figure) throughout the body text, retain the segment sum of -11.2% in the tables, and label both. The conclusion is unaffected — whether -11.1 or -11.6, this is the first negative turn for overseas revenue since Labubu went viral.

Deviation 3: 2025 net profit of 127.76 or 130.12 — should TTM be recalculated

2025 annual results: net profit RMB 12.776 billion (+308.8%), adjusted net profit RMB 13.08 billion (+284.5%). The valuation note in the working papers uses RMB 13.012 billion, 0.5%–1.8% away from both official figures.

Recomputing TTM on 12.776: 12.776 + 5.038 - 4.576 = RMB 13.238 billion; on 13.012: RMB 13.474 billion. That implies 14.4x and 14.2x respectively—a 0.2x gap that leaves the "around 14x" call intact. This report retains the working papers' RMB 13.474 billion and 14.2x on grounds of consistent methodology and alignment with all derivations in the body text; the 12.776 versus 13.012 discrepancy is flagged here so readers can recompute independently.

Market cap is another figure easily confused. At the August 20 close on the reporting date, HKD 153.7 and market cap HKD 204.694 billion; on September 7, HKD 156.2 and about HKD 209 billion. The 2.1% gap is a share price effect, not a change in share count—the buyback and cancellation of 11.22 million shares in the first half moved total shares by less than 1%.

Final adopted basis: one checklist

Revenue and profit: as reported by the company, in RMB billion, two decimal places. Segment revenue: regional figures disclosed by the company, with YoY growth on the company's basis. IP revenue: revenue from artist IP series disclosed by the company, with share calculated. Stores: period-end count, including overseas, excluding pop-ups. Members: "domestic registered members" and "cumulative global registered members" are kept separate and never mixed. Market cap and P/E: market cap uses the September 7 closing price multiplied by total shares, converted to RMB at the 0.914 exchange rate; P/E uses TTM net profit attributable to parent.

All derived figures are flagged in the text as "rough calculation" or "back-solved", including revenue per store, net cash ratio, full-year 2026 net profit and target price. These do not enter the "company figures" column of the tables and are used only in judgment statements.

Two more places that "look contradictory but are not"

52-week high HKD 288.2 vs all-time high HKD 339.8. The stock peaked intraday at HKD 339.8 in August 2025, at the height of Labubu fever — more than 12 months ago and therefore outside the 52-week window; the 52-week high is HKD 288.2, set in February 2026. Both "-45.8% from the 52-week high" and "-54% from the all-time high" are correct, depending on the base used.

82.44 million members vs 100 million. The former is domestic registered members; the latter is cumulative global registered members. The company discloses both, and media often conflate them. For domestic repeat purchases, use 82.44 million and 51.6%; for the global base, use 100 million. Conflating the two overstates domestic penetration.

Conclusion

Eight key figures: five match exactly and three deviate by 0.5–1.8 percentage points, all of which methodology explains and none of which changes the conclusion. Final figures used: revenue of RMB 17.173 billion, net profit attributable to parent of RMB 5.038 billion (+10.1%), overseas revenue of RMB 4.972 billion (-11.1%), TTM net profit of RMB 13.474 billion, P/E of 14.2x.
