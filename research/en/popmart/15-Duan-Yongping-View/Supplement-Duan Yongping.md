# Pop Mart（09992）— Duan Yongping Supplement

- Research date：2026/09/07
- Price：156.2 HKD
- Market cap：About HKD 209 billion

## Notes

Duan Yongping's shareholding data comes from HKEX disclosure of interests (2026/07/06, H&H International's stake rising from 6.85% to 7.65%, 102 million shares) and Shanghai Securities News (2026/07/10); "As a shareholder, I actually feel pretty good about it" is quoted from 21st Century Business Herald's paraphrase of his remarks on an investment platform on the evening of 2026/08/20. The Q&A is this report's inference based on his public investment framework, not his own words (except where quoted). Valuation parameters: 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion, 2027 net profit of RMB 10–12 billion, 15x PE, 0.914 exchange rate, 1.342 billion shares.

## Question 1: Is this a good business?

Good. Good in three ways.

First, gross margin of 69.7%. A company selling small toys for a few dozen yuan that can hold this gross margin shows it is not selling plastic — it is selling "liking." Liking costs little to produce and has very low price elasticity. This is what a good business looks like — no need to fight price wars.

Second, collect cash first, ship later. 82.44 million members; the online blind-box draw takes payment before opening, so cash flow is positive. Inventories are tied up for 201 days this year, but that is an expansion problem, not a model problem.

Third, expansion does not require heavy spending. Stores are leased, factories belong to others; the company only puts up design and rent. A company that can reach RMB 37.1 billion in revenue on "renting space + outsourcing manufacturing" — that is a very good model.

Where is it not good? Demand is "want," not "need." What you want today, you may not want tomorrow. This is different from Moutai or Coca-Cola.

## Question 2: Will it still be around in ten years?

The company will most likely still be here, the business will most likely still be here, but whether "it is still the leader" I do not know.

The designer-toy category exists in Japan, the US and China, and has survived twenty years, so demand will not disappear. But look at Funko, the US designer-toy leader: it was hot at IPO, and now the stock has fallen from USD 30 to USD 5. Revenue is still there; profit is gone. The category survives; the company may not. The company survives; the profit may not.

Pop Mart's edge over Funko is proprietary IP (artist IP RMB 15.29 billion) rather than licensing. Proprietary IP carries higher gross margin and a longer life cycle. But it shares Funko's problem: it must keep releasing new characters to sustain heat, and momentum drops the moment it stops.

So my answer is: in ten years it will most likely still be here, but I cannot be sure it will still be this profitable in ten years. That "uncertainty" is the discount I demand on the price.

## Question three: Can you understand it?

The business is understandable; the earnings are not. Keep the two separate.

The business is understandable: sign artists, make products, open stores, sell to young people. I can grasp that chain at a glance; no need to understand technology or policy.

The earnings are not: when does the next hit arrive? When does Labubu fade? When do overseas sales stop falling? Wang Ning himself cannot answer these three questions. He has said as much: "many of this year's difficulties were unforeseen."

Not understanding the earnings does not mean you cannot invest. If you understand the business, you can buy when "the price is low enough," using price to hedge what you cannot understand. But if the price is not low, not understanding is a reason not to buy.

## Question 4: At what price to buy?

Rough estimate. 2026 net profit of about RMB 11 billion; 2027, if overseas stops declining, possibly RMB 12 billion; if it does not stop declining, RMB 10 billion. What is this company worth? At 15x as a consumer stock, that is RMB 150 billion to 180 billion, or HKD 164 billion to 197 billion, corresponding to a share price of HKD 122 to 147.

So above HKD 147, I would have no interest; below HKD 122, it starts to get interesting; below HKD 100, it is "clearly cheap."

The current price is HKD 156.2, 6% above my ceiling. It is not that you cannot buy it, it is that after buying there is no margin of safety. My habit when buying stocks is "not to panic even if it falls 30% after I buy." Buying at HKD 156, a 30% drop takes it to HKD 109, and at that point I would panic, because I am not sure whether it is worth 109 or 80.

## Question 5: What worries you most?

Two things worry me most.

First, the risk that it turns into a "distribution company." If it ever stops creating its own IP and instead licenses other companies' characters and sells goods through stores, it shifts from an "IP company" to a "retail company," gross margin falls from 70% to 40%, and the valuation multiple falls from 15x to 8x. It has not reached that point yet (artist IP accounts for 89%), but the risk of straying is highest when overseas expansion moves too fast.

Second, the risk that Wang Ning loses discipline. Not that he lacks it now — he voluntarily slowed growth, spoke plainly and bought back stock, all of which show discipline. The fear is that one day he starts "telling stories": the metaverse, AI, the ecosystem. Pop Mart does not need these stories; it only needs to create a few characters each year that people want to buy. When a company starts telling stories unrelated to its own business, it is usually a signal that the core business is in trouble.

## Question 6: You are already the second-largest shareholder, so why not buy more?

He would probably answer the question this way: it was cheap when I bought it.

HKEX disclosures show H&H raised its stake from 6.85% to 7.65%, or 102 million shares, on 6 July. Note the timing — he had been buying since 2024, at a cost far below HKD 156. Adding in July was "topping up after a fall", not "the current value is worth buying".

After the interim results, someone asked him whether overseas was too weak, and he said, "As a shareholder, I actually think it's quite good." That does not mean "the share price will rise"; it means "the business is not broken". Shareholder and buyer are two identities: the shareholder asks whether the company will still be here in ten years; the buyer asks whether today's price is a good deal.

So do not treat my holding as a buy signal. My cost may be HKD 80 and yours HKD 156 — we face the same company, but not the same investment.
