Core call: this is a stock waiting on price, not on earnings

Three-scenario expected value of HKD 148.75 sits 4.8% below the current price of HKD 156.2, so the position is now 0. Entry range below HKD 120, first tranche 3%; add to 5% if overseas revenue stops declining sequentially in the Q3 report; cap 8%, never 10%.

The stance is not bearish but uncertain. The make-or-break factor for this business is the next hit product, and hit products cannot be predicted—you can only bear the uncertainty with a small position, not bet on it with a heavy one.

Why a 0.0 position now: not bearish, just insufficient risk-reward

The maths is simple: three scenarios of HKD 100 / 150 / 225 with probabilities of 30% / 45% / 25% give an expected value of HKD 148.75. Against the current price of HKD 156.2, that is an expected return of -4.8%. A negative-expectation bet warrants a 0 position, however good the company.

The counter-argument — "bullish long term, short-term volatility doesn't matter" — holds only in two cases: you hold for ten years without touching the position, or intrinsic value compounds at more than 15% a year. Pop Mart satisfies neither today. Its intrinsic value hinges on the next hit product, which makes time an adversary, not an ally.

A 0 position is therefore not "avoidance" but "waiting." The cost of waiting is opportunity cost if the stock rallies; the benefit is odds if it falls. At the current valuation, waiting is the better trade.

Add, trim, stop-loss: three hard-coded price levels

Add: below HKD 120, in three tranches. First tranche at HKD 120 (3%), second at HKD 110 (+1%), third at HKD 100 (+1%). Do not rush to buy at HKD 130—that level merely moves the stock from very expensive to expensive, not to cheap.

Trim: above HKD 200. That equates to 24x 2026E, already discounting most of the optimistic scenario (HKD 225). At that level, trim regardless of how good the fundamentals look. Pop Mart is not Moutai; it does not deserve a 30x P/E.

Stop-loss/re-rating: below HKD 100 with deteriorating fundamentals. Note the "and"—a simple break below HKD 100 is a buying opportunity; only when paired with domestic same-store sales turning negative or gross margin falling below 66% does it become a stop-loss. The distinction: price declines reflect market sentiment, whereas deteriorating fundamentals mean the business has changed.

One more discipline: no position-building within one week before or after earnings releases. Pop Mart's earnings swings are extreme (down 6% in a single day after the August 20 interim report, up 4% on August 24). Buying during information-dense periods hands judgment over to luck. Wait for the dust to settle; the price is usually better.

Correlation: it will fall together with your other holdings

Pop Mart does not diversify the portfolio; it amplifies the same beta. New consumer names sold off across the board in 2026: Laopu Gold down 46% from its high, Mixue down 45%, MINISO down 48% year to date and Pop Mart down 45.8% from its 52-week high. All four trade in close lockstep because they share a single macro variable: Chinese consumers' discretionary spending.

For a portfolio that already holds Laopu, Mixue and MINISO, adding Pop Mart is equivalent to levering up the new-consumer position 1.5x, not diversifying. True diversification means allocating to staples or cash-flow assets.

The second correlation is FX. Overseas revenue accounts for 29%; a stronger renminbi produces FX losses (RMB 720 million in the first half) and a weaker renminbi produces FX gains. That correlation runs opposite to export stocks and can serve as a hedge, but do not buy it for the sake of hedging.

Conclusion

Portfolio recommendation: 0 position; place three buy orders below HKD 120, cap 8%. Treat this as a "review once a quarter" position, not a "buy and forget" one. Three-item checklist: overseas sequential growth, inventory turnover, domestic same-store sales. If two of the three deteriorate, halve the position unconditionally; if all three hold up, add to the cap.
