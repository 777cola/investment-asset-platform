## Why a 0.0 position now: not bearish, just insufficient risk-reward

A simple calculation: three scenarios of HKD 100 / 150 / 225, probabilities 30% / 45% / 25%, expected value HKD 148.75. Current price HKD 156.2, expected return -4.8%. A negative-expectation bet warrants a 0 position, no matter how good the company is.

Some will say "bullish long term, short-term volatility doesn't matter." That argument holds in two cases: you hold for ten years without touching it, or the company's intrinsic value grows more than 15% a year. Pop Mart satisfies neither today — its intrinsic value depends on the next hit product, it is no friend of time.

So a 0 position is not "avoidance," it is "waiting." The cost of waiting is opportunity cost (if it rallies), the benefit of waiting is odds (if it falls). At the current valuation, waiting is the better trade.

## Add, trim, stop-loss: three hard-coded price levels

Add: below HKD 120, in three tranches. First tranche at HKD 120 (3%), second at HKD 110 (+1%), third at HKD 100 (+1%). Do not rush to buy at HKD 130—that level is merely "from very expensive to expensive," not "cheap."

Trim: above HKD 200. That corresponds to 24x 2026E, already pricing in most of the optimistic scenario (HKD 225). At that level, no matter how good the fundamentals, trim. Pop Mart is not Moutai; it does not deserve a 30x PE.

Stop-loss/re-rating: below HKD 100 with deteriorating fundamentals. Note the "and"—a simple break below HKD 100 is a buying opportunity; only when combined with "domestic same-store sales turning negative" or "gross margin falling below 66%" is it a stop-loss. The difference: price declines reflect market sentiment; deteriorating fundamentals mean the business has changed.

One more discipline: no position-building within one week before or after earnings releases. Pop Mart's earnings swings are extreme (down 6% in a single day after the August 20 interim report, up 4% on August 24). Buying during information-dense periods hands "judgment" over to luck. Wait for the dust to settle; the price is usually better.

## Correlation: it will fall together with your other holdings

Pop Mart's role in the portfolio is not diversification but an amplified version of the same beta. New consumer stocks retreated across the board in 2026: Laopu Gold down 46% from its high, Mixue down 45%, MINISO down 48% year to date, and Pop Mart down 45.8% from its 52-week high. These four stocks move in close lockstep because they share one macro variable: Chinese consumers' discretionary spending.

If the portfolio already holds Laopu, Mixue and MINISO, adding Pop Mart is equivalent to levering up the new-consumer position 1.5x, not diversifying. True diversification means allocating to staples or cash-flow assets.

The other correlation is FX. Overseas revenue accounts for 29%; a stronger renminbi produces FX losses (RMB 720 million in the first half), a weaker renminbi produces FX gains. This correlation runs opposite to export stocks and can serve as a hedge, but do not buy it for the sake of hedging.
