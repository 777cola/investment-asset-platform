Earnings Read — Pop Mart

Core call: having worked through all four layers, the issue is not revenue itself but the quality of that revenue

The interim report breaks down into four clear layers. Revenue: domestic +47.3% held up while overseas -11.1% dragged, reversing the mix from "overseas engine" to "domestic carrying it alone." Gross margin: 69.7%, down only 0.6pp; the cost of shifting the category mix from figures to plush is manageable. Profit: net profit +10.1%, FX gains and losses netted out at RMB 840 million, and expenses ran ahead of revenue. Cash: inventory turnover stretched from 123 to 201 days, net cash ratio about 0.7x, and cash is tied up in inventory.

First revenue layer: a structural reversal, not domestic offsetting overseas

Mainland China accounted for RMB 12.20 billion of the RMB 17.17 billion total, or 71.0%, up from 59.7% a year earlier. Overseas markets contributed RMB 4.97 billion, or 29.0%, down 11.3pp from 40.3%. This is not "China offsetting overseas weakness" — it is a structural reversal: overseas has gone from growth engine to drag.

By region: Asia-Pacific -9.7%, Americas -16.5%, Europe +5.9% (the smallest at RMB 510 million). Two of the three regions are shrinking, and the only one growing accounts for just 3%.

The channel split explains it: the decline comes almost entirely from online. Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas -45.6%, Europe -59.0%, Shopee -62.1%. Over the same period, overseas stores rose by a net 36 (Americas 41→86, Europe 18→45, Asia-Pacific 90). Offline is growing, online is collapsing, and together they net to -11.1%. Management attributes this to "fading external traffic dividends" — in other words, the organic traffic from going viral on short video is gone, and the company must now grind out money store by store.

One more detail: domestic online was RMB 4.78 billion, +62.7% YoY, of which the blind-box machine app alone accounted for RMB 2.06 billion. Domestic growth is therefore not being dragged up by stores — it is the "online blind-box draw" format spreading. The economics of blind-box machines are excellent: pay first, open later, no inventory display cost, and gross margins higher than stores. This is Pop Mart's most undervalued asset in China.

Second-tier gross margin of 69.7% leaves the cost of category mix shift manageable

Gross margin slipped from 70.3% to 69.7%, a decline of just 0.6pp — stable, given the sharp category mix shift over the half-year.

The mix shift: plush toys RMB 9.825 billion, 57.2% of revenue, +60.0% YoY; figures RMB 5.192 billion, 30.2%, +0.3% YoY. Plush became the largest category for the first time, while figures essentially stopped growing. Plush is harder to make (stitching, filling, fabric), carries longer lead times and higher unit prices, and earns a gross margin a few points below figures — yet scale effects limited the drag on overall gross margin to 0.6pp.

Management cites two reasons: a lower share of high-margin overseas business (overseas gross margin exceeds domestic), and higher raw material costs. The first is structural, the second cyclical. The first is the real concern — overseas revenue fell from 40.3% to 29.0% of the total, and every point lost pulls the blended gross margin down. If overseas keeps falling, gross margin will break below 69%.

One caveat on disclosure: the company does not break out gross margin by category. The "plush a few points below figures" above is the industry's common judgment, not a company figure. The blended gross margin nonetheless lets us infer it: plush rose from under 40% to 57.2% of revenue while overall gross margin fell only 0.6pp, implying a narrow gap between the two — 3 to 5 points at most. That is good news: the category shift did not come at the expense of profitability.

Tier three — costs and profit: FX is noise, expenses are a choice

Net profit +10.1% against revenue +23.8% — a 13.7 percentage point gap. Two items explain it.

FX: a RMB 720 million loss in the first half versus a RMB 120 million gain a year earlier, an RMB 840 million swing. Against net profit of RMB 5.038 billion, this item consumed 14% of profit. Expenses: distribution and selling expenses +23.1%, roughly in line with revenue at first glance, but the mix is very different — rent +43.3%, employee compensation +45.7%, with the sales team expanding from 6,219 to 9,734 people. Store-opening costs come first; revenue follows.

The two are fundamentally different in nature. FX is uncontrollable, unsustainable and does not reflect operations; expenses are a deliberate choice that reflects "trading profit for stores." Excluding FX, profit growth is about 28%, above revenue growth; operations have not deteriorated — the pressure comes from the financial and investment levels. To judge this company, look at operating profit excluding FX, not reported net profit.

One more calculation: adding back the RMB 840 million FX difference gives first-half net profit of RMB 5.878 billion, +28.4% YoY, and a net margin of 34.2%. That is higher than last year's 33.0%. So the claim of "declining net margin" holds only on a reported basis; on an operating basis it is rising. The market reads reported figures, so the stock falls; you read operations, so you know the business is intact. That gap is where the opportunity comes from — but the price has not yet reflected it.

Tier four: cash flow and balance sheet — where the money went

Cash fell from RMB 13.78 billion at the start of the year to RMB 12.44 billion, a decrease of RMB 1.34 billion. Over the same period the company earned RMB 5.038 billion, paid RMB 3.15 billion in dividends and bought back about RMB 1.55 billion of stock — RMB 4.7 billion in total. On a rough calculation, 5.038 - 4.7 = RMB 340 million, so cash should in theory have risen slightly; instead it fell by RMB 1.34 billion. The roughly RMB 1.7 billion gap sits mainly in inventory and working capital.

Inventory turnover went from 123 days to 201 days; on first-half cost of sales of RMB 5.2 billion, that ties up roughly RMB 1.1 billion more. The remainder sits in accounts receivable (payment terms with overseas channels) and prepayments (new-store fit-out, raw materials).

A debt-to-asset ratio of 24.9% and zero bank borrowings is the most reassuring item — even if cash flow deteriorates for another two quarters, the company will not be in trouble. But "not in trouble" and "highly efficient at making money" are two different things. The problem now is that half the profit earned turns into goods sitting in warehouses and fit-out for overseas stores, rather than cash on the books.

Linking the four layers together yields an easily overlooked conclusion: in an expansion phase, this company's operating cash flow naturally lags net profit — this is not accounting fraud, it is dictated by the business model. Collecting cash first (contract liabilities) and paying suppliers later (accounts payable) should make cash flow better than profit, but inventory has grown faster than advance receipts, which has eaten up that advantage. The test is simple: when inventory growth is below revenue growth, cash flow recovers; when it is above, cash flow keeps deteriorating. First-half revenue rose 23.8% while inventory turnover rose 63%; the latter is far above the former, which is why the net cash-to-profit ratio is 0.7.

Conclusion

Four layers, in one sentence: revenue is real, profit is discounted, and cash is tied up in inventory. This is not an earnings report that "blows up" but one that marks a "growth model switch" — from an asset-light online breakout to asset-heavy store expansion. Mid-switch, margins, cash flow and inventory all look ugly; the question is whether they normalise within two quarters.
