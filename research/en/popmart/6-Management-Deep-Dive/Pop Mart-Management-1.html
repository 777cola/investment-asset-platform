Core call: a clear-eyed founder, a governance structure with no brakes

Wang Ning is the biggest variable at this company — and the moat beyond the biggest moat. In 2026 he framed the year as one of "operational adjustment", publicly conceding that last year "one good thing covered up a hundred ugly ones", voluntarily cutting the growth target from 30% to no less than 20%, and simultaneously launching the largest buyback since listing (HKD 2–5 billion). Telling the truth when results blow up is far harder than saying pretty things when results are good.

The deduction is on governance: he holds 49.14% through trusts and holding platforms, a single dominant shareholder, with no AB share structure and no second-largest shareholder able to check him (Duan Yongping's 7.65% is a financial investor and does not participate in operations). The upside is fast decisions; the downside is that no one can stop the new ventures — city theme parks, desserts, accessories, film.

Three things Wang Ning got right: signing, slowing down, telling the truth

The first was signing MOLLY in 2016. Pop Mart was still a general merchandise retailer at the time. Wang Ning flew to Hong Kong to meet designer Wang Xinming and secure the MOLLY licence, transforming the company from "selling other people's toys" into "a company that owns IP." That step set the gross margin for the next decade — 30% for a retailer, 70% for an IP owner.

The second was deliberately slowing growth in 2026. At the results meeting he said that while 2025's numbers looked good, they were in some sense "one good thing covering a hundred flaws" — customers either found shelves sold out or had to queue, which is not normal; 2026 would require adjustment, like a race car "pitting to change tires." A company that had just delivered 184.7% growth voluntarily cut its guidance to 20% — rare in both A-shares and Hong Kong stocks. Most management teams would tough it out and then blow up the following quarter.

The third was the consistency of straight talk. He admitted the 30% target set at the start of the year would most likely be missed, admitted that overseas "a large number of new users lack familiarity with designer toy culture — they are just here to grab LABUBU," and admitted that organisational capability was lagging (the sales team expanded from 6,219 to 9,734, with the group now over 1.2 thousand people). Any one of these three statements could have gone unsaid; saying them only adds fuel to the stock price. He chose to say them.

Capital allocation: generous dividends, buybacks at high prices, new business formats are a bet

Dividend: the 2025 final dividend is RMB 2.3817 per share, totalling about RMB 3.15 billion, or 24.6% of net profit. For a growth company still opening stores, that payout ratio is on the high side — a signal of more cash on hand than it can deploy, with period-end cash of RMB 13.78 billion and zero bank borrowings.

Buyback: in the first half it cancelled 11.22 million shares for HKD 1.74 billion at prices of HKD 140.9–194.9, averaging roughly HKD 155 — about level with the current HKD 156.2. The money was neither cleverly nor foolishly spent: the tranche bought at HKD 190 is now 20% underwater, but cancellation is genuine EPS accretion. The new HKD 2–5 billion programme, executed at the current price, equals 1%–2.4% of market cap.

New formats are the real bet: City park, POP BAKERY desserts, POPOP accessories, plus wholesale and other revenue of RMB 553 million, up 111.7% YoY. Standalone that is not small, but it is only 3.2% of total revenue of RMB 17.173 billion, and it comes off a very low base. Its significance is not how much it earns this year but turning "buying a toy" into "spending a day at Pop Mart." If it works, IP life cycles stretch; if not, the cost is a few hundred million a year in trial and error.

Governance: 49.14% ownership, a good car with no brakes

Wang Ning controls a combined 49.14% through private trusts and multiple holding platforms, of which 654,381,407 shares, or 48.73%, are held directly. Three implications follow.

First, control is absolutely secure. There are no AB shares, and none are needed—just under half is de facto control. A hostile takeover is impossible, which supports a long-term orientation.

Second, there are no internal checks. No force on the board can say "no" to him; Duan Yongping's 7.65% is a financial investment, and southbound capital at 24.46% is dispersed. Strategic mistakes can therefore only be corrected by him alone—the 2026 "adjustment year" shows he has the ability to correct course, but that is survivorship bias, not an institutional safeguard.

Third, related-party transactions and expenses need watching. Many of the new formats—city theme parks, dessert shops, accessories—are incubated internally, and their related-party transaction structures with the listed company are complex. No negative events have surfaced so far, but this is the one governance area that requires continuous tracking.

No integrity flaws found: six years since listing, no financial restatements, no regulatory penalties, no records of major shareholder selling (buybacks instead). By Hong Kong new-consumer standards, this is clean.

Succession: not a problem now, but one thing to note

Wang Ning was born in 1987 and turns 39 in 2026. On a normal timeline, succession is a matter for fifteen years from now, and discussing it today is pointless. Two observations are nonetheless worth recording.

First, Si De. As COO with a 0.77% stake, he was the one who fielded questions on the overseas decline at the 2026 results meeting, and in blunt terms ("they're only here to grab LABUBU")—evidence that he both understands the business and is willing to speak his mind. Should Wang Ning ever step back to the chairman role, Si De is the most likely operating successor.

Second, the organisation. Explosive growth in 2025 took the company from a few thousand people to 12,000, with the sales team doubling to 9,734, opening gaps in cross-border management—something Wang Ning himself acknowledged at the results meeting.For a company moving from RMB 37.1 billion in revenue toward RMB 50 billion, what carries it is not the founder alone but a replicable methodology.That methodology is still being built.

Conclusion

Management scores 7, one of the few uncontested high marks in this report. Wang Ning did not get carried away in the best years, nor did he hide in the hardest ones — together, those two things are more persuasive than any strategy document. Points are deducted only on governance structure: a 49.14% single dominant stake, no checks and balances, and all course correction rests on his personal judgment. For investors, that means "following the person" matters more than "following the system" — acceptable, but not at a position size that would still feel safe without him.
