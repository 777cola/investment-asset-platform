Edited and polished: a publishable earnings analysis — Pop Mart

Pop Mart 2026 Interim Results: Behind RMB 5 billion in profit, three numbers are at odds
Research date 2026/09/07  ·  Price HK$156.20 (+1.43%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 209 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 14.2x

Core view

On August 20, Pop Mart reported its 2026 interim results: revenue of RMB 17.17 billion, up 23.8% year on year, and net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 5.04 billion, up 10.1%. The stock closed at HKD 153.7 that day, giving a market cap of HKD 204.69 billion, down more than 40% from this year's February peak. Three numbers in this report contradict one another.

The first contradiction: stores are increasing while overseas revenue is falling

In the first half, Pop Mart added 36 net new stores overseas: the Americas grew from 41 to 86, Europe from 18 to 45, and Asia-Pacific stood at 90. Total stores worldwide reached 676, with 2,827 Robo Shops.

Conventionally, doubling stores should lift revenue. In reality, overseas revenue was RMB 4.972 billion, down 11.1% YoY, with its share falling from 40.3% to 29.0%. Stores +19%, revenue -11% — put the two numbers together and there is only one explanation: output per store is falling sharply.

Management did not sidestep the issue. COO Si De was blunt on the earnings call: last year's overseas surge was driven mainly by LABUBU, and many new users lack familiarity with designer-toy culture — "they just came to grab LABUBU." The report also notes that the external traffic dividend for online channels is fading.

The breakdown is clearer still: Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas online -45.6%, Europe online -59.0%, Shopee -62.1%. What is collapsing is online; what is growing is offline. This is not demand disappearing but a shift in how customers are acquired — from "scrolling short videos and ordering" to "walking into a store and buying."

The second contradiction: gross margin fell only 0.6 points, while net margin fell 3.7 points

Gross margin 69.7% versus 70.3% a year earlier, down just 0.6 percentage points. In a half-year of dramatic category mix shift, that counts as stable — plush went from supporting act to lead, with revenue of RMB 9.825 billion, 57.2% of the total, up 60% year on year, while figures came in at RMB 5.192 billion, up only 0.3%.

But net margin fell from 33.0% to 29.3%, down 3.7 percentage points. Where did the gap go? Two places.

One is FX.First-half FX losses were RMB 720 million versus a RMB 120 million gain a year earlier — an RMB 840 million swing, 4.9 percentage points of revenue.The other is expenses.Distribution and selling expenses rose 23.1%, roughly in line with revenue, but rental costs rose 43.3% and employee compensation 45.7%, with the sales team expanding from 6,219 to 9,734 people.

The two are entirely different in nature. FX is one-off and uncontrollable; expenses are deliberate and investment-driven. Strip them out and profit growth is about 28%, faster than revenue.So the "profit decline" framing needs correcting: what fell was reported profit, not operating profit.

The third discrepancy: RMB 5 billion in profit, yet cash fell by RMB 1.3 billion

This is the most easily overlooked point. First-half net profit was RMB 5.038 billion, while cash fell from RMB 13.78 billion at the start of the year to RMB 12.44 billion, a decrease of RMB 1.34 billion. Over the same period, dividends paid were RMB 3.15 billion and buybacks about RMB 1.55 billion, totaling RMB 4.7 billion.

A rough calculation: 5.038 - 4.7 = RMB 0.34 billion, so cash should have risen slightly, but it actually fell by RMB 1.34 billion. The gap of about RMB 1.7 billion is tied up in inventory and working capital. Inventory turnover days stretched from 123 to 201; based on first-half cost of sales of RMB 5.2 billion, that tied up roughly RMB 1.1 billion more in goods.

The company's explanation is advance stocking overseas. That explanation is half valid — stores in the Americas grew from 41 to 86, and new stores do require inventory. The half that does not hold: stores are increasing, revenue is falling, and inventory is still rising. When these three move in different directions, the problem is usually on the demand side.

The good news is a clean balance sheet: debt-to-assets ratio of 24.9%, zero bank borrowings, and RMB 12.44 billion in cash. Even if cash flow deteriorates for another two quarters, the company will not run into trouble. The bad news is that "no trouble" and "high earning efficiency" are two different things — half of the profit earned has turned into goods sitting in the warehouse.

So, is a 14x PE cheap?

HKD 156.2 corresponds to a market cap of about HKD 209.0 billion and 14.2x TTM PE. At first glance it looks cheap: a consumer company with a 70% gross margin, and 14x is hard to find in either A-shares or Hong Kong.

But look closely at the denominator. The RMB 13.47 billion TTM net profit is composed of: full-year 2025 RMB 13.012 billion + first-half 2026 RMB 5.038 billion - first-half 2025 RMB 4.576 billion. Unpacked, that means — the second half of 2025 alone earned RMB 8.436 billion, a half-year when Labubu went viral globally and stores had queues every day.

On a rough full-year 2026 net profit of RMB 11.0 billion, PE is 17.4x; based on JPMorgan's forecast of -35% revenue in the third quarter and -20% in the fourth, 2026 net profit may be only RMB 9.5–10.5 billion, implying a PE of 18–20x. HSBC's August 20 report wrote that the current price equates to 16.4x 2026 forecast PE.

Three bases point to the same thing: it is not 14x, it is around 17x. For a company with 10% profit growth and overseas still declining, 17x is reasonable, not cheap.

Conclusion: wait for two signals

The question with this company is not "is it good" but "is it expensive." The strengths: business model, management, balance sheet. The weaknesses: sustainability of growth and cash conversion.

Wait for two signals; either one alone justifies a re-rating: ① Overseas revenue stops falling quarter on quarter in the Q3 report, and inventory turnover returns to within 150 days; ② The share price falls below HKD 120, equivalent to about 13x 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion.

Until then, the rating is "Watch, wait for a pullback." Not a lack of conviction, but the price has yet to factor in the bad news.

Conclusion

One-line summary: The RMB 5 billion profit is real, but three sets of numbers are in conflict — store count rising while revenue falls; gross margin steady while net profit falls; profit rising while cash falls. All three contradictions point to the same conclusion: the quality of growth is deteriorating. A 14x PE cannot correct for this conclusion; 17x is the true price.
