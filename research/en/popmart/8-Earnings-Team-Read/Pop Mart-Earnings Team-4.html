Core view

When Li Lu reads financial statements, he looks at "the duration of revenue"—whether that money will still be there next year, and whether it will still be there five years from now. By that standard, the duration embedded in Pop Mart's RMB 17.17 billion varies widely.

Reclassify revenue by "duration"

First, revenue from decade-old IP: MOLLY RMB 900 million, DIMOO RMB 1.615 billion, SKULLPANDA RMB 1.551 billion. All are over ten years old and still growing at double digits, with the exception of MOLLY at -33.6%. This revenue has the longest duration and can be valued as a consumer stock.

Second, new hits: Twinkle Twinkle RMB 2.65 billion (+580.6%), CRYBABY RMB 1.633 billion (+34%), Hirono RMB 1.009 billion (+38.5%). Twinkle Twinkle only took off in 2025; 580% growth is unsustainable and turns negative next year once the base rises. This revenue has a duration of three to five years and must be discounted.

Third, fading: THE MONSTERS RMB 4.454 billion (-7.5%). The duration of this revenue is already counting down.

The three categories total RMB 15.29 billion, 89% of revenue; the remaining RMB 1.88 billion is licensed IP, third-party products and new formats. The split shows that 89% of the company's revenue rests on "characters", and the lifespan of a character is not decided by the company.

New business formats contributed RMB 553 million — the single most important line in the results

Wholesale and other revenue reached RMB 553 million, up 111.7% YoY, driven by the city park, POP BAKERY desserts and POPOP accessories. At just 3.2% of revenue the absolute number is small, but this is the only line that extends IP duration.

The logic is simple: sell a doll and the revenue is one-off; keep consumers in a park for a day, serve them a dessert, sell them a necklace, and revenue recurs without depending on another new hit. At Disney, tickets and merchandise account for the bulk of revenue; films are merely a traffic driver — that is the endgame of IP operation.

Pop Mart has only just started down this road: the city park opened four new zones around THE MONSTERS and DIMOO, desserts entered Aranya and Sentosa in Singapore, and a live-action LABUBU animated film is in development with Sony Pictures. The direction is right, but each initiative takes five to ten years to prove out. That RMB 553 million in the financials is an option premium, not cash flow.

The direction of capex matters more than the amount

46 stores and 190 robot shops opened in 1H; capex was modest — stores are leased, with fit-out costs partly expensed and partly capitalised. The real spending is on overseas flagship stores and parks, but that lands in 2H and beyond.

Li Lu would focus on one ratio: what share of net profit goes into "extending IP duration"? Today that is parks, desserts, films and flagship stores — roughly RMB 1 billion-plus a year, about 10% of net profit. That ratio is reasonable — enough room to experiment without denting dividends or buybacks.

At 30%, the company would be signalling anxiety, trying to buy time with money; at 3%, it would have given up on being an "IP group" and settled for being a "designer toy retailer". At 10%, it is healthy.

Conclusion

Li Lu's verdict: these results prove the company can keep creating new IP, but not yet that it can keep a single IP alive for twenty years. The first deserves a 15x P/E, the second 25x. The market is currently pricing the former, and rightly so.
