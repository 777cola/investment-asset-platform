Core view

Read together, the four deep dives agree more than they disagree. Four points of consensus: an excellent business model, a clean balance sheet, credible management, and deteriorating growth quality. Two points of contention: whether FX effects should be stripped out of profit growth, and whether overseas stores are an asset or a burden. The comparison follows, point by point.

Consensus 1: A 69.7% gross margin is the hardest evidence for this business

All four cite gross margin as evidence that "the business isn't broken": Duan Yongping frames it as pricing power, Buffett as a moat, Munger as proof costs are under control, and Li Lu as the efficiency of monetising IP assets.

That consensus matters because it rules out one scenario—"the company is finished." A business still posting 70% gross margin, 29% net margin and RMB 12.4 billion of cash on the books will not suddenly collapse. The risk is "growth going to zero," not "operational failure."

Consensus 2: The overseas issue is not demand but a channel shift

All four, unexpectedly, agreed on the overseas picture: offline growing, online collapsing, net negative. Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, the Americas -45.6%, Europe -59.0%, Shopee -62.1%, while stores rose by a net 36 over the same period.

The consensus read-through: this is not simply "overseas failure." Had demand truly vanished, stores would be falling too; instead stores are opening while online collapses, which points to a broken customer acquisition model rather than a product nobody wants. That gives a testable checkpoint: same-store sales at the 86 new Americas stores a year out.

Divergence one: should profit be read as 10.1% or 28%

Buffett and Duan Yongping strip out FX and focus on 28%; Munger and Li Lu insist on the reported 10.1%.

Verdict: use 28% to judge operating quality and 10.1% to judge market pricing. The market trades on reported numbers, so the share price reacts to 10.1%; but to judge what the company is worth, use 28%, because FX does not affect long-term cash flow. Know how to use both numbers, not just one.

Divergence 2: Are overseas stores assets or liabilities?

Duan Yongping and Li Lu lean toward "asset": the 221 stores are real location assets, the single-store model is proven in China, and overseas simply needs time. Munger leans toward "burden": stores +19%, revenue -11%, single-store output halved, and rent is rigid.

Verdict: still an asset now, but turning into a burden. The dividing line is whether annualised overseas revenue per store can hold at RMB 40 million. If it holds, the 221 stores are a moat; if not, every new store adds another slice of rent to the losses. The company does not disclose this figure, so it can only be roughly estimated as overseas revenue / number of overseas stores. The error is large, but the direction is usable.

Conclusion

Synthesis: All four agree on "don't buy now" but diverge on "why." Consensus sets the floor—this company won't collapse; the disagreement sets the ceiling—is it worth 12x or 20x. Rating: "Watch, wait for pullback," trigger price HKD 120.
