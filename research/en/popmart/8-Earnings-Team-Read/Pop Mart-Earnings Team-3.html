Core view

Munger's approach to reading financial reports is to "look for inconsistencies." Three stand out, and all three point to the same conclusion.

Contradiction one: store count is rising while overseas revenue is falling

Overseas stores rose from 185 to 221, a net addition of 36, yet overseas revenue fell 11.1% over the same period. Stores +19%, revenue -11%. Those two numbers admit only one explanation: revenue per store is falling sharply.

Rough math: 185 stores at end-2025 against full-year 2025 overseas revenue of RMB 16.27 billion implies RMB 87.95 million annualised per store; 221 stores in H1 2026 against RMB 4.972 billion implies RMB 45 million annualised. Revenue per store has nearly halved. The calculation is imperfect—new stores are still ramping and half-year data is seasonal—but the direction is right.

The implication: last year's overseas surge was mainly traffic driven by a single IP, not a store model that works. The traffic is gone, the stores remain, and the rent is still due.

Contradiction 2: gross margin fell just 0.6pp while net margin fell 3.7pp

If costs had gone wrong, gross margin would have fallen first. It fell only 0.6pp, showing the product still has pricing power and cost control has not slipped. Net margin, though, fell 3.7pp—where did the gap come from?

Two places: FX (RMB 840 million, 4.9pp of revenue) and expenses (lease +43.3%, compensation +45.7%). FX is one-off; expenses are structural. Adding FX back, net margin is 34.2%, higher than last year—operations have not actually deteriorated.

The "profit decline" framing therefore needs correcting: what fell was reported profit, not operating profit. The true operating margin (excluding FX) rose. That distinction matters because the market prices off reported figures, and reported figures were distorted by FX.

Contradiction 3: RMB 5 billion of profit, yet cash fell by RMB 1.3 billion

This is the most alarming point. It earned RMB 5 billion, paid RMB 3.15 billion in dividends, bought back RMB 1.55 billion, and cash fell by RMB 1.34 billion. A rough calculation shows RMB 1.7 billion did not convert into cash and is stuck in inventory and working capital.

Munger would say: accounting profit is an opinion, cash is a fact. A company can grow profit for three straight years with negative cash flow and blow up in the fourth. Pop Mart is only in its first year, not yet dangerous, but the direction must be right.

Put the three contradictions together: stores up, revenue down; gross margin stable, net profit down; profit up, cash down. Together they tell one story: the company is sustaining growth through "opening stores + stocking inventory," and the marginal return on that approach is declining. This is not a collapse, it is declining efficiency. A company with declining efficiency deserves a valuation discount.

Conclusion

Munger's conclusion: the financials are not fraudulent, but neither do they bring good news. Three contradictions point the same way: the quality of growth is deteriorating. That alone would not make him bearish, but it would stop him paying a premium for "growth." A 14x P/E is not cheap in his eyes; it is "fair," because the denominator deserves a discount.
