Six-Gate Screen — Pop Mart

Pop Mart (09992) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/09/07  ·  Price HK$156.20 (+1.43%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 209 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 14.2x
← Back to the hub

Core call: good company, reasonable price, bad timing

Six gates score 39 out of 60 — a fail — and the rating stays at the working paper's "Watch, wait for a pullback." The first four gates are not much in dispute: the business is understandable within three years, gross margin is 69.7%, net margin 29.3%, there are 676 company-owned stores plus 2,827 robot vending machines, and expansion consumes almost no capital. The problem lies in the last two gates.

Gate five, 6 points: HKD 156.2 corresponds to 14.2x P/E (TTM), which looks like a consumer stock. But the RMB 13.47 billion denominator contains the high base from the peak of Labubu mania in the second half of 2025; on a rough RMB 11.0 billion net profit for 2026, the real multiple is 17.4x. Gate six, 4 points: the buy case rests on only two points — "a new IP picks up" and "overseas stops falling" — and neither will be known until the Q3 report. That is betting on a turning point, not waiting for a price.

Gate one and two: the business is easy to understand and genuinely profitable, but what it earns is a "hype rent"

Circle of competence: 8 points. The business in three sentences: sign artists, turn their images into blind boxes and plush toys, sell to young people through self-operated stores and online blind-box machines. No technology roadmap, no major customers, no reliance on subsidies. The only thing genuinely hard to understand is one question — when will the next image everyone wants appear. That does not affect "understanding the business," only "forecasting performance."

The second gate also scores 8 points, and the numbers are solid: first-half 2026 revenue of RMB 17.173 billion, gross profit of RMB 11.97 billion (roughly 69.7%), net profit of RMB 5.038 billion, net margin 29.3%. Among global consumer companies, only luxury goods and premium liquor match this margin. Laopu Gold has a net margin of 21.5%, MINISO has stayed below 10% for years, and Pop Mart sits far above both.

The deduction comes from "where the profit comes from." Rough per-store output: 2025 revenue of RMB 37.12 billion across 630 stores implies RMB 58.92 million annualized per store; first-half 2026 revenue of RMB 17.173 billion across 676 stores implies RMB 50.80 million annualized, down 14% year on year. Store count is still rising while per-store output falls, which shows growth is propped up by new openings, not by older stores selling more. When the hype fades, this structure will make profit fall faster than revenue.

Gate three: distribution is the real moat, not Labubu

This is the easiest test to misjudge. Many people treat Labubu as the moat, but Labubu is a product. Products go out of fashion: in the first half of 2026, THE MONSTERS revenue was RMB 4.454 billion, down 7.5% year on year, and its share fell from 34.7% to 26.0% — that is the evidence.

The real barriers are three. First, site selection capability: 676 self-operated stores, 221 overseas (90 in Asia-Pacific, 86 in the Americas, 45 in Europe and other regions); prime locations in core commercial districts are a limited supply. Second, member assets: 82.44 million registered members in China, nearly 10 million added in six months; members contribute 92.9% of mainland China revenue, with a repurchase rate of 51.6%. Third, the organizational capability to push a new IP onto global shelves within 12 months: Twinkle Twinkle rose from RMB 389 million in the year-earlier period to RMB 2.65 billion, growth of 580.6% — the system is running, not luck.

A score of 6 rather than 8, because all three are "operational barriers", not "mindshare barriers". Consumers enter the store because the character looks good, not because "this is Pop Mart". The former can be swapped; the latter cannot.

Level four: Wang Ning's honesty is worth these 7 points

Management comes down to two things: the record of telling the truth, and the record of returning cash.

Telling the truth: on the August 20, 2026 earnings call, Wang Ning framed 2026 as a "year of operational adjustment," stating plainly that boosting sales is not the primary goal and that last year's "one good thing covering a hundred flaws" — stores either sold out or queuing — was not normal. Of the 30% growth target set at the start of the year, he conceded it will most likely not be met and cut guidance to no less than 20%.A company that has just posted 184.7% growth and voluntarily slows down is more trustworthy than one propping up an unachievable target.

Returning cash: 2025 final dividend of RMB 2.3817 per share, about RMB 3.15 billion in total; in the first half of 2026, 11.22 million shares repurchased and cancelled for HKD 1.74 billion at HKD 140.9–194.9; the interim report announced a further HKD 2–5 billion buyback, the first time since listing that a program of this size has been disclosed at an earnings call. With RMB 12.44 billion in cash, zero bank borrowings and a debt-to-asset ratio of 24.9%, this buyback is not putting on a brave face.

Deduct 3 points for governance: Wang Ning holds 49.14% through trusts and holding platforms — one dominant shareholder, no checks and balances. The upside is fast decisions and the ability to withstand short-term pressure; the downside is that no one can stop him from putting money into city theme parks, desserts and accessories, businesses with unclear returns.

Gate 5: the 14.2x P/E rests on a denominator set at the H2 2025 peak

Market cap is roughly HKD 209 billion, or about RMB 191 billion, which on TTM net profit of RMB 13.47 billion gives 14.2x. At first glance that looks cheap: a consumer company with 70% gross margin that is still opening stores is hard to find at 14x in either A-shares or Hong Kong.

Break it down and it is not cheap. TTM is built as full-year 2025 RMB 13.012 billion + 2026H1 RMB 5.038 billion - 2025H1 RMB 4.576 billion. The second half of 2025 alone earned RMB 8.436 billion — a half-year when Labubu went viral globally and stores queued every day, hardly the norm. A low P/E calculated on a peak half-year is the textbook form of a valuation trap.

On a rough full-year 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion (H1 RMB 5.038 billion; H2 is usually peak season but overseas weakness must be deducted), the actual P/E is 17.4x. On JPMorgan's forecast of Q3 revenue -35% and Q4 -20%, 2026 net profit may be only RMB 9.5–10.5 billion, implying 18–20x. HSBC's August 20 report puts the current price at 16.4x 2026E P/E. All three measures point to the same thing: it is not 14x, it is around 17x. For a company whose profit may decline, that is no margin of safety.

Gate 6: the buy thesis cannot be verified in advance — if it fails, it fails

Discipline asks one question: what are you betting on when you buy now?

There are only two answers. First, that Twinkle Twinkle, CRYBABY and DIMOO can absorb the 26% of revenue Labubu gives up — in the first half the five mid-tier IPs combined earned 1.89x THE MONSTERS, which suggests they have, but Twinkle Twinkle's 580.6% growth cannot persist; once the base rises next year, it turns negative. Second, that the overseas online pullback is a one-off clearing — Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas online -45.6%, Shopee -62.1%. Management calls it fading traffic dividends, but that is a structural change, not quarterly noise.

Neither can be solved by "cheap"; both require data. Discipline means: do not predict the inflection point, only pay after the data confirms it. So this gate scores 4 — not because the company is bad, but because the timing is wrong.

Veto item: both revenue and profit are decelerating, and profit is falling faster than revenue (+23.8% vs +10.1%), showing operating leverage working in reverse. Items to verify: ① whether overseas revenue stops falling quarter on quarter in the Q3 report, with same-store sales at the 86 Americas stores the key; ② whether inventory turnover days can fall from 201 days back below 150 days; ③ the actual pace of execution of the HKD 2–5 billion buyback, and whether Wang Ning increases his stake.

Conclusion

Six gates, 39 points, fail, watch, wait for pullback. This is not a bad company; it is a company whose price has yet to price in room for bad news. Triggers, stated plainly: ① the share price returns below HKD 120 (about 13x 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion), or ② overseas revenue in the Q3 report stops declining quarter on quarter and inventory turnover returns to within 150 days. If either appears, the score can be recalculated to above 44. Until then, any purchase is paying full price for the probability that "new IP takes over."
