Core view

Demand in the designer toy industry is real, and the market is growing: China's 2024–2030 CAGR is forecast at 20.9%, with retail sales of RMB 110.1 billion in 2026; the global art toy market reaches USD 6.94 billion in 2026, with a CAGR of 11.6%.But Munger would offer a reminder: industry growth and leader profits are not the same thing.The industry's history over the past decade is this — the market kept growing, and the No. 1 player changed hands several times.

Why the industry could grow: supply creates demand

Designer toys do not "meet existing demand"; they "create new demand." Consumers do not know which character they want before entering the store; they want it only after seeing it. This determines the industry's growth logic: not rising penetration, but increased supply of SKUs and IP. Pop Mart launches several hundred SKUs a year; if just 5% become hits, revenue can double.

This logic has a side effect: demand is conjured by supply, and can also be killed by supply turning mediocre. When every brand can roll out 20 new characters a week, consumer attention is diluted. In the first half of 2026, Pop Mart's figure category revenue was RMB 5.192 billion, up only +0.3% year on year — that is the signal: figures, the most classic category, have stopped growing.

Landscape: one dominant player and several strong contenders, but the leader's position is unstable

Pop Mart is the only Chinese company to have made all three things work: proprietary IP, self-operated stores and overseas expansion. Revenue reached RMB 17.173 billion in the first half of 2026, more than ten times that of second-place Bloks (Bloks revenue was RMB 595 million in Q1 2026, or roughly RMB 2.4 billion annualized, on a rough calculation).

But its position as the runaway leader is unstable, because competitors are taking different paths. Bloks pursues licensed IP, low prices and lower-tier markets, with a 55% gross margin, relying on volume. TOP TOY pursues multi-brand stores and co-branding, essentially a distribution business, with RMB 985 million in the first half of 2026 and still loss-making. 52TOYS pursues hardcore collectibles at a scale an order of magnitude smaller, with a net margin of just 4.7%. None of these three can take Pop Mart's customer base, but they are dragging down the price band of the designer-toy category.

Its real rival for mindshare is not a Chinese company but Jellycat. In the European and US plush markets, Jellycat's brand recognition runs far deeper than Pop Mart's, and plush already accounts for 57.2% of Pop Mart's revenue. This is a frontal-battlefield rival, not a flanking one.

Lifecycle: Labubu went from breakout hit to decline in 18 months

This is the section Munger cared about most. Labubu went globally viral in the first half of 2025; in the first half of 2026 revenue fell 7.5% year on year, with its share dropping from 34.7% to 26.0%. From peak to negative growth: 18 months.

Compare with history: MOLLY launched in 2006, was productized over 10 years, and still booked RMB 900 million in revenue in the first half of 2026, but down 33.6% year on year; DIMOO and SKULLPANDA are both roughly decade-old IPs, still generating first-half revenue of RMB 1.5 billion with double-digit growth. So it is not that "all IPs are short-lived," but that "mega-head IPs are short-lived while mid-tier IPs are long-lived."

The investment implication is direct: do not extrapolate the future from Labubu's peak; extrapolate from the portfolio of mid-tier IPs. In the first half, the top five non-THE MONSTERS IPs combined generated 1.89x THE MONSTERS' revenue — that is the normalized revenue base. And a portfolio of mid-tier IPs is worth 12–15x PE, not 25x.

Two paths to globalization: traffic going global vs stores going global

Pop Mart's overseas growth in 2024–2025 was essentially "traffic going global" — short videos on TikTok and Instagram turned Labubu into a global phenomenon, consumers ordered on Shopee and the official website, and the company barely needed to open stores. The upside of this path is speed, lightness and high gross margin; the downside is that it is uncontrollable.

In the first half of 2026, this path ran out: Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas online -45.6%, Europe online -59.0%, Shopee -62.1%. The company is pivoting to "stores going global": Americas 41 to 86 stores, Europe 18 to 45, Asia-Pacific 90, with London set as the European headquarters and flagship stores planned in Milan, London, Paris and New York.

Munger would say: this is a switch from "luck" to "hard work." Opening stores requires site selection, leases, local teams and inventory, with an 18–24 month payback period. Growth on this path is slower but cumulative. The problem is that the market is still valuing it as "traffic going global."

Conclusion

Industry score 8, company position 7, valuation fit 5. The industry is growing, but that does not mean the leader can keep taking the biggest slice. The historical pattern in designer toys: during category expansion the leader captures a premium; as the category matures the leader gets diluted by value-for-money rivals and channel shifts. Chinese designer toys are moving from the former to the latter. Pop Mart's response is "overseas expansion + multiple IPs + new formats" — the right direction, but each takes time.
