Financial analysis (Buffett's lens): profits are real, but cash flow needs a haircut — Pop Mart

Core view

Of the three statements, the income statement looks the best, the balance sheet the cleanest, and the cash flow statement warrants the most caution. First-half net profit was RMB 5.038 billion, yet operating cash flow inferred from the change in cash was only about RMB 3.5 billion — not all of the profit turned into cash. Inventory days stretching from 123 to 201 is direct evidence of this.

Income statement: revenue still growing, profit eaten away by three things

Revenue +23.8%, net profit +10.1% — a 13.7 percentage point gap. Three drivers: FX (a RMB 720 million loss versus a RMB 120 million gain a year earlier, an RMB 840 million swing), gross margin (70.3%→69.7%, reflecting a lower mix of high-margin overseas revenue and higher raw material costs) and expenses (rent +43.3%, compensation +45.7%, both outpacing revenue).

How would Buffett read this? He would strip out FX and look at operating profit — excluding FX, profit growth is about 28%, slightly ahead of revenue. That shows operations are not out of control; finance and investment are the drag. FX is uncontrollable; expenses are a deliberate choice (store openings). Different in nature: the former is noise, the latter is investment.

But one thing he would not let pass: net margin fell from 33.0% to 29.3%, down 3.7 percentage points in a year. Margin compression during a store-opening cycle is normal; the question is whether it stops. If store count reaches 800 in 2027 and net margin is still falling, the unit economics have broken down.

Balance sheet: as clean as a company that borrows nothing

Cash of RMB 12.44 billion (RMB 13.78 billion at the start of the year), zero bank borrowings and a debt-to-asset ratio of 24.9%: taken together, these three numbers mean the company could survive two years without turning a profit. Cash fell by RMB 1.34 billion in the first half, and the destination is clear — RMB 3.15 billion in dividends and about RMB 1.55 billion in buybacks, RMB 4.7 billion combined, more than 90% of net profit of RMB 5.038 billion for the period.

The liability side is mainly contract liabilities (prepaid blind-box payments) and accounts payable, both non-interest-bearing operating liabilities. This is the best liability structure for a consumer goods company: money owed to suppliers and customers, not to banks.

The only problem on the asset side is inventory. Turnover of 201 days means that at the current sales pace, the goods in the warehouse would take 6.6 months to sell. Designer toys do not go out of season, so impairment pressure is lower than for apparel, but if overseas sales keep falling, this inventory will be a gross-margin killer in 2027.

Cash flow: RMB 5 billion of profit converted into just RMB 3.5 billion of cash

This is the paragraph that most needs spelling out in the entire financial analysis. Backing it out from "closing cash - opening cash + dividends + buybacks," first-half operating cash flow was about RMB 3.5 billion (rough calculation, not adjusted for FX or wealth-management subscriptions and redemptions), a net cash ratio of 0.7x.

Where did the gap go? Inventory build is the biggest piece: turnover went from 123 days to 201 days, which, on first-half operating cost of RMB 5.2 billion, tied up roughly RMB 1.1 billion of additional inventory; overseas receivables and prepayments account for part of the rest.This is not fraud; it is the cost of expansion: new stores need stocking, overseas markets need inventory build, and channels need payment terms.

Buffett's attitude toward companies like this is clear: a one-off working-capital tie-up is acceptable, three consecutive years is not. The test is "whether inventory turnover can return to 150 days within two quarters." If it cannot, the goods are not selling, and the profit is only on paper.

Conclusion

Financial quality scores 7: real profits, clean assets, ample cash, but a declining cash conversion rate. Finances alone will not land this company in trouble; unsold inventory will. Two numbers bear watching: inventory turnover days and the net cash ratio. Once the former returns to 150 days and the latter to above 0.9x, the financial bar is cleared again.
