Core view

Taken together, the four analyses agree more than they differ: good business, clean financials, credible management, decelerating growth, valuation neither expensive nor cheap. Only three genuine disagreements emerge, each bearing on position sizing rather than direction. The verdicts follow.

Four independent analyses — business (Duan Yongping), financial (Buffett), industry (Munger) and risk (Li Lu) — are now complete; this page sets out the cross-reading and the verdict.
Disagreement one: Duan Yongping's holdings vs Munger's odds

This is the most commonly misread point. In July, Duan Yongping raised his stake to 7.65%, or 102 million shares, and after the interim results said, "As a shareholder, I actually feel pretty good about it." Many read this as "the big name is bullish, so it's a buy."

But Duan's actual words contain no "this price is worth buying." He built the position in 2024–2025 at a cost far below the current price; adding was "topping up after a fall," not "the current value is worth buying." And his position-sizing logic is "heavy if I understand it, hands off if I don't." What he may understand is "Pop Mart's organizational capability," not "this year's profit."

Munger's angle is entirely different: he does not care who holds it, only what assumptions the current price implies. The current price implies that "overseas stops declining + a new IP takes over" both hold at once, with odds below half. Verdict: the two are not in conflict — one says the company is good, the other says the price is expensive. Investing requires both conditions; right now only one is met.

Disagreement two: the profit is real, but how real

Through the Buffett lens: stripping out the RMB 840 million FX distortion, profit grew 28%, ahead of revenue growth of 23.8% — operating leverage is positive. That call is correct.

Through the Li Lu lens: net cash ratio 0.7, inventory days 201, cash down RMB 1.34 billion. That is also correct.

Both hold because they look at different layers. The income statement says "earned"; the cash flow statement says "not yet collected." In an expansion phase the two diverge by nature — stocking new stores, building overseas channels and extending payment terms all mean spending first and collecting later.

Verdict: the profit is real, but distributable free cash flow must be discounted. How large that discount is depends on whether inventory can be worked down. The test: if the 2026 annual report shows inventory turnover back within 150 days and the net cash ratio back to 0.9x, it was a timing issue; if it comes in above 180 days, the goods genuinely are not selling, and profit then needs a 15%–20% cut.

Divergence 3: Can industry beta rescue company alpha?

Munger's industry data holds up: China's designer toy market is set to grow at a 20.9% CAGR from 2024–2030, with retail sales of RMB 110.1 billion in 2026, while the global art toy market stands at USD 6.94 billion, growing 11.6%. The industry is still expanding.

But Duan Yongping would press immediately: if the industry grows 20%, why did the company grow only 23.8% (and that supported by +47.3% domestic)? If the industry is β, the company should be β plus α. The reality: the company's overseas α is negative (-11.1%), while its domestic α is positive (+47.3%).

Verdict: the industry upside is real, but it cannot substitute for company-level competitiveness. Judging this company requires just two numbers—domestic same-store growth and overseas sales per store. The former determines how long the base business can hold up; the latter determines whether going overseas is a false premise. The RMB 110.1 billion in industry reports does nothing for this year's share price; it matters for the ceiling five years out.

Verdict: Watch — wait for a pullback; three triggers

Across all four analyses the rating is Watch, wait for pullback, consistent with the working papers. Three triggers, any one of which would justify a re-rating:

① Price: below HKD 120, implying about 13x 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion and 25% upside to the neutral-case HKD 150 — enough to turn risk-reward positive. ② Fundamentals: overseas revenue stops declining sequentially in the Q3 report and inventory turnover returns to within 150 days. ③ Structure: quarterly revenue from Hirono or CRYBABY reaches RMB 2 billion and THE MONSTERS' share falls below 20% while total revenue still grows — genuine de-concentration away from a single IP.

All three are verifiable; no forecasting required. That is the point of waiting: not waiting for the share price, but waiting for evidence.

Conclusion

Four analyses, three disagreements, one conclusion: watch, wait for a pullback. The debate here is not whether this is a good company but whether it is expensive. The strengths lie in the business model, management and balance sheet; the weaknesses lie in growth sustainability and cash conversion. The verdict rests on the three points above: if any one of them holds, the rating can be upgraded to "Buy (small position)"; if overseas sales keep declining at a double-digit rate in the Q3 report and inventory stays at 200 days, the rating should be downgraded to "Avoid."
