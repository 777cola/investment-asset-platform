## Title and lead

**Pop Mart Halves as Wang Ning Says Three Blunt Truths**

On August 20, Pop Mart reported its 2026 interim results. Revenue came in at RMB 17.17 billion, up 23.8% year on year, and net profit at RMB 5.04 billion, up 10.1%. The numbers are not ugly. The stock closed at HKD 153.7 that day, down 46% from HKD 288.2 in February this year.

What really rattled the market was not these two figures, but three things Wang Ning said at the results briefing.

## "Last year, one standout performance masked a multitude of weaknesses."

The gist of the remark: 2025's numbers look good, but in some ways one good thing covers up a hundred ugly ones — customers walk into a store and either the product is sold out or there is a queue, and that should not be the norm.

Translated, that means: **Last year's 184.7% growth was luck brought by a single Labubu character, not the company's baseline capability.**

The data says the same. THE MONSTERS, the family LABUBU belongs to, posted first-half revenue of RMB 4.454 billion, down 7.5% year on year, with its share falling from 34.7% to 26.0%. This is its first half-year decline since going viral. The 20-year-old MOLLY fared worse: RMB 900 million, down 33.6%.

Taking over is Twinkle Twinkle: RMB 2.65 billion, up 580.6%, vaulting from RMB 389 million in the year-earlier period to the second-largest IP. Add CRYBABY at RMB 1.63 billion, DIMOO at RMB 1.62 billion and SKULLPANDA at RMB 1.55 billion, and 11 IPs exceed RMB 100 million, with 6 topping RMB 1 billion.

**So the accurate statement is not "Labubu retreated, the company is finished" but "Labubu retreated, and the company rotated a new batch in."** The former is collapse; the latter is rotation.

## The second sentence: "New overseas users are only here to grab LABUBU."

This is what COO Si De said. His actual words were more direct: last year's overseas high growth was mainly driven by LABUBU, and many new users lacked any awareness of designer toy culture — "they just came to grab LABUBU."

The result: first-half overseas revenue was RMB 4.972 billion, down 11.1% year on year, with its share falling from 40.3% to 29.0%. Asia-Pacific fell 9.7%, the Americas fell 16.5%, and only the smallest region, Europe, grew 5.9%.

But break it down and the collapse is entirely online: Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas online -45.6%, Europe online -59.0%, Shopee -62.1%. **Over the same period, overseas stores added a net 36 — the Americas went from 41 to 86 stores, Europe from 18 to 45.**

What does this show? It shows overseas demand has not disappeared — the "order while scrolling short videos" route has run its course, and now it depends on "walking into a store to buy." **The former is renting traffic; the latter is buying location.** Renting traffic is fast, asset-light and high-margin; buying location is slow, heavy and long-payback. These are two entirely different businesses, and should carry different valuations.

## "The 30% target most likely will not be met this year."

Of the 30% growth target set at the start of the year, Wang Ning himself said it most likely will not be met, and guidance was cut to no less than 20%. At the same time, the company announced a buyback of HKD 2 billion to 5 billion over the next 6 months — the first time such a scale has been disclosed at an earnings briefing since listing.

Backing off and buying back are not contradictory. **Backing off is honesty about performance; the buyback is a statement about value.** The company holds RMB 12.44 billion in cash, has zero bank borrowings and a debt-to-asset ratio of 24.9%, so a HKD 5 billion buyback poses no financial strain.

But a buyback is not growth. It can support the share price, not profit. In the first half the company already repurchased 11.22 million shares for HKD 1.74 billion at HKD 140.9 to 194.9, an average of about HKD 155, roughly the current level — **management itself bought halfway up the hill.**

## One more number matters more than the three above.

Net profit was RMB 5.038 billion, yet cash fell from RMB 13.78 billion at the start of the year to RMB 12.44 billion, a decrease of RMB 1.34 billion. Over the same period, dividends were RMB 3.15 billion and buybacks about RMB 1.55 billion.

Rough math: 5.038 - 4.7 = RMB 340 million, so cash should have edged up; instead it fell RMB 1.34 billion, a gap of about RMB 1.7 billion. **Where did the money go? Inventory.** Inventory turnover days stretched from 123 to 201; on first-half operating cost of RMB 5.2 billion, that tied up roughly RMB 1.1 billion more in goods.

Management's explanation is overseas pre-stocking — Americas stores grew from 41 to 86, and new stores do need inventory. But the other half of the explanation does not hold: **stores are increasing, revenue is falling, and inventory is still rising. When these three move in different directions, the problem is usually on the demand side.**

This is not fraud. It is the cost of expansion: half the profit earned turned into goods in the warehouse rather than cash on the books. The net cash ratio is about 0.7x, below the healthy threshold of 1.

## So, is a 14x PE cheap?

HKD 156.2, market cap about HKD 209 billion, 14.2x TTM PE. At first glance, a consumer company with a 69.7% gross margin trading at 14x looks absurdly cheap.

But look closely at the denominator. TTM net profit of RMB 13.47 billion is composed of: full-year 2025 RMB 13.012 billion + first-half 2026 RMB 5.038 billion - first-half 2025 RMB 4.576 billion. **Broken out, that means: the second half of 2025 alone earned RMB 8.436 billion — the half-year when Labubu went viral globally and stores had queues every day.**

On 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion, the PE is 17.4x. HSBC wrote in its August 20 report that the current price equates to 16.4x 2026 forecast PE. JPMorgan is harsher, forecasting third-quarter revenue -35% and fourth-quarter -20%, cutting its rating straight to "Underweight" with a target price of HKD 120.

Four major banks cut in unison on August 20-21: JPMorgan 120 (Underweight), CMS International 121 (Sell), HSBC 136.5, BofA 153 (Neutral). **Not one rates it "Buy"; all four target prices sit below the current price.**

So it is not 14x, it is around 17x. For a company with 10% profit growth and overseas still declining, 17x is reasonable, not cheap.

## Why doesn't Duan Yongping sell?

The most widely circulated rumor: Duan Yongping is the second-largest shareholder, added to his position in July, raising his stake from 6.85% to 7.65%, or 102 million shares. After the interim results, someone asked him whether overseas was too weak, and he said, "As a shareholder, I actually think it's quite good."

Many read this as "the big name is bullish." **But he did not say this price is worth buying.** He built his position in 2024 and 2025 at a cost far below HKD 156; the July add was "topping up after a drop," not "the current value is worth buying."

Shareholder and buyer are two identities: a shareholder asks "will the company still be here in ten years," a buyer asks "is today's price a good deal." **His cost may be HKD 80, yours is HKD 156 — the same company, but not the same investment.**

## Conclusion: wait for two signals

The question with this company is not "is it good" but "is it expensive." The strengths are the business model, management and balance sheet; the weaknesses are the sustainability of growth and cash conversion.

Wait for either of two signals before re-rating: **1) Q3 overseas revenue stops falling quarter on quarter and inventory turnover returns to within 150 days; 2) the share price falls below HKD 120, equivalent to about 13x 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion.**

Until then, the rating is "watch, wait for a pullback." Not a lack of conviction, but the price has yet to factor in the bad news.

In one sentence: **the RMB 5 billion profit is real, but three sets of numbers are in conflict — stores are rising while revenue is falling; gross margin is stable while net profit is falling; profit is rising while cash is falling. All three contradictions point to one conclusion: the quality of growth is deteriorating.**
