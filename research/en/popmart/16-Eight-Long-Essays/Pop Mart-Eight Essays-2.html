02 The business essence: a RMB 59 box filled with "uncertainty" — Pop Mart

Core view

What Pop Mart sells at its core is not the figure itself but "the three seconds before you open it." Part of a blind box's price is the option value of "possibly drawing the hidden edition." Only by understanding this mechanism can you understand why resale-market premiums determine first-hand sales, and why the Labubu ebb tide arrived so fast.

Unit-box economics: of the RMB 59, RMB 20 is cost and RMB 39 is emotion

A standard blind box sells for RMB 59 to 69. Rough cost structure: contract manufacturing RMB 12–15, allocated design RMB 2–3, packaging RMB 2, logistics RMB 2 — under RMB 20 in total. Gross margin of about 70%, consistent with the financials.

Why will consumers pay RMB 59 for something that costs RMB 20? Three reasons. First, the thrill of the "unboxing" — the pleasure of uncertainty, which neuroscience calls "variable ratio reinforcement," the same mechanism as a slot machine. Second, the desire to "collect" — a series has 12 designs, and completing the set delivers a sense of achievement. Third, the need to "show off" — unboxing a hidden edition is worth a WeChat Moments post; it is social currency.

Of the three, the first is product design, the second is SKU strategy, the third is social propagation. Only the three combined sustain a 70% gross margin. Remove one and gross margin falls below 50% (the level of ordinary toys).

Second-hand premium: the hidden pillar of new-home sales

In the economics of blind boxes, the secondary market is not an appendage; it is part of the pricing mechanism. Secret-edition figures sell for several hundred to over a thousand yuan on Xianyu, and that premium is what makes "59 yuan for a box" reasonable — because the expected value may exceed 59 yuan.

When the premium narrows and expected value falls below 59 yuan, demand drops immediately. Consumers did not change their minds; the arithmetic changed. In the first half of 2026, Labubu's secondary-market premium narrowed markedly, and over the same period THE MONSTERS revenue fell 7.5% — the two curves are the same event.

This is also why the company must control supply. The scarcity of secret-edition figures is engineered (odds are disclosed, typically 1/144); release more volume and the premium disappears, and demand collapses. Pop Mart's real operational challenge is not "creating good-looking characters" but "pacing scarcity." In 2025, to capture surging global demand, it ramped up volume; in 2026, as demand ebbed, inventory stretched to 201 days.

Membership: a 51.6% repurchase rate is where this business most resembles a consumer stock

Domestic registered members reached 82.44 million, adding nearly 10 million in six months, contributing 92.9% of mainland revenue, with a repurchase rate of 51.6%. Taken together, these four numbers show it runs a genuine private-domain operation: blind-box machine app, membership tiers, limited releases, points redemption.

How good is a 51.6% repurchase rate? Cosmetics sits around 40%, apparel around 30%, coffee around 50%. Pop Mart has achieved "staples-category" repurchase rates in a "non-staples" category — its most underrated capability.

But look at what drives the repurchase. Members come back because "a new series dropped," not because "the last one ran out." The former runs on supply, the latter on demand. Supply-driven repurchase means that once new products disappoint, the repurchase rate falls faster than revenue — which is why 51.6% needs to be watched every six months.

Three-sentence summary of the business essence

First: It sells the thrill of uncertainty, not toys. So its costs are extremely low and gross margins extremely high, but it is therefore highly dependent on novelty.

Second: Its pricing power comes from the secondary market, not the brand. So it must control scarcity and cannot scale volume without limit. The 2025 volume ramp was meant to capture demand, at the cost of 2026 inventory.

Third: Its repeat purchases come from supply, not demand. So it must launch a new hit every year; stop and it falls. This is the most fundamental difference between it and Moutai or Coca-Cola.

Conclusion

Judging the nature of the business: this is a "high gross margin, low certainty" business. High gross margin comes from sentiment premium; low certainty comes from the attention cycle. You cannot value this kind of business with DCF (because you cannot predict the hit product ten years out); you can only use "normalized profit × consumer-stock multiple." On 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion and 15x, fair market cap is RMB 165 billion, corresponding to HKD 122. That is where the HKD 120 entry point comes from.
