Core view

The most easily misread line in this report is "net profit of RMB 5.038 billion, up 10.1% year on year." It looks like slowing growth, but it is in fact three things stacked together: slowing growth, FX disturbance and falling cash conversion. Only by separating these three can you see the true quality of the profit.

Revenue: mix reverses as overseas shifts from growth engine to drag

Of the RMB 17.17 billion total, domestic contributed RMB 12.20 billion (+47.3%) and overseas RMB 4.97 billion (-11.1%). Overseas share fell 11.3pp YoY, from 40.3% to 29.0%.

By region: Asia-Pacific RMB 2.58 billion (-9.7%), Americas RMB 1.89 billion (-16.5%), Europe RMB 510 million (+5.9%). Two of the three regions posted negative growth, and the only positive one is the smallest. By channel: Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas online -45.6%, Europe online -59.0%, Shopee -62.1%, while stores added a net 36.

The structure points to one conclusion: overseas demand has not disappeared; the customer-acquisition method has failed. Last year's growth rode short-video traffic; with that traffic receding this year, store openings are the only lever left. Store openings (36 in six months) cannot keep pace with the traffic ebb (online -45%), so the total is negative.

FX losses of RMB 840 million and higher expenses took another bite out of profit

Net profit RMB 5.038 billion, +10.1%, held back by three factors: a RMB 720 million FX loss (versus a RMB 120 million gain last year, an RMB 840 million swing), gross margin slipping from 70.3% to 69.7%, and expense growth outpacing revenue (leasing +43.3%, compensation +45.7%).

Excluding FX, profit growth was about 28%, ahead of revenue growth of 23.8% — evidence of no deterioration at the operating level, with margins stable, expenses contained and scale effects intact. The real problem: the market prices the reported numbers, not the ex-FX numbers.

Net margin 29.3%, down 3.7pct YoY, split into FX -4.9pct, gross margin -0.6pct and expenses +1.8pct (partly offset by scale effects). FX is uncontrollable, expenses are discretionary, gross margin is structural. Of the three, only the gross margin decline is a real concern.

Cash: earned RMB 5 billion, cash down RMB 1.3 billion

Cash fell from RMB 13.78 billion to RMB 12.44 billion, a decrease of RMB 1.34 billion. Dividends of RMB 3.15 billion and buybacks of about RMB 1.55 billion were paid during the period, totaling RMB 4.7 billion. A rough calculation gives 5.038 - 4.7 = RMB 340 million, implying a slight increase; the actual figure was -RMB 1.34 billion, a gap of RMB 1.7 billion.

Two sources explain the gap: inventory (turnover days 123 to 201, tying up about RMB 1.1 billion more) and working capital (receivables, prepayments, new-store fit-out). Net cash ratio is about 0.7x, below the healthy threshold of 1.

The severity hinges on whether this is one-off. If it reflects one-off tie-up from overseas stockpiling, it will release in the second half; if it is unsold goods, it means a permanent earnings cut. Test: whether inventory turnover days return to 150 in the 2026 annual report. If they do, it is one-off; if not, a write-down is needed.

Taken together, the three statements point to a company that will survive but slow down

Balance sheet: cash of RMB 12.44 billion, zero bank borrowings, debt ratio 24.9%. This statement means the company will not die. Even if overseas markets stay weak for another two years and inventory is fully written down, it will not be forced to raise capital.

Income statement: gross margin 69.7%, net margin 29.3%, ROE roughly 35%. This statement means the company is a good company. Among consumer goods companies, this margin level is top-tier.

Cash flow statement: net cash ratio 0.7x, inventory days 201, free cash flow consumed by expansion. This statement determines the multiple the company deserves. The market gives companies with poor cash flow only 12–15x.

Conclusion

Financial truth in one sentence: the profit is real, but its quality is deteriorating; the assets are clean, but efficiency is declining. This is not an earnings report that "blows up"; it is one that shows a shift "from asset-light to operation-heavy." The financial signature of this transition is "falling margins, worsening cash flow, rising inventory." The key question is whether these metrics normalise within two quarters.
