05 Industry and competition: a RMB 110.1 billion pool shifting from "expansion" to "dividing the pie" — Pop Mart

China pop toy retail sales forecast at RMB 110.1 billion in 2026 · CAGR 20.9% · six major players

Core view

The designer toy industry is still growing: China's compound growth rate for 2024–2030 is forecast at 20.9%, with retail sales of RMB 110.1 billion in 2026; the global art toy market will reach USD 6.94 billion in 2026, growing 11.6%.But the driver of growth has shifted from "penetration gains" to "IP rotation," which means the leader's share will be repeatedly challenged.

Industry structure: five segments, three business models

Designer toys are not one industry but five: collectibles (blind boxes/figures/plush, RMB 110.1 billion), building-block toys (Bloks, 30.3% share), trading cards (Kayou, 71.1% share), designer-toy multi-brand retail (TOP TOY), and hardcore collectibles (52TOYS, annual revenue of about RMB 600 million).

These five segments are three kinds of business.The first is the IP business (Pop Mart, 52TOYS): built on proprietary characters, high gross margin, high volatility.The second is the retail-channel business (TOP TOY, MINISO): built on site selection and supply chain, 40% gross margin, fast turnover.The third is the licensing business (Bloks, Kayou): built on purchased IP or tournament rights, 50%–55% gross margin, stable but with a low ceiling.

Pop Mart is the only company doing both "IP + channel," which is its strength and also its complexity—it has to run on both legs, and if one slows it drags down the whole.

Competitive landscape: one dominant player and several strong rivals, but the leader is losing momentum

Pop Mart's H1 2026 revenue was RMB 17.173 billion, 7x Bloks' annualized revenue (about RMB 2.4 billion) and 17x TOP TOY's (RMB 985 million). On scale it is one dominant player among many, with no room for argument.

But the dominant player is losing speed: revenue +23.8% (versus +184.7% last year), net profit +10.1%, overseas -11.1%. Over the same period Bloks grew +2.7% (worse), TOP TOY +32.7% (faster but loss-making).The entire industry is moving from rapid expansion into stock competition.

Stock competition has a defining feature: share shifts from who opens stores fastest to whose IP has the longest staying power. That favors Pop Mart — its IP matrix is the deepest (11 IPs above RMB 100 million); but it also works against it — its base is the largest, making growth hardest to sustain.

The real rival in globalization isn't a Chinese company — it's Jellycat

57.2% of Pop Mart's revenue comes from plush, while the global mindshare leader in plush is Jellycat. The British company does not rely on any single character going viral; it relies on a whole aesthetic system of "soft, quirky, cute," releasing new styles every year without retiring old ones, with customers ranging from age 5 to 45.

The two business models differ: Jellycat is a "brand," Pop Mart is a "hit machine." The advantage of a brand is a long lifecycle and resilience through cycles; the advantage of a hit machine is explosive upside and a high ceiling. In Western markets, brands have more pull; in Southeast Asia and Latin America, hits have more pull.

So Pop Mart's globalization will split into two phases: Southeast Asia scaling fast on hits (already happening), and Western markets penetrated slowly through brand-building (underway, and the -45.6% in the Americas is evidence the penetration is not going smoothly). The second phase is ten times harder than the first.

Cycle position: from "category expansion" to "IP rotation"

The three phases of the industry have clear characteristics. 2021–2023, the category-expansion phase: opening stores alone drove growth, and the industry grew 25%+. 2024–2025, the single-product breakout phase: one Labubu lifted industry growth to 30%+.From 2026, the industry enters an IP-rotation phase: no single product can carry a full year, growth depends on a portfolio of 5–7 mid-tier IPs, and industry growth falls back to 15%–20%.

The traits of the winners change in this phase: from "who is best at creating hit products" to "who is best at managing an IP portfolio." Pop Mart's first-half performance shows it is adapting — 11 IPs exceeded RMB 100 million, and the five mid-tier IPs combined were 1.89x THE MONSTERS. But adapting is not winning, because portfolio management carries lower gross margins and higher expenses.

Investment implication:as industry growth steps down, the leader's valuation must step down too.20% growth warrants 20x PE, 15% growth 15x, 10% growth 10–12x. The current 17x 2026E embeds a "15% growth" assumption — if actual growth is only 10%, it is still expensive.

Appendix: Southeast Asia and the West are two different battles

Split overseas into two markets and the conclusion becomes much clearer.

Southeast Asia: the hit-product logic holds.Young population, high social media penetration, strong acceptance of Chinese brands, price-sensitive. Pop Mart's playbook here is "pop-ups + e-commerce + celebrity endorsement," and the 2024–2025 surge came mainly from here. The problem is a low ceiling—the six major Southeast Asian markets combined have fewer than 300 million middle-class consumers, and local brands are catching up fast.

Europe and the US: brand logic, hit products don't work.Consumers have seen too many toy brands and feel no natural affinity for "a new character from China"; Jellycat owns the plush mindshare; channel costs are high (rent in core commercial districts is three to five times that of Southeast Asia). Pop Mart's playbook is "flagship stores + localized design + film and TV tie-ins"—a five-to-ten-year path that may not succeed.

In the first half of 2026, the Americas online channel fell 45.6% and Europe online fell 59.0%—evidence that "using the Southeast Asia playbook in Europe and the US" failed.The company has already made London its European headquarters and plans to open flagship stores, showing it recognizes the problem, but turning the ship takes time.

Conclusion

Industry view: The pool is growing, but the way the pie is split has changed. From "staking out territory" to "intensive cultivation", from "single-product explosions" to "portfolio management". Pop Mart has a first-mover advantage in portfolio management (11 IPs each above RMB 100 million), but it has hit a ceiling in brand strength on globalization (Americas online -45.6%). The industry β is still there, just thinner.
