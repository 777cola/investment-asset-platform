Core view

A decision framework has to be executable, which means writing it as "if..., then..." rather than "watch" or "pay attention to." The three prices, three pieces of evidence and one cap below constitute the full set of operating rules.

Three price levels: build at 120, trim at 200, reassess at 100

HKD 120: build the position. That is about 13x 2026 net profit of RMB 11 billion, implying 25% upside to our HKD 150 neutral case. At this level the message is simple: even if overseas doesn't stop falling, we don't lose big.

HKD 200: trim. That is 24x 2026E, already capturing most of the optimistic case (HKD 225). Trim at this level regardless of how good the fundamentals look—Pop Mart is not Moutai and does not deserve 30x.

HKD 100: reassess. Not a "stop-loss" but a "re-examination"—if it breaks below HKD 100 with domestic same-store sales turning negative or gross margin falling below 66%, admit the mistake and exit; if it is only market panic and fundamentals are intact, add to 5%–8%.

Three data points matter here; ignore the share price

Evidence 1: Overseas sequential growth. Sequential growth in overseas revenue in the Q3 report — even with the line still down YoY — would show that "stores going global" is starting to work. This is the most important signal.

Evidence 2: Inventory turnover. Back within 150 days means goods are moving; stuck above 200 days means demand is genuinely impaired. This metric reflects reality earlier than profit does.

Evidence 3: Non-THE MONSTERS share. Above 80% while total revenue still grows would mean the de-risking from a single IP has succeeded, opening the way for a re-rating from 15x to 18–20x.

One ceiling: 8%, never 10%

Why cap it at all? Because this business hinges on the "next blockbuster," and blockbusters cannot be forecast. What cannot be forecast can only be managed through position sizing, not solved through research.

What does 8% mean in practice? If the stock goes to zero, the portfolio loses 8%; if it doubles, the portfolio gains 8%. For a name that could either double or halve, that is a reasonable risk-reward.

Correlation matters more. Pop Mart rises and falls in lockstep with Laopu, Mixue and MINISO (all down more than 45% in 2026). If the portfolio already holds these names, Pop Mart's 8% must be counted within a "total new consumer position" that should not exceed 20%.

Under what circumstances would we change our view

Getting better (upgrade to Buy): Overseas turned positive QoQ in Q3, inventory back to 150 days and domestic still growing above +30%. All three holding at once would signal the "adjustment year" is over and justify 18x.

Getting worse (downgrade to Avoid): Domestic same-store sales turn negative, gross margin falls below 66%, or overseas declines exceed 20% for two consecutive quarters.

Unchanged: Share price moves alone do not change the view. From HKD 156 down to HKD 140, still 0 position; up to HKD 180, still 0 position. Only data changes the view.

Conclusion

Decision framework in one sentence: 0 position while waiting for HKD 120; build 3% at HKD 120; add to 5% once Q3 results confirm; cap at 8%. Three invalidation lines are set in advance and do not move with the share price. This is the full execution detail behind "watch, wait for a pullback."
