06 Management: a founder who kept his head in the best of years — Pop Mart

Wang Ning holds 49.14% · Deliberate slowdown · First large buyback
Research date 2026/09/07  ·  Price HK$156.20 (+1.43%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 209 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 14.2x

Core view

Judge management not by what they say, but by what they say when they could have stayed silent. At the 2026 August 20 results briefing, Wang Ning said three things he did not have to: that last year "one good result covered a hundred flaws," that the 30% target set at the start of the year would not be met, and that new overseas users "are only here to grab Labubu." Saying this after the stock has already halved is far harder than pretty words at a high share price.

The first thing he got right: signing MOLLY in 2016

Before 2016, Pop Mart was a general merchandise retailer selling other companies' toys at a 30% gross margin. Wang Ning flew to Hong Kong to meet designer Kenny Wong and signed the MOLLY license, transforming the company from "selling toys" to "owning IP."

The value of that step showed up a decade later: in the first half of 2026, MOLLY still generated RMB 900 million in revenue at a 70% gross margin. Without that step, Pop Mart today would be a bigger Miniso, with a 40% gross margin and a 10x valuation.

This is also the key to judging management: whether it does "the hard and right thing" when "making money is easy." Signing IP in 2016 was hard, because at the time no one believed Chinese consumers would pay a premium for "plastic figurines with no story."

The second thing he got right: deliberately tapping the brakes in 2026

2025 revenue reached RMB 37.12bn, up 184.7%, and the market assumed 2026 would be stronger still. Wang Ning said the opposite: 2026 is a "year of operational adjustment" in which driving sales is not the primary goal; the company needs to adjust the way a race car "pits to change tires." Guidance was cut from 30% at the start of the year to no less than 20%.

The decision carries a financial cost: first-half net profit rose only 10.1%, and the stock is down 45.8% from its 52-week high.Organizationally, though, it is the right call: headcount has expanded from a few thousand to 12,000, the sales team from 6,219 to 9,734, and gaps have emerged in cross-border management—left unresolved, next year will be worse.

Most management teams would choose to "tough it out for a year, dress up the numbers, then exit." He did not. That is what "benfen" means in business: do the right thing, even if the near-term numbers look ugly.

Capital allocation: generous dividends, buybacks made mid-slope, new business formats as options

Dividends: final dividend for 2025 of RMB 2.3817 per share, totalling about RMB 3.15 billion, a 24.6% payout ratio. Buybacks: 11.22 million shares cancelled in the first half for HKD 1.74 billion at HKD 140.9–194.9; the interim report announced a further HKD 2–5 billion. New formats: City Park, POP BAKERY, POPOP accessories; wholesale and other revenue RMB 553 million, +111.7%.

Assessment: Both dividends and buybacks are restrained — no reckless spending. The average buyback price of about HKD 155 is roughly in line with the current price, which shows this is not "precise bottom-fishing" but "buying because it is cheap on a long-term view." Such buying produces paper losses in the short term but is right over the long term.

New formats are the only area that warrants caution. Theme parks, desserts, accessories, films — each needs five to ten years of validation, and none sits within the "designer toy" circle of competence. Fortunately the investment is small (RMB 1–2 billion a year, 10% of net profit), so a mistake would not do real damage.

Governance: a single dominant shareholder at 49.14% — a good car with no brakes

Wang Ning holds 49.14% via trusts and holding platforms and 654,381,407 shares directly, or 48.73%. Duan Yongping is the second-largest shareholder at 7.65%, southbound capital holds 24.46%, and the remainder is dispersed.

Upside: fast decision-making, tolerance for short-term pressure and no hostile takeover risk. Downside: no internal checks and balances, leaving strategic errors correctable only by him. The 2026 "adjustment year" shows he can correct course, but that reflects survivorship bias rather than an institutional safeguard.

The item to keep tracking is related-party transactions: many of the parks, desserts and accessories are incubated internally, and the transaction structures with the listed company are complex. No negative events so far, but this is the only governance watchpoint. No financial restatements, regulatory penalties or major shareholder selldowns found — instead there are buybacks, which counts as clean among Hong Kong new-consumption names.

Conclusion

Management 7.0/10: the right person, but weak institutions. Wang Ning did not get carried away in the best years, nor did he hide in the hardest ones; together those two things are more convincing than any strategy document. Points are deducted on governance structure alone: a single dominant shareholder at 49.14% means all course correction rests on him personally. Investment implication: you can "follow the person," but not "follow only the person" — leave room in the position for "he gets it wrong."
