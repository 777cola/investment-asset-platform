07 Risk list: six risks, two of them principal contradictions — Pop Mart

IP fatigue has set in; overseas localisation is under way; watch inventory write-downs

Core view

Rank risks by "probability × impact", not by "how scary it sounds". Of the six, IP lifecycle (already materialized) and overseas localization (underway) are the principal contradictions; inventory, FX, regulation and valuation are secondary. The principal contradictions set the direction; the secondary ones set the magnitude.

Risk 1: IP lifecycle — already happening

THE MONSTERS RMB 4.454 billion, -7.5%, share 34.7%→26.0% — the first half-year decline since Labubu went viral. MOLLY RMB 900 million, -33.6%: a 20-year-old IP is fading too.

The takeaway: it is not that "a given IP will fade" — all IPs fade. Only the timeline differs — 18 months for Labubu, 20 years for MOLLY. Management's answer is the "matrix": 11 IPs above RMB 100 million, with the 5 mid-tier IPs together at 1.89x THE MONSTERS. The matrix hedges single-IP risk; it cannot hedge the risk of "all IPs fading at once."

Implication: absent a new RMB 2 billion-class IP taking over by 2027, revenue falls below RMB 15 billion and profit to RMB 8 billion, implying a P/E of 25x. This is the worst case, with 20%–30% probability.

Risk 2: Overseas localization is already under way

Overseas revenue -11.1%, net store additions 36, and JPMorgan estimates a 14pct decline in overseas operating margin to 30%. Read together: stores are opening, revenue is falling, margin is compressing. That is the classic signature of uneconomic expansion.

The root cause is customer acquisition failure. Last year's growth leaned on TikTok traffic; this year that tide receded (Shopee -62.1%), leaving store openings as the only lever. Store-opening costs (rent +43.3%, compensation +45.7%) are rigid while revenue is elastic, so margin gets squeezed.

Impact: if overseas revenue falls another 20% in 2027 and overseas operating margin drops to 20%, full-year profit would be RMB 1.5–2.0 billion lower. This is the most likely scenario, with 40% probability.

Risks 3-6: inventory, FX, regulation, valuation

Inventory: 201 days, with roughly RMB 5.7 billion of goods tied up. Clearing at a 30% discount would cost RMB 1.7 billion — a third of half-year profit. Medium probability, medium impact. But it is the thermometer of the main contradiction: falling inventory signals overseas is recovering; if it does not fall, demand has genuinely broken.

FX: Overseas is 29% of sales; the first-half FX loss was RMB 720 million. This risk recurs every year and is uncontrollable and unpredictable — 100% probability, RMB 0.5–1.5 billion impact. It does not change fundamentals, only the reported statements.

Regulation: Domestic blind-box rules are already in force (sales banned under age 8, odds disclosure), with no further tightening. The risk is overseas: US consumer-protection legislation on "blind-box-style randomized goods" is advancing. If it extends to designer toys, the 86 stores in the Americas must be reworked. Low probability, high impact.

Valuation: 14.2x TTM looks cheap; 17.4x 2026E is the real price. If profit is cut to RMB 9.5 billion, P/E is 20x. This is the risk that least looks like a risk, because it needs no bad news — only slower growth.

Under what circumstances would this investment thesis be completely wrong?

Write three lines that admit the thesis is wrong; if any one triggers, exit unconditionally:

① Domestic same-store sales turn negative. This is the base business. Domestic revenue of RMB 12.201 billion, +47.3%, is the pillar of the entire thesis; if it collapses, the company is left with nothing but a story of overseas losses.

② Gross margin falls below 66%. This is pricing power. A drop below 66% would show it has degraded from an "IP company" to a "retail company," and valuation would have to be calculated at 8–10x.

③ Overseas revenue falls more than 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters. This is the overseas expansion thesis. If it happens, it means the "store expansion overseas" path is unworkable, and the 221 overseas stores will become 221 burdens.

Appendix: If you must hold, watch these three numbers

Some investors already hold the stock, or would rather not sit in cash and wait. For them, narrow the monitoring metrics to three and review them once a quarter.

First, overseas revenue on a quarter-on-quarter basis. Not YoY — quarter-on-quarter. The YoY figure is distorted by last year's high base; the sequential number shows whether this quarter was better or worse than the last. Two consecutive quarters of sequential growth would show that overseas store expansion is working.

Second, inventory turnover days.201 days is the warning line; 240 days is the red line. This number reflects real demand earlier than profit does — goods that will not sell show up first in inventory, then in discounting, and only lastly in profit.

Third, member repurchase rate.51.6% is the current level. A drop below 45% would signal that the core base is loosening — the most dangerous signal of all, because members are this company's most solid asset.

If two of the three deteriorate, halve the position; if all three deteriorate, exit entirely.Do not watch the share price — it is the result, not the cause.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: Six risks — two already materializing, four "possible." The two already materializing dictate "don't buy expensive"; the four possible ones dictate "don't take a heavy position." Hence the call: 0 position, build only at HKD 120 — not a lack of conviction, but room for "more bad news to keep coming."
