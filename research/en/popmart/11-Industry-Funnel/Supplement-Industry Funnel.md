## Initial screen: why four were eliminated

MINISO: Market cap HKD 22.5 billion, PE about 9x — looks the cheapest. But it is a retailer: revenue topped RMB 10 billion in the first half of 2026 while net profit fell 15.7%; designer toys contribute only RMB 985 million through TOP TOY, which is still loss-making with an operating loss of RMB 72 million. Buying MINISO is not buying designer toys; it is buying a general-merchandise chain weighed down by overseas expansion.

Bloks: Revenue of RMB 595 million in the first quarter of 2026, up only 2.7% year on year, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 28.9 million. Growth has all but stopped, yet the stock still trades at 21x PE. This is the classic "peak at IPO" — it listed in 2025 on the building-block toy story, and by 2026 the growth was gone.

52TOYS: Annual revenue of about RMB 600 million, with own IP at only 24.5% and licensed IP at 64.5%, and a net margin of 4.7%. An order of magnitude smaller, with no IP pricing power. Excluded.

Kayou: A 71.1% share of trading cards, a good business, but the category is cards, not designer toys, and it carries high regulatory risk over minors. It does not sit on the same shelf as Pop Mart. Excluded.

## Final pick: why Pop Mart

Three reasons, all indispensable.

First, it is the only Chinese designer-toy company with a global network of self-operated stores. 676 stores, 221 overseas — 86 in the Americas, 45 in Europe, 90 in Asia-Pacific. This network cannot be bought quickly with money — site selection, leases, local teams and supply chain each take time. Bloks relies mainly on distribution, TOP TOY on MINISO's channels; neither has its own overseas store system.

Second, it has the ability to build IP systematically, not through a single hit. In the first half, 11 IPs exceeded RMB 100 million and 6 topped RMB 1 billion; Twinkle Twinkle grew from RMB 389 million to RMB 2.65 billion; MOLLY, at 20 years old, still did RMB 900 million. This portfolio shows its incubation process is replicable, not luck. This is the biggest difference from Funko — Funko relies on licensing, Pop Mart on its own IP.

Third, its balance sheet can withstand pressure. RMB 12.44 billion in cash, zero bank borrowings, 24.9% debt ratio. Even if overseas underperforms for another two years, it will not be forced to raise capital or sell assets. In an industry where hits disappear, resilience is the biggest advantage.

## But "most worth watching" does not mean "buy now"

The purpose of screening is "to find companies worth researching," not "to find stocks that will rise tomorrow." Pop Mart cleared the first hurdle but failed the second.

Whether it is expensive is clearest through PEG: PE 14.2x (TTM) or 17.4x (2026E), net profit growth 10.1%. PEG is 1.4–1.7, above 1. Compare Bloks: PE 21x, growth 2.7%, PEG near 8 — more expensive. So Pop Mart is "the least expensive name in this track," but in absolute terms it is still not cheap.

Another reverse indicator: big-bank target prices. On August 20–21, JPMorgan HKD 120 (underweight), CMS International HKD 121 (sell), HSBC HKD 136.5, BofA HKD 153 (neutral). All four are below or near the current price of HKD 156.2, and none rates it "buy." This is no coincidence — it is the consensus of professional institutions on "profit downgrades."

## The funnel's conclusion: the top name on the watchlist

Of the six, Pop Mart is the only company that simultaneously meets the criteria of "large enough scale, genuine channels, a balance sheet that can hold up, and a business that can be understood." The other five: MINISO is not a pure play on designer toys, Bloks' growth has stalled, 52TOYS is too small, Kayou operates in a different category, and Jellycat cannot be bought.

So it ranks first on the watchlist, but it is not on the buy list. There is only one difference between the watchlist and the buy list: price. Below HKD 120, it enters the buy list; above HKD 180, it can be removed even from the watchlist—at that point the risk-reward has completely inverted.

One more point: why not MINISO? Because its net profit fell 15.7% in the first half of 2026 and the stock is down 48% year to date, dragged down by overseas expansion and Yonghui. Buying it is buying "a general merchandise chain in the middle of a turnaround," with designer toys merely an option embedded within. If TOP TOY is ever spun off and listed, that would be a pure designer-toy target and could be reassessed.
