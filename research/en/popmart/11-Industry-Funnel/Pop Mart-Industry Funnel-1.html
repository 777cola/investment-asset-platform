Core call: the only company in this industry worth watching, but not a stock worth buying right now.

The designer toy universe offers only six investable names: Pop Mart, MINISO (parent of TOP TOY), Bloks, and the unlisted 52TOYS, Kayou and Jellycat. A rough screen eliminates four, leaving Pop Mart as the final pick — but "most worth watching" does not mean "buy now."

Pop Mart wins on channels and IP matrix, loses on valuation and growth. At 14.2x TTM it looks cheaper than Bloks at 21x, but Bloks' profit is growing while Pop Mart's is declining, so on PEG it is the more expensive of the two.

Initial screen: why four were eliminated

MINISO: Market cap HKD 22.5 billion, PE about 9x — looks the cheapest. But it is a retailer: revenue topped RMB 10 billion in the first half of 2026 while net profit fell 15.7%; designer toys contribute only RMB 985 million through TOP TOY, which is still loss-making with an operating loss of RMB 72 million. Buying MINISO is not buying designer toys; it is buying a general-merchandise chain weighed down by overseas expansion.

Bloks: Revenue of RMB 595 million in the first quarter of 2026, up only 2.7% year on year, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 28.9 million. Growth has all but stopped, yet the stock still trades at 21x PE. This is the classic "peak at IPO" — it listed in 2025 on the building-block toy story, and by 2026 the growth was gone.

52TOYS: Annual revenue of about RMB 600 million, with own IP at only 24.5% and licensed IP at 64.5%, and a net margin of 4.7%. An order of magnitude smaller, with no IP pricing power. Excluded.

Kayou: A 71.1% share of trading cards, a good business, but the category is cards, not designer toys, and it carries high regulatory risk over minors. It does not sit on the same shelf as Pop Mart. Excluded.

Final pick: why Pop Mart

Three reasons, all indispensable.

First, it is the only Chinese designer-toy company with a global network of self-operated stores. 676 stores, 221 overseas — 86 in the Americas, 45 in Europe, 90 in Asia-Pacific. That network cannot be bought quickly: site selection, leases, local teams and supply chain each take time. Bloks relies mainly on distribution, TOP TOY on MINISO's channels; neither has its own overseas store system.

Second, it builds IP systematically rather than through a single hit. In the first half, 11 IPs exceeded RMB 100 million and 6 topped RMB 1 billion; Twinkle Twinkle grew from RMB 389 million to RMB 2.65 billion; MOLLY, at 20 years old, still did RMB 900 million. The portfolio shows a replicable incubation process, not luck. This is the biggest difference from Funko — Funko relies on licensing, Pop Mart on its own IP.

Third, its balance sheet can withstand pressure. RMB 12.44 billion in cash, zero bank borrowings, 24.9% debt ratio. Even if overseas underperforms for another two years, it will not be forced to raise capital or sell assets. In an industry where hits disappear, resilience is the biggest advantage.

But "most worth watching" does not mean "buy now"

Screening is about finding companies worth researching, not stocks that will rise tomorrow. Pop Mart cleared the first hurdle but failed the second.

PEG is the clearest gauge of valuation: P/E 14.2x (TTM) or 17.4x (2026E) against net profit growth of 10.1% gives a PEG of 1.4–1.7, above 1. Bloks, by contrast, trades on 21x P/E with 2.7% growth — a PEG near 8, and more expensive. Pop Mart is thus "the least expensive name in this track," but on an absolute basis it is still not cheap.

Big-bank target prices offer a second reverse indicator. On August 20–21, JPMorgan set HKD 120 (underweight), CMS International HKD 121 (sell), HSBC HKD 136.5 and BofA HKD 153 (neutral). All four sit below or near the current price of HKD 156.2, and none carries a "buy" rating. That is no coincidence — it reflects the consensus of professional institutions on "profit downgrades."

The funnel's conclusion: the top name on the watchlist

Of the six, Pop Mart is the only one that simultaneously clears four hurdles: scale, genuine channels, a balance sheet that holds up, and a business model that can be understood. The other five fall short for different reasons: MINISO is not a pure play on designer toys, Bloks' growth has stalled, 52TOYS is too small, Kayou operates in a different category, and Jellycat cannot be bought.

That puts it first on the watchlist, but not on the buy list. The watchlist and the buy list differ on one variable only: price. Below HKD 120 it enters the buy list; above HKD 180 it can be dropped even from the watchlist—by then the risk-reward has fully inverted.

And why not MINISO? Because net profit fell 15.7% in the first half of 2026 and the stock is down 48% year to date, dragged down by overseas expansion and Yonghui. Buying it means buying a general merchandise chain mid-turnaround, with designer toys merely an embedded option. If TOP TOY is ever spun off and listed, that would be a pure designer-toy target and could be reassessed.

Conclusion

Pop Mart is the final pick from a screen of six companies, but the conclusion is "first on the watchlist, not on the buy list". It wins on channels, IP matrix and balance sheet; it loses on growth, cash flow and valuation. A PEG of 1.4–1.7 and four major banks' target prices all below the current price — those two numbers alone are enough to justify waiting. Wait for HKD 120, or wait for the Q3 report.
