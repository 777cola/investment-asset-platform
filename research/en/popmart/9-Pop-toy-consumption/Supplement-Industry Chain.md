## IP is the only scarce resource in the value chain and the one link we cannot control

Pop Mart has two upstream models. The first is proprietary artist IP: in 1H, revenue from artist IP reached RMB 15.2895 billion, up 25% YoY, with 11 IPs exceeding RMB 100 million and 6 surpassing RMB 1 billion. Pop Mart signs these artists directly and splits revenue itself, so gross margin is highest here. The second is licensed IP—Disney, Sanrio and the 2026 World Cup; LABUBU is the first Chinese original IP ever invited in World Cup history. Licensing requires minimum guarantees and revenue splits, and gross margin runs 10–15 percentage points lower.

Bargaining power is the key. With a designer who is not yet famous, Pop Mart is the principal; with an IP that is already famous, the designer is the principal. Now that Labubu has exploded, its creator Kasing Lung will inevitably command better terms on renewal. Hence the hard push to build a matrix of proprietary IP—not to diversify risk, but to keep bargaining power in its own hands.

Raw materials sit furthest upstream but carry little value, at 8%–12% of retail price. In 1H26, rising raw material costs dragged on gross margin—evidence that while this link is worth little, it can still transmit pressure, because contract manufacturers have no capacity to absorb it.

## Midstream: foundry is both the upside and the Achilles' heel

Pop Mart does not manufacture in-house. Vinyl figures and plush toys are outsourced to contract manufacturers in Guangdong and Vietnam, with the company handling only design, quality control and delivery scheduling. This is the essence of the asset-light model—capacity flexes with demand, and there is no need to carry factories through the off-season.

But the 2026 category shift exposed the model's soft spot. Plush rose from under 40% last year to 57.2%. Plush involves more process steps than vinyl (cutting, sewing, filling, flocking), takes 30–50% longer to deliver, and fewer factories can make it. Inventory turnover stretched from 123 days to 201 days in the first half, partly on the longer plush stocking cycle.

Contract manufacturers run gross margins of only 15%–25% and have weak bargaining power, so in theory the brand could push all the pressure onto them. In reality: when a hit product ramps, the contract manufacturers pick their customers, not the other way round. The 2025 global Labubu sellout was the extreme version of this logic. So "asset-light" is a liability, not an asset, when demand explodes.

## Downstream: self-operated stores are the moat; third-party e-commerce is the funnel

The downstream business has two legs. The first is 676 self-operated stores (221 overseas) plus 2,827 Robo Shops, carrying a gross margin of about 70% because it captures both the retail markup and an experience premium. Stores are not just points of sale; they are where consumers can touch, hug and photograph the product — and with plush now at 57.2% of the mix, that function matters more than ever.

Third-party e-commerce is the other leg, and the one that fell hardest in 2026: Shopee -62.1%, Asia-Pacific online -39.8%, Americas online -45.6%.Platform e-commerce is essentially renting traffic: when traffic is cheap it is a growth engine, when traffic is expensive it is a cost center. Pop Mart captured the TikTok dividend over the past two years; now that dividend is over and the platforms are collecting rent.

So the company is shifting its focus to stores, setting up a European headquarters in London and planning flagship stores in Milan, London, Paris and New York.This is a shift from renting traffic to buying location — heavy upfront investment, but the assets are its own. The direction is right; the payback period simply stretches from 3 months to 18–24 months.

## Cycle position: from "channel expansion phase" to "IP rotation phase"

The designer toy industry has been through two cycles in five years. 2021–2023 was the channel expansion period: the fastest store rollout won, and Pop Mart reached RMB 6 billion in revenue on more than 400 stores. 2024–2025 was the single-product breakout period: Labubu alone took the company from RMB 13 billion to RMB 37.1 billion, growth of 184.7%.

2026 marks a third stage: the IP rotation period. No single IP can carry growth; revenue depends on a portfolio of 5–7 IPs each worth RMB 1 billion. Stores are still opening, but output per store is falling. The online traffic dividend is over, and growth returns to the hard grind of site selection and operations. Industry growth slows from 20%+ to 10%–15%, and the leader's net margin falls from 33% to 25%–28%.

Cycle position: not a recession, but normalization. Going from doubling in a year back to up 15% in a year is the inevitable path for every hit-product category. The problem is valuation — the multiple the market gives it still reflects the single-product breakout period.
