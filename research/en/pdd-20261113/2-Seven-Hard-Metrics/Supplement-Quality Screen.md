## Seven out of five criteria met: this company's problem is capital allocation, not operations

The seven hard metrics are a tool for weeding out the bad, not for picking the best. Their job is to eliminate, within ten minutes, companies that look cheap but are actually traps. Pinduoduo passed five; the two it failed point to cash-flow coverage and shareholder returns—one is seasonal, the other structural.

## Article 4 holds: 0.95 cash-flow coverage reflects working capital, not a quality problem

Operating cash flow in Q2 2026 was RMB 25.7 billion, and net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 27.18 billion, a ratio of 0.95 — just short of 1. The gap is not a buildup of receivables — Pinduoduo's merchant payments are prepaid, so it barely fronts any capital itself — but rather quarter-to-quarter swings in merchant deposits and accounts payable.

Over a longer horizon, full-year 2025 Non-GAAP net profit was RMB 107.3 billion, and book cash rose RMB 34.1 billion over the year, even as the company spent on its "RMB 100 billion support" program — cash generation is not in question. So this item fails the test, but it does not constitute a risk; it is definitional noise.

## Article 7 is not the real problem: zero return is

A 0% dividend payout and 0% buyback — two zeros together mean shareholders get not a single cent of cash from this company. Compare with peers: Alibaba still had buybacks and dividends in fiscal 2026, JD.com has buybacks, and Tencent's annual buybacks of HKD 100 billion have become the anchor for its valuation.

Failing this test has a direct consequence: you cannot put a floor under the valuation using cash flow. A company trading at 7x earnings, earning RMB 100 billion a year and parking all its cash in the bank is theoretically worth more; but as long as it does not distribute, the market can only price it on the basis that "this money may be burned." Pinduoduo's USD 112.4 billion market cap against RMB 456.4 billion of cash is a discount of more than half — and that discount is the market's price for zero returns.

## The narrow passage of Article 6 is less alarming than what deserves greater vigilance

Total revenue +8% looks decent, but break it down: advertising +3.5%, transaction services +13%. Advertising is the entirety of domestic monetization on the main platform, and 3.5% means the domestic business is no longer growing; all the double-digit growth comes from Temu commissions, and Temu is being chased down by tariffs and compliance costs.

Headline growth masks the structural shift. Looking only at +8%, you would misjudge this company as still in its growth phase; look at advertising's 3.5% and you know it is already playing defense. The two readings differ threefold — two worlds inside the same earnings report.

## The eighth criterion that the seven screens cannot capture: policy exposure

All seven are financial metrics; none measures the risk of "regulators rewriting gross margin." Pinduoduo's three 2026 variables—the new US rule on small parcels under USD 2,500, the EU's fixed cost of EUR 5 per category, and the 6% of revenue penalty cap under the product safety investigation—are all absent from the table.

This is the boundary of hard metrics: they tell you whether the company made money in the past, not whether the business can keep earning under the same rules next year. So the seven can only serve as the first gate; industry-chain and policy analysis must fill in behind them.
