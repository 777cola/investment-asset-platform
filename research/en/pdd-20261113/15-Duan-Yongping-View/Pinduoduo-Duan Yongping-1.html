Core call: the business is legible, the people are not

Run it through Duan Yongping's checklist: a good business — rent collection, 57% gross margin, no inventory build-up; it will still be here in ten years, though the cross-border half is uncertain; the business is understandable, the people are not — RMB 456.4 billion of cash with no explanation for five years; at 3.4x ex-cash P/E, worth buying a little.

Q: Is this a good business?

Good — but where is the good? It holds no inventory, builds no warehouses, employs no army of couriers. Merchants list goods, users place orders, and it collects commissions and sells advertising. This is rent collection, not running a business.

When I ran Bubugao, inventory and channels were the hardest part — a single unsold item was dead stock on my hands. Pinduoduo does not have this problem: unsold goods are the merchant's problem. Hence its 57% gross margin and 24% net margin, while JD.com sells RMB 346.4 billion of goods and earns just RMB 7.1 billion in a quarter. The difference is not effort; it is the model.

Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

It will survive. Demand for cheap goods does not disappear—that has held for a decade. Six hundred million people in China earn less than RMB 2,000 a month, so this business will always have a base.

But "still here" and "still the way it looks now" are two different things. In ten years it could be a purely domestic company, with Temu reduced to a local-warehouse business in Europe—or the reverse, with overseas becoming the main body. These two paths differ by a factor of two in valuation, and I cannot judge which one it will be. What I cannot see clearly, I do not value—I only give it a price range.

Q: Can you understand it?

The business is understandable; the people are not. Those are two separate questions.

The business in three sentences: low prices pull in users, white-label goods supply the inventory, and advertising plus commissions monetise it. Every stock I buy — NetEase, Apple, Moutai — can be summed up in three sentences. Pinduoduo too.

Capital allocation is what I cannot understand. RMB 456.4 billion sits on the books, five years have passed without a dividend or buyback, and there is no clear statement of what the money is for. I understand the intent to invest in supply chain and overseas, but "investing" and "accumulating" are two different things — cash rose another RMB 34.1 billion in a year, which shows little was actually deployed.When management won't return cash to shareholders, you can't value the company on discounted cash flow — only on a discount to assets.

Q: At what price should we buy?

At USD 78.97 the stock is a modest buy. Market cap is RMB 79.80 billion; net of RMB 45.64 billion in cash, the business costs RMB 34.16 billion and earns RMB 9.95 billion a year — 3.4x.

What does 3.4x mean? Apple was in the teens when I bought it; Moutai was over 20x. At 3.4x you are paying almost nothing for growth — only for "it doesn't die." At this price you don't need to understand ten years; three is enough.

But "buy a little" and "buy a lot" are two different things. This price is worth buying, not worth betting on.

Q: What is the biggest fear?

What worries me most is capital spent with no return.

Slow growth does not trouble me. A slow-growing company that generates profit and pays dividends is a good company. What I fear is a company earning RMB 100 billion a year and sitting on over RMB 400 billion of cash, which then one day tells you it needs to build overseas warehouses, meet compliance requirements and fund subsidies — burns RMB 80 billion in a year, and cannot say when it will earn that back.

The EU bill has already started to land: a EUR 200 million fine in July, a EUR 3 tariff per product category from July, and an additional EUR 2 handling fee from November. A fixed cost of EUR 5 per category is fatal for a business with an average order value of EUR 10. It either raises prices and loses customers, or absorbs the cost itself. Neither looks good.

Q: Why buy Pinduoduo instead of Alibaba or JD.com?

I look at Alibaba too, but AI cloud now accounts for half its valuation, and that's the part I don't understand. Operating profit fell 57% YoY in the June quarter this year — when earning power is declining, you can't just buy the e-commerce half.

JD is a good company and logistics is a real moat, but the 1P model runs a net margin of only 2% — hard-earned money — and revenue has already turned negative (2026Q2 −2.9%). Cheap as both are, I'd rather buy the one with 57% gross margin.

Q: Does this fit your "benfen" (duty)?

Benfen means doing the right thing and doing it well. For users, Pinduoduo is benfen: it genuinely lets poor people buy cheap goods, and that is a virtue. For merchants it is not quite benfen—penalty rules and refund-only policies breed deep resentment, yet merchants stay, which shows the burden remains bearable.

For shareholders it is not benfen. If you take shareholders' money, you owe shareholders an account. Not necessarily a dividend—but an explanation: how the money will be used and when results will show. Five years without an explanation is not benfen. So I do not hold a heavy position. This is not a valuation issue; it is a people issue.

Conclusion

In one sentence: the business scores 8, the people score 4, the price scores 9 — so buy a little, not a lot.

PDD is a company I understand and am willing to own a little of at 3.4x ex-cash P/E. It is not, however, a company I would entrust my money to for ten years, because I don't know where the RMB 456.4 billion will ultimately end up.

On execution: cap the position at 3%, build over four quarters, add below USD 70, trim above USD 120, and do nothing in between. No forecasts, no timing calls — just act on price.
