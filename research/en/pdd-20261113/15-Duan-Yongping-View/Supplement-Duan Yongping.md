## Q: Is this a good business?

Good. Where is the good? It does not hold inventory, does not build warehouses, does not employ hundreds of thousands of couriers. Merchants list goods, users place orders, and it takes commissions and sells advertising. This is rent collection, not running a business.

When I ran Bubugao, the hardest things were inventory and channels — a single unsold item was dead stock on my hands. Pinduoduo does not have this problem — unsold goods are the merchant's problem. Hence its 57% gross margin and 24% net margin, while JD.com sells RMB 346.4 billion of goods and earns just RMB 7.1 billion in a quarter. The difference is not effort; it is the model.

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

It will survive. Demand for cheap goods does not disappear—that has held for a decade. Six hundred million people in China earn less than RMB 2,000 a month, so this business will always have a base.

But "still here" and "still the way it looks now" are two different things. In ten years it could be a purely domestic company, with Temu reduced to a local-warehouse business in Europe—or the reverse, with overseas becoming the main body. These two paths differ by a factor of two in valuation, and I cannot judge which one it will be. What I cannot see clearly, I do not value—I only give it a price range.

## Q: Can you understand it?

The business is understandable; the people are not. These are two separate matters.

The business in three sentences: low prices pull in users, white-label goods supply the inventory, advertising and commissions monetize it. Every stock I buy — NetEase, Apple, Moutai — can be summed up in three sentences. Pinduoduo too.

What is not understandable is capital allocation. With RMB 456.4 billion on the books, five years without a dividend or buyback, and no clear statement of what the money is for. I understand they want to invest in supply chain and overseas, but "investing" and "accumulating" are two different things — cash rose another RMB 34.1 billion in a year, which shows little was actually deployed.When management won't return cash to shareholders, you can't value the company on discounted cash flow — only on a discount to assets.

## Q: At what price should we buy?

At this price you can buy a little. USD 78.97, market cap of RMB 79.80 billion; net of RMB 45.64 billion in cash, the business sells for RMB 34.16 billion and earns RMB 9.95 billion a year — 3.4x.

What does 3.4x mean? When I bought Apple it was in the teens; when I bought Moutai it was over 20x. 3.4x means you are paying almost nothing for growth — only for "it doesn't die." At this price you don't need to understand ten years; three is enough.

But "buy a little" and "buy a lot" are two different things. This price is worth buying, not worth betting on.

## Q: What is the biggest fear?

What I fear most is money being spent where there is no return.

I am not afraid of slow growth. A slow-growing company that makes money and pays dividends is a good company. What I fear is a company that earns RMB 100 billion a year, holds over RMB 400 billion in cash, and then one day tells you it needs to build overseas warehouses, meet compliance requirements and pay subsidies, burns RMB 80 billion in a year, and cannot say when it will earn that back.

The EU bill has already started: a EUR 200 million fine in July, a EUR 3 tariff per product category from July, and an additional EUR 2 handling fee from November. A fixed cost of EUR 5 per category is fatal for a business with an average order value of EUR 10. It either raises prices and loses customers, or absorbs the cost itself. Neither looks good.

## Q: Why buy Pinduoduo instead of Alibaba or JD.com?

I look at Alibaba too, but half its price is now priced by AI cloud, and I don't understand that part. Its operating profit in the June quarter this year fell 57% year on year — when earning power is declining, you can't just buy the e-commerce half.

JD is a good company, and logistics is a real moat, but the 1P model's net margin is only 2% — hard-earned money — and revenue has already turned negative (2026Q2 −2.9%). Cheap as both are, I'd rather buy the one with 57% gross margin.

## Q: Does this fit your "benfen" (duty)?

Benfen means doing the right thing and doing it well. For users, Pinduoduo is benfen—it genuinely lets poor people buy cheap goods, and that is a virtue. For merchants, it is not quite benfen: penalty rules and refund-only policies breed deep resentment, but merchants stay, which shows the burden remains bearable.

For shareholders, it is not benfen. If you take shareholders' money, you owe shareholders an account. Not necessarily a dividend—but an explanation: how the money will be used and when results will show. Five years without an explanation—that is not benfen. So I do not hold a heavy position. This is not a valuation issue; it is a people issue.
