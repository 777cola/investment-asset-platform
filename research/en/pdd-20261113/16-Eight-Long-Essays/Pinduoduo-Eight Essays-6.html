Management: good at business, poor at sharing the spoils — Pinduoduo

Core view

PDD's decision-making is simple: Huang Zheng sets direction (roughly 26% stake, super-voting rights), Chen Lei runs execution (CEO since 2020) and Zhao Jiazhen runs supply (co-CEO since 2023). No parachuted professional managers, and no CFO speaking publicly.

This structure delivers fast decisions, strong execution and almost no M&A, but also almost no checks and balances.

Capability: this point is not in dispute

Eight years, two achievements: the main site went from zero to 800 million users, and Temu from zero to a GMV scale of USD 100 billion. In between there was not a single major strategic misstep, not one acquisition that blew up, not one goodwill impairment.In Chinese internet, this execution record is scarce.

Organisational efficiency matters more: roughly 15,000 employees run a business that Alibaba runs with 200,000 and JD.com with 500,000.Revenue per employee is so high it doesn't look like a retail company.

Stance: this item is uncontroversial too

RMB 456.4 billion of cash, five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, with no explanation of capital allocation. Asked on the earnings call, management said it was "investing in the supply chain and ecosystem." Not wrong, but it is not an account to shareholders.

The contrast with peers is starker: Alibaba continues buybacks plus dividends, JD.com buys back regularly, and Tencent's annual HKD 100 billion of buybacks has become a valuation anchor. At similarly cheap levels, peers buy their own stock while this company hoards cash. This is not an industry-norm issue; it is a choice.

Integrity: no fraud, but a string of compliance bills

The good: eight years since listing with no SEC enforcement action, no qualified auditor opinion and no major related-party scandal. Among China concept stocks, that alone filters out most companies.

The bad: a staff sudden-death incident in 2021, removal of the app from Google Play over security issues in 2022–2023, EU DSA proceedings opened in 2024, a EUR 200 million fine in July 2026, and a product-safety investigation still ongoing. These are not financial problems; they are licensing problems.

Succession: the organization runs, but the bench is thin

Five years into Chen Lei's tenure, the handover has been smooth and the strategy continuous, demonstrating that the organisation can now run without its founder. Zhao Jiazhen, an internally groomed second-generation leader, was promoted to co-CEO in 2023.

The risk sits at the third layer. Core decisions still rest on the Huang Zheng–Chen Lei dual core, and no one has yet emerged who can carry the load independently. Alibaba has Jiang Fan and JD.com has Xu Ran; Pinduoduo's management bench looks visibly thinner by comparison. Were Chen Lei to leave, there would be no near-term problem, but the quality of long-term strategic judgment would decline.

A counterexample: had Colin Huang not given up shares in 2020

In 2020 he donated 2.37% and transferred 3.77%, cutting his stake from 43.3% to around 29%. Had he not done so, his stake today would exceed 35%, giving him tighter control of the company — but for minority shareholders it would make no difference at all.

The significance lies less in the percentage than in the signal: his willingness to give up shares shows he does not treat the company as a private fiefdom. That sits oddly with "no dividend for five years" — a man willing to cede shares, yet unwilling to distribute cash. The likely explanation: he treats the company as a long-term endeavour, not an ATM.

The most notable move of 2026

The 2025 annual report references the formation of "Xinpinmu" to test the waters in private label. This marks Pinduoduo's first step from "platform" toward "brand/private label." The rationale is clear: private label lifts gross margin, tightens quality control and counters the "low price, low quality" criticism.

The cost is twofold: private label compromises the platform's neutrality and ties up capital. If "Xinpinmu" scales, Pinduoduo shifts from "the one collecting rent" to "the one collecting rent and running a business too"—and the valuation framework will need to change once again.

Conclusion

Management scorecard: capability 8/10, attitude 3/10, governance 6/10, overall 4/10.

This gate does not veto a buy, but it caps position size. With no dividends or buybacks, returns can only come from the share price, and the timing of the share price is not yours to decide.

What to watch: whether the 2026 annual report sets out a capital-return policy for the first time. If it does, the score rises from 4 to 6 and the position cap moves from 3% to 5%.
