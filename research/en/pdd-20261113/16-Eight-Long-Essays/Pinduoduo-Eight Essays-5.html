Core view

China's online retail sales reached roughly RMB 15 trillion in 2025, with penetration at about 45% and growth slowing to 7%–10%. This is a stock market: Alibaba holds 45% share, JD.com 20%, Pinduoduo 15% and Douyin 10%.

Pinduoduo ranks third: the two ahead are larger, the one behind is faster. Its strategic room is squeezed in the middle.

In a mature market the rule is to win share through efficiency, not subsidies

The 2020–2023 subsidy war taught all four players the same lesson: share bought with subsidies does not stick. By 2026, competition has shifted back to efficiency — who runs the lowest fulfilment cost, the highest monetisation rate, the best merchant retention.

Pinduoduo holds the advantage in this fight: it does not have to run logistics or warehouses, nor carry hundreds of thousands of staff. In the same price war, it bleeds the slowest.

Douyin is the only game-changing rival

What Douyin captures is not "cheapness" but the moment of "deciding what to buy." Users buy in passing while scrolling videos and no longer go to Pinduoduo to compare prices. That downgrades Pinduoduo from an "entry point" to a "price-comparison tool."

The process is slow but irreversible. Metrics to watch: Pinduoduo's average daily opens per user, search share and new-customer acquisition cost. If daily opens fall while the selling-expense ratio rises, its entry-point status is eroding.

The real shock comes from legislation, not competition

US: the de minimis exemption was fully suspended in August 2025, and a CBP proposed rule dated October 8, 2026 subjects all shipments under USD 2,500 to strict scrutiny. EU: the EUR 150 duty-free threshold was removed on July 1, with an added EUR 3 tariff per category, followed by an additional EUR 2 handling fee per category on November 1; product safety investigations are still proceeding, with fines capped at 6% of global annual revenue.

A legislative shock differs from a cyclical shock in one respect: it is irreversible. Costs shift permanently higher and the business model must be rewritten. Temu's response—local warehouses plus semi-managed operations—essentially converts "cross-border arbitrage" into "local retail." This is a different business.

How will the share change?

Short term (1 year): Pinduoduo holds 15% domestic share, while overseas rising localisation costs drag GMV growth from its peak to below 20%. Medium term (3 years): if Temu's localisation works, overseas becomes a second growth engine; if not, it retreats to China as a cash cow.

Industry position: it will neither take all the winnings nor be eliminated. It will become a mature platform with high margins, low growth and ample cash — exactly why the market now gives it an 8x P/E.

Who does Temu actually compete with overseas?

Not Amazon. Amazon's users want "here tomorrow"; Temu's users want "so cheap it doesn't hurt". The two are chasing different wallets.

The real same-lane competitors are SHEIN, AliExpress and national discount retailers (Germany's Aldi, America's Dollar General). What these rivals share: they too do not rely on brand premium, but on supply-chain efficiency. Temu leads in this lane, but by a smaller margin than it enjoys at home.

Beyond Share: How Much Incremental Growth Is Left in the Industry

China's online retail sales total roughly RMB 15 trillion, with penetration at around 45% and growth of 7%–10%. At 45% penetration there is still room to rise, but the pace will be very slow: the remaining incremental users sit in lower-tier cities and among the elderly, where customer acquisition costs run several times those of early users.

For Pinduoduo, this implies domestic growth will stay below GDP growth for a long time, and its revenue curve will increasingly resemble that of a utility rather than an internet company. The valuation should change accordingly.

Conclusion

Industry position: third in a mature market is a decent spot; the real threat is legislation, not peers.

On competition it can hold its ground (lowest cost, slowest cash burn); on expansion it is constrained by market access, with a fixed cost of EUR 5 per category.

The valuation therefore prices in "can hold its ground" (8–12x), not "can expand" (20x) — the difference between USD 99 and USD 128.
