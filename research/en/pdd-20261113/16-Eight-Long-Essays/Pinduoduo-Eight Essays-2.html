The business model: one rent collector, two rent payers — Pinduoduo

Core view

Pinduoduo is essentially a rent collector. It does not manufacture, hold inventory or deliver; it supplies traffic and a marketplace, charging merchants for advertising and commissions. Of its RMB 112.36 billion revenue in 2026Q2, RMB 57.6 billion came from advertising and RMB 54.7 billion from commissions, with not a single cent from product markups.

The financial signature of a rent-collection model is three highs and one low: high gross margin (57.3%), high net margin (24.2%), high cash flow (RMB 25.7 billion per quarter) and low capex (close to zero).

The Rent Collector: The Platform Captures the Most Stable Slice of the Chain

A product with a factory price of RMB 10 sells for RMB 40 at retail. The factory earns RMB 0.5 to 1.5, the courier RMB 1.5, the platform RMB 8 (RMB 2 commission + RMB 6 amortized advertising), and the rest goes to traffic and channels. The platform takes the thickest, most stable slice of the value chain.

That split is no accident. The platform controls two things: demand (800 million users) and the rules — how traffic is allocated, how prices are set, when money settles. Whoever writes the rules captures the excess profit.

The first to pay rent: white-label factories

Why do factories accept this? On Pinduoduo, a small Yiwu factory can reach 800 million users — reach that would take dozens of distributors and years through traditional channels. The platform gives "volume" and takes "price."

But this exchange has a tipping point. Once factory gross margins are squeezed below 5%, there is no money to improve quality, upgrade equipment or build a brand. At the end of a low-price moat lies the ceiling on supply quality — Pinduoduo's most long-term risk, and more fundamental than tariffs.

The second rent payer: Temu's overseas consumers and merchants

Temu's rent-collection model works in China but not overseas, where an entire additional layer of "access rent" applies: tariffs, handling fees, compliance and local warehousing. In China it collects rent; overseas it pays rent.

From November 2026, the EU will impose a fixed cost of EUR 5 per product category (EUR 3 tariff + EUR 2 handling fee). On a EUR 10 parcel, that is EUR 5. This is no longer rent collection—it is collecting taxes on behalf of customs.

The ceiling and floor of a business

Ceiling: domestic ad growth of 3.5%, a user base that has peaked, and a monetisation rate above 5% that would push merchants away. Floor: RMB 456.4 billion of cash, zero debt, and RMB 100 billion of shareholder earnings a year.

For a business with a low ceiling and a high floor, the right framework is "bond plus option": cash and existing profit are the bond (RMB 341.6 billion for the core business plus RMB 456.4 billion of cash), while Temu is the option, currently valued at zero.

Why this business requires no capital expenditure

A domestic platform's marginal cost is just servers and payment rails: one more user, one more order, and cost barely moves. That is why capex has long run at only 1%–2% of revenue, whereas JD.com must spend tens of billions of RMB building warehouses.

Overseas is different. Local warehouses, local procurement and local compliance teams are all fixed assets and headcount that cost money. Temu's localisation effort, in essence, turns Pinduoduo from a "no capex needed" model into one that needs capex. That single shift is enough to take its valuation multiple down a notch.

What does a rental business fear most: tenants leaving

The biggest risk to a rent-collection model is not rent cuts but tenants failing to show up. For Pinduoduo, the tenants are merchants and the rent is advertising and commissions. Once merchants conclude that "selling on Pinduoduo doesn't make money," supply thins out first and only then does the price advantage disappear.

What really deserves attention, then, is not user count — it has peaked and carries no information — but merchant-side metrics: number of merchants, GMV per merchant and monetisation rate. The company discloses none of these three numbers; they can only be inferred from the intensity of the "RMB 100 billion support" programme and the sales expense ratio.

Conclusion

What this business really is: an excellent rent collector, with rent drawn from merchants' capacity to pay — and that capacity has a ceiling.

Domestic is close to that ceiling (advertising +3.5%), while overseas is being eaten by access rent (EUR 5 per category). Fair value is therefore "bond plus option": cash and existing profit are the bond, Temu is the option.

The bond is worth RMB 341.6 billion for the core business plus RMB 456.4 billion of cash; the option is valued at zero. Together they give a neutral target price of USD 99.
