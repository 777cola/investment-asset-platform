Core view

Separate risk into two buckets: what can permanently destroy your principal (real risk) and what merely moves the share price (fake risk). Most investors mistake fake risk for real risk and sell at the bottom.

Four real risks: regulatory access, cash burn, supply-side starvation, governance and delisting. Two fake risks: slowing growth, China ADR sentiment.

Real risk one: regulatory access (highest weight)

The statutory cap on fines under the EU product safety investigation is 6% of global annual revenue — about RMB 25.9 billion on 2025 revenue of RMB 431.8 billion.That equates to a quarter of quarterly profit.

The longer-term issue is the fixed cost of EUR 5 per category. This is not a fine but a permanent step-up in the cost base.A fine can be absorbed once; fixed costs must be absorbed every year.

Real risk two: cash burn

RMB 456.4 billion looks substantial, but a full shift by Temu to local warehousing, local procurement and local compliance could plausibly burn RMB 10–30 billion a year. Over three years that is RMB 30–90 billion, or 7%–20% of cash.

Key metric to watch: cash and short-term investments, quarter on quarter. Two consecutive quarters of sequential decline would signal that the "cash accumulation" phase is over and the valuation logic needs reworking.

Real risk three: supply-side shakeout (slow-burn, most lethal)

Once merchant gross margins are squeezed below 5%, factories have no money to improve quality, replace equipment or build brands. Low-price supply thins out first; only then does the price advantage disappear.

The signals to watch are merchant count and GMV per merchant. Neither is disclosed, so both can only be inferred from the scale of the "RMB 100 billion support" programme—the larger the subsidies, the greater the pressure on the supply side.

Real risk four: governance and delisting

VIE structure, super-voting rights, no segment disclosure. In an extreme scenario, delisting or forced divestiture would compress the valuation toward liquidation value. This risk cannot be eliminated through research, only through position sizing.

Two false risks: don't sell on them

Slowing growth is already in the price at 8.2x. It will move the stock, but it will not cause permanent capital loss—unless you are valuing it on a growth-stock framework.

China ADR sentiment is the quintessential fake risk. The 7.3% drawdown in September had nothing to do with the company; selling on it is trading beta as alpha. Real risk management targets permanent loss of capital, not net value volatility.

Under what circumstances would these risks erupt simultaneously?

The worst case is not a single blow-up but a resonance: new US regulations take effect + EU fines + rising delisting risk for Chinese ADRs, all three in the same quarter. The stock could then fall below USD 50.

But that resonance is precisely the buying point. RMB 456.4 billion in cash will not disappear just because three things happen at once, while the share price will price in the worst case. The premise is that you still have dry powder and your position is not full — which is why you scale in and cap it at 3%.

How should these risks be priced?

Discounting risk into the price: regulatory risk is priced as a one-off RMB 25.9 billion fine, or about RMB 18 per share (USD 2.5); cash-burn risk as RMB 60 billion burned over three years, or about RMB 42 per share (USD 6); and delisting risk, low probability but more than 50% impact, at a 3% probability translates to about USD 1.2 per share.

Together the three total about USD 10 per share, or 12.6% of the current price.The discount the market is applying (3.4x ex-cash vs 8–10x for peers) is far larger than this number—which shows the market is not pricing these three risks; it is pricing "no payout".

Conclusion

Where the risks land: three of the four real risks cannot be hedged, so exposure can only be capped through position limits; the two false risks should not trigger any action.

The metric to watch most closely is the quarter-on-quarter change in cash — it speaks to both "Temu burns cash" and "management changes its mind." Two consecutive quarters of declining cash and the story changes.

Risk management targets "permanent loss of principal," not "net asset value volatility." Confusing the two makes people sell at the bottom.
