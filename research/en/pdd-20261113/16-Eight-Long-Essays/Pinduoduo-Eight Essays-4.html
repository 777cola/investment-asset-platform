Core view

"3.4x ex-cash P/E" is the most eye-catching number in this report, and the easiest to misuse. It rests on three implicit assumptions, each of which needs a discount.

Three lies: cash is fully free, earnings can be extrapolated, and the decline is one-off. Strip out all three and 3.4x becomes 4x to 4.5x — still cheap, but less startling.

Myth one: cash is "free"

Of the RMB 456.4 billion, part is merchant funds pending settlement and security deposits—the platform collects first and pays later, creating float. This is legally a liability and does not belong to shareholders. Another portion sits in overseas subsidiaries and would incur tax on repatriation.

So the act of "deducting cash" effectively deducts, at face value, money that is not fully accessible.A conservative approach is to apply a 10% haircut.After the 10% haircut, the cash-deducted P/E moves from 3.4x to 3.9x.

Lie 2: Which year's profit goes in the denominator

The 3.4x uses the 2026 consensus of RMB 99.5 billion. Using TTM RMB 93.5 billion, it is 3.7x; using the 2027 bear case of RMB 70 billion, it is 4.9x; using the best case of RMB 107.3 billion in 2025, it is 3.2x.

The same "cash-adjusted P/E" can differ by 50% just by changing the denominator. That is why sell-side research can put both 3x and 5x on the same company — nobody miscalculated; they simply picked different years.

Lie 3: The decline is one-off

The company and other sell-side analysts attribute the profit decline to the "RMB 100 billion support" program and a "one-off fine." The fine is indeed one-off (EUR 200 million), but the rise in expense ratios is not: administrative expenses +53.3%, R&D +27.8%, and the sales expense ratio at 26.4% has not fallen.

These three items are structural and will not disappear just because there is no fine next year. So the claim that "profit will recover after excluding one-off items" is only half right—removing the fine helps somewhat, but margins will not return to 2025 levels.

After the break-up, is it still cheap?

Yes. Apply a 10% haircut to cash (RMB 410.8 billion), use RMB 99.5 billion as the denominator, and leave the multiple unadjusted: ex-cash P/E is 3.9x. Using neutral 2027 profit of RMB 90 billion, it is 4.5x.

At 4.5x, buying a business with 57% gross margin, zero debt and RMB 90–100 billion of annual profit is still cheap. Just not as dramatic as "3.4x." Cheap enough that precision is unnecessary, but not so cheap you can close your eyes.

Quality of cash flow: where did RMB 25.7 billion come from

Break it into three parts: first, depreciation and amortization added back within profit (RMB 27.18 billion); second, changes in working capital (merchant payables, deposits); third, the non-cash portion of equity incentives. Pinduoduo's working capital is negative year-round, which is the main reason cash flow exceeds profit.

This means cash flow quality does not depend on profit but on whether merchants are still willing to leave their money on the platform first. Once merchants start demanding faster settlement, cash flow will deteriorate immediately—and that will certainly happen as competition intensifies.

What if only half the cash is real?

Extreme assumption: only half of RMB 456.4 billion is genuinely deployable (the rest is float, overseas cash, restricted funds). That puts ex-cash market cap at 7,980−2,282=RMB 569.8 billion, or 5.7x the RMB 99.5 billion profit.

5.7x for a 57%-gross-margin, zero-debt business is still cheap, but not the dramatic "70% off" some claim.The point of this test is not to arrive at a more precise number but to remind you: the margin of safety on this investment depends on the true deployable share of cash, which the company does not disclose.

Conclusion

Financial anchor: discount the RMB 456.4 billion; adjust the 3.4x to around 4x — still cheap.

The real question is not the multiple, but whether this cash gets burned. If Temu eats a third of the cash, the adjusted 4x becomes 6x, and "cheap" no longer holds.

So watch just one number each quarter: cash and short-term investments, quarter on quarter. It says more about what is happening at this company than profit does.
