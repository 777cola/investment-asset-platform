Core view

The previous seven pieces covered "what it is"; this one covers "how to do it". The decision framework has only three things: how much to buy, at what price, and when to admit you are wrong. Everything else is noise.

Answer: a 3% position, first tranche at USD 78–80, halve at USD 120, exit when the thesis breaks.

First: how much to buy

3%. This number is not derived from valuation; it is derived from the "source of return": without dividends or buybacks, returns can only come from valuation repair; the timing of valuation repair is not yours to decide; and the tail risk of China ADRs cannot be hedged. Add the three together, and 3% is the ceiling.

If it starts buybacks one day, that ceiling can rise to 5%; if advertising turns negative, it drops straight to 0. Position sizing follows the thesis, not the share price.

The second point: at what price

Buy: USD 78–80. 3.4x net of cash; the bear case of USD 66 implies only 17% downside, a 3.6:1 payoff.

Sell: above USD 120. 7.6x net of cash; payoff falls below 1.5:1. Note this is not a target price but a trim line. The base-case target is USD 99, but at USD 99 you should not sell—by then the thesis may already have played out (buybacks, advertising recovery), warranting continued holding.

The third point: when to admit you are wrong

No price stop-loss. A stock at 3.4x ex-cash that falls 20% becomes 2.7x — add, don't cut. Set stops on the thesis:

  • Online marketing revenue posts two consecutive quarters of year-on-year decline — the domestic business is genuinely shrinking.
  • Transaction services revenue growth drops below 5% — Temu's unit economics are broken.
  • Cash and short-term investments fall more than 10% year on year — the company starts burning its reserves.
  • Auditor change or a qualified opinion — governance problems.

If any one of these appears, exit regardless of price. That is not a stop-loss; it is admitting the call was wrong.

The fourth point: how often to check

Review once a quarter, and look at only four lines: revenue mix (advertising vs transaction services), gross margin, cash balance, operating cash flow. Ignore everything else (share price, ratings, news).

The frequency is deliberate. Look too often and noise takes over (the 7.3% in September was noise); look too rarely and you miss the inflection (a turn to negative advertising growth takes two quarters to confirm). Quarterly frequency matches this company's information cadence.

A common mistake: panicking at USD 66

Assume advertising turns negative in Q1 next year and the stock falls to USD 66 (bear case). Most people react by selling: "the thesis is broken." But under this framework, USD 66 implies 2.7x ex-cash, which is a level to add, provided cash has not shrunk.

The source of panic is not the price; it is the absence of rules written in advance. If you had spelled out at USD 79 "under what conditions I add, under what conditions I exit," then USD 66 is just an execution point, not an emotional one.

When does this framework break down?

1. If Pinduoduo becomes a company that "shares the money" — buybacks plus dividends — then the valuation framework must shift from "asset discount" to "discounted cash flow," the target price rises markedly, and the 3% position cap should be lifted.

2. If Temu lists separately or is spun off — sum-of-the-parts replaces consolidated valuation, Temu's value gets priced on its own, and the current "zero valuation" treatment no longer applies.

3. If regulators shut the cross-border channel entirely — Temu goes to zero, the company reverts to a pure domestic cash cow, and the valuation should be about 6x, corresponding to roughly USD 90. All three scenarios would force a rewrite of this framework, but none of them would lose you money.

Conclusion

Decision points: 3% position, first tranche at 78–80, halve at 120, exit if any of the four logic lines breaks, review only four lines each quarter.

The value of these rules is not precision — it is that you don't have to re-decide when the stock falls 30%, because the decision was written down in advance.

Final line: buy a high-gross-margin business at a 70% discount, betting management won't burn the cash overseas. Right bet returns 60%; wrong bet loses 20%.
