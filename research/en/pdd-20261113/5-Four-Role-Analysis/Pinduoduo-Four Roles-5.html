Core view

All four reports agree the stock is genuinely cheap, and that the reasons for the cheapness are just as real. Three frame it as "repricing, not indiscriminate selling"; one calls it "best-positioned, but the hit is irreversible."

Three disagreements, each adjudicated: value Temu at zero (a free option), score management's ability and attitude separately, build one-third of the position first and wait for the Q3 results. Bottom line: Buy, 3% position cap, scale in.

Four perspectives on one table

All four reach broadly the same conclusion, diverging only on how much position to give it. The business analyst assigns an 8/10 business score but sees the ceiling as reached; the financial analyst calculates a 3.4x ex-cash P/E and a 29% ex-cash shareholder yield, while grading capital allocation as failing; the industry researcher calls the position not bad, but views regulation as an irreversible legislative shock; the risk assessor lists four tail risks and considers the discount broadly reasonable.

What the four reports share is one sentence: the cheapness is real, and so are the reasons for it.None calls this a mispricing; all four call it the pricing.

Disagreement one: what is Temu actually worth

Business view: Temu is the only growth engine. Transaction services rose 13%, holding total revenue growth at 8%, so Temu cannot simply be treated as a negative asset. Financial view: Temu is not disclosed as a separate segment and cannot be valued, so it can only be carried at zero, leaving RMB 456.4 billion in cash as the only certain asset.

Verdict: "zero valuation, negative option." Uncertainty is too high: localisation investment, compliance fines and tariff costs cannot be modelled. If Temu succeeds, it is a free option; if it fails, it will not damage my valuation floor. The conservative valuation here is not caution; it is the only feasible method.

Disagreement two: how far should management be marked down

Business perspective: Chen Lei built Temu in five years with first-class execution; the absence of a dividend is no reason to dismiss his ability. Risk perspective: governance is the source of tail risk — the VIX structure, super-voting rights and non-disclosure all warrant heavy penalties.

Verdict: ability 8, attitude 3 — score the two separately, do not average. Ability determines whether the business can be defended; attitude determines whether you earn a return. The former is proven; the latter has not been proven in five years. The conclusion takes the form of a 3% position cap, not of not buying.

Disagreement 3: buy now or wait for earnings

Financial view: at 3.4x the stock is already cheap enough; waiting is a bet on timing. Risk view: Q3 results land in late November and transaction services growth could slow to single digits, so waiting two weeks removes one landmine.

Verdict: build one-third first, decide after earnings. Valuation already offers sufficient margin, but Q3 is the key window to validate Temu's unit economics; keeping room to add is better scaling discipline than buying to full size at once.

My final judgment

Buy, but size the position: cap any single name at 3% and build over three to four quarters. This is not a "bullish on Chinese e-commerce" call; it is a call to own a high-gross-margin business at a 70% discount while accepting the cash may never come back.

Three triggers, stated plainly. Add: main-site advertising growth returns above 6%, or the company announces a meaningful buyback. Trim: share price above USD 120 (more than 7x ex-cash). Exit: advertising revenue turns negative YoY and transaction services growth falls below 5%.

Conclusion

Final call: Buy, 3% position, build over three to four quarters.

Add on ad growth back to 6% or a buyback announcement; trim above USD 120; exit if ads turn negative and transaction services growth falls below 5%.

The thesis is essentially buying an undervalued asset package and betting management will not burn it down.
