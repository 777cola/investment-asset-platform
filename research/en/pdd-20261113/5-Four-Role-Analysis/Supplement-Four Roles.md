## Four perspectives on one table

All four reach broadly the same conclusion and diverge only on position sizing. The business analyst assigns an 8/10 business score but sees the ceiling as reached; the financial analyst calculates 3.4x ex-cash P/E and a 29% ex-cash shareholder yield, yet judges capital allocation a failure; the industry researcher calls the position not bad but views regulation as an irreversible legislative shock; the risk assessor lists four tail risks and considers the discount broadly reasonable.

What the four reports share is one sentence: the cheapness is real, and so are the reasons for it.None calls it a mispricing; all four call it the pricing.

## Disagreement one: what is Temu actually worth

Business perspective: Temu is the only growth engine. Transaction services grew 13%, holding total revenue growth at 8%, so Temu cannot simply be treated as a negative asset. Financial perspective: Temu is not disclosed as a separate segment and cannot be valued, so it can only be carried at zero, leaving RMB 456.4 billion in cash as the only certain asset.

Verdict: treat it as "zero valuation, negative option." Uncertainty is simply too high—localisation investment, regulatory fines and tariff costs cannot be modelled. If Temu succeeds, it is a free option; if it fails, it will not damage my valuation floor. Conservative valuation here is not caution; it is the only feasible method.

## Disagreement two: how far should management be marked down

Business perspective: Chen Lei built Temu in five years with first-class execution, and his ability should not be dismissed over a refusal to pay dividends. Risk perspective: governance is the source of tail risk — the VIE structure, super-voting rights and non-disclosure all warrant heavy penalties.

Verdict: ability 8, attitude 3, scored separately rather than averaged. Ability determines whether the business can be defended; attitude determines whether you earn a return. The former is proven; the latter is not, five years in. The conclusion takes the form of a 3% position cap, not a decision not to buy.

## Disagreement 3: Buy now or wait for earnings

Financial view: at 3.4x the valuation is cheap enough; waiting is a bet on timing. Risk view: Q3 results land in late November, transaction services growth could slow to single digits, and waiting two weeks removes one landmine.

Verdict: build one-third first, decide after earnings. Valuation already offers sufficient margin, but Q3 is the key window to validate Temu's unit economics; keeping room to add fits the discipline of scaling in better than buying in full at once.

## My final judgment

Buy, control position size, cap any single name at 3%, build over three to four quarters. This is not a "bullish on Chinese e-commerce" call. It is a call to buy a high-gross-margin business at a 70% discount while accepting that the cash may never come back.

Three triggers, stated plainly: add — main-site advertising growth returns above 6%, or the company announces a meaningful buyback; trim — the stock reaches USD 120 or above (more than 7x ex-cash); exit — advertising revenue turns negative YoY and transaction services growth falls below 5%.
