Industry research: the third-place player in a mature market, hit by a legislative shock — Pinduoduo

Core view

China e-commerce is now a mature market (growth of 7%–10%), with share split Alibaba 45%, JD.com 20%, Pinduoduo 15%, Douyin 10%. Pinduoduo ranks third: larger players ahead, faster-growing ones behind.

Key call: US and EU tariffs are legislation, not a cycle, and are irreversible. Temu's localization is a change of business model, not riding out a cycle.

Industry structure: a stock market split four ways

China's online retail sales reached about RMB 15 trillion in 2025, with penetration at roughly 45%, and growth has fallen from above 20% to 7%–10%. This is a classic stock market: one player's extra bite comes out of another's plate.

Market share is roughly Alibaba 45%, JD.com 20%, Pinduoduo 15%, Douyin 10%. Pinduoduo ranks third: the two ahead are larger, the one behind is faster. That position leaves narrow strategic options: extract efficiency upstream, or expand outward. It has done both.

Invert: when would this business turn bad?

Munger's habit was to ask first "how could it die", not "how could it win". Pinduoduo has three ways to die:

  • Supply side withers: merchant gross margins are squeezed to the limit, they exit in bulk, and low-price supply thins out.
  • Demand side shifts: user habits move from "search and compare prices" to "order from recommendations", and Douyin catches this migration.
  • Access closes: Europe and the US raise the cost of cross-border small parcels to unprofitable levels, and Temu retreats to the domestic market.

Of the three ways to die, the first is the slowest but most fatal, the third the fastest and most likely.

What is different this time: regulation is not a cycle

Many treat tariffs as a cyclical headwind and wait for it to pass. This is not a cycle; it is legislation. The US de minimis exemption formally ended in August 2025, the EU exemption was formally abolished on July 1, 2026, and a handling fee was added on November 1. These statutes will not reverse with the economy.

Legislative shocks are irreversible: costs shift permanently higher and business models must be rewritten. Temu's response is local warehousing plus semi-managed operations, essentially converting "cross-border arbitrage" into "local retail." This is switching to a different business, not riding out a cycle.

Is there a better position within the industry?

Within the same business, Pinduoduo is actually well positioned: highest gross margin, lightest asset base, most cash. Alibaba has to fund cloud and instant retail, JD.com has to fund logistics and hundreds of thousands of employees; only Pinduoduo can stand aside.

Munger would concede the point, then add: a good position does not mean a good price. Its position is worth 8 to 12x earnings; at 8.2x now, it already sits at the low end.

All three together: where the positioning gap lies

Alibaba's June 2026 quarter revenue was RMB 268.953 billion, +9%, yet operating profit fell 57% YoY — it is paying for AI cloud and instant retail. JD.com's Q2 2026 revenue was RMB 346.4 billion, −2.9%, with a net margin of 2.0%; it earns hard money. Pinduoduo's revenue was RMB 112.36 billion, +8%, with an operating margin of 24.7%.

All three grew around +9%: one is bleeding to buy the future, one defends scale on thin margins, one quietly collects rent. Pinduoduo does hold the best position in the industry, but it is also the only one betting growth offshore.

Conclusion

Sector view: well positioned, but the hit is real.

With the highest gross margin, the lightest asset base and the most cash, its position is among the best in the industry. But a regulatory rewrite of the cost structure does not spare a company just because it is excellent.

Reasonable sector positioning: a mature platform at 8–12x P/E. It now trades at 8.2x, at the low end.
