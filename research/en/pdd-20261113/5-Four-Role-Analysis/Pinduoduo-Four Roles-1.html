Business analysis: what exactly makes this business good — Pinduoduo

Core view

In three sentences: aggregate white-label supply, pull in users with low prices, monetise through advertising commissions. No inventory risk, so gross margin is 57% and net margin 24% — an order of magnitude ahead of peers.

A good business, but it has hit the ceiling: advertising +3.5% is the read. Frame it as a mature platform, not a growth platform.

Business model: a toll-collecting business explained in three sentences

PDD does not manufacture, hold inventory, build warehouses or deliver parcels. It does three things: aggregate goods from white-label factories, pull users in on low prices, and charge merchants advertising fees and commissions. Of its RMB 112.36 billion revenue in 2026Q2, advertising contributed RMB 57.6 billion and transaction services RMB 54.7 billion — not a single cent came from product markups.

The core of this model is "not bearing inventory risk." JD.com sells RMB 346.4 billion of goods — its own goods, its own warehouses, its own staff — and earns a 2% net margin; PDD, on the same scale of gross merchandise volume, earns a 24% net margin. The difference is not effort, but who bears the risk.

User value: it genuinely lets poor people buy cheap goods

To judge whether a business is good, first look at whose problem it solves. Pinduoduo solves the problem of "someone earning RMB 2,000 a month who wants to buy a RMB 10 mop." Taobao is unwilling to do this seriously (brand-premium conflict), JD cannot do it (self-operated costs too high), and only Pinduoduo does.

This is real social value, and the foundation of the moat. Its 800 million-plus users were not lured in by subsidies; they stayed for the prices. Users who come back after subsidies end are the real users — the 2026 sales expense ratio of 26.4% did not decline at all, showing that the company itself knows this has not yet fully held true.

Unit economics: how the money from an order is split

Back-of-envelope: on an order with a ticket size of RMB 40, the platform takes roughly 5% commission from the merchant (RMB 2) and merchant ad spend allocated per order runs about RMB 6, implying platform revenue per order of about RMB 8. Fulfillment sits with the merchant, so the platform's marginal cost is limited to servers and the payment channel — under RMB 1.That leaves gross profit per order above RMB 7, and it is the source of the 57% gross margin.

Temu's per-order economics are entirely different: under the fully managed model the platform absorbs line-haul, customs clearance and last-mile itself, so fulfillment cost per order far exceeds commission revenue. Costs return to the merchant only under the semi-managed or local-warehousing model.Which is why "Temu growing" and "Temu making money" are two different things.

Where is the ceiling for this business?

Domestic users and time spent have peaked, and +3.5% ad revenue growth is the ceiling. That leaves only three ways up: lift the monetisation rate from 5% toward 7% (which would drive merchants away), raise average order value (which conflicts with the "low price" perception), or go overseas (where tariffs are biting). None of the three is easy.

The fair framing, then, is a "high-margin mature platform," not a "growth platform." That is no bad thing—a mature platform warrants 8 to 12 times, and the stock now trades at 8.2 times, or 3.4 times ex-cash.

Versus Moutai and Apple: where it wins, where it falls short

Business quality: Apple's gross margin is 46%, Moutai's 91% and Pinduoduo's 57%, placing it between the two. But Apple and Moutai set their own prices, whereas Pinduoduo's prices are set by merchants and voted on by users. Pricing power is not in its own hands — the biggest difference between it and a truly good business.

Capital allocation: Apple has bought back hundreds of billions of dollars over a decade, Moutai pays dividends every year, Pinduoduo pays zero. Same good business, yet the others return profit to shareholders while Pinduoduo parks it in the bank. The gap in the business is small; the gap in returns is large.

Conclusion

Business judgment: 8/10 — the business has hit its ceiling

Business quality is impeccable: asset-light, high gross margin, zero interest-bearing debt, and cash flow above reported profit. But PDD is no longer a growth story — domestic users have peaked, advertising growth is running at 3.5%, and overseas expansion remains dogged by tariffs.

The case to buy rests on "cheap," not "growth."
