Financial analysis: how cheap is it, and why is it cheap — Pinduoduo

Buffett lens | Profit quality · Asset structure · Shareholder earnings · Capital allocation
Research date 2026/11/13  ·  Price $78.97 (+1.86%)  ·  Market cap About USD 112.4 billion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 8.2x
← Back to the hub

Core view

The profit is real money: operating cash flow/net profit 0.95, positive in all four quarters. The balance sheet is exceptionally clean: RMB 456.4 billion in cash, zero interest-bearing debt, cash at 57% of market cap. Shareholder earnings roughly RMB 100 billion a year; ex-cash yield 29%.

But capital allocation fails: five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, with the earnings turned into deposits. That is exactly why it is cheap.

Start with earnings quality: is the profit real cash or paper profit?

2026Q2 net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 27.18 billion, operating cash flow RMB 25.7 billion, a ratio of 0.95. That ratio is below 1, but the gap is small, and Pinduoduo's working capital is negative — merchants pay first and the platform settles later, so cash flow should in theory be better than profit.

Looking at the four quarters together makes it clearer: full-year 2025 Non-GAAP was RMB 107.3 billion, full-year operating cash flow was positive and above net profit, and 2026H1 Non-GAAP was RMB 42.59 billion. This company does not have the problem of "good profit, ugly cash."

Look at assets again: RMB 456.4 billion in cash and zero debt

Cash and short-term investments RMB 456.4 billion, no bank borrowings, no bonds, no interest-bearing debt. Market cap RMB 798 billion, cash at 57%. Strip out cash, and the business sells for RMB 341.6 billion.

This is one of the most extreme balance-sheet weightings in the entire market. Buffett would immediately ask two questions: can this money be moved? Will this money be returned to shareholders? The answer to the first is "mostly yes," and to the second, "five years, and no."

Rough calculation of owner earnings

Shareholder earnings = net income + depreciation and amortization − maintenance capital expenditure. Pinduoduo has very few fixed assets, so maintenance capex is close to zero and depreciation and amortization are small. A rough calculation puts shareholder earnings at roughly Non-GAAP net income, about RMB 100 billion per year.

That implies a shareholder earnings yield of 12.5% on a market cap of RMB 798 billion; after deducting cash, a market cap of RMB 341.6 billion against RMB 100 billion gives a yield of 29%. That number is cheap in any market.

Capital allocation: the sole negative

The money earned went to three places: cash (RMB 456.4 billion), merchant subsidies (the RMB 100 billion support program), and Temu investment. The first two generate no return; the third has an unknown return. Five years with zero dividends and zero buybacks — not even a token buyback.

Buffett's test is straightforward: a dollar retained in the company must create more than a dollar of market value. The value created by the money Pinduoduo retains is "deposit interest." By that standard, its capital allocation fails.

Conclusion

Financial view: so cheap it needs no valuation work — but it is cheap for a reason.

At 3.4x ex-cash P/E and a 29% ex-cash shareholder earnings yield, the price already discounts a very bad future. The only question: good assets do not equal good returns. If management never distributes, you are relying on someone else discovering the value.

The premise for buying is accepting the wait, not accepting that it must go up.
