## Three-month picture: down 9%, mostly in two stretches

USD 86.94 on August 17, USD 78.97 on November 13, a 9.2% decline over three months. The path was not a one-way fall: it spiked to USD 90.2 on August 19 (the three-month high), plunged to USD 75.38 on October 2 (the low, down 16% from the high), then rebounded to around USD 79 in November. **The steepest drop came in September; the steadiest gains ran from mid-October to early November.**

## Attribution 1: The earnings report was not the cause of the decline, but the excuse for it

When earnings came out on August 24, revenue was RMB 112.36 billion, below the consensus of 3%, and Non-GAAP net profit was −13% — the negative was clear. Yet the stock fell only 1.5% that day and rose 0.8% the next. **This shows the market already knew the numbers would be ugly**; 2026Q1 net profit was already −15%, so another weak quarter was no surprise.

The real downgrade came after earnings: on August 29, Soochow cut its 2026 net profit forecast to RMB 99.5 billion, and on August 31 the stock fell −2.0%. **Sell-side downgrades hurt the stock more than the company's earnings**, because what they change is the anchor for the next four quarters.

## Attribution 2: the 7.3% in September had nothing to do with the company

From USD 84 on August 31 to USD 77.84 on September 10, down 7.3% over seven trading sessions, with no company-specific news in between. Over the same period Hang Seng Tech and China ADRs broadly fell as the market's focus shifted from the AI narrative to cash flow. **This leg is beta, not alpha.**

The test is simple: if comparable assets fell by a similar amount over the same period, attribute it to the market; only if it fell alone does it belong to the company. September's move was the former.

## Attribution 3: Tariff news broke twice, and the market reaction shrank each time

When CBP's proposed rule landed on October 8, bringing everything below USD 2,500 under strict scrutiny — a substantive negative — the stock only edged down on October 8 and actually rose 3.95% on October 9. **A textbook "bad news priced in"**: a policy trailed for six months finally issued, uncertainty gone, shorts covered.

The EU's proposed EUR 6 billion levy on November 7 sent the stock down 3.05% on November 9; the EU's announcement of a EUR 2 per-item handling fee on October 25 drew only a 1.2% decline on October 26. **Bad news in the same direction, with the market's marginal reaction fading** — evidence these costs are being written into the price.

## Which parts are noise, and which are worth remembering

**Noise:** The 7.3% drawdown in September, the 1.9% drop on November 12 (no Singles' Day sales report — there was never going to be one), the down day on October 21. None of these change any assumption.

**What matters:** ① Sell-side is cutting earnings estimates (the late-August round) — this is a trend, not a one-off; ② the market reaction to tariff news is fading, suggesting pricing is becoming fuller; ③ the stock has support in the USD 75–80 range — both probes into that range, on October 2 and November 12, were bought back.

**Next catalyst: Q3 results (late November).** This is the most important event of the next three months. The market expects revenue growth of around 6% and a double-digit year-on-year decline in Non-GAAP net income. I am watching one thing only: whether transaction services revenue growth holds at 10%.

## Why bad news no longer crushes the market: three technical reasons

**1. Valuation is already too low to leave room for shorting.** At 8.2x TTM and 3.4x ex-cash, shorts can only make real money by betting on a profit collapse, and the probability of that is blocked by RMB 456.4 billion in cash.

**2. Institutional ownership is already light.** Institutions hold about 28.66%, insiders about 32%, and the rest is retail and passive funds. **No crowded long means no stampede.**

**3. The marginal impact of bad news is fading.** Tariffs went from "rumor" to "proposed rule" to "formal fees," and the market priced in part of it each time; by the time the actual document lands, it turns into good news.
