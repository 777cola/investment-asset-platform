# Pinduoduo（PDD）— Industry Chain Supplement

- Research date：2026/11/13
- Price：78.97 USD
- Market cap：About USD 112.4 billion

## What the chain looks like: three layers, with the platform at the very center

Upstream is supply: white-label factories, industrial belts, agricultural production bases, brand owners. Midstream is platforms: Pinduoduo, Alibaba, JD.com, Douyin E-commerce, plus payments, logistics and third-party operations. Downstream is two groups: China's 800 million-plus price-sensitive users, and Temu's overseas users.

Value distribution along this chain is extremely uneven. For a good with a factory-gate price of RMB 10, the factory earns RMB 0.5 to 1.5, express delivery earns RMB 1.5, the platform earns RMB 2 to 3 (advertising plus commission), and the rest is traffic and channel cost. The platform is the only link that reliably makes big money — which is why Pinduoduo's gross margin is 57% while factories' gross margin is under 15%.

## How the value is split: both ends of the smile curve are occupied by others

The classic smile curve says both ends are high and the middle is low: one end is brand and R&D, the other is channel and users. Pinduoduo has secured the "channel and users" end, but has no brand end. This is its structural weakness—it can make factories cheap, but it cannot make factories valuable.

So it can only keep squeezing efficiency from the supply side: C2M, direct links to industrial belts, and refund-only policies forcing quality improvements. The ceiling of this playbook is clear: once factory gross margins are squeezed below 5%, no one is willing to improve quality for it. The end of the low-price moat is the ceiling on supply quality.

## Temu has stretched the chain by an entire layer

The domestic e-commerce chain runs "factory—platform—courier—consumer," four segments. Temu's fully managed model runs "factory—platform—freight forwarder—customs declaration—line haul—overseas customs clearance—local delivery—consumer," eight segments. Every extra segment adds a cost item and a regulatory checkpoint.

In 2026 this layer picked up two charges: a flat EUR 3 tariff per product category in the EU from July 1, plus a EUR 2 handling fee per category from November 1, for EUR 5 per category in total. For a product with an average order value of EUR 10, that is 50% in cost.Temu's response is semi-managed plus local warehouses: the US fully managed share has already been cut from 70% to below 30%, with the goal of 80% of US orders fulfilled from local warehouses by the end of 2026.

Localization solves tariffs but not another problem: it turns Temu from an asset-light platform into a quasi-retailer with inventory, warehousing and a compliance team. The valuation method has to shift from platform multiples to retailer multiples.

## A new shift: the platform is moving from asset-light to asset-heavy

The 2025 annual report mentions the formation of "Xinpinmu" to test own-brand products; Temu is building local warehouses in Europe and the US; Duoduo Maicai has its own front-warehouse system. All three point in the same direction: Pinduoduo is moving downstream into the supply side and the fulfillment side.

Moving downstream buys two things—control over costs and the initiative on compliance. The price is that capex and inventory risk shift from the factories onto the company itself. If this path works, its gross margin will be diluted but its moat will widen; if it fails, it will have given up the asset-light model without gaining market access.

## Cycle position: not a demand cycle, a rule cycle

China e-commerce growth has slowed from over 20% to 7%–10% (2025 online retail sales of about RMB 1.5 trillion, penetration about 45%). This is a stock market, and share is won by taking it. Pinduoduo holds about 15% of the domestic market, Alibaba about 45%, JD.com about 20% and Douyin about 10% — it ranks third, with two larger players ahead and one faster-growing player behind.

The cross-border segment is in the early stage of "rule repricing": the US de minimis exemption ended in August 2025, and the CBP proposed rule of October 2026 brings all shipments under USD 2,500 into strict supervision; the EU scrapped the EUR 150 duty-free threshold in July and added a handling fee in November; product safety investigations are still proceeding. This is not a demand downturn; the cost structure is being rewritten by legislation. Until that rewrite is complete, no one knows where the new equilibrium lies.

## If the chain reorders, who gets hurt first

First, white-label factories: once platform self-operation and local procurement start, orders will concentrate among the few factories able to meet standards, squeezing out smaller ones. Second, logistics providers like J&T that are deeply tied to Temu: local warehouses replace direct shipping, so cross-border small-parcel volumes will fall.

PDD itself has a buffer — it holds RMB 456.4 billion in cash, while other links in the chain have none. That is the difference between the chain leader and its subordinates: when the rules change, the leader can rearrange the chain; the subordinates can only be rearranged.
