## PDD fell 35% despite RMB 456.4 billion of net cash on its books — that is not cheap, that is mispricing

Bottom line first: PDD trades at USD 78.97, a market cap of USD 112.4 billion, with RMB 456.4 billion of cash and short-term investments on the books. Fifty-seven cents of every dollar you pay is cash.

The obvious reaction: strip out the cash and the business sells for just 3.4x earnings — free money, surely?

No. The market is not stupid. This is not a mispricing; it is the pricing.

## I. Three numbers to get straight first

The first number is 3.5% — Pinduoduo's YoY advertising revenue growth in 2026Q2. Advertising accounts for all of main-site monetisation, so 3.5% means the domestic business has stopped growing.

The second number is −13%, the YoY change in Non-GAAP net profit over the same period and the third consecutive quarterly decline.

The third number is RMB 456.4 billion — cash and short-term investments on the books, equal to 57% of market cap and RMB 34.1 billion more than at end-2025.

Put the three together and the picture is clear: the company is still making money, and a lot of it, but it is no longer growing; the cash pile keeps building, yet not a cent goes back to shareholders.

## II. Why It Is Cheap: The Market Has Done the Math, Not Failed to Understand It

Pinduoduo has paid no dividend in five years and has not run any sizable buyback. Asked on earnings calls how it intends to deploy its cash, management has consistently said it will "invest in the supply chain and ecosystem."

The problem: for all the talk of heavy investment, cash rose RMB 34.1 billion in a single year. Investment intensity is far lower than the narrative implies, and the company has the capacity to buy back stock but chooses not to.

For comparison, Alibaba is buying back stock and paying dividends, JD.com runs a regular buyback programme, and Tencent's annual buybacks of HKD 100 billion have become an anchor for its valuation. When everyone else's shares were cheap, they bought their own; Pinduoduo saved.

The discount the market assigns is therefore not a discount on the business—it is the price of the fact that this cash does not belong to shareholders. The cash sits on the balance sheet, not in your pocket.

## Three, the real rival is not Taobao, it is customs

In 2026, Pinduoduo's biggest rival files no quarterly earnings report.

The US ended the USD 800 de minimis exemption for small parcels in August 2025, and on October 8, 2026, Customs proposed a rule subjecting all low-value shipments under USD 2,500 to stricter declaration and bond requirements, with comments open until December 7.

The EU has gone further: it scrapped the EUR 150 de minimis on July 1, added a flat EUR 3 tariff per product category, then layered on a EUR 2 handling fee per category from November 1.

Do the math: a parcel worth EUR 10 carries EUR 5 in fixed import costs. Either raise prices 50% or absorb it. Raise prices and lose customers; absorb it and lose margin.

That is why Temu's transaction services revenue growth fell from high double digits last year to 13%. Demand has not disappeared; unit economics have been cut.

## IV. So should you buy it or not

Buy, but only a little.

The bull case is solid: 3.4x ex-cash P/E, 57.3% gross margin, zero interest-bearing debt, RMB 25.7 billion of operating cash flow in a single quarter, and the stock 35.6% below its USD 122.66 high. Across three scenarios, the bear case is USD 66, the base case USD 99 and the bull case USD 128 — a 3.6-to-1 payoff.

The case against a heavy position is equally solid: five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, with management offering no explanation of how it uses its cash; advertising growth of 3.5%, with the domestic market already at its ceiling; and unlimited compliance exposure at Temu, where an adverse outcome in the EU product-safety investigation could bring a fine of up to 6% of global annual revenue.

My approach, then: buy, cap the position at 3%, and build it over three to four quarters. Treat "the re-rating never comes" as the base case, not a surprise.

## For example

A friend of yours in a tier-2 city owns a building that collects RMB 10 million in rent a year, and the building also holds over RMB 40 million in cash. Now he says: this building plus the cash, I'll sell it to you for RMB 30 million.

Would you buy it on the spot?

First ask three questions: Is the cash real? Will he move the money out? Can the rent still bring in RMB 10 million next year?

Pinduoduo is in exactly this position now. The building is real, the rent is real, the cash is real. The only answer is: it has not paid shareholders a single cent in five years, nor said how it plans to use the money.

So the right bid is: buy, but only a small stake, and hold back the rest until it speaks up on its own.

## One last point.

This is not a bet on Pinduoduo reaccelerating growth. It is buying a high-gross-margin toll-road business at a 70% discount, while betting management will not burn RMB 456.4 billion overseas.

Win the bet and a re-rating pays you 60%. Lose it and the cash cushion limits your loss to 20%.

But remember one thing: never use net cash as a reason to buy. Cash sits on the balance sheet, not in your pocket. It can only cap your downside; it cannot earn you a return.

Returns can come from only one thing — the company finally starts returning money to shareholders. Only then has the story truly changed.
