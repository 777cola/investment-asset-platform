## PDD fell 35%, with RMB 456.4 billion sitting on its books: this is not cheap, this is pricing

Conclusion first: PDD trades at USD 78.97, a market cap of USD 112.4 billion, with cash and short-term investments of RMB 456.4 billion on the books. Of every dollar you pay, fifty-seven cents is cash.

Many people get excited here: strip out the cash and the business sells for just 3.4x earnings — isn't that free money?

No. The market is not stupid. This price is not a mispricing; it is the pricing.

## I. Three numbers to get straight first

The first number is 3.5%. That is Pinduoduo's year-on-year advertising revenue growth in the second quarter of 2026. Advertising is the entirety of main-site monetization, and 3.5% means the domestic business has stopped growing.

The second number is −13%. That is the year-on-year change in Non-GAAP net profit over the same period, the third consecutive quarterly decline.

The third number is RMB 456.4 billion. That is cash and short-term investments on the books, 57% of market cap and RMB 34.1 billion more than at the end of 2025.

Put the three numbers together and the picture is clear: the company is still making money, and a lot of it, but it is no longer growing; the cash pile keeps building, yet not a cent goes back to shareholders.

## II. Why It Is Cheap: Not That the Market Cannot Understand It, but That the Market Has Done the Math Clearly

Pinduoduo has paid no dividend for five years and has not conducted any sizable buyback. When asked on earnings calls how it plans to use its cash, management has consistently answered: "invest in the supply chain and ecosystem."

The problem is that while it talks up heavy investment, its cash grew by RMB 34.1 billion in a single year. That shows the intensity of investment is far lower than the narrative suggests, and that it has the capacity to buy back stock but chooses not to.

For comparison: Alibaba is buying back stock and paying dividends, JD.com has a regular buyback program, and Tencent's annual buybacks of HKD 100 billion have become an anchor for its valuation. When everyone else's shares were cheap, they bought their own; Pinduoduo saved.

So the discount the market assigns is not a discount on the business—it is a price on the fact that this cash does not belong to shareholders. The cash is on the balance sheet, not in your pocket.

## Three, the real rival is not Taobao, it is customs

In 2026, Pinduoduo's biggest rival has no quarterly earnings report.

The US ended the duty exemption for small parcels under USD 800 in August 2025, and on October 8, 2026, Customs issued a proposed rule to subject all low-value shipments under USD 2,500 to stricter declaration and bond requirements, with comments open until December 7.

The EU is more direct: it scrapped the EUR 150 de minimis on July 1, adding a flat EUR 3 tariff per product category, then added a EUR 2 handling fee per category on November 1.

Do the math: a parcel worth EUR 10 carries EUR 5 in fixed import costs. Either raise prices 50% or absorb it. Raise prices and lose customers; absorb it and lose margin.

That is why Temu's transaction services revenue growth fell from high double digits last year to 13%. Demand has not disappeared; unit economics have been cut.

## IV. So should you buy it or not

Buy, but only a little.

The case for buying is solid: 3.4x ex-cash P/E, 57.3% gross margin, zero interest-bearing debt, RMB 25.7 billion of operating cash flow in a single quarter, and the stock 35.6% off its USD 122.66 high. Across three scenarios, the bear case is USD 66, the base case USD 99 and the bull case USD 128 — a 3.6-to-1 payoff.

The case against a heavy position is equally solid: five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, with management offering no explanation of how it uses its cash; advertising growth of 3.5%, with the domestic market already at its ceiling; and unlimited compliance exposure at Temu, where an adverse outcome in the EU product-safety investigation could bring a fine of up to 6% of global annual revenue.

So my approach: buy, cap the position at 3%, and build it over three to four quarters. Treat "the re-rating never comes" as the base case, not a surprise.

## For example

You have a friend in a tier-2 city who owns a building that collects RMB 10 million in rent a year, and the building also holds over RMB 40 million in cash. Now he says: this building plus the cash, I'll sell it to you for RMB 30 million.

Would you buy it on the spot?

First ask three questions: Is the cash real? Will he move the money out? Can the rent still bring in RMB 10 million next year?

Pinduoduo is in exactly this position now. The building is real, the rent is real, the cash is real. The only answer is: it has not paid shareholders a single cent in five years, nor said how it plans to use the money.

So the right bid is: buy, but only a small stake, and hold back the rest until it speaks up on its own.

## One last point.

This investment is not a bet that Pinduoduo reaccelerates growth. It is buying a high-gross-margin toll-road business at a 70% discount, while betting management will not burn RMB 456.4 billion overseas.

Win the bet, and a re-rating pays you 60%. Lose it, and the cash cushion limits your loss to 20%.

But remember one thing: never use net cash as a reason to buy. Cash sits on the balance sheet, not in your pocket. It can only cap your downside; it cannot earn you a return.

Returns can come from only one thing — the company finally starts returning money to shareholders. Only then has the story truly changed.
