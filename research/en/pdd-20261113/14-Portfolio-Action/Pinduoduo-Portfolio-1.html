Core view: a small position with acceptable risk-reward, not a core holding

So 3% is the cap, built in four tranches: 1% now, 1% after Q3 earnings, 1% on buybacks or ad revenue returning to 6%. Halve at USD 120; add below USD 72 only if cash is not drawn down. Set stops on the thesis, not on price.

Why 3%, not 8%

On odds of 62% upside, 17% downside (3.6:1), Kelly would imply a sizeable position — roughly f=(0.62×3.6−0.38)/3.6≈0.51, or half the book. That number is clearly unusable: Kelly assumes known probabilities, and these are my own estimates.

Three factors actually drive position size. First, returns can only come from valuation repair, with no dividend or buyback floor. Second, tail risks cannot be hedged (regulation, geopolitics, delisting). Third, China ADR correlation is too high — a 3% Pinduoduo position adds more than 3% of actual China exposure to the portfolio. Together these make 3% a ceiling, not a starting point.

Earnings, not price, should drive decisions: the fourth batch's rhythm

Build the first 1% tranche now: the stock trades at 3.4x ex-cash, and the cash cushion limits downside. Hold the second tranche until the Q3 report (late November) confirms transaction services growth holds at 10%. Hold the third until a catalyst emerges — a buyback, or advertising growth back at 6%.

The key to this pacing is that each tranche is a different bet.The first buys "cheap"; the second buys "Temu hasn't collapsed"; the third buys "the valuation starts to repair." Three slugs of capital, three distinct theses — if any one breaks, stop.

Price triggers: trim above 120, add below 72

Trim line at USD 120, equivalent to 7x ex-cash P/E. By then risk-reward has already fallen from 3.6:1 to below 1.5:1, so holding on offers less value than switching into cash.

Add line at USD 72, equivalent to 3.0x ex-cash — but only if cash has not shrunk. If the stock falls to USD 72 while cash drops 10% YoY, that is not cheapness but someone burning money: cut rather than add.

A stop-loss does not apply here: price alone cannot trigger an exit from this position

Price-based stop-losses get you out at the worst possible moment. A stock on 3.4x ex-cash that falls 20% is on 2.7x — that is when you add, not cut. Set stops on the logic instead: two consecutive quarters of negative advertising growth, transaction services growth dropping below 5%, cash down 10% YoY, audit issues.

Position discipline is the only exception. If the stock rises above 5% of the portfolio (because it went up), trim unconditionally back to 3%. That is not a bearish call; it caps the risk budget for a single name.

Correlation: it diversifies less than you think

PDD's correlation with the Hang Seng Tech Index is about 0.75, with Alibaba about 0.70 and with JD.com about 0.60. In a portfolio it is essentially a synthetic exposure to "China internet e-commerce + China exports."

If the portfolio already holds Alibaba, Tencent and Meituan, a further 3% in PDD would markedly raise actual China internet concentration. The right move is not to skip the position but to cut the other China internet holdings at the same time.

Only cash and short-duration bonds can truly hedge it. Even gold does not necessarily work in extreme markets — during those few days in mid-November, all risk assets fell together.

Conclusion

Conclusion: 3% cap, built in four tranches (1% now, 1% after Q3, 1% on a catalyst); halve the position at USD 120 and add only below USD 72, provided cash has not declined.

No price stop. The stop is logic-based: two consecutive quarters of negative advertising growth, transaction services growth <5%, cash down 10% YoY, or audit issues. If any one appears, exit regardless of price.

This position cannot change the portfolio's fate; it is a small position with favourable odds. Treat it as an option to buy a cash cow at a 70% discount, not as a core holding.
