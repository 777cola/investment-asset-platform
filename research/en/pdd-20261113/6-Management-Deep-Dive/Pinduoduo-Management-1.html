Core thesis: good at business, bad at sharing the spoils

Huang Zheng, Chen Lei and Zhao Jiazhen run on one logic: technical backgrounds, fast decisions, almost no M&A, no accounting fraud, clean successions. These are real strengths. But five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, RMB 456.4 billion of cash with no stated use, and no segment disclosure for Temu — competence is not the issue; attitude toward shareholders is.

Score 4/10. This does not veto a buy, but it caps the position at 3%.

Three people, one logic

Pinduoduo's decision-making structure is simpler than at most China concept stocks: Huang Zheng sets direction, Chen Lei runs execution and Zhao Jiazhen runs supply. All three come from engineering or operations backgrounds; there are no professional managers and no parachuted-in CFO fronting the company to investors. The strength of this structure is fast decisions; the weakness is the absence of checks and balances.

When Huang Zheng stepped down as CEO in 2020 and as chairman in 2021, he donated 2.37% of shares to the Fanxing Charity Fund and transferred a further 3.77% to the partner collective, cutting his personal stake from 43.3% to around 29%. That is rare among Chinese internet founders — most sell down at share-price highs; he gave shares away. It earns him credit on character but does not change his absolute say over capital allocation.

Capital allocation: full marks for M&A discipline, zero for shareholder returns

PDD has made almost no acquisitions. In the eight years since listing it has not done a single USD billion-scale deal, and carries no goodwill impairment landmines. Set against Alibaba and Tencent buying their way forward, that restraint is a scarce quality—it means management does not need to "get bigger" to prove itself.

The flip side is cash piling up. At end-June 2026 it stood at RMB 456.4 billion, up RMB 34.1 billion from end-2025, even as the company spent heavily on its "RMB 100 billion support" program and kept investing in Temu localization.Heavy investment alongside a rising net cash balance means investment intensity is far smaller than the narrative suggests.

The real criticism is here: the stock fell from USD 122.66 to USD 78.97, an ex-cash P/E of 3.4x, and not one meaningful buyback at this level. Management says it "believes in long-term value," but the best expression of long-term value is buying your own stock when it is cheap.Not doing so admits only two explanations: either they don't think it's cheap, or they don't care about the share price.

Integrity and governance: no fraud, but a string of compliance bills

First, the good news: eight years since listing, no allegations of financial fraud, no SEC investigation, no qualified auditor's opinion. Among China concept stocks, that alone filters out most of the field. Colin Huang's share donation on stepping down and Chen Lei's smooth succession show a clean transfer of power.

Now the bill: in 2021, an employee's sudden death triggered a public backlash; in 2022–2023, the app was removed from Google Play over security vulnerabilities; from 2024, the EU opened a DSA investigation and in July 2026 imposed a EUR 200 million fine; as of October 2026, the EU product safety investigation is still ongoing, with a worst-case fine of 6% of global annual revenue. These are not financial problems; they are license-to-operate problems, and the ceiling on license-to-operate problems is far higher than on financial ones.

Compare what the other two companies' management teams are doing across the board

Alibaba kept buying back stock and paying dividends through fiscal 2026, JD.com has a standing buyback, and Tencent's annual HKD 100 billion-scale buyback has become the valuation anchor. All three are deploying cash to buy their own shares; only Pinduoduo is saving.

This is not an industry-norm issue; it is a choice. Same China ADR regulatory environment, same share-price discount: peers chose to buy when it was cheap, while it chose to hold the cash. For shareholders, the cost of that choice accrues every year.

Chen Lei's first five years as successor: the organisation is already his

Whether a company can survive without its founder comes down to two things: whether the handover is smooth and whether the organisation keeps iterating. Chen Lei took over in 2020 and accomplished two things in five years — Temu went from zero to a GMV scale of USD 100 billion, and the main site held onto its low-price mindshare. Zhao Jiazhen was promoted to co-CEO in 2023, the second generation grown internally.

The risk lies in depth. Core decisions still rest on the Huang Zheng–Chen Lei dual core, and no one has emerged at the third layer who can shoulder responsibility independently. Against Alibaba's Jiang Fan and JD.com's Xu Ran, Pinduoduo's management pipeline is visibly thinner. If Chen Lei left tomorrow, there would be no near-term crisis, but the quality of strategic judgment would decline.

Three governance questions

Question 1: Whose cash is it? Legally it belongs to the company; economically it should belong to shareholders. The two have now diverged, and that divergence underpins every valuation discount.

Question 2: Is the super-voting right protection or risk? Colin Huang holds about 26% but controls a majority of voting power. The upside is that it shields the company from short-term pressure; the downside is that no force can compel him to pay dividends.

Question 3: Is disclosure sufficient? No segment disclosure for Temu, no separate disclosure for Duoduo Maicai, no full-year guidance, no external CFO. That style is efficiency in the growth phase; in the mature phase it is opacity.

Conclusion

Conclusion: management capability 8, attitude toward shareholders 3, governance structure 6, overall 4/10.

No M&A, no fraud, clean handover — these are real strengths. But five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, RMB 456.4 billion of cash with no stated use, and no segment disclosure for Temu mean the stock cannot be valued on discounted cash flow — only on a sum-of-the-parts discount.

What to watch: whether the 2026 annual report sets out a capital return policy for the first time, and whether Temu begins disclosing revenue and profit separately. If either happens, the management score rises from 4 to 6 and the position cap can go from 3% to 5%.
