Six-Gate Screen — Pinduoduo

Core call: cheap is genuinely cheap — the problem is it isn't yours

PDD now sells as a "cash plus business" package: RMB 456.4 billion of cash is 57% of market cap, and the remaining business trades at just 3.4x 2026 expected earnings. Four of the six gates pass and two are in doubt, and those two doubtful gates are exactly what determines position size — the moat rests on policy acquiescence, and management does not return capital to shareholders.

The conclusion is buy, but no single position above 3%, built over three to four quarters.

First, a quick calculation: what is this price pricing in?

On November 13, 2026, Pinduoduo closed at USD 78.97, a market cap of about USD 112.4 billion. On the same statement, cash and short-term investments were RMB 456.4 billion, which at an exchange rate of 7.1 is RMB 456.4 billion of a market cap of about RMB 798 billion. In other words, 57% of what you pay is cash, and the remaining 43% is the business.

The business piece: Non-GAAP net profit was RMB 107.3 billion in 2025 and RMB 42.59 billion in the first half of 2026. On the sell-side consensus, full-year 2026 adjusted net profit is about RMB 99.5 billion. RMB 341.6 billion buys RMB 99.5 billion of profit, 3.4x. The six gates must answer one question: if it is this cheap, why is no one grabbing it.

Stage one, stage two: understandable, and genuinely a good business

Nothing about Pinduoduo's model is hard to understand: aggregate goods from white-label factories, pull users in with low prices, then charge merchants advertising fees and commissions. It does not hold inventory, build warehouses or employ couriers. Gross margin was 57.3% in the second quarter of 2026, net margin 24.2%, and operating cash flow reached RMB 25.7 billion in a single quarter.

This structure is an order of magnitude better than JD.com's — JD.com reported revenue of RMB 346.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion, and a net margin of 2%. Both sell goods, but Pinduoduo earns from merchants while JD.com earns the spread. The second gate scores 8 points; the two points deducted reflect that this business's profit depends heavily on merchants remaining willing to spend on advertising on the platform.

Third gate: the moat rests on customs' tacit consent, so only 6 points

The low-price mindset is real. Taobao and JD.com spent three years and RMB 10 billion in subsidies and never won it back; Douyin diverted traffic through content, taking time, not price-comparison occasions. The direct connection to industrial-belt factories is also real — that is why it can cut prices while sustaining a 57% gross margin.

The problem is that this moat rests on two external preconditions. The first is the US de minimis exemption — in August 2025 de minimis was fully suspended, and on 8 October 2026 CBP issued a proposed rule bringing all low-value shipments under USD 2,500 into stricter declaration and bond requirements, with comments open until 7 December. The second is the EU — on 1 July it scrapped the EUR 150 exemption and added a flat EUR 3 duty per product category, with a further EUR 2 handling fee per category from 1 November.With both preconditions removed at once, the moat was not dug open by competitors — it was dug open by customs.

Gate 4: Management is the only source of a discount on this business

RMB 456.4 billion in cash sits on the balance sheet, with no dividend for five years and no buyback at scale. Asked about capital allocation at the earnings call, management has consistently answered "investing in the supply chain and ecosystem." That answer is not wrong—full-year 2025 net profit fell 12%, and the money did go into the "RMB 100 billion support" program.

But for shareholders the outcome is clear: you cannot buy the cash flow; you can only wait for a re-rating. Gate four scores 4, not because management is weak (in five years under Chen Lei, Temu went from zero to a GMV scale of USD 100 billion), but because their attitude toward shareholders defies scoring. This gate determines position size, not whether to buy.

Gate five, gate six: price provides the margin, discipline must be self-supplied

Gate 5: 9 points. Net of cash, 3.4x P/E; even under the most bearish assumption — net profit falling to RMB 70 billion in 2027 — it is only 4.9x. Bear-case target price USD 66, 17% downside from the current price; bull case USD 128, 62% upside. Odds of 3.6 to 1 — a rare buyer-favourable structure in this portfolio.

Gate 6: 6 points. The buy case rests on verifiable premises: whether advertising growth at the main site stops declining, and whether Temu's unit economics turn positive — two quarters of results will show. But "when the cash returns to shareholders" cannot be verified; you can only wait. So the discipline is not a stop-loss price, but a position cap.

Veto items and items to be verified

No veto items. Gate 4 scoring 4/10 is not a veto—management capability is not the issue; it simply does not return cash to shareholders. But it does mean this investment cannot be a heavy position: with no dividends or buybacks, returns depend solely on a re-rating, and the timing of that re-rating is not yours to decide.

Four things to verify, ranked by importance:

  • Online marketing revenue growth at the main site: just 3.5% in Q2, the most direct read on the domestic market topping out. Back above 8%, and the valuation method has to change.
  • Temu unit economics: the share of US orders from local warehouses and the share of European orders under semi-managed model—the levels reached determine whether the fixed cost of EUR 5 per category is an "expense" or "fatal".
  • Capital return policy: even a token buyback would be a re-rating trigger. None in five years, so one cannot be assumed next year.
  • Final ruling in the EU product safety investigation: worst case is a fine of 6% of global annual revenue—keep that ceiling in mind.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Buy, control position size, cap any single name at 3%.

Four of six gates passed, two in doubt, 42 points. The two doubtful gates (moat, management) are not veto items, but both point to one thing: returns on this investment can only come from re-rating, not from dividends or buybacks. Build the position in tranches over three to four quarters, and treat "no re-rating" as the base case.

The trigger to add is core app advertising growth returning above 6%, or the company announcing a meaningful buyback for the first time; the trigger to exit is advertising revenue turning negative year on year while transaction services growth falls below 5%.
