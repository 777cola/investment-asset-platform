Six-Gate Screen — Pinduoduo

Core call: cheap is genuinely cheap — the problem is it isn't yours

PDD now trades as a "cash plus business" package: RMB 456.4 billion of cash is 57% of market cap, leaving the remaining business at just 3.4x 2026 expected earnings. Four of the six gates pass and two are in doubt — and those two doubtful gates are precisely what determines position size: the moat rests on policy acquiescence, and management does not return capital to shareholders.

Conclusion: buy, but no single position above 3%, built over three to four quarters.

First, a quick calculation: what is this price pricing in?

Pinduoduo closed at USD 78.97 on November 13, 2026, implying a market cap of roughly USD 112.4 billion. On the same basis, cash and short-term investments stood at RMB 456.4 billion which, at an exchange rate of 7.1, equates to RMB 456.4 billion against a market cap of about RMB 798 billion. In other words, 57% of what you pay is cash; the remaining 43% is the business.

The business piece: non-GAAP net profit was RMB 107.3 billion in 2025 and RMB 42.59 billion in 1H26. Sell-side consensus puts full-year 2026 adjusted net profit at about RMB 99.5 billion. RMB 341.6 billion buys RMB 99.5 billion of profit, or 3.4x. The six gates must answer one question: if it is this cheap, why is no one grabbing it.

Stage one, stage two: understandable, and genuinely a good business

Pinduoduo's model is straightforward: aggregate goods from white-label factories, draw users in on low prices, then monetise merchants through advertising fees and commissions. It holds no inventory, runs no warehouses and employs no couriers. Gross margin was 57.3% in 2026Q2, net margin 24.2%, and operating cash flow reached RMB 25.7 billion in a single quarter.

That structure is an order of magnitude better than JD.com's. JD.com reported 2026Q2 revenue of RMB 346.4 billion, net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion and a net margin of 2%. Both sell goods, but Pinduoduo earns from merchants while JD.com earns the spread. The second gate scores 8 points; the two points deducted reflect that this business's profit depends heavily on merchants remaining willing to spend on advertising on the platform.

The third gate: the moat rests on customs' tacit consent, so only 6 points

The low-price mindset is real. Taobao and JD.com spent three years and RMB 10 billion in subsidies without winning it back; Douyin diverted traffic through content, capturing time rather than price-comparison occasions. The direct link to industrial-belt factories is real too — that is what lets Pinduoduo cut prices while sustaining a 57% gross margin.

The problem is that this moat rests on two external preconditions. The first is the US de minimis exemption: de minimis was fully suspended in August 2025, and on 8 October 2026 CBP issued a proposed rule bringing all low-value shipments under USD 2,500 into stricter declaration and bond requirements, with comments open until 7 December. The second is the EU, which scrapped the EUR 150 exemption on 1 July and added a flat EUR 3 duty per product category, with a further EUR 2 handling fee per category from 1 November.With both preconditions removed at once, the moat was not dug open by competitors — it was dug open by customs.

Gate 4: Management is the only source of a discount on this business

RMB 456.4 billion of cash sits on the balance sheet, with no dividend for five years and no buyback at scale. Pressed on capital allocation on the earnings call, management has consistently pointed to "investing in the supply chain and ecosystem." The answer is not wrong: full-year 2025 net profit fell 12%, and the money did go into the "RMB 100 billion support" programme.

For shareholders, though, the outcome is clear: you cannot buy the cash flow; you can only wait for a re-rating. Gate four scores 4, not because management is weak (in five years under Chen Lei, Temu went from zero to a GMV scale of USD 100 billion), but because their attitude toward shareholders defies scoring. This gate determines position size, not whether to buy.

Gate five, gate six: price provides the margin, discipline must be self-supplied

Gate 5: 9 points. On a net cash basis the stock trades at 3.4x P/E; even under our most bearish assumption — net profit falling to RMB 70 billion in 2027 — it is only 4.9x. Bear-case target price USD 66, 17% downside from the current price; bull case USD 128, 62% upside. Odds of 3.6 to 1 — a rare buyer-favourable structure in this portfolio.

Gate 6: 6 points. The buy case rests on verifiable premises: whether advertising growth at the main site stops declining, and whether Temu's unit economics turn positive — two quarters of results will show. But "when the cash returns to shareholders" cannot be verified; you can only wait. So the discipline is not a stop-loss price, but a position cap.

Veto items and items to be verified

No veto items. A 4/10 score on Gate 4 is not a veto—management capability is not the issue; the company simply does not return cash to shareholders. It does, however, rule out a heavy position: with no dividends or buybacks, returns depend solely on a re-rating, and the timing of that re-rating is not yours to decide.

Four things to verify, ranked by importance:

  • Online marketing revenue growth at the main site: just 3.5% in Q2, the most direct read on the domestic market topping out. Back above 8%, and the valuation method has to change.
  • Temu unit economics: the share of US orders from local warehouses and the share of European orders under the semi-managed model—the levels reached determine whether the fixed cost of EUR 5 per category is an "expense" or "fatal".
  • Capital return policy: even a token buyback would be a re-rating trigger. There has been none in five years, so one cannot be assumed next year.
  • Final ruling in the EU product safety investigation: worst case is a fine of 6% of global annual revenue—keep that ceiling in mind.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Buy, but control position size and cap any single name at 3%.

Four of six gates passed, two in doubt, 42 points. The two doubtful gates (moat, management) are not veto items, but both point to the same conclusion: returns can only come from re-rating, not from dividends or buybacks. Build the position in tranches over three to four quarters, and treat "no re-rating" as the base case.

Add on core app advertising growth returning above 6%, or on the company announcing a meaningful buyback for the first time; exit on advertising revenue turning negative YoY while transaction services growth falls below 5%.
