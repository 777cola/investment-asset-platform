## The one-sentence version of this paper

**Buy a 57%-gross-margin rent-collecting business at 3.4x ex-cash P/E, betting on two things: ad growth stops deteriorating, and management does not burn RMB 456.4 billion overseas.** The return comes from re-rating, not dividends.

## H1 advertising growth bottoms out: the most fragile line item

3.5% is the lowest reading in three quarters. It means domestic merchants' willingness to spend has saturated—not "slowing growth," but "near-zero growth." **If Q3 falls to zero or negative, this assumption is directly falsified and the entire thesis must be rewritten.**

Verification method: each quarter, look at just one line—online marketing services revenue year-over-year. Ignore total revenue (it gets masked by Temu) and ignore profit (it gets distorted by investment).

## H2 Temu unit economics: now "proven but fragile"

Transaction services revenue rose 13%, the only segment still growing at a double-digit rate. But it was achieved with tariffs already in place—showing localization is working, and that the benefit is only enough to offset part of the cost.

**The weak point is the acceleration.**This line grew at a high double-digit rate last year, 13% this year; if it falls to single digits next year, localization is not outrunning cost. The observation window is Q3 and Q4.

## H3 Cash: the most reliable, and the least useful

Net cash rose RMB 34.1 billion in the half-year, with zero interest-bearing debt — an assumption that is almost impossible to break. But it is also the most useless one — **rising cash generates no return, only "safety."** What is safety worth? The market's answer is "nothing," hence the 3.4x.

## H4 Regulatory costs: only just starting to count, hardest to see clearly

The EU's EUR 200 million fine is already booked. The EUR 5 fixed cost per category takes effect only on November 1, and the US rule for items under USD 2,500 is still in the comment period (closing December 7). **Two of the three have not run long enough to see the full picture.**

My assumption is an annualized impact within RMB 15 billion (about 3.5% of 2025 revenue). Above that, the RMB 90 billion net profit in the base case would need to be revised down.

## H5 valuation re-rating: it needs a catalyst, and the catalyst is not in my hands

For 3.4x to become 6x, one of two things needs to happen: a buyback announcement, or ad growth back to 6%. **Neither is up to me, so I treat it as an "option" rather than an "assumption."**

That is also why the position is only 3% — if this were the base case, the position should be 8%; precisely because it is an option, the position can only be 3%.

## Falsification conditions: what would prove me wrong

**Exit immediately (regardless of price):** ① Online marketing revenue posts negative year-on-year growth for two consecutive quarters; ② transaction services revenue growth falls below 5%; ③ cash and short-term investments decline more than 10% year on year; ④ auditor change or a qualified opinion.

**Reduce position:** A single EU fine or provision exceeds RMB 10 billion; Temu announces large-scale self-built warehouses, causing capex to jump.

**Add position:** The company announces a buyback of over USD 5 billion; or advertising growth returns above 6%.

## Watch calendar

**2026/12/7**: Comment period closes on the US CBP proposed rule, after which it moves to finalization. **Late November 2026**: Q3 results; watch transaction services growth. **2027/3**: Annual report and Q4 data; watch for any change in capital return policy. **2027/5**: Q1 results; watch whether advertising growth stops declining. **Unscheduled**: Conclusion of the EU product safety investigation — the only event that could take a one-off hit of more than RMB 20 billion.
