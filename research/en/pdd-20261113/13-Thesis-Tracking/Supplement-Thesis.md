## Notes

The assumption table is my own work, not company guidance. PDD did not disclose a standalone YoY figure for 2026Q1 online marketing revenue, so the evidence column for H1 cites Q2 only. The assumed annualised regulatory cost of RMB 15 billion equals 3.5% of 2025 revenue of RMB 431.8 billion; this is my own estimate for sensitivity analysis, not a forecast. The falsification thresholds — two consecutive quarters of negative advertising growth, transaction services <5%, cash down 10% YoY, a single fine >RMB 10 billion — are discipline lines I set, with no external standard behind them. The 2026/12/7 entry in the watch calendar is the comment deadline on CBP's proposed rule; the company has previously said Q3 results would land in late November.

## The one-sentence version of this paper

Buy a 57%-gross-margin rent-collecting business at 3.4x ex-cash P/E on two bets: ad growth stops deteriorating, and management does not burn RMB 456.4 billion overseas. The return comes from re-rating, not dividends.

## H1 advertising growth bottoms out: the most fragile line item

At 3.5%, this is the lowest reading in three quarters: domestic merchants' willingness to spend has saturated—not "slowing growth" but "near-zero growth." Should Q3 print at zero or negative, the assumption is directly falsified and the entire thesis must be rewritten.

Verification: each quarter, track a single line—online marketing services revenue YoY. Ignore total revenue, which Temu masks, and ignore profit, which investment distorts.

## H2 Temu unit economics: now "proven but fragile"

Transaction services revenue rose 13%, the only segment still growing at a double-digit rate. That growth came with tariffs already in place—evidence that localisation is working, though the benefit only offsets part of the cost.

The weak point is the deceleration.This line grew at a high double-digit rate last year and 13% this year; if it slows to single digits next year, localisation is not outrunning cost. Watch Q3 and Q4.

## H3 Cash: the most reliable, and the least useful

Net cash rose RMB 34.1 billion in the half-year, with zero interest-bearing debt — an assumption that is almost impossible to break, and also the most useless one: rising cash generates no return, only "safety." What is safety worth? The market's answer is "nothing," hence the 3.4x.

## H4 Regulatory costs: only just starting to count, hardest to see clearly

The EU's EUR 200 million fine is already booked. The EUR 5 fixed cost per category does not take effect until November 1, and the US rule for items under USD 2,500 remains in the comment period (closing December 7). Two of the three have not run long enough to show the full picture.

I assume an annualised impact within RMB 15 billion (about 3.5% of 2025 revenue). Above that, the RMB 90 billion net profit in the base case would need to be revised down.

## H5 valuation re-rating: it needs a catalyst, and that catalyst is not in my hands

For 3.4x to re-rate to 6x, one of two things has to happen: a buyback announcement, or ad growth back to 6%. Neither is within my control, so I treat it as an option rather than an assumption.

That is also why the position is only 3%. If this were the base case, it should be 8%; precisely because it is an option, it can only be 3%.

## Falsification conditions: what would prove me wrong

Exit immediately (regardless of price): ① Online marketing revenue posts negative YoY growth for two consecutive quarters; ② transaction services revenue growth falls below 5%; ③ cash and short-term investments decline more than 10% YoY; ④ auditor change or a qualified opinion.

Reduce position: A single EU fine or provision exceeds RMB 10 billion; Temu announces large-scale self-built warehouses, causing capex to jump.

Add position: The company announces a buyback of over USD 5 billion; or advertising growth returns above 6%.
