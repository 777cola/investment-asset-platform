Core view

Li Lu's habit when reading financial reports is to first separate "institutional costs" from operating costs, because the two have completely different durations. Operating costs dilute with scale; institutional costs do not.

In this report, institutional costs became explicit for the first time: a EUR 200 million fine, management expenses +53.3%, and transaction services growth falling from high double digits to 13%.

Why calculate institutional costs separately?

Because its nature is an "access rent." To do business in a country, you pay this rent; if you cannot afford it, you exit. It does not shrink as you scale, nor does it disappear as you become more efficient.

The impact on valuation is structural: if 30% of a company's revenue comes from businesses that must pay access rent, that portion of revenue deserves a lower multiple than domestic operations. Transaction services already account for 48.7% of Temu's revenue.

Which of the three readings is the most critical

The EU charges EUR 5 per category. The fine is one-off (RMB 1.6 billion); the US rules are still out for comment (uncertain); only the EU regime is already in force, priced by category, and hits unit economics directly.

A EUR 10 parcel plus EUR 5 of cost leaves two options: raise the price (lose customers) or absorb it (lose margin). This is not an operating problem; it is arithmetic.

What is Temu's response worth

Local warehouses plus semi-managed operations can reduce the tariff impact, but they also make the company heavier: inventory, warehousing, local compliance teams. This is trading capex for market access.

Li Lu would view this shift positively—because it shows management is responding seriously rather than waiting for policy to change. But he would also say: the financials during the transition will look ugly; don't expect good numbers within two years.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: this earnings report reveals the scale of "institutional costs" for the first time.

The RMB 1.6 billion fine is one-off; the EU's fixed cost of EUR 5 per category is permanent. The latter is what valuation should reflect.

Investment implication: assign zero valuation to Temu and look only at the domestic business and cash. The 3.4x that this yields is the true margin of safety.
