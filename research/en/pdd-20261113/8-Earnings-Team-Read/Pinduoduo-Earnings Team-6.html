Core view

Pinduoduo's Q2 2026 results are out, and the market's first reaction was "profits fell again." That reaction is not wrong, but it only scratches the surface. Read the report line by line and three more important truths emerge.

Truth one: the business is not broken, but the growth is gone. Truth two: the money is real, but it is not in your pocket. Truth three: the real rival is not Taobao or JD.com, it is customs.

I. The business is intact; the growth is gone

Revenue RMB 112.36 billion, +8% YoY, below the consensus of 3%. Breaking it down: advertising RMB 57.6 billion, up just 3.5%; transaction services RMB 54.7 billion, up 13%.

Put the two numbers together and the picture is clear: the domestic business has stopped growing; all incremental growth comes from overseas. Advertising is the entirety of main-site monetization, and 3.5% is the ceiling; transaction services are mainly Temu commissions, the only part still growing at a double-digit rate.

Yet gross margin was 57.3%, up 1.4 percentage points YoY. That reading matters — it shows monetization quality has not deteriorated and the platform's bargaining power over merchants remains. If a company were in decline, gross margin would fall first; it did not.

So the accurate description is: this is a company that has gone from growth stock to cash cow, not a company in decline. The two warrant entirely different valuation methods.

II. The money is real, but it is not in your pocket

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 27.18 billion, down 12% year on year — the third consecutive quarterly decline. On the surface it looks like "the more it earns, the less it keeps," but operating cash flow was RMB 25.7 billion, up 19% year on year — the profit is real cash.

The main driver of the decline is not hard to find: other income swung from +RMB 120 million in the year-earlier period to −RMB 7.4 billion, including the EUR 200 million fine the EU imposed on Temu over product safety issues. Strip that out and the decline narrows to single digits.

The real story is not profit but cash. Cash and short-term investments totaled RMB 456.4 billion, up another RMB 34.1 billion from the end of 2025. The company says it is stepping up investment while cash keeps piling up — which shows the investment intensity is nowhere near as large as the narrative suggests, and that it has the capacity to buy back stock but chooses not to.

Market cap is about USD 112.4 billion, or RMB 798 billion. Net of RMB 456.4 billion in cash, the business sells for just RMB 341.6 billion, against expected 2026 profit of about RMB 99.5 billion — a P/E of 3.4x.

That number looks too cheap to be true. It exists because for five years the company has not paid a single cent of dividends and has not done one meaningful buyback. The market is not discounting the business; it is pricing the fact that the cash does not belong to shareholders.

Three, the real rival is customs

In 2026, Pinduoduo's biggest rival has no quarterly earnings report. The US suspended de minimis in August 2025, and on October 8, 2026, CBP issued a proposed rule bringing all low-value shipments under USD 2,500 into stricter declaration and bond requirements, with comments open until December 7.

The EU is more direct: it scrapped the EUR 150 duty exemption on July 1, adding a flat EUR 3 duty per product category; on November 1 it adds a further EUR 2 handling fee per category. For a parcel worth EUR 10, fixed import-stage costs are EUR 5 — 50%.

Temu's response is local warehouses plus semi-managed operations: the fully managed share in the US has already been cut from 70% to under 30%, with the goal of 80% of US orders fulfilled from local warehouses by the end of 2026. That path solves tariffs, but not another problem — it turns Temu from an asset-light platform into a quasi-retailer with inventory, warehousing and a compliance team.

The valuation method has to change with it: from platform multiples to retailer multiples. That is why transaction services revenue growth fell from high double digits last year to 13% — not because demand is gone, but because unit economics have been eroded.

So should we buy now?

Buy, but only a little.

Reasons to buy: 3.4x ex-cash P/E, three-scenario odds of 3.6:1 (bear USD 66, base USD 99, bull USD 128), downside cushioned by RMB 456.4 billion of cash, and a 57% gross margin showing the business quality is intact.

Reasons against a heavy position: five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks, with management not explaining how it will use the cash; advertising growth of 3.5%, with the domestic market already at its peak; Temu's regulatory exposure has no cap, and the worst case in the EU product-safety investigation is a fine of 6% of global annual revenue.

The conclusion: buy, with a 3% cap per position, building the stake over three to four quarters.

Do not use "net cash" as the reason to buy—the cash is on the balance sheet, not in your pocket. This investment is essentially buying a high-gross-margin business at a 70% discount, while betting management will not burn that money overseas.

Conclusion

One-line summary: Pinduoduo is now "a high-margin cash cow + RMB 456.4 billion in cash + a management team that returns none of it"; the market prices in only the first two at 3.4x — that is both the opportunity and the risk.

Buy, 3% position cap, scale in. Wait for buybacks, or for ad growth to return to 6%.
