## Revenue structure: two worlds on one table

Total revenue of RMB 112.36 billion, +8% YoY, looks decent on the surface. By segment: advertising RMB 57.6 billion, +3.5%; transaction services RMB 54.7 billion, +13%. Advertising represents the entirety of domestic monetisation on the main app, so +3.5% means it has peaked. Transaction services are mainly Temu commissions — the only segment still growing at a double-digit rate.

The two segments are now close to a 50/50 split (51.3% vs. 48.7%); three years ago advertising accounted for over 70%. That structural shift tells us two things: there is no incremental growth left in the domestic business, and the company's future alpha rests entirely on an overseas business that tariffs are chasing down.

## Second layer gross margin: 57.3%, up 1.4 percentage points

Gross profit of RMB 64.34 billion, with gross margin at 57.3%, up 1.4 percentage points YoY. This is the most important positive signal in the report: monetisation quality has not deteriorated, and the platform's bargaining power over merchants remains intact.

The gross margin gain came not from higher prices but from revenue mix. Temu's transaction services revenue carries a higher gross margin than main-site advertising, since commissions have almost no marginal cost, so its rising share naturally lifts the overall gross margin. The 1.4 percentage points should therefore be read not as "the main site got stronger" but as "overseas now accounts for a larger share."

## Line up the four quarters: the decline began in 2025Q4

Q4 2025 non-GAAP net profit was RMB 26.295 billion, down 12% YoY, followed by RMB 14.1 billion in Q1 2026 (-15%) and RMB 28.49 billion in Q2 2026 (-13%). Three consecutive quarters of decline, with the magnitude holding steady between 10% and 15% — that stability itself shows this is not an accident but a pattern.

The drivers are equally steady: merchant fee waivers under the "RMB 100 billion support" programme on one side, Temu's localisation and compliance spending on the other. Both are active choices, not passive damage. This differs from the many companies that "cut prices because competition forces them to" — Pinduoduo is actively spending money to buy ecosystem stability and overseas market access.

## Tier 3 costs: spending goes to compliance and governance, not subsidies

R&D of RMB 4.6 billion (+27.8%), G&A of RMB 2.3 billion (+53.3%) and a sales expense ratio of 26.4% that barely moved all point to the same conclusion: the company is paying for "compliance," not for "growth."

G&A up five tenths reflects the legal, tax, customs and staffing costs of Temu's localisation; R&D up nearly three tenths reflects algorithm and risk-control systems. The sales expense ratio has not fallen, showing domestic subsidy intensity has not eased at all—subsidies to defend share on one side, compliance adding cost on the other, squeezing profit from both ends.

## Where the money sits: the structure of RMB 456.4 billion

The cash sits mainly in time deposits, government bonds and money-market funds, with a small equity allocation. The company carries no bank borrowings and has issued no bonds, so interest income is a net contribution — in 2025, interest and investment income contributed to profit on the scale of RMB 10 billion.

The result is counterintuitive: part of Pinduoduo's profit is earned from "deposit interest", not from selling goods. As rates drift lower, that slice of profit thins. It also explains why the net margin looks more fragile than the gross margin — the balance sheet supplies a small part of the 57% gross margin.

## Tier four, profit: half of the −13% came from one-off items

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell 12% YoY to RMB 27.18 billion, and non-GAAP net profit fell 13% to RMB 28.49 billion — the third consecutive quarter of YoY decline. On the surface this looks like "earning less and less," but the breakdown tells a different story: other income swung from +RMB 120 million a year ago to −RMB 7.4 billion, a RMB 7.5 billion swing that includes the EU's EUR 200 million (about RMB 1.6 billion) fine and FX losses.

Excluding this item, the profit decline narrows to single digits. But even excluding it, the trend is still downward — Q1 net profit was RMB 12.5 billion, −15%, and Q2 was −12%. This is not a one-off issue; it is an investment-cycle issue.

## Layer 5 Cash flow and balance sheet: strong cash generation, though some of the cash belongs to others

Operating cash flow reached RMB 25.7 billion, up 19% and ahead of net profit — confirming earnings are backed by real cash. Cash and short-term investments totalled RMB 456.4 billion, up RMB 34.1 billion in six months, with zero interest-bearing debt. On the balance sheet alone, this is a company that is nearly impossible to fail.

One detail demands attention, however: part of Pinduoduo's cash is merchant settlement funds and deposits — the platform collects first and settles later, creating float. Legally this is a liability, not shareholder money. The company does not disclose the breakdown, so the "3.4x ex-cash P/E" is overly optimistic. To be conservative, I applied an implicit 10% haircut to cash in my valuation.

## Three numbers to watch most closely

1. Advertising revenue growth. 3.5% is the make-or-break line. If Q3 growth slows to zero or turns negative, the main site shifts from "mature" to "declining" and the valuation method must change.

2. Transaction services growth. 13% is Temu's thermometer. A drop below 5% would signal that localisation has failed, or that European customers are churning.

3. Sequential change in cash. This matters more than profit. A sequential decline in cash of more than 5% would signal that Temu has begun burning through the company's reserves; continued growth in cash would signal that management still prefers to hoard money rather than invest it or return it to shareholders.

## Set against peers' results: similar growth, but an order of magnitude apart in quality

JD.com 2026Q2 revenue was RMB 346.4 billion, −2.9% YoY, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion and a net margin of 2.0%. Alibaba's June 2026 quarter revenue was RMB 268.953 billion, +9%, while operating profit fell 57% YoY to RMB 15.161 billion, an operating margin of 5.6%. Pinduoduo, on the same revenue scale, posts an operating margin of 24.7%.

Four times Alibaba's operating margin, twelve times JD.com's net margin. The gap reflects business model rather than management efficiency: one collects rent, the other two do the work. Read this far into the filings and it is clear why the stock deserves a look even when cheap.
