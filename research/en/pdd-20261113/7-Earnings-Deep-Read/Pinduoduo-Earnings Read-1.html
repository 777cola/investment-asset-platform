Core call: a mature, high-margin toll-road-style business

2026Q2 revenue RMB 112.36 billion, +8% YoY, split into advertising +3.5% and transaction services +13%. Gross profit RMB 64.34 billion, gross margin 57.3% (+1.4pct), operating cash flow RMB 25.7 billion (+19%), zero interest-bearing debt. Net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 27.18 billion, −12%, declining for three consecutive quarters, of which RMB 7.5 billion came from other income turning negative (including a EUR 200 million EU fine).

In one sentence: growth has stalled, but the ability to make money has not. Valued as a mature rent-collecting company, it is cheap; valued as a growth stock, it should never be bought.

Layer one, revenue structure: two worlds written on the same table

Total revenue RMB 112.36 billion, +8% YoY — looks decent. Broken down: advertising RMB 57.6 billion, +3.5%; transaction services RMB 54.7 billion, +13%. Advertising is the entirety of domestic monetization on the main app; 3.5% means it has peaked. Transaction services are mainly Temu commissions, the only segment still growing at a double-digit rate.

The two segments are now close to a 50/50 split (51.3% vs. 48.7%). Three years ago advertising accounted for over 70%. This structural shift says two things: there is no incremental growth left in the domestic business; and the company's future alpha is entirely staked on an overseas business that tariffs are chasing down.

Second layer gross margin: 57.3%, up 1.4 percentage points

Gross profit RMB 64.34 billion, gross margin 57.3%, up 1.4 percentage points year on year. This is the most important positive signal in the report — it shows monetization quality has not deteriorated and the platform's bargaining power over merchants remains intact.

The gross margin gain came not from higher prices but from revenue mix: Temu's transaction services revenue carries a higher gross margin than main-site advertising (commissions have almost no marginal cost), so its rising share naturally lifts the overall gross margin. The 1.4 percentage points should therefore be read not as "the main site got stronger" but as "overseas now accounts for a larger share."

Line up the four quarters: the decline began in 2025Q4

Q4 2025 Non-GAAP net profit was RMB 26.295 billion, down 12% YoY; Q1 2026 was RMB 14.1 billion, down 15%; Q2 2026 was RMB 28.49 billion, down 13%. Three consecutive quarters of decline, with the magnitude steady between 10% and 15% — that stability itself shows this is not an accident but a pattern.

The causes are equally steady: on one side, merchant fee waivers under the "RMB 100 billion support" program; on the other, Temu's localization and compliance spending. Both are active choices, not passive damage. This differs from many companies that "cut prices because competition forces them to" — Pinduoduo is actively spending money to buy ecosystem stability and overseas market access.

Tier 3 Costs: spending goes to compliance and governance, not subsidies

R&D RMB 4.6 billion, +27.8%; G&A RMB 2.3 billion, +53.3%; sales expense ratio 26.4%, barely moved. These three readings point to the same thing: the company is paying for "compliance," not for "growth."

G&A up five tenths reflects the legal, tax, customs and staffing costs of Temu's localization; R&D up nearly three tenths reflects algorithm and risk-control systems. The sales expense ratio has not fallen, showing domestic subsidy intensity has not eased at all—subsidies to defend share on one side, compliance adding cost on the other, squeezing profit from both ends.

Where the money sits: the structure of RMB 456.4 billion

The money is held mainly as time deposits, government bonds and money-market funds, with a small equity portion. The company has no bank borrowings and has issued no bonds, so interest income is a net contribution — in 2025, interest and investment income contributed to profit on the scale of RMB 10 billion.

This produces a counterintuitive result: part of Pinduoduo's profit is earned from "deposit interest", not from selling goods. As rates drift lower, that slice of profit thins. This is also why its net margin looks more fragile than its gross margin — a small part of the 57% gross margin is supplied by the balance sheet.

Tier four, profit: half of the −13% came from one-off items

Net profit attributable to shareholders was RMB 27.18 billion, down 12% YoY; Non-GAAP was RMB 28.49 billion, down 13%, the third consecutive quarter of year-on-year decline. On the surface, it looks like "earning less and less," but breaking it down: other income swung from +RMB 120 million in the year-ago period to −RMB 7.4 billion, a difference of RMB 7.5 billion, which includes the EU's EUR 200 million (about RMB 1.6 billion) fine and foreign exchange losses.

Excluding this item, the profit decline narrows to single digits. But even excluding it, the trend is still downward — Q1 net profit was RMB 12.5 billion, −15%, and Q2 was −12%. This is not a one-off issue; it is an investment-cycle issue.

Layer 5 Cash flow and balance sheet: strong cash generation, but some of the cash belongs to others

Operating cash flow reached RMB 25.7 billion, up 19%, exceeding net profit — confirming earnings are backed by real cash. Cash and short-term investments totaled RMB 456.4 billion, up RMB 34.1 billion in six months, with zero interest-bearing debt. On the balance sheet alone, this is a company that is nearly impossible to fail.

But one detail demands attention: part of Pinduoduo's cash consists of merchant settlement funds and deposits — the platform collects first and settles later, creating float. Legally this is a liability, not shareholder money. The company does not disclose the breakdown, so the "3.4x ex-cash P/E" calculation is overly optimistic. To be conservative, I applied an implicit 10% haircut to cash in my valuation.

Three numbers to watch most closely

1. Advertising revenue growth. 3.5% is the make-or-break line. If Q3 growth falls to zero or turns negative, the main site shifts from "mature" to "declining," and the valuation method must change.

2. Transaction services growth. 13% is Temu's thermometer. A drop below 5% would signal that localization has not worked, or that European customers are churning.

3. Sequential change in cash. This item matters more than profit. A sequential decline in cash of more than 5% would signal that Temu has begun burning through the company's reserves; continued growth in cash would signal that management still chooses to hoard money rather than invest it or return it to shareholders.

Set against peers' results: similar growth, but an order of magnitude apart in quality

JD.com 2026Q2 revenue RMB 346.4 billion, −2.9% YoY; net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 7.1 billion, net margin 2.0%. Alibaba's June 2026 quarter revenue RMB 268.953 billion, +9%; operating profit RMB 15.161 billion, −57% YoY, operating margin 5.6%. Pinduoduo, on the same revenue scale, posts an operating margin of 24.7%.

Four times Alibaba's operating margin, twelve times JD.com's net margin. The gap is not management efficiency but business model: one collects rent, the other two do the work. Read this far into the filings and it is clear why it deserves a look even when cheap.

Conclusion

Conclusion: growth has stalled, profitability has not, and cash is not going back to shareholders.

A 57.3% gross margin, RMB 25.7 billion of operating cash flow and zero interest-bearing debt show the quality of the business is intact; advertising +3.5%, net profit −13% and a further RMB 34.1 billion of cash show growth is gone and there is no willingness to distribute.

The right way to read this report: treat it as a mature, high-gross-margin toll collector, not a growth company. On that framework, 8.2x PE and 3.4x ex-cash is cheap. On a growth-company framework, it is "stalled growth" and should never be bought.Pick the wrong framework and the conclusion flips.
