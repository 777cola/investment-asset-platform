## I. Company Overview

PDD Holdings (NASDAQ: PDD) was founded by Colin Huang in 2015 and listed on Nasdaq in July 2018 at an offering price of USD 19/ADS. Its core businesses are the domestic low-price e-commerce platform Pinduoduo and cross-border e-commerce Temu (launched in September 2022), alongside community group buying (Duoduo Maicai) and investment in agricultural technology.

Q2 2026 revenue was RMB 112.36 billion, Non-GAAP net profit RMB 28.49 billion, cash and short-term investments RMB 456.4 billion, and zero interest-bearing debt. The stock closed at USD 78.97 on 13 November, a market cap of about USD 112.4 billion (roughly RMB 798 billion), PE(TTM) about 8.2x, and 35.6% below its 52-week high of USD 122.66.

One detail in the ownership structure deserves attention: insiders hold about 32% combined (Colin Huang about 26%, Chen Lei about 3%, the rest held collectively by partners and executives), while institutions hold about 28.66%. This makes it a company absolutely controlled by its founder with limited institutional participation — there is no pressure from a major shareholder selling down, and no institution able to push it to change capital allocation.

In eight years since listing, the company has never done a major refinancing, nor used complex instruments such as convertible bonds or preferred shares. Its capital structure is simple enough to sum up in one sentence: no debt, only cash and ordinary shares (with AB share voting-right differences).

## II. Industry analysis

China's online retail sales reached about RMB 15 trillion in 2025, with penetration at roughly 45%; growth has slowed to 7%–10%, marking a shift into stock competition. Share (GMV basis): Alibaba about 45%, JD.com about 20%, Pinduoduo about 15%, Douyin E-commerce about 10%.

Two main threads run through the industry. First, domestically the market has shifted from incremental to stock, and the marginal effect of subsidies in buying share is diminishing. Second, cross-border has moved from a "duty-free dividend" to "compliance costs": the US suspended de minimis in August 2025 and in October 2026 proposes extending the scope to goods under USD 2,500; the EU scrapped the EUR 150 exemption in July and in November added a EUR 2 handling fee per category. This second thread is the core variable for current valuations.

One overlooked structural change: domestic e-commerce growth has fallen below total retail sales growth, signalling that online substitution of offline is nearing its end. Industry growth now comes from "higher average order value" rather than "rising penetration" — and Pinduoduo's average order value is precisely the lowest. This is the fundamental reason its long-term growth will trail the industry.

Industry concentration is rising: the top four together hold about 90% share, and long-tail platforms (Vipshop, Suning and others) have been marginalised. This is good for Pinduoduo — competition has shifted from a "free-for-all" to a "standoff among four", making tacit price-war coordination easier.

## III. Business model

Platform model with two revenue streams: online marketing services (advertising) and transaction services (commission). No inventory holding, no warehousing, logistics outsourced, hence a 57.3% gross margin, 24.2% net margin and minimal capex.

Temu runs two models: fully managed (the platform bears line-haul, customs clearance, last-mile delivery and pricing) and semi-managed/local warehouse (merchants or local warehouses handle fulfillment). The 2026 strategy is to cut the US fully-managed share from about 70% to below 30%, with 80% of US orders fulfilled from local warehouses by the end of 2026. This means the company is migrating from an asset-light platform toward a quasi-retailer; gross margin will be diluted, but access risk falls.

Rough unit economics: on an order with a RMB 40 average ticket, the platform collects about RMB 2 in commission and about RMB 6 in allocated advertising, fulfillment sits with the merchant, and the platform's marginal cost is under RMB 1. Gross profit per order exceeds RMB 7. This structure explains the 57% gross margin and why its bargaining power over merchants matters so much.

The mix of advertising and commission determines resilience. Advertising tracks merchant profitability; commission tracks GMV. When merchants stop making money, they cut advertising first, not shipments, so advertising growth flags problems earlier than GMV. That is the core reason this report focuses on advertising.

## IV. Financial analysis

Revenue: +8% in Q2 2026, of which advertising +3.5% and transaction services +13%. The domestic market has peaked and overseas is the only source of incremental growth; the mix is now close to half and half.

Profit: net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 27.18 billion, down 12% year on year, the third consecutive quarterly decline. Half the cause is deliberate investment (the RMB 100 billion support program, supply chain), half is regulatory cost (the EUR 200 million EU fine turned other income into a RMB 7.4 billion loss). Gross margin actually rose 1.4 percentage points, showing that monetization quality has not deteriorated.

Cash flow and assets: operating cash flow RMB 25.7 billion, +19%; cash of RMB 456.4 billion, up RMB 34.1 billion in six months; zero interest-bearing debt. This is a balance sheet that is almost impossible to break.

One easily overlooked cash-flow detail: Pinduoduo's working capital is negative — merchants pay deposits first and settle for goods later, so the platform effectively holds a float for free. That float is both a source of cash and the reason a cash-deducted valuation needs a discount: it does not belong to shareholders, it belongs to merchants.

The expense structure has changed. Three years ago R&D expense was around 3% of revenue and administrative expense 1.5%; now they are 4.1% and 2.1% respectively. This is not one-off; it is the new normal after greater scale and tighter compliance. Half of the margin decline comes from here.

Putting the four layers of financials together, the conclusion is: the income statement is deteriorating, the balance sheet is thickening, and cash flow is strengthening. These three things happening at once usually mean a company is switching from expansion to rent collection — and the market assigns a lower valuation multiple to that switch, which is exactly why it is cheap.

## V. Management

Huang Zheng (founder, ~26% stake, super-voting rights) sets direction; Chen Lei (CEO, took over in 2020) runs execution; Zhao Jiazhen (co-CEO since 2023) runs supply. All three come from technical/operational backgrounds; no outside professional managers were parachuted in.

Capital allocation record: eight years with almost no M&A and no goodwill impairment; in 2020 Huang Zheng donated 2.37% of shares and transferred 3.77%; from 2022 capital went into Temu; in 2025 the "RMB 100 billion support" program cut net profit by 12%; five years of zero dividends and zero buybacks. This is the main reason for the valuation discount, and the reason this report caps the position at 3%.

Governance also has a structural fact: the company has no publicly disclosed CFO, gives no full-year guidance, and discloses neither Temu nor Duoduo Grocery as separate segments. For institutional investors, this opacity is itself a reason for a discount, and it will not change with performance.

The succession question is about depth, not ability. Chen Lei and Zhao Jiazhen can both deliver, but no one at the third layer has emerged who can carry things independently. For a company of 15,000 people earning RMB 100 billion a year, this bench is thin.

## VI. Growth drivers

Driver one: Temu localization. Replacing cross-border small parcels with local warehouses plus local sourcing hedges tariffs, shortens fulfillment and lifts repurchase. Success hinges on two numbers: the share of orders from local warehouses, and transaction services growth.

Driver two: monetization rate on the main site. It is about 5% now, with theoretical room to rise, but pushing higher will drive merchants away. Driver three: Duoduo Maicai and the agricultural supply chain. Small in scale, but a differentiated moat.

Of the three drivers, only Temu can bend the profit curve within two years. The other two are about defending, not growing.

Temu's second growth engine is Europe. Europe already accounts for roughly 40% of Temu's GMV (third-party estimates of 33%–45%), but it is also the most heavily regulated market: the tax exemption was scrapped in July, a handling fee added in November, and a product-safety case opened. The fastest-growing region is precisely where costs are rising fastest — the worst combination.

Duoduo Maicai's value lies in frequency. Fresh produce is the only category that gets people to open the app every week, and it makes an implicit contribution to user retention on the main site. But it is also asset-heavy: forward warehouses, fulfillment and spoilage all eat into gross margin.

## VII. Competitive landscape

Alibaba (June 2026 quarter revenue RMB 268.953 billion, +9%, operating profit −57%) plays brand and ecosystem; JD.com (2026Q2 revenue −2.9%, net margin 2.0%) plays fulfillment; Douyin (share ~10%) plays content and recommendation. Pinduoduo's position is "lowest cost, slowest cash burn," but its gateway status is being eroded by content e-commerce.

The overseas rivals are not Amazon but SHEIN, AliExpress and national discount retailers. What they share is that none rely on brand premium—they rely on supply-chain efficiency. Temu leads on this track, but by a smaller margin than in China.

Competition has another dimension: the fight for merchants. Alibaba rolled out multiple merchant support programs in 2026, JD.com opened third-party POP, Douyin trades traffic for supply. Four players chasing the same factories is lifting the factories' bargaining power—the root reason Pinduoduo's monetization rate struggles to rise.

Douyin's real threat is not GMV but time spent. Users spend two hours a day on Douyin and ten minutes on Pinduoduo. Time spent determines the opportunity for "discovery," and discovery converts more efficiently than search.

## VIII. Risk factors

1. Regulation and market access: the EU product safety investigation carries a maximum fine of 6% of global annual revenue (about RMB 25.9 billion); if the proposed US CBP rule is finalized, the cost of small parcels under USD 2,500 will rise significantly.

2. Cash burn: Temu localization could require RMB 10–30 billion per year, or RMB 30–90 billion cumulative over three years. 3. Supply side: once merchant gross margins are squeezed to the limit, low-price supply thins out. 4. Governance: VIE, super-voting rights, no segment disclosure, zero shareholder returns. 5. Geopolitics: delisting or forced divestiture in extreme scenarios.

The risk transmission path deserves to be spelled out: tariffs rise → Temu unit economics deteriorate → localization investment increases → cash begins to decline → the market worries that "money is being burned" → valuation de-rates further. The trigger for this chain is "cash declining quarter-on-quarter for two consecutive quarters," not tariffs themselves.

One risk that is rarely mentioned: data and algorithm regulation. Recommendation algorithms, differential pricing and platform "either-or" exclusivity are all on the regulatory radar. This kind of regulation will not kill you with a one-off fine, but it will keep raising compliance costs.

## IX. Earnings forecast

2026E: revenue RMB 460 billion (+6.5%), of which advertising RMB 233 billion (+1.7%) and transaction services RMB 227 billion (+12%); Non-GAAP net profit RMB 99.5 billion (−7.3%). 2027E: revenue RMB 490 billion (+6.5%), Non-GAAP net profit RMB 90 billion (−9.5%).

Three key assumptions behind the forecast: (1) advertising growth drops to 1%–2% in 2026 and recovers slightly in 2027; (2) transaction services +12% in 2026 and +11% in 2027, reflecting localization as a tariff hedge; (3) gross margin drifts down from 57.3% to 57.0% as localization raises fulfillment costs. A 5-percentage-point deviation in any of the three assumptions moves net profit by 8%–12%.

Forecast sensitivity: each 1-percentage-point deviation in advertising growth moves 2026 revenue by about RMB 2.3 billion and net profit by about RMB 1.8 billion; each 1-percentage-point deviation in transaction services growth moves revenue by about RMB 2.0 billion and net profit by about RMB 1.5 billion. In other words, advertising growth is the most sensitive variable in this forecast.

2026 quarterly cadence: Q1 RMB 106.2 billion, Q2 RMB 112.36 billion, Q3 estimated at about RMB 112 billion (+3%), Q4 estimated at RMB 129.4 billion (+4.5%, including peak season). The full-year RMB 460 billion assumption rests on the premise that Q3 does not deteriorate further.

## X. Valuation

Method: sum of the parts. Core business at 3–8x P/E, cash added back at face value. This method suits Pinduoduo because cash is 57% of market cap, so a consolidated P/E is badly distorted (8.2x consolidated vs 3.4x ex-cash).

Base case USD 99 (2027: RMB 90 billion, 6x, plus cash), bear case USD 66 (RMB 70 billion, 3x), bull case USD 128 (RMB 105 billion, 8x). Odds 3.6:1. Sensitivity: every RMB 10 billion change in net profit moves the target price by about USD 10; every 1x change in the multiple moves it by about USD 12–21.

Why not DCF: Pinduoduo's free cash flow depends heavily on whether the cash gets burned, and that cannot be modeled; the discount rate for China ADRs also embeds institutional risk that cannot be quantified. Sum of the parts is crude, but every assumption in it can be tested—which is more useful than a precise error.

Versus historical valuation: at the 2023 low the market gave it 9x TTM, the 2024 rebound took it to 14x, 2025 fell back to 10x, and now it is 8.2x. The historical range is 8–14x; it now sits at the low end.

## XI. Investment recommendation

Buy, control position size (3% cap per name). 12-month target price USD 99 (+25%), bear case USD 66 (−17%).

Entry pace: build 1% at USD 78–80; after Q3 results, add 1% if transaction services growth ≥10% or the stock is ≤USD 72 with cash unchanged; add another 1% on announcement of a ≥USD 5 billion buyback or ad growth back to 6%. Trim: halve above USD 120. Exit: two consecutive quarters of negative ad growth, transaction services growth Core thesis in one line: buy a 57%-gross-margin toll-road business at a third of the price, betting management will not burn RMB 456.4 billion overseas.

Difference from consensus: sell-side 2027 net profit forecasts cluster at RMB 100–110 billion; this report uses RMB 90 billion, more conservative. The gap comes from estimates of Temu localization costs: I think localization will compress the Non-GAAP net margin from 24.9% to 18.4%, while the market generally assumes it holds above 21%.

One operational detail: do not add the day before Q3 results. Earnings are the only certain, two-way risk event; wait for the print before deciding — the cost is only possibly missing a 3%–5% rally.

Recommendations differ by holder type: those with existing China ADR exposure should treat Pinduoduo as a "like-for-like swap" rather than an addition — trim first, add later; those without China ADR exposure can build up to the 3% cap, because cash cushions the downside; those who cannot tolerate a 20% drawdown should not buy, because the bear case is −17%.
