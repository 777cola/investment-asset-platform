Core call: the numbers hold up; the differences are all in definitions, not in the facts

We cross-checked eight key metrics against two sources: company filings/10-Q versus Chinese financial media and the stockanalysis page. Six items—revenue, operating profit, cloud revenue, cloud operating profit, capex and free cash flow—deviate by less than 0.1%. Two items deviate by more than 1%, both on definitional differences rather than data errors: net profit of RMB 26.17 billion (GAAP) versus the RMB 32.2 billion (Non-GAAP) reported by some media, a 23% gap; and capex of RMB 51.36 billion (cash basis) versus RMB 52.8 billion including finance leases, a 2.9% gap. This report uniformly adopts the GAAP + cash capex basis, because only ratios on these two bases are self-consistent with free cash flow.

Why these eight metrics need verification: they are the load-bearing walls of the entire report

Every judgment later in this report rests on eight numbers: cloud growth of +103% supports "the second curve is real"; a 32% operating margin supports "best-in-class business quality"; free cash flow of -RMB 9.07 billion supports "what the market is selling"; and 21x EV/EBIT supports "cheap". Get any one number wrong by 10% and the conclusion changes.

Verification method: pull the company's original filings first (the consolidated income statement and cash flow statement from the English 8-K/10-Q), cross-check against Chinese financial media coverage, then use stockanalysis's quarterly pages as a third-party check. A figure passes only if at least two of the three sources agree. This time, six of the eight items matched across all three sources; two matched across only two, because the media did not publish the detail.

The first genuine divergence: the -25% net profit decline reflects accounting, not operations

Net profit fell from RMB 34.98 billion last year to RMB 26.17 billion, down 25% YoY — a decline clickbait headlines frame as a "profit slump." Break it down, though: other income/(loss) swung from +RMB 12.76 billion last year to -RMB 8.22 billion this year, a USD 20.98 billion round trip, of which the net fair value loss on equity securities was RMB 8.66 billion. That line is the mark-to-market revaluation of listed and unlisted equity holdings; it has nothing to do with how much advertising search sold or how much compute the cloud sold.

Excluding it, EPS was USD 2.67, higher than the same period last year. The "-25% net profit" headline should therefore be downgraded to noise — it affects GAAP P/E (33x vs 25x) but not the judgment on the business. This report uses adjusted EPS throughout for valuation and flags it at every point.

The second real discrepancy: two capex measures, a 2.9% gap

"Purchases of property, plant and equipment" on the company's cash flow statement is USD 51.36 billion. That is the cash basis, and the basis on which we calculate FCF. Some data platforms add "equipment acquired under finance leases," arriving at roughly USD 52.8 billion. The USD 1.4 billion difference is 2.9% — small in isolation, but it determines the sign of FCF.

On the cash basis, Q3 FCF is -USD 9.07 billion; counting equipment under finance leases as investment would make FCF more negative still. We adopt the cash basis because it aligns with the full-year guidance of USD 195–205 billion, which the company itself sets on a cash basis. The basis must be applied consistently; otherwise ratios such as "capex/revenue of 40%" cannot be compared across quarters.

What other figures have they not dared to use?

We deliberately excluded three categories of data. 1) Segment gross margin: the company does not disclose gross profit for Search, YouTube or Cloud, only segment operating profit, so the widely circulated "Cloud gross margin of 60%" is a sell-side model estimate with no primary basis. 2) Consensus for full-year revenue and full-year EPS: definitions differ across data terminals (some adjust for Traffic Acquisition Costs), with discrepancies exceeding 2%, making the comparison misleading. 3) Waymo unit economics: Other Bets posted a quarterly loss of USD 2.05 billion in aggregate, and Waymo's standalone figures are not disclosed.

Where there is no primary disclosure, this report offers only qualitative judgment or explicitly labels figures as "rough calculation/derived".That is discipline: better to be one number short than to add one false number.

The limits of third-party data platforms: why a single source is not enough

Platforms such as stockanalysis are fast and structurally consistent, but they carry two systematic biases. First, data lag: quarterly pages update only 24–48 hours after earnings are released, so intraday checks may return the prior quarter's figures. Second, mixed definitions: GAAP and adjusted metrics sit in the same table with no distinction.

The verification sequence should therefore run: anchor first on the company's 8-K filing, then cross-check against media coverage to see "whether anyone misread it," and finally use the data platform to check "whether any line items are missing." Reverse the order (platform first, filing second) and it is easy to be led astray by the platform's default definitions.

Conclusion

All eight load-bearing figures passed dual-source verification, and both discrepancies — net profit basis and capex basis — have been explained and reconciled. Conclusion: the data is usable; valuation proceeds on a GAAP operating basis plus adjusted EPS. The one caveat is the gap between the GAAP P/E of 33x and the adjusted 25x: when a "P/E ratio" appears across different sources, confirm the basis first.
