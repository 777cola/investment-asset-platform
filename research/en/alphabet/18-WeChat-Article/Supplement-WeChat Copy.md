## I. First, a set of numbers that will split your mind

In the third quarter of 2026, Alphabet delivered earnings with two faces.

The good: revenue of USD 127.58 billion, up 25% year on year, marking 13 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth. Operating income of USD 40.58 billion, up 30% year on year. Operating margin of 32%, up 1 percentage point from a year earlier.

The bad: operating cash flow of USD 42.29 billion, capital expenditure of USD 51.36 billion, free cash flow of negative USD 9.07 billion. That is the second consecutive quarter in negative territory. Over the past 12 months, free cash flow totaled just USD 19.7 billion — a 0.6% yield on a USD 3.42 trillion market cap.

On the same day, the company did three more things: issued USD 28.7 billion of debt, sold 36 million additional shares (net proceeds of USD 12.4 billion), and cut buybacks from USD 11.5 billion a year earlier to 0.

Put those three together and you can probably see why the market knocked the stock down 10% after hours.

## 2. But the real bright spot is not revenue — it is cloud

The most valuable information in this earnings report is Google Cloud.

Revenue was USD 30.71 billion, up 103% year on year — it doubled. More important, cloud operating profit was USD 11.12 billion, up 209% year on year. Operating margin was 36.2% — versus only 23.7% a year earlier.

The combination of these three numbers says one thing: cloud has not just grown bigger, it has started making money, and profit is growing twice as fast as revenue.

Cloud is an asset-heavy business. Servers and data centers are bought up front; costs come first, revenue later. Once utilization passes the inflection point, each extra dollar of revenue brings far more profit than before. Alphabet is at that inflection point now.

One more number: cloud backlog was USD 56.1 billion, of which slightly more than 55% will be recognized within 24 months. Demand is backed by contracts.

## III. So what exactly is the market afraid of?

The fear is depreciation. Not that it isn't making money now, but that the money it earns two years from now will have to pay for what it is spending today.

Capex of USD 200 billion a year, depreciated over six years, adds USD 33.3 billion of cost annually. Management says it will "increase significantly" again in 2027. The depreciation peak arrives in 2027 and 2028, when operating margin could fall from 32% to 30%, or 26% at the most pessimistic.

The second fear is search. AI now delivers answers directly, and users no longer click links. Search advertising is priced per click, and no clicks means no revenue. Q3 search revenue rose 12% against a consensus of 14% — a 2 percentage point gap, the first yellow light.

The third fear is debt-funded expansion. Why would a company sitting on USD 265.1 billion in cash and securities still issue bonds and sell new shares? There is only one reasonable explanation: management expects cash to flow out steadily over the next two to three years.

## IV. Why I see this as an opportunity

Because the price already reflects the bad news.

It now trades at USD 279.84, down 30.5% from its 52-week high of USD 402.62. Note another number: it is just 2.3% above its 52-week low of USD 273.5. In other words, market sentiment is already near an extreme.

On valuation, enterprise value is about USD 3.28 trillion, or 21x 2026E EV/EBIT. What does that mean? A company growing revenue 25%, with cloud up 103% and a 32% operating margin, at 21x.

Break it down again. The search advertising business is worth USD 2.2 trillion at 15x EBIT; cloud is worth USD 1 trillion on 2027 revenue of USD 160 billion, a 25% margin and 25x EBIT. Together the two total USD 3.2 trillion.

In other words, the market is paying only for the existing search and cloud businesses. Gemini (already 1 billion app users), Waymo and external TPU sales are essentially free.

There is also a piece of good news everyone overlooks: the worst antitrust outcome — a breakup of Chrome or Android — was ruled out in the December 2025 ruling. All that remains is data sharing and a choice screen; the moat has been thinned a little, but the moat is still there.

## V. But I won't go fully invested, and here's why

Depreciation is certain to arrive; only its magnitude is uncertain.

In 2027, depreciation doubles to above USD 50 billion, which is 10 percentage points against annualized revenue of USD 500 billion. Scale effects in cloud revenue can absorb part of that, but not all of it.

There is a more troublesome issue: Waymo. Other Bets lost USD 2.05 billion this quarter, an annualized USD 8 billion or more. If its unit economics do not work over ten years, that is USD 8 billion of cash outflow every year.

So my judgment is: this is a good company, and the price is reasonable, but "reasonable" does not mean "cheap." Overpaying for a good company still loses money.

## Six, how exactly to execute

My rule is this:

At the current price of USD 279.84, open the first position, buying half the target position. I set the target position at 8% of the portfolio, so buy 4% first.

If it falls below USD 240, fill to 8%. That corresponds to roughly 18x EV/EBIT, leaving a buffer for depreciation risk.

If it rises above USD 350, do not add; start evaluating trimming.

No price stop-loss. The stock's one-year range is 273.5 to 402.62, a 47% swing; a mechanical stop would only shake us out and force us to chase back in at a higher level.

Exit on three conditions: cloud revenue growth below 30% for two consecutive quarters with capex not falling; operating margin below 26% for two consecutive quarters; search revenue growth below 8% for two consecutive quarters. Any one triggers a halving.

## Seven, a final blunt note

The most likely thing for this stock over the next year is not a surge but sideways rangebound trading — because depreciation pressure will show up quarter by quarter, while cloud revenue has to prove itself quarter by quarter.

Buying it requires some patience; you likely won't see attractive free cash flow for four to six quarters.

If you can't wait, don't buy. That's not the company's problem, it's a mismatch problem.

Conversely, if you can wait and believe that "AI revenue will eventually outrun depreciation," then Google at USD 280 is a level worth buying in tranches.

That's it. The above does not constitute investment advice; I'm just writing out how I think about it myself.
