Moat — Alphabet

Alphabet (GOOGL) | Moat

Core call: the moat is intact, but depreciation is eroding it

Moat scorecard: brand 9, cost (TPU vertical integration) 9, network effects 9, switching costs 7, licences and regulation 6, weighted 8.2. The antitrust remedy ruling dismantled none of these; it mandated only data sharing and a choice screen — the moat has been thinned a little, but it still stands. The real threat is neither Microsoft nor OpenAI; it is time. USD 200 billion of annual capex, depreciated over six years, adds USD 33 billion of cost a year, with the depreciation peak in 2027–2028. The moat determines whether it can defend revenue; depreciation determines whether it can defend profit.

Position in the value chain: from power generation through to applications

Upstream is compute and power: in-house TPUs (which remove dependence on Nvidia and are the biggest cost advantage over Microsoft and Meta), data centres (servers and networking accounted for about 65% of Q3 capex, data centres 35%) and long-term power contracts. Midstream is models and platforms: the Gemini 4 series, Search, YouTube, Android, Chrome and Google Cloud Platform. Downstream is advertisers, enterprise customers and consumers.

Vertical integration is a huge advantage when capacity is tight—no Nvidia quotas to constrain you and no Nvidia pricing to pay; it is a huge burden when demand is weak, because fixed costs cannot be exited.That is why, in October 2026, the same company was both "the only one of the four to achieve a 32% operating margin" and "the only one with negative free cash flow."

The first moat: the default entry point, thinned but not dismantled

Chrome accounts for roughly two-thirds of global browser share and Android for about 70% of the global mobile OS market, reinforced by the default search agreement with Apple. Together these three form the structure in which "users use Google even without choosing to." Once the choice-screen obligation takes effect in April 2026, European users will be able to pick a default search engine during setup—eroding some default traffic, but not changing the fact that "most people will not go and change their settings."

The DOJ's December 2025 remedies ruling stopped short of forcing a divestiture of Chrome or Android—the lightest outcome available. Set against how the 2000 Microsoft case ended (behavioural remedies rather than a breakup), Alphabet has this time received almost the same template. Legal risk is therefore cleared, leaving only compliance costs.

The second moat: TPU, the most underrated one

Globally, Alphabet is the only company that can own all three layers—training chips, models and cloud services—without building its own fabs.Q3 cloud revenue doubled, with external TPU sales a major contributor: it lowers its own costs and sells that cost advantage to others. This is a cost moat, far harder to breach than a brand moat because it takes more than a decade to build.

But this moat has a boundary: TSMC manufactures the TPU, leaving advanced-node capacity at the mercy of others; and the TPU ecosystem (software stack) is less mature than CUDA, so switching costs are high for external customers.The ceiling on external TPU sales is therefore set not by performance but by ecosystem. That is also why it currently sells mainly to a handful of large customers rather than opening up fully.

The most dangerous rival: not Microsoft, but depreciation

Microsoft has OpenAI exclusively tied in, Amazon holds the larger cloud share, and OpenAI has captured the mindshare for "conversational search." But all three face the same problem as Alphabet: when does the money put in come back. The real opponent is time — USD 200 billion of annual capex, depreciated over six years, turns into USD 33 billion of new cost each year, and that number will start to pressure operating margin in 2027.

The second threat is search cannibalising itself: if AI answers directly and users stop clicking links, advertising's billing basis (clicks) will collapse before traffic does. Q3 search +12% (expected +14%) shows this has not happened yet, but "not yet" does not mean "never." Of the two threats, depreciation is certain and substitution is uncertain — so position sizing should be built around the certain one.

The economic test of a moat: does ROIC exceed the cost of capital?

The ultimate test of a moat is not "will users leave" but "can invested capital earn a return above the cost of capital." A rough calculation: 2026 invested capital of about RMB 200 billion (capex) plus existing assets, annualised operating profit of about RMB 160 billion, or about RMB 125 billion after tax. On invested capital of RMB 600–700 billion, that implies ROIC of 18%–20%, clearly above the 8%–9% weighted cost of capital.

The issue is at the margin: if the new RMB 200 billion earns only a 15% pre-tax return, it dilutes overall ROIC. Cloud operating margin rising from 23.7% to 36.2% shows new investment is currently earning a decent return, but whether it falls below 20% once depreciation ramps is the key question for 2027.

The test is fixed: if ROIC falls below 12% in 2028, the moat has failed to convert into economic returns—at that point, even if the business survives, the valuation should be that of a utility.

The relationship between moat and price

A moat score of 8.2 measures business quality, not stock quality. Business quality determines whether the company can make money over the long run; price determines whether the investment makes money. Buying an 8.2 moat at 21x EV/EBIT is reasonable; at 35x you are overpaying for the moat.

The dividend yield makes this clear: a quarterly dividend of USD 0.21, or USD 0.84 annualized, is 0.3% of the USD 279.84 share price—almost no shareholder return. Everything you pay for is therefore "future growth + future buyback resumption." The moat protects "growth can be delivered"; it does not protect "the price you paid is reasonable."

Conclusion

Moat 8.2/10, among the thickest in large-cap tech, with legal risk now cleared.Conclusion: the moat supports "hold for the long term"; depreciation does not support "hold a full position."Start at one-half; wait for the 2027 depreciation data before deciding whether to fill out or halve.
