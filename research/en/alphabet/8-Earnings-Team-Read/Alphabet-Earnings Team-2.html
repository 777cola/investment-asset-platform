Earnings Deep Dive (Buffett's Lens) — Alphabet

The income statement is for others to see; the cash flow statement is for yourself.

Core view

The best part of this earnings report is the operating margin: 32%, up 1 percentage point even as capex doubled. The worst part is the cash flow statement: operating cash flow of RMB 42.29 billion, capex of RMB 51.36 billion, free cash flow of -RMB 9.07 billion. My reading order for financial statements is cash flow statement first, then balance sheet, and income statement last, because the first two cannot lie. This quarter's cash flow statement tells me this company is using today's cash to buy tomorrow's revenue, and how far away tomorrow is, the financial statements do not say.

Margin 32%: I would apply a discount to that

A 32% operating margin is impressive, but I know how it came about: depreciation on RMB 200 billion of capex has not yet been recognized at scale. Servers are depreciated over six years and data centers over fifteen, so money invested in 2026 only becomes a cost on the income statement in 2027–2028. So today's 32% is a point-in-time figure where revenue has arrived but costs have not.

I test this with a simple approach: if depreciation as a share of revenue doubles from its current level, how much would the operating margin fall? A rough calculation puts the drop at 8–10 percentage points. That means 22%–24%. At that level, 21x EV/EBIT is no longer cheap. So I do not see 32% as the "new normal"; I see it as "near the peak."

Cash flow: three moves at once, and the nature of the game changes

If I looked only at free cash flow of -RMB 9.07 billion, I would say "investment phase, normal." But put three things together: buybacks falling from RMB 11.5 billion to 0, RMB 28.7 billion in bond issuance, and 36 million new shares issued. These are not standard moves for an "investment phase" — they are standard moves for "cash is tight but we don't want to say so."

With RMB 265.1 billion in cash and securities sitting on the books, why issue bonds and shares? The reasonable explanation: management expects cash burn over the next two to three years to exceed what the cash on hand can comfortably cover. That is an implicit guidance for 2027–2028, more credible than any public statement. I will note it down, and then demand a lower price.

Cloud: this is the only part I would pay for

Three cloud numbers together: revenue RMB 30.71 billion, +103%; operating income RMB 11.12 billion, +209%; margin 36.2%. Operating income growing at twice the rate of revenue is the cleanest evidence of scale effects. Every good business I have bought has this trait — railroads, utilities, credit card networks — once scale builds, margins move on their own.

But one thing to watch: how much of cloud revenue is external TPU sales? Selling chips and selling compute are two different businesses — the former carries high gross margin and poor durability, the latter low gross margin and strong stickiness. The company did not break this out, which is what I dislike most about this earnings report.

Conclusion

Income statement: 9 points. Cash flow statement: 5 points. Conclusion: the company is worth owning, but the price is not cheap enough. I will buy half at USD 280 and hold the rest for two quarters — if FCF turns positive in the first half of 2027, I will admit I was wrong and add to the position; if it stays negative, I will stop at half.
