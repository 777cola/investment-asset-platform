Edited and polished: Two faces in one earnings report — Alphabet

Core view

Alphabet's latest quarterly report was its most contradictory in years: a record income statement and an alarming cash flow statement. Revenue came in at USD 127.58 billion, up 25% YoY, operating income at USD 40.58 billion, up 30%, and operating margin at 32% — up 1 percentage point even as capex doubled YoY. The other side of the same report: operating cash flow of USD 42.29 billion, capex of USD 51.36 billion and free cash flow of -USD 9.07 billion. Same company, same quarter, two completely different faces.

First, the good news: the cloud business is finally profitable

Google Cloud Q3 revenue USD 30.71 billion, +103% YoY; operating income USD 11.12 billion, +209% YoY; operating margin 36.2% versus just 23.7% a year ago. Together these three numbers say one thing: the cloud business has not just grown bigger, it has become more profitable, and profit is growing twice as fast as revenue.

This is the cleanest evidence of scale effects. Cloud is an asset-heavy business: servers and data centers are bought up front, costs come first and revenue later; once utilization crosses the inflection point, each additional dollar of revenue carries a far higher marginal profit than in the early phase. Alphabet is at that inflection point now. Backlog stands at USD 56.1 billion, of which slightly more than 55% will be recognized within 24 months — demand is backed by contracts.

On the other side, advertising is still growing but slowing: Search and other USD 63.35 billion, +12% (market expected +14%); YouTube USD 11.21 billion, +9.2%. Advertising is decelerating while cloud is accelerating — that is the single most important sentence of this quarter.

Now the bad side: where did the money go

Capex of USD 51.36 billion, up 114% YoY, with full-year guidance of 195–205 billion and a further "significant increase" in 2027. That puts 2026 capex at 40% of revenue. Apple's ratio, by comparison, is under 3%. Alphabet is turning from an asset-light advertising company into an asset-heavy infrastructure company.

Spend at this level and cash flow turns negative: free cash flow of -9.07 billion, negative for two consecutive quarters and just 19.7 billion over the past 12 months — a 0.6% yield on a 3.42 trillion market cap. More telling still are the financing moves: USD 28.7 billion of senior unsecured notes issued in Q3, 36 million shares sold for net proceeds of 12.4 billion, and buybacks cut from 11.5 billion a year earlier to 0.0.

Why would a company holding USD 265.1 billion in cash and securities issue debt, sell stock and halt buybacks? There is only one reasonable explanation: management expects cash burn to keep exceeding operating cash flow for the next two to three years. That is the most credible implicit guidance for 2027–2028 — more credible than anything said on the earnings call.

Don't be fooled by net profit

The headline reads "net profit plunges 25%", but the actual figure is USD 26.17 billion. The reason is simple: other gains and losses swung from +USD 12.76 billion last year to -USD 8.22 billion this year, including a net fair value loss of USD 8.66 billion on equity securities. This is a mark-to-market revaluation of equity holdings; not a cent of cash moved. Excluding it, EPS is USD 2.67, higher than the same period last year.

This is why you read the cash flow statement first. Accounting rules can dress up profit; cash flow rarely can. This quarter's cash flow tells you not that "the company earned less" but that "the company put all its money to work."

So how much is this company worth?

At USD 279.84 the stock carries a USD 3.42 trillion market cap, 30.5% below its 52-week high of 402.62 and just 2.3% above its 52-week low of 273.5 — the market has already priced it as an AI-capex loser. Yet 21x EV/EBIT implies 25% revenue growth, a 36.2% cloud margin and USD 56.1 billion of backlog.

Breaking it down: search advertising at 15x EBIT is worth USD 2.2 trillion; cloud, on 2027 revenue of USD 160 billion, a 25% operating margin and 25x EBIT, is worth USD 1 trillion — USD 3.2 trillion combined. In other words, the market is paying only for existing search and cloud, and essentially nothing for Gemini, Waymo or external TPU sales. The worst antitrust outcome — a Chrome/Android breakup — was already ruled out by the December 2025 ruling.

The conclusion: this is not a question of whether it is expensive, but of whether you believe a 30% operating margin survives 2027 depreciation. If you do, this is the opportunity; if not, wait for USD 240.

Conclusion

One earnings report, two faces: the income statement shows the business has improved, the cash flow statement shows it has gotten heavier. Rating: Buy, in tranches. Initial position one-half; fill to full below USD 240. The only sell trigger is cloud growth falling below 30% without capex coming down—that would mean the RMB 200 billion was spent wrong.
