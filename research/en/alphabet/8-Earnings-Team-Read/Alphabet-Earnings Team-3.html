Earnings Deep Dive (Munger's Lens) — Alphabet

Forget growth for now — work out when the costs arrive

Core view

Three numbers in this earnings report are real; the rest is explanation. The three real ones: capex RMB 51.36 billion, free cash flow -RMB 9.07 billion, cloud operating profit RMB 11.12 billion. The other numbers—net profit -25%, EPS 2.12, revenue below the consensus of RMB 400 million—are noise or accounting. My method for reading earnings reports: find the irreversible numbers and discard everything else.

Irreversible figure one: RMB 51.36 billion spent

Money spent does not come back. RMB 200 billion a year, RMB 1 trillion over five years. If that money buys servers with a six-year life, it means RMB 33.3 billion a year in amortization; if it buys data centers with a fifteen-year life, RMB 13.3 billion a year. Either way, depreciation doubling to over RMB 50 billion by 2027 is a matter of arithmetic, not judgment.

I have seen too many companies get this wrong: they underwrite the investment on the assumption that demand will keep rising, then discover at depreciation time that demand rose for only two years. That is how the telecom industry died in 2000 — the fiber was laid, the traffic never came. Alphabet is in far better shape than telecom was then (it has real cloud revenue), but the math is the same.

Irreversible figure two: free cash flow of -RMB 9.07 billion

This number needs no explanation: it means the company generated no free cash this quarter. Negative for two consecutive quarters, leaving only RMB 19.7 billion over the past 12 months — 0.6% of a RMB 3.42 trillion market cap. A company growing 25% that generates a free cash flow yield of just 0.6% has historically done so at cycle tops.

I am not saying this is definitely the top. I am saying: buying at this level means more than half of what you pay is a bet on the future. Betting on the future is not wrong; mistaking the bet for fact is. So keep the position small and the entry price low.

Noise: net profit -25% tells you nothing

Net profit fell 25%, and media headlines called it a "profit collapse." But this stems from the fair value of equity securities swinging from +RMB 8.6 billion to -RMB 8.7 billion — a RMB 17.3 billion round trip. It is a book entry; not a single cent of cash moved. Anyone selling the stock on this news is paying tuition to accounting rules.

The reverse holds too: if equities rise next quarter, net profit will suddenly "surge 40%," and no one should get excited then either. This company requires watching only four numbers: cloud revenue growth, cloud margin, capex, and free cash flow. Everything else can be ignored.

Conclusion

Four numbers tell the story: cloud +103% (good), cloud margin 36.2% (very good), capex RMB 51.36 billion (very expensive), FCF -RMB 9.07 billion (very poor). Conclusion: two good, two bad — price decides everything. 21x EV/EBIT is a fair price, not a cheap one, so buy only half.
