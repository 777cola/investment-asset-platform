Earnings Deep Dive (Duan Yongping's Lens) — Alphabet

First check whether the business still makes money, then look at how much it has spent

Core view

When I read financial statements, I look at two things first: whether the revenue is real and whether the profit is real. This quarter, both are real — revenue of RMB 127.58 billion, +25%, and operating profit of RMB 40.58 billion, +30%, achieved even with capex doubling. What really made me stop was capex: RMB 51.36 billion, 40.2% of revenue. That number tells me this business is no longer the business I knew ten years ago.

Revenue: advertising still growing, cloud soaring

Search RMB 63.35 billion, +12%; YouTube RMB 11.21 billion, +9.2%; Cloud RMB 30.71 billion, +103%. The two advertising lines together total RMB 74.56 billion and are still growing, which is no small feat — the base is already RMB 300 billion annualized. Cloud doubling does not surprise me; what surprises me is Cloud's margin: 36.2%, versus only 23.7% last year.

From running businesses I know one thing: when a business goes from loss to profit, the hardest part is crossing the scale inflection point. Once across, every extra dollar of revenue grows profit faster than revenue. Cloud is now at that point — operating profit +209% year on year, far faster than revenue +103%. This is real operating leverage, not accounting.

Spending: what does 40% of capex mean?

Capex of RMB 51.36 billion, up 114% year on year, with full-year guidance of RMB 195–205 billion. That is 40% of revenue. For comparison: Apple's capex is under 3% of revenue, and Moutai barely needs to invest to expand capacity. Alphabet now looks more like a power utility. What defines a utility? Huge upfront investment, stable returns later, and margins capped by regulation.

I am not saying the decision is wrong. Cloud demand is real, and the backlog of RMB 56.1 billion is real. I am only saying: the valuation method for this business has to change. It used to be a "money printer," deserving 25–30x PE; now it is a "water plant builder," and 15–20x is more reasonable. The market currently gives it 21x EV/EBIT, right inside that range—so I would not call it cheap enough to rush in.

One detail I care about: why the buyback stopped

Last year Q3 buybacks were RMB 11.5 billion; this year, 0. At the same time, RMB 28.7 billion in bonds were issued and 36 million shares were placed. Put these three things together, and my first reaction is not "short of cash" but "management believes the next two years of cash all need to be invested." This is a very strong signal, stronger than anything said on any earnings call.

I am not against investing money; I am against investing money without regard to returns. So I set a rule for myself: buy only half at this price, and wait for the Q1 and Q2 2027 cloud growth and depreciation data for the rest. If the data confirms, add; if not, don't. This is not hesitation, this is discipline.

Conclusion

A good quarter, but not a "buy with eyes closed" quarter. Conclusion: scale in, half-size initial position, fill up below USD 240. Still a good business, but it has shifted from asset-light to asset-heavy, so the valuation multiple needs to step down a notch.
