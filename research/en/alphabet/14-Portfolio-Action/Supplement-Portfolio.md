## Why 8%, not 15%

On business quality alone, Alphabet deserves 15%. But the binding constraint in portfolio management is not "the best company gets the most money" — it is "the worst misjudgment must not cause serious damage." If 2027 depreciation compresses the operating margin to 26% and cloud growth slows to 30%, the bear-case target price is USD 200, 28.5% below 279.84. An 8% position implies -2.3% for the portfolio, which is bearable; a 15% position implies -4.3%, enough to start affecting annual performance.

Concentration is the other constraint: if we also hold Microsoft, Amazon and Meta, all four are exposed to the same direction on the AI capex theme. Combined, 8% plus each of their positions could easily exceed 25% — that is not diversification, it is one big bet. So my target for Alphabet is 8%, provided combined exposure stays below 12%.

## Adding, trimming, stopping out: three price lines plus three thesis lines

Price levels: open a 4% position at 279.84; add to a full 8% at 240; cap at 10% below 200; no adding above 350. These four levels are discipline, not forecasts — act when price reaches them, and stay put when it does not.

The thesis levels matter more and take priority over the price levels: ① cloud growth below 30% for two consecutive quarters with capex guidance not cut → halve; ② operating margin below 26% for two consecutive quarters → halve; ③ search growth below 8% for two consecutive quarters → halve. If any two of the three trigger, exit and re-underwrite. No mechanical price stop-loss — the stock traded 273.5–402.62 over the past year, and a mechanical stop would shake you out and force you to chase back in at a higher level.

## Rebalancing: When to Sell

Three sell scenarios, in priority order: ① Thesis invalidated (the three points above); ② Valuation overshoot — above USD 450, equivalent to 32x 2027 EPS and above the upper end of the historical range, trim to 4%; ③ Position imbalance — if gains push the weight above 12%, trim back to 8%.

The no-sell scenarios must also be spelled out: do not sell on negative single-quarter FCF, do not sell on EU fines, do not sell on net profit decline from equity gains/losses, do not sell if the stock falls to 240 (that is an add point). Writing down the reasons not to sell in advance means no on-the-spot judgment is needed when sentiment is at its worst.

## Stress test: portfolio impact under three scenarios

Assume a total portfolio market value of 100 with an 8% Alphabet position. In the base case the stock reaches 350 (+25.1%), adding +2.0% to the portfolio; in the bull case it reaches 450 (+60.8%), adding +4.9%; in the bear case it falls to 200 (-28.5%), dragging the portfolio down -2.3%.

With a 4% initial position, the impact in all three scenarios halves: +1.0% / +2.4% / -1.1%. That is the mathematical point of scaling in — it compresses the worst-case portfolio impact from -2.3% to -1.1%, at the cost of compressing the best case from +4.9% to +2.4%.

Now add correlation: hold Microsoft, Amazon and Meta at 5% each alongside it and combined exposure to the AI capex theme is 23%. In the bear case — a broad de-rating of the AI trade — all four fall 30% simultaneously and the portfolio impact is -6.9%; that is the number that actually needs managing, not the 8% in a single stock.

## Why not buy to full allocation in one go: the relationship with cash

If the target is 8%, buy 4% at the current price and hold the remaining 4% in cash waiting for USD 240. The opportunity cost is that "if the stock does not fall, the remaining 4% can only be put into something else or sit idle." For a name with "negative free cash flow over the next 4–6 quarters," though, holding cash is reasonable—it gives you the ability to add on bad news.

In other words, scaling in is not just risk control; it is also preserving optionality. A fully invested investor can only watch at USD 200; a half-position investor can buy.
