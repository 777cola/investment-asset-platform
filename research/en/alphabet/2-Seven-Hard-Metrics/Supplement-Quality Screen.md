# Alphabet（GOOGL）— Quality Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/30
- Price：279.84 USD
- Market cap：About USD 3.42 trillion

## Notes

Metric definitions: revenue growth is GAAP YoY; gross margin is approximate, calculated as revenue less cost of revenue (including traffic acquisition costs), and is marked "approx." because full 10-Q detail was unavailable; net margin is shown on both a GAAP basis and a basis excluding gains and losses on equity securities; ROE is approximate, calculated as trailing-four-quarter net income divided by shareholders' equity at the end of the third quarter; operating cash flow/net income uses single-quarter data to avoid seasonality; free cash flow = operating cash flow − capex (company definition); net cash excludes short-term debt and operating lease liabilities.

## Six cleared the screen, and not merely cleared it: they beat the threshold by a wide margin

Revenue growth of +25% is 2.5x the 10% threshold; gross margin of about 57% is 1.4x the 40% bar; net margin is 25.5% versus 15%; ROE is about 30% versus 15%; operating cash flow runs at 1.62x net income; and net cash stands at USD 140 billion. All six clear the bar by a wide margin, not merely scrape past it.

The most telling is operating cash flow at 1.62x net income. Q3 net income was depressed to USD 26.17 billion by a USD 8.66 billion fair-value loss on equity securities, yet operating cash flow came in at USD 42.29 billion—evidence that earnings contain little "water" and the paper loss did not actually drain cash. This is the key metric separating "accounting profit" from "real money," and it is where many high-growth companies stumble.

## The one that failed: negative FCF, but the driver is deliberate investment rather than operational deterioration

Q3 operating cash flow was RMB 42.29 billion against capex of RMB 51.36 billion, leaving free cash flow negative at RMB 9.07 billion — the second consecutive quarter in negative territory. On this metric alone, Alphabet would be screened out outright, which is exactly what hard metrics are for: they force you to explain where the money went.

It went to three places: servers and networking (about 65% of Q3 capex), data centers and land-use rights (about 35%), and TPU inventory build (one reason operating cash flow fell YoY). This is capacity buildout, not maintenance spending. Maintenance capex is roughly 30%; the remaining 70% is incremental compute, in theory corresponding to future incremental revenue. The verdict should therefore read "a fail, but not fatal."

## Not fatal, but worth underlining: when does this clause turn fatal?

If any two of the three conditions hold, negative FCF turns from "investment" into "cash burn": ① cloud revenue growth falls below 30%, meaning the compute it bought cannot be sold; ② operating margin drops below 28% after depreciation steps up, meaning the return on new capacity is below the cost of capital; ③ the company keeps plugging cash flow with debt and equity issuance (it issued RMB 28.7 billion of debt in Q3 and raised RMB 12.4 billion net from 36 million new shares) rather than self-funding from operating cash flow.

Only half of the third condition holds for now: the company is indeed raising external financing, but the scale is small relative to its RMB 265.1 billion cash pile, looking more like "financing into an attractive rate window" than "being short of money." Once conditions ① and ② appear, this hard metric turns from yellow to red, and there will be no need to wait for the annual report — the Q2 results should trigger a trim.

## What this screen actually rules out

The seven metrics are built for a "ten-minute elimination", not a "ten-minute selection". Alphabet clearing six means it no longer has to spend time being eliminated — it can go straight into deep research. What the screen eliminates are the companies that tell a story off a single metric: high revenue growth on a 15% gross margin, ROE propped up by leverage, operating cash flow sitting below net profit for years, or relentless capacity expansion with no cash on the books.

Take this round's AI concept stocks: CoreWeave-type names grow revenue far faster than Alphabet, but depreciation and power eat into gross margin, free cash flow stays negative for years and expansion must be debt-funded; Alphabet's FCF is negative because it has money to invest, theirs because they cannot survive without investing. The same "fail" carries an entirely different meaning.
