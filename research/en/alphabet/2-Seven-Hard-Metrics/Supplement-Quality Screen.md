# Alphabet（GOOGL）— Quality Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/30
- Price：279.84 USD
- Market cap：About USD 3.42 trillion

## Notes

Metric definitions: revenue growth uses GAAP year-over-year; gross margin is a rough calculation as "revenue − cost of revenue (including traffic acquisition costs)," marked "approx." because full 10-Q detail was unavailable; net margin is given on both a GAAP basis and a basis excluding gains and losses on equity securities; ROE is a rough calculation using trailing-four-quarter net income / shareholders' equity at the end of the third quarter; operating cash flow/net income uses single-quarter data to avoid seasonality; free cash flow = operating cash flow − capital expenditure (company definition); net cash excludes short-term debt and operating lease liabilities.

## Seven hard metrics to weed out the weak (2026 Q3 data)

| Indicator | Company figures | Threshold | Determination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | USD 127.58 billion, +25% YoY (13 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth) | ≥10% | Through |
| Gross margin | Approximately 57% (rough calculation based on the 2025 cost structure, with traffic acquisition costs included in operating costs) | ≥40% | Passed |
| Net margin | GAAP 20.5%; 25.5% excluding gains and losses on equity securities | ≥15% | Through |
| ROE | Approximately 30% (last four quarters' net profit / shareholders' equity at end of Q3, rough calculation) | ≥15% | Passed |

## The six that passed: not just passed, but cleared the threshold by a wide margin

Revenue growth of +25% against the 10% threshold is 2.5x; gross margin of about 57% against 40% is 1.4x; net margin of 25.5% against 15%; ROE of about 30% against 15%; operating cash flow at 1.62x net income; net cash of USD 140 billion. **None of these six merely clears the bar—all exceed it by a wide margin.**

The most telling is operating cash flow at 1.62x net income. Q3 net income was depressed to USD 26.17 billion by a USD 8.66 billion fair-value loss on equity securities, yet operating cash flow came in at USD 42.29 billion—**showing that earnings contain little "water" and the paper loss did not actually drain cash.** This is the key metric separating "accounting profit" from "real money," and it is where many high-growth companies stumble.

## The one that didn't pass: FCF was negative, but the nature is "deliberate investment," not "operational deterioration"

Q3 operating cash flow was RMB 42.29 billion, capex RMB 51.36 billion, and free cash flow negative RMB 9.07 billion. This is the second consecutive quarter in negative territory. **On this metric alone, Alphabet would be screened out outright — which is exactly what hard metrics are for: they force you to explain where the money went.**

The money went to three places: servers and networking (about 65% of Q3 capex), data centers and land-use rights (about 35%), and TPU inventory build (one reason operating cash flow fell year on year). **This is capacity buildout, not maintenance spending.** Maintenance capex is roughly 30%, and the remaining 70% is incremental compute, in theory corresponding to future incremental revenue. So the verdict should read "a fail, but not fatal."

## Not fatal, but worth underlining: when does this clause turn fatal?

**If any two of the three conditions hold, negative FCF turns from "investment" into "cash burn":** ① cloud revenue growth falls below 30%, meaning the compute it bought cannot be sold; ② operating margin drops below 28% after depreciation steps up, meaning the return on new capacity is below the cost of capital; ③ the company keeps plugging cash flow with debt and equity issuance (it issued RMB 28.7 billion of debt in Q3 and raised RMB 12.4 billion net from 36 million new shares) rather than self-funding from operating cash flow.

Only half of the third condition holds for now — the company is indeed raising external financing, but the scale is small relative to its RMB 265.1 billion cash pile, looking more like "financing into an attractive rate window" than "being short of money." **When conditions ① and ② appear, this hard metric turns from yellow to red, and there will be no need to wait for the annual report — the Q2 results should trigger a trim.**

## What this screen actually rules out

The seven metrics are designed for a "ten-minute elimination", not a "ten-minute selection". Alphabet passing six means it no longer needs to spend time being eliminated — it can go straight into deep research. **What it eliminates are the companies that tell a story off a single metric**: the ones with high revenue growth but 15% gross margin, the ones whose ROE is propped up by leverage, the ones whose operating cash flow sits below net profit for years, the ones that keep expanding capacity hard with no cash on the books.

Compare this round's AI concept stocks: CoreWeave-type companies grow revenue far faster than Alphabet, but gross margin is eaten by depreciation and power, free cash flow is negative for years, and they must expand on debt; **Alphabet's FCF is negative because it has money to invest, theirs is negative because they cannot survive without investing.** The same "fail" carries an entirely different meaning.
