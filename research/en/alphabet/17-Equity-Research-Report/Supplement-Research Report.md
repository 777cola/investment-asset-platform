## Chapter 1 Company Overview

Alphabet is Google's parent company; its 2015 reorganization created a three-tier structure of "Google Services + Google Cloud + Other Bets." **Q3 2026 revenue was USD 127.58 billion, of which Search and other was USD 63.35 billion, YouTube ads USD 11.21 billion, Google Network USD 7.46 billion, Google Cloud USD 30.71 billion, and Other Bets USD 372 million.** Headquartered in Mountain View, California, with roughly 200,000 employees worldwide; under the dual-class share structure the founders hold about 51% of voting power.

Share price USD 279.84 (close of October 30, 2026, -1.4%), market cap USD 3.42 trillion, total shares 12.22 billion (A+B+C). 52-week range 273.5–402.62, -30.5% from the high, +2.3% from the low.

The main line of business evolution is clear: the 2015 reorganization was meant to let "Google" focus on advertising and let "Other Bets" raise capital independently; a decade later, it is instead "Cloud" that has grown from within Google into a second curve, while Other Bets is still burning cash. **Alphabet in 2026 is no longer the structure it was at the reorganization—Cloud accounts for 24.1% of revenue and 27.4% of operating profit, and Other Bets' losses are less than 5%.**

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

The internet industry is undergoing its third structural shift: from "users seeking information" to "information seeking users." **Profit in the value chain concentrates at both ends—upstream compute chips (NVIDIA gross margin above 70%) and downstream monetization (advertising operating margin around 40%)—while the model layer in between is loss-making across almost the entire industry.**

Position in the industry cycle: year three of AI capex investment, year one of depreciation recognition. **The four major North American cloud providers will spend several hundred billion USD in combined capex in 2026, with further increases in 2027; depreciation cost recognition concentrates in 2027–2028.** Divergence has begun: CoreWeave has retreated more than 70% from its high, and Oracle's free cash flow will not turn positive until the second half of fiscal 2028. **The market is distinguishing "compute backed by cash flow" from "compute backed by financing"—Alphabet belongs to the former.**

The growth rate of the advertising market itself is a constraint. Global digital advertising growth has slowed from double digits to high single digits; Alphabet's search advertising still manages +12%, driven by share gains and higher unit prices, not by industry tailwinds. **This means two things: first, its growth will increasingly depend on "taking budget from others"; second, in a macro downturn its advertising revenue will decline faster than the industry.**

One additional industry observation: **in 2026, incremental cloud market growth comes almost entirely from AI training and inference, while traditional IT migration to the cloud has slowed to single-digit growth.** This is both an opportunity (large increment) and a risk (a single source of increment)—if AI demand slows in 2027, the cloud market will lose its only growth engine.

## Chapter 3 Business model

Alphabet sells three kinds of "inventory": **intent** (search keywords, near-zero marginal cost), **attention** (YouTube video, content costs externalized), and **compute** (cloud, high fixed costs, utilization determines profit). The three inventories have completely different cost structures, mapping to three margins: Google Services operating margin 40.7%, Google Cloud 36.2%, and Other Bets deeply loss-making.

Vertical integration is the core feature: **in-house TPU (chips) → self-built data centers (infrastructure) → Gemini (models) → Search/YouTube/Workspace (applications) → advertising and subscriptions (monetization).** No second company globally owns all five links. This is a huge advantage when capacity is tight (free from Nvidia allocation constraints) and a huge burden when demand is insufficient (fixed costs cannot be exited).

Both ends of unit economics deserve clarity. On the search side: revenue per query minus traffic acquisition cost leaves gross profit, and this portion carries almost no variable cost. **On the cloud side: revenue per GPU/TPU hour minus power, depreciation and O&M leaves gross profit, and this portion swings sharply with utilization.** One company, two completely different cost curves — this is the hardest part of analyzing it.

One revenue item is easily overlooked: **external sales of TPU systems are booked into cloud, not into hardware.** This means cloud revenue gross margin blends in some "equipment sales" revenue, whose sustainability is weaker than "selling compute." The company does not break it out, so this is a gray area in valuation.

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

Income statement: revenue RMB 127.58 billion (+25%), operating profit RMB 40.58 billion (+30%), operating margin 31.8% (+1.3pct). **Net profit of RMB 26.17 billion (-25%) is pure accounting noise—fair value on equity securities swung from +RMB 8.6 billion to -RMB 8.7 billion, a RMB 17.3 billion round trip.** Excluding it, EPS was USD 2.67, up year on year.

Cash flow statement: operating cash flow RMB 42.29 billion (-12.6%), capex RMB 51.36 billion (+114%), free cash flow -RMB 9.07 billion (versus +RMB 24.46 billion last year). **FCF over the past 12 months is down to RMB 19.7 billion, a 0.6% yield on the RMB 3.42 trillion market cap.** Balance sheet: cash and securities RMB 265.1 billion, long-term debt RMB 125.1 billion, net cash RMB 140 billion—no near-term risk, but the direction is weakening.

A rough return-on-capital calculation: **annualized operating profit of about RMB 160 billion, roughly RMB 125 billion after tax, on invested capital of RMB 600–700 billion, puts ROIC at 18%–20%, well above the 8%–9% weighted cost of capital.** That shows capital allocation over the past decade has been effective. The risk is at the margin: if the incremental RMB 200 billion earns only a 15% pre-tax return, it will dilute overall ROIC.

## Chapter 5 Management

CEO Sundar Pichai (since 2015) took three years to move the company from "behind on AI" to "cloud revenue doubled, Gemini at 1 billion users." **Execution: 9.0/10. Total 2024 compensation was USD 10.7 million with no stock awards; his main wealth comes from shares already held, so incentives align with shareholders.**

Capital allocation is the markdown item: **2026 capex of USD 195–205 billion, USD 28.7 billion in debt issued, 36 million shares issued for net proceeds of USD 12.4 billion, and buybacks cut from USD 11.5 billion to zero.** A company with USD 140 billion in net cash doing all three at once is not internally consistent—either it does not need money or it does, not both. On governance, founders hold about 51% of voting power through Class B shares; outside shareholders have no veto, and correction mechanisms are absent. **Overall management score: 8/10.**

Organization adds another variable: the efficiency of 200,000 employees. **AI's first value to Alphabet is not selling to others but transforming itself—customer service, moderation and sales operations, the repetitive roles, are the first replaced by internal models.** These cost savings do not show up in the financials, but they surface in revenue-per-employee gains of "revenue up 20%, headcount flat."

Succession is not a near-term problem but a latent one. **Pichai is around 58, and the No. 2s (President Porat, CFO Ashkenazi) each have less than five years of tenure.** If the central figure leaves, the board must either hire externally or hand over to someone unfamiliar with the AI capex cycle—a real governance risk at the 2027 depreciation peak.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Four drivers, ranked by certainty. **① Cloud: revenue +103%, margin 36.2%, backlog 56.1 billion** — the most certain, because demand is backed by contracts and capacity. **② AI improves ad efficiency: AI Overviews and Gemini raise conversion rates, supporting search pricing** — already happening, but the direction is uncertain (it could also reduce click volume). **③ TPU external sales: from cost center to revenue line** — scale undisclosed, high optionality. **④ Waymo and Gemini subscriptions: options** — not included in valuation.

**The first driver is the most critical.** If Cloud sustains 55% growth in 2027, Cloud revenue reaches 195 billion and operating profit about 49 billion, enough to offset the 25 billion rise in depreciation. **If growth falls to 30%, Cloud revenue is only 160 billion and operating profit about 32 billion, which is not enough to offset it — operating margin would then fall to 26%.**

The underappreciated fourth driver is subscriptions and hardware. **Subscription revenue from Google One, YouTube Premium and Workspace sits inside "Google Services" and is not disclosed separately, but it is one of the highest-gross-margin segments.** If a premium tier of Gemini can be built into a subscription (similar to ChatGPT Plus), it is a growth source that consumes no capex — exactly the hedge against depreciation pressure.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

The Big Four landscape: **Alphabet (search + cloud + models + chips), Microsoft (software + cloud + OpenAI tie-up), Amazon (e-commerce + cloud + Trainium), Meta (social advertising + in-house AI).** Alphabet's differentiation lies in "winning across all four links" and its in-house TPU; both Microsoft and Amazon depend on Nvidia, while Meta has no external cloud business.

Among the challengers, the most dangerous is not a big tech company but **OpenAI**: it has captured the mindshare of "conversational search." Though it has no proprietary compute and no distribution gateway, its brand and developer ecosystem are strong. **Alphabet's response is to embed Gemini into existing gateways (Chrome, Android, Search, Workspace), using distribution to counter brand.** 1 billion app users show this approach is working for now.

One easily overlooked player in the competitive landscape: Apple. **Google pays Apple tens of billions of dollars a year for the default search slot — a cost that is also part of its moat.** If renewal terms deteriorate in 2027 (a price hike or opening the slot to multi-party bidding), Alphabet's traffic cost and gateway stability would both suffer. This is the line to watch most closely in the "competition" chapter.

Meta offers another contrast: **all of its AI investment is for internal use; Q3 capex was RMB 33.94 billion and free cash flow was -RMB 2.12 billion, with no external cloud business.** That means for the same money, Meta can only monetize through advertising ROI, while Alphabet can charge directly through cloud. That is the difference between having a second growth curve and not.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

- **Depreciation risk (high probability):** Depreciation doubles to over RMB 50 billion in 2027; operating margin could fall from 32% to 30% (bear case 26%).
- **Cloud growth risk (low probability, high impact):** Cloud growth drops below 30% while capex stays flat, meaning the compute it bought cannot be sold.
- **Search substitution risk (existential):** AI answers queries directly, click volume declines. Q3 search +12% (expected +14%) is the first yellow light.
- **Regulatory risk (low impact):** Cumulative EU fines under EUR 8.5 billion; ad-tech breakup affects about 3% of market cap.
- **Governance risk:** Founder holds 51% voting power; outside shareholders have no means of correction.

**The five risks rank by probability × impact. The first two are the ones that truly need monitoring; the other three are already priced in.**

The risks amplify one another. **The worst chain: AI demand disappoints → cloud growth drops to 30% → compute utilization falls → depreciation continues, margin drops to 26% → the company keeps investing to sustain growth → FCF negative for three consecutive years → rating downgrade, financing costs rise → forced to cut spending and take impairments.** Five leading indicators: cloud growth, backlog, depreciation as a share of revenue, FCF, credit spreads.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026E revenue RMB 495 billion (+24%), operating profit RMB 158 billion (31.9%), adjusted EPS USD 11.0, capex RMB 200 billion, free cash flow about -RMB 5 billion. **2027E revenue RMB 605 billion (+22%), operating profit RMB 181 billion (29.9%), adjusted EPS USD 14.0, free cash flow about +RMB 10 billion.**

Three key assumptions: **1) cloud +100% in 2026, +55% in 2027; 2) advertising +11% in 2026, +9% in 2027; 3) depreciation as a share of revenue rising from about 5% in 2026 to about 9% in 2027.** Of the three, the third is the most certain (mathematics), the first the least certain (demand), the second in between. **The biggest source of forecast error is the first: every 10-percentage-point difference in cloud growth moves 2027 EPS by about USD 0.8.**

Sensitivity analysis: **every 10-percentage-point difference in cloud growth moves 2027 EPS by about USD 0.8; every 2-percentage-point difference in operating margin moves EPS by about USD 1.1; every one-year extension of depreciation lives moves EPS by about USD 0.6.** Of the three variables, operating margin has the largest impact, and it is mainly determined by depreciation — so the single word "depreciation" explains almost all of the forecast error.

The most critical assumption for 2027 is capex: **I assume RMB 230 billion (+15% YoY); if management truly "significantly increases" it to RMB 260 billion, free cash flow swings from +RMB 10 billion to -RMB 20 billion**, the valuation must be recalculated as an "asset-heavy utility", and the target price cut to below 300.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

The two methods validate each other. **① Sum-of-the-parts: search advertising RMB 2.2 trillion (15x EBIT) + cloud RMB 1.0 trillion (25x 2027 EBIT) + net cash RMB 0.14 trillion = RMB 3.34 trillion, a 2% discount to the current market cap of RMB 3.42 trillion.****② Multiple valuation: 2026E adjusted EPS of USD 11.0, current price implies 25.4x PE; 21x EV/EBIT.**Both methods point to "fair, not cheap."

Target price USD 350 = 2027E EPS 14.0 × 25x.**Why 25x rather than 20x? Because cloud's share is rising and margins are climbing, so the growth premium is not yet exhausted; why not 30x? Because after the shift to heavy assets, the market will no longer award light-asset multiples.**25x is the balance point between these two forces.

Historical range for reference: over the past five years Alphabet's EV/EBIT has fluctuated between 20–30x, with a median of about 24x.**At 21x now, it sits at the low end of the range — this is "cheap from the selloff," not "cheap from fundamentals."**After the shift to heavy assets, the fair range may shift down as a whole to 15–22x, in which case 21x becomes the high end. The difference between these two perspectives is the difference between "buy in full" and "buy half."
