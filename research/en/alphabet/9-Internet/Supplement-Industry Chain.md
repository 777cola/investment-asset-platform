# Alphabet（GOOGL）— Industry Chain Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/30
- Price：279.84 USD
- Market cap：About USD 3.42 trillion

## Notes

The industry gross margin range is an industry estimate drawn from public sources rather than a figure disclosed by any single company. Our view on the AI capex cycle rests on the 2024–2026 capex trends of the four major cloud providers and on general assumptions for server and data centre depreciation lives (6 years for servers, 15 years for data centres).

## Upstream is where the money is made, and Alphabet is the only buyer with bargaining power

The bulk of AI capex flows upstream: chips, memory, networking equipment. Nvidia's gross margin exceeds 70%, TSMC's is 58%, and HBM is a seller's market in 2026. If a cloud provider can only buy from Nvidia, its cost structure is dictated by the upstream. That is why Microsoft, Meta and Amazon all design their own chips — not for performance, but for pricing power.

Alphabet was the earliest to do this along the chain: TPU has been used internally since 2015 and was already being sold externally in 2026. It does not need to fight Nvidia for allocation, nor accept Nvidia's pricing. That is extremely valuable in a year of tight capacity — and 2026 is such a year. The cost is that it bears tape-out risk itself and is constrained by TSMC's capacity.

## Midstream: the model layer is a meat grinder; the cloud layer collects the rent

The model layer is now the least profitable part of the business. OpenAI and Anthropic are both burning cash to buy share, while open-source families (Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek) have driven closed-source pricing down to near cost.Call volume of 32 billion tokens per minute sounds intimidating, but unit prices are falling fast — capacity quadruples while revenue only doubles, and that is precisely the point.

Cloud is a different story. Cloud sells "deterministic compute": customers do not care which model you use, only whether it runs, what it costs and whether it is stable.This is a rent-collection business — once built, marginal cost is low and customer switching costs are high.Google Cloud's 36.2% operating margin is the rent.Alphabet's strategy is therefore clear: the model layer defends the entry point and captures mindshare, while cloud makes the money.Model-layer losses do not matter, as long as they bring cloud demand.

## Downstream, advertisers ultimately pay and are the first to run

Money across the entire chain ultimately comes from two pockets: advertisers and enterprise IT budgets. Advertisers pay for attention; enterprises pay for customer experience and efficiency. The 2026 change: ad budgets are migrating from "search keywords" to "AI conversations" — slowly, but the direction is clear. Q3 search +12% (expected +14%) is the first yellow light.

Enterprise IT budgets, meanwhile, are tilting toward AI infrastructure — the root cause of cloud revenue doubling. But the sustainability of this budget depends on whether enterprises can calculate ROI. If enterprises find in 2027 that AI projects have not delivered profit, cloud growth will fall faster than anyone's, because this spending is not locked in by long-term contracts (backlog covers only 13% of quarterly cloud revenue).

## Cycle position: investment phase over, return phase about to begin

Capex at the four major North American cloud providers has accelerated since 2024 and is running at a "USD 200 billion a year" level in 2026. What makes this cycle unusual: investment has grown at a high rate for three consecutive years, yet depreciation recognition has only just begun. With servers depreciated over six years and data centers over fifteen, 2027 will see the highest YoY depreciation growth.

Divergence signals have already appeared in the industry: CoreWeave has retreated more than 70% from its high, Oracle's free cash flow will not turn positive until the second half of fiscal 2028, and Meta's Q3 free cash flow was -USD 2.12 billion. The market is starting to distinguish "compute backed by cash flow" from "compute backed by financing." Alphabet belongs to the former, but its cash flow is thinning too—the awkwardness of its position in the cycle: it sits at the tail end of the investment phase and the eve of the return phase, with no buffer between the two.

## Value is shifting from the "monetisation side" to the "compute side"

In 2020, 80% of the profit in this chain sat at the monetisation end (advertising, e-commerce, subscriptions), while the compute end captured only equipment spend.By 2026 the compute end had begun taking the excess profit: Nvidia gross margins above 70%, HBM in short supply, TSMC advanced nodes at full utilisation.

The shift cuts both ways for Alphabet.On the downside, costs are rising: of the RMB 200 billion in capex, chips and memory account for the bulk, and pricing power sits upstream.On the upside, Alphabet now occupies that position itself: selling TPU externally turns it from "the party paying" into "the party collecting," even if the scale is still small.

To judge whether the shift persists, watch one metric: the growth rate of AI compute supply vs demand.Supply is constrained by TSMC capacity and power; demand is constrained by enterprise budgets. If supply catches up in 2027, profit will flow back from the compute end to the monetisation end — and Alphabet will be better off then.
