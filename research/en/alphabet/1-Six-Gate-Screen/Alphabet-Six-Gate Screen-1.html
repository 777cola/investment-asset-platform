Six-Gate Screen — Alphabet

Alphabet (GOOGL) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/10/30  ·  Price $279.84 (-1.40%)  ·  Market cap About USD 3.42 trillion  ·  EV/EBIT(2026E) Approximately 21x
← Back to the hub

Core call: good business, good price, held back by discipline

Alphabet scores near full marks on the first three of six gates: search advertising is a business with near-zero marginal cost, cloud has just crossed the profitability inflection point (Q3 operating margin 36.2%), and its four moats—default entry points, the data flywheel, in-house TPU development, and Android/Chrome—reinforce one another. The fourth gate, management, is marked down for the pace of capital allocation; the fifth gate, margin of safety, for the reliability of the denominator; the sixth gate, discipline, for buy conditions that are too demanding. Overall 47/60, and the conclusion is "Buy, in tranches"—not because it is cheap enough to buy blindly, but because at USD 279.84 the price pays only for the existing search and cloud businesses, with Gemini, Waymo and external TPU sales essentially thrown in for free. The real risk is not the business but when USD 200 billion of capex turns into depreciation.

Gate one: circle of competence—you need to understand the business, but distinguish "understanding the business" from "being able to value the option"

Alphabet's revenue mix is the most straightforward of any tech giant: Search and other USD 63.35 billion, YouTube ads USD 11.21 billion, Network USD 7.46 billion, Cloud USD 30.71 billion, Other Bets USD 372 million. The first four all make clear "who pays, why they pay, and whether they will keep paying next year." Search is advertisers paying for intent, YouTube is paying for attention, Cloud is paying for compute and software. No revenue line requires a story to explain.

The real difficulty is pricing the AI option. The Gemini App has 1 billion users, and the model API processes nearly 32 billion tokens per minute (four times the level a year ago), but this usage is currently monetized mainly indirectly, through "improving search quality" and "selling cloud compute," with no standalone P&L. Understanding the business does not mean you can value the option. That is why this criterion scores 9 rather than 10.

Gate two: a good business — delivering a 32% operating margin on RMB 200 billion of capex

Q3 revenue USD 127.58 billion, +25% YoY, the 13th consecutive quarter of double-digit growth; operating income USD 40.58 billion, +30% YoY; operating margin 32%, up 1 percentage point from a year earlier. This is the only one of the four hyperscalers to raise margins while doubling capex YoY. Meta's capex over the same period was USD 33.94 billion with free cash flow of -USD 2.12 billion; Alphabet spent more on capex yet posted higher margins.

The breakdown is clearer: Google Services operating income USD 39.16 billion on revenue of USD 96.3 billion, a 40.7% operating margin; Google Cloud operating income USD 11.12 billion on revenue of USD 30.71 billion, a 36.2% margin—versus just 23.7% a year earlier. A mature cash cow running at 40% margins plus a second curve that has just crossed its inflection point with margins still climbing—no other large-cap US tech company has this combination.

Third gate: moat — the four are not parallel, but circular

The default entry points (Chrome, Android, and the default search agreement with Apple) generate queries, queries generate data, data trains Gemini, Gemini lifts search and ad conversion, and a better product defends the entry points.This loop has run for twenty years; AI did not interrupt it, it added fuel to it.

The fourth block is TPU. Alphabet is the only company globally that can hold all three layers — training chips, models and cloud services — without building its own fabs. Q3 cloud revenue rose 103% year on year, with external TPU system sales a major contributor —it cuts its own costs and sells that cost advantage to others.On antitrust, the DOJ's December 2025 remedies ruling did not require divesting Chrome or Android, only data sharing and a choice screen, with the choice-screen obligation taking effect in April 2026.The lightest outcome available has already been secured.

Stage 4: Management — right call, but capital allocation shifts from conservative to aggressive

Pichai's case for credit here is solid: playing passive defense against ChatGPT in 2023, reorganizing the Gemini team in 2024, pushing the models into the top tier in 2025–2026, doubling cloud revenue in Q3, and reaching 1 billion Gemini App users — none of that was luck. Getting this done inside a 200,000-person company that has been tangled in antitrust for five years takes rare execution.

Two points off for capital allocation. Q3 capex was 51.36 billion, up 114% year on year, with full-year guidance of 195–205 billion, and a further "significant increase" in 2027. At the same time it did three things it would not have done over the past decade: issued USD 28.7 billion in senior unsecured debt, sold 36 million new shares for net proceeds of 12.4 billion, and suspended buybacks (it bought back 11.5 billion in the year-earlier period). A company with USD 140 billion in net cash starting to borrow to expand capacity means management believes the window cannot be missed — that may be right, or it may be the most expensive judgment call of this cycle.

Gate 5: Margin of safety — is 21x EV/EBIT cheap?

Market cap USD 3.42 trillion, cash and securities on the books USD 265.1 billion, long-term debt USD 125.1 billion, net cash of about USD 140 billion, enterprise value of about USD 3.28 trillion. Backing out from 21x EV/EBIT, the market is implying 2026 EBIT of about USD 156 billion — Q3 operating profit was USD 40.58 billion, or USD 162.3 billion annualized, which is consistent on the same basis.

Paying 21x for a company with revenue +25%, cloud +103% and a 32% operating margin sits at the cheap end of the past decade. But the cheapness rests on the denominator holding: with 2027 depreciation doubling to over USD 50 billion, the operating margin could fall from 32% to 28%, and EBIT would no longer be USD 156 billion. So this gate scores only 6 — the price offers a buffer, but not a thick enough one to ignore depreciation.

Gate 6: Discipline — write down clear entry conditions so you can hold the position

The question here is not whether the company is good, but whether "I can hold it." The current buy case for Alphabet requires two conditions to hold at the same time: cloud growth stays above 50% in 2027, and rising depreciation does not push the operating margin below 28%. Both conditions can be falsified by quarterly reports, which is a good thing—far stronger than an unverifiable narrative like "AI will win in ten years."

A score of 6, because the conditions are demanding: Q3 cloud +103% had a low base (RMB 15.1 billion last year) and a one-off component from external TPU sales; a slowdown in 2027 is inevitable—the only question is whether it drops to 60% or 35%. So operationally, one must first build a half position, then wait for the cloud growth and depreciation data from Q1 and Q2 2027 before deciding whether to add.

Veto items and items pending verification

Disqualifiers: none. No sign of financial fraud, no incomprehensible revenue sources, no insolvency risk (net cash RMB 140 billion), no fatal governance flaw (the founder's dual-class structure is a known variable, not a new risk). The worst antitrust outcome—a breakup of Chrome or Android—was ruled out in the December 2025 remedies ruling.

  • To verify 1: the slope of cloud growth.Whether it can stay above 50% in 2027. Watch point: cloud revenue and backlog in the February 2027 Q4 report (Q3 was RMB 56.1 billion, slightly more than 55% of which is recognized within 24 months).
  • To verify 2: the depreciation curve.Annual depreciation on RMB 200 billion of capex doubles to more than RMB 50 billion in 2027—can the operating margin hold at 28%. Watch point: the depreciation and amortization detail in the 10-Q.
  • To verify 3: when free cash flow turns positive.Q3 was -RMB 9.07 billion, leaving only RMB 19.7 billion over the past 12 months. If full-year 2027 is still negative, the current valuation must be recalculated as an asset-heavy utility.
  • To verify 4: search cannibalizing itself.Q3 search +12% (below the consensus of +14%). If it falls below 8% for two consecutive quarters, AI answers are eating clicks.

Conclusion

Six gates 47/60, five passed, discipline barely. This is not a "so cheap you don't have to think" opportunity; it is an opportunity where "the business quality is high enough, the price gives a 30% buffer, but the denominator needs verification." Action: open half of the initial position at USD 279.84, fill out below USD 240; no leverage, and do not sell just because a single quarter's free cash flow is negative. The only hard sell condition: cloud growth falls below 30% while capex does not come down—that would mean the RMB 200 billion was spent wrong.
