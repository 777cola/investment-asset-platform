## Tier one: revenue mix — advertising decelerating, cloud accelerating

Total revenue of RMB 127.58 billion, +25%, came in below the RMB 128.2 billion consensus — a RMB 400 million miss, or 0.3%, which is noise. The mix is more telling: Search +12% (expected +14%), YouTube +9.2%, Network flat, Cloud +103%. The three advertising lines combined generated RMB 82.61 billion, with growth slowing to just over 10%, while Cloud alone reached RMB 30.71 billion, growing at a triple-digit rate.

This mix shift changes the quality of revenue: advertising carries zero marginal cost and high cash flow, whereas Cloud carries heavy capex and high growth. Cloud rose from 14.8% of total revenue last year to 24.1%, meaning a quarter of revenue now has to be "repaid" through depreciation. That is no bad thing, but the valuation method must change accordingly — the Cloud business cannot be priced at a pure advertising company's multiple.

## Layer two: gross profit and expenses. Depreciation has yet to bite, and that is the key

Operating margin 31.8%, up 1.3 percentage points YoY. Delivering that in a quarter when capex rose 114% YoY admits only two explanations: first, the new compute generated revenue immediately (cloud +103% supports this); second, depreciation recognition lags capex (an accounting-rule outcome). Both hold, and the second matters more—it means the current margin is not a "new normal" but a "timing gap."

Two cost lines bear watching: traffic acquisition cost (TAC) scales with search revenue and is a rigid expense; AI R&D sits within "corporate items," and the Q3 corporate-items loss was USD 7.66 billion, larger than last year's scale. Revenue growth will not dilute this expense; it is permanent investment.

## Tier 3: Profit and tax — the -25% net profit is accounting noise

Net profit RMB 26.17 billion, -25%, EPS 2.12 versus 2.87. Ugly on the surface, but meaningless once broken down: other gains/losses swung from +RMB 12.76 billion last year to -RMB 8.22 billion this year — a RMB 20.98 billion round trip, including a net fair value loss of RMB 8.66 billion on equity securities. That is the mark-to-market revaluation of equity holdings (including unlisted companies), unrelated to operations and with no cash impact.

Excluding it, EPS is USD 2.67, higher than a year earlier. The correct reading: operating profit grew 30% while book profit fell 25% — both true, but only the former has anything to do with the business. The market's reaction that day (down as much as 10% after hours) shows many read only the headline — which is where the opportunity comes from.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the reason the share price fell

Operating cash flow RMB 42.29 billion (RMB 48.41 billion last year, -12.6%), capex RMB 51.36 billion (RMB 23.95 billion last year), free cash flow -RMB 9.07 billion (+RMB 24.46 billion last year). Two one-offs drove the operating cash flow decline: cash tied up in the TPU inventory build and the USD 5.2 billion EU fine payment. Add both back and FCF only roughly breaks even.

Balance sheet: cash and securities RMB 265.1 billion, long-term debt RMB 125.1 billion, net cash RMB 140 billion. No near-term solvency risk, but the direction has changed — the company has gone from "steadily growing net cash" to "net cash consumed by capex and topped up with debt." FCF over the past 12 months was just RMB 19.7 billion, a 0.6% yield on a RMB 3.42 trillion market cap. A company growing 25% on a 0.6% FCF yield — that is the entire reason for the market's repricing.

## The three things to watch most closely

- The slope of cloud growth. Q3 +103% was flattered by a low base (RMB 15.1 billion last year) and a one-off contribution from external TPU sales. Whether growth can hold 50% in 2027 should be clear within two quarters.

- The depreciation curve. Annual depreciation on RMB 200 billion of capex doubles to over RMB 50 billion in 2027; can the operating margin hold at 28%–30%? That figure sits in the depreciation detail of the 10-Q, not in the press release.

- When free cash flow turns positive. This is the only hard metric for judging whether AI capex is investment or cash burn. Management guides capex to "increase significantly" in 2027; if FCF is still negative at that point, the valuation has to be recalculated as an asset-heavy company.

## Across four consecutive quarters, the trend matters more than any single quarter

Single-quarter data is easily distorted by base effects; the trend only becomes reliable when four quarters are viewed together. Three observable trends: ① cloud revenue share rises each quarter (14.8% last year → 24.1% this year); ② capex/revenue rises each quarter (23.4% last year → 40.2% this year); ③ free cash flow weakens each quarter (+RMB 24.46 billion last year → -RMB 9.07 billion this year).

All three point the same way, suggesting this is not a one-quarter anomaly but a structural change. The implication for investors: judge the story not by whether next quarter is better, but by when this structure stabilises. The marker of stabilisation is capex/revenue ceasing to rise—given management's guidance of a "significant increase in 2027," that means the second half of 2027 at the earliest.

One more easily overlooked trend: operating cash flow growth is slowing (Q3 -12.6%). If the slowdown is structural (slower ad growth plus a working capital drag), FCF turning positive will come later; if it is one-off (fines plus TPU stockpiling), it recovers in 2027. I lean toward the latter but cannot be certain—this is the question I most want to press management on in this earnings report.

## Two details on the balance sheet

Detail one: A substantial portion of the RMB 265.1 billion in cash and securities is long-held equity (including unlisted assets) rather than readily available cash. The "RMB 140 billion net cash" figure is therefore an accounting construct; the true liquidity buffer is smaller. Detail two: The RMB 28.7 billion of new debt issued in Q3 skews long in tenor (over ten years), signalling that the company is locking in long-term funding costs rather than addressing short-term working capital. This is a classic move to prepare for multi-year capex.

The conclusion: no near-term liquidity risk, but the impression of "lots of money on the books" needs correcting — freely deployable cash is not RMB 265.1 billion. This also explains why a net-cash company would issue debt.
