## Funnel step one: translate the "AI narrative" into "who has revenue"

In 2026, too many names in the market call themselves "AI companies." The first step is not to compare whose technology is stronger, but to compare whose income statement the AI revenue actually lands on. By that standard, only four types of players remain: those selling compute (Nvidia, TSMC), those renting compute (cloud providers), those selling models (OpenAI, Anthropic), and those using AI to lift the efficiency of their core business (Meta, Amazon e-commerce).

Alphabet is unusual in being the first three at once: selling chips (TPU externally), renting compute (Google Cloud) and selling models (Gemini API), plus the fourth (using AI to lift ad efficiency at Search and YouTube). There is no second "four-in-one" in the industry. Microsoft is three (missing external sales of in-house chips); Amazon is three (missing a say at the model layer).

## Screening out: why three were eliminated

Meta is excluded not because it is weak, but because it lacks a second curve. Q3 capex was RMB 33.94 billion and free cash flow -RMB 2.12 billion, with all of it going into its own recommendation system and advertising tools and none into an external cloud business. Its AI spending can therefore only be monetised through ad ROI gains, and the ceiling is set by the advertising market. Alphabet has cloud as a second curve; Meta does not.

Oracle is excluded because the good news is already in the price. OCI's backlog leads the industry, but free cash flow will not turn positive until the second half of fiscal 2028, and the stock has risen 12% since July. Buying it means buying expectations already realised. Baidu is excluded because the thesis is different: its core search advertising business is under pressure, AI monetisation is slow, and it faces a regulatory and market environment entirely different from Alphabet's, so it does not belong in the same comparable group.

## Final pick: why Alphabet over Microsoft

Microsoft is the most recognised AI winner of this cycle: the exclusive OpenAI tie-up, Office distribution and Azure's enterprise customer base. But it carries two structural problems: first, model dependence on an outside party (if OpenAI splits, the narrative breaks); second, compute dependence on Nvidia, a passive cost structure. Alphabet depends on neither — Gemini is in-house, TPU is in-house.

The third reason is price. Alphabet trades 30.5% below its 52-week high and only 2.3% above its 52-week low; the market has already priced it as an AI-capex loser. Microsoft's valuation already embeds the AI victory. Buying Alphabet is buying "the misjudged loser"; buying Microsoft is buying "the confirmed winner" — the former offers better odds, the latter higher certainty. When the odds are close to 1 to 1 but the probability skews upward, choose the odds.

## We set out the elimination criteria upfront, so there is no hindsight rationalisation later

Three hard criteria define this funnel: ① AI revenue must reach the income statement, not just the narrative; ② there must be at least two independent monetisation curves (at least two of advertising/cloud/subscription/chips); ③ valuation must not already price in the optimistic scenario. Meta fails ②, Oracle fails ③, OpenAI fails ① (it has revenue but no public stock), and Baidu fails both ① and ③.

Alphabet passes all three: cloud +103% is AI revenue, advertising and cloud are two curves, and 21x EV/EBIT does not price in the optimistic scenario. That is the entire answer to "why this one" — there is no other mystery.

## Three blind spots in the funnel

Every screen has blind spots; better to write them down than hide them. Blind spot one: the comparison covers only "AI monetization," not the stability of non-AI businesses. Alphabet's advertising business is cyclical. If the economy turns down in 2027, advertising falls first and cloud growth will not offset it. Blind spot two: the comparison covers only "current valuation," not "governance structure." Alphabet's dual-class share structure lets it make a 200 billion decision, and also leaves no one to correct it when that judgment is wrong.

Blind spot three: companies that do not invest in AI are left out. If 2027 proves that the return on AI capex is below expectations, the best targets may be the companies that "did not invest that much"—for example, software companies with stable cash flow. This blind spot means: the Alphabet position cannot be too large, otherwise the entire portfolio is betting on the single assumption that "AI investment pays off."
