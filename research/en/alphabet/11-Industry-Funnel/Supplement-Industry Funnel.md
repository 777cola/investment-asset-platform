## Funnel step one: translate the "AI narrative" into "who has revenue"

In 2026 the market has too many names calling themselves "AI companies." The first step is not comparing whose technology is stronger, but comparing **whose income statement the AI revenue actually lands on**. By that standard only four types of players remain: those selling compute (Nvidia, TSMC), those renting compute (cloud providers), those selling models (OpenAI, Anthropic), and those using AI to lift the efficiency of their core business (Meta, Amazon e-commerce).

Alphabet is unusual in that it is the first three at once: selling chips (TPU externally), renting compute (Google Cloud), selling models (Gemini API), plus the fourth (using AI to lift ad efficiency at Search and YouTube). **There is no second "four-in-one" in the industry.** Microsoft is three (missing external sales of in-house chips); Amazon is three (missing a say at the model layer).

## Screening out: why three were eliminated

**Meta is out not because it is weak, but because it has no second curve.** Q3 capital expenditure was RMB 33.94 billion and free cash flow was -RMB 2.12 billion, with all the money going into its own recommendation system and advertising tools and no external cloud business. That means its AI spending can only be monetized through ad ROI gains, and the ceiling is set by the advertising market. **Alphabet has cloud as a second curve; Meta does not.**

**Oracle is out because the good news is already in the price.** OCI's backlog leads the industry, but free cash flow will not turn positive until the second half of fiscal 2028, and the stock has risen 12% since July. Buying it means buying expectations already realized. **Baidu is out because the thesis is different**: its core search advertising business is under pressure, AI monetization is slow, and it faces a regulatory and market environment entirely different from Alphabet's, so it does not belong in the same comparable group.

## Final pick: why Alphabet over Microsoft

Microsoft is the most recognized AI winner of this cycle: exclusive OpenAI tie-up, Office distribution, Azure enterprise customers. **But it has two structural problems: first, model dependence on an outside party (if OpenAI splits, the narrative breaks); second, compute dependence on Nvidia (a passive cost structure).** Alphabet depends on neither — Gemini is in-house, TPU is in-house.

The third reason is price. Alphabet is 30.5% off its 52-week high and only 2.3% above its 52-week low; the market has already priced it as an AI-capex loser. **In Microsoft's valuation, the AI victory has already been paid for.** Buying Alphabet is buying "the misjudged loser"; buying Microsoft is buying "the confirmed winner" — the former has better odds, the latter higher certainty. In a setup where the odds are close to 1 to 1 but the probability skews upward, choose the odds.

## Setting out the elimination criteria upfront, so there is no hindsight rationalisation later

The three hard criteria of this funnel: **① AI revenue must reach the income statement (not just narrative); ② there must be at least two independent monetization curves (at least two of advertising/cloud/subscription/chips); ③ valuation must not already reflect the optimistic scenario.** Meta fails ②, Oracle fails ③, OpenAI fails ① (it has revenue but no public stock), Baidu fails ① and ③.

Alphabet passes all three: cloud +103% is AI revenue, advertising plus cloud are two curves, and 21x EV/EBIT does not reflect the optimistic scenario. **That is the entire answer to "why this one" — there is no other mystery.**

## Three blind spots in the funnel

Every screen has blind spots; better to write them down than hide them. **Blind spot one: the comparison covers only "AI monetization," not the stability of non-AI businesses.** Alphabet's advertising business is cyclical. If the economy turns down in 2027, advertising falls first and cloud growth will not offset it. **Blind spot two: the comparison covers only "current valuation," not "governance structure."** Alphabet's dual-class share structure lets it make a 200 billion decision, and also leaves no one to correct it when that judgment is wrong.

**Blind spot three: companies that do not invest in AI are left out.** If 2027 proves that the return on AI capex is below expectations, the best targets may be the companies that "did not invest that much"—for example, software companies with stable cash flow. **This blind spot means: the Alphabet position cannot be too large, otherwise the entire portfolio is betting on the single assumption that "AI investment pays off."**
