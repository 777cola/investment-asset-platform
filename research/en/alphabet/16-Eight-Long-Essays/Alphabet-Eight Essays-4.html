Core view

This note works through a single calculation: how much operating profit USD 200 billion a year of capex will consume over the next few years. Assuming six years for servers and fifteen for data centres, a blended weighted average of five and a half years, USD 200 billion implies roughly USD 36 billion of annual depreciation; adding existing depreciation, total depreciation doubles to over USD 50 billion by 2027. Against annualised revenue of USD 500 billion, that is 10 percentage points. Operating leverage from doubling cloud revenue absorbs part of it, leaving the neutral case with operating margin down from 32% to 30% and the bear case at 26%. The output of this calculation is the denominator of the three-scenario valuation.

Layer one: why the margin is still 32% now

Q3 operating profit was RMB 40.58 billion, with operating margin at 31.8%, up 1.3 percentage points YoY. Delivering that in a quarter when capex rose 114% YoY admits only one answer: depreciation recognition lags cash spending. Servers bought today begin amortizing next year; data centres built today are capitalized the year after.

Today's 32% is therefore no "new normal" — it is a point-in-time figure reflecting the fact that "spending has already happened, cost has not yet been recognized." To judge whether that holds, watch one metric: depreciation as a share of revenue. If the ratio has risen over the past two years, cost is already biting; if it is flat, it has not started. Most investors look only at operating margin and not at this ratio — the most easily overlooked point in this report.

Layer two: run the numbers on the RMB 200 billion

Of the RMB 200 billion, 65% is servers and networking (RMB 130 billion) and 35% is data centres and land (RMB 70 billion). Servers depreciated over six years come to RMB 21.7 billion a year; data centres over fifteen years come to RMB 4.7 billion a year—RMB 26.4 billion combined. That is the most optimistic calculation (new additions only). On a blended five-and-a-half-year basis, it is RMB 36 billion.

Then layer on the existing base: 2024–2025 capex (roughly RMB 50 billion + RMB 90 billion) is also steadily entering fixed assets, so total 2027 depreciation will likely double from the current RMB 25 billion level to above RMB 50 billion. RMB 50 billion against RMB 500 billion of annualised revenue is 10 percentage points. If gross margin does not improve, operating margin falls from 32% to 22%.

But gross margin will improve, because cloud revenue is doubling. Cloud operating margin rising from 23.7% to 36.2% shows that new compute earns a higher return than its depreciation cost. So the neutral outcome is a drop to 30%, not 22%. That 8-percentage-point gap is the dividing line between "investment" and "burning cash".

Layer three: the three measures of cash flow

Free cash flow can be calculated at least three ways, and the method must be applied consistently when reading the financial statements.① Company definition: operating cash flow of RMB 42.29 billion − capital expenditure of RMB 51.36 billion = -RMB 9.07 billion.② Adding back working capital changes: TPU inventory stockpiling tied up some cash; adding it back could bring the figure close to break-even (rough estimate).③ Deducting finance leases: the figure turns more negative.

Of the three definitions, the company definition is best suited to cross-period comparison, because it is derived from the same basis as full-year guidance (RMB 195–205 billion).One caveat: operating cash flow of RMB 42.29 billion was down 12.6% YoY, and part of that decline is one-off (the RMB 5.2 billion EU fine payment) while part is structural (TPU stockpiling).Fail to separate the two, and you will mistake a structural problem for a one-off.

My view:free cash flow may still be negative in 2027, with a turn positive possible only in 2028.Capex will "increase significantly" again in 2027, while operating cash flow growth is dragged down by advertising growth. If this view is wrong, it is wrong on the optimistic side—which would be good for the position.

How much would valuation change under a different depreciation period?

Depreciation periods are an accounting estimate, not a fact. The same RMB 200 billion of capex is RMB 40 billion a year over five years and RMB 25 billion over eight, a RMB 15 billion difference equal to 9% of operating profit. How high the depreciation peak runs is therefore half math, half accounting policy.

Alphabet's historical practice is four years for servers, five for network equipment and up to twenty-five for data centers (including leasehold improvements). If the company extends server useful lives in 2027 (say from four years to five), depreciation falls immediately and margins look better — but that is accounting improvement, not operating improvement. This is the kind of window-dressing to watch for when reading financial statements.

Conversely, if AI chip iteration accelerates (Nvidia on an annual cadence, TPU also speeding up), actual economic lives may run shorter than accounting lives, forcing the company to shorten periods and book impairments. This is the hidden risk I worry about most: it gives no advance warning and surfaces suddenly in a single quarter. How to track it: the "change in useful lives" footnote in the 10-Q, and the asset impairment loss line.

The three drivers of profit margin

Break operating margin into three drivers: (1) cloud revenue share (a higher share drags on near-term margin, since cloud margin sits below advertising); (2) cloud's own margin (a higher margin offsets); (3) depreciation as a share of revenue (a higher share drags). In Q3 the three combined as follows: cloud at 24.1%, cloud margin at 36.2%, depreciation at about 5% of revenue — they offset exactly, so margin still rose 1.3 percentage points.

By 2027: cloud share rises to about 35%, cloud margin may reach 38%, and depreciation rises to 9%–10% of revenue. The first two drivers improve by 2 percentage points, the third deteriorates by 5 percentage points, a net impact of -3 percentage points that takes operating margin from 32% to 29%. That is the arithmetic behind the 30% base case.

Conclusion

The depreciation question is the crux: depreciation doubles to above RMB 50 billion in 2027, with operating margin at a neutral 30% and a bear case of 26%. Cloud revenue scale effects absorb half the hit, not all of it. Settle that and valuation becomes clear: 21x on a 30% margin is reasonable; 21x on 26% is expensive.
