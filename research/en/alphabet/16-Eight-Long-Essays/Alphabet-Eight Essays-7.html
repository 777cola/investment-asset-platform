Risk list: five risks, one real — Alphabet

Chapter 7: Rank risks by "probability × impact"

Core view

Five risks: ① depreciation erodes margins (high probability, medium impact); ② cloud growth stalls (low probability, high impact); ③ search traffic diverted by AI (medium probability, extremely high impact); ④ forced breakup of the ad-tech business (low probability, low impact); ⑤ Waymo keeps losing money (high probability, low impact). Ranked by "probability × impact", there is only one real risk: search diversion. The other four either have small impact or are already priced in. Antitrust is not a risk — the worst-case outcome was already ruled out in the December 2025 ruling.

Risk one: depreciation, the only bad thing that is "certain to happen"

RMB 200 billion of capex depreciated over a blended five and a half years is RMB 36 billion a year; stacked on the existing base, total depreciation doubles from the RMB 25 billion level to over RMB 50 billion in 2027, equal to 10 percentage points against RMB 500 billion of annualized revenue. This will happen without any external trigger.

The only offset is scale in cloud revenue. Cloud operating margin rising from 23.7% to 36.2% shows that each extra dollar of revenue grows profit faster than depreciation. So the real question is not whether depreciation arrives, but whether cloud revenue growth can outrun it. The neutral answer is yes, but only partly — operating margin 32% → 30%.

Tracking method: each quarter, watch the depreciation and amortization / revenue ratio in the 10-Q. If that ratio rises more than 1 percentage point for two consecutive quarters, the offset has failed.

Risk 2: search disintermediation — an existential risk

Search ads are billed on "clicks." When AI answers directly, users stop clicking links, click volume falls, ad inventory shrinks and revenue declines. The frightening part of this chain: it does not require users to leave Google, only to stop clicking.

Q3 search grew 12% against expectations of 14%, a 2-percentage-point gap. This is the first observable yellow light. But do not overread it: FX is the single largest factor, and the prior-year base was high. The real threshold is two consecutive quarters below 8% — at that point it is no longer a valuation issue but a business-model issue.

What hedges this is precisely Gemini. If users shift from "searching keywords" to "asking Gemini," as long as Gemini belongs to Google and ads can be inserted into the conversation, the revenue stays in its own pocket. So the key metric is not "search growth" but "share of total time spent across Google's AI entry points." The company does not disclose this figure; it can only be observed indirectly through third-party data.

Risks three to five: none of them deserve attention

Ad tech divestiture: Brussels argues for spinning off AdX; if it happens, it affects about USD 100 billion, or 3% of market cap. This is a legal process, not an operating issue, and the timeline runs in years.

Waymo losses: Other Bets lost 2.05 billion in the quarter, an annualized 8 billion or more. This is "the time cost of an option"—as long as Alphabet earns 40 billion in operating profit a year, an 8 billion loss is bearable. The real risk is that Waymo never makes money, but that is a problem five years out.

Financing environment: Q3 bond issuance of 28.7 billion; if rates rise sharply, financing costs will climb. But the company has an AA+ rating and 140 billion in net cash—this is the lowest-priority risk.

Add these four risks together and the impact on valuation does not exceed 10%. The real risk is search—one risk worth four.

Interlocking risks: the worst case is not a single point

Each risk on its own is not fatal, but risks compound. The worst-case scenario runs along this chain: AI demand falls short of expectations → cloud growth drops from 103% to 30% → utilization of the RMB 200 billion in compute capacity declines → depreciation is still booked and margin falls from 32% to 26% → the company keeps investing to sustain growth → free cash flow stays negative for three consecutive years → credit rating is downgraded and financing costs rise → it is forced to cut capex and take impairments.

Every link in this chain has a leading indicator: cloud growth (demand), backlog (contracts), depreciation as a share of revenue (cost), FCF (cash), credit spreads (financing). Of these five, once the first three deteriorate together, the last two follow.

This is why I insist on buying only half the initial position: not out of pessimism, but because once this chain starts, the reaction window is only one to two quarters. Keeping half in cash is what gives you the ability to add at USD 240 or even USD 200; those fully invested can only watch at that point. Position sizing is not about predicting risk; it is about leaving room to act when risk arrives.

Risks I may have missed

Two things I admit I cannot see clearly. First, technology-route risk: if AI compute shifts from "large-scale pre-training" to "small models + on-device inference," demand for ultra-large clusters will fall, and part of the RMB 20 billion compute investment will be invalidated. There is no evidence for this now, but it is an industry-level paradigm risk. Second, the long-term compliance cost of antitrust: fines are minor; the real cost is that "product design must leave room for competitive interfaces," which will erode the consistency of product experience over the long term.

Neither risk appears in the quarterly report, and neither can be quantified. The way to handle them is not forecasting but position sizing—the 8% cap itself prices in the "risks I cannot see clearly."

Conclusion

Risk ranking: search disintermediation (existential) > depreciation (certain) > cloud stall (low probability, high impact) > the rest (noise). Position sizing should be designed around the first two, not the last three. This is also why the initial position is only half-sized—leaving room to respond to "certain bad news."
