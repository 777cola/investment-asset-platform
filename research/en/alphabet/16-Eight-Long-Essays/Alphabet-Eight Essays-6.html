Management: a man who judged correctly made a very expensive decision — Alphabet

Core view

In three years Pichai took Alphabet from "AI laggard" to "cloud revenue doubled, Gemini 1 billion users" — his execution is not in dispute. But the 2026 capital allocation decision is the most aggressive in a decade: capex at 40% of revenue, USD 28.7 billion in debt issued, 36 million new shares, buybacks at zero. On governance, the founders hold roughly 51% of voting power through Class B shares, and outside shareholders cannot block this decision. So buying this stock means buying the judgment of three people — Pichai, Page and Brin.

Three things done right

First, merge Brain and DeepMind. The problem in 2023 was fragmented teams and scattered resources; after the merger, model iteration visibly accelerated. Second, turn TPU from an internal tool into an external product. This is not just an extra revenue line — it converts a cost advantage into a commercial advantage. Third, make search itself AI search, rather than building a separate entry point.

The third is the most critical. Most companies defend against disruption by building a new product, and end up with two products cannibalizing each other. Pichai chose to "rebuild the core business" — AI Overviews sits directly on the search results page, and Gemini goes directly into Chrome and Android. That choice kept search at +12% in Q3, but it also made "monetization efficiency per search" opaque.

That Expensive Decision

2026 capex of RMB 195–205 billion, "significantly increased" in 2027. At the same time, RMB 28.7 billion in bond issuance, 36 million new shares, and buybacks cut to zero. Put these three together and the message is unambiguous: over the next two to three years, all cash goes into investment.

The evidence for the decision is hard: cloud +103%, cloud margin 36.2%, backlog RMB 56.1 billion. The evidence against is equally hard: FCF -RMB 9.07 billion, TTM down to just RMB 19.7 billion, depreciation doubling in 2027. This is a classic "very high payoff if right, very high cost if wrong" decision.

In judging management, I separate "judgment" from "discipline." On judgment, I think Pichai is right — demand is real, and the cloud margin proves it. On discipline, I give him 6 out of 10 — a company with RMB 140 billion in net cash issuing bonds, issuing shares and halting buybacks all at once is not internally consistent. Either it doesn't need the money (then it shouldn't issue bonds or shares), or it does (then it shouldn't keep paying dividends).

Governance: what 51% voting power means

Page and Brin together hold about 11% and control about 51% of voting power through Class B shares (10 votes per share). This means outside shareholders have no veto over any major decision — including RMB 200 billion of annual capex. This structure has existed since the IPO; it is not a new risk, but it means "voting with your feet" is the only check.

The benefit of this structure is long-termism: management can avoid compromising for quarterly results and dare to invest in ten-year projects. The drawback is the absence of a correction mechanism: if the judgment is wrong, no one can make them stop. The slow AI response in 2023 is one example — the company moved only after external pressure reached a certain level.

So buying this stock requires accepting one premise: you trust the judgment of these three people. If you do not trust them, you should not buy at any valuation; if you do trust them, then the 2027 depreciation peak is a normal test, not a disaster.

Are incentives aligned with shareholders?

Pichai's total compensation in 2024 was about USD 10.7 million, of which USD 2 million was base salary and the rest mainly security and personal use costs; he received no stock awards. This is rare among big-tech CEOs — Musk, Zuckerberg and Nadella are all compensated mainly in equity. The benefit of equity incentives is "alignment with shareholders"; the drawback is "possible short-term decisions to support the share price."

Alphabet's approach substitutes "shares already held" for "newly granted equity": Pichai holds hundreds of millions of dollars of stock, and its rise or fall directly affects his wealth. This is more "long-term" than annual option grants, because he cannot hedge a decline with new grants. From this angle, incentives are aligned.

But note the other side: the founders control 51% of voting rights through Class B shares, and their incentive comes from "long-term company value," not "quarterly EPS." That is good for decisions like RMB 200 billion of capex (willing to invest over a decade) and bad for decisions like "whether to stop" (no one can make them stop). So I give governance a 6: stable structure, but no correction mechanism.

Organization: how 200,000 people use AI to transform themselves

An often overlooked fact: Alphabet has 200,000 employees, a large share of them doing repetitive support work (customer service, moderation, sales operations). AI's first source of value for Alphabet is not selling to others but transforming itself — cost savings that do not show up on the financial statements.

The 1.3 percentage point rise in the Q3 operating margin came partly from internal efficiency, not just cloud scale effects. If headcount stays flat through 2027 while revenue grows 20%, revenue per employee will rise markedly, and that profit requires no capital expenditure. This is my counterpoint to the bearish call that depreciation will swallow profit: gains in internal efficiency are not in the models.

Conclusion

Management conclusion: Execution 9, capital allocation discipline 6, governance structure 6. Overall 8. Buying it means buying the judgment of three people, and that must be clear before buying — because 51% of the voting power means you cannot change any decision.
