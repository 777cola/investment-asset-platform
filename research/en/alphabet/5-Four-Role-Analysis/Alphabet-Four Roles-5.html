Core view

All four perspectives agree on the facts but diverge sharply on weighting. Business scores 9 (good business, but getting heavier), financial 7 (good profit, poor cash flow), industry 7 (good positioning, poor cycle) and risk 6 (two real risks). Three points of divergence need adjudication: ① whether capex is investment or cash burn; ② whether the 32% operating margin can hold; ③ whether search self-cannibalization is a matter of a few years. Verdict: buy in tranches, with a half-size initial position, absorbing uncertainty through price and position sizing.

Disagreement one: is the RMB 200 billion capex investment or cash burn

The business lens says "invest": cloud revenue is growing 103% at a 36.2% margin, so demand is real. The financial lens says "can't tell": free cash flow is negative and funded by debt and equity issuance. The industry lens says "the cycle position is unfavourable": the depreciation peak has not yet arrived. All three are right, because each is answering a question on a different time scale.

Verdict: treat it as an investment, but require verification. Three reasons: ① cloud operating margin rose from 23.7% to 36.2%, showing new capacity is earning money rather than sitting idle; ② backlog stands at RMB 56.1 billion, 55% of it to be recognised within 24 months, so demand is backed by contracts; ③ net cash is still RMB 140 billion, so financing is a deliberate choice, not a forced one. If cloud growth falls below 30% in 2027 while capex does not come down, the verdict reverses immediately.

Disagreement two: can the 32% operating margin hold

This is the most technical disagreement, and the most important. The financial view holds that 32% is a point-in-time figure reflecting costs not yet recognised and will fall below 28% in 2027; the commercial view holds that cloud scale effects will offset depreciation. Run the numbers: RMB 200 billion of capex depreciated over six years is RMB 33.3 billion a year; by 2027 depreciation doubles to more than RMB 50 billion, which is 10 percentage points against annualised revenue of RMB 500 billion. Absent gross margin improvement, operating margin drops 10 points.

Verdict: base case 30%, bear case 26%, bull case 32%. The reasoning: scale effects from doubling cloud revenue absorb part of the depreciation — cloud operating margin rising from 23.7% to 36.2% is the evidence — but not all of it. This verdict directly determines the 2027 EPS differences across the three-scenario valuation.

Divergence 3: Search self-cannibalisation plays out over a few years

On the risk lens this is an existential threat that warrants tracking over a 3–5 year window; on the industry lens the migration is already under way and only its pace is unknown; on the business lens, "revolutionising yourself" beats being revolutionised by others, so it is not a risk at all. The three differ on probability, not direction.

Verdict: treat it as "no collapse within 3 years, but 1–2 points of growth lost each year." The basis: Q3 search grew 12% (against a tougher prior-year comparison), AI Overviews is already live without breaking revenue, and Gemini App's 1 billion users show the company has secured a new entry point. Metric to watch: whether search revenue growth stays below 8% for two consecutive quarters. At that point it is no longer a valuation question but a business-model question.

Final call: Buy, scale in, three iron rules

Across the four lenses the weighted conclusion is "good company, reasonable price, uncertain return horizon." The standard playbook for that combination is to buy in tranches — neither all-in nor fully in cash. Specifically: open an initial position at USD 279.84 sized at half the target allocation (if the target is 8%, buy 4% first), complete the position below USD 240, and stop adding above USD 350 while starting to evaluate trimming.

  • Iron rule one: no leverage. The stock traded 273.5–402.62 over the past year; leverage will shake you out.
  • Iron rule two: no price stop-loss; use a thesis stop. Halve the position unconditionally if cloud growth falls below 30% without capex coming down, or if search growth stays below 8% for two consecutive quarters.
  • Iron rule three: make only one decision per quarter. Within 48 hours of earnings, decide whether to add, trim or hold; don't watch the tape the rest of the time.

One final counterpoint: if the Q2 2027 report shows the operating margin dropping below 26% and cloud growth falling to 35%, then buying now was wrong, and you have to own that. The point of buying in tranches is not "buy more as it falls" but "leave room to admit you were wrong."

Conclusion

Four-lens summary: Buy, scale in, half position initially, fill to full below USD 240. All three points of disagreement are now settled: capex is treated as investment but requires verification, the neutral assumption for 2027 operating margin is 30%, and search substitution is modelled as a 1–2 point annual deceleration. If any one is disproven, halve the position.
