Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Alphabet

What would truly destroy this company's value
Research date 2026/10/30  ·  Price $279.84 (-1.40%)  ·  Market cap About USD 3.42 trillion  ·  EV/EBIT(2026E) Approximately 21x

Core view

The real risk is not antitrust, not EU fines, and not competition. The worst antitrust outcome (breaking up Chrome/Android) was ruled out by the December 2025 remedies ruling; EU fines of a few billion euros a year are 0.1%-level against a market cap of USD 3.42 trillion. The real risks are two: search being diverted by AI faster than ads can be monetized, and the return on USD 200 billion of capex falling below the cost of capital. The former is an existential risk, the latter a valuation risk. Neither shows up in the quarterly report; both show up in time.

Risk one: search cannibalizing itself — an existential problem

Search advertising is billed on "clicks." When AI answers directly, users stop clicking links, and click volume falls; falling clicks shrink ad inventory; shrinking inventory drags revenue down. What makes this chain frightening is that it does not require users to leave Google — only to stop clicking. Q3 search grew 12%, showing this has not happened yet, but growth below the consensus of 14% is the first yellow light.

Alphabet's response is to put AI answers into the search results page (AI Overviews), cannibalizing its own clicks. That is the right strategy, but the cost is that monetization efficiency per search may decline. The company does not disclose revenue per thousand impressions for AI search — the number we most want and cannot get.

Risk two: return on capital, which is a valuation issue

At USD 200 billion of annual capex, a 20% pre-tax return should add USD 40 billion of EBIT two years out; at 10%, only USD 20 billion. The market now prices this at 21x EV/EBIT, implying "this money earns at least a 15% return." Whether that assumption holds will be settled in 2027–2028.

The leading indicator for that return is cloud backlog: USD 561 billion in Q3, slightly above 55% of which is confirmed within 24 months, or about USD 309 billion. That number is too small relative to USD 200 billion of annual capex — meaning current cloud revenue growth comes mainly from "on-demand demand," not "locked-in long-term contracts." On-demand demand offers flexibility; the downside is it can vanish at any time.

A risk that isn't a risk: antitrust and EU fines

The market spent two years pricing in a Chrome breakup, but the December 2025 remedy ruling only required data sharing and a choice screen. The choice-screen obligation takes effect in April 2026, and default search on Apple devices may be switched by users—this erodes the moat but does not dismantle it. EU fines (EUR 2.95 billion in September 2025, EUR 890 million in July 2026, EUR 460 million in September 2026) total less than EUR 5 billion, noise to cash flow.

The only remaining open item is structural relief on the ad-tech business. Brussels argues for spinning off AdX; if it happens, ad-tech valuation goes to zero, a roughly USD 100 billion impact—3% of market cap, not 30%. This risk should therefore be downgraded, not given attention.

Conclusion

Two real risks: one existential (search being diverted), one valuation-related (return on capital). Conclusion: risks are manageable but not ignorable; position sizing must leave room to "prove yourself wrong." The initial position is capped at half the target position precisely for this reason — if search growth comes in below 8% in Q1 2027, there is still cash that can stay put and position that can be trimmed.
