Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — Alphabet

The internet's third structural shift, and where it sits in the cycle

Core view

The internet industry has seen only two format shifts in twenty years: from PC web pages to mobile apps, and from apps to recommendation feeds. A third is now underway—from "users finding information" to "information finding users," with AI delivering answers directly to people. This shift cuts both ways for Alphabet: it is both the driver of the shift (Gemini) and the object being shifted (the search box). At the industry level, one point deserves emphasis: the AI capex cycle is now in its third year, and 2027 marks the peak of depreciation recognition and the first checkpoint for this round's "compute surplus or shortage" debate.

Value chain: the money sits at both ends; the middle is the hardest place to be

The two most profitable ends of this chain are the furthest upstream — compute chips (NVIDIA at over 70% gross margin, with demand outstripping supply) — and the furthest downstream — distribution and monetisation (Google and Meta's advertising businesses at around 40% operating margin). The hardest part is the model layer in the middle: massive investment, homogeneous products, customers who can switch at any time, and now losses across almost the entire industry.

What sets Alphabet apart is that it stands on all four links at once: in-house TPU (upstream), Google Cloud (infrastructure), Gemini (models), and Search and YouTube (monetisation). That means it captures a slice of profit from every segment of the chain — and must also bear the capex of every segment itself. Vertical integration is an advantage when capacity is tight and a burden when demand is insufficient.

Cycle position: third year of capex, first year of depreciation

Capex at the four major North American cloud providers began accelerating in 2024, stepped up again in 2025 and by 2026 is running at the "USD 200 billion a year" level.What makes this cycle unusual is that investment has grown at a high rate for three consecutive years, yet depreciation recognition has only just begun. Assuming six-year depreciation for servers and fifteen years for data centres, 2027 is the year with the highest YoY depreciation growth.

Another signal at the industry level: CoreWeave has retreated more than 70% from its high, and Oracle's free cash flow will not turn positive until the second half of fiscal 2028.The market has begun distinguishing "compute backed by cash flow" from "compute funded by financing." Alphabet belongs to the former, but its cash flow is also thinning—the awkwardness of its position in the cycle: at the tail end of the investment phase, on the eve of the return phase.

Think in reverse: what would kill the good times for this industry?

Three risks: 1) Compute oversupply. If enterprise AI demand disappoints in 2027 and cloud providers' utilisation falls, the RMB 20 billion capex becomes RMB 20 billion of stranded assets; 2) Model commoditisation. Open-source models catch up with closed-source, the API price war compresses the cost line, the model layer never turns a profit, and cloud can only earn thin margins selling compute; 3) Ad budget migration. If AI assistants become the primary entry point, ad inventory shifts from the "search results page" to "between conversation turns," and monetisation efficiency could fall by more than 30%.

Of these three, the first is already playing out (CoreWeave pullback, Oracle delaying breakeven), the second has partly happened (open-source models closing the capability gap), and the third has no evidence yet. But note: the industry's good times do not depend on how strong Alphabet is, but on whether demand growth can keep pace with supply growth.

Conclusion

The industry is at the "tail end of the investment phase, on the eve of the return phase" — the most uncomfortable position, where costs arrive first and revenue later. Conclusion: 9 out of 10 for industry position, 5 out of 10 for cycle position. Alphabet holds the best position along this chain, but a good position does not mean going all in right now.
