🧘 Duan Yongping's lens — PDD (PDD)

💬 Q&A

Q: How do you view Pinduoduo as a company?

I followed the company founded by Huang Zheng (Colin Huang) from the very beginning.

Pinduoduo does something very simple—Help users save money. This "saving" is not the saving of cutting corners; it is the saving that comes from pushing supply-chain efficiency to the limit. The C2M model puts factories directly in front of consumers and removes the middlemen, and that direction is right.

Many people look down on Pinduoduo, seeing it as a seller of cheap goods. But cheap is not the problem; the problem is whether it can stay cheap. Pinduoduo has proven it can—its operating efficiency is higher than Alibaba's, higher than JD.com's, higher than anyone's.

Q: Mr. Duan, were you an early investor in Pinduoduo?

I have known Huang Zheng for many years. We met at Zhejiang University; he later went to Google and then founded his own company. When he started Pinduoduo, I invested a little—not much—but I have held it ever since.

I invested in him not because he was my student, but because his logic of doing things was right. Huang Zheng is very smart, but his greatest strength is not being smart, it isDutyHe knows which money to make and which money not to make.

Q: Huang Zheng stepped back from the front line in 2021 — are you still comfortable with that?

A good organization should not depend on any one person. Pinduoduo performed well while Huang Zheng was there, and it has done even better since he left—which shows that organizational capability has taken root.

How well has Chen Lei done? Well. He came up through the CTO role and has a deep understanding of Pinduoduo's systems. Zhao Jiazhen is also a long-tenured employee. Management continuity is strong.

If the CEO retires, can this company stay competitive?
For Pinduoduo, the answer is yes. Because its core competitiveness is not any one person, butSystem—The system that knows how to help users save money.

Q: A PE of just 9x — isn't that too cheap?

Valuation is an outcome, not a cause.

A company trading at a low PE means either the market is wrong or the market sees something you don't. PDD trades at a low PE, and I think the market is mainly worried about two things: first, domestic growth has hit a ceiling; second, whether Temu's overseas expansion can make money.

The first concern is valid. Domestic growth has indeed slowed, but slowing and stalling are two different things.

The second concern needs time to verify. Whether Temu's model can work overseas remains unclear. The fully managed model carries policy risk abroad—both the EU and the US are watching closely.

But is a PE of 9x cheap?A company with 30% ROE, free cash flow growing every year, and over RMB 400 billion in cash on its books, trading at under 10x PE—by any measure, that is not expensive.

Q: So why doesn't the company pay dividends or buy back shares despite holding so much cash?

I have to criticize this.

Withholding cash from shareholders is irresponsible. If you have more cash on the books than you know what to do with, either buy back shares or pay dividends—return the money to shareholders and let them allocate it themselves.

When Duan Yongping ran BBK in its early years, he paid out dividends whenever the company made money and never hoarded cash. Because I know that,The return I could generate with the cash in my hands is not necessarily higher than what shareholders could generate with it in theirs.。

More than half of Pinduoduo's cash on the books exceeds half its market cap—which in a sense means that when you buy Pinduoduo stock, half your money is buying its cash. That valuation logic itself shows the market does not trust management.

We hope management can improve on this.

Q: How should we view the risks facing Temu?

Temu is headed in the right direction: the competitiveness of China's supply chain is overwhelming globally, and selling low-cost Chinese-made goods overseas is inevitable.

But doing business overseas is different from doing it domestically. In China, Pinduoduo could rely on WeChat viral referrals to acquire customers quickly; overseas there is no WeChat. Temu can only burn cash to buy traffic, with high user acquisition costs and retention rates below those in China.

The key question is: Can Temu build user stickiness before it burns through its cash? If Temu cannot forge the same "low-price mindset" moat as the domestic flagship site, it is just a story of burning cash for GMV, not a good business.

I am still watching for now. I will comment once there are results.

Q: So is now the time to buy Pinduoduo?

I don't give buy or sell recommendations; I'll just share my thoughts.

Good investing requires three conditions:

① The right business
✅
Low-price e-commerce saves users money. Clear model, extremely high efficiency.
② The right people
⚠️
Management execution is solid, but capital allocation needs improvement.
③ The right price
✅
PE 9x, FCF yield 18%, net cash at half of market cap.

Two ✅, one ⚠️. When all three are ✅, that is the best time to buy.

The problem now is not price, but uncertainty— Whether Temu can succeed and whether domestic growth can continue will take time to verify. If you can bear this uncertainty, buy. If it keeps you up at night, don't.

Investing is simple, but not easy.

Q: If the stock market closed for five years starting tomorrow, would you be willing to hold PDD at the current price?

I do.

Five years from now, Pinduoduo should be earning more money than it does today. The domestic main site is still generating cash flow, and in five years Temu will either have proven itself or been shut down. If Temu succeeds, Pinduoduo will become a truly global company.
