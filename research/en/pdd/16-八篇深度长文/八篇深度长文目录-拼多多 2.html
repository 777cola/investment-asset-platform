Part One: The Misunderstood Pinduoduo — You Are Not Its Target User ▶
📄 Full text approx. 14,438 characters | 📅 2026/07/05
When your spending level differs from that of Pinduoduo's users, your judgment of the company may be wrong.

Of China's 1.4 billion people, only about 350 million live in tier-1 and tier-2 cities. The remaining 1.05 billion are the true foundation of China's consumer market—and Pinduoduo is the only e-commerce platform designed entirely for those 1.05 billion.

I. A phenomenon that confounds the middle class

You rarely see anyone share Pinduoduo links on WeChat Moments—if your feed consists mainly of white-collar workers in tier-1 cities. Yet at the same time, Pinduoduo has over 800 million annual active buyers (about 870 million by the end of 2025) and more than 700 million monthly active users.

Who are these 870 million people? Why don't they use Taobao and JD.com?

A 2024 consumer survey published by Peking University shows that Pinduoduo users spend about RMB 350 a month on online shopping, just 58% of Taobao users and 42% of JD.com users. Yet they shop most frequently — 7.8 orders a month on average, above Taobao's 5.2 and JD.com's 3.1.

Low ticket size × high frequency, this is Pinduoduo's consumer engine, completely different from traditional e-commerce.

1.2 The truth about the market beyond the Fifth Ring Road

"Outside the Fifth Ring Road" is not a geographic concept, but rather aConsumer psychology and the segmentation of market structure。

In tier-1 and tier-2 cities, the consumer logic runs quality → brand → price. Users will pay RMB 500 for a pair of Nike shoes because the brand vouches for the quality.

The consumption logic in markets beyond the Fifth Ring Road is:Price → Quality → BrandUsers first check whether the price is acceptable, then whether the product works, and only then consider the brand. Shoes made in the same white-label factory sell for RMB 80 on Pinduoduo but may be priced at RMB 300 at a mall counter—for users outside the Fifth Ring Road, paying an extra RMB 220 for a label is irrational.

This is not "consumption downgrading"; rather, it isThe return of consumer rationality— only the rational boundary differs across income levels.

II. The RMB 200 gap: a microeconomics case study

2.1 The real ledger of consumers outside the Fifth Ring Road

Let us build a profile of a typical Pinduoduo user's life:

Xiao Wang, 35, works at a garment factory in a county town in Henan, earning about RMB 4,500 a month.

Monthly fixed expenses:

  • Rent/mortgage: RMB 1,200
  • Food: RMB 1,500 (family of three)
  • Child education: RMB 500
  • Transport/Communications: RMB 300
  • Daily incidentals: RMB 400
  • Remaining disposable: RMB 600

This RMB 600 is the entire monthly budget for Wang's family of three on "non-essentials" — clothing, snacks, household goods, and the occasional small appliance to improve their lives.

Under this budget constraint, every purchase is aOptimal allocation decision：

  • Pinduoduo down jacket: RMB 80 → RMB 520 left
  • Taobao equivalent: RMB 120 → only RMB 480 left
  • Tmall branded items: RMB 300 → only RMB 300 left

The gap between RMB 80 and RMB 120 is RMB 40. To Xiao Wang, RMB 40 is:

  • A child's weekly allowance
  • The cost of a family's weekend splurge meal
  • One month's mobile phone bill

So Wang chose Pinduoduo not because he was "chasing bargains," but because heKnow rationally— There is no qualitative difference in warmth retention between an RMB 80 down jacket and an RMB 120 one. The extra RMB 40 is a stack of brand premium and distribution costs, not a "better product."

2.2 Asymmetry of price elasticity

In economics,Price elasticity of demandThis can explain Pinduoduo's success. For non-essential consumer goods, the price elasticity of tier-1 and tier-2 city users is about -0.8 to -1.2 (a 10% price cut lifts purchase volume by 8-12%). But for users outside the fifth ring, elasticity runs as high as -2.5 to -4.0.

This means that when Pinduoduo cuts the price of a product from RMB 100 to RMB 50, purchase intent among users outside the fifth ring may rise by more than 3.0 times.

Going further, Pinduoduo achieved through its group-buying mechanismPrice cuts without profit erosion— The volume growth from price cuts far exceeds the magnitude of the price decline, and merchants' total profit is actually higher. This is the opposite of the economics of "cheap grain hurts farmers":Volume up, price down。

III. It is not that "cheap goods are never good," but that "good goods need not be expensive"

Chinese consumers harbor a deep-rooted prejudice against "cheap goods." That prejudice stems from an era's collective memory: from the 1990s through the early 21st century, low-priced goods in the Chinese market were indeed often shoddy—unbranded, unlicensed products from small rural workshops, counterfeit and substandard goods.

But Pinduoduo's "low price" differs fundamentally from the "cheapness" of that era.

3.1 The product of China's excess manufacturing capacity

China is the world's largest manufacturing nation, with 41 major industrial categories, 207 medium industrial categories and 666 minor industrial categories, making it the only country with the full range of industrial sectors.

A key feature of this vast manufacturing system is:Severe overcapacity。

Take power banks as an example:

  • About 90% of the world's power banks are made in China
  • Annual production capacity of about 2 billion units
  • Actual demand is about 800 million to 1 billion units
  • Capacity utilization below 50%

This means many factories hold first-rate production lines and equipment and skilled workers, but lack orders. The choice they face is:

  • Taking OEM orders from major brands—thin margins (10-15% gross margin), but stable
  • Building your own brand requires heavy marketing spend with uncertain returns
  • Partnering with Pinduoduo — direct-to-consumer, cutting brand premiums and intermediaries

A growing number of factories are choosing a third path.

3.2 The industrial logic of C2M

Pinduoduo's C2M (Customer-to-Manufacturer) model, whose core logic can be expressed in one formula:

Terminal price = production cost + platform fee + logistics cost + reasonable profit

Compare this with the price structure of traditional e-commerce:

Traditional e-commerce price = production cost + brand premium (30-50%) + multi-tier distributors (20-40%) + marketing expenses (10-20%) + logistics costs (5-10%) + platform fees (3-5%) + reasonable profit

Under the C2M model, what gets cut is:

This is not "small profits, high volume"—this isStructural cost reductionPinduoduo is not eroding profit to buy volume; it is rebuilding its cost structure.

4.4 A minimalist product decision path

Decision path of traditional e-commerce:

Search → browse search results → view details → read reviews → compare products → add to cart → place order

Pinduoduo's decision path:

Scroll product feed → place order (or swipe away)

This minimalist approach rests on two core mechanisms:

  • AI recommendations replace search: Pinduoduo's homepage recommendation feed captures nearly all traffic. Users do not need to search—the system uses behavioral data and deep-learning models to predict what each user is most likely to buy.
  • Low prices eliminate decision costs: When product prices are low enough, users' decision time shrinks sharply. Buying a RMB 30 T-shirt does not require the repeated comparison that a RMB 300 T-shirt does. Pinduoduo has pushed the prices of most products into the "no need to hesitate" range.

The result of this design is:

  • Average time from opening the app to placing an order: about 47 seconds
  • Taobao: about 102 seconds
  • JD.com: about 158 seconds

Time is money— The shorter the decision, the higher the conversion rate and the more efficient the traffic utilization.

Six: Cognitive traps—why smart people also misjudge Pinduoduo

6.1 The elite illusion

A common cognitive bias in investment and business analysis:Elite illusion—Projecting one's own spending habits and values onto everyone else.

This is Pinduoduo's biggest "understanding gap."

Bulge-bracket investment banking analysts, private-fund researchers, tech-media reporters — these groups happen to be exactly Pinduoduo's "non-users":

  • Monthly income RMB 30,000–100,000
  • Accustomed to shopping on Tmall, JD.com and Sam's Club
  • No one in my WeChat Moments is sharing Pinduoduo links
  • Impressions of Pinduoduo remain stuck on negatives such as "knockoff," "counterfeit" and "bargain-haggling."

But the spending habits of these people differ completely from those of the 1.05 billion among China's 1.4 billion population. Judging a mass-market business model by the consumption logic of the elite is like evaluating Shaxian Delicacies with Michelin judges' standards—the dimensions are entirely different.

6.2 Historical path dependence

In the early days of Pinduoduo's rise from 2015 to 2018, the platform did host a large volume of knockoff goods and counterfeit or shoddy products:

  • "Liri" laundry detergent (imitating "Liby")
  • "Kangshuaifu" instant noodles (a knockoff of "Master Kong")
  • "Yue Li Yue" biscuits (imitating "Oreo")

These phenomena branded Pinduoduo as "low-end" and "counterfeit." For many people, that perception froze at that moment—even after Pinduoduo later cleaned up these problems on a large scale.

In fact, Pinduoduo's anti-counterfeiting efforts may be the most aggressive among Chinese e-commerce platforms:

  • 2020: More than 100 million counterfeit listings removed
  • 2021: shut down more than 100,000 stores involved in counterfeiting
  • 2022: Launched the "Brand Pavilion," bringing in official flagship stores
  • 2023-2024: Built a dual AI plus manual review system

But perception always lags reality. Many people still believe "domestic phones are no good" — even though Huawei and Xiaomi have already proven themselves in global markets.

6.3 Brand worship and consumerism

The deeper reason may be:China's elite class has an almost religious worship of brands。

In Chinese society, "what you buy" is an important expression of identity:

  • Using an iPhone = tech taste
  • Wearing Nike = fashion
  • Drinking Starbucks = petit-bourgeois flair

In this cultural context, the "de-branding" that Pinduoduo represents is a challenge to this status system. Not everyone can accept the fact that "a RMB 30 rice cooker works just as well as a RMB 300 one"—because it undermines the consumer worldview they have already built.

But this is precisely Pinduoduo's "evolutionary" opportunity: as more consumers begin to question the value of brand premiums, Pinduoduo's user base will "counterattack" from outside the Fifth Ring Road into the Fifth Ring Road.

Seven: Pinduoduo's growth story—from "overlooked" to "unignorable"

7.1 2015: Born in the "Red Ocean of E-commerce"

In 2015, China's e-commerce landscape was as follows:

  • Taobao/Tmall: market share ~60%
  • JD.com: market share ~25%
  • Other: ~15%

Almost every analyst assumes the e-commerce landscape is settled—that the Alibaba-JD.com "duopoly" cannot be shaken.

That same year, Huang Zheng founded Pinhaohuo, the predecessor of Pinduoduo. Pinhaohuo began as a social e-commerce platform for fruit — users grouped their orders to buy fruit at prices 30-50% below market.

In September 2015, Pinhaohuo and Pinduoduo merged, officially launching Pinduoduo.

7.2 2016-2017: The super dividend of the WeChat ecosystem

Pinduoduo's biggest external tailwind is:WeChat's social ecosystem。

In 2016, WeChat's monthly active users already exceeded 800 million. But WeChat's monetization was just beginning—WeChat Official Accounts and WeChat Pay had launched, yet the concept of "WeChat e-commerce" did not exist.

Pinduoduo made full use of WeChat's social viral mechanism:

  • Group-buy links can spread through WeChat groups and Moments
  • WeChat Pay provides a complete transaction loop
  • WeChat's 1 billion users are Pinduoduo's potential user pool

By the end of 2016, Pinduoduo's user base topped 100 million. By the end of 2017, it exceeded 300 million. The steepness of that growth curve ranks second in Chinese internet history only to WeChat itself.

7.3 2018: Doubts from the Moment of Listing

In July 2018, Pinduoduo listed on Nasdaq at an issue price of USD 19, valuing it at about USD 24 billion.

The public opinion environment before and after the listing was extremely hostile:

  • "Counterfeit platform"
  • "Consumption downgrading"
  • "The next e-commerce player to fall"
  • "Sustaining growth by burning cash"

On the day of the IPO bell-ringing, founder Colin Huang did not travel to the US, joining instead by video link from Shanghai — yet even so, media coverage still focused on "knockoffs" and "counterfeits."

In all of 2018, Pinduoduo lost RMB 10.8 billion. The mainstream market view was that this model was unsustainable.

7.4 2019-2020: The comeback powered by RMB 10 billion in subsidies

In mid-2019, Pinduoduo launched "Ten Billion Subsidies" — a marketing campaign in which the platform subsidizes branded goods with real money.

This strategy is extremely clever:

  • Core objective: Attracting tier-1 and tier-2 city users, shedding the "low-end label"
  • Merchandise selection strategy: iPhone, Dyson, SK-II and other brands familiar to tier-1 and tier-2 users
  • Subsidy method: Platform direct subsidies ensure the lowest prices across all platforms
  • Trust mechanism: Pledge "fake one, compensate ten" to build brand trust

The effect of the RMB 10 billion subsidy was immediate:

  • By the end of 2019, the share of users in tier-1 and tier-2 cities had risen from 15% to 25%
  • Branded goods rose from 12% to 28% of GMV
  • Annual active buyers rose from 420 million to 590 million

More importantly, the billions in subsidies made the market realize:Pinduoduo is not scraping small profits in a "cheap goods" market; it is a platform capable of penetrating the mainstream consumer market.。

7.5 2021-2023: From growth to profitability

In the first quarter of 2021, Pinduoduo achieved quarterly profitability for the first time.

This marks a milestone in the history of Chinese e-commerce: Pinduoduo went from loss to profit in under six years. By comparison:

  • JD.com: profitable only after 12 years
  • Amazon: profitable only after 14 years
  • Meituan: first profitable after nine years

From 2021 to 2023, Pinduoduo's net profit grew from RMB 7.7 billion to RMB 60 billion—an nearly 8x increase in 3 years.

But this stage is also a period of "fragmentation":

  • On the consumer side, Pinduoduo continued to grow, with its brand image improving steadily
  • On the investor side, the market has persistently assigned Pinduoduo a low valuation—from 2021 to 2023, its PE long ranged between 15x and 25x, far below Alibaba's 20-30x and Meituan's 30-50x

The market still does not trust Pinduoduo. But the data has already told the real story.

7.6 2024-2025: Temu's global expansion and profit release

In 2024, Pinduoduo's full-year net profit reached RMB 112.4 billion, up 87% year on year. Meanwhile, Temu launched in 60+ countries worldwide, becoming Pinduoduo's second growth curve.

In 2025, the domestic business remained strong, but Temu's losses weighed on overall profit. Net profit fell back to RMB 97.8 billion. Yet margins at the domestic business (Pinduoduo's main site plus Duoduo Maicai) were in fact still improving.

Pinduoduo has completed its evolution from "Chinese e-commerce dark horse" to "global e-commerce disruptor." Yet it remains widely misunderstood.

Eight, the challenge of continued growth—the real risks

While setting the record straight on Pinduoduo, we must also objectively recognize its challenges:

8.1 Balance of the merchant ecosystem

Pinduoduo's extreme low prices for consumers come at the cost of strong bargaining power over merchants:

  • High margin requirement
  • Strict after-sales rules (refund only)

Total revenue model: R = GMV × take rate (monetization rate)

Pinduoduo's take rate is about 4-5% (versus Tmall's roughly 5-6%).

Profit = R - RMB 20 billion - 0.18R - marketing expenses

When R< 244亿时：即使没有营销费用，也在亏损

When R > RMB 50 billion: profitable even with a marketing expense ratio of 30%

When R > RMB 150 billion: scale effects begin to unleash explosively

Scale effects are not linear but exponential.This is the core financial code of the platform economy.

  • The self-operated model is too heavy — it requires control over the supply chain, warehousing and quality control

1.4 Huang Zheng's three key strategic decisions

Decision one: target the market outside the Fifth Ring Road (2015)

In 2015, China's e-commerce landscape was already a duopoly of "Alibaba + JD.com." Almost every entrepreneur was thinking about how to be "better than Taobao" or "more distinctive than JD.com."

Decision 2: Make social viral growth the core engine (2016)

The concept of group buying was not invented by Huang Zheng—before him, Groupon (the American pioneer of group buying) had already globalized the group-buying model.

  • User A starts a group buy → shares it with WeChat friend B → B joins the group → both save money
  • B finds it a good deal → starts a new group buy → shares it with C → repeat

This mechanism where "every consumer is a salesperson" keeps Pinduoduo's customer acquisition cost extremely low.

Why did only Pinduoduo pull it off?

  • The boost from the WeChat ecosystem: 2016 was the period of peak activity on WeChat's social graph
  • Extreme low pricing: The savings from group buying are enough to incentivize sharing behavior.
  • Gamified design: Bargaining, assistance, cash red packets and other mechanics make "sharing" fun

Pinduoduo's C2M model is: factory → consumer

Six: The Core Controversy Around Pinduoduo's Management

6.1 Has the "Benfen" culture turned into "wolf-like" aggression?

The "benfen" principle proposed by Duan Yongping originally meant "do the right thing, do what should be done." But in Pinduoduo's execution, "benfen" gradually evolved into an extreme wolf-culture.

Common pain points anonymously vented online by Pinduoduo employees:

  • Excessively long working hours: 9-10-6 (9am to 10pm, six days a week) is the norm
  • Hyper-competition: KPI rankings for each department are made public, with a bottom-elimination system
  • Lacks a sense of boundaries: Work group chats bombard you even on weekends and late at night
  • Insufficient humanistic care: Benefits are about average for the industry

Pinduoduo's response: "We are a startup (even with over RMB 100 billion in profit), and it is not yet time to rest."

The cost of this culture is:High turnover, high employee burnout, limited room for innovation.But the benefits it brings are also significant:Exceptional execution efficiency, rigorous cost control and rapid iteration.

6.2 "Harvesting" merchants or "win-win"?

This controversy is essentially a "trust problem" — management says "money is worth more in my hands than in yours," but investors see no evidence.

I. Why Douyin e-commerce is a "different species"

1.1 The fundamental difference between the two e-commerce paradigms

Traditional e-commerce (Taobao, JD.com, Pinduoduo) follows the same logic:Users arrive with demand, and the platform helps them find products.Search, compare prices, place orders—this is the standard consumer behavior path on e-commerce platforms. Although Pinduoduo used social fission to lower customer acquisition costs, it remains fundamentally a "demand-driven" retail channel.

Douyin e-commerce follows a completely different logic:Users have no demand; platforms create demand through content.While scrolling short videos and watching livestreams, users have their desire to buy sparked by the content—this is not "I buy what I need" but "I see this product is cool, so I decide I need it."

1.2 Douyin e-commerce development timeline

Douyin e-commerce's development can be divided into four stages:

Phase 1: exploratory period (2018-2019)

  • In March 2018, Douyin partnered with Taobao to test "short-video commerce" — users saw product links on Douyin and jumped to Taobao to complete the transaction
  • In 2019, Douyin launched a product showcase feature, allowing certain creators to display product links
  • At this stage, Douyin e-commerce is essentially a "traffic upstream" for Taobao: Douyin supplies traffic, Taobao completes the transaction.

Phase 2: Self-build period (2020-2021)

  • In June 2020, ByteDance formally established its e-commerce business unit, elevating it to a first-tier department.
  • In October 2020, Douyin cut off external e-commerce links (Taobao links were restricted) and began building its own closed e-commerce loop.
  • In 2021, Douyin e-commerce GMV exceeded RMB 800 billion (including GMV redirected to Taobao; self-operated GMV was about RMB 300 billion)
  • Douyin Store (Douyin's self-operated e-commerce system) rolls out on a large scale

Phase 3: Outbreak period (2022-2023)

  • In 2022, Douyin e-commerce GMV surpassed RMB 1.4 trillion
  • In 2023, Douyin e-commerce GMV surpassed RMB 2.2 trillion (including livestreaming, short video and marketplace)
  • Interest-based e-commerce model validated; Douyin Mall (shelf e-commerce) launches
  • Douyin e-commerce upgraded its strategy from "interest-based e-commerce" to "omnichannel interest-based e-commerce" — covering both content scenarios and shelf scenarios

Phase 4: Maturity (2024-2026)

  • In 2024, Douyin e-commerce GMV was approximately RMB 3.5 trillion
  • In 2025, Douyin e-commerce further improved its infrastructure: launching a standalone e-commerce app (rumored), upgrading after-sales service, and building a logistics system
  • In 2026, Douyin E-commerce targets GMV above RMB 4 trillion, becoming China's second-largest e-commerce platform (after Taobao Tmall)

1.3 ByteDance's E-commerce Strategy Evolution

ByteDance's e-commerce push has gone through a strategic upgrade from a "traffic monetization tool" to a "second growth curve."

Early stage (2018-2019): traffic monetization mindset

Within ByteDance, e-commerce is positioned as "an extension of advertising monetization" — users see an ad on Douyin, click and buy, and the platform collects an advertising fee. At this stage, ByteDance earns "advertising fees," not "transaction commissions."

Mid-term (2020-2021): Platform thinking

When ByteDance realized that the ceiling for e-commerce GMV was far higher than for advertising revenue, it began building its own e-commerce infrastructure. The key decision at this stage was "cutting off Taobao links" — a risky move, because it meant Douyin had to give up the vast product catalog Taobao provided and start from scratch on its own.

But it proved correct: if Douyin were merely a traffic funnel for Taobao, its value ceiling would be "a highly efficient advertising channel." Only by building its own closed e-commerce loop could Douyin capture the full value of e-commerce GMV.

Current (2022–): ecosystem thinking

ByteDance no longer treats Douyin e-commerce as a "transaction platform" but as a composite ecosystem of "transaction + content + social + local services." Douyin e-commerce, Douyin local services (dining group buys), Douyin Pay and Douyin logistics are forming a closed loop.

II. In-depth analysis of Douyin e-commerce's competitive advantages

2.1 Advantage 1: Time — the scarcest resource on the internet

Chinese users spend an average of more than 2 hours a day on Douyin. Pinduoduo is about 30 minutes. WeChat is about 80 minutes.

Time is the ceiling for e-commerce GMV.The logic is simple: the more time users spend on the platform, the more opportunities they have to be shown products, and the greater the likelihood they place an order.

Douyin's 2 hours vs Pinduoduo's 30 minutes — a 4x gap. That means at equal recommendation efficiency, Douyin has 4x Pinduoduo's "monetizable time."

Let us make an estimate:

  • Douyin has about 900 million MAU and 120 minutes of average daily time spent per user.
  • Daily Douyin user time = 900 million × 120 minutes = 108 billion minutes
  • Assume e-commerce content's share rises from 5% to 15% (Douyin actively controls this to avoid over-commercialization that would damage user experience)
  • Daily time available for e-commerce is 108 billion × 15% = 16.2 billion minutes
  • Compare with Pinduoduo: about 800 million MAU, 30 minutes of daily use per user, 24 billion minutes of total daily usage

Douyin's "e-commerce-ready time" has already approached 70% of Pinduoduo's.And that gap is narrowing—because Douyin is still growing, while Pinduoduo's user time has nearly hit its ceiling.

2.2 Advantage 2: Recommendation efficiency — the world's best content recommendation system

Douyin's recommendation algorithm may be the world's best content recommendation system. This is not marketing talk—the data shows that Douyin's recommendation accuracy (user dwell time, engagement rate) is significantly higher than that of competitors such as Facebook, YouTube and Instagram.

By transferring this capability to product recommendations, Douyin has a chance to become "better than you at knowing what you want to buy."

Why is Douyin's recommendation more effective?

The reason is that Douyin possesses "dual-characteristic data":

  • Characteristics of goods consumption— Douyin knows what you have bought and browsed

When these two datasets are combined, Douyin can achieve "recommending beauty products to people who like watching beauty videos"—a level of precision far higher than a pure e-commerce platform's "people who bought XX also bought YY."

2.3 Advantage 3: Merchant ecosystem — the humanoid shelf

Douyin has a vast creator ecosystem. Every influencer and every uploader can be a "human shelf" — they produce content, and consumers place orders. This ecosystem is unique.

Three tiers of the creator ecosystem:

Top tier: leading KOLs (over 1 million followers)

  • Characteristics: built-in traffic, strong bargaining power
  • Representatives: Crazy Little Yang Brother, East Buy, Li Jiaqi (Taobao cross-platform)
  • Value: a single livestream can generate GMV of tens of millions to RMB 100 million
  • Challenge: risk of over-reliance on top streamers (platforms need to manage this)

Mid-tier: vertical KOLs (100,000–1 million followers)

  • Traits: influential in vertical niches, high fan loyalty
  • Representatives: tech bloggers, beauty bloggers, food bloggers
  • Value: precise reach to specific consumer groups
  • Advantage: conversion rates are typically higher than top KOLs because fan trust is stronger

Bottom tier: ordinary creators (10,000-100,000 followers)

  • Features: broad content coverage and large volume
  • Value: creates a long-tail effect that covers countless niche categories
  • Model: CPS commission — pay only on sales, near-zero risk for the platform

The power of this ecosystem lies in:Taobao/Pinduoduo's merchant ecosystem is centered on "products" (merchants list products, users come to buy), while Douyin's merchant ecosystem is centered on "people" (creators recommend products, followers come to buy). The latter has a stronger emotional connection and a shorter conversion path.

2.4 Advantage 4: Data flywheel

Douyin e-commerce has a "data flywheel" that other platforms find hard to replicate:

More users → more content → more consumption data → better recommendation algorithms → higher conversion → more merchants onboard → more products → more users (loop)

Once this flywheel starts spinning, it has a very strong self-reinforcing effect.

  • On the user side:The more precise the recommendations, the more willing users are to buy on Douyin
  • For merchants:The higher the conversion rate, the more willing merchants are to commit resources and cut prices
  • For creators:The higher the commission income, the more willing creators are to produce e-commerce content

III. Douyin e-commerce's disadvantages and soft spots

3.1 Weakness 1: Supply chain — an empire of traffic, an infant in supply chain

Douyin's strength is traffic, not supply chain.

Pinduoduo spent eight years building its C2M factory network. The vast array of white-label and factory-direct goods on Pinduoduo originates from China's massive manufacturing base. Pinduoduo and these factories are now deeply bound to each other: Pinduoduo gives the factories orders, and the factories give Pinduoduo their lowest prices.

Douyin cannot replicate this system overnight.

  • In 2024 it launched the "Douyin factory store" model, emulating Pinduoduo's C2M approach
  • Increase traffic support for white-label goods in 2025

But supply-chain depth takes time to build—it cannot be solved by money alone. Trust between factories and platforms, the alignment of processes, and the debugging of customized production all take time.

3.2 Weakness 2: User mindset—"come here for content" or "come here to shop"?

Users go to Pinduoduo because "it's cheapest here" — this is not emotional attachment but a rational choice. Users go to Douyin because "there's good content here," and buy things along the way.

This discrepancy creates two fundamental problems:

First, the purchasing scenario is unstable.If Douyin's recommendation algorithm changes one day, or users lose interest in the content, e-commerce GMV will fall accordingly. Pinduoduo's users come for the "savings" — as long as Pinduoduo stays cheap, users will come back.

Second, weak active shopping mindset.When users want to buy something, they open Pinduoduo (because "it's cheap there"), not Douyin (because "there are no videos I want to watch"). Douyin is addressing this gap through Douyin Mall (shelf-based e-commerce), but user habits cannot be changed overnight.

3.3 Weakness 3: Logistics system

Pinduoduo relies on J&T Express, part of the Pinduoduo ecosystem, for strong logistics coverage in lower-tier cities and rural markets. Douyin E-commerce currently depends mainly on third-party carriers (SF Express, ZTO, YTO, etc.) for logistics and has no logistics network of its own.

Logistics cost comparison:

  • Pinduoduo (via J&T Express): logistics cost per order of about RMB 2.5-3.5 (leveraging scale advantages)
  • Douyin e-commerce (third-party): logistics cost per order about RMB 3.5-5.5

The difference is small, but under Pinduoduo's "extreme low price" framework, every RMB 1 of cost difference shows up in the final price.

3.4 Weakness 4: After-sales service pressure

Douyin e-commerce's return rate (about 15-25%) is significantly higher than Pinduoduo's (about 5-10%). The reason is straightforward: after impulse purchases, users are prone to regret.

Knock-on effects of high return rates:

  • Increase logistics costs (return logistics fees)
  • Higher customer service costs (processing returns requires substantial manual labor)
  • Affects merchant profits (merchants bear part of the return cost)
  • Affects user experience (a cumbersome returns process lowers repurchase rates)

Douyin is using "seven-day no-reason returns" and "return shipping insurance" to reduce users' concerns about returns, but these measures further squeeze the platform's profitability.

Eight, the ultimate judgment: the real threat Douyin e-commerce poses to Pinduoduo

8.1 Three possible scenarios

Scenario 1: Douyin e-commerce and Pinduoduo coexist (probability: 60%)

The best possible outcome. Douyin e-commerce and Pinduoduo each occupy distinct consumption scenarios:

  • Douyin: content-driven "impulse consumption" — beauty, apparel, food, novel products
  • Pinduoduo: demand-driven "rational consumption" — household goods, agricultural products, 3C accessories

In this scenario, Pinduoduo holds a market share of around 15%, while Douyin e-commerce reaches 20-25%, but mainly by taking share from Taobao and JD.com rather than from Pinduoduo.

Scenario 2: Douyin e-commerce squeezes Pinduoduo's share (probability: 25%)

If Douyin e-commerce succeeds in building a "shelf scenario" (Douyin Mall) and users develop the habit of "searching for products on Douyin," then Pinduoduo will lose share in core categories. Ultimately Pinduoduo will fall to a 12-13% market share, while Douyin e-commerce exceeds 25%.

Scenario 3: Douyin e-commerce poses limited threat, Pinduoduo holds its ground (probability: 15%)

If Douyin e-commerce's content monetization progress stalls (users resent ad-driven e-commerce content, merchants are unhappy with commission rates), or if Pinduoduo further extends its supply-chain advantage (AI-driven C2M customization), Pinduoduo could hold its 16-17% share.

8.2 Our assessment

We see Scenario 1 as the most likely.The reasons are as follows:

  • Two consumption scenarios genuinely exist and are not mutually exclusive.Users get inspired to buy on Douyin and compare prices on Pinduoduo. The two scenarios serve different user needs and decision logic; one does not replace the other.
  • Pinduoduo's supply-chain moat is deepening— Each year more factories join Pinduoduo's C2M system, and Douyin will struggle to replicate this network effect.
  • Pinduoduo has no plans to fight Douyin on contentThis is strategic restraint, and strategic wisdom. Pinduoduo knows it cannot beat Douyin on content, so it chooses not to fight.
  • Pinduoduo still has room to grow— Penetration in lower-tier markets still has room to rise, and the upside for agricultural products flowing to cities is even greater.
  • Douyin e-commerce faces challenges of its own— Over-commercialization could damage the content ecosystem (user aversion to e-commerce content is rising), and livestream e-commerce growth is slowing.

8.3 But for investors, one unavoidable question

Even if Pinduoduo holds its share, slower growth is a certainty.

  • 2019-2023: Pinduoduo GMV grew about 50% annually
  • 2024-2026: Pinduoduo GMV may grow only 5-10% annually

The valuation logic needs to shift from "high-growth company" to "value company".PE has fallen from 25-30x previously (a growth premium) to 9-10x now (a value discount), essentially a rational reflection by the market of declining growth.

But Pinduoduo, at 9x PE and an 18% free cash flow yield, remains an attractive investment even under pressure from Douyin e-commerce — provided you believe Pinduoduo can defend its profit base against Douyin's onslaught.

Douyin's threat to Pinduoduo is real but not fatal. Pinduoduo will most likely hold a 14-16% market share amid Douyin's pressure—it does not need to win, only avoid losing. And a 9x PE already reflects a considerable degree of pessimistic expectation.

9.2 The specific path by which Douyin erodes Pinduoduo's profit

Douyin e-commerce's erosion of Pinduoduo's profit does not happen by "directly taking profit away" but through the following paths:

Path 1: Divert GMV → reduce Pinduoduo's scale effects

Pinduoduo's business model depends heavily on economies of scale: the larger the order volume, the stronger its bargaining power with suppliers, the lower its logistics costs, and the more thinly customer acquisition costs are spread.

As Douyin e-commerce takes incremental market share from Pinduoduo, Pinduoduo's economies-of-scale growth is slowing—meaning Pinduoduo can no longer lower unit costs by continuously expanding scale as it once did.

Path two: push up customer acquisition costs

As Douyin e-commerce became a major competitor for traffic, the cost of acquiring e-commerce users across the internet rose. Douyin itself is also competing for e-commerce users, forcing Pinduoduo to spend more (coupons, subsidies, advertising) to sustain user growth.

III. In-depth analysis of the US de minimis policy

3.1 What is the de minimis rule?

Section 321 of the U.S. Tariff Act of 1930 sets out the de minimis rule:Goods valued under USD 800 entering the US are exempt from tariffs, customs inspection and declaration.In 2016, the Obama administration raised this threshold from $200 to $800 to reduce customs workload and lower the clearance cost of small parcels.

What this means for Temu:

The $800 threshold means that nearly all Temu products have a per-package value within this limit. With Temu's average order value of $10-15, a package containing 3-5 items still falls far below $800.

If not exempted, what does Temu face?

Assume de minimis is repealed or the threshold is lowered to $50-100:

  • Each parcel is subject to tariffs of about 15-25%
  • A $10 package carries about $1.5-2.5 in tariffs
  • This directly implies Temu's per-order cost rises by USD 2-3
  • For Temu, with its extremely low gross margin, this could be the inflection point between profit and loss

3.2 The political game over de minimis

Why does the US want to shut down this policy?

  • Protect domestic US manufacturing— USD 2.99 T-shirts are devastating to American apparel manufacturing. The AFL-CIO is one of the main forces pushing to abolish de minimis.
  • Trade protectionism against Chinese goods—Against the backdrop of the China-US trade war, restricting duty-free entry of low-value Chinese goods into the US is the "politically correct" choice.
  • Customs workload surges— As Temu and SHEIN's businesses surged, small parcels processed daily by US Customs jumped from about 2 million in 2015 to about 5 million in 2024. The customs system is overwhelmed.
  • Tax fairness— Local US retailers such as Walmart and Target must pay tariffs on imported goods, while Temu and SHEIN's small parcels can enter duty-free — which US merchants see as unfair competition.

  • Guide users to buy more items (multipacks, bundles) to raise average order value
  • Even if tax is levied on each order, the tax cost spread across each item will still fall
  • Drawback: the strategy of raising average order value conflicts with Temu's "ultra-low price" positioning.

  • Tariff exemptions secured through trade agreements such as the USMCA
  • But this approach takes time to rebuild supply chains, and factories in Southeast Asia lag Chinese plants in efficiency and cost control.

Option D: Absorb tariffs (short-term strategy)

  • Temu absorbs tariff costs itself rather than passing them on to consumers
  • This will further squeeze Temu's gross margin but help sustain its "low-price" user perception
  • Before profitability, this strategy is unsustainable

Our view:The most likely outcome is that Temu will combine options A and D—building some local warehousing in the US while absorbing part of the tariff cost itself. This will delay Temu's path to profitability, but it will not be fatal.

1.2 Duan Yongping's Investment Philosophy System

Duan Yongping's investment philosophy blends Buffett's value-investing principles with his own experience running businesses. We summarize it as "one core, three criteria, four principles".

One core principle: buying a stock is buying a company

Practical implications of this thesis:

  • Ignore short-term share price moves—a falling share price is instead a reason to buy (if the company's value is unchanged)
  • What matters is the quality of the business itself—not candlestick patterns, not main-force fund flows
  • Before making an investment decision, ask yourself: if the stock market closed for 5 years, would I still be willing to hold it?

Three criteria: good business, good management, good price

This is Duan Yongping's core framework for selecting companies. Not one element can be missing.

Four principles:

Method 1: Kelly Criterion

f* = (bp - q) / b
Where:
  f* = fraction of capital to bet
  b = odds (profit/loss)
  p = probability of winning
  q = probability of losing (1-p)
