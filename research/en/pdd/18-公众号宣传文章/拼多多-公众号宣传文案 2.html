PDD Is Being Abandoned by the World — But That Is Precisely the Best Time to Buy In July 2026, PDD's share price was USD 82.39. PE 8.8x. How low is that? Among major global e-commerce platforms, Amazon trades at 40x PE, Alibaba at 15x, JD.com at 19x, and even eBay — growing slower than PDD — trades at 15x. PDD is the only large e-commerce company whose PE has fallen to single digits. More striking still is the 17.8% FCF yield. That means for every RMB 100 you put into PDD stock, you get back nearly RMB 18 of cash from its books each year. In a world where bank deposits yield under 2%, that number looks like a mistake. But market logic is never random. It assigns PDD such a low valuation for a reason. I Start with PDD's 2025 scorecard: revenue of RMB 431.9 billion, up 9.7% year on year. This is PDD's slowest growth since listing. In 2023 it still grew 89.7%, then 59% in 2024, and by 2025 only single digits remained. The cliff-edge deceleration is not a management failure — user penetration has hit its ceiling. PDD's annual active buyers already exceed 800 million; essentially every Chinese person with internet access is already its user. Net profit was RMB 97.8 billion, down 13% year on year. This is PDD's first annual profit decline. Gross margin fell from 60.9% to 56.3%, the third consecutive year of decline. Looking only at these numbers, you would think this is a company sliding into mediocrity. But numbers do not tell the whole story. The gross margin decline is not because PDD has become worse at selling things, but because of Temu — the overseas business grows fast but carries a far lower gross margin than the domestic main site. As Temu's share of total revenue rises, overall gross margin is "dragged down." An analogy: you run a profitable hotpot restaurant with a 70% gross margin. Then you open a milk tea shop with only a 40% gross margin. When the milk tea shop's revenue share goes from 10% to 30%, your overall gross margin falls. That does not mean your hotpot restaurant is failing — only that your business mix has changed. The same goes for the net profit decline. PDD's investment in Temu in 2025 far exceeded prior years; this is strategic spending, not operational deterioration. The number that truly deserves attention is free cash flow. FCF was RMB 105.8 billion, with FCF/net profit = 1.08. That means every yuan of profit turned into hard cash, not paper profit eaten by receivables. Five consecutive years of FCF exceeding net profit — you can barely find a second company among Chinese internet firms that does this. II The most absurd number on PDD's balance sheet is RMB 416.9 billion. That is its net cash — cash plus short-term investments minus all interest-bearing debt. PDD's market cap at USD 82.39 is roughly RMB 829.3 billion. In other words, net cash accounts for 50% of market cap. Let us do a simple calculation. You spend RMB 100 to buy PDD stock; RMB 50 of that buys not its business but its cash. The remaining RMB 50 buys an e-commerce platform that generates nearly RMB 100 billion of cash each year. The annual return on that RMB 50 is RMB 18 — a 36% return. If this business were packaged whole and sold to you, would you take it? The market says no, because it does not believe the business can last. But that is not all. PDD's ROE has long stayed above 25%. In 2024 it even reached 36%. Across the entire Chinese internet industry, apart from Tencent at its peak, no second company has reached this level. This shows management has squeezed every yuan of shareholder capital to the maximum. Its operating expense ratio is only 34.7%. Alibaba is around 50%, JD.com around 40%. PDD serves 800 million users with 15,000 employees, generating nearly RMB 30 million of revenue per employee. Alibaba's headcount is 13 times its own, yet revenue per employee is only one-fifth. This efficiency gap is not a management issue but a DNA issue — PDD has been an "efficiency machine" from day one: a lean organization, AI-driven traffic allocation, and extremely low marketing costs. III So what is the market worried about? First, growth is gone. From 90% to 10% — a decline steep enough to make any investor reassess a company's long-term value. When a company no longer grows at high speed, its valuation center shifts from growth stock to value stock; a PE moving from 30x to 10x is normal. The question is: the market has already compressed PDD's PE to 8.8x — is that excessive? Second, can Temu actually work? This is the biggest uncertainty. Temu entered more than 30 countries within two years of launch, an astonishing pace, but it has never disclosed financials separately. Investors cannot tell whether it is a "strategic loss" or simply "cannot make money at all." If the US truly scraps the duty-free policy for small parcels under USD 800, Temu's entire cost structure would need rebuilding. Third, Douyin e-commerce is here. Douyin users average over 2 hours of daily use; PDD users average only 30 minutes. Douyin is expanding from "interest-based recommendation" into full-category e-commerce, posing the most direct threat to PDD's low-price market. Fourth, management does not care about shareholders. With over RMB 400 billion of cash on the books, there are no buybacks and no dividends. There are signs of small-scale buybacks starting in the second half of 2025, but the scale is far from enough. Every one of these concerns is real. IV But the key point is: these concerns are already fully priced in. A PE of 8.8x means the market is already assuming the worst case. When the share price has already priced in all the bad news, even slightly better news brings enormous upside. Let us run three scenarios. Bear case: Temu fails completely and domestic profit keeps declining. Even then, PDD's domestic main site remains a cash machine generating RMB 80-100 billion of FCF annually, and net cash stays substantial. In the worst case, your investment does not go to zero. Base case: Temu breaks even and domestic growth stabilizes. PDD's net profit in three years could reach about RMB 116 billion; at 12x PE, the share price would be about USD 146 — up 78% from now. Bull case: Temu turns profitable and the market re-rates the stock. PDD's net profit in three years could exceed RMB 130 billion; at 15x PE, the share price would exceed USD 200 — up 150% from now. This is a classic asymmetric return: limited downside in the worst case, substantial gains in the best case. V PDD's core investment logic is actually simple. It is a cash-printing machine driven by extreme efficiency, and the market's extremely pessimistic pricing gives you a chance to buy cheap. Net cash at half of market cap is your safety cushion. Temu's option value is priced at nearly zero by the market — that is your free lottery ticket. The opportunity cost is equally clear: if you do not buy now and wait until you see Temu's profit data, the stock may already have doubled. If you buy now and Temu fails, the domestic business you own is still worth today's price. PDD's management is not perfect. They have done far too little on shareholder returns. But their execution is top-tier — from the RMB 10 billion subsidy program to Temu's overseas expansion, from profit release to organizational leanness, every step has landed precisely. This is a company with flaws but cheap enough. In investing, perfection is not required — cheapness is. VI Writing this, I recall a line from Duan Yongping: "A good business at a 20% discount, a good price at a 50% discount." Is PDD's business worth a 20% discount? Its business model is clear — save users money, efficiency first. Growth fell from 90% to 10%, but its earning power has not collapsed, and FCF has exceeded net profit for five consecutive years. A 20% discount is reasonable. Is PDD's price worth a 50% discount? PE 8.8x, the lowest in global e-commerce. Net cash at half of market cap. For RMB 100 you buy a cash machine earning RMB 100 billion a year plus over RMB 400 billion of cash. A 50% discount is no exaggeration. 80% times 50% equals 40%. This is not precise mathematics, but it describes a fact: PDD's current price has deviated from its intrinsic value. Of course, undervalued companies do not necessarily rise right away. The market may stay pessimistic for another year or two, until some catalyst appears — Temu turning profitable, management buybacks, or simply a change in market sentiment. Investing in PDD requires not courage, but patience. (The above is investment research analysis only and does not constitute investment advice.
