Pinduoduo Is Being Abandoned by the World — But That Is Exactly the Best Time to Buy In July 2026, Pinduoduo's share price was USD 82.39. PE 8.8x. How low is that? Among major global e-commerce platforms, Amazon trades at 40x, Alibaba at 15x, JD.com at 19x, and even eBay — growing slower than Pinduoduo — trades at 15x. Pinduoduo is the only large e-commerce company whose PE has fallen to single digits. More striking still is the 17.8% FCF yield. That means for every 100 yuan you put into Pinduoduo stock, you get back nearly 18 yuan of cash from its books each year. In a world where bank deposits pay under 2%, that number looks like a mistake. But market logic is never random. There must be reasons it assigns Pinduoduo such a low valuation. I Start with Pinduoduo's 2025 report card: revenue of RMB 431.9 billion, up 9.7% year on year. This is the slowest growth since Pinduoduo went public. In 2023 it still grew 89.7%, then 59% in 2024, and by 2025 only single digits remained. The cliff-like deceleration is not a management failure — user penetration has hit its ceiling. Pinduoduo's annual active buyers already exceed 800 million; essentially every Chinese person with internet access is already a user. Net profit was RMB 97.8 billion, down 13% year on year. This is Pinduoduo's first annual profit decline. Gross margin fell from 60.9% to 56.3%, the third consecutive year of decline. Looking only at these numbers, you would think this is a company sliding into mediocrity. But numbers do not tell the whole story. The gross margin decline is not because Pinduoduo has become worse at selling things, but because of Temu — the overseas business grows fast but carries a far lower gross margin than the domestic main site. As Temu takes a larger share of total revenue, overall gross margin gets "dragged down." An analogy: you run a profitable hotpot restaurant with a 70% gross margin. Then you open a milk tea shop with only a 40% gross margin. When the milk tea shop's revenue share goes from 10% to 30%, your overall gross margin falls. That does not mean your hotpot restaurant is failing — only that your business mix has changed. The same goes for the net profit decline. Pinduoduo spent far more on Temu in 2025 than in prior years; this is strategic investment, not operational deterioration. The number that truly deserves attention is free cash flow. FCF was RMB 105.8 billion, with FCF/net profit = 1.08. That means every yuan of profit turned into hard cash, not paper profit eaten by receivables. Five consecutive years of FCF exceeding net profit — you can hardly find a second Chinese internet company that does this. II The most absurd number on Pinduoduo's balance sheet is RMB 416.9 billion. That is its net cash — cash plus short-term investments minus all interest-bearing debt. Pinduoduo's market cap at USD 82.39 is roughly RMB 829.3 billion. In other words, net cash accounts for 50% of market cap. Let us do a simple calculation. You spend 100 yuan on Pinduoduo stock; 50 yuan of that buys not its business but its cash. The remaining 50 yuan buys an e-commerce platform that generates nearly RMB 100 billion of cash each year. The annual return on that 50 yuan is 18 yuan — a 36% return. If this business were packaged whole and sold to you, would you take it? The market says no, because it does not believe the business can last. But that is not all. Pinduoduo's ROE has long stayed above 25%. In 2024 it even reached 36%. Across the entire Chinese internet industry, apart from Tencent at its peak, no other company has reached this level. This shows management squeezes every yuan of shareholder capital to the maximum. Its operating expense ratio is only 34.7%. Alibaba is around 50%, JD.com around 40%. Pinduoduo serves 800 million users with 15,000 employees, generating nearly RMB 30 million of revenue per employee. Alibaba has 13 times its headcount but only one-fifth its revenue per employee. This efficiency gap is not a management issue but a DNA issue — Pinduoduo has been an "efficiency machine" from day one: a lean organization, AI-driven traffic allocation, extremely low marketing costs. III So what is the market worried about? First, growth is gone. From 90% to 10% — a decline steep enough to make any investor reassess a company's long-term value. When a company no longer grows fast, its valuation center shifts from growth stock to value stock; a PE moving from 30x to 10x is normal. The question is: the market has already compressed Pinduoduo's PE to 8.8x — is that excessive? Second, can Temu actually work? This is the biggest uncertainty. Temu entered more than 30 countries within two years of launch, growing at a stunning pace, but it has never disclosed financials separately. Investors cannot tell whether it is a "strategic loss" or simply "cannot make money at all." If the US really scraps the duty-free policy for small parcels under USD 800, Temu's entire cost structure would need rebuilding. Third, Douyin e-commerce has arrived. Douyin users spend over 2 hours per day on the app; Pinduoduo users spend only 30 minutes. Douyin is expanding from "interest-based recommendation" into full-category e-commerce, posing the most direct threat to Pinduoduo's low-price market. Fourth, management does not care about shareholders. With over RMB 400 billion of cash on the books, no buybacks and no dividends. There are signs of small-scale buybacks starting in the second half of 2025, but the scale is far from enough. Every one of these concerns is real. IV But the key point is: these concerns are already fully priced in. A PE of 8.8x means the market is already assuming the worst case. When the share price has already priced in all the bad news, even slightly better news brings enormous upside. Let us run three scenarios. Bear case: Temu fails completely and domestic profit keeps declining. Even then, Pinduoduo's domestic main site remains a cash machine generating RMB 80-100 billion of FCF annually, and net cash stays substantial. In the worst case, your investment does not go to zero. Base case: Temu breaks even and domestic growth stabilizes. Pinduoduo's net profit could reach about RMB 116 billion in three years; at 12x PE, the share price would be about USD 146 — up 78% from now. Bull case: Temu turns profitable and the market re-rates the stock. Pinduoduo's net profit could exceed RMB 130 billion in three years; at 15x PE, the share price would exceed USD 200 — up 150% from now. This is a classic asymmetric return: limited downside in the worst case, substantial gains in the best case. V Pinduoduo's core investment logic is actually simple. It is a cash-printing machine driven by extreme efficiency, and the market's extremely pessimistic pricing gives you a chance to buy cheap. Net cash at half of market cap is your safety cushion. Temu's option value is priced at nearly zero — that is your free lottery ticket. The opportunity cost is also clear: if you do not buy now and wait until you see Temu's profit data, the stock may already have doubled. If you buy now and Temu fails, the domestic business you own is still worth today's price. Pinduoduo's management is not perfect. They fall far short on shareholder returns. But their execution is top-tier — from the 10-billion-yuan subsidy program to Temu's overseas expansion, from profit release to organizational leanness, every step has landed precisely. This is a company with flaws but cheap enough. In investing, perfection is not required — cheapness is. VI Writing this, I recall a line from Duan Yongping: "A good business at a 20% discount, a good price at a 50% discount." Is Pinduoduo's business worth a 20% discount? Its business model is clear — save users money, efficiency first. Growth fell from 90% to 10%, but its earning power has not collapsed, and FCF has exceeded net profit for five consecutive years. A 20% discount is reasonable. Is Pinduoduo's price worth a 50% discount? PE 8.8x, the lowest in global e-commerce. Net cash at half of market cap. For 100 yuan you buy a cash machine earning RMB 100 billion a year plus over RMB 400 billion of cash. A 50% discount is no exaggeration. 80% times 50% equals 40%. This is not precise mathematics, but it describes a fact: Pinduoduo's current price has deviated from its intrinsic value. Of course, a low-valuation company does not necessarily rise right away. The market may stay pessimistic for a year or two, until some catalyst appears — Temu turning profitable, management buying back stock, or simply a change in market sentiment. Investing in Pinduoduo requires not courage, but patience. (The above is investment research analysis only and does not constitute investment advice.
