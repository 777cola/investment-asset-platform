Industry and competition: density is everything — JD.com

Why the food-delivery war was unwinnable from the start

Core view

Competition in Chinese e-commerce is no longer about "who is bigger" but "who dominates at which density." Pinduoduo's density is supply (white-label factories), Meituan's is riders (3 km in 30 minutes), Douyin's is traffic (content conversion), JD's is warehouse network (nationwide next-day delivery). Four densities map to four businesses, and the boundaries of mutual encroachment are clear.

Four densities, four boundaries

Pinduoduo's moat sits on the supply side: it aggregates China's most fragmented white-label production capacity and matches it to demand algorithmically, enabling prices no rival can match. It defends that boundary firmly; JD.com cannot break in.

Meituan's moat sits on fulfilment: dozens of riders per square kilometre in core cities push per-order cost to its limit. JD.com spent RMB 40 billion over two years and still could not breach this boundary, holding only 7%–10% share.

Douyin's moat is attention: content is the shelf and the conversion path is the shortest, but it lacks fulfilment capability and suffers high return rates—that is its ceiling.

JD.com's moat is its warehouse network: over 1,500 warehouses nationwide, a 211 delivery promise, and fulfilment capability for heavy and bulky goods that no one can match. Of the four boundaries, JD.com's is the heaviest, the hardest to replicate and the hardest to expand.

State subsidies: a misunderstood cycle

The 2025 home-appliance trade-in subsidy pulled demand forward, and its rollback in 2026 pushed electronics and home appliances to -11.8% in Q2. Many read that as JD losing share; in fact it is a cyclical pullback.

Two data points support this: first, daily necessities, which receive no subsidy, are still up 5.6%; second, JD's upstream position is unchanged (it remains one of the largest single online channels for 3C and home-appliance brands). This is a wound, not an illness. Time to heal: the first half of 2027, once the high base is fully absorbed in 2027Q2.

Instant retail: structural substitution has begun

The real long-term threat is not any single rival but "delivery in 30 minutes" becoming the default expectation. That shift redefines "fast": JD.com's 211 next-day delivery is fast today, but in five years it may be table stakes.

JD.com has no seat at this table. Rider density, merchant coverage and order volume all lag, and all three take time and density to build — money cannot buy them. Its long-term ceiling is therefore that of a heavy-cargo fulfilment specialist, not an integrated retail platform — not a high ceiling, but a stable one.

What happens in 2027: cycle up, structure down

Overlay the two curves and the 2027 picture becomes fairly clear. The cyclical line points up: the high base from national subsidies is fully absorbed by 2027Q2, and electronics and home appliances return to positive growth; food-delivery subsidies recede and all three players cut losses in tandem; if JD.com's own new-business losses follow the current slope, they fall to around RMB 8 billion by end-2027. The structural line points down: instant retail keeps cannibalizing high-frequency categories, and JD.com's daily necessities and supermarket share erodes slowly.

Where the two lines cross: 2027 financials will look better than 2026 (profit recovery, revenue back to positive), but the market will not restore a valuation multiple above 15x — because the structural growth story is over. So the fair price for 2027 is HKD 110–125, not 175.

HKD 175 requires a third condition: Joybuy scaling in Europe. Q2 summer Black Friday revenue up 35% QoQ is a signal, but the scale is too small to support the valuation in 2027. It is an option, not the base case.

If Meituan raises prices, does JD.com have an opportunity?

A scenario worth working through: if Meituan raises prices in instant retail — higher delivery fees, higher merchant commissions — does JD.com have an opening? Only a very limited one, and again the reason is density. A price increase frees up pricing room, not density room. To capture that opening, JD.com still needs a rider network and merchant coverage, and neither can be conjured by a price increase.

What would truly change the landscape is regulation or technology: if delivery pricing comes under strict regulation, or large-scale unmanned delivery flattens fulfillment costs, the density advantage would weaken. Neither has a visible timetable in 2026, so neither qualifies as a base-case assumption; they are tail options only.

Conclusion

Industry view: JD.com is strong in far-field e-commerce but ranks third in instant retail, and the industry is migrating from far-field to instant. The rollback of national subsidies is a cyclical wound that heals in the first half of 2027; instant retail substitution is a structural wound that will not.
