Financial reality: of the RMB 5.4 billion profit improvement, RMB 5.9 billion came from cost savings — JD.com

Break down the Q2 earnings to the last detail

Core view

2026Q2 operating profit came in at RMB 4.5 billion against -RMB 0.9 billion a year earlier, an improvement of RMB 5.4 billion. Three items drive the swing: RMB 5.9 billion of marketing savings, RMB 4.9 billion of narrower food-delivery losses, and a RMB 5.4 billion drag from lower revenue. These three components sum to exactly RMB 5.4 billion—that arithmetic is the whole story behind the profit inflection.

Revenue side: composition of the -2.9%

Total revenue RMB 346.4 billion, -2.9%. By segment: electronics and home appliances RMB 157.9 billion, -11.8% (lapping trade-in subsidies); general merchandise RMB 109.2 billion, +5.6%; services RMB 79.3 billion, +6.8%, of which platform advertising RMB 30.9 billion, +8.3% and logistics services RMB 48.4 billion, +5.9%.

Goods -5.4%, services +6.8% — the mix is improving. But goods are 77.1% of revenue and services only 22.9%, so 6.8% services growth adds just 1.5 points of buffer to total revenue, not enough to offset the -5.4% drag from goods. That is the arithmetic trap of structural improvement: the good business is too small and the bad business too big.

Cost side: source of RMB 5.9 billion

Marketing spend was RMB 20.3 billion, down 24.8% and equal to 5.9% of revenue (7.6% last year). 1.7 percentage points × RMB 346.4 billion = RMB 5.9 billion. R&D, by contrast, rose 37.7% to RMB 7.3 billion, roughly RMB 2 billion more than last year.

Net savings therefore came to about RMB 3.9 billion, which together with the operating margin uplift accounts for the RMB 5.4 billion improvement. The key judgment: a 5.9% marketing expense ratio is already near the floor for retail. Cutting further would accelerate the revenue decline; not cutting leaves no profit increment next year. This path has run its course.

Cash side: the quality of RMB 31.4 billion

Trailing 12-month free cash flow was RMB 31.4 billion (RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier), with Q2 alone at RMB 31.8 billion, +44.6%. Three drivers: capex compression, payables terms and lower delivery fulfilment spend.None of the three is a permanent improvement; all are dividends of contraction.

Cash stood at RMB 235.1 billion, up RMB 9.7 billion from the start of the year—in a quarter when loss-making businesses were still burning cash, cash still rose, and that can only come from operations. This is the hardest part of the report to argue against. But it also shows one thing: the company has entered "harvest mode" and is no longer investing in growth. Harvest mode is good for cash flow and bad for long-term growth.

Three bases aligned: why segment sum does not equal total revenue

The most common mistake in reading JD.com's financials is to sum segment revenue and treat it as total revenue. Retail RMB 295.4 billion + logistics RMB 64.1 billion + new businesses RMB 7.26 billion = RMB 366.8 billion, against consolidated revenue of RMB 346.4 billion — a gap of RMB 20.4 billion. That gap is internal revenue from logistics fulfilling retail orders, eliminated on consolidation. The RMB 366.8 billion sum is not an error; it is a different basis of measurement.

The second pitfall is net profit. Operating profit of RMB 4.5 billion, net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion and non-GAAP profit of RMB 8.9 billion are all correct, but they answer different questions: operating profit reflects the core business, attributable net profit reflects shareholders, and non-GAAP reflects cash earnings power after excluding share-based compensation. The RMB 1.8 billion gap between 8.9 and 7.1 is mainly share-based compensation and amortisation of intangible assets, not one-off gains.

The third is cash. The RMB 235.1 billion includes restricted cash (platform merchant deposits and the like) and is not net of interest-bearing debt. So "nine-tenths of market cap is cash" is an order-of-magnitude observation, not liquidation value. Only once all three bases are aligned can the valuation discussion begin.

Three numbers you must calculate yourself, never copy

Media outlets cite figures from the financials daily, but the methodology is often wrong—you have to run the numbers yourself. First, net cash per share: RMB 235.1 billion ÷ 2.818 billion shares = RMB 83.5, about HKD 91—not market cap divided. Second, cash coverage: 235.1 ÷ (HKD 280 billion ÷ 1.09) = 91.5%—the denominator must be converted to RMB. Third, the food delivery loss ratio: 98.5 ÷ 72.6 = 135.7%—both numerator and denominator use the new-business scope and must not mix in allocated retail expenses.

Work through these three numbers yourself and you won't be led astray by claims like "JD's cash exceeds its market cap" or "food delivery loses RMB 30 per order." Methodology is the basic skill of investing, and it matters more than opinion.

Conclusion

The financial reality: profit improved by RMB 5.4 billion, with RMB 5.9 billion from marketing savings and RMB 4.9 billion from narrower food-delivery losses, offset by RMB 5.4 billion absorbed by the revenue decline. The arithmetic points to one conclusion: the recovery is real, but the fuel behind it — cost cuts — is running out.
