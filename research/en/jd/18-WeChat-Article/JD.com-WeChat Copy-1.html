Title and lead-in

Headline options (pick one of three)

  • JD.com at 7x PE: so cheap it's hard to buy
  • JD.com holds RMB 235.1 billion in cash — 90% of its market cap is cash. Then what?
  • Two years of food delivery war, share down from 31% to 7%: what did JD.com get for its RMB 40 billion?

Lead: One company's cash equals 90% of its own market cap; of its HKD 99.4 share price, HKD 91 is cash. That sounds like a free lunch. But the market is not naive — every reason behind that price is written in the financials.

Part one of the main text: start with that tantalizing number

JD.com trades at HKD 99.4, a market cap of HKD 280 billion. In the Q2 2026 report, cash and short-term investments on the books stood at RMB 235.1 billion, equivalent to about HKD 256.9 billion—equal to 91.5% of market cap, or roughly HKD 91 per share.

Do the math: of the HKD 99.4 you pay per share, HKD 91 is cash, and the remaining HKD 8 buys JD Retail (China's largest self-operated retailer), JD Logistics (over 1,500 warehouses, 900,000 people), plus the food delivery business and over 600 million users.

Adjusted net profit for 2026 at RMB 34 billion implies 7.5x P/E. On an enterprise value basis—netting out cash—just 0.6x. The market has priced almost none of these three businesses.

Part two of the main text: where the profit comes from

In Q2 2026, JD.com revenue was RMB 346.4 billion, down 2.9% year on year — a rare negative growth since listing. But operating profit swung from a loss of RMB 0.9 billion in the year-earlier period to a profit of RMB 4.5 billion, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion, up 14.5% year on year.

Recession on one side, an inflection on the other. Where did the money come from? Breaking it down, only two places:

First, marketing spend was cut 24.8%. From 7.6% of revenue to 5.9% — on revenue of RMB 346.4 billion, that saved roughly RMB 5.9 billion. Second, food delivery losses shrank by RMB 4.9 billion. New business losses narrowed from RMB 14.78 billion last year to RMB 9.85 billion.

Together the two far exceed the RMB 5.4 billion profit improvement; the gap was eaten by the revenue decline. So this is not a story of a business getting better — it is a story of a business spending less.

Spending less has a limit. With the marketing expense ratio pressed to 5.9%, already near the efficiency frontier for retail, further cuts mean revenue falls faster — this quarter's -2.9% is the price of over-cutting. Next quarter's profit increment can only come from gross margin or revenue, and neither shows any sign of it for now.

Part Three of the main text: What did RMB 40 billion buy?

Food delivery is the most expensive six characters in this earnings report. In 2025 JD.com went all-in on food delivery, on the logic of "high frequency drives low frequency": users order food every day and casually buy things on JD.com while they're at it.

Two years on, that assumption can now be tested. Order-volume share in food delivery fell from a peak of 31% in Q2 2025 to 7% to 10%; over the same period JD Retail revenue fell 4.7%, and the group's general merchandise grew only 5.6%. If frequency really could drive low frequency, general merchandise should be growing at a double-digit rate.

Why hasn't it worked? Because the infrastructure isn't shared. Food delivery is "3 kilometers, 30 minutes, local merchants"; JD.com is "national warehouses, next-day delivery." The two systems aren't shared, so users aren't shared. A user who comes to JD.com to buy an iPhone won't order food twice more just because he can.

New businesses now lose RMB 9.85 billion a quarter, only RMB 0.5 billion less than the RMB 10.35 billion in Q1. At this pace, it will take until mid-2027 to bring that down to RMB 8 billion, and 2028 before breakeven is possible — in other words, food delivery will drag on the financial statements for another two years.

Part four of the main text: so what is the market afraid of

The market is not blind to that RMB 235.1 billion; it simply does not believe it will return to shareholders.

That skepticism is grounded. A company willing to spend RMB 40 billion over two years fighting in food delivery can spend another RMB 40 billion fighting somewhere else. JD.com uses a dual-class share structure: the founder controls more than 70% of voting rights, and outside shareholders have no veto over major capital allocation.A cheap price is essentially pricing in the expectation that management will stop burning cash.

In October, three brokers reached completely opposite conclusions: JPMorgan at HKD 148, Bank of America at HKD 145, and Morgan Stanley cutting straight to underweight. Their revenue assumptions are similar; the entire disagreement is over what that cash is worth.

Part Five of the main text: When to buy

Not now. But not far off either.

Two triggers, both resolved within five weeks: First, the share price falls below HKD 90. At that level, the share price equals net cash per share, and you get retail, logistics and food delivery for free. Second, the Q3 results hit two numbers at once: retail revenue turns positive, and food delivery's quarterly loss narrows to within RMB 8 billion. Q3 results land in mid-November; Double 11 wraps up on November 14.

Conversely, any one of three events and this stock comes off the list: announcement of major new investment, free cash flow turning negative, cash falling more than RMB 20 billion in a single quarter.

At HKD 99.4, 7.5x P/E, cash at 90% of market cap — the price isn't bad, but not good enough to pay for management's lack of discipline.

A genuine bargain doesn't require you to rush.
