# JD.com（9618.HK）— Quality Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/26
- Price：99.4 HKD
- Market cap：Approximately HKD 280 billion

## Notes

The seven thresholds are the AI Berkshire negative screen. ROE and gross margin could not be verified under retail reporting conventions, since gross profit is not disclosed separately by quarter; operating margin and net margin were used instead — see the main text for the rationale. All figures are from JD.com's 2026Q2 results announcement (2026/08/13). Trailing 12-month free cash flow of RMB 31.4 billion and RMB 10.1 billion for the year-earlier period are taken from the cash flow discussion in the announcement. The 4% dividend yield is a rough calculation: 2026E adjusted net profit of RMB 34 billion × 30% payout ratio ÷ market cap of RMB 256.9 billion = 3.97%, not derived from the cash balance.

## Which three passed, and how much substance is there

Point one: cash flow quality at 5.3x. Operating cash flow of RMB 37.8 billion against net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion. That multiple is typical of the self-operated retail model: collect first, pay later, and the payment cycle itself throws off cash. Part of the 5.3x is seasonal — Q2 is the 618 stock-up season and payables rise — so it cannot simply be annualised. Still, it has held above 3x for several consecutive quarters, which tells you profit is real money, not receivables.

Point two: free cash flow of RMB 31.8 billion, up 44.6% YoY. The harder metric is the trailing 12-month figure: RMB 31.4 billion versus RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier, a threefold increase. This is JD's most solid point right now — a company with negative revenue growth tripled its free cash flow, evidence it is cutting investment and recovering capital.But note that this "improvement" is itself a defensive move, not an offensive result.

Point three: net cash. RMB 235.1 billion in cash and short-term investments against a market cap of RMB 256.9 billion — a coverage ratio of 91.5%. That is extremely rare among large caps in both the A-share and Hong Kong markets. Most retailers hold cash for working capital; JD's cash has reached market-cap-scale assets.

## Three negatives: two are industry traits, one is a genuine problem

A 1.3% operating margin and 2.1% net margin necessarily fail the 10% and 8% thresholds. But a distinction is needed: no global self-operated retailer clears this threshold—Costco's operating margin has long run at 3%–4%, Amazon's retail business is lower still, and Walmart sits around 4%. Screening retailers on a 10% operating margin screens out the entire industry. Failing these two tests means "lacks the characteristics of a high-margin business," not "the company has a problem."

Revenue -2.9% is different. This is not an industry attribute—total retail sales were still growing over the same period, and Pinduoduo's growth rate was above JD's. It is JD's own problem: electronics and home appliances -11.8% (high base from the national subsidy), product revenue -5.4%, retail segment -4.7%. The subsidy rollback explains part of it, not all of it, because general merchandise grew only 5.6%—the categories that should have grown faster did not fill the gap.

The 2.1% net margin carries another hidden risk: this figure is extremely sensitive to subsidies. Q2 operating profit was RMB 4.5 billion; if food delivery quarterly losses widen by another RMB 4.5 billion, the group returns to breakeven. A low net margin means no room for error.

## The one still to be verified: shareholder returns

Criterion seven is marked "to be verified" solely because there is no explicit dividend commitment. JD's cash pile has reached the point where it must answer "whose money is this": RMB 235.1 billion in cash and short-term investments, which at a 30% payout ratio would imply a dividend yield of about 4% on the current market cap; deployed entirely into buybacks, it could theoretically repurchase ninety percent of the market cap. Neither has yet been institutionalised as a committed ratio.

Why this matters: for a company that does not grow and does not commit to dividends, the cash on its books creates no value for shareholders—only optionality for management. And management has just spent that optionality on a two-year food-delivery war. Criterion seven does not pass; its "to be verified" status is in fact the core risk of this entire report.

## But is it fatal: a tiered judgment

Rank the four failed criteria by severity: negative revenue growth (fatal, but with a clear repair path), low net margin (structural; cost control has hit its limit), low operating margin (an industry trait, not grounds for elimination) and unclear shareholder returns (a governance issue that a valuation discount can offset).

The conclusion is therefore not "eliminate" but "switch valuation language." On a growth-stock framework, JD.com passes three of seven criteria and should be eliminated; on a cash-flow-asset framework, it generates RMB 31.4 billion of free cash flow and holds RMB 235.1 billion of net cash against a market cap of RMB 256.9 billion — equivalent to paying RMB 21.8 billion for a machine that generates over RMB 30 billion of free cash flow a year, provided that machine is no longer deployed into a food-delivery war.
