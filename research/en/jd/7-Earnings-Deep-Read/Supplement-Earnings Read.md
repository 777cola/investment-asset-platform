## Tier one: revenue mix — goods retreating, services advancing

RMB 346.4 billion, down 2.9% YoY — a rare negative growth print for JD.com since listing. The breakdown is clear: electronics and home appliances RMB 157.9 billion, down 11.8% YoY, mainly on the high base created by the 2025 national trade-in subsidy programme, which pulled demand forward and left it exhausted once the policy tapered off; general merchandise RMB 109.2 billion, up 5.6%, still growing; service revenue RMB 79.3 billion, up 6.8%, of which platform advertising was RMB 30.9 billion, up 8.3%, and logistics services RMB 48.4 billion, up 5.9%.

One easily overlooked detail in this mix: product revenue accounted for 77.1% and services 22.9%. Service revenue carries a far higher gross margin than products, so every percentage point gained in the service mix improves the group's gross-margin structure. In Q2, products fell 5.4% while services rose 6.8% — the mix is improving while the total is shrinking, and the improvement is not yet outpacing the contraction.

Another detail: general merchandise grew only 5.6%. General merchandise is JD.com's main battleground against Pinduoduo and Douyin, and the category most severely eroded by instant retail. Against low-single-digit growth in overall retail sales, 5.6% is not bad, but for a company that needs a "second growth curve," that pace is not enough.

## Layer two: gross profit and expenses — RMB 5.9 billion of profit preserved

The profit improvement came not from gross margin expansion but from expense contraction. Marketing spend was RMB 20.3 billion, down 24.8% YoY, and its share of revenue fell from 7.6% to 5.9%. On revenue of RMB 346.4 billion, that 1.7 percentage points equals RMB 5.9 billion — almost exactly the entire gap in operating profit moving from -RMB 0.9 billion to +RMB 4.5 billion, an improvement of RMB 5.4 billion.

R&D spend, by contrast, rose 37.7% against the trend to RMB 7.3 billion — evidence this is not indiscriminate cost-cutting but a shift of money from "buying traffic" to "building capability." The direction is right: JD's user growth long ago passed the stage where it was driven by ad spend, and the marginal return on acquisition spending is very low.

But that is also where the cost lies. Marketing expense at 5.9% of revenue is already close to the efficiency limit for retail; cut further and revenue will fall faster. In other words, this quarter's profit improvement is one-off and cannot be repeated. Next quarter's profit growth must come from gross margin — pricing power, which JD does not have — or from revenue growth, which has not appeared.

## Tier 3: Food delivery — losses narrow, but the loss ratio deteriorates

New businesses (food delivery, Jingxi, JD Property, Joybuy) generated RMB 7.26 billion of revenue, -47.6% YoY, and an operating loss of RMB 9.85 billion. For comparison, Q2 last year lost RMB 14.78 billion and 2026Q1 lost RMB 10.35 billion. The absolute loss is narrowing, by RMB 500 million in a single quarter.

But the loss ratio rose from 106.7% (14.78 ÷ 13.85) to 135.7% (9.85 ÷ 7.26), because revenue shrank faster than losses. This is not evidence of deteriorating efficiency; it shows that JD food delivery is actively contracting, not improving while expanding. The two paths differ fundamentally in nature: improving while expanding means "the more you do, the more you earn"; improving while contracting means "the more you do, the smaller you get."

Extrapolating at the current slope (about RMB 500 million of narrowing per quarter), losses would only reach RMB 8 billion by mid-2027 and could approach breakeven only in 2028 — and that assumes no rebound at all. In other words, food delivery will drag on the financials for another two years. Those two years of roughly RMB 30 billion in annual losses are the direct reason the market gives 7.5x P/E rather than 15x.

## Layer four: cash — the healthiest part of the financial statements

Operating cash flow RMB 37.8 billion, +54.5%; free cash flow RMB 31.8 billion, +44.6%; trailing 12 months RMB 31.4 billion (RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier). In a quarter where a loss-making business is still burning cash, cash can only build this way from two sources: payment terms (supplier payables) and capex compression.

Cash and short-term investments on the books RMB 235.1 billion, RMB 9.7 billion above the RMB 225.4 billion at the start of the year.Against a market cap of HKD 280 billion (about RMB 256.9 billion), that cash equals 91.5% of market cap, or about RMB 83.5 per share, roughly HKD 91.

Three qualifiers apply: gross, including restricted cash, before deducting interest-bearing debt. Restricted cash cannot be touched — platform merchant deposits, for instance. The correct statement is therefore "cash equals nine-tenths of market cap," not "there is HKD 91 per share you can be paid." That distinction determines whether the margin of safety is a magnitude judgment or a liquidation value.

## Layer five: the three numbers must be read together

No single number tells the story. Revenue alone, at -2.9%, says the company is finished; operating profit alone, at RMB 4.5 billion, says an inflection point has arrived; cash alone, at RMB 235.1 billion, says the stock is being given away. Only the three together give the full picture: revenue is retreating, profit is repairing, cash is rising—what retreats is volume, what repairs is cost, what rises is the shrinkage dividend.

Together they also support a key judgment: operating profit of RMB 4.5 billion plus free cash flow of RMB 31.8 billion makes the company a qualified cash machine; a net margin of 2.1% plus negative revenue growth means that machine's output is shrinking. Buying this kind of company is essentially buying the day it stops shrinking, not the day it grows. Those two expectations imply completely different valuation methods, holding periods and margins for error.

One final comparison that is easy to miss: food delivery lost RMB 9.85 billion, while group operating profit was RMB 4.5 billion. Without food delivery, operating profit this quarter would be roughly RMB 14 billion (rough calculation: 45 + 98.5, not accounting for expense allocation)—two-thirds of the income statement has been cut away. This is not an invitation to add the numbers up; it makes one point clear: food delivery is not a minor drag—it determines the shape of profit.
