Core view

We run China's six e-commerce and local-life names through a funnel, starting with three coarse screens: is revenue growing, are margins stable, and does the valuation leave enough room for error? PDD, Meituan and Alibaba stay; JD, Vipshop and Douyin E-commerce (not investable) drop out. The final stage weighs business quality against price. PDD has the best business — high gross margin, asset-light, steady profit — and is also the cheapest; JD has the cheapest assets but the worst business. The funnel's conclusion is not "buy JD" but "JD has unique value in its own slot": it is the only one that offers certainty in heavy-goods fulfilment.

Rough screening: three criteria, half eliminated

First criterion: revenue growth. JD.com (-2.9%) and Vipshop (negative growth) are out; Pinduoduo (growing), Meituan (single-digit) and Alibaba (low single-digit) stay in. This is the harshest filter: a retailer's re-rating headroom hinges on revenue elasticity, and companies with negative revenue growth can lean only on cost cuts and buybacks, not on growth.

Second criterion: margin stability. Meituan's profit has been crushed to extremely low levels by the food-delivery war (which is also why its P/E looks expensive), Alibaba is dragged down by flash-sale investment, and JD.com turned positive only by slashing expenses. All three sit in a state of profit distorted by war; only Pinduoduo's margin is stable. The purpose of this filter is to exclude companies relying on one-off profit repair.

Third criterion: does valuation offer enough margin of safety. JD.com at 7.5x offers enough, Pinduoduo at 8–10x offers enough, Meituan above 20x offers none, and Alibaba at 15–18x is barely adequate.

Crossing the three filters: Pinduoduo passes all, JD.com passes one (valuation), Meituan passes one and a half, Alibaba passes one and a half, Vipshop passes zero.

Final pick: why not JD, but why JD deserves a separate look

If you could own only one Chinese e-commerce name, it would be Pinduoduo: 55%–60% gross margin, asset-light, stable profitability and a valuation below 10x. Its business quality is an order of magnitude better than JD.com's—what JD.com needs 1,500 warehouses and 900,000 employees to achieve, Pinduoduo delivers through algorithms and supply.

JD.com nonetheless holds one unique position in this funnel: it is the only national player that can deliver certainty in categories involving heavy goods, bulky items, and installation and after-sales service. Home appliances, 3C, furniture, building materials—fulfillment complexity in these categories is high enough that the platform model cannot do it well. Pinduoduo cannot, and Meituan certainly cannot. This position is not large, but it is very stable.

The right way to use the funnel, then, is not to "pick the single best one to buy" but to "assign a price to each cell." A fair price for Pinduoduo is 10–12x P/E; a fair price for JD.com is 8–11x adjusted P/E. JD.com now trades at 7.5x, already below the lower bound of its fair range; Pinduoduo at 8–10x is also at the lower bound. Both are cheap, for different reasons: Pinduoduo's growth is slowing, while JD.com is not growing at all.

Why "cheap" does not equal "opportunity" for JD

The funnel screens out two kinds of cheap stocks: the "misunderstood" and the "seen through." Pinduoduo is the former—the market worries growth has peaked, yet its margins are still there. JD.com is the latter—the market has already tallied the RMB 235.1 billion in cash and knows food delivery is burning money; the 7.5x P/E is the price of that perception.

For a company that has been seen through to re-rate, what it needs is a "perception-changing event," not a "cheaper price." For JD.com, there are only two such events: food delivery stops losing money, or management starts returning cash to shareholders. The former takes two years; the latter takes one decision.

So in the funnel, JD.com sits at "watch," not "final pick." This is not a rejection of the company but a judgment on timing.

Conclusion

Funnel conclusion: Six screened down to three; final pick Pinduoduo, JD.com on watch. The JD.com case rests not on "best business" but on "cheapest heavy-cargo fulfilment assets" plus "cash equal to 90% of market cap." The reason to own it is assets and price, not a growth story.

Actionable next step: track JD.com and Pinduoduo side by side — if JD.com's Q3 revenue turns positive while Pinduoduo's growth slows to single digits, the valuation gap between the two narrows, and that is the window to switch positions.
