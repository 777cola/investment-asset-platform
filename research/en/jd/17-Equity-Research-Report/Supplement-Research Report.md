## Chapter 1 Company Overview

JD.com (9618.HK) is China's largest self-operated retailer. It started from a counter in Zhongguancun in 1998, moved online in 2004 and built its own logistics in 2007 — a step that shaped its form for the next two decades. The business has three parts: JD Retail (self-operated procurement and sales plus platform commission and advertising), JD Logistics (warehousing and fulfilment, including external customers) and new businesses (food delivery, Jingxi, JD Property, Europe's Joybuy).

2026Q2 mix: retail RMB 295.4 billion (85%), logistics RMB 64.1 billion (18%), new businesses RMB 7.26 billion (2%), segment total RMB 366.8 billion, consolidated elimination RMB 20.4 billion (logistics fulfilment for retail). Total shares 2.818 billion, of which Class B shares carry 20 votes each, giving the founder over 70% of voting power.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

Chinese e-commerce has entered a zero-sum game: social retail growth is in the low single digits and online penetration has peaked, with the four players (Alibaba, Pinduoduo, JD.com, Douyin) invading each other's turf. The essence of competition is "density" — Pinduoduo's density lies in supply (white-label factories), Meituan's in riders (3 km in 30 minutes), Douyin's in traffic (content conversion), and JD.com's in its warehouse network (nationwide next-day delivery).

The industry is shifting from far-field to instant delivery. With 30-minute delivery becoming the default expectation, next-day delivery has gone from "fast" to "passing grade." This is structural pressure for JD.com: its infrastructure is "national warehouses, next-day delivery," which does not share the "3 km, 30 minutes" model of instant retail, and users will not migrate across systems. **The loss in the food delivery battle (share 31%→7%–10%) is fundamentally density math, not execution.**

## Chapter 3 Business model

JD.com earns from three sources: direct-sales margin (product revenue RMB 267.1 billion), platform commission and advertising (RMB 30.9 billion, +8.3%), and logistics fulfillment (RMB 48.4 billion, +5.9%). Gross margin rises across the three, but scale shrinks in the same order—direct sales dominate volume, advertising carries the fattest profit. This structure sets its margin ceiling: **low-margin business accounts for 77%, high-margin business only 23%.**

Profitability rests on three levers: turnover (inventory around 30 days), payment terms (operating cash flow is 5.3x net profit), and expenses (marketing expense ratio 5.9%). All three are near their limits, so the profit curve is flat. **The essence of the business is efficiency, not pricing power.**

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

Revenue: RMB 346.4 billion, -2.9%. Electronics and home appliances RMB 157.9 billion, -11.8% (high base from trade-in subsidies), general merchandise RMB 109.2 billion, +5.6%, services RMB 79.3 billion, +6.8%. Goods -5.4%, services +6.8% — mix improving but total shrinking.

Profit: operating profit RMB 4.5 billion (versus -RMB 0.9 billion last year). Sources of improvement: marketing expense savings of RMB 5.9 billion and a RMB 4.9 billion smaller food-delivery loss, offset by a RMB 5.4 billion hit from lower revenue. Retail operating margin 4.6% (+0.1pct), the best ever for a major promotion quarter, but low quality — it is a function of expense, not of competitive strength.

Cash: operating cash flow RMB 37.8 billion (+54.5%), free cash flow RMB 31.8 billion (+44.6%), trailing 12 months RMB 31.4 billion (RMB 10.1 billion last year). Cash and short-term investments RMB 235.1 billion, up RMB 9.7 billion from the start of the year. **This is the healthiest part of the report, and the floor for valuation.**

## Chapter 5 Management

Richard Liu (founder, executive chairman) controls more than 70% of voting rights and sets strategy; Xu Ran (CEO since May 2023) handles execution, with a finance background and prior experience as a PwC audit partner. The division of labor is clear, but results diverge: **Grade A on execution (marketing spend -24.8%, free cash flow +44.6%), Grade D on strategy (RMB 40 billion into food delivery, 7%–10% share).**

Governance is a source of long-term discount: dual-class shares prevent outside shareholders from vetoing major capital allocation; the 2018 Minnesota incident, though no charges were filed, leaves lasting reputational damage. Succession is not a near-term concern (Xu Ran), but has no medium-term solution (no public arrangement at the strategy level).

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three possible growth drivers, ranked by certainty. **First, external logistics**: JD Logistics Q2 revenue was RMB 64.1 billion, +24.3%, with external customers now more than half; this is the only business with certain double-digit growth, but its margin is only 3.5%. **Second, service revenue and advertising**: +6.8%, 22.9% of the total; each point gained improves the gross-margin mix. **Third, Joybuy Europe**: it ran a "summer Black Friday" in Q2, revenue +35% QoQ; still small, it is the only growth line that does not depend on China's existing market.

Drags in the other direction: electronics and appliances (-11.8%, absorbed in the first half of 2027), food delivery (revenue -47.6%, deliberate contraction), and general merchandise (+5.6%, eroded by instant retail). **Overall, 2027 revenue growth will be low single digit, not double digit.**

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

The four players have clear division of labor: Pinduoduo captures price-sensitive demand (white-label supply + asset-light, 55%–60% gross margin), Meituan captures immediacy (51% order share, leading density), Alibaba captures merchant ecosystem (42% blended share of flash sales), and Douyin captures content traffic (short conversion path, weak fulfillment). JD.com's position is authenticity + speed, covering 3C appliances and supermarket staples.

Threat ranking: **Meituan > Pinduoduo > Douyin > Alibaba.** Meituan's threat is the most fundamental—it redefines "fast," and once user expectations shift, JD.com's core experience depreciates; Pinduoduo's threat is the slowest but most fundamental—it makes "cheap" the default option.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

**1. Cash continues to burn.** Food delivery loses about RMB 40 billion annualized; if sustained for two years, cash coverage falls from 91.5% to 58%, and the valuation logic shifts from "cash floor" to "cash drain." **2. Revenue keeps contracting.** If Q3 retail remains negative, the base-case target price drops from 125 to 105. **3. Mindshare displacement.** Instant retail rewrites high-frequency categories; JD.com's share erodes slowly — the most lethal over a ten-year horizon. **4. Governance risk.** Dual-class shares leave shareholders unable to constrain capital allocation; another large-scale investment cycle is possible. **5. Policy and macro.** Pace of national subsidy rollback, strength of consumption recovery, Hong Kong market liquidity.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026: revenue of about RMB 1.35 trillion (rough calculation based on Q2 RMB 346.4 billion and a single-digit decline in each of the following two quarters), adjusted net profit of about RMB 34 billion (own model, back-solved from BofA's 11x corresponding to HKD 145). 2027: base case assumes retail returns to low single-digit growth and food delivery losses narrow to within RMB 8 billion, adjusted net profit RMB 36 billion; bear case RMB 28 billion (delivery losses stop narrowing, revenue continues to decline); bull case RMB 42 billion (delivery near break-even + Joybuy scales up).

Sensitivity of key assumptions: every additional RMB 5 billion of delivery losses cuts net profit by about RMB 5 billion; every 1 point of retail revenue growth shifts revenue by about RMB 11 billion and net profit by about RMB 400–500 million.**The former is decisive, the latter gradual.**

## Chapter 10 Valuation

Cross-check with two methods. Market-cap basis: HKD 280 billion / 2026 adjusted net profit of RMB 34 billion ≈ 7.5x. Enterprise-value basis: market cap RMB 256.9 billion − cash RMB 235.1 billion = RMB 21.8 billion, which against RMB 34 billion of net profit is 0.6x—**the market is pricing almost nothing for the retail, logistics and food-delivery businesses.**

Three scenarios: bear case HKD 80 (7x, 0.85x PB), base case HKD 125 (9x), bull case HKD 175 (11x). Probability weights 30%/50%/20%, expected value HKD 121.5, implying +22%.**Sixty percent of the expected return comes from the single assumption that management stops burning cash, not from business growth.**

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

**Rating: Watch. 12-month target price HKD 125 (Neutral), implying +26%. No position at the current price of HKD 99.40.**

Rationale: valuation already reflects "no growth" (7.5x), but not "stop burning cash." Two buy triggers: **1) share price ≤HKD 90** (implying net cash per share, cash coverage 101%); **2) Q3 results simultaneously show retail revenue turning positive and food delivery losses ≤RMB 8 billion.** Whichever comes first triggers a position of 2%–5%, with total China e-commerce exposure capped at 8%.

Three logical stop-losses: announcement of new large-scale investment, free cash flow turning negative, single-quarter cash decline exceeding RMB 20 billion. **No price stop-loss**—for a company underpinned by cash, the lower the price, the better the odds.

## Chapter 13: Divergence from Market Consensus

Sell-side views on JD.com diverged publicly in October: JPMorgan at HKD 148, BofA at HKD 145 (11x 2026 adjusted PE), Morgan Stanley cut to underweight (about 8x). **The three differ little on revenue and profit assumptions; the entire gap is "what is RMB 235.1 billion of cash worth."**

This report differs from the bulls in two places: **First, we count the cash at gross but do not give it full marks**, because it includes restricted funds, is not net of interest-bearing debt, and has historically proven to be invested away; **second, we assign no valuation to an "food delivery reversal"**, because with 7%–10% share and a 135.7% loss ratio, no reversal path exists.

It differs from the bears in one place: **we believe 7.5x already fully reflects pessimistic expectations**, and further downside requires new facts (cash outflow), not new opinions. So our target price of 125 is above Morgan Stanley's implied level but below JPMorgan and BofA — essentially "agree it is cheap, disagree on the speed of recovery."

## Chapter 14 Quarterly Tracking Checklist

Six questions every quarterly report must answer, ranked by importance. **1) JD Retail revenue year over year:** a return to positive growth = base case holds; still negative = target price cut to 105. **2) New business operating loss:** narrowing ≥ RMB 1 billion quarter over quarter = the contraction is real; ≤ RMB 0.5 billion = merely seasonal. **3) Marketing expense ratio:** a rebound above 6.5% means traffic is being bought again and the profit recovery has stalled. **4) Free cash flow:** below RMB 5.4 billion (25÷4 plus a safety buffer) requires an explanation. **5) Cash and short-term investments:** a single-quarter decline of more than RMB 20 billion is a veto signal. **6) Management's language on investment:** "optimizing efficiency" means contraction; "seizing opportunities" means the fight continues.

Of the six questions, the first two determine the target price and the last four determine whether the stock stays on the list. **Recommendation: turn them into a table, fill it in once a quarter, no commentary, numbers only.** If the numbers turn for two consecutive quarters, the conclusion changes on its own; no debate needed.

## Chapter 15 Segment Forecasts and Key Assumptions

Retail: on a rough basis of RMB 295.4 billion in Q2 2026 and a single-digit decline in H2, full-year 2026 revenue is about RMB 1.13 trillion; 2027 assumes a neutral recovery to 0%–2% growth. Retail operating margin of 4.6% is treated as the ceiling, with 4.5% assumed for 2027 (no room left to compress expenses; a higher advertising mix provides partial offset).

Logistics: Q2 RMB 64.1 billion, +24.3%, the only confirmed double-digit growth; 2027 assumes +15%, with margin held at 3.5% — a higher share of external customers dilutes margin while scale effects lift it, roughly offsetting each other.

New initiatives: 2026 revenue of about RMB 30 billion (Q2 RMB 7.26 billion, with further contraction thereafter), and losses of about RMB 38 billion in 2026, a neutral RMB 28 billion in 2027 and RMB 15 billion in 2028. **This line is the biggest swing factor in valuation: every additional RMB 5 billion of loss cuts 2027 net profit by RMB 5 billion and the target price by about HKD 15.**

## Chapter 16 Valuation Sensitivity

Cross two variables: annual food-delivery loss (RMB 28/33/38 billion) against retail revenue growth (-2%/0%/+2%). **The best combination (food delivery loss of RMB 28 billion, retail +2%) maps to 2027 adjusted net profit of about RMB 42 billion and 11x PE, giving HKD 175; the worst combination (food delivery loss of RMB 38 billion, retail -2%) maps to RMB 28 billion and 7x, giving HKD 80.**

The four intermediate cells fall between HKD 95 and HKD 140, with a median of about HKD 118. **This shows that the downside protection at the current price of HKD 99.4 comes from "the worst case is only -20%", not from "the best case offers +76%".**

Implication for position sizing: this is a trade with symmetric odds and a below-even win rate. Symmetric odds are worth waiting for; a below-even win rate is not worth a heavy position—hence a 5% cap and a 3% initial position.
