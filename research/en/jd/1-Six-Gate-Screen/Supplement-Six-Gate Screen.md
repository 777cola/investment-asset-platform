> Scoring: 10 points per level, 60-point scale. Financial data from JD.com's 2026Q2 results announcement (2026/08/13); share price and market cap as of HKEX close on 2026/10/26.

## Gate 1: Circle of competence (9/10, pass)

JD.com's business breaks into three parts, disclosed separately in the financials: JD Retail RMB 295.4 billion, JD Logistics RMB 64.1 billion, and New Business RMB 7.26 billion. The three add up to RMB 366.8 billion, RMB 20.4 billion more than consolidated revenue of RMB 346.4 billion — that is intersegment elimination: logistics fulfills for retail, and the revenue is eliminated on consolidation. Anyone who cannot see this layer would assume the sum of segments should equal total revenue.

Retail earns money three ways: direct-sales margin, platform commissions, and advertising (Q2 platform advertising RMB 30.9 billion, +8.3%); logistics earns fulfillment fees and revenue from external customers (Q2 revenue +24.3%, now shifted to serving third-party merchants directly); New Business is food delivery, Jingxi, JD Property and Europe's Joybuy, all currently loss-making.

Understandable does not mean simple — it means predictable. JD.com's revenue is predictable: electronics and home appliances RMB 157.9 billion, general merchandise RMB 109.2 billion, services RMB 79.3 billion, with clear drivers for all three lines (replacement cycle, category expansion, merchant count). Profit is not predictable: it depends on how much management decides to subsidize in a given quarter. That is where the 1 point is deducted.

## Gate 2: Good business (5/10, fail)

This test is hard numbers, not sentiment. Group operating margin 45 ÷ 3,464 = 1.3%; JD Retail 135 ÷ 2,954 = 4.6%; JD Logistics 22.6 ÷ 641 = 3.5%. Put the three together: this is a business that turns hundreds of billions in assets into tens of billions in profit, and the return comes from turnover, not pricing power.

4.6% is JD Retail's best-ever level for a promotional quarter, and it is no small achievement — but it was reached after marketing spend was cut 24.8% year on year (RMB 20.3 billion, 5.9% of revenue, versus 7.6% last year). That 1.7 percentage points equals roughly RMB 5.9 billion, almost exactly the entire swing in operating profit from -RMB 0.9 billion to +RMB 4.5 billion. This quarter's margin was saved, not earned. Savings have a ceiling; earnings do not.

The mark of a good business is raising prices without losing customers. JD cannot raise prices: 3C and home appliance prices are anchored by Pinduoduo's RMB 10 billion subsidy, daily necessities are siphoned off by Douyin and white-label brands, and any delivery fee increase loses orders. The 5 points go to turnover efficiency and cash conversion, not pricing power.

## Gate 3: Moat (7/10, Pass)

JD.com has three things rivals cannot build in the short term: its warehousing and delivery network (over 1,500 warehouses, 900,000 ecosystem employees, 1,000 autonomous delivery vehicles in routine operation, 211 same-day/next-day delivery), its 3C and home-appliance mindshare (buying expensive big-ticket items on JD.com is one of Chinese consumers' most stable habits), and its supply-chain payment terms and rebates (an implicit profit source from brands).

But the moat is leaking at both ends. On low prices, Pinduoduo's white-label supply has moved price-sensitive users away in whole cohorts; on immediacy, Meituan and Taobao Shangou have made 30-minute delivery the default expectation. JD.com is stuck in the middle: it cannot beat Pinduoduo on cheapness or Meituan on speed, so it can only defend authentic goods plus next-day delivery. This position holds the existing base (3C, home appliances, essential supermarket goods) but not the incremental base (daily necessities, fresh food, instant retail).

A score of 7 means: the moat is real, verifiable and able to keep out new entrants, but it cannot keep out the two rivals already on the field.

## Gate 4: Management (6/10, questionable)

Score capability and judgment separately. On capability, Xu Ran delivers: Q2 marketing expenses fell 24.8%, R&D rose 37.7% against the trend (RMB 7.3 billion, 2.1% of revenue), operating cash flow +54.5%, free cash flow +44.6%, and trailing 12-month free cash flow of RMB 31.4 billion (RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier). Pulling a company from a quarterly loss back to profit in just over a year is execution.

Judgment loses points. Going all-in on food delivery in 2025, JD had no rider network, no merchant base and no relative advantage in subsidy budget, yet chose to fight on Meituan's and Taobao Shangou's home turf. After roughly RMB 40 billion invested over two years, order volume share fell from a peak of 31% to 7%–10%, and new businesses still lost RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter.This is not a matter of luck; it is running against the grain of someone else's economies-of-scale curve.

6 points: good at saving money, good at execution, bad at choosing battlefields. For a cash-flow company, that last item is precisely the most expensive.

## Gate 5: Margin of safety (8/10, pass)

The arithmetic is straightforward: HKD 99.40 × 2.818 billion shares = about HKD 280 billion market cap, or roughly RMB 256.9 billion at 1.09. Cash and short-term investments of RMB 235.1 billion equal 91.5% of market cap, or about RMB 83.5 per share, roughly HKD 91 — nine-tenths of the share price is cash.

Net of cash, the market values the retail, logistics and food-delivery business at just over RMB 20 billion. On 2026 adjusted net profit of about RMB 34 billion, that is 7.5x on market cap and 0.6x to 1x on enterprise value.

Two reasons this scores 8 out of 10 rather than full marks: first, the RMB 235.1 billion is gross, not net of interest-bearing debt, and includes restricted cash; second, the money does not fully belong to shareholders — two years of food delivery have already burned about RMB 40 billion, and the same decision can be made again. The real source of margin of safety is not the valuation multiple but the unproven premise that management stops wasting money.

## Level 6: Discipline (4/10, fail)

The case for buying JD over the past two years came down to one sentence: food delivery is a high-frequency entry point that can pull users back into retail, letting high frequency drive low frequency. That assumption can now be tested, and it does not hold.

The evidence chain: delivery order volume share fell from a peak of about 31% in Q2 2025 to 7%–10% (UBS order-volume basis 7%, iResearch blended basis 15%); over the same period JD Retail revenue fell -4.7% YoY in 2026Q2, with the group at -2.9%; new business revenue was RMB 7.26 billion, -47.6% YoY. The entry point was never built, the money is spent, and the core business has gone backwards.

The discipline test is not whether the company is good, but whether my reason for buying still holds. The answer is no, and there is no substitute reason — cheap is not a reason, it is a price; prices change, but a reason must be able to validate itself. The 4 points are for the fact that it is at least still narrowing losses and still generating cash, not for its strategy.

## Veto items and items to be verified

Veto: discipline. Not a valuation veto — a logic veto. For a stock at 7.5x PE with 90% of market cap in cash, the only reason it stays cheap is that management keeps destroying value. Until that is disproven, any purchase is a bet on management's self-restraint, not on the business.

To verify 1: the slope of food-delivery losses. New-business operating loss of RMB 9.85 billion (Q1: RMB 10.35 billion; Q2 last year: RMB 14.78 billion) narrowed by just RMB 500 million in a quarter. At that pace, it reaches RMB 8 billion only by mid-2027. A sequential quarterly narrowing of more than RMB 1 billion is needed to confirm losses are truly being stemmed.

To verify 2: whether retail revenue turns positive. Q2 was -4.7%, and management guides Q3 back to positive. If Q3 is still negative, the wound from the fading national subsidy runs deeper than expected, and the HKD 125 neutral-case target must be cut by 20%.

To verify 3: the intensity of Double 11 spending. In-stock launch at 20:00 on October 12, closing on November 14; management has explicitly said it will step up investment. A sequential decline in Q4 profit is unavoidable — the key is how far. If Q4 operating profit falls below RMB 2 billion, the full-year adjusted forecast of RMB 34 billion must be cut.
