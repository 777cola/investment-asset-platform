Six-Gate Screen — JD.com

JD.com (9618.HK) | Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/10/26  ·  Price HK$99.40 (-0.60%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 280 billion  ·  PE (2026E adjusted) Approximately 7.5x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Six gates, three and a half passed: circle of competence, moat and margin of safety pass; good business and discipline fail; management is questionable — 39/60 overall. The sticking point is clear: the problem is not that the business is hard to understand, but that it does not make money — and management has just shown it will trade profit for a battlefield that is not its own. Q2 2026 revenue was RMB 346.4 billion, -2.9% YoY, with a group operating margin of 1.3%, yet operating cash flow reached RMB 37.8 billion and net cash per share was about HKD 91. At 7.5x adjusted P/E the stock is cheap enough that the company need not improve — only stop deteriorating. Conclusion: watch; revisit buying below HKD 90 or once quarterly food-delivery losses fall within RMB 8 billion.

Gate 1: Circle of competence (9/10, pass)

JD.com's business breaks into three reporting segments: JD Retail at RMB 295.4 billion, JD Logistics at RMB 64.1 billion and New Business at RMB 7.26 billion. These sum to RMB 366.8 billion, RMB 20.4 billion above consolidated revenue of RMB 346.4 billion; the gap is intersegment elimination, as logistics fulfils for retail and the revenue is eliminated on consolidation. Without visibility on this layer, one would assume segment revenue should sum to the group total.

Retail monetises three ways: direct-sales margin, platform commissions and advertising (Q2 platform advertising RMB 30.9 billion, +8.3%). Logistics earns fulfilment fees plus revenue from external customers (Q2 revenue +24.3%, now increasingly serving third-party merchants directly). New Business spans food delivery, Jingxi, JD Property and Europe's Joybuy, all currently loss-making.

Understandable does not mean simple — it means predictable. JD.com's revenue is predictable: electronics and home appliances RMB 157.9 billion, general merchandise RMB 109.2 billion and services RMB 79.3 billion, each with clear drivers (replacement cycle, category expansion, merchant count). Profit is not: it hinges on how much management chooses to subsidise in any given quarter. That is where the 1 point is deducted.

Gate 2: Good business (5/10, fail)

The hard numbers, not sentiment: group operating margin 45 ÷ 3,464 = 1.3%; JD Retail 135 ÷ 2,954 = 4.6%; JD Logistics 22.6 ÷ 641 = 3.5%. Taken together, this is a business that turns hundreds of billions in assets into tens of billions in profit, and the return comes from turnover, not pricing power.

4.6% is JD Retail's best-ever level for a promotional quarter and no small achievement — but it was reached after marketing spend was cut 24.8% YoY (RMB 20.3 billion, 5.9% of revenue, versus 7.6% last year). That 1.7pp equals roughly RMB 5.9 billion, almost exactly the entire swing in operating profit from -RMB 0.9 billion to +RMB 4.5 billion. This quarter's margin was saved, not earned. Savings have a ceiling; earnings do not.

The mark of a good business is raising prices without losing customers. JD cannot: 3C and home appliance prices are anchored by Pinduoduo's RMB 10 billion subsidy, daily necessities are siphoned off by Douyin and white-label brands, and any delivery fee increase loses orders. The 5 points go to turnover efficiency and cash conversion, not pricing power.

Gate 3: Moat (7/10, Pass)

JD.com has three things rivals cannot build in the short term: its warehousing and delivery network (over 1,500 warehouses, 900,000 ecosystem employees, 1,000 autonomous delivery vehicles in routine operation, 211 same-day/next-day delivery); its 3C and home-appliance mindshare (buying expensive big-ticket items on JD.com is one of Chinese consumers' most stable habits); and its supply-chain payment terms and rebates (an implicit profit source from brands).

But the moat is leaking at both ends. On price, Pinduoduo's white-label supply has peeled away price-sensitive users in whole cohorts; on immediacy, Meituan and Taobao Shangou have made 30-minute delivery the default expectation. JD.com is stuck in the middle: it cannot out-cheapen Pinduoduo or out-run Meituan, so it can only defend authentic goods plus next-day delivery. That position holds the existing base (3C, home appliances, essential supermarket goods) but not the incremental base (daily necessities, fresh food, instant retail).

A score of 7 means the moat is real, verifiable and able to keep out new entrants, but cannot keep out the two rivals already on the field.

Gate 4: Management (6/10, questionable)

Score capability and judgment separately. On capability, Xu Ran delivers: Q2 marketing expenses fell 24.8%, R&D rose 37.7% against the trend (RMB 7.3 billion, 2.1% of revenue), operating cash flow +54.5%, free cash flow +44.6%, and trailing 12-month free cash flow of RMB 31.4 billion (RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier). Pulling a company from a quarterly loss back to profit in just over a year is execution.

Judgment loses points. Going all-in on food delivery in 2025, JD had no rider network, no merchant base and no relative advantage in subsidy budget, yet chose to fight on Meituan's and Taobao Shangou's home turf. After roughly RMB 40 billion invested over two years, order volume share fell from a peak of 31% to 7%–10%, and new businesses still lost RMB 9.85 billion in a single quarter.This is not a matter of luck; it is running against the grain of someone else's economies-of-scale curve.

6 points: good at saving money, good at execution, bad at choosing battlefields. For a cash-flow company, that last item is precisely the most expensive.

Gate 5: Margin of safety (8/10, pass)

The arithmetic is simple: HKD 99.40 × 2.818 billion shares gives a market cap of about HKD 280 billion, or roughly RMB 256.9 billion at 1.09. Cash and short-term investments of RMB 235.1 billion equal 91.5% of market cap — about RMB 83.5 per share, or roughly HKD 91. Nine-tenths of the share price is cash.

Net of cash, the market is valuing the retail, logistics and food-delivery business at just over RMB 20 billion. On 2026 adjusted net profit of about RMB 34 billion, that is 7.5x market cap and 0.6x to 1x enterprise value.

Two reasons this scores 8 out of 10 rather than full marks. First, the RMB 235.1 billion is gross — not net of interest-bearing debt — and includes restricted cash. Second, the money does not fully belong to shareholders: two years of food delivery have already burned about RMB 40 billion, and management can make the same call again. The real source of margin of safety is not the valuation multiple but the unproven premise that management stops wasting money.

Level 6: Discipline (4/10, fail)

The bull case for JD over the past two years rested on a single sentence: food delivery is a high-frequency entry point that pulls users back into retail, so high frequency drives low frequency. That assumption is now testable, and it fails.

The evidence chain: delivery order volume share fell from a peak of about 31% in Q2 2025 to 7%–10% (7% on UBS order-volume basis, 15% on iResearch blended basis); over the same period JD Retail revenue fell -4.7% YoY in 2026Q2, with the group at -2.9%; new business revenue was RMB 7.26 billion, -47.6% YoY. The entry point was never built, the money is spent, and the core business has gone backwards.

The discipline test is not whether the company is good, but whether my reason for buying still holds. It does not, and there is no substitute reason — cheap is not a reason, it is a price; prices change, but a reason must be able to validate itself. The 4 points are for the fact that it is at least still narrowing losses and still generating cash, not for its strategy.

Veto items and items to be verified

Veto: discipline. This is a logic veto, not a valuation veto. On 7.5x P/E with 90% of market cap in cash, the only reason the stock stays cheap is that management keeps destroying value. Until that is disproven, any purchase is a bet on management's self-restraint, not on the business.

To verify 1: the slope of food-delivery losses. New-business operating loss of RMB 9.85 billion (Q1: RMB 10.35 billion; Q2 last year: RMB 14.78 billion) narrowed by just RMB 500 million in the quarter. At that pace, it reaches RMB 8 billion only by mid-2027. A sequential quarterly narrowing of more than RMB 1 billion is needed to confirm losses are truly being stemmed.

To verify 2: whether retail revenue turns positive. Q2 was -4.7%, and management guides Q3 back to positive. If Q3 is still negative, the wound from the fading national subsidy runs deeper than expected, and the HKD 125 neutral-case target must be cut by 20%.

To verify 3: the intensity of Double 11 spending. In-stock launch at 20:00 on October 12, closing on November 14; management has explicitly said it will step up investment. A sequential decline in Q4 profit is unavoidable — the key is how far. If Q4 operating profit falls below RMB 2 billion, the full-year adjusted forecast of RMB 34 billion must be cut.

Conclusion

Six gates: 39/60, fail. But the manner of failure matters: the stock was not rejected on valuation; it was rejected for not growing, not making money and a fondness for fighting. Two of the first three gates pass and the fifth scores 8, so the asset-side safety cushion is real; the second and sixth fail, so neither the earnings side nor the decision-making side offers assurance.

Operationally there are only two entry points: the price entry — a break below HKD 90 (6.8x adjusted P/E, the level at which per-share cash equals the share price); the event entry — Q3 results simultaneously deliver positive retail revenue growth and new-business losses narrowed to within RMB 8 billion. If either entry point appears, the sixth gate can be re-scored; until then, watch.
