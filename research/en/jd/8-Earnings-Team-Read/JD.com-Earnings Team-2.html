Buffett's close read: who gets the RMB 235.1 billion — JD.com

Cash flow matters far more than the income statement

Core view

He starts with the cash flow statement, moves to the balance sheet, and only then looks at the income statement. Order matters: cash flow shows how much money the company can generate, the balance sheet shows where that money sits, and the income statement shows which action produced it. For JD.com, the three answers are RMB 31.8 billion, RMB 235.1 billion, and cost savings.

Cash flow statement: the buy case

Operating cash flow of RMB 37.8 billion, +54.5%; free cash flow of RMB 31.8 billion, +44.6%; trailing 12-month free cash flow of RMB 31.4 billion versus RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier. Taken together, these are the only numbers in this report that need no explanation. A company with negative revenue growth tripled its free cash flow — it is recovering capital, not deploying it.

The question is where that threefold increase came from. The answer: capex, compressed; payment terms, with payables stretched; food delivery, with fulfilment spend cut. None of the three is permanent. Capex cannot be compressed indefinitely, payment terms cannot be stretched indefinitely, and food delivery still requires the investment it requires. So if revenue is still negative in 2027, that RMB 31.4 billion falls back to around RMB 20 billion.

Balance sheet: this is the valuation discount

Cash and short-term investments of RMB 235.1 billion against a market cap of RMB 256.9 billion — a coverage ratio of 91.5%. Per share that is RMB 83.5, or about HKD 91, against a share price of HKD 99.4. In other words, the market is pricing retail, logistics and food delivery plus a 600 million-user platform at HKD 8 per share.

Two explanations fit that price: the market is foolish, or the market does not believe the money will return to shareholders. Buffett would pick the second, having seen too many companies sit on piles of cash that shareholders never touch. His test is rigid: value of cash = cash × willingness to distribute. JD's willingness to distribute is currently discounted — an annual dividend since 2024 and a USD 3 billion buyback plan, but both too small relative to RMB 235.1 billion of cash.

His conclusion would therefore be: the price is right, the precondition is not. A 7.5x P/E prices in "no more reckless spending", not "a good business".

Conclusion

Buffett's close reading: cash flow is A-grade, the balance sheet is A-grade, the income statement is C-grade. The precondition for buying is not valuation but evidence that management returns cash to shareholders.
