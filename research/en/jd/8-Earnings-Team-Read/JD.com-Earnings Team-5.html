Synthesis of views: where the four converge and diverge — JD.com

Look at the four deep reads together.

Core view

The intersection of the four deep dives is threefold: profit improvement comes from expenses rather than gross profit, cash is a real asset but the willingness to allocate it is insufficient, and food delivery must contract. The divergence is also threefold: whether the profit recovery can be sustained, what the cash is really worth, and where JD.com's long-term position lies. These three points of divergence determine the target price range.

Intersection: three uncontested judgments

First, the quality of the profit improvement is poor. Four people used four formulations: Duan Yongping called it "squeezed out"; Buffett said "the source is contraction"; Munger said "losses reduced by shrinking scale"; Li Lu said "stopping losses, not growth." The same thing.

Second, the cash is real, but it does not belong to shareholders. Buffett put it most directly: the value of cash equals cash multiplied by the willingness to distribute. RMB 235.1 billion times a discount — that is its support for market cap.

Third, food delivery must contract. Munger believes it should stop immediately; Li Lu believes contraction is right but the direction is set; Duan Yongping and Buffett care only about the result, not the process (stop burning cash).

Divergence: three questions that determine the target price

Fork one: Can profit recovery hold? Duan Yongping says no (costs have bottomed), Buffett says it depends on revenue (revenue has not turned positive), Munger says it depends on food delivery (two years out), Li Lu says it does not matter (he is buying assets, not profits). This determines whether the neutral case is 125 or 105.

Fork two: What is the cash really worth? On a gross basis it covers 91.5% of market cap; on a net basis (after interest-bearing debt, excluding restricted cash) it may be only a bit over 70%. This determines whether the floor of the bear case is 80 or 70.

Fork three: Where does this sit long term? Li Lu's answer is heavy-cargo fulfillment; Duan Yongping would agree but considers that a small market. This determines whether the bull case deserves 175 — because 175 requires Joybuy to scale in Europe, not a recovery in the China business.

Synthesis conclusion

Take the median of each of the three branches: the profit-recovery leg persists (food delivery losses narrow but do not break even), cash is valued at 80%, and the long-term position sits in heavy-cargo fulfillment. The blended target price is HKD 115–125, implying +16% to +26% versus the current price.

That upside does not adequately cover risk. The four analysts' combined conclusion is therefore: watch, do not buy; wait for the price to reach HKD 90 (the cash-equivalent level) or for both Q3 targets to be met. There is no disagreement among the four on this point—the only disagreement is "how much to buy once we have waited."

Conclusion

The value of synthesis lies not in averaging, but in identifying which disagreements are verifiable. Two of the three forks can be verified within five weeks (Q3 revenue, Q4 food delivery losses); one takes a year (Joybuy). So this report's shelf life is five weeks, not a year.
