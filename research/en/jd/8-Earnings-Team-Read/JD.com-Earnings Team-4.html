Li Lu's Close Reading: What Remains After Contraction — JD.com

Long-term view from the -47.6% figure

Core view

He would first confirm the direction of the trend: new business revenue -47.6%, which shows food delivery is retreating. Then he would ask a longer-horizon question: after the retreat, what does JD.com have left? The answer is not the residual value of food delivery, but the "self-operated + self-built logistics" infrastructure and the mindshare of 600 million-plus users.

He looks at ten years, not two.

-47.6% is a bad number in a quarterly report, but may be a good number from a ten-year perspective—it means the company has finally begun to stem losses. Li Lu's framework is "whether this company will still occupy some irreplaceable position ten years from now."

Where does JD.com's position lie? In categories that are "heavy goods, large items, requiring installation and after-sales service": home appliances, 3C, furniture, building materials. These categories are characterized by complex fulfillment, high return costs and a need for trust—exactly what instant retail cannot do, and what Pinduoduo's white-label model cannot do well.

So his conclusion would be milder than Munger's: the failure in food delivery does not change JD.com's position in heavy-goods fulfillment, only its income statement. The real question is whether the market size of this position is large enough—home appliances and 3C are a trillion-level market, but growth is only in the single digits, which means JD.com's long-term ceiling is visible.

Why he still won't buy

Because "irreplaceable position" and "a business worth buying" are two different things. What Li Lu wants is "irreplaceable + able to raise prices"; JD.com has only the first half. He would classify JD.com as an "infrastructure-type company": its value lies in lowering society's transaction costs, not in extracting excess profit.

The right way to buy this kind of company is: buy when the market treats it as "finished", sell when the market treats it as a "growth stock". At 7.5x PE now, with 90% of market cap in cash, it falls into the former category, but has not yet reached the extreme — the true extreme is the share price falling below net cash per share, that is, below HKD 91.

Conclusion

Li Lu's close-reading conclusion: the contraction was right, but post-contraction JD.com is an "infrastructure company with a visible ceiling." It is worth buying when extremely cheap, not worth buying on a "recovery story."
