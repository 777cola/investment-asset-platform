Core view

Below is the cleaned-up publishable version, with tables and terminology removed, leaving only the judgments and the supporting evidence.

Body

On the day JD.com reported its Q2 2026 results, the market priced it in a deeply contradictory way: revenue fell 2.9% year on year, a rare negative growth since listing; but operating profit swung from a loss of RMB 0.9 billion in the year-earlier period to a profit of RMB 4.5 billion, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion, up 14.5% year on year. In the same set of statements, there is both decline and an inflection point.

First, where the money came from. Profit improved by RMB 5.4 billion this quarter, and the sources break down into just two: marketing spend fell from 7.6% of revenue to 5.9%, saving roughly RMB 5.9 billion; and food delivery losses narrowed from RMB 14.78 billion to RMB 9.85 billion, a reduction of RMB 4.9 billion. The two together far exceed RMB 5.4 billion, with the difference eaten up by the revenue decline.In other words, this is not the story of a business getting better, but of a business spending less.

Spending less has limits. With the marketing ratio at 5.9%, it is already close to the efficiency boundary for retail; cutting further means revenue falls faster — this quarter's -2.9% is the price of cutting too far. So next quarter's profit increment must come from gross margin or revenue, and neither shows any sign of that yet.

Now food delivery. New business revenue was RMB 7.26 billion, halved by 47.6% year on year, with a loss of RMB 9.85 billion. The loss is narrowing, but the loss ratio rose from 106.7% to 135.7% — because revenue shrank faster than the loss. This is not deteriorating efficiency but deliberate contraction: cutting low-efficiency order volume, exiting low-density cities, spreading fixed costs over a smaller revenue base. The direction is right, but the endpoint is not profit, it is a smaller loss.

The arithmetic on the food delivery battle should have been clear two years ago: JD.com had no rider network, no merchant base and no subsidy advantage, yet opened a fight on Meituan's and Taobao Shangou's home turf. Roughly RMB 40 billion was invested over two years, order share fell from a peak of 31% to 7%–10%, and retail revenue retreated 4.7% over the same period. The high-frequency-to-low-frequency hypothesis has been refuted by the data — users who come to JD.com to buy an iPhone will not order food delivery twice more just because they can.

Finally, cash — the healthiest part of the entire report. Operating cash flow was RMB 37.8 billion, up 54.5% year on year; free cash flow RMB 31.8 billion, up 44.6%; trailing twelve months RMB 31.4 billion, versus only RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier. Cash and short-term investments on the books were RMB 235.1 billion, up RMB 9.7 billion from the start of the year. Against a market cap of HKD 280 billion, that cash equals about 90% of market value, or roughly HKD 91 per share — with the stock at HKD 99.4, you are paying just HKD 8 for the retail, logistics and food delivery businesses combined.

That is where the 7.5x P/E comes from. The market has not failed to see the cash; it does not believe the cash will return to shareholders. The doubt is grounded: a company willing to spend RMB 40 billion over two years on food delivery can spend another RMB 40 billion on something else.

So the question was never "is JD.com cheap" but "will JD.com keep getting worse". The answer to the first is clear: cheap. The answer to the second can only wait: wait for the Q3 report to confirm retail revenue turns positive and quarterly food delivery losses fall below RMB 8 billion; or wait for the stock to drop below HKD 90, down to the level of cash per share.

Until either happens, the best move is to put it on the watchlist and do nothing. Real cheapness does not require you to hurry.

Conclusion

The key line for the publishable version: this is not a story of a business getting better, it is a story of a business spending less. Spending less has its limits; revenue recovery is the only way out.
