## Two conditions for establishment: cash and cash flow

Only the first and second criteria can be checked off for now. Operating cash flow of RMB 37.8 billion, free cash flow of RMB 31.8 billion and RMB 31.4 billion on a trailing 12-month basis have been verified across three consecutive financial reports — not a single-quarter fluctuation. Cash plus short-term investments of RMB 235.1 billion against a market cap of RMB 256.9 billion gives 91.5% coverage.

The second criterion, however, comes with a condition: a "valuation floor" holds only if cash is no longer being deployed in large amounts. If food delivery loses another RMB 30 billion next year, cash falls below RMB 200 billion, coverage drops to 78% and the floor shifts down by 13%. Both criteria therefore hold, but they protect against "losing big money," not against "failing to make money."

## A marginal one: the slope of food delivery

147.8 → 103.5 → 98.5: three numbers tracing a flattening curve. The first quarter's loss narrowed by 4.4 billion (seasonality plus deliberate contraction); the second narrowed by just 0.5 billion. If the third quarter narrows by a similar 0.5 billion, full-year 2027 losses still exceed 35 billion and the 36 billion net profit in our base case is simply unattainable.

The question is therefore not whether losses are shrinking but whether the pace of shrinkage is accelerating. Verifiable thresholds: sequential quarterly narrowing of more than 1 billion, and below 7 billion before 2027Q1. Absent these, the base-case target price must be cut from 125 to 105.

## One item to verify: Q3 revenue

Management expects Q3 retail revenue to "return to positive growth." That guidance rests on two things: whether the high base from national subsidies has been absorbed by Q3 (Q3 2025 was the subsidy peak, so the base is theoretically higher and a return to positive growth harder), and whether daily necessities and services revenue can keep holding up (+5.6% and +6.8% respectively in Q2).

Stack these two factors together and a Q3 return to positive growth is in fact no small challenge—if management dares to guide "expected to turn positive" in a quarter with an even higher base, it means the July and August operating data support it. That is a positive signal, but only the earnings report can confirm it. The Q3 report lands in mid-November, almost simultaneous with the close of Double 11 (11/14), so two variables resolve within two weeks.

## One unverified item, and the only one that matters

The fifth point cannot be verified with data; it can only be assessed through behavioural observation. Its importance exceeds the sum of the first four: the first four determine whether JD.com is worth 105 or 125; the fifth determines whether it becomes 70.

Why frame it this way: JD.com's asset quality (RMB 235.1 billion in cash, 1,500 warehouses, 600 million users) does not go to zero under any scenario, so the floor is set by how much cash gets burned, not by how much the business deteriorates. A company generating RMB 30 billion of free cash flow a year will certainly be worth more in ten years than it is now, provided it does not invest recklessly; burn RMB 30 billion a year and it could be worth nothing in ten years. That is the weight of the fifth point.

Three observable behavioural indicators: whether the food-delivery team is downsized, whether it exits some cities, and whether Q4 brings an announcement of a new round of subsidies or new business investment. If none of the three occurs, the fifth point can be upgraded from "unconfirmed" to "preliminarily established" — only then does the discussion turn to how much to buy.
