## Why retail companies' numbers are the easiest to get wrong

Retail earnings reports carry three definitional traps. First, sum of segments ≠ consolidated revenue: retail 295.4 + logistics 64.1 + new businesses 7.26 = RMB 366.8 billion, against a consolidated figure of RMB 346.4 billion. The RMB 20.4 billion gap is internal revenue from logistics fulfilling retail orders, eliminated on consolidation. Screenshot media typically just adds the segments and concludes, incorrectly, that "JD revenue is RMB 366.8 billion."

Second, four versions of net profit: operating profit of RMB 4.5 billion, net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 7.1 billion, non-GAAP net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 8.9 billion, and net profit including minority interests. All four are "correct," but they answer different questions. This report uses two consistently: net profit attributable to shareholders and adjusted net profit attributable to shareholders. The others are not mixed in.

Third, the definition of adjusted: JD's non-GAAP measure excludes share-based compensation and amortisation of intangible assets, but not gains or losses on disposal of assets. The RMB 1.8 billion difference between RMB 8.9 billion and RMB 7.1 billion is therefore mainly share-based compensation and amortisation, not "one-off gains." This must be stated clearly; otherwise "adjusted profit looks better" will be misread as "managing earnings."

## Two deviations above 1%: share and cash

Point one: delivery share differs by a factor of two. UBS's March 2026 instant retail tracker, on an order-volume basis, puts Meituan at 51%, Taobao Shangou at 42% and JD at 7%; iResearch 2026Q2, on a composite basis (in-store, flash purchase and food delivery combined), puts Meituan at 43%, Taobao Shangou at 42% and JD at 15%.Neither number is wrong; they answer different questions: the order-volume basis answers "who delivers more meals", the composite basis answers "who holds the larger share of the instant retail pie". This report uses 7%–15% throughout and treats the slide from the 2025 peak of 31% as the real conclusion — the trend points the same way under both bases, which matters more than the precise value.

Point two: the cash basis. The announced RMB 235.1 billion of "cash and short-term investments" comprises cash equivalents, restricted cash and short-term investments. Restricted cash is mainly deposits and platform merchant funds and cannot fund buybacks or dividends; media citing "cash of about RMB 230 billion" are counting the restricted portion. This report cites the announced gross amount but explicitly flags in the valuation section that it is "not net of interest-bearing debt, includes restricted cash" — these two qualifiers determine how strong the claim that nine-tenths of market cap is cash really is: an order-of-magnitude judgment, not liquidation value.

## Final adopted basis and three in-house estimates

Basis: RMB, consolidated statements, FY2026 Q2 (2026/04–06). Segment margins are calculated as segment operating profit divided by segment revenue, with no elimination adjustments. For YoY data, disclosed percentage figures in the announcements take priority; base-period back-calculated values are used only where the announcements do not give absolute figures, and are flagged as such.

Three self-built estimates, each with sources and methodology flagged: ① 2025Q2 base of RMB 356.7 billion = 346.4 ÷ (1−2.9%), back-calculated on the same basis as general merchandise 282.3, electronics and appliances 179.0, daily necessities 103.4 and services 74.2; these are estimated values; ② 2026 adjusted net profit of approximately RMB 34 billion, from our own model (derived by back-calculating from BofA's 2026/10/13 report, where 11x P/E corresponds to a HKD 145 target price); this is not company guidance; ③ net cash per share of RMB 83.5, approximately HKD 91 = 235.1 ÷ 2.818 billion shares, then converted at 1.09.
