Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — JD.com

What drives this business's profitability, and how defensible is that profit?
Research date 2026/10/26  ·  Price HK$99.40 (-0.60%)  ·  Market cap Approximately HKD 280 billion  ·  PE (2026E adjusted) Approximately 7.5x

Core view

JD.com's business model has three parts: first-party procurement and distribution earning the price spread, logistics earning fulfilment fees, and the platform earning commissions and advertising. Together they produce a high-turnover, low-margin, asset-heavy business whose moat rests on infrastructure rather than brand premium. That model's fate is binary: every efficiency gain adds a point of profit, and the moment efficiency stalls, profit falls to zero.

Revenue mix: 77% goods, 23% services

Goods accounted for RMB 267.1 billion (77.1%) of Q2 revenue of RMB 346.4 billion, with services at RMB 79.3 billion (22.9%). Within goods, electronics and appliances were RMB 157.9 billion and general merchandise RMB 109.2 billion. The mix tells one story: JD remains a goods seller, not a rent collector. A goods seller earns the spread on turnover; a rent collector earns the spread on traffic. The former's margin is inherently capped at two to three points.

Services were the only bright spot, up 6.8%: platform advertising came to RMB 30.9 billion (+8.3%) and logistics services RMB 48.4 billion (+5.9%). Advertising is the highest-gross-margin revenue line, so every point of mix shift toward it lifts group profit. But advertising revenue is capped by merchant count, merchant count by platform traffic, and traffic by category breadth—and general merchandise grew just 5.6%. The chain's starting point is weak.

Unit economics: how much profit per order

A rough back-of-envelope calculation: retail revenue of RMB 295.4 billion and operating profit of RMB 13.5 billion works out to RMB 4.6 of operating profit per RMB 100 of sales. That RMB 4.6 must cover warehousing and delivery, customer service, after-sales, technology and headquarters; only what remains is net profit. Group net margin is 2.1% (71 ÷ 3,464), so for every RMB 100 sold, RMB 2.1 ultimately reaches shareholders.

Food delivery tells a different story: revenue of RMB 7.26 billion against a loss of RMB 9.85 billion, meaning every RMB 100 of business loses RMB 135.7. The loss ratio in the same period last year was 106.7% (loss of RMB 14.78 billion / revenue of RMB 13.85 billion). The higher loss ratio does not signal deteriorating efficiency—it reflects revenue shrinking faster than losses, i.e. deliberate contraction.

Duan Yongping would pause here: a business that loses RMB 135 for every RMB 100 of revenue has only two reasons to exist—buying traffic or buying time. JD bought neither—traffic did not flow back to retail (retail -4.7%), and time did not buy share (7%–10%). So there is no third reason.

Where the business is strong, and where it is weak

Three strengths.First, a negative cash conversion cycle: collect first, pay later. Operating cash flow of RMB 37.8 billion is 5.3x net profit of RMB 7.1 billion, and the larger the scale, the more cash accumulates—the most underappreciated advantage of self-operated retail.Second, scarce infrastructure: over 1,500 warehouses, 900,000 ecosystem employees, 211 delivery. Money cannot buy the time rivals would need to replicate this.Third, stable category mindshare: repeat purchases in 3C appliances need no marketing push—the cheapest traffic there is.

Three weaknesses.First, no pricing power: raise prices and orders are lost; profit can only be squeezed out through efficiency.Second, rigid capex: warehouses, vehicles, autonomous delivery vehicles, technology—every year requires investment, and not investing means falling behind.Third, easy to be dragged into attrition warfare: food delivery in 2025 is the example—its cash-cow status gives it the ability to start a war, and also makes it more easily depleted by one.

In one sentence:this is a business of "using other people's money to earn its own efficiency," and efficiency is the only variable. Management has pushed efficiency to its limit (marketing spend cut 24.8%, R&D up 37.7%), but has also spent the efficiency dividend in advance.

Conclusion

Commercial view: JD.com's business quality is above average — a clear model, strong cash conversion and scarce infrastructure. The weaknesses are its margin structure and management's appetite for expansion.At 4.6%, the retail margin is neither an endpoint nor a starting point; it is a function of expenses, which means profit can be managed — and can also be destroyed.
