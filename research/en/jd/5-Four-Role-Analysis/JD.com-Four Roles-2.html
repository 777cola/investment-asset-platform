Reading the three statements: where the cash is, where the profit comes from, where it leaks

Core view

The income statement looks weak (revenue -2.9%, operating margin 1.3%), the cash flow statement looks strong (free cash flow RMB 31.8 billion, RMB 31.4 billion on a trailing 12-month basis), and the balance sheet is the most unusual (cash and short-term investments of RMB 235.1 billion, equal to 91.5% of market cap). Taken together, the three statements tell one story: the company is getting healthier as it shrinks, and losing growth as it gets healthier.

Income statement: the inflection is real, but the quality of earnings deserves a discount

Operating profit went from -RMB 0.9 billion last year to +RMB 4.5 billion this year, an improvement of RMB 5.4 billion. Breaking down the sources: marketing spend fell from RMB 27.1 billion to RMB 20.3 billion (backing out from 7.6% → 5.9% of revenue), saving RMB 5.9 billion; retail operating margin was 4.6%, up 0.1pct YoY; logistics margin was 3.5%; food delivery losses narrowed from RMB 14.78 billion to RMB 9.85 billion, a reduction of RMB 4.9 billion.

The three improvements together far exceed RMB 5.4 billion; the gap was partly eaten by the revenue decline—this is the difference between "profit saved" and "profit earned": the former gets eroded by revenue decline, the latter does not. Buffett looks at only one thing on the income statement: will this profit still be there next year. Marketing spend has already been cut to 5.9%; cutting further would hurt revenue, so next year's profit growth must come from gross margin or revenue, and neither shows any sign of that yet.

Cash flow statement: the healthiest part of the entire report

Operating cash flow RMB 37.8 billion, +54.5%; free cash flow RMB 31.8 billion, +44.6%; trailing 12-month RMB 31.4 billion, versus only RMB 10.1 billion a year earlier. Three numbers point to one thing: the company is recovering capital, not deploying it. Capex is being compressed, payables days are lengthening, and delivery fulfillment spend is falling.

But mind the nature of it: free cash flow tripled not because the company earned more, but because it spent less. This improvement has a ceiling—capex cannot be compressed indefinitely; warehouses need maintenance, vehicles need replacing, technology needs investment (R&D actually rose 37.7%). Truly sustainable free cash flow needs a return to revenue growth to support it. If revenue is still negative in 2027, the RMB 31.4 billion figure will fall back to around RMB 20 billion.

Balance sheet: 90% of market cap is cash, but don't treat it as liquidation value

Cash and short-term investments of RMB 235.1 billion, up RMB 9.7 billion from RMB 225.4 billion at the start of the year — cash is still rising even as loss-making businesses continue to burn it, and that can only come from operating cash flow. For a company with a market cap of RMB 256.9 billion, this cash equals 91.5%, or about RMB 83.5 per share, roughly HKD 91.

But Buffett would immediately add three qualifiers: gross, including restricted cash, and before deducting interest-bearing debt. Restricted cash is money that cannot be touched, such as platform merchant deposits, and the announcement does not break out interest-bearing debt separately. So the correct statement is "cash equals nine-tenths of market cap," not "there is HKD 91 per share available to pay you."

He would also run an "if I were the boss" calculation: with RMB 235.1 billion in cash, buying back 30% of shares would cost only about RMB 77 billion, and the remainder would be more than enough to sustain operations. The fact that this calculation can be done but nobody does it shows not that it cannot be done, but that they do not want to do it. That is the real source of the valuation discount, and the reason he scores the margin of safety 8 out of 10 rather than 10.

Conclusion

The financial view: cash flow and balance sheet are A-grade; the income statement is C-grade. At 7.5x adjusted PE, the market is pricing only the income statement and assigning no value to the balance sheet. This mismatch usually appears in two situations: either the market has lost trust in management, or it believes the cash will keep being burned — JD.com is both.
