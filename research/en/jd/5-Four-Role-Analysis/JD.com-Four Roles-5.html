Core view

The four perspectives agree completely on six points: mid-tier business quality, excellent cash flow, net cash as a real floor, food delivery as a failed investment, revenue that needs repair, and the current price not being a buying point. Only two genuine conflicts remain: whether food delivery should be stopped immediately (business and financial perspectives say stop; industry and risk perspectives say keep it, scaled down), and whether the neutral target price should be 125 or 105 (depending on whether retail revenue can turn positive in Q3). Where they agree: six things all four perspectives share

First, business quality is middling. The commercial lens says "high turnover, low margin"; the financial lens says "grade C income statement"; the industry lens says "strong in far-field, weak in instant delivery"; the risk lens says "the infrastructure layer gets displaced." Four statements, one meaning: this is not a business that prints money on its own.

Second, cash flow is excellent. RMB 37.8 billion operating cash flow, RMB 31.8 billion free cash flow, RMB 31.4 billion on a trailing 12-month basis — none of the four lenses questions the authenticity of these figures. A 5.3x cash flow to net profit ratio is the payment-terms dividend of the self-operated model, not an accounting trick.

Third, net cash is a real floor. RMB 235.1 billion against a market cap of RMB 256.9 billion, 91.5% coverage. The risk lens adds caveats (restricted cash included, interest-bearing debt not deducted), but no one denies the magnitude.

Fourth, food delivery was a failed investment. Roughly RMB 40 billion over two years, share falling from 31% to 7%–10%, retail revenue not lifted. The four lenses do not disagree on this; they differ only on "how to fix it."

Fifth, revenue must recover. All four lenses treat "retail revenue turning positive" as the precondition for a valuation re-rating; none believes cost savings alone can support HKD 125.

Sixth, the current price is not a entry point. HKD 99.4 corresponds to 7.5x adjusted PE — cheap, but the reason for the cheapness (management capital allocation) remains unresolved.

Disagreement one: should food delivery be shut down immediately, or kept in a reduced form?

The business and financial perspective argues for stopping immediately. The reasoning is a syllogism: unit economics are negative (RMB 135.7 lost per RMB 100 of revenue), strategic value is unproven (retail revenue -4.7%, no frequency migration), and the opportunity cost is clear (RMB 20 billion a year could buy back 7% of shares). Keeping it means spending RMB 20 billion a year for a 7% stake and an uncertain option.

The industry and risk perspective argues for keeping it, scaled down. The reasoning is more realistic: a full exit would forfeit the rider network and merchant relationships, and if the instant retail landscape shifts in future (regulatory intervention, Meituan raising prices, for example), JD.com would not even qualify to re-enter; a one-off exit would also incur large restructuring charges, making the Q4 report uglier still.

Verdict: give it a six-month proving period. Not sentiment — the data is not yet sufficient to decide. Losses are narrowing (147.8 → 103.5 → 98.5); the direction is right, the slope too slow. Set a hard condition: single-quarter loss must fall below RMB 7 billion before 2027Q1 (a further RMB 2.8 billion sequential reduction), otherwise divest. The condition is verifiable and non-negotiable, avoiding the "give it another year" loop.

Disagreement two: neutral target price of 125 or 105

The financial-view HKD 125 is based on 2027 adjusted net profit of RMB 36 billion × 9x PE. This assumption embeds one premise: retail returns to low single-digit growth. The risk view points out that this premise currently has no evidence; Q2 retail was -4.7%, and management's "Q3 expected to turn positive" is guidance, not fact.

Verdict: tiered treatment. Q3 retail revenue turns positive → maintain HKD 125; Q3 still negative → cut target price to HKD 105 (corresponding to 2027 adjusted net profit of RMB 32 billion × 8.5x). HKD 105 offers only 5.6% upside from the current price, meaning "if it doesn't turn positive, the opportunity cost of this investment exceeds the return"—which is itself a reason not to buy.

The benefit of this tiering is that it hands the judgment to imminent facts rather than to analyst confidence. The Q3 earnings report is in mid-November, and Double 11 wraps up on November 14; both events will land within five weeks. In other words, this report has a shelf life of only five weeks—it must be rewritten after five weeks, and views cannot be held on inertia.

Action plan following the ruling

Rating: Watch, wait for food delivery losses to stop. No position; add to watchlist with two triggers.

Price trigger: HKD 90. Implies market cap of HKD 253.6 billion (about RMB 232.6 billion), cash coverage 101% — the share price equals net cash per share. Buying at this level means getting retail, logistics and food delivery for free, a completely different risk-reward structure.

Event trigger: Q3 double hit. Retail revenue turns positive and new business losses ≤ RMB 8 billion. Both conditions met simultaneously would confirm that "contraction + repair" are both underway, giving a basis for the HKD 125 neutral-case target and allowing an initial position in the HKD 100–110 range (no more than 3% of the portfolio).

Veto triggers: announcement of a new large-scale investment round, free cash flow turning negative, or cash balance falling more than RMB 20 billion in a single quarter — any one of these removes the stock from the watchlist outright, with no further valuation discussion.

Conclusion

The four perspectives converge strongly, indicating that the judgment on this company does not depend on which perspective is chosen, only on two things: whether revenue can return to growth, and whether management will fight another battle. The former will be clear within five weeks (Q3 results + the conclusion of Double 11); the latter can only be assessed through observed behavior.

Conclusion: watch, do not build a position. A price of HKD 90 or Q3 hitting both targets — whichever comes first — triggers a position-building review; an announcement of new investment or cash burn removes it from the list outright.
