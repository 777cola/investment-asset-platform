Core thesis

Seven developments at JD.com between August and October 2026 are worth recording: the Q2 results, the taper of the national subsidy, Q3 guidance, ratings from three brokers, the Double 11 launch, and a steady slide to -24.6% from the 52-week high. Three are genuine signals — the subsidy taper sets the depth of the wound in electronics and home appliances, the delivery contraction sets the pace of profit recovery, and Morgan Stanley's stake reduction captures how the market prices "cash that never returns to shareholders"; the other four are noise or semi-noise. The share price fell from HKD 131.8 to HKD 99.4; what fell was not earnings but the narrative.

Signal one: how deep the wound from the fading national subsidy

Electronics and home appliances revenue was RMB 157.9 billion in Q2, down 11.8% YoY and the main drag on total revenue growth of -2.9%. The figure is often misread as "JD losing share"; in reality, the 2025 trade-in subsidy pulled demand forward, and the 2026 policy rollback produced a natural payback.

Two comparisons help judge the nature of this: first, general merchandise +5.6%, with categories outside the subsidy scheme still growing; second, JD's upstream bargaining position is unchanged, with 3C and home appliances remaining one of brands' largest single online channels. Both point to a cyclical wound rather than structural bleeding. The wound should heal in H1 2027, as the high base will not be fully absorbed until Q2 2027.

That is also why guidance for "Q3 retail revenue turning positive" is worth watching: if revenue turns positive before the national subsidy base is fully absorbed, growth in general merchandise and services is already offsetting the appliance gap — and that would signal real capability.

Signal 2: Food delivery contraction — pace matters more than direction

New business losses: RMB 14.78 billion (2025Q2) → RMB 10.35 billion (2026Q1) → RMB 9.85 billion (2026Q2). The direction is right, but the slope is too slow — just RMB 0.5 billion narrower per quarter. At this pace, losses reach RMB 8 billion by mid-2027 and only approach breakeven in 2028.

The revenue side is more telling: new business revenue of RMB 7.26 billion, down 47.6% YoY.Revenue halved while losses fell only a third, showing the contraction came from cutting order volume rather than improving efficiency. Cutting order volume quickly reduces variable costs, but fixed costs (technology, organization, rider base pay) cannot be spread, so the loss ratio actually rose from 106.7% to 135.7%.

Together, these tell us the food delivery business has entered a "maintain minimum scale" state: there is no more share story, only a cost story. The impact on valuation — it has gone from an "option" to a "liability," and the market will no longer price in any upside.

Signal three: 148 and the reduction — the disagreement is not about earnings but about cash

JPMorgan set HKD 148 and BofA HKD 145 the same month, while Morgan Stanley cut to Underweight (implying about 8x P/E). Revenue assumptions differ little across the three; the entire gap sits in what RMB 235.1 billion of cash is worth.

The bull case: cash equals 90% of market cap, so once management stops burning it, the valuation must converge. The bear case: the cash has been burning for two years and will keep burning, so it cannot be counted at face value. Both are right; they differ on the probability they assign to management behavior.

That is why the October share price did not react to good news (JPMorgan's HKD 148 failed to hold) and did not crash on bad news (still near 99 after Morgan Stanley's Underweight) — the market has already made the "cash discount" the consensus, and what it awaits is not opinion but action.

Which parts are noise

First noise: target price. The 145 and 148 figures rest on two optimistic assumptions — that food delivery stops losing money, and that cash is counted at face value. That is scenario analysis, not a forecast. A target price cut or hike changes no fundamental.

Second noise: Double 11 marketing volume. "Step up investment" and "in-store debut" are the same lines every year. The real information sits in the Q4 expense ratio, not in the launch event.

Third, semi-noise: the stock near its 52-week low. HKD 96 means nothing in itself; what matters is the P/E multiple and cash coverage it implies — HKD 99.4 is 7.5x adjusted P/E, HKD 96 is 7.2x. A 3% drop changes no judgment.

The signal beyond the noise: how management describes food delivery on the Q3 call — "optimize efficiency" means contraction, "seize the opportunity" means keep fighting. One phrase sets the direction.

Conclusion

No black swans in the past three months — just three things being slowly priced in: the wound from the rollback of national subsidies (cyclical, healing in 1H27), the slope of food-delivery contraction (too slow, breakeven unlikely before 2028), and disagreement over where the cash belongs (the standoff between the 148 and the stake reduction). The stock fell from 131.8 to 99.4; what de-rated was the "food delivery can work" narrative, not earnings.

On positioning: none of these events is a buy or sell signal. The real signal comes in five weeks — Q3 results (mid-November) and the final Double 11 tally (11/14).
