## Why not buy a 1% starter position now?

The point of a starter position is to "spend a little money to buy research time," but JD.com does not need a starter position to buy time—all its key variables resolve within five weeks (Q3 results in mid-November, Double 11 close on 11/14). Spending five weeks waiting for one certain fact is far more worthwhile than spending 1% of position size to feel the volatility.

Another reason is high correlation. JD.com's daily volatility correlation with Alibaba, Meituan and Pinduoduo is above 0.7, and around 0.8 with the Hang Seng Tech Index. If the portfolio already holds China internet e-commerce exposure, adding another 1% in JD.com amounts to leveraging the same risk factor rather than diversifying. For highly correlated names, the information value of a starter position is close to zero—what you feel is the sector, not the company.

## Specific discipline for adding positions, trimming positions and stop-losses

Adding: triggers only, not sentiment. Three price triggers at 90/86/82, one-third at each level; event trigger: Q3 double target met, build to 3% in one go. Only when both overlap is 5% allowed.

Trimming: by logic, not by price. Three trim conditions—food delivery losses widen quarter on quarter (signaling the contraction has stopped), retail revenue negative growth for two consecutive quarters, announcement of major new investment. Any one appears, halve the position; two appear together, exit entirely.

Stop-loss: no price stop, a logic stop. This is the key distinction: if the share price falls from 90 to 75 while cash remains at RMB 235.1 billion, that is a better price, not a reason to stop out (HKD 75 implies a market cap of about RMB 193.9 billion, cash coverage 121%). The real stop-loss is "cash disappearing"—free cash flow turning negative or the cash balance falling more than RMB 20 billion in a single quarter. At that point, even at 60, you should leave.

## Relationship to other portfolio holdings

Before adding JD.com to the portfolio, calculate three correlations first. With China internet e-commerce (Alibaba, Pinduoduo, Meituan): 0.7–0.8, sharing three factors: "China consumption + platform regulation + Hong Kong liquidity." With the Hang Seng Tech Index: about 0.8, essentially beta replication. With A-share consumer/manufacturing: around 0.3, a low correlation, but this does not justify diversification, because JD.com's drivers sit on the Hong Kong side.

So the portfolio-level rule is: total China internet e-commerce exposure capped at 8%. If Meituan or Alibaba is already held, JD.com's allocation must come out of that 8%, not be counted separately. The reason is that all three are the same bet on "food delivery subsidy retreat"—all betting the subsidy war ends and margins recover. Betting on three names is not diversification; it is leverage.

The only diversification value lies in the time dimension: JD.com's catalysts (Q3 results, food delivery loss cut) land earlier than Alibaba's (returns on cloud and AI capex) and Meituan's (instant retail margins). If using it as an "event position," it suits better than the other two.
