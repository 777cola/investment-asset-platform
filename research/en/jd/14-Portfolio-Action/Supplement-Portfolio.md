## Why not buy a 1% starter position now?

A starter position exists to "spend a little money to buy research time," but JD.com does not need one to buy time—every key variable resolves within five weeks (Q3 results in mid-November, Double 11 closing on 11/14). Spending five weeks waiting for a single certain fact beats spending 1% of position size to feel the volatility.

High correlation is the second reason. JD.com's daily volatility correlation with Alibaba, Meituan and Pinduoduo is above 0.7, and around 0.8 with the Hang Seng Tech Index. If the portfolio already carries China internet e-commerce exposure, another 1% in JD.com leverages the same risk factor rather than diversifying it. For highly correlated names, the information value of a starter position is close to zero—what you feel is the sector, not the company.

## Specific discipline for adding, trimming and stopping out positions

Adding: triggers only, not sentiment. Three price triggers at 90/86/82, one-third at each level; event trigger: Q3 double target met, build to 3% in one go. Only when both overlap is 5% allowed.

Trimming: by logic, not by price. Three trim conditions: food delivery losses widen QoQ (signaling the contraction has stopped), retail revenue negative growth for two consecutive quarters, or announcement of major new investment. Any one appears, halve the position; two appear together, exit entirely.

Stop-loss: no price stop, a logic stop. This is the key distinction: if the share price falls from 90 to 75 while cash remains at RMB 235.1 billion, that is a better price, not a reason to stop out (HKD 75 implies a market cap of about RMB 193.9 billion, cash coverage 121%). The real stop-loss is "cash disappearing"—free cash flow turning negative or the cash balance falling more than RMB 20 billion in a single quarter. At that point, even at 60, you should leave.

## Relationship to other portfolio holdings

Before adding JD.com to the portfolio, run three correlations first. Versus China internet e-commerce (Alibaba, Pinduoduo, Meituan): 0.7–0.8, on three shared factors: China consumption, platform regulation and Hong Kong liquidity. Versus the Hang Seng Tech Index: about 0.8, essentially beta replication. Versus A-share consumer/manufacturing: around 0.3 — a low correlation, but no grounds for diversification, since JD.com's drivers sit on the Hong Kong side.

The portfolio-level rule follows: cap total China internet e-commerce exposure at 8%. If Meituan or Alibaba is already held, JD.com's allocation must come out of that 8%, not sit on top of it. All three are the same bet on "food delivery subsidy retreat" — that the subsidy war ends and margins recover. Owning three names is not diversification; it is leverage.

The only diversification value is in the time dimension: JD.com's catalysts (Q3 results, food delivery loss cut) land earlier than Alibaba's (returns on cloud and AI capex) and Meituan's (instant retail margins). As an event position, it works better than the other two.
