Core view

Four people read the same financial report, and their common ground is wider than expected: no dispute over business quality, the gross margin decline is structural, customer concentration is fate, and 18x is a compromise price, not a cheap one.

There is only one disagreement: whether the off-balance-sheet guarantees should be an outright veto. Three say yes; one says position sizing can hedge it.

Intersection one: this is a "high-quality, deteriorating-quality" earnings report

All four acknowledge the numbers in this report are solid: revenue +86%, AI +221%, net margin 55%, free cash flow RMB 13.67 billion. All four also flag the same problem: gross margin is on a downward slope, and the slope is structural.

This consensus matters because it rules out two common misreadings. The first is "results beat, so buy" — results did beat, but the beat was in revenue, while gross margin is heading lower. The second is "gross margin is falling, so leave" — the decline is a result of the business model, not a loss of competitiveness.

The correct read is: trade earnings growth for a valuation multiple, not a valuation multiple for earnings growth. Broadcom's current earnings growth (FY27 EPS +48%) exceeds its valuation multiple (18x), and that is its only bull case.

Disagreement: are off-balance-sheet guarantees a "risk" or an "uncertainty"?

Buffett and Munger call it risk; Li Lu calls it uncertainty; Duan Yongping says "if you can't understand it, don't take a heavy position." Three formulations, one meaning: this exposure has no probability distribution.

In investing, risk can be priced; uncertainty cannot. Given RMB 59.6 billion of on-balance-sheet debt, you can calculate worst-case interest coverage; given a guarantee with no cap, no maturity and no recourse clause, you can calculate nothing.

Verdict: treat it as "uncertainty" — do not value it, but demand price compensation. How large should that compensation be? Use the bear-case USD 225 as the reference — that scenario assumes AI revenue reaches only USD 85 billion, the financing platform runs into trouble, and EPS is USD 15. At USD 355, the current price sits 58% above it, implying the market assigns a very low probability to that scenario. I put the probability at no less than 20%, so the current price offers no compensation.

Synthesis: One earnings report changes the valuation approach, not the rating

Together, the four of them change not whether to buy, but how to value Broadcom.

It used to be treated as a "chip + software" twin engine, deserving 25–30x; now it is treated as "AI hardware + software attachment," deserving 18x. That shift has already happened, between September 2 and September 15, 2026 — the stock fell from 367 to 339, and what fell was not earnings but the valuation framework.

With the framework shift complete, Broadcom has become a company judged on EPS growth, not on narrative. That is good for value investors: EPS can be calculated, narrative cannot.

The combined conclusion: maintain "Watch," but change the metrics being watched from "AI revenue growth" to "EPS delivery + gross margin floor." Both metrics have quarterly readings and both can be falsified.

Conclusion

Synthesis: the four converge on "good company, bad structure, compromise price"; they diverge on "whether off-balance-sheet items should be an outright veto." The verdict treats this as uncertainty and demands price compensation. Rating stays at Watch; the watch metrics shift to EPS delivery and the gross margin floor. December 9 earnings is the next data point.
