Buffett-style Earnings Deep Dive — Broadcom

Broadcom FY26Q3 | A handsome income statement, with an invisible piece on the balance sheet

Core thesis

Buffett starts with the articulation across the three financial statements: whether profit is cash, whether cash is free, and whether free cash is eaten away by debt and commitments.

Broadcom clears the first two: net margin 55.3%, free cash flow RMB 13.67 billion. It fails the third — beyond RMB 59.6 billion of on-balance-sheet debt, there is also a commitment to finance customers, of undisclosed size.

Income statement: the three numbers tie together

Revenue RMB 29.59 billion, +86% YoY; AI RMB 16.7 billion, +221%; software RMB 8.75 billion, +30%. Revenue grew by RMB 13.68 billion, with AI contributing RMB 11.5 billion and software RMB 2 billion — RMB 13.5 billion combined, broadly matching. Growth therefore rests on real shipments from two business lines, not accounting treatment.

Margins hold up: Non-GAAP gross margin 75%, net margin 55.3%. Gross margin fell 2.1 points while net margin rose 2.5 points, because opex ratios fell further — the classic effect of M&A integration.

The one item to watch is GAAP net profit of RMB 13.1 billion, +216% YoY, far faster than Non-GAAP's +95%. The gap between the two is only RMB 3.27 billion, narrower than historical levels. A narrowing gap implies lower amortization, and possibly one-off gains. Buffett would go through the footnotes; here we can only flag a question mark.

Cash flow statement: the money printer is still running, but slowing down

Free cash flow reached USD 13.67 billion, a record high for a single quarter; annualized at USD 52 billion, the free cash flow yield is 3.1%. For a company still expanding at high speed, this figure is rare — most AI companies ran negative free cash flow this quarter.

But the conversion rate fell from around 1.0 in the past to 0.83. The USD 2.7 billion gap sits in receivables and inventory: AI revenue rose 54% quarter on quarter, customer payment terms were unchanged, so receivables rose in step; and to support next quarter's USD 21.7 billion of shipments, materials stocking is also rising.

This is normal during expansion, but it means two things: first, the income statement's growth rate looks better than the cash flow growth rate; second, if demand turns, this batch of stocked materials becomes an impairment.Buffett's stance on inventory has always been: inventory in a growth phase is an asset, inventory in a downturn is an expense, and only one quarter separates the two.

Balance sheet: RMB 59.6 billion is not the scary part—what you can't see is

The balance sheet is clean: USD 59.6 billion of fixed-rate debt, long maturities, 25x interest coverage. With USD 52 billion of annualized free cash flow, half can be repaid in three years. Buffett would like this structure — low-cost long-term liabilities matched with high-return assets.

Off balance sheet is another matter. The company provides credit support for AI customers' chip purchases, with arrangements tied to Apollo and Blackstone of undisclosed size; BofA estimates new debt issuance could reach USD 150 billion in 2027 and cumulative senior debt of USD 370 billion by mid-2029.

The nature of this arrangement is "credit enhancement," not Broadcom lending money. If all goes well, Broadcom pays nothing and sells more chips; if customer deployments fall short, the guarantees return to the financial statements as purchase commitments or residual-value guarantees. The Buffett-style question is: how much capital must I add in the worst case? Without an answer to that question, this company should not get a "Buy" conclusion.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: a good company, but I cannot calculate the worst case. All three statements tie out; earnings are real, cash is abundant, debt is manageable; the only problem is that off-balance-sheet commitments have no cap. Action: file it under "too expensive or too ugly" and wait for one of two conditions — the price falls below USD 250 (which would cover the worst case), or the guarantee terms are fully disclosed and are limited-recourse.
