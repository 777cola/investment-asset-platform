Edited and polished: The bad news in Broadcom's earnings is not in the numbers — Broadcom

Core view

Revenue USD 29.591 billion, +86% YoY; AI semiconductors USD 16.7 billion, +221% YoY; Non-GAAP net margin 55%. These are the numbers Broadcom delivered on September 2, 2026 — all but impeccable.

Then the stock fell 2.7% on September 3 and slid to USD 339 by September 15, down 20% from its August high of USD 427. What the market sold off was not the results, but two things unrelated to the results.

First point: gross margin is trending toward hardware

Non-GAAP gross margin 75.0%, down 2.1 percentage points QoQ; Q4 guidance 73%, versus 78% a year earlier. Down 5 points in a year, one direction only.

Management explains it clearly: XPU hardware is a rising share, and HBM is a rising share of cost. In plain terms — Broadcom is increasingly reselling other people's hardware and increasingly less selling its own designs.

The brutal part is that this is the same thing as success. The faster AI revenue grows, the higher the hardware mix, the lower the gross margin. The more successful custom ASIC becomes, the more the company looks like a hardware company.

So is it still a good business? Yes. A 75% gross margin still tops TSMC's 58%; 73% also tops any large semiconductor company. But it no longer commands a "software company" valuation — the market will not pay 30x for a company whose gross margin falls 2 points a year. 18x is the price of that shift.

Second point: it is borrowing money on behalf of clients

The company provides credit support for AI chip customers' purchases; the size of arrangements involving Apollo and Blackstone has not been disclosed. BofA estimates that the platform could issue USD 150 billion in new debt in 2027, accumulating USD 370 billion in senior debt by mid-2029.

This is not financial fraud, nor even aggressive accounting. It is an extension of a business model—translating "customers cannot afford to buy" into "I lend you the money to buy." The problem is that this money does not appear on Broadcom's financial statements.

If all goes well, Broadcom pays nothing and sells more chips; if customer deployment falls short of expectations, the guarantees return to the financial statements in the form of purchase commitments or residual-value guarantees. As a shareholder, you are buying a chip order with an implicit guarantee attached.

This is also why, for the same profit growth of around 50%, the market gives Nvidia thirty times and Broadcom eighteen times. This is not mispricing; it is risk pricing.

The third point: the acceleration behind the numbers is slowing

Quarterly AI revenue: about RMB 8.76 billion in Q1, about RMB 10.84 billion in Q2, RMB 16.7 billion in Q3, guidance of RMB 21.7 billion for Q4. QoQ +24%, +54%, +30%.

Q3's +54% is an outlier, reflecting Google TPU v7 volume shipments and Anthropic's initial deployment; Q4 falls back to +30%, signaling the high base has arrived. FY27 guidance of USD 115 billion, +98% YoY, sounds explosive—but annualizing Q4's USD 86.8 billion, FY27 needs only another 32%.

"Doubling" is the YoY measure; "+32%" is the run-rate measure. The market trades the latter. That is why the stock did not rise after guidance.

This is not bad news—it is the normal marker of a company moving from "breakout phase" to "delivery phase." Valuation in the delivery phase rests on EPS, not on story.

So what should be done?

USD 355 corresponds to 18.3x FY27 expected earnings (EPS USD 19.38), and 27x FY26. The base case of USD 388 offers only 9% upside; the bear case of USD 225 implies 37% downside. The range is symmetric; the certainty is not.

The path up requires six things to hold at once: AI revenue materializes, six customers spend on plan, gross margin holds at 71%, software does not decelerate, the financing platform does not blow up, and the market is willing to pay 20x. The path down requires only one thing to fail.

So the answer is watch, not buy. Two triggers, stated plainly: a drop below USD 300 (about 15.5x FY27), or the financing arrangement being explicitly disclosed as limited recourse with quantifiable exposure. The December 9 FY26Q4 earnings report is the nearest checkpoint.

A good company does not need a good story on top of a good price. What it lacks now is a clear account of that off-balance-sheet item.

Conclusion

Conclusion: good company, good price, but missing an off-balance-sheet picture that can be quantified. 18x FY27 is not expensive; margin compression and financing guarantees are capping the multiple. Build the position below USD 300 or after the financing structure is disclosed; the December 9 earnings report is the test point.
