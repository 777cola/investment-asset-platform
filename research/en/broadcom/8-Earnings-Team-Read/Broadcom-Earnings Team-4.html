Core thesis

When Li Lu reads financial reports, he first asks: what technology trend does this USD 16.7 billion buy? If the trend lasts a decade, short-term fluctuations do not matter; if it lasts three years, the valuation must be set as a cyclical stock.

The answer: ASIC replacing GPU is a ten-year trend, but this round of infrastructure buildout is a three-year cycle. Only the two overlaid together capture the true meaning of this financial report.

XPU's RMB 12.19 billion: the trend is real

The economics of custom chips are simple: once the model architecture stabilizes, purpose-built chips outperform general-purpose ones, at half the cost of a GPU. This is not Broadcom marketing talk; it is a fact the TPU has already proven over the past decade.

What has changed is this: inference volume now exceeds training volume. Training demands flexibility, where general-purpose GPUs prevail; inference demands only cost, where ASICs prevail. As inference becomes the dominant workload, ASIC share will rise. This is a structural, irreversible shift, and Broadcom stands at its entry point.

So the USD 12.19 billion in XPU revenue is an early reading on the trend, not a cyclical peak. Li Lu would assign a higher certainty weighting to this revenue stream.

Meituan's RMB 4.51 billion: the underrated second leg

AI networking revenue was RMB 4.51 billion, 27% of AI revenue. This figure corresponds to the Tomahawk 6 switch chip, which solves the problem of how to connect tens of thousands of chips.

Nvidia's answer is NVLink plus InfiniBand — closed but high-performance; Broadcom's answer is Ethernet — open but requiring stronger scheduling capability. As cluster scale moves from thousands of chips to hundreds of thousands, Ethernet's cost advantage widens.Tomahawk 7 has already taped out at 200Tbps, built for the next generation of clusters.

The significance of this leg is that it does not depend entirely on those six XPU customers. Anyone building an AI data center has to buy switch chips, so the customer base is more diversified and cyclicality is weaker. Broadcom is therefore not "a supplier to six customers" but "a supplier to AI data centers" — a distinction that should be reflected in valuation, yet the market currently prices it only as the former.

Cycle: this infrastructure buildout lasts three years, not ten

The technology trend spans a decade; financial performance spans three years. This round of AI infrastructure capex is funded by six companies' financing, and the financing window will not stay open forever.

Li Lu would run two separate calculations: the trend account — ASIC share will climb from roughly 20% today to 40%, Broadcom is the biggest beneficiary, and that part is worth paying for; the cycle account — the USD 115 billion guidance for 2027 marks the peak zone of this infrastructure buildout, after which growth will fall to 20%–30%.

Adding the two together, the conclusion is "a good company, but don't pay growth-stock valuations at a cycle peak." 18x is exactly the price for that judgment: no growth-stock premium, no cyclical discount.

His move would be: small position, long hold, add on the downcycle. Not now.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: right direction, right position, uncertain timing. ASIC replacing GPU is a ten-year trend, Broadcom holds the best position, and networking chips are the undervalued second leg. But USD 115 billion is the peak of this infrastructure cycle, not the starting point. Action: hold a small position (2%–3%) for long-term tracking, and add on a cyclical downturn when valuation returns to 12x.
