## Layer one: revenue mix — AI already past half, the other two flat

Total revenue USD 29.591 billion, +86% YoY, split as follows: AI semiconductor USD 16.7 billion (+221%), non-AI semiconductor USD 4.14 billion (+4.05%), infrastructure software USD 8.751 billion (+30.1%).

Taken together, the message is clear: Broadcom now has only one leg running. Non-AI semiconductor is essentially flat, software holds 30% on VMware price hikes and subscription conversion, and all remaining incremental growth comes from AI. Of the USD 13.68 billion revenue increase, AI contributed USD 11.5 billion, or 84%.

AI itself is splitting: XPU USD 12.19 billion (73%), AI networking USD 4.51 billion (27%). XPU is custom silicon with only six customers; networking chips sell to virtually everyone building AI data centers, with a more dispersed customer base and stronger cyclicality. The higher XPU's share, the more concentrated revenue and the lower gross margin—deteriorating on both fronts at once.

## Layer two: gross margin — a 5-point decline in a year reflects structural pressure, not the cycle

Non-GAAP gross margin 75.0%, down 2.1pct QoQ; GAAP 69%. The four-quarter trajectory: FY25Q4 78%, FY26Q2 77.1%, FY26Q3 75.0%, FY26Q4 guidance 73%.

Management cites two drivers: a higher share of XPU hardware and a higher share of HBM in cost. In one sentence: a growing share of what Broadcom sells is "reselling someone else's hardware," and a shrinking share is "selling its own designs."

Can this trend reverse? No. FY27 AI revenue guidance is USD 115 billion, double FY26's USD 58 billion, and the increment is almost entirely XPU hardware. Gross margin therefore keeps sliding; the question is where it bottoms.My rough floor is around 71% (FY27 revenue USD 173.5 billion, AI at 66%); a break below 70% would signal a loss of pricing power, not just a mix problem.

## Tier 3: costs and profit — a 55% net margin is the best on record

Non-GAAP net income came in at USD 16.372 billion, up 95% YoY, for a net margin of 55.3%, 2.5 percentage points above the same period last year. Gross margin is falling while net margin is rising, and the reason is that the expense ratio is falling faster than gross margin.

Broadcom's cost structure is very light: the R&D expense ratio has long been 9%–10% and SG&A 2%–3%, because it has only a few dozen customers and does not need a large sales force. Cutting costs after acquisitions is Hock Tan's signature move, and the expense ratio fell further after the VMware integration.

Two things need flagging, however. First, GAAP net income was 13.1 billion, up 216% YoY, far faster than the 95% Non-GAAP growth; the gap between the two is only 3.27 billion, indicating that the amortization burden is lightening and may also include one-off items that the financial statements do not separately disclose. Second, the 55% net margin is on a Non-GAAP basis, excluding stock-based compensation and acquisition amortization; on a GAAP basis it is 44.3%. Using the former for valuation is industry practice, but the latter must not be forgotten.

## Tier 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — strong cash generation, but conversion is falling

Quarterly free cash flow of USD 13.67 billion, or roughly USD 52 billion annualized, equates to a 3.1% FCF yield on a USD 1.69 trillion market cap. With the dividend yield at 0.66%, the company retains most of its cash to pay down debt and buy back stock.

The flaw lies in conversion: FCF/Non-GAAP net income of 0.83, down from last quarter. The reason: AI revenue rose 54% QoQ, and receivables and inventory expanded in tandem — a meaningful portion of revenue recognized this quarter has not yet been collected, while the company pre-builds inventory for next quarter's USD 21.7 billion of AI shipments.

The on-balance-sheet picture is clean: USD 59.6 billion of fixed-rate debt, long maturities, 25x interest coverage, half of it repayable within three years. Off-balance-sheet is another matter: the financing platform built for AI customers, size undisclosed, which BofA estimates could accumulate USD 370 billion of senior debt by mid-2029. That sentence is the only item in the entire report that cannot be quantified yet could change the conclusion.

## Chain the four quarters together: this is acceleration, not a step change

The quarterly AI revenue trajectory can be reconstructed: roughly RMB 8.76 billion in FY26Q1, RMB 10.84 billion in Q2, RMB 16.7 billion in Q3, and guidance of RMB 21.7 billion for Q4 (Q1 and Q2 back-solved from full-year guidance of RMB 58 billion). Sequential growth was +24%, +54% and +30%, respectively.

These three numbers carry more information than the +221% YoY figure. YoY compares against last year's low base; sequential shows current acceleration or deceleration. Q3's +54% is an outlier, reflecting Google TPU v7 volume shipments plus Anthropic's initial deployment; Q4 falling back to +30% signals the high base has arrived.

FY26 full-year RMB 58 billion and FY27 guidance of RMB 115 billion imply growth of +98%. That number needs unpacking: if Q4 annualises to RMB 86.8 billion, then FY27's RMB 115 billion means a further 32% for the full year—not a doubling, but sustaining Q4's run rate for four quarters. So "doubling" is a YoY measure, not a growth-rate measure. The market is sensitive to this distinction, which is why the stock did not rise after guidance.

## Three items that are not on the income statement

Three items in Broadcom's financials have to be filled in yourself, or you will misprice the stock in either direction.

First, stock-based compensation. It sits inside the USD 3.27 billion gap between Non-GAAP and GAAP, bundled with acquisition amortization, and the company does not break it out. Stock-based compensation across semis typically runs 3%–5% of revenue; Broadcom's ratio is lower given its lean headcount, but the absolute dollar amount still runs into the billions. It is a real cost, it simply does not show up as cash.

Second, acquisition amortization. Intangibles from VMware, CA and Symantec are still being amortized at several billion dollars a year. It does not touch cash, but it does hit book profit and net assets—one reason the 60% ROE looks absurd (the denominator is suppressed).

Third, off-balance-sheet guarantees. This item does not even get a footnote. Accounting-wise it is not a liability; a provision is recognized only when a loss is "probable"—and where "probable" sits is management's judgment.In other words, when this risk hits the financial statements depends on when management decides it is a problem. Together, these three items are the distance between Non-GAAP profit and true economic profit.

## Guidance and the three things to watch most closely

Q4 revenue guidance USD 34.8 billion, 1% below consensus of USD 35.03 billion; AI semiconductors USD 21.7 billion, +236% YoY; gross margin 73%. FY26 AI revenue guidance raised from USD 56 billion to USD 58 billion; FY27 guided at USD 115 billion.

Three things to check every quarter: - Whether gross margin can stop falling near 73%. XPU mix will keep rising; a break below 70% would signal the hardware business is eating the software business's profit; - Whether the FY27 USD 115 billion is "supply locked in" or "contracts signed." The former is capacity planning; only the latter is a revenue commitment; - Any disclosure on the financing platform — guarantee size, tenor, recourse. The appearance of any one of these three terms forces a repricing of the stock.
