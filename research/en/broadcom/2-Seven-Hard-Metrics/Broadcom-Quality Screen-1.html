Quality Screen — Broadcom

Core view

Six of the seven hard metrics pass, and pass easily: ROE around 60%, ROIC around 42%, Non-GAAP gross margin 75%, net margin 55.3%, interest coverage 25x and free cash flow yield 3.1%. For a manufacturer, these numbers are outliers.

Cash conversion is the sole failure: single-quarter free cash flow of USD 13.67 billion against Non-GAAP net profit of USD 16.372 billion, a ratio of 0.83. The miss reflects not a deteriorating business but the hardware model's natural cash absorption during expansion—AI revenue up 54% quarter on quarter, with receivables and inventory swelling in tandem. Worth watching, but not fatal.

Six solid points, no room for debate

ROE of 60% and ROIC of 42% need explaining, because they are so high they look like a calculation error. Years of buybacks and amortisation of VMware's intangible assets have compressed Broadcom's book equity, and a small denominator produces a high return. ROIC is what really matters: invested capital of about USD 150 billion (equity 105 billion + debt 59.6 billion − cash), after-tax operating profit of about 62.5 billion, a 42% return. Even if the denominator were doubled, the return would still be above 20%; the capital efficiency of this business is real.

Gross margin of 75.0% (Non-GAAP) and net margin of 55.3% are far above TSMC's 58%. Not because Broadcom is more irreplaceable than TSMC, but because it sells design services and IP and carries no fab depreciation. On the same revenue, Broadcom's incremental capex is only a single-digit number of hundred million dollars, while TSMC needs tens of billions.

Interest coverage of 25x shows that the USD 59.6 billion of fixed-rate debt is not a problem. The market's worry is the off-balance-sheet portion, not this 59.6 billion.

Gross margin: verdict "pass", trend "warning"

A 75% versus 40% threshold looks like no contest. But lay out four quarters: FY25Q4 78%, FY26Q2 77.1%, FY26Q3 75.0%, FY26Q4 guidance 73%. That is a 5 percentage point drop in a year, one-directional and accelerating.

Management gives the reason plainly: XPU hardware is a rising share, and HBM is a rising share of cost. Translation: selling chips earns less than selling IP, and selling fully built systems with memory earns even less than selling chips. The more successful custom ASIC becomes, the lower the gross margin. This is the most counterintuitive thing about Broadcom's business: growth and margin move in opposite directions.

Is 73% the bottom? We lean toward another 1-2 points of downside, because XPU will only take a larger share of the USD 115 billion in FY27 AI revenue. A break below 70% would no longer be just a mix issue — it would mean pricing power in this business is weakening.

The only one that fails: cash conversion of 0.83x

Single-quarter free cash flow of USD 13.67 billion looks impressive and marks a company high, but Non-GAAP net profit for the same period was USD 16.372 billion, implying cash conversion of only 83%. Where did the missing USD 2.7 billion go? Mainly receivables and inventory. AI revenue rose 54% QoQ, so a large portion of revenue recognised this quarter has not yet been collected, while materials were stockpiled in advance for next quarter's USD 21.7 billion of AI shipments.

This is normal during expansion—even healthy: you only dare build inventory when orders are in hand. But it carries two implications: first, the cash quality of revenue is lower than the income statement suggests; second, if demand suddenly reverses, this inventory build becomes an impairment.

Why set the threshold at 1.0x? If a company's cash conversion stays below 1 over the long term, its profit needs to be discounted. Broadcom has mostly been around 1.0 over the past five years; this quarter's drop to 0.83 is characteristic of a cyclical peak, not structural deterioration. So it fails the test, but it is not fatal.

Three things the metrics don't show

First, customer concentration. Six XPU customers underpin USD 16.7 billion of quarterly AI revenue, and if any one of them cuts orders it leaves a hole in the financials. None of the seven metrics captures this risk.

Second, off-balance-sheet guarantees. The credit support extended to finance AI customers appears in none of the metrics. BofA estimates the platform could carry USD 370 billion of senior debt by mid-2029—if part of that needs Broadcom to backstop it, ROE and interest coverage would distort overnight.

Third, stock-based compensation and amortisation. GAAP net income was USD 13.1 billion versus Non-GAAP USD 16.372 billion, a gap of USD 3.27 billion spanning both amortisation of acquisition-related intangibles and stock-based compensation.The 55% net margin calculated on a Non-GAAP basis is the company's best-looking face. Valuing on a Non-GAAP basis is industry practice, but you need to know which basis you are using.

Conclusion

Six of seven criteria pass, pointing to a first-class company rather than a flawless one. The sole miss—cash conversion of 0.83x—is typical of an expansion phase and acceptable. The real caution flags are the one-way decline in gross margin (78%→73%) and the off-balance-sheet guarantees that none of the seven metrics capture. As a threshold, this scorecard supports "worth researching," not "buy now"—because the most dangerous item is not on the list.
