Core view

Condense the previous seven pieces into one decision table: do not buy at the current price; build a position below USD 300; upgrade after the financing structure is disclosed; exit if gross margin falls below 70% or the guarantees come back on balance sheet.

The value of a framework lies not in prediction, but in giving you an emotion-independent reference when emotions run high.

Step one: first determine who you are

The same stock calls for different actions from different investors.

If you are a long-term value investor focused on cash flow five years out, the question now is whether paying 18x for a company with a 25% five-year CAGR is worth it. The answer hinges on the floor for gross margin and the scale of the guarantee—neither has an answer, so wait.

If you are an event-driven investor focused on the December 9 earnings date, the move now is to wait for the report and hold no position. Three reactions are possible after the report, each pointing to a different direction.

If you are a trend investor, the stock now sits in the lower half of its 52-week range (355 versus 293–481), the trend is unclear, and you should not participate. The first step in the framework is not calculating the price, but confirming which category you belong to.

Step two: define "cheap" clearly

USD 355 is 18.3x FY27 and 27.1x FY26. Within AI semiconductors, that is on the cheap end; within Broadcom's own history, it is upper-middle (12–15x in 2018–2023).

So "cheap" has two reference frames: cross-sectionally versus peers, it is cheap; longitudinally versus itself, it is not cheap. Which frame you use depends on whether you see it as an "AI company" or a "cash-flow company."

My judgment: it is now a hybrid of "AI hardware company + software annuity," with a fair multiple of 18–22x. By that standard, the current price sits at the lower edge of the fair range, with no margin of safety.

The genuinely cheap levels are USD 300 (15.5x) and USD 280 (14.4x). Those two prices correspond to "the pessimistic scenario being priced at a discount," meaning the market starts worrying about the worst case.

Step 3: hard-code the sell signal

Buying can be vague; selling must be precise. Four sell signals:

  • FY27 AI revenue guidance cut by more than 15%, indicating customers are cutting orders;
  • Non-GAAP gross margin below 70% for two consecutive quarters, indicating loss of pricing power;
  • Impairment or reconsolidation of financing guarantees, indicating the worst case has begun;
  • Google or Meta publicly confirms its next-generation project has been awarded to a second supplier, indicating share loss.

None of these four depends on the share price, only on public information.Their advantage: they will not trigger falsely on market panic, nor fail during market euphoria.

One soft signal: if Hock Tan starts explaining gross margin decline with "structural" and "long-term trends" on earnings calls instead of giving specific numbers, he himself lacks conviction.

Step 4: Timeline

Key milestones over the next 12 months: FY26Q4 results on 9 December 2026, cloud capex guidance in January 2027, FY27Q1 results in March, FY27Q2 results in June, FY27Q3 results in September.

Three things to watch at each milestone: AI revenue, gross margin, financing disclosures. All three pass: upgrade. Two pass: keep watching. One passes: downgrade.

This framework is valid for 12 months. After 12 months, redo it regardless of whether the conclusions were right or wrong—because in AI, one year equals three years in any other industry.

Conclusion

Decision framework conclusion: watch at the current price (0%), build a position below USD 300 (3%), fill out below 280 (5%), upgrade if disclosure becomes clear. Four sell signals, all based on public information, not on the share price.The point of this framework is not to predict direction — it is so that on December 9 you do not have to make the decision on the spot.

The framework's invalidation conditions must also be spelled out: if the technology route for AI compute changes fundamentally (optical computing, compute-in-memory reaching large-scale commercial use), or if Broadcom's customer base goes from six to twenty, every assumption in this framework has to be rebuilt.The framework is written for the current business, not for the three words "Broadcom".
