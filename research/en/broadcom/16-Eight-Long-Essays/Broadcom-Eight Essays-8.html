Core view

Distil the previous seven pieces into a single decision table: do not buy at the current price; build a position below USD 300; upgrade once the financing structure is disclosed; exit if gross margin falls below 70% or the guarantees return to the balance sheet.

A framework earns its keep not by predicting, but by giving you an emotion-independent reference when emotions run high.

Step one: first determine who you are

The same stock warrants different actions from different investors.

For long-term value investors focused on cash flow five years out, the question is whether 18x is worth paying for a company with a 25% five-year CAGR. The answer hinges on the gross margin floor and the scale of the guarantee—neither is resolved, so wait.

For event-driven investors focused on the December 9 earnings date, the move is to wait for the report and hold no position. Three reactions are possible post-report, each pointing in a different direction.

For trend investors, the stock now sits in the lower half of its 52-week range (355 versus 293–481), the trend is unclear, and you should not participate. The first step in the framework is not calculating the price, but confirming which category you belong to.

Step two: define "cheap" clearly

At USD 355 the stock trades at 18.3x FY27 and 27.1x FY26. That is cheap versus AI semiconductor peers but upper-middle versus Broadcom's own history (12–15x in 2018–2023).

So "cheap" depends on the reference frame: cross-sectionally versus peers it is cheap; longitudinally versus itself it is not. Which frame applies hinges on whether you treat it as an "AI company" or a "cash-flow company."

My view: it is now a hybrid of "AI hardware company + software annuity," deserving 18–22x. On that basis the current price sits at the lower edge of fair value, with no margin of safety.

The genuinely cheap levels are USD 300 (15.5x) and USD 280 (14.4x), where the pessimistic scenario is priced at a discount — i.e. the market begins to worry about the worst case.

Step 3: hard-code the sell signal

Buying can be vague; selling must be precise. Four sell signals:

  • FY27 AI revenue guidance cut by more than 15%, a sign customers are cutting orders;
  • Non-GAAP gross margin below 70% for two consecutive quarters, a sign of lost pricing power;
  • Impairment or reconsolidation of financing guarantees, a sign the worst case has begun;
  • Google or Meta publicly confirms its next-generation project has gone to a second supplier, a sign of share loss.

None of the four depends on the share price, only on public information.That is their advantage: they will not trigger falsely on market panic, nor fail during market euphoria.

One soft signal: if Hock Tan starts explaining gross margin decline with "structural" and "long-term trends" on earnings calls instead of giving specific numbers, he himself lacks conviction.

Step 4: Timeline

Key milestones over the next 12 months: FY26Q4 results on 9 December 2026, cloud capex guidance in January 2027, FY27Q1 results in March, FY27Q2 results in June, FY27Q3 results in September.

Three things to watch at each milestone: AI revenue, gross margin, financing disclosures. All three pass: upgrade. Two pass: keep watching. One passes: downgrade.

This framework holds for 12 months. After that, redo it regardless of whether the conclusions were right or wrong—because in AI, one year equals three years in any other industry.

Conclusion

Decision framework: watch at the current price (0%), build below USD 300 (3%), add below 280 (5%), upgrade if disclosure improves. Four sell signals, all based on public information rather than the share price. The framework is not a call on direction — it exists so that on December 9 you are not making the decision on the spot.

The invalidation conditions also need spelling out: a fundamental shift in the technology route for AI compute (optical computing or compute-in-memory reaching large-scale commercial use), or Broadcom's customer base widening from six to twenty, would require every assumption here to be rebuilt. The framework is written for the current business, not for the three words "Broadcom".
