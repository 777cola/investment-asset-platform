② Business essence: three customer types, three cash-flow streams, one valuation challenge — Broadcom

Core thesis

Broadcom runs three businesses with entirely different cash-flow profiles: software is an annuity (predictable, high margin, low growth), AI chips are projects (lumpy, low margin, highly concentrated), and non-AI chips are rent (stable, mid-margin, zero growth).

Applying a single multiple to all three businesses combined is the root of how this stock gets misread. Value them separately and the answer becomes far clearer.

Software: an annuity being repriced

VMware, CA and Symantec: three software assets with FY26 revenue of about USD 35 billion (rough estimate) and a 90% gross margin. The essence of this business is "the stuff you can't rip out of the enterprise data center": the virtualization layer, identity management, backup.

In 2024, Hock Tan switched perpetual licenses to subscriptions. Customers complained loudly, but revenue rose from USD 21.5 billion to USD 35 billion. Why the increase? Because replacing VMware means rebuilding the entire IT architecture, so the customer's migration cost far exceeds the price increase.

The risk in this business is not competition; it is customers bypassing it with "cloud native." New companies containerize from day one and do not need a virtualization layer. So software growth will gradually slow from 30% to 10%, but cash flow will be very stable. A valuation of 20-25x, and no higher.

AI chips: a project business delivered in GW

The USD 16.7 billion in AI semiconductor revenue is essentially project-based: the customer says "I want 1GW of compute," and Broadcom designs the chip, locks in TSMC capacity, buys HBM, packages and delivers.

Project-based revenue has lumpy recognition, gross margins that swing with the hardware mix, and high customer concentration. Cash flow is less predictable than software, but default risk is low because the customers are the six largest tech companies.

The most counterintuitive thing about this business: the more successful it is, the more the company looks like a manufacturer. Design fees fall as a share of revenue, wafer and memory costs rise, and gross margin drifts from 78% toward 70%. This is not a decline in competitiveness; it is a business model in drift.

Is the direction right? My call: right in the short term (scale buys share), uncertain in the long term (manufacturing businesses command a 12–15x valuation).

Non-AI chips: a forgotten toll-road business

The non-AI semiconductor business, at USD 4.14 billion, grew only 4% year on year, and the market largely ignores it. But it is a good business: broadband chips, Wi-Fi, FBAR filters, storage controllers, industrial chips, with customers spread across thousands of enterprises and gross margins around 65%.

This business acts as a stabilizer: when the AI cycle turns down, it does not collapse with it. Its problem is the absence of growth — these markets are already mature and share is stable, so all Broadcom can do is hold price.

We assign it 12x, because it is simply a mature manufacturing business.Many people ignore this segment when valuing Broadcom, or apply the AI business multiple to it, which overstates the value.

Breaking it down: where the USD 386 comes from

Run a sum-of-the-parts: Software USD 41 billion revenue × 90% gross margin − expenses ≈ USD 32.9 billion operating profit × 20x = USD 658 billion; AI semiconductors USD 115 billion × 58% gross margin − expenses ≈ USD 60.7 billion × 18x = USD 1,092.6 billion; non-AI USD 17.5 billion × 65% − expenses ≈ USD 7.4 billion × 12x = USD 88.8 billion.

The three parts total USD 1,839.4 billion; divided by 4.77 billion shares, that is USD 386 per share. At the current price of USD 355.14, that is an 8% discount.

This figure is almost identical to the USD 388 under the neutral scenario, and that is no coincidence—both methods point to "the market is pricing this roughly right." The difference: the sum-of-the-parts uses FY27 revenue assumptions, while the neutral scenario uses FY27 EPS assumptions; two paths, same destination.

Conclusion

Conclusion on the nature of the business: three businesses, three multiples; blending them leads to error. Software is an annuity, AI chips are projects, non-AI is rent. Valued separately: USD 386; current price USD 355; a 8% discount. This discount is not a margin of safety; it is the market pricing "gross margin decline + off-balance-sheet exposure." To buy, wait for the discount to widen beyond 20%, or for the uncertainty to clear.

The fragility of the sum-of-the-parts also needs stating: of the three assumption blocks, AI semiconductors is the most uncertain. Its 18x embeds the premise of "USD 115 billion revenue delivered"; if revenue reaches only 85 billion, this segment's valuation falls from USD 1,092.6 billion to about USD 830 billion, or USD 55 less per share. The USD 386 figure itself carries a 15% error band and cannot be used as a precise target price.
