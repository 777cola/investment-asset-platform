## Why is the cap 5%?

Two constraints. First, correlation. Broadcom's correlation with Nvidia, TSMC and Micron runs between 0.6 and 0.8 (rule of thumb); if the portfolio already holds these, Broadcom's marginal diversification benefit is weak, and adding it amounts to adding leverage.

Second, tail risk. The financing platform's exposure is uncapped, and a single USD 50 billion guarantee loss would wipe out a year of free cash flow. This risk cannot be soothed away as "low probability"; only position limits can backstop it. A 5% position going to zero inflicts 5 points of damage on the portfolio, which is bearable; a 20% position going to zero is a disaster.

If the portfolio holds no other AI exposure, the cap can be relaxed to 8% — but that is an asset-allocation question, not a question about this stock.

## Three price levels for adding, trimming and stopping out

Add: USD 320 (about 16.5x), USD 300 (15.5x), USD 280 (14.4x). Build 1–2 points at each level; do not buy the full position at once.

Trim: if FY27 AI guidance is cut by more than 15% after the position is built, halve unconditionally, regardless of price. If gross margin stays below 70% for two consecutive quarters, exit in full.

Stop-loss: no price stop, an event stop.A price stop makes no sense for this kind of stock—40% swings are normal, and a stop only shakes you out. An event stop does: guarantees brought back onto the balance sheet, a major customer shifting orders, a guidance cut—any one of these three and you leave.

The key to this rule set: the reason to buy is "clear structure or cheap price"; the reason to sell is "structure deteriorating", not "it fell".

## Correlation with other holdings

Broadcom is most correlated with Nvidia (around 0.8), as both draw on the same demand source—AI capex; correlation with TSMC is 0.7, reflecting its customer relationship; 0.6 with Micron, on HBM cost pass-through; and 0.5 with Microsoft, where cloud capex is the common variable.

Buying Broadcom is therefore not "diversification" but "betting on the same thing in a different way."If AI exposure in the portfolio already exceeds 20%, cap the Broadcom position below 3%, otherwise the entire portfolio becomes an AI thematic fund.

Correlation figures are empirical estimates (based on weekly returns from 2024–2026) without rigorous regression; actual correlations will shift with market conditions.

## Rebalancing discipline

Three rules. First, no single AI-related position exceeds 8% and total AI exposure stays below 20%. Second, if Broadcom trades above USD 450 after the position is established (about 23x FY27), trim it to 2%. Third, reassess after each quarterly earnings report and do not hold out of "habit".

The discipline sounds mechanical, but it guards against two things: adding to positions when sentiment runs high, and liquidating in a panic.A stock like Broadcom, with structural doubts but an inexpensive valuation, is the most prone to swinging between these two emotions.
