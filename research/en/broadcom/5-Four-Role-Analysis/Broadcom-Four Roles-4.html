Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Broadcom

Broadcom (AVGO) | Five risks, one unknowable

Core view

List every risk: customer concentration, financing guarantees, technology route, policy control, valuation. The first four can be quantified or at least described; the fifth is an output of valuation, not a cause.

Only one thing truly makes this investment uninsurable: the financing guarantee exposure cannot be quantified. The other four can all be compensated for through price.

Customer concentration: not a risk, but the definition of this business

Six customers underpin USD 16.7 billion of single-quarter AI revenue, 80% of semiconductor revenue. If any one of them cuts orders by 30%, Broadcom's AI revenue falls by more than 20%, and the market's 18x valuation instantly becomes 25x.

This risk cannot be diversified away, because it is the business model itself. What can be done is tracking: the customer deployment progress (GW figures) disclosed on each quarterly call, and customers' capex guidance.Anthropic from 1GW to 5GW, OpenAI to 1.3GW, Meta 3GW cumulative—if these figures are not revised up for two consecutive quarters, that is the alarm.

Financing guarantees: the only unpredictable variable

This is the only risk in the entire report that "cannot be calculated." The company provides credit support for AI customers' procurement, and the size of arrangements involving Apollo and Blackstone has not been disclosed; BofA estimates that by mid-2029, USD 370 billion in senior debt could accumulate.

Why is it more dangerous than customer concentration? Because customer concentration means "slightly less revenue," while guarantees mean "principal may be lost." The former affects the growth rate of the numerator; the latter affects the survival of the denominator. A USD 50 billion guarantee loss equals wiping out Broadcom's free cash flow for a year.

There are only two ways to handle it: wait for disclosure, or demand compensation through price. Li Lu's approach is "if you don't know, don't bet," not "if you don't know, bet less"—because a smaller position still goes to zero in a tail event.

Technology roadmap and policy: trackable, no need for excessive concern

The risk on the technology roadmap is Nvidia catching up in the inference market. ASIC's current cost advantage is "half"; if Nvidia prices inference-specific GPUs at only a 20% premium, customers' incentive to customize weakens. The tracking metrics are specific: cost per token and power consumption of GB-series inference cards, and whether Nvidia opens NVLink to third-party ASICs.

On policy risk, Broadcom's China revenue share is low, with exposure mainly in WiFi/RF and some networking chips; its XPU customers are all US companies. If export controls expand to "custom chips containing US IP," the impact would transmit through TSMC's capacity allocation.This risk is smaller in magnitude than customer concentration, but it is uncontrollable.

Valuation risk is the outcome: 18.3x FY27 is built on USD 19.38 EPS; if AI revenue reaches only USD 85 billion, EPS is USD 15, and the same 18x implies USD 270, down 24%.Of the four risks, only this one can be offset by price; the other three require time or disclosure.

Conclusion

Of the five risks, four can be compensated by price; one cannot. Customer concentration, technology roadmap, policy, valuation—below USD 300, all of these are bearable. Financing guarantees are not, because they have no cap.Conclusion: put Broadcom on the "wait for structural clarity" list, not the "wait for cheap" list.
