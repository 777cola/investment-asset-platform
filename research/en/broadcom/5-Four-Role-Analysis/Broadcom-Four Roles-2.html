Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — Broadcom

Broadcom (AVGO): strong P&L, stronger cash flow, and a balance sheet of real complexity

Core thesis

Free cash flow of USD 13.67 billion in the quarter, a Non-GAAP net margin of 55% and 25x interest coverage — on any composite, that is the financial quality Buffett would nod at.

Beyond the statements, however, sits a layer the reported figures do not capture: alongside USD 59.6 billion of on-balance-sheet debt, the company runs a financing platform built for AI customers. A company's value equals the present value of its future cash flows, and guarantees convert future cash flows into future liabilities.

Income statement: growth is real, but earnings quality needs unpacking

Revenue +86%, AI +221%, Non-GAAP net income +95%. The three figures corroborate each other: of the RMB 13.68 billion revenue increase, AI contributed RMB 11.5 billion and software RMB 2 billion — the sum ties out exactly.

Gross margin is the sole blemish: 75.0%, down 2.1pct QoQ, with Q4 guidance at 73%. Management attributes the decline to a rising mix of XPU hardware and higher HBM costs. This is structural, not cyclical — the more successful AI becomes, the lower the gross margin.

GAAP net income was RMB 13.1 billion, +216% YoY, far outpacing the +95% Non-GAAP growth, with the gap between the two at just RMB 3.27 billion. That gap is small by Broadcom's historical standards (M&A amortisation is the main component). A narrowing gap implies the amortisation burden is lightening, but may also include one-off items — the report does not break these out, so this is a flag only, not a conclusion.

Cash flow: a money printer, but whose money is it printing?

Quarterly free cash flow of USD 13.67 billion, or roughly USD 52 billion annualised, implies a 3.1% FCF yield on a USD 1.69 trillion market cap. Dividends over the same period annualise to USD 2.36 per share, about USD 11.3 billion, or 22% of free cash flow, with the balance going to debt repayment and buybacks.

One blemish on cash flow quality: FCF/net income of 0.83. The USD 2.7 billion gap sits in receivables and inventory — AI revenue rose 54% quarter on quarter, and both input stocking and payment terms are expanding. Working capital absorption during an expansion phase is normal, but if revenue stops growing quarter on quarter next quarter, that absorption reverses or turns into impairment.

The point Buffett would focus on: capex is only a single-digit number of billions of dollars. That is the benefit of a design company — the capacity belongs to TSMC, the depreciation belongs to TSMC. It is also the source of its 42% ROIC.

Balance sheet: RMB 59.6 billion is not scary; off-balance-sheet is

The table itself is simple: USD 59.6 billion of fixed-rate debt, very long maturities, 25x interest coverage. On Broadcom's cash flow, half that debt could be repaid in three years. On this table alone, Broadcom looks like a company with a clean balance sheet.

Off the table is another matter. The company provides credit support for AI customers' chip purchases, through arrangements with Apollo and Blackstone of undisclosed size; BofA estimates the platform could add USD 150 billion of new debt in 2027 and USD 370 billion of cumulative senior debt by mid-2029.

The nature of the arrangement needs to be clear: Broadcom is not borrowing; it is providing credit enhancement for customers' structured financing. If all goes well, Broadcom pays nothing and sells more chips; if customer deployments fall short of expectations, the guarantees return to the financial statements as "purchase commitments" or "residual value guarantees." The Buffett-style question is: in the worst case, how much additional capital do I need to put in? Right now there is no answer.

Conclusion

Top-tier financial quality, questionable underwriting capacity. The income statement and cash flow statement each score 9, as does the on-balance-sheet portion of the balance sheet; the off-balance-sheet portion cannot be scored. Buffett's move would be to put it on the watchlist and wait for one of two things: the price falls far enough to cover the worst case (below USD 250), or the guarantee terms are fully disclosed and are limited recourse.
