Core view

Four people argued for four hours, and consensus outweighed disagreement: a first-class business with a real moat, customer concentration is fate, and valuation is not expensive. Only one point divided them—whether off-balance-sheet financing exposure should be a reason not to buy.

The verdict: it should. It is the only unmeasurable variable, and the first principle of investing is not losing money.

Consensus: the quality of this business is not in dispute

At the business level the four are in near-total agreement. AI semiconductors USD 16.7 billion, +221% YoY, with XPU at 73%; seven generations of TPU built with Google; Tomahawk 6 covering nearly every XPU customer; Non-GAAP gross margin 75%, net margin 55%. Within the AI chain this is the position second only to Nvidia, and the only company selling both "compute" and "the roads between compute."

The disagreement is over what a good business is worth. Duan Yongping sees 18x as fair, because customers cannot switch away; Buffett sees it as not cheap enough, because the worst case has not been fully reckoned; Munger sees it as one to avoid, because the cycle position is wrong; Li Lu sees the direction as right but the timing as uncertain.

Divergence one: customer concentration — flaw or feature?

Duan Yongping and Munger see it as a flaw: six customers, and losing any one of them leaves a hole. Buffett and Li Lu see it as a feature: every frontier technology business starts with a handful of customers, and what matters is those customers' own credit.

Verdict: a feature, but hedge it with position sizing. Customer credit is genuinely high — all six rank among the world's top 20 companies by market cap — but their ability to pay depends on the capital markets, and that dependence undercuts the "high credit" label. So you can buy, just not heavily: cap any single position at 5%.

Disagreement 2: Does the off-balance-sheet guarantee argue against buying the stock?

This is the crux. Buffett's position is zero tolerance: a guarantee is a promise in good times and a liability in a downturn, and at a turning point you cannot exit. Li Lu's position: if it is unmeasurable, do not bet. Munger said outright that this is a standard feature of a cycle top.

Duan Yongping is more moderate: he would first ask "how much do I lose in the worst case," and if that cannot be calculated, he would not take a heavy position.

Verdict: this is a reason not to buy. Not because the risk is large, but because it is unmeasurable. Investing can bear a known bad outcome; it cannot bear an unknown one. This verdict directly determines that the rating is "Watch" rather than "Buy."

Divergence 3: Is 18x actually expensive?

18.3x FY27 and 27.1x FY26, versus Nvidia at around 30x and TSMC at around 20x — on this view Broadcom is among the cheapest names in AI semiconductors.

But all four agree on the caveat: it is cheap for a reason. The market pays 30x for Nvidia because it collects cash; it pays 18x for Broadcom because part of its order book is "financed on behalf of customers." This is not mispricing; it is risk pricing.

Verdict: valuation alone is neither a reason to buy nor a reason to sell. It only lowers the cost of waiting — on an 18x stock, waiting two quarters does not cost you much.

Execution plan

Bottom line: rated Watch, pending earnings confirmation; no position.

Three hard triggers: - Price below USD 300 (about 15.5x FY27): open a 3% position, with the price compensating for unquantifiable risk; - December 9 earnings clearly establish the financing platform as limited-recourse with quantifiable exposure: upgrade to Buy and open a 3% position; - Gross margin below 70% with the financing platform still undisclosed: downgrade to Avoid and stop tracking valuation.

Position cap 5%; combined AI exposure across Nvidia and TSMC no more than 20% of the portfolio.

Conclusion

Verdict: watch, do not build a position; position cap 5%. All four perspectives agree the business is first class; they diverge on unmeasurable off-balance-sheet risk. When risk cannot be measured, the only correct action is to wait—for the December 9 earnings report, or for a price below USD 300. Act if either trigger is met; if neither is, keep watching and do not chase.
