## The first three hurdles: a business where customers cannot switch away

Broadcom's revenue splits into four blocks, each with a different rhythm. AI semiconductors were USD 16.7 billion, 80% of semiconductor revenue and 56% of total revenue. Non-AI semiconductors were USD 4.14 billion, up only 4% YoY and essentially flat. Infrastructure software was USD 8.751 billion, up 30.1% YoY, propped up by VMware's shift to subscriptions.Only one leg of the company is running.

That leg is genuinely high quality. Custom chips take two years from architecture definition to tape-out, and once a customer picks a supplier, switching for the next generation is hard. Hock Tan said on the call that chips customised for specific large-model workloads can cost half as much as GPUs. That is the business logic of the whole ASIC trade — not stronger than GPUs, cheaper than GPUs.

The moat is not one but three stacked together. First, a decade of co-design with Google from TPU v1 through v7, a relationship no quote can dislodge. Second, in-house SerDes and advanced packaging IP, which determine whether chips can link compute together. Third, the Tomahawk family of Ethernet switch chips, global number one, with Tomahawk 6 covering almost every customer that co-develops XPUs.The three moats reinforce each other: whoever sells XPUs also sells switch chips, and whoever sells switch chips understands the customer's network topology better.

## Stage four: Hock Tan faced a multiple-choice question

Management scores 7 points here, and the deduction is not about capability. Since taking over Avago in 2006, Hock Tan has done deal after deal—LSI, Brocade, CA, Symantec's enterprise business, VMware—turning a maker of RF and storage controllers into a "chip + software" twin engine, while also fending off Broadcom's 2018 reverse acquisition that CFIUS blocked. It is one of the best capital-allocation records in semiconductors over the past fifteen years.

The deduction reflects a new choice: to make its products affordable for customers, Broadcom has begun arranging financing for them. Arrangements tied to Apollo and Blackstone provide credit support for AI chip purchases; specific terms were not disclosed. BofA estimates the platform could add USD 150 billion in new debt issuance in 2027 and USD 370 billion in cumulative senior debt by mid-2029.

This is not financial fraud, nor even aggressive accounting. It is an extension of the business model—translating "customers cannot afford it" into "I will lend you the money to buy it." The problem is that this money sits off Broadcom's financial statements, and once customer deployment falls short of expectations, the guarantees will return to the statements. As a shareholder, what you are buying is a chip order with an implicit guarantee attached.

## Level five: the arithmetic behind 18x is not hard; the hard part is the denominator

USD 355.14 equates to a USD 1.69 trillion market cap on roughly 4.77 billion shares, 18.3x FY27 consensus EPS of USD 19.38 and 27.1x FY26 EPS of USD 13.1 on this report's rough estimate. The multiple compressed from 27x to 18x within a year not because the stock fell 26% but because earnings expectations rose 48%.

At 18x, a company with AI revenue up 221% YoY and FY27 guidance of USD 115 billion is the cheapest tier in AI semiconductors. Nvidia trades at around 30x, more than half again as much as Broadcom. That gap is not the market erring; the market is pricing order quality—Nvidia collects cash, while part of Broadcom's orders are ones it helped finance itself.

Hence this gate scores 6 rather than 8: not because it is expensive, but because the numerator (EPS) is not clean enough. The neutral case of USD 388 implies just 9% upside; the bear case of USD 225 implies 37% downside. The payoff is roughly symmetric, but certainty is not.

## Gate six: what cannot be calculated cannot be papered over with "it should be fine"

The discipline rule requires the buy thesis to be written as falsifiable numbers. Two of Broadcom's three premises can be written down: FY27 AI revenue of USD 115 billion, and Non-GAAP gross margin of no less than 71%. The third cannot: not a single word has been disclosed on the financing platform's actual exposure, guarantee tenor or trigger conditions.

If it cannot be written down, it cannot be underwritten. This is not a question of "high risk" but of "unmeasurable risk." Risk you can understand can be bought at a discount; risk you cannot understand can only be waited on until it becomes understandable.

The FY26Q4 earnings report on 9 December is the window: if management is willing to explain the financing platform's structure clearly, the sixth gate can be lifted from 4 points to 6 and the rating can move from "Watch" to "Buy." If it stays vague, 18x is the valuation it deserves, not a discount.
