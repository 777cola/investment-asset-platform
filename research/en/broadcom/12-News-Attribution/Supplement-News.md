## The only informative one: the September 2 earnings report

Revenue USD 29.591 billion, +86% YoY; AI semiconductors USD 16.7 billion, +221% YoY — a beat. But Q4 guidance of USD 34.8 billion came in 1% below the consensus of USD 35.03 billion.

The stock reaction was telling: it fell after the close on September 2, closed at USD 357.16 on September 3, down 2.7%, rebounded slightly over the next two days to 357.9, then returned to 368.56 on September 8.**The market took three trading days to digest it, and the final verdict was "the results are fine, the guidance is a touch light."**

This move is a signal: it tells you not to worry about demand, but to worry about the size of the beat. An 18x stock depends on beating consensus every quarter; once it merely meets expectations, the valuation cannot hold.

## The second time carries information: the mid-September financing doubts

It fell 4.8% on September 14 and dropped to USD 339.27 on September 15, a three-month low. Over the same period, the media began questioning two things: AI chip orders surging while the stock kept sliding, and the opacity of the financing platform built for AI customers.

This time it is a real signal. It marks the market starting to price "off-balance-sheet exposure" — not by working it out, but by applying a discount first and asking questions later.**From USD 427.76 on August 7 to USD 339.27 on September 15, the stock fell 20.7%, at least half of which this issue contributed.**

The subsequent moves prove the point: it rebounded to 357.61 on September 18 and 364.54 on September 22, but never regained 380. The market accepted the results, but not the structure.

## Noise: five sector swings, no explanation needed

July 8 +4.8%, July 16 −5.0%, August 4 +6.6%, August 14 −5.9%, October 2 +3.35% — none of these five moves came with a company-level announcement.

What they share is large magnitude, short duration and synchrony with peers. The +6.6% on August 4 was a sector-wide rally driven by upward revisions to AI capex expectations; the −5.9% on August 14 was a giveback as the AI trade cooled. **The right way to handle this kind of move: don't trade, don't attribute, just record.**

One workable test: if a stock moves more than 4% in a single day with no announcement that day, and peers move more than 3% in the same direction, treat it as noise. Broadcom had five such moves over these three months.

## One rule of thumb: the three trading days after earnings decide the quarter

Broadcom's September 2 earnings report offers a reusable template. It fell after hours, dropped 2.7% the next day, rebounded on the third day, and returned to 368 a week later—**the market finished pricing it in three trading days, then traded in that range for a month.**

The operational takeaway: the three days after earnings are the densest information window, and their direction largely sets the stock's center of gravity for the quarter. If it falls back to its pre-earnings level within three days, the market is rejecting the results; if it recovers within three days, it was only sentiment.

Broadcom this time "recovered halfway": from 367 down to 357, back to 368, then down again to 339. The real pricing happened around mid-September on financing concerns, not on the earnings day itself.**For this stock, news about its financing structure matters more than the earnings numbers.**

## What the market is worried about, judging from the unusual moves

Lining up the seven unusual moves chronologically reveals a clear thread: from July to August the market traded "how strong is AI demand"; after September it traded "how high is the quality of AI orders."

The August 7 high of USD 427.76 reflected optimistic pricing of "FY27 AI revenue of USD 115 billion"; the September 15 level of USD 339.27 reflected cautious pricing of "73% gross margin + unclear financing exposure." **Same fundamentals, two prices — the difference is how many turns the market is willing to pay.**

Now at USD 355.14, 18.3x FY27. This level shows the market has completed the switch from "growth stock" to "cyclical growth stock." What comes next: either EPS beats consensus to hold the multiple up, or a clearer financing structure compresses the risk premium.
