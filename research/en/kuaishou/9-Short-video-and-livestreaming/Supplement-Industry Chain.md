## Who makes money in the chain: three layers split the pie

**The first layer is compute.** Video generation and recommendation algorithms both consume GPUs. Nvidia's gross margin exceeds 70%; cloud providers run 30%–40%. This layer's good times stem from scarcity, and scarcity has not eased in 2026—tight supply of memory and HBM has propped up prices across the entire AI infrastructure stack.

**The second layer is platforms.** Kuaishou's gross margin is 51.6%; Douyin's is higher. Platforms earn money by "allocating attention"—they need neither to produce content nor to fulfill orders, theoretically the best position to occupy. **But this layer is paying ever more for content**—revenue sharing has risen from 2025 levels to today, directly consuming 4.1pct of gross margin.

**The third layer is content and fulfillment.** Short-drama producers run 20%–30% gross margins, surviving on platform revenue sharing; express delivery and warehousing run 5%–10% gross margins, surviving on scale. Neither layer holds pricing power, but the short-drama layer briefly gained bargaining power in 2026 due to scarce supply.

## Kuaishou's position in the chain: in the middle, but both ends are moving

In one sentence: **Kuaishou is China's third-largest attention wholesaler, its customers are advertisers and its suppliers are creators.** Its position is better than JD.com's (no inventory to carry) and better than iQiyi's (no content rights to buy), but worse than Douyin's (lower-spending audience, weaker ad products).

Upstream changes work against it: creators demand ever-higher revenue shares (because Douyin and Hongguo are poaching them), and compute costs are rising on AI training. Downstream changes are also unfavorable: advertiser budgets are concentrating on platforms with higher ROI, and Kuaishou's ROI data trails Douyin's.

The one change in its favor is the iteration of content formats: short dramas, livestream e-commerce and AI-generated content each reshuffle creator supply once more.**Kuaishou has kept pace well in short dramas (supply up 5x in six months), but Hongguo has poached its talent with higher revenue shares.**

## Another shift in the chain: e-commerce is moving from livestreaming to shelf-based retail

Value distribution in livestream e-commerce is shifting toward the "shelf" side. In livestreaming, merchants pay hosts slot fees and commissions, a high-cost model; shelf e-commerce incurs only advertising and commission costs, a low-cost model. Douyin and Kuaishou are both channeling traffic from livestream rooms to their malls — an industry-wide move.

For Kuaishou, this shift cuts both ways: it lowers merchants' fulfillment costs and helps retain them, but livestream slot fees and tipping commissions are its high-margin revenue sources, and the shift to shelf-based commerce pushes the revenue mix toward lower-margin commissions. **This is one of the structural reasons behind "other services +18.5%, gross margin -4.1pct."**

## The only path upward: from distribution to tools

The platform has only one way out of being squeezed from both sides: move upstream and become a production tool. Kling is exactly that move—it turns Kuaishou from "helping you be seen" into "helping you produce."

The two have completely different economic structures: the marginal cost of distribution approaches zero, while the marginal cost of tools is inference compute; distribution's customers are advertisers (whose budgets can migrate), while tools' customers are enterprises (hard to switch once embedded in workflows). **If Kling succeeds, Kuaishou's gross margin structure will move from 51.6% to above 60%, because the cost of subscription revenue is mainly compute, which can be diluted by scale.**

But if it fails, Kuaishou is an advertising platform with a continued gross margin decline, and its valuation drifts toward that of a utility. This is the single most important fork in the road for the company right now.

## Cycle position: three cycles, two trending down

**Advertising cycle: bottoming out.** Macro consumption will be weak in 2026 and advertisers are cutting budgets, but this is characteristic of a cyclical trough—as consumption recovers in 2027, ad growth has room to rebound.

**Content cycle: the short-drama dividend is being carved up.**In the first half of 2026, short dramas were Kuaishou's most effective lever for time spent; in the second half, Hongguo pulled supply away with a free model plus higher revenue sharing. The peak of this cycle has passed.

**Technology cycle: early stage, and uncertain.**Demand for video generation is real, but the business model is not yet settled, and a price war has already begun (API unit prices fell sharply within a year).

With the three cycles overlapping, Kuaishou's position is: **the core business waits at the cyclical bottom for recovery, new businesses wait in the early cycle for a settled model.** Both kinds of waiting take time, and the share price gives no time.
