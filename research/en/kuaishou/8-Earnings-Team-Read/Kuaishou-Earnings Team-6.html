Kling cannot save Kuaishou (publishable version) — Kuaishou

One earnings report, one subtraction
Research date 2026/10/12  ·  Price HK$29.26 (-2.79%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 126.7 billion  ·  P/E (TTM) About 10.5x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Kuaishou reported 2026Q2 revenue of RMB 35.535 billion, up 1.4% YoY — a figure that has been cited in many reports as evidence of "resilience." One subtraction changes that conclusion: total revenue rose RMB 495 million YoY, of which Kling contributed RMB 567 million. Strip out Kling and Kuaishou's revenue was negative growth in aggregate across all businesses.

I. The true scale of the AI story: 2.4%

Kling generated RMB 850 million of revenue this quarter, up 200% YoY — the fastest commercialisation of any video-generation model globally. First-half revenue totalled more than RMB 1.5 billion, with 100 million users worldwide and nearly 50,000 enterprise customers; March ARR approached USD 500 million. In July, Beijing Kling completed a USD 3 billion capital raise at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion, diluting Kuaishou's stake by 16.67% to 83.3%.

All of this is real. But RMB 850 million is only 2.4% of total revenue. On an equity basis, Kling accounts for about HKD 116.3 billion of Kuaishou's value — 92% of its current HKD 126.7 billion market cap. That is both the most alluring part of the AI story and the most dangerous: either the private-market valuation is wrong, or the market believes the core business is a hard way to make money.

II. None of the three main business lines is trending up

Advertising came in at RMB 20.6 billion, +4.4%, below the Bloomberg consensus of 6.3% and trailing the overall China internet advertising market. Livestreaming was RMB 8.7 billion, -13.5%, contracting for a second consecutive year. E-commerce commission is now folded into "other services" and reported on a single line alongside Kling, and the company no longer discloses GMV.

More alarming is what the company itself says. On the August 18 earnings call, management explicitly guided that e-commerce marketing services and commission revenue would face YoY pressure in the second half.This is not an outside analyst's inference; it is the company's own guidance.

III. Profit: AI spend absorbs 30%, revenue-sharing costs absorb 40%

Gross margin of 51.6%, down 4.1pct YoY, gave up RMB 1.46 billion of gross profit, while R&D of RMB 4.6 billion, +34.7%, added RMB 1.185 billion of cost. Together the two items total RMB 2.64 billion, compressing adjusted net profit from RMB 5.614 billion to RMB 3.913 billion (-30.3%).

Had the R&D expense ratio held at last year's 9.7%, net profit would have fallen only 9.7%. In other words, two-thirds of the profit decline was management's own choice; one-third was forced by competition. The voluntary part can be stopped; the forced part cannot — stop it, and creators leave.

IV. Cheapness is static; becoming expensive is dynamic

A 10.5x P/E (TTM) looks cheap, and RMB 24.27 billion of net cash, at 21% of market cap, looks safe. But the denominator of that P/E is shrinking: 2026 adjusted net profit has been cut to RMB 8.99 billion, pushing the forward P/E back to 12.9x. A forward multiple richer than TTM is a textbook signal of earnings deterioration.

Three-scenario valuation: bear case HKD 16 (-45%), base case HKD 26.6 (-9%), bull case HKD 46 (+57%). Weighted 35/45/20, expected return is -8.4%. The upside requires three things to fall into place at once; the downside requires only one.

V. Conclusion: avoid, wait for two verifiable signals

Our Underweight rating rests not on the 65% share price decline but on the fact that the financials cannot verify the buy case. Kling's valuation anchor is its last funding round, not daily trading, while the core business is decelerating rather than bottoming.

A re-rating needs one of two things: online marketing growth back above 8% YoY on a quarterly basis, or Kling disclosing revenue and P&L on a comparable basis with annualised revenue above RMB 6 billion. Until then, a single quarter of negative advertising growth is enough to take the stock from 29 to 20 — that is not a black swan, it is the base case.

Conclusion

Kuaishou is not a bad company; it is a good company being displaced. Its 400 million DAU, its RMB 24.27 billion in cash and Kling's technology all remain. But investing does not pay for what "remains"; it pays only for what "gets better." This quarter's results offer no evidence of improvement.
