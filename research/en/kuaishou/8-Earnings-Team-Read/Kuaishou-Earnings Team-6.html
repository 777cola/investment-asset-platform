Kling cannot save Kuaishou (publishable version) — Kuaishou

One earnings report, one subtraction
Research date 2026/10/12  ·  Price HK$29.26 (-2.79%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 126.7 billion  ·  PE(TTM) About 10.5x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Kuaishou's revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was RMB 35.535 billion, up 1.4% year on year. That number made its way into many reports of "resilience." But one subtraction changes the conclusion: total revenue rose RMB 495 million year on year, of which Kling contributed RMB 567 million. Excluding Kling, Kuaishou's revenue across all businesses was negative growth in aggregate.

I. The true scale of the AI story: 2.4%

Kling generated RMB 850 million in revenue this quarter, up 200% year on year, the fastest commercialization in the global video-generation space. First-half cumulative revenue exceeded RMB 1.5 billion, with 100 million users worldwide and nearly 50,000 enterprise customers; March ARR approached USD 500 million. In July, Beijing Kling completed a USD 3 billion capital increase at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion, diluting Kuaishou by 16.67% to an 83.3% stake.

All of this is real. But RMB 850 million is only 2.4% of total revenue. On an equity basis, Kling contributes about HKD 116.3 billion of value to Kuaishou, equal to 92% of its current HKD 126.7 billion market cap—which is both the most alluring part of the AI story and the most dangerous: either the private-market valuation is wrong, or the market believes the core business is a hard way to make money.

II. None of the three main business lines is trending up

Advertising RMB 20.6 billion, +4.4%, below the Bloomberg consensus of 6.3% and below the overall China internet advertising market; livestreaming RMB 8.7 billion, -13.5%, contracting for a second consecutive year; e-commerce commission folded into "other services" and lumped together with Kling on a single line, and the company no longer discloses GMV.

More alarming is what the company itself says. On the August 18 earnings call, management explicitly stated that e-commerce marketing services and commission revenue would come under year-on-year pressure in the second half.This is not an outside analyst's inference; it is the company's own guidance.

III. Profit: AI spending eats 30%, revenue-sharing costs eat 40%

Gross margin of 51.6%, down 4.1pct YoY, gave up RMB 1.46 billion of gross profit; R&D of RMB 4.6 billion, +34.7%, cost an extra RMB 1.185 billion. Together, the two items total RMB 2.64 billion, compressing adjusted net profit from RMB 5.614 billion to RMB 3.913 billion (-30.3%).

Interestingly, had the R&D expense ratio held at last year's 9.7%, net profit would have fallen only 9.7%. In other words, two-thirds of the profit decline was management's own choice; one-third was forced by competition. The voluntary part can be stopped; the forced part cannot — stop it, and creators leave.

IV. Cheapness is static; becoming expensive is dynamic

A 10.5x TTM PE looks cheap, and RMB 24.27 billion of net cash at 21% of market cap looks safe. But the denominator of that PE is shrinking: 2026 adjusted net profit has been cut to RMB 8.99 billion, pushing forward PE back to 12.9x. A forward multiple richer than TTM is a textbook signal of earnings deterioration.

Three-scenario valuation: bear case HKD 16 (-45%), base case HKD 26.6 (-9%), bull case HKD 46 (+57%). Weighted 35/45/20, expected return is -8.4%. The upside requires three things to fall into place at once; the downside requires only one.

V. Conclusion: avoid, wait for two verifiable signals

We rate it Underweight not because the stock has fallen 65%, but because the buy case cannot be verified by the financials. Kling's valuation anchor sits in its last funding round, not in daily trading; the core business's growth is decelerating, not bottoming.

A re-rating requires seeing one of two things: online marketing quarterly growth back above 8%; or Kling disclosing revenue and P&L on a comparable basis, with annualized revenue exceeding RMB 6 billion. Until then, a single quarter of negative advertising growth is enough to take the price from 29 to 20 — that is not a black swan, that is the base case.

Conclusion

Kuaishou is not a bad company; it is a good company being displaced. Its 400 million DAU remains, its RMB 24.27 billion in cash remains, and Kling's technology remains. But investing does not pay for what "remains"; it pays only for what "gets better." This quarter's results offer no evidence of improvement.
