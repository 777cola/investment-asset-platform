Core view

The most valuable information in this earnings report sits not at the bottom of the income statement but on the first line: of RMB 35.535 billion in revenue, only RMB 850 million came from Kling, yet it accounted for nearly all of the incremental growth. A company's revenue structure says more about what business it is in than its profit does.

Incremental attribution: 4.95 - 5.67 = -0.7

The first thing I do with any set of financials is break down incremental revenue and identify who contributed it. Total revenue rose RMB 495 million, of which Keling added RMB 567 million and all other businesses combined contributed -RMB 70 million. This is not "slowing growth"; it is "the core business has stopped growing and is being masked by a single new business."

On the earnings call, management flagged pressure on e-commerce marketing and commissions in the second half — effectively a warning that next quarter's incremental revenue will also shrink. That raises the question: once Keling's incremental contribution slows too, what does total revenue look like?

The quality of the two revenue streams is entirely different.

Advertising revenue is traffic wholesaling: what is sold this quarter must be sold again next quarter, and clients can walk at any time. Kling is subscription plus usage-based pricing: clients have embedded the workflow, renewal rates are visible, and revenue compounds.

On business quality, Kling's segment is far superior to advertising. But it is only 2.4% of the total. Using a 2.4% good business to explain a 97.6% bad trend is the most easily misread aspect of this earnings report.

Unit economics: how much is a user worth

Each DAU generates RMB 50 a quarter from advertising, RMB 21 from livestreaming and RMB 15 from other, or RMB 86 in total—RMB 344 a year. That is low for short video, reflecting both the weak spending power of Kuaishou's user base and the fact that its advertising products have yet to fully monetise the traffic.

For investors the figure serves two purposes: first, to gauge the ceiling—if monetisation efficiency rises to the industry average, revenue still has 30%–40% of headroom; second, to gauge the floor—if users begin to churn, this figure falls first, being the most sensitive to advertisers' bids.

If we look at revenue alone, what P/S multiple should be applied?

Quarterly revenue of RMB 35.535 billion, or RMB 142.1 billion annualised, against a market cap of RMB 116 billion, puts the stock on just 0.82x PS — extremely low for an internet company, given A-share media names generally trade at 2–3x and Hong Kong-listed China ad platforms at 1.5–2x.

The discount is earned, not anomalous: net margin is only 11%, and falling. A 0.82x PS on an 11% net margin implies 7.5x P/E — the market is pricing in precisely the expectation of further margin compression. On PS alone, the stock is not cheap.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style conclusion: the revenue mix tells you this is a company being displaced, not one transforming. Transformation means the core business is still growing while new businesses scale; displacement means the core has stalled while new businesses remain too small. Kuaishou is the latter.
