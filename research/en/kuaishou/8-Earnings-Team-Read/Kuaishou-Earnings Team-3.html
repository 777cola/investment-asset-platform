Earnings Deep Dive (Munger) — Kuaishou

Gross margin fell 4.1 points. Who took the RMB 1.46 billion?
Research date 2026/10/12  ·  Price HK$29.26 (-2.79%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 126.7 billion  ·  P/E (TTM) About 10.5x

Core view

Gross margin fell from 55.7% to 51.6%, the steepest decline in four quarters. The RMB 1.46 billion went not to costs but to competition: revenue shares paid to streamers rose, and revenue shares paid to short-drama creators rose. Both outlays went toward "retaining supply."

Why economies of scale stopped working

In theory, a platform with 400 million DAU should enjoy economies of scale: more users means lower unit content cost and a higher advertising gross margin. Kuaishou went the other way this quarter — revenue +1.4%, gross margin -4.1pct.

The reason is that the marginal cost of content is no longer zero. In the short-video era the cost was server bandwidth, with marginal cost trending to zero; in the livestreaming and short-drama era it is people and copyright, and marginal cost does not fall. As the revenue mix shifts from "watching video" to "watching people perform, watching drama," economies of scale disappear.

Incentive mismatch

Performance reviews are tied to AI revenue share, so resources shift to Kling; the algorithm is measured on time spent, so content skews toward stimulation; livestreaming operations are measured on tipping volume, so revenue share tilts toward streamers. Each metric is reasonable in isolation; together they produce a steadily declining gross margin.

Munger would say: show me the incentives and I will tell you the outcome. Kuaishou's outcome is already written in its gross margin.

Two possibilities behind the RMB 4.6 billion R&D spend

The bull case is investment: Kling is the second curve, and this RMB 4.6 billion is the price of admission. The bear case is defence: ByteDance's Seedance forces Kuaishou to keep pace, and this RMB 4.6 billion is military spending. The difference is that investment pays back, while military spending only grows.

The test is simple: if the R&D expense ratio is still rising next year and Kling's revenue share is still below 3%, it is military spending.

The hardest cost to cut

Within the cost structure, bandwidth and servers can be optimised, marketing spend can be compressed (the expense ratio is already being squeezed) and headcount can be cut. The one line that cannot be cut is revenue sharing. It is not a cost — it is a contract. The platform signs long-term revenue-sharing agreements with guilds, creators and short-drama producers alike; cutting it means tearing up contracts, and tearing up contracts means supply attrition.

That is why the decline in gross margin has a ratchet effect: revenue shares that have gone up do not come back down. When competition eases and the platform moves to renegotiate the split, creators answer with "then I'll leave."

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: Gross margin is a line that does not lie. It shows the company paying ever more to retain supply, with no end in sight—until it can no longer afford to pay.
