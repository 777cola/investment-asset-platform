Earnings Deep Dive (Li Lu) — Kuaishou

400 million people are still there, but their time has been fragmented

Core view

User data is the most easily misread part of this earnings report. DAU of 412 million, +0.8%, looks stable; MAU of 797 million, +11.5%, looks like growth. Put the two together: DAU/MAU fell from 57.2% to 51.7%, meaning that of the 80 million new monthly active users, fewer than 3 million became daily active users.

Quality of user acquisition: bought versus retained

MAU grew 11.5% over the year, while DAU grew only 0.8%. There is only one explanation for this combination: a large share of the new users are low-frequency, coming from paid acquisition, the lite app, and pre-installed channels. They contributed to the MAU number, not to advertising value.

The bigger problem is cost: MAU rose by 80 million, yet sales expense did not rise proportionally (the expense ratio is still compressing), indicating these users were pulled in mainly through product mechanics (check-ins, coins, short dramas), not through brand. The retention curve for this type of user is steep; losing half within three months is the norm.

The battle over duration: not Kuaishou versus Douyin, but short video versus everything

What is racing against time in 2026 is not another short-video app, but short dramas, AI applications, games, and the shelves of livestream e-commerce. Hongguo has taken over "time-killing" with a completely different product form, and its revenue-sharing is more aggressive than Kuaishou's.

This means Kuaishou no longer faces competition over "whose recommendations are more accurate," but over "whose unit of time is cheaper." This is a cost war, and Kuaishou has no advantage on the cost side—its content procurement and revenue-sharing are both rising.

Reassessing user value in the AI era

If Kling truly drives content production costs close to zero, platform value shifts from "aggregating content" to "aggregating demand." At that point, 400 million DAU remains an asset, but the monetization model changes: not selling ad slots, but selling generation capability.

This path holds only if Kling stands on its own, rather than being folded into an advertising company's financials. The spin-off is the right call, but it stakes the parent company's long-term value on a minority stake.

Overseas: an underappreciated option

Kuaishou's overseas Kwai has a stable user base in Brazil and Indonesia, with low monetization but fast growth. This business has long been overshadowed by the domestic market and is not disclosed separately in financial reports.

For a Li Lu-style judgment, overseas is the only variable that could rewrite the story: in a growth market, the experience of 400 million DAU can be reused, and the competitor is not Douyin. But its scale is not yet enough to change the valuation and can only be treated as a free option.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: user scale is a stock of assets; user quality is the increment. Kuaishou's stock is solid; its increment is hollow. In the next round of media transition, companies like this are usually the ones being consolidated, not the consolidators.
