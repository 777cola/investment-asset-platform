Core view

Only one number matters in this earnings report: revenue +1.4%, gross profit -6.0%. Once that scissors gap appears, every number after it is a consequence — net profit -36%, adjusted net profit -30.3%, operating leverage working in reverse. What remains is the balance sheet: RMB 24.27 billion in net cash, and a buyback executed at HKD 45.

Income statement: gross profit first, then expenses

Revenue RMB 35.535 billion (+1.4%), gross margin 51.6% (55.7% last year), gross profit RMB 18.34 billion (-6.0%). Revenue rose RMB 490 million while gross profit fell RMB 1.18 billion—the entire RMB 1.67 billion gap comes from revenue-sharing costs. This is the first and most important entry point to understanding these results.

On the cost side: R&D RMB 4.6 billion (+34.7%, 12.9% of revenue), while sales and marketing and administrative expenses were broadly stable on the company's basis. The extra RMB 1.185 billion in R&D plus the RMB 1.18 billion shortfall in gross profit totals RMB 2.36 billion, which exactly explains why adjusted net profit, after falling from RMB 5.614 billion to RMB 3.913 billion (-RMB 1.7 billion), still remains positive—other expenses and the tax rate contributed positively.

At the operating profit level, adjusted EBITDA was RMB 7.1 billion, -7.7%, far better than the -30.3% decline in net profit. The difference lies in depreciation and amortization and share-based compensation, indicating that part of the profit decline is non-cash and the cash picture is not as grim.

Cash flow: only RMB 1.2 billion left between EBITDA and capex

Capex came in at RMB 5.9 billion, a sharp sequential pullback (the prior quarter carried higher compute investment). Adjusted EBITDA of RMB 7.1 billion less capex of RMB 5.9 billion leaves RMB 1.2 billion—a rough free cash flow figure that excludes taxes and working-capital movements; the actual number will be lower.

Read this figure with care: Kuaishou runs a prepaid model (advertisers pay in advance, livestreaming users top up first), so working capital typically contributes positive cash flow and true free cash flow may exceed RMB 1.2 billion. But the direction is clear: AI investment has squeezed once-ample cash flow to a tipping point. If capex returns above RMB 10 billion in 2027, free cash flow turns negative.

Balance sheet: net cash of RMB 24.27 billion, 21% of market cap

Cash assets of RMB 24.27 billion, with no major interest-bearing debt pressure. Spread over 4.33 billion shares, that is RMB 5.6 per share, about HKD 6.1, or 20.9% of the share price. Enterprise value = 1,267 - 265 = about HKD 100.2 billion. This structure gives the company time and gives shareholders a floor.

But "net cash" is often an illusion among Chinese companies listed in Hong Kong: part is trapped in domestic subsidiaries, part must be held as regulatory margin, and part offsets liabilities for supplier prepayments. What can actually be used for buybacks or dividends is free cash flow, not book cash.

Capital returns: HKD 880 million of buybacks, purchased at HKD 45

Q2 buyback of 19.60 million shares for HKD 880 million, average price HKD 44.9. At the current price of HKD 29.26, that block is worth HKD 573 million, a mark-to-market loss of HKD 307 million, or 35%.

Buffett has one hard rule on buybacks: repurchase only when the share price is below intrinsic value. At HKD 45, the stock traded at 20x 2026E net profit — that is not undervaluation. This track record says more about management's ability to price their own business than any interview ever could.

Conclusion

The conclusion from a financial perspective: a solid balance sheet, a deteriorating income statement, and a cash flow statement that has just reached a critical point. Together the three statements paint a picture of "a company that still has money but is using that money to buy time." Buying time is not the problem; the problem is what the time is used for—if it is used to buy back advertising growth, it is worth it; if it is used to funnel cash into Kling, that has to be assessed separately.
