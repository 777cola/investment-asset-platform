Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Kuaishou

First calculate how much you can lose, then calculate how much you can gain

Core view

Risk is not "the share price may fall" but "the probability of permanent loss of principal." Kuaishou trades at HKD 29.26, down 64.7% from its high and only 2.5% above its 52-week low; the market has already priced in a lot of bad news. But having fallen a lot does not mean it has bottomed; the possibility of permanent loss must be assessed item by item.

Risk 1: core business stalls and turns into a core business recession (high probability, medium impact)

One step separates +1.4% from negative growth: advertising fell from +4.4% to -2%. On the August 18 earnings call, management itself said e-commerce marketing services and commission revenue would come under pressure in the second half — this is not outside speculation. If revenue contracts in 2027, the valuation framework shifts from "PE" to "PS or EV/EBITDA," and that usually means a lower multiple.

Risk 2: AI investment yields no return (medium probability, high impact)

R&D RMB 4.6 billion, +34.7%; capex RMB 5.9 billion. Combined, the two exceed RMB 10 billion per quarter. Kling Q2 revenue was RMB 850 million; even counting all of it as incremental, the input-output ratio is 1:0.08. If Kling revenue reaches RMB 6 billion in 2027 and it is still loss-making, that RMB 10 billion of annual investment becomes pure margin erosion.

Risk 3: Distribution of benefits after the spin-off (medium probability, high impact but hard to quantify)

In July, Kling raised USD 3 billion at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion, with Kuaishou holding 83.3%. The spin-off makes valuation visible and financing independent; the drawback is that losses stay in the consolidated statements while the valuation sits outside minority interests. If Kling keeps losing money and the parent absorbs its share under the equity method, reported profit takes a second hit. This cannot be quantified now because the company has not disclosed Kling's standalone P&L.

Risk 4: Regulation (low probability, medium impact)

Three areas are tightening: protection of minors in livestream tipping, filing requirements for algorithmic recommendations, and labelling obligations for AI-generated content. For Kuaishou, regulation typically shows up as "higher compliance costs plus restrictions on certain monetisation methods" — not a fatal blow, but a persistent drag on margins.

Risk 5: Valuation anchor breaks (medium-high probability, high impact)

The current bull case rests on "Kling is worth USD 18 billion." Once that anchor is marked down to USD 8 billion in the next funding round, the parent's equity value falls from HKD 116.3 billion to HKD 51.7 billion, equivalent to 41% of market cap vanishing into thin air. A primary-market valuation markdown needs no reason—only a lead investor unwilling to take the paper.

Tail risk: three things happen at once

Ads turning negative + Kling's valuation halving + Hong Kong liquidity contracting. Each is unlikely on its own, but if all three hit at once, a share price below HKD 15 would be no surprise (5x 2027E net profit + net cash). This is not the base case, but it sets the position cap.

Conclusion

Conclusion from a risk perspective: Of the five risk categories, two (core business decline and a broken valuation anchor) are happening now, not hypothetical. The 65% decline only shows the market has priced in part of it in advance; it does not show the risks have cleared. Until the probability of permanent loss falls materially, the right move is to stay out.
