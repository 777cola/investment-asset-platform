Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — Kuaishou

First work out how much you can lose, then how much you can gain

Core view

Risk is not "the share price may fall" but "the probability of permanent loss of principal." At HKD 29.26, Kuaishou sits 64.7% below its high and just 2.5% above its 52-week low, so the market has already priced in a lot of bad news. But a big fall does not mean the stock has bottomed; the possibility of permanent loss has to be assessed item by item.

Risk 1: core business stalls, tipping into outright contraction (high probability, medium impact)

A single step separates +1.4% growth from contraction: advertising swung from +4.4% to -2%. On the August 18 earnings call, management itself flagged pressure on e-commerce marketing services and commission revenue in the second half — this is not outside speculation. Should revenue contract in 2027, the valuation framework shifts from P/E to P/S or EV/EBITDA, which typically means a lower multiple.

Risk 2: AI investment yields no return (medium probability, high impact)

R&D came in at RMB 4.6 billion, +34.7%, with capex of RMB 5.9 billion; together the two exceed RMB 10 billion a quarter. Kling generated RMB 850 million of revenue in Q2, so even counting all of it as incremental, the input-output ratio is 1:0.08. If Kling revenue reaches RMB 6 billion in 2027 and the business is still loss-making, that RMB 10 billion of annual investment becomes pure margin erosion.

Risk 3: Benefit distribution post-spin-off (medium probability, high impact but hard to quantify)

In July, Kling raised USD 3 billion at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion, with Kuaishou holding 83.3%. The spin-off makes the valuation visible and financing independent; the drawback is that losses stay in the consolidated statements while the valuation sits outside minority interests. If Kling keeps losing money and the parent absorbs its share under the equity method, reported profit takes a second hit. That cannot be quantified now, as the company has not disclosed Kling's standalone P&L.

Risk 4: Regulation (low probability, medium impact)

Three regulatory fronts are tightening: protection of minors around livestream tipping, filing requirements for algorithmic recommendations, and labelling obligations for AI-generated content. For Kuaishou, regulation typically manifests as higher compliance costs plus restrictions on certain monetisation methods — not a fatal blow, but a persistent drag on margins.

Risk 5: Valuation anchor breaks (medium-high probability, high impact)

The bull case rests on "Kling is worth USD 18 billion." Mark that anchor down to USD 8 billion in the next funding round and the parent's equity value drops from HKD 116.3 billion to HKD 51.7 billion—41% of market cap vanishing into thin air. A primary-market markdown needs no reason, only a lead investor unwilling to take the paper.

Tail risk: three things happen at once

Negative ad growth, a halving of Kling's valuation and tightening Hong Kong liquidity: each is unlikely on its own, but should all three land together, a share price below HKD 15 would surprise no one (5x 2027E net profit plus net cash). Not our base case, but it caps position size.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: two of the five risk categories — core business decline and a broken valuation anchor — are live today, not hypothetical. The 65% decline shows only that the market has pre-priced part of this; it does not show the risks have cleared. Until the probability of permanent loss falls materially, the right call is to stay out.
