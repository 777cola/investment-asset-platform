Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — Kuaishou

Ask first what the user got, then ask what the company earned.

Core view

Kuaishou sells three things: cheap entertainment for users, a stage where creators can monetize, and a pool of affordable audiences for merchants. All three were scarce in 2018; in 2026 they are not. The skeleton of the business model is intact; what has broken is the pricing power at every layer — user time is split among four rivals, creators are poached by higher revenue shares, and merchants are taken by more precise platforms.

User value: 400 million people come every day — for what

Kuaishou's user profile is clear: lower-tier cities, over 30, price-sensitive, weak social ties. They do not come to Kuaishou for polished content; they come to see "people like me." The industry calls this the laotie economy, which in essence is substituting authenticity for production quality—a gap Douyin left unaddressed early on and later filled with its "rural track."

That value is real. 412 million DAU and average daily time spent in the two-hour range show it occupies users' idlest hours. But "authenticity" as a selling point has a fatal weakness: it requires no exclusivity. The same person can scroll Kuaishou, Douyin and WeChat Channels simultaneously, at zero switching cost. Users have not left Kuaishou; they just no longer come only to Kuaishou.

Three-layer monetization: advertising, livestreaming, commissions — where does each ceiling lie?

Advertising RMB 20.6 billion, 58% — the main engine, and the segment most sensitive to merchant ROI. Kuaishou's problem is not insufficient traffic (400 million DAU is enough for any advertiser); it is low consumer spending power among its users, so the same impressions cannot command Douyin's prices. Q2 +4.4% shows advertisers put Kuaishou at the back of the queue when adding budget.

Livestreaming RMB 8.7 billion, -13.5% — contracting for two consecutive years. The reason is simple: tipping is discretionary spending and the first thing cut in a downturn; meanwhile Douyin's livestream tipping and WeChat Channels' concert livestreams pull away top streamers. This business has hit its ceiling; what remains is only the pace of decline.

Other services RMB 6.2 billion, +18.5% — this houses two things: e-commerce commissions and Kling. It is the only part still growing, and the only exciting part of this earnings report. But it accounts for just 17.5%.

Unit economics: what a user is worth per quarter

206 ÷ 4.12 = RMB 50 per DAU per quarter in advertising; 87 ÷ 4.12 = RMB 21 in livestreaming; 62 ÷ 4.12 = RMB 15 in other. Total RMB 86/DAU/quarter, about RMB 344 a year. Against Douyin (industry estimates put ad revenue per user above RMB 500 a year), Kuaishou's monetization efficiency is roughly 60-70% of Douyin's.

Half of that gap comes from the spending power of the user base, half from advertising product capability. The former can't be changed; the latter can — but it takes a team still fighting, and Kuaishou's R&D resources are moving to Kling.

Is Kling the same business?

No. Advertising is a traffic wholesale business; Kling is a tool-subscription business. The former sells on impressions, with millions of merchants as customers; the latter sells on generated seconds, with tens of thousands of content teams and enterprises as customers. The two differ entirely in sales organization, gross-margin structure and competitive moat.

On business quality, Kling is better: subscription revenue is predictable, gross margin rises as inference costs fall, and customer switching costs are high (workflows get embedded). But its current scale is RMB 850 million per quarter, just 2.4% of the core business, and it is still burning cash.Using a 2.4% new business to save a 97.6% old business does not hold up logically.

Conclusion

Conclusion from a business perspective: Kuaishou's core business is a good business going bad, not a bad business. It once had pricing power (advertising growth of 50%+ in 2020–2021) and lost it to competition and consumer spending power. Kling is a separate business, worth valuing on its own, but it cannot be used to value the core business.
