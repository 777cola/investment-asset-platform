TeamLead summary — Kuaishou

Points of divergence across four independent perspectives, and the verdict

Core view

The four roles worked independently and never saw each other's drafts. Their conclusions converged strongly (none would buy), but the reasoning sat at different levels: the business role assessed business quality, the financial role the quality of the numbers, the industry role positioning, the risk role probabilities. A shared conclusion is worth less than the points of disagreement—so the three genuine disagreements are set out below and adjudicated.

Independent conclusions for the four roles

Business (Duan Yongping): The core business is a good business going bad. Kling is a separate business, and a 2.4% new business cannot be used to value the 97.6% old business. Financials (Buffett): A solid balance sheet, a deteriorating income statement and cash flow at a critical point; the buyback price shows management cannot price the stock. Industry (Munger): In a mature market, the third-place player is fated to lose share and to a utility-like valuation. Risk (Li Lu): Two of the five risk categories are materializing, and the probability of permanent loss has not fallen.

The four score it 4, 5, 4 and 3 respectively (out of 10), averaging 4.0, below our 6.0 threshold for building a position.

Disagreement one: is Kling really a "real business"

The bull case: it counts. RMB 850 million a quarter, +200%, USD 500 million ARR, 50,000 enterprise customers and 100 million global users — real paid demand, not subsidies. The bear case: it does not. No disclosed P&L, no disclosed retention, no disclosed ARPU, and USD 18 billion is 36x PS — at that multiple you are buying imagination.

Verdict: both are right, on different time scales. Kling is a real business today, with revenue and customers; whether it is a good business tomorrow — profitable, with a moat — cannot be verified. We therefore weight it at 0 in our base case and apply only a 50% haircut in the bull case: not a rejection of Kling, but a refusal to pay for the unverifiable part.

Disagreement two: is 10.5x PE cheap or not

The financial role says it is cheap: net cash is 21% of market cap and EV/2026E is only 10.2x, so it cannot fall further. The industry role says it is not cheap: the denominator is shrinking, with 2026E cut from RMB 12.0 billion at the start of the year to RMB 8.99 billion, and the forward 12.9x sits above the TTM 10.5x.

Verdict: the industry role wins. The test of cheapness is not the level of the multiple but whether it matches the direction of growth. A 13x forward P/E on a company with -30% profit growth assumes the decline stops next year—an assumption with no supporting evidence. On 2027 net profit of RMB 7.0 billion, the same share price implies 16.6x.

Divergence 3: whether stable DAU constitutes a moat remains unresolved

The bull case: it counts. DAU of 412 million, +0.8% — in a mature market, holding flat is a skill in itself. The bear case: it doesn't. MAU +11.5% bought only DAU +0.8%, and DAU/MAU fell from 57.2% to 51.7%, implying the new users are low quality.

Verdict: the bear case is partly right. Stable DAU does show core users have not left, and that is where Kuaishou beats Weibo and Bilibili; but a 5.5pp drop in DAU/MAU means the MAU metric is losing meaning, and the company's MAU-led user-acquisition narrative is misleading. The moat remains — it just no longer widens.

If one had to name a buy price

Splitting the difference between the HKD 16 bear case and the HKD 26.6 neutral case gives HKD 20 as the level at which "serious research can begin." That implies 7.3x 2027E adjusted net profit plus net cash, equivalent to the market pricing Kling at zero and the core business as a declining asset—at which point any positive progress at Kling is a free option.

But the move from 29.26 to 20 needs only one quarter of negative advertising growth; no black swan required. "Waiting for HKD 20" is therefore not discipline but luck. True discipline is setting conditions, not prices: advertising growth back to 8%, or Kling disclosing its P&L.

Final ruling

Synthesizing the four parties: rating "Avoid", no upper target price, only a neutral value anchor of HKD 26.6. Reasons ranked by weight: ① core business growth of 1.4% and profit of -30.3%, with management itself guiding to second-half pressure (40% weight); ② Kling's valuation is unverifiable and accounts for only 2.4% (30%); ③ forward P/E of 12.9x is not cheap (20%); ④ management's capital allocation record is poor (10%).

The two conditions that would trigger a re-rating match the six gates: online marketing growth returning above 8%, or Kling disclosing profit and loss on a comparable basis with annualized revenue exceeding RMB 6 billion. Until then, no price constitutes a reason to buy—because what is missing is not price, but verifiability.

Conclusion

Four-party consensus: pass. All disagreement centres on how much Kling is worth, and that answer sits not in the financial statements but in the next primary-market funding round.Building a valuation on a variable we cannot verify ourselves is the sole reason this report declines to follow.
