# Kuaishou（01024）— Six-Gate Screen Supplement

- Research date：2026/10/12
- Price：29.26 HKD
- Market cap：About HKD 126.7 billion

## Notes

The six-gate score follows the AI Berkshire six-gate framework (circle of competence / good business / moat / management / margin of safety / discipline), with 10 points per gate. Revenue and profit data are taken from Kuaishou's 2026Q2 and interim results announcement (2026/08/18): total revenue RMB 35.535 billion, online marketing RMB 20.6 billion, live streaming RMB 8.7 billion, other services RMB 6.2 billion, Kling RMB 850 million, gross margin 51.6%, R&D RMB 4.6 billion, adjusted net profit RMB 3.913 billion, DAU 412 million, MAU 797 million, cash-like assets RMB 24.27 billion, Q2 buyback HKD 880 million / 19.6 million shares. 2025Q2 line items are back-solved from the year-on-year growth rates disclosed in the announcement; the 57.2% DAU/MAU ratio and 80 million net new MAU per quarter are back-solved from MAU +11.5% YoY. Kling equity value HKD 116.3 billion = USD 18 billion × 83.3% × 7.1 (USD/CNY) × 1.092 (HKD/CNY), a rough calculation. The 10.5x PE is on the working-paper TTM basis; forward 12.9x = RMB 116 billion ÷ RMB 8.99 billion.

> Scoring: 10 points per checkpoint, out of 60. Financial data from Kuaishou 2026Q2 and interim results announcement (2026/08/18); share price and 52-week range from batchdata close (2026/10/12). 2026E adjusted net profit of RMB 8.99 billion is on a sell-side consensus basis.

## Gate 1: Circle of competence (7/10, pass)

Kuaishou earns money four ways, and its financials compress into three lines: online marketing RMB 20.6 billion (58.0%), live streaming RMB 8.7 billion (24.5%), and other services RMB 6.2 billion (17.5%, including e-commerce commissions and Kling subscriptions). The mechanics are not hard: advertising is traffic times fill rate times eCPM, live streaming is gross billings less streamer revenue share, and other services is transaction commissions plus AI revenue. One quarter lets you extrapolate the next; this is a business insiders can model.

The 3-point deduction is not for complexity but for unpredictability. eCPM is set by bidding, and bidding depends on merchants' view of next quarter's ROI; live-streaming revenue-share ratios are an annual negotiation between platform and agencies; Kling's pricing has changed three times in a year.**None of the three key variables sits with the company.** At its 2021 IPO the market was buying user growth; today it is buying AI monetization—two narratives in five years. This is not a comprehension problem; the business is changing shape.

## Gate 2: Good business (4/10, fail)

A textbook ad platform runs gross margins around 80% (Meta has held that level for years). Kuaishou's is just 51.6%, down 4.1pct year on year. Two reasons: livestream tipping must be shared with hosts and agencies, and e-commerce and Kling carry fulfillment and inference costs. Together these drag the margin structure from "pure advertising" to "mixed media." Adjusted net margin fell from 16.0% to 11.0%, five points wiped out in a year.

Pricing power matters more. Q2 online marketing grew 4.4%, below the Bloomberg consensus of 6.3% and below the overall China internet advertising market. For advertisers, switching platforms means changing one backend parameter — that is zero switching cost.**The test of a good business is raising prices without losing customers, and Kuaishou cannot do it; all it can do is cram more ad slots into the same feed.**

## Gate 3: Moat (5/10, barely passes)

412 million DAU is a real asset, and 797 million MAU is larger still, but put the two together and the weakness shows: DAU/MAU of 51.7%, versus 57.2% a year earlier (backing out roughly 715 million for 2025Q2 from MAU growth of +11.5% year on year). **A year of buying more than 80 million MAU left only just over 3 million DAU** — the new users are low-frequency, pulled in by AI ad spend and the lite app, and they leave after watching.

Three bricks in the moat: the laotie social graph (trust in lower-tier markets), short-drama supply (the most effective duration lever in 2026), and algorithmic distribution (solid but no generational gap). All three can defend; none can counterattack. Douyin is pressing on three fronts at once — duration, e-commerce and AI video — while Channels and Hongguo carve traffic from both flanks. The 5 score reflects "can hold the installed base," not "can win incremental share."

## Gate 4: Management (5/10, questionable)

Cheng Yixiao took over as CEO in October 2021, and his biggest call in the role was right: Kling launched in June 2024 and within two years reached ARR approaching USD 500 million, with its July spin-off financing valuing it at USD 18 billion post-money. At a company whose ad growth is only 4%, this is the only new business that external capital has priced.

But the same hands sent the opposite signal on capital allocation. In 2026Q2 it bought back HKD 880 million of stock, 19.6 million shares, at an average price of about HKD 45; six months later the share price is HKD 29.26, **a 35% unrealized loss on that buyback**. Buybacks are the one capital-allocation action that needs no approval and no stated rationale, and the execution price is management's quote on intrinsic value—a quote of HKD 45 that the market today honors at only 65 cents on the dollar.

## Gate 5: Margin of safety (6/10, questionable)

Run the numbers three ways. First pass: HKD 126.7 billion market cap ÷ TTM net profit = 10.5x PE. Second pass: market cap of about RMB 116 billion, 2026E adjusted net profit of RMB 8.99 billion, forward 12.9x — **the forward multiple is richer than TTM, which means earnings are heading down**. Third pass: net out RMB 24.27 billion of net cash, enterprise value of RMB 91.7 billion, or 10.2x 2026E.

Net cash is 20.9% of market cap, about HKD 6.1 per share — a real buffer. But the denominator of the margin of safety moves: if 2027 adjusted net profit falls to RMB 7 billion, the same EV becomes 13x. Cheap is static; getting more expensive is dynamic — the textbook shape of a value trap.

## Level 6: Discipline (4/10, fail)

The question at this gate is not whether the company is good, but whether my reason for buying can be verified. Kuaishou's only bull case right now is that "Kling is worth USD 18 billion, the parent holds 83.3%, and that stake alone is worth HKD 116.3 billion, equal to 92% of market cap" — but the USD 18 billion is a July primary-market post-money valuation, implying **36x PS** on USD 500 million ARR. That multiple pays for the imagination of three years from now, not today's cash flow.

Primary-market prices cannot be shorted, are not quoted daily, and are not audited. Using one to support a buy means treating an unverifiable anchor as margin of safety. This discipline gate fails not because it is expensive, but because **the reason it looks cheap cannot be falsified, and therefore cannot be proven.**

## Veto items and items to be verified

**Veto item: the core business is stalling with no evidence of stabilization.** Revenue +1.4%, advertising +4.4%, livestreaming -13.5% — none of the three lines shows an inflection point. Harder still is the company's own guidance: on the August 18 earnings call, management said e-commerce marketing services and commission revenue would come under year-on-year pressure in the second half. That is the company's own statement, not outside analysis.

**Three items to verify:** ① Whether online marketing growth can return above 8% — the only switch that re-rates the stock from 10x to 15x; ② How minority interest P&L will be allocated after the Kling spin-off, and whether losses stay at the parent while valuation stays at the subsidiary; ③ E-commerce no longer discloses GMV, leaving only merchant count and commission revenue to watch, and commission revenue is obscured by Kling's high growth.
