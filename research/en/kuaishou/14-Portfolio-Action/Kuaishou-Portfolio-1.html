Core thesis

The conclusion is blunt: current position 0%. This is not a bearish call that warrants shorting; the trade's current risk-reward (expected return -8.4%) does not justify any position. If the portfolio needs "short video" exposure, it ranks behind Tencent.

Two triggers to add: price ≤HKD 20 (build a 1%–2% observation position), or ad growth back above 8% (build a 3%–4% standard position). The stop-loss condition is not price-based; it is logic-based.

Why 0%, not "a small position to test the waters"

The rationale for a small position is participation, not return. A 1% position that doubles still adds only 1% to portfolio return, while consuming attention and distorting judgment on other opportunities. For a stock with negative expected return, the most expensive cost is not the loss but the attention.

A small position is justified only when the payoff is asymmetrically skewed to the upside—if the price falls below HKD 20, for instance, with 20% downside and 100% upside, then 1%–2% is a reasonable option premium.

Three add-on price levels, each tied to a distinct thesis

HKD 20: 2027E net profit of RMB 5.5–7.0 billion × 7x + net cash of RMB 24.27 billion, which means the market is pricing Kling at 0. Buying here is buying a "free option," and you can accept continued decline in the core business.

HKD 24: A 10% discount to the neutral-case HKD 26.6. If ad growth also returns to 8%, this level can justify 4% — because the denominator stops shrinking, the multiple can rise from 9x to 12x.

HKD 29.26 (current price): No position. Not because the price isn't low enough, but because there is no evidence that things will stop getting worse.

Trim and stop-loss: follow the thesis, not the price

If you already hold the stock (say, bought near HKD 45), the current level is not a stop-loss but a re-decision point. Ask yourself: If I were flat today, would I buy at this price? If not, trim.

Three hard exit conditions: advertising turns negative YoY, Kling fails to grow QoQ for two consecutive quarters, or cash-like assets fall below RMB 19 billion. If any one holds, exit unconditionally, regardless of price.

Rebalancing: where the money comes from and where it goes

If a buy trigger occurs, where should the money come from? Three candidates: trim Tencent, trim Meituan, or trim cash. The correct order is to trim the overlapping exposure first (Tencent's short-video exposure), given the two are more than 0.7 correlated; switching positions leaves portfolio risk unchanged and only alters the payoff odds. Only then consider total position size.

If a sale becomes necessary, who gets sold first? Within the same China internet basket, Kuaishou is the only name combining 1.4% core business growth, margin compression and 40% of its upside resting on a story — it should be the first to be sold, not the last.

Correlation: it is the same money as China internet stocks

Kuaishou's correlation with Tencent, Alibaba, Meituan and Pinduoduo exceeds 0.7: they share the same macro factor (Chinese consumption), the same flow backdrop (southbound plus foreign allocation to China internet ADRs) and the same regulatory environment. Buying Kuaishou is not diversification; it is another bet on China internet.

For genuine diversification, replace short-video exposure with Tencent (which spans Video Accounts, gaming, payments and cloud) and allocate the freed-up position to assets with low correlation to China internet (gold, Vietnamese manufacturing, AI hardware).

Conclusion

Portfolio action: no position, no participation, watchlist only. Where the portfolio already holds China internet exposure, Kuaishou adds no diversification; where it does not, Tencent is the better expression.

Discipline rules: single-name cap 5%, and 0% under an avoid rating. Where both apply, the stricter limit governs.
