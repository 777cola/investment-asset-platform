Moat anatomy: five layers, none of them widening — Kuaishou

Break "400 million DAU" into five verifiable dimensions
Research date 2026/10/12  ·  Price HK$29.26 (-2.79%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 126.7 billion  ·  PE(TTM) About 10.5x

Core view

Saying "Kuaishou has a moat" is easy; specifying which one, how wide and in which direction is not. We break the moat into five layers, set out the evidence and counter-evidence for each, and close with three financial indicators as verification. Conclusion: total score 4.9/10; not one of the five layers is widening.

Layer one: network effects 6/10

Evidence: 412 million DAU and 797 million MAU, with the creator-consumer two-sided network intact. Kuaishou's "Laotie" relationships are genuine social capital: streamers remember fans' names, and fans tip streamers and buy again. Such relationships struggle to grow inside Douyin's algorithmic feed.

Counter-evidence: DAU up 0.8% YoY, so the network has stopped expanding. MAU rose 11.5%, but DAU/MAU fell from 57.2% to 51.7%, and new users are low-frequency.A network effect creates value only when each additional user raises the value to other users; the users now being added do not produce that effect.

What a score of 6 means: the network still exists but is saturated. A saturated network confers no offensive capability, only defensive cost.

Tier two: content ecosystem 6/10

Evidence: short-drama supply grew 5x in half a year, the most effective lever for watch time in 2026; livestream supply draws on a large pool of small and mid-tier hosts; and agriculture, rural life, crafts and local opera are content categories unique to Kuaishou.

Counter-evidence: content flows. Hongguo used a free model plus higher revenue sharing to poach short-drama teams, and Douyin used traffic support to poach top hosts. The moat in a content ecosystem is not "how much content you have" but "whether creators would feel the loss if they left" — and Kuaishou cannot offer revenue sharing that makes leaving painful.

What a score of 6 means: the ecosystem has distinctive features but no exclusivity. Distinctiveness delivers differentiation; only exclusivity delivers pricing power.

Tier 3: Algorithms and technology 5/10

Evidence: the recommendation algorithm is top-tier in the industry, and the 70% increase in AIGC creative consumption shows progress in ad tools; Kling has entered the global top tier in video generation.

Counter-evidence: there is no generational gap in algorithms. Douyin's recommendation system is equally strong and has more data; in video generation, ByteDance's Seedance leads in multiple third-party evaluations.The premise of a technology moat is "I have it and others don't"; Kuaishou is "I have it, others have it too, and rivals have more money."

What a score of 5 means: not behind, but not ahead. Technology that does not lead is simply cost in a price war.

Tier 4: E-commerce closed loop 4/10

Evidence: trust-based e-commerce occupies a unique position in lower-tier markets, with high repurchase rates, and merchant count is growing.

Counter-evidence: shelf e-commerce is pinned down by Pinduoduo and livestream e-commerce by Douyin, while fulfillment lags JD.com and Alibaba. Management itself flags pressure on commission revenue in the second half — the most direct counter-evidence.

What a score of 4 means: it has the scenario, not the infrastructure. E-commerce is a heavy business; Kuaishou has built only the traffic layer.

Layer five: Kling's technology assets 5/10

Evidence: a global top-tier player, USD 500 million ARR, 50,000 enterprise customers, USD 18 billion raised independently — all backed by external capital with real money at risk.

Counter-evidence: model capabilities will be caught up, the price war has already begun, and compute costs are priced by Nvidia. Kling's moat is more likely "workflow embedding" than "model leadership", and workflow moats take time — and time is exactly what Kuaishou lacks.

What a score of 5 means: assets, but no certainty. An option, not a moat.

Financial verification: three indicators weaken simultaneously

A narrowing moat leaves financial traces. Three metrics can verify it:

① Gross margin: 55.7% → 51.6%, down 4.1pct in a year. Wide-moat companies defend gross margin; narrowing-moat companies concede margin to retain users.

② DAU/MAU: 57.2% → 51.7%. Falling engagement means falling user stickiness — a direct measure of weakening network effects.

③ Revenue per user: Livestreaming fell from RMB 24.6 per quarter to RMB 21.1 per quarter — the most direct evidence of declining pricing power.

All three metrics weakening in the same direction is more reliable than any qualitative analysis. A moat is not breached — it is worn thin, year after year, by concession.

Conclusion

Moat score 4.9/10: two of the five layers pass (network, content), two fail (algorithm, e-commerce) and one remains an option (Kling). The score supports a "stable cash flow + option" valuation rather than a "platform monopoly" one.
