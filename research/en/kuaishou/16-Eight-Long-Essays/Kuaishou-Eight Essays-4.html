Financial reality: four numbers, peeled back layer by layer — Kuaishou

From growth and profit to cash and the valuation denominator
Research date 2026/10/12  ·  Price HK$29.26 (-2.79%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 126.7 billion  ·  PE(TTM) About 10.5x

Core view

A single earnings report can contain dozens of numbers, but only four truly matter: where incremental growth comes from, why profit fell, whether the cash is usable, and whether the denominator will keep shrinking. Answer these four questions and the valuation debate is settled.

All incremental growth comes from a business that accounts for 2.4%

Total revenue RMB 35.535 billion, +1.4% YoY, up RMB 495 million. Kling rose from about RMB 280 million to RMB 850 million, an increase of RMB 567 million. 495 - 567 = -RMB 70 million.

Excluding Kling, Kuaishou's revenue declined across every business combined. This subtraction is the only entry point to understanding the company's current predicament, and it is the fact most easily obscured by the phrase "total revenue is still growing."

A level deeper: advertising +4.4%, livestreaming -13.5%. Advertising growth came almost entirely from higher eCPM (pricing), not increased inventory (time spent did not rise). Growth sustained by raising prices reverses immediately once advertiser ROI declines.

Truth two: two-thirds of the profit decline was deliberate

Adjusted net profit came in at RMB 3.913 billion, down 30.3% YoY — a decline of RMB 1.70 billion. Two sources, broken out:

Gross profit fell RMB 1.18 billion (gross margin -4.1pct × revenue) — passive: revenue-share costs rose, and without them creators would leave.

R&D rose RMB 1.185 billion (+34.7%) — discretionary: AI training spend that could have been avoided.

Together that is RMB 2.36 billion, more than the RMB 1.70 billion net profit decline; the gap was offset by lower selling expenses and interest income.

The split matters: if the decline came mainly from R&D, it is temporary and reverses once the investment phase ends; if mainly from gross margin, it is structural and does not come back. In reality it is half and half, meaning even if AI spending peaks, net profit can only recover half.

Truth three: the cash is real, but not all of it is usable

Cash assets of RMB 24.27 billion, equal to 20.9% of market cap, or about HKD 6.1 per share. The number is real, not padded with receivables.

But putting it to work requires answering three questions first: Where does it sit? Most is held in domestic operating companies, subject to foreign exchange controls, and cannot be remitted offshore. Is it someone else's money? Part corresponds to merchant deposits and unsettled creator payouts — liabilities, not assets. Is it restricted? Regulatory reserve funds and performance deposits all carry usage restrictions.

Freely deployable cash is estimated at around half. "Net cash at 21% of market cap" is therefore a buffer, not dividend capacity, and not the whole margin of safety.

Another number matters more: capex of RMB 5.9 billion, down sharply quarter on quarter. The company is actively reining in compute investment — slowing down in the AI arms race is an admission it cannot afford to compete.

Truth four: the TTM base is inflated by a high prior-period figure that has already dropped out

A HKD 126.7 billion market cap at 10.5x P/E implies TTM net profit of HKD 12.07 billion (about RMB 11 billion). Yet adjusted net profit for 1H26 totals just over RMB 7 billion (Q2 RMB 3.913 billion plus roughly RMB 3.1 billion in Q1).

A large chunk of that TTM figure therefore comes from 2H25 — the peak profit period. By the end of 2026 this high base will roll off, TTM net profit will fall to RMB 8–9 billion, and at the same share price the P/E will automatically rise to 13–14x.

This is the mechanism of "the denominator shrinking." It needs no bad news — only time.

Accounting quality: four good, two poor

Four strengths: all R&D is expensed, with no capitalisation games to flatter earnings; no history of large goodwill impairments; operating cash flow has stayed positive over the long run; no dual-high cash-and-debt position and no siphoning of funds by the controlling shareholder.

Two weaknesses: from 2026 the company will stop disclosing e-commerce GMV, leaving third parties unable to verify e-commerce growth; and after the Kling spin-off, revenue and losses are not disclosed separately, so the parent-company statements do not reveal the AI business's true profitability.

Neither weakness affects the accuracy of the numbers, but both affect verifiability. Lower verifiability warrants a valuation discount — one reason we apply the low end of the multiple range.

One-sentence summary of the three financial statements

Income statement: revenue flat, with share-based payments eating into gross profit and R&D into net profit.

Cash flow statement: EBITDA RMB 7.1 billion, capex RMB 5.9 billion and free cash flow of roughly RMB 1.2 billion on our rough calculation — positive, but thin.

Balance sheet: RMB 24.27 billion of cash, no material interest-bearing debt and 4.33 billion shares — clean, though not all of that cash belongs to shareholders.

Put the three statements together and the picture is of a company that still has money and is using it to buy time. Buying time is not the problem; what the time is used for is.

Conclusion

The financial reality comes down to four numbers pointing the same way: the denominator is shrinking. Growth rests on 2.4% of the business, the halving of profit is half deliberate and half passive, cash is a buffer rather than dividend capacity, and the TTM high base rolls off next year. Any claim that the stock is "cheap" must first answer whether the denominator keeps shrinking.
