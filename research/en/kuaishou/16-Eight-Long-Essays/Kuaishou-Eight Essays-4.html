Financial reality: four numbers, peeled back layer by layer — Kuaishou

From growth and profit to cash and the valuation denominator

Core view

A single earnings report can contain dozens of numbers, but only four truly matter: where the incremental growth comes from, why profit fell, whether the cash is usable, and whether the denominator will keep shrinking. Once these four questions are answered, the company's valuation no longer needs to be debated.

Truth one: all incremental growth comes from a business accounting for 2.4%

Total revenue RMB 35.535 billion, +1.4% YoY, an increase of RMB 495 million. Kling rose from about RMB 280 million to RMB 850 million, an increase of RMB 567 million. 495 - 567 = -RMB 70 million.

Excluding Kling, Kuaishou's revenue across all businesses combined declined. This subtraction is the only entry point to understanding the company's current predicament, and it is the fact most easily obscured by the phrase "total revenue is still growing."

A level deeper: advertising +4.4%, livestreaming -13.5%. Advertising growth came almost entirely from higher eCPM (pricing), not increased inventory (time spent did not rise). Growth sustained by raising prices reverses immediately once advertiser ROI declines.

Truth two: two-thirds of the profit decline was a deliberate choice

Adjusted net profit was RMB 3.913 billion, down 30.3% YoY, a decline of RMB 1.70 billion. Two sources, broken out:

Gross profit fell RMB 1.18 billion (gross margin -4.1pct × revenue) — this is passive: revenue-share costs rose, and without them creators would leave.

R&D rose RMB 1.185 billion (+34.7%) — this is discretionary: AI training spend that could have been avoided.

Together that is RMB 2.36 billion, more than the RMB 1.70 billion net profit decline; the gap was offset by lower selling expenses and interest income.

The split matters: if the decline came mainly from R&D, it is temporary and reverses once the investment phase ends; if mainly from gross margin, it is structural and does not come back. In reality it is half and half, meaning even if AI spending peaks, net profit can only recover half.

Truth three: the cash is real, but not all of it is usable

Cash assets RMB 24.27 billion, 20.9% of market cap, about HKD 6.1 per share. This number is real, not padded with receivables.

But to use it, three questions must be answered first: At which entity? Most sits in domestic operating companies, subject to foreign exchange controls, and cannot be remitted offshore. Is it someone else's money? Part corresponds to merchant deposits and unsettled creator payouts — liabilities, not assets. Is it restricted? Regulatory reserve funds and performance deposits all carry usage restrictions.

Freely deployable cash is estimated at around half. So "net cash at 21% of market cap" is a buffer, not dividend capacity, and not the whole margin of safety.

Another number matters more: capex RMB 5.9 billion, down sharply quarter on quarter. This shows the company is actively reining in compute investment — slowing down in the AI arms race is an admission it cannot afford to compete.

Truth four: the TTM contains a high base that has already disappeared

HKD 126.7 billion market cap ÷ 10.5x PE = HKD 12.07 billion TTM net profit (about RMB 11 billion). Yet adjusted net profit for the first half of 2026 totals only just over RMB 7 billion (Q2 RMB 3.913 billion + Q1 roughly RMB 3.1 billion).

This means a large chunk of the TTM figure comes from the second half of 2025 — the peak profit period. By the end of 2026, this high base will roll off, TTM net profit will fall to RMB 8–9 billion, and at the same share price, PE will automatically rise to 13–14x.

This is the mechanism of "the denominator shrinking." It needs no bad news — only time.

Accounting quality: four good, two poor

Four good ones: all R&D is expensed, no capitalisation games to flatter earnings; no history of large goodwill impairments; operating cash flow positive over the long run; no dual-high cash-and-debt, no controlling-shareholder fund siphoning.

Two bad ones: from 2026 it stops disclosing e-commerce GMV, so third parties cannot verify e-commerce growth; after the Kling spin-off, revenue and losses are not disclosed separately, so the parent-company statements do not show the AI business's true profitability.

The two "bad" items do not affect the accuracy of the numbers, but they do affect verifiability. Lower verifiability warrants a valuation discount — one reason we apply the low end of the multiple range.

One-sentence summary of the three financial statements

Income statement: revenue flat, gross profit eaten by share-based payments, net profit eaten by R&D.

Cash flow statement: EBITDA RMB 7.1 billion, capex RMB 5.9 billion, free cash flow about RMB 1.2 billion (rough calculation) — positive, but thin.

Balance sheet: RMB 24.27 billion cash, no material interest-bearing debt, 4.33 billion shares — clean, but not all the cash belongs to shareholders.

Put the three statements together and you get "a company that still has money and is using it to buy time". Buying time is not the problem; the problem is what the time is used for.

Conclusion

The conclusion on the financial reality: four numbers point in the same direction—the denominator is shrinking. Growth rests on 2.4% of the business, the halving of profit is half deliberate and half passive, cash is a buffer rather than dividend capacity, and the TTM high base will disappear next year. Any conclusion of "cheap" must first answer whether the denominator will keep shrinking.
