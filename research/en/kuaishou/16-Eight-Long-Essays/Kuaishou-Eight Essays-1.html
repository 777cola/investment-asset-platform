Cognitive reset: Kuaishou is not an AI company — Kuaishou

First, let's dispel three widely held misconceptions, then turn to valuation
Research date 2026/10/12  ·  Price HK$29.26 (-2.79%)  ·  Market cap About HKD 126.7 billion  ·  PE(TTM) About 10.5x

Core view

Three popular judgments about Kuaishou circulate in the market: that it is an AI company, that it has a moat of 400 million DAU, and that at 10x P/E it is cheap. Each is half true, and the half that is true is precisely not enough to support a buy.

Misconception 1: Kuaishou is an AI company

Kling is real. Launched in June 2024, it has reached 100 million users globally, nearly 50,000 enterprise customers and ARR approaching USD 500 million within two years, and raised at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion in its July spin-off financing. It is the fastest Chinese AI application to commercialise.

But having an AI business and being an AI company are two different things. Kling's Q2 revenue was RMB 850 million, 2.4% of Kuaishou's total revenue; part of the RMB 4.6 billion R&D spend goes to it, the rest to the recommendation algorithm.Slapping an AI label on the whole company on the strength of a business that is 2.4% of revenue is the first step to fooling yourself.

More important, Kling has already been spun off. The parent holds an 83.3% stake; if it lists independently, Kuaishou becomes "an advertising company holding an AI equity stake," and its valuation logic would resemble a holding platform more than an AI company.

The right question is not whether Kuaishou is an AI company but how much parent shareholders get if Kling succeeds. At USD 18 billion and 83.3%, that is about HKD 116.3 billion, equal to 92% of the current market cap. That number is tempting, but it rests on a price the public market has not tested.

Misconception 2: 400 million DAU is a moat

412 million DAU is real and scarce—fewer than five apps in China exceed 400 million DAU. But a moat is not defined by "large scale"; it is defined by "competitors cannot break in."

Two data points. First: DAU +0.8%, MAU +11.5%, DAU/MAU falling from 57.2% to 51.7%. A year of acquisition brought in 80 million MAU but retained only just over 3 million DAU—new users are low-frequency, and scale is turning "hollow."

Second: advertising revenue per DAU per quarter is RMB 50, livestreaming RMB 21 (RMB 24.6 last year). Livestreaming's per-user output is declining, and advertising only barely compensates through price increases. Scale has not brought pricing power, only costs.

In the stock market, the value of 400 million DAU is not "the ability to attack" but "the ability to defend cash flow." That is an asset, not a moat. Assets can be valued; only moats command a premium.

Misconception 3: A 10x PE is cheap

At 10.5x P/E (TTM), the stock is genuinely not expensive among Hong Kong internet names. But the denominator of that P/E can change.

Consensus 2026 adjusted net profit has already been cut from RMB 12 billion at the start of the year to RMB 8.99 billion. On that denominator, a market cap of RMB 116 billion (converted to RMB) implies a forward P/E of 12.9x — 23% more expensive than TTM.

A forward multiple above the TTM multiple has only one cause: profit is declining. This is the textbook shape of a value trap: cheap on a static basis, more expensive on a dynamic one. Buyers of this kind of company are usually using last year's profit to justify this year's price.

Run it a third way: net out RMB 24.27 billion of net cash and EV is RMB 91.8 billion, or 10.2x 2026E and 10.2x 2027E neutral profit of RMB 9 billion. For an advertising platform growing 2%, that multiple is reasonable, not cheap.

The correct read: an attention wholesaler losing its pricing power

Strip away the three misconceptions and Kuaishou's shape is clear: it is China's third-largest attention wholesaler, its customers are advertisers, its suppliers are creators, and its product is the time of 400 million people.

In 2018–2021 this was an excellent business: scarce supply, robust demand, limited competition, advertising growth of 50%+, and gross margin rising all the way. After 2022, three things changed at once: Douyin scaled up, Channels entered via the social graph, and macro consumption weakened. Pricing power shifted from the platform to advertisers.

The 2026 numbers are the result: advertising +4.4% (below the market), livestreaming -13.5%, gross margin -4.1pct. All three say the same thing — it can only retain supply by conceding margin and retain demand by cutting price.

Kling is the company's only variable. It is not an extension of the core business but a different business: customers shift from advertisers to enterprises, revenue from bidding to subscription, costs from bandwidth to compute. Valuing it within the core business's framework understates it; using it to mark up the core business overstates it.

From RMB 1.7 trillion to RMB 126.7 billion: how three valuation logics collapsed

Phase one (2021): the user-growth thesis. At IPO the market cap briefly reached HKD 1.7 trillion as the market bought "the next Douyin." The thesis broke in the second half of 2021, when DAU growth fell to single digits.

Phase two (2022–2024): the margin-recovery thesis. The market was buying "the end of loss-funded growth and a margin recovery," and the stock recovered from HKD 30 to HKD 80. The thesis delivered in 2025, then expired: margin recovery has a ceiling, and in 2026Q2 the net margin fell from 16.0% back to 11.0%.

Phase three (2025–2026): the AI-monetization thesis. The market was buying Kling. The thesis peaked at the July 2026 spin-off (HKD 46.92), then was disproved after the interim results: Kling is only 2.4% of revenue and cannot rescue the core business.

Three theses, three collapses. This is not management incompetence; it is the ceiling on this business getting lower each time. What is the next thesis? Possibly "return of cash flow": a company that does not grow, does not burn cash and buys back stock in size. But that corresponds to 8x PE, not 13x.

Conclusion

The cognitive reset, in short: treating Kuaishou as an "AI company" overvalues it, treating it as a "declining platform" undervalues it, and treating it as a "mature advertising company plus a Kling option" is the right frame. The first implies 20x, the second 5x, the third 10x. At 12.9x forward P/E, the stock sits right at the upper bound for a "mature advertising company", with the option thrown in for free.
