Core view

Research ultimately comes down to four questions: what is this business worth, how much am I willing to pay, under what conditions would I change my mind, and what if I am wrong. The first two are valuation; the last two are discipline. The problem with Kuaishou is that the first two produce a negative expected value, and the last two have no trigger conditions.

Question 1: How much is this business worth

Sum-of-the-parts: 2027E core business net profit of RMB 9 billion at a neutral 9x = RMB 81 billion, plus net cash of RMB 24.27 billion = RMB 105.3 billion, or HKD 115 billion, equivalent to HKD 26.6 per share.

Kling is carried at 0 in the neutral case and at a 50% discount in the optimistic case. Why 0 in the neutral case? Its valuation is set in the primary market, with no public-market anchor and no disclosed P&L.An asset that cannot be verified is worth 0 in a conservative valuation.

The method rests on a single premise: 2027 net profit of RMB 9 billion. If advertising continues to deteriorate, that premise fails and the valuation must be recalculated.

Question 2: How much am I willing to pay?

Current price HKD 29.26 = neutral value + 9%. That 9% is the option premium the market assigns to Kling — HKD 11.7 billion, or 9% of the USD 18 billion post-money valuation.

I am unwilling to pay a 9% premium for this option, for three reasons: ① the underlying (Kling) does not disclose profit or loss, making it impossible to value; ② the strike price (next-round valuation) may be marked down; ③ the option has already been priced once through a separate financing, and parent-company shareholders get no liquidity.

The price I am willing to pay is HKD 20 — at which point the option is free.

Question 3: Under what conditions would we change our mind?

Three conditions, any one of which triggers a re-rating:

1. Online marketing revenue up ≥8% YoY for two consecutive quarters. Hard evidence of business recovery, and more reliable than any management commentary.

2. Kling discloses revenue and P&L on a comparable basis, with annualised revenue ≥RMB 6 billion. That is the threshold at which the story becomes a business.

3. Share price falls below HKD 20. A price trigger that needs no fundamental confirmation, though position size is capped at 2%.

Of the three, the first is most likely to appear first (Q3 results in November), the third could appear at any time, and the second will have to wait until 2027.

Question 4: What if we get it wrong?

If I am wrong on the upside — ads recover to 8%, Kling breaks out, the stock reaches HKD 46 — my loss is opportunity cost, not principal. That mistake is acceptable.

If I am wrong on the buy — ads turn negative, Kling's valuation halves, the stock falls to HKD 16 — I lose 45% of principal. That mistake is unacceptable.

The costs of the two mistakes are asymmetric, and that asymmetry is the entire case for staying away. Not because I am bearish, but because the odds are wrong.

Review mechanism: what to look back at after three months

Write down today's judgments and check them item by item in three months: 1) whether ad growth is ≥6%; 2) whether Keling continues to grow quarter on quarter; 3) whether the R&D expense ratio exceeds 13.5%; 4) whether buybacks continue and at what average price; 5) whether cash-like assets fall below RMB 22 billion.

If three of the five deteriorate, the call was right; if three improve, I was wrong and need to redo the work.

A review exists not to prove yourself right but to catch your mistakes early.

Conclusion

Decision framework in sum: fair value HKD 26.6, price we would pay HKD 20, three conditions that would change our mind, two asymmetric mistakes. Put the four together and only one action remains: don't buy, wait.
