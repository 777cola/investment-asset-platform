## Layer one: revenue structure — one subtraction reveals the position

355.35 - 350.4 = RMB 495 million — that is the entire increment. Kling rose from about RMB 280 million to RMB 850 million, an increment of RMB 567 million. 4.95 - 5.67 = -RMB 70 million. For the entire company excluding Kling, revenue was negative year on year.

The arithmetic is not hard, but few bother to do it, because it demolishes the persuasiveness of the line that "total revenue is still growing." One layer deeper: within the RMB 6.2 billion of other services, e-commerce commissions and Kling are lumped together, and the company does not break them out. Kling at RMB 850 million implies e-commerce commissions plus other of roughly RMB 5.35 billion, growing around 8% year on year (off a base of RMB 5.23 billion) — faster than advertising, but nowhere near enough to fill the livestreaming gap.

The e-commerce line is now almost impossible to observe independently. The company no longer discloses GMV quarterly, only merchant count and commission revenue trends; commissions sit alongside Kling under "other services." By industry convention, livestreaming e-commerce commission rates run 2%–5%; backing out from the RMB 6.2 billion of other services, e-commerce-related revenue is roughly RMB 5 billion, implying GMV in the trillion range — but this is only an order-of-magnitude estimate, the company has given no definition, and it cannot be used as a conclusion.

The direction of the mix shift is worth noting: advertising 58.0% (56.3% last year), livestreaming 24.5% (28.7% last year), other 17.5% (14.9% last year). The revenue mix is moving toward "asset-light, low revenue share," a long-term positive for gross margin — but near term it is masked by rising revenue-share costs.

## Tier two: gross profit — RMB 1.46 billion lost to revenue sharing

Gross margin 51.6%, down 4.1pct YoY. Revenue RMB 35.535 billion; 1pct equals RMB 355 million, so 4.1pct equals RMB 1.46 billion given up. The company attributes this to "higher revenue-sharing costs," specifically two items: higher revenue-share ratios paid to guilds and streamers on livestreaming (to retain top talent), and higher content-split fees for short dramas (to counter Hongguo).

Both outlays are forced by competition, not proactive investment.The distinction matters: proactive investment can be stopped, but competition-driven spending cannot—stop it and creators leave.

Another measure confirms this: gross profit RMB 18.34 billion, down RMB 1.18 billion from last year, while revenue rose RMB 495 million. In other words, each additional yuan of revenue brought no gross profit and instead carried a cost subsidy.This is a textbook case of unprofitable growth.

## Tier 3: Costs and profit — R&D spending up RMB 1.185 billion

R&D RMB 4.6 billion, expense ratio 12.9% (9.7% last year). The extra RMB 1.185 billion went almost entirely into AI: Kling's training and inference, the multimodal overhaul of the recommendation algorithm, and the compute scheduling team. This is not waste—Kling did get built—but it has reshaped the income statement into an investment-phase profile.

Put the three together: gross profit -RMB 1.18 billion, R&D -RMB 1.185 billion, other expenses and tax items roughly flat, totaling -RMB 2.36 billion. Yet adjusted net profit fell only RMB 1.70 billion, the gap coming from compressed selling expenses and higher interest income. In other words, the company cut marketing while raising R&D, moving the income statement from one hole to another.

Another angle on expense elasticity: if the R&D expense ratio had held at last year's 9.7%, adjusted net profit this quarter would have been RMB 5.07 billion (RMB 1.185 billion more), down only 9.7% YoY—that is, two-thirds of the net profit decline came from deliberately stepping up AI investment, one-third from gross margin erosion. This split is critical to the judgment: the former is controllable, the latter is passive.

EBITDA RMB 7.1 billion, down only 7.7%, indicating a rising share of depreciation and amortization and share-based compensation—a harbinger of asset-heavy operations. AI investment will eventually turn into depreciation, and depreciation does not fall when revenue declines.

## Tier 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — another RMB 1.2 billion of headroom

Capex of RMB 5.9 billion, a sharp sequential pullback (compute investment was heavier last quarter). Adjusted EBITDA of RMB 7.1 billion less capex of RMB 5.9 billion leaves RMB 1.2 billion. This is a rough free cash flow calculation — no tax deducted, no working capital movement deducted — so it is a "ceiling."

The good news is Kuaishou's working capital structure works in its favor: advertiser prepayments, live-streaming top-ups, and e-commerce merchant deposits all mean cash collected before services rendered. This typically contributes positively to cash flow, so true free cash flow is likely better than RMB 1.2 billion.

The bad news is this RMB 1.2 billion buffer cannot absorb any surprise: one compute buying spree, one short-drama copyright war, one dividend commitment, and it is wiped out. The RMB 24.27 billion of cash on the books provides a cushion, but a cushion is not profit.

## Three details that are easily overlooked

Detail 1: Quarterly gross margin volatility is widening. Gross margin ranged roughly 54%–56% across the four quarters of 2025, fell to around 53% in 2026Q1 and 51.6% in Q2. The trend is one-way down, not seasonal. Detail 2: The capitalization ratio within R&D expense is low. The RMB 4.6 billion was almost entirely expensed, showing the company did not use capitalization to dress up profit—cleaner on this point than most China concept stocks. Detail 3: Interest income is providing a floor. On RMB 24.27 billion of cash-like assets at a 2% annualized yield, the quarterly contribution is about RMB 120 million, equal to 3% of adjusted net profit.

Put the three details together: profit quality is not bad, only the trend is. That matters for the call—it means this is "a good company getting worse," not "a bad company that was always bad." The former has a higher valuation floor; the latter has a weaker rebound.

## Quarterly and half-year: trend matters more than a single point

Kling's revenue exceeded RMB 1.5 billion in the first half, at about RMB 650 million in Q1 and RMB 850 million in Q2, up 31% QoQ. This is the only curve in the entire company that accelerated on a quarterly basis. If this slope holds for four quarters, Kling's annualized revenue could reach more than RMB 6 billion in 2027—only then would it deserve the term "second growth curve."

Conversely, advertising QoQ: RMB 20.6 billion in Q2, roughly around RMB 20 billion in Q1 (a rough seasonal estimate), a slight QoQ increase and +4.4% YoY. A curve that is flat QoQ with 4% YoY growth is what a mature business looks like, not a stalling one—what is stalling is livestreaming.
