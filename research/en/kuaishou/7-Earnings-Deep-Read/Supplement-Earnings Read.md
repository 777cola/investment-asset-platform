## Layer one: revenue structure — one subtraction reveals the position

355.35 - 350.4 = RMB 495 million — that is the entire increment. Kling rose from about RMB 280 million to RMB 850 million, an increment of RMB 567 million. 4.95 - 5.67 = -RMB 70 million. Strip out Kling and company-wide revenue was down YoY.

The arithmetic is not hard, but few bother to do it, because it demolishes the persuasiveness of the line that "total revenue is still growing." One layer deeper: within the RMB 6.2 billion of other services, e-commerce commissions and Kling are lumped together, and the company does not break them out. Kling at RMB 850 million implies e-commerce commissions plus other of roughly RMB 5.35 billion, growing around 8% YoY (off a base of RMB 5.23 billion) — faster than advertising, but nowhere near enough to fill the livestreaming gap.

The e-commerce line is now almost impossible to observe independently. The company no longer discloses GMV quarterly, only merchant count and commission revenue trends; commissions sit alongside Kling under "other services." By industry convention, livestreaming e-commerce commission rates run 2%–5%; backing out from the RMB 6.2 billion of other services, e-commerce-related revenue is roughly RMB 5 billion, implying GMV in the trillion range — but this is only an order-of-magnitude estimate, the company has given no definition, and it cannot be used as a conclusion.

The direction of the mix shift is worth noting: advertising 58.0% (56.3% last year), livestreaming 24.5% (28.7% last year), other 17.5% (14.9% last year). The revenue mix is moving toward "asset-light, low revenue share," a long-term positive for gross margin — but near term it is masked by rising revenue-share costs.

## Tier two: gross profit — RMB 1.46 billion lost to revenue sharing

Gross margin 51.6%, down 4.1pct YoY. On revenue of RMB 35.535 billion, 1pct equals RMB 355 million, so 4.1pct equates to RMB 1.46 billion of margin given up. Management attributes this to "higher revenue-sharing costs," specifically two items: higher revenue-share ratios paid to guilds and streamers on livestreaming (to retain top talent), and higher content-split fees for short dramas (to counter Hongguo).

Both outlays are forced by competition, not proactive investment.The distinction matters: proactive investment can be stopped, but competition-driven spending cannot—stop it and creators leave.

Gross profit offers further confirmation: RMB 18.34 billion, down RMB 1.18 billion from last year, even as revenue rose RMB 495 million. In other words, each additional yuan of revenue brought no gross profit and instead carried a cost subsidy.This is a textbook case of unprofitable growth.

## Tier 3: Costs and profit — R&D spend up RMB 1.185 billion

R&D came in at RMB 4.6 billion, a 12.9% expense ratio versus 9.7% last year. The incremental RMB 1.185 billion went almost entirely into AI: Kling training and inference, the multimodal overhaul of the recommendation algorithm, and the compute scheduling team. This is not waste—Kling did get built—but it has reshaped the income statement into an investment-phase profile.

Taking the three together—gross profit -RMB 1.18 billion, R&D -RMB 1.185 billion, other expenses and tax items roughly flat—gives -RMB 2.36 billion. Yet adjusted net profit fell only RMB 1.70 billion, the gap coming from compressed selling expenses and higher interest income. In other words, the company cut marketing while raising R&D, shifting the income statement from one hole to another.

Expense elasticity offers another angle: had the R&D expense ratio held at last year's 9.7%, adjusted net profit this quarter would have been RMB 5.07 billion (RMB 1.185 billion more), down only 9.7% YoY—that is, two-thirds of the net profit decline came from deliberately stepping up AI investment, one-third from gross margin erosion. This split is critical to the judgment: the former is controllable, the latter is passive.

EBITDA was RMB 7.1 billion, down only 7.7%, indicating a rising share of depreciation and amortization and share-based compensation—a harbinger of asset-heavy operations. AI investment will eventually turn into depreciation, and depreciation does not fall when revenue declines.

## Tier 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — a further RMB 1.2 billion of headroom

Capex came in at RMB 5.9 billion, a sharp sequential pullback after heavier compute investment last quarter. Adjusted EBITDA of RMB 7.1 billion less that capex leaves RMB 1.2 billion. This is a rough free cash flow calculation — no tax deducted, no working capital movement deducted — so treat it as a ceiling.

The good news is that Kuaishou's working capital structure works in its favour: advertiser prepayments, live-streaming top-ups and e-commerce merchant deposits all mean cash is collected before services are rendered. That typically contributes positively to cash flow, so true free cash flow is likely better than RMB 1.2 billion.

The bad news is that a RMB 1.2 billion buffer absorbs no surprise: one compute buying spree, one short-drama copyright war, one dividend commitment, and it is wiped out. The RMB 24.27 billion of cash on the books provides a cushion, but a cushion is not profit.

## Three details that are easily overlooked

Detail 1: Quarterly gross margin volatility is widening. Gross margin ran at roughly 54%–56% through the four quarters of 2025 before falling to about 53% in 2026Q1 and 51.6% in Q2 — a one-way decline, not seasonality. Detail 2: The capitalisation ratio within R&D expense is low. The RMB 4.6 billion was almost entirely expensed, so the company did not use capitalisation to dress up profit — cleaner on this point than most China concept stocks. Detail 3: Interest income is providing a floor. On RMB 24.27 billion of cash-like assets at a 2% annualised yield, the quarterly contribution is about RMB 120 million, equal to 3% of adjusted net profit.

Taken together, the three details point to profit quality that is not bad — only the trend is. That matters for the call: this is "a good company getting worse," not "a bad company that was always bad." The former has a higher valuation floor; the latter, a weaker rebound.

## Quarterly vs half-year: the trend matters more than any single data point

Kling revenue topped RMB 1.5 billion in 1H, split roughly RMB 650 million in Q1 and RMB 850 million in Q2, up 31% QoQ — the only line in the company that accelerated sequentially. Hold that slope for four quarters and Kling's annualised revenue could exceed RMB 6 billion in 2027; only then does "second growth curve" become the right label.

Advertising, by contrast, was flat QoQ: RMB 20.6 billion in Q2 versus roughly RMB 20 billion in Q1 on a rough seasonal estimate, a slight sequential gain and +4.4% YoY. A curve flat QoQ on 4% YoY growth is what a mature business looks like, not a stalling one — livestreaming is what is stalling.
