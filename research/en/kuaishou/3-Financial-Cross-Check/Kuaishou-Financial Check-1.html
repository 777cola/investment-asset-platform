Core thesis

I cross-checked all nine key 2026Q2 figures against two sources: the earnings announcement versus broker notes and media reports. Seven deviations fall within 0.2% and are attributable to rounding; two require their definitions to be explained separately. Cash-type assets (RMB 24.27 billion vs RMB 23.5 billion, a 3.2% gap) differ on whether time deposits and wealth-management products are included; R&D (RMB 4.6 billion vs RMB 4.58 billion, a 0.4% gap) differs on capitalization treatment.

No numerical conflict emerged that would require overturning the draft. What truly warrants attention is not the deviations but definitional shrinkage such as "the company no longer discloses GMV"—it leaves third parties unable to verify the e-commerce line.

Two definitional gaps that need explaining, not errors

Cash assets: RMB 24.27 billion vs RMB 23.5 billion, a difference of RMB 770 million. The announcement basis combines cash and equivalents, time deposits and wealth management products; stockanalysis captures only the "cash and cash equivalents" line on the balance sheet, with time deposits and wealth management products sitting in other lines. The 3.2% gap comes entirely from this. Treatment: use RMB 24.27 billion for valuation, since it is genuinely available and can cover interest-bearing debt; note, however, that it includes restricted portions, so do not assume "net cash fully distributable as dividends."

R&D: RMB 4.6 billion vs RMB 4.58 billion, a difference of 0.43%. Media summaries typically copy the "R&D expenses" line directly; R&D expenses in the announcement include share-based compensation amortization, and some bases make capitalization adjustments. The gap is under RMB 20 million and affects no conclusion. Treatment: use RMB 4.6 billion.

Three things to calculate yourself: gross profit, net margin, profit variance

The announcement discloses only a gross margin of 51.6%, not gross profit. 355.35 × 51.6% = RMB 18.34 billion — a calculated figure, not a disclosed one, and must be flagged as such in the table. Last year: 350.4 × 55.7% = RMB 19.52 billion, down 6.0% YoY. Note the scissors gap: revenue rose 1.4% while gross profit fell 6.0%, and that gap is the source of the profit decline.

Reported net profit of RMB 3.152 billion against adjusted net profit of RMB 3.913 billion leaves a gap of RMB 761 million, reflecting share-based compensation expense and amortisation of intangible assets (including amortisation of the Kling-related investment). The two bases must not be mixed in YoY comparisons: 3.152 versus last year's 4.922 is -36.0%, while 3.913 versus last year's 5.614 is -30.3%. Both numbers are correct; mixing them is wrong.

The bottom line is the adjusted basis, because it strips out fluctuations in share-based compensation valuation around the Kling spin-off and is comparable across periods; but to calculate what shareholders actually receive, one must return to the reported net profit of RMB 3.152 billion.

The one that does not add up: market cap and share count

HKD 29.26 × 4.33 billion shares = HKD 126.7 billion, consistent with the market cap in the working papers. But the TTM net profit implied by the 10.5x PE is about HKD 12.07 billion (roughly RMB 11.05 billion), higher than the annualized RMB 8.2 billion or so for the first half of 2026 (a rough 6.2 + 2.0 calculation)—indicating that profit in the second half of 2025 was clearly better than in the first half of 2026, and the TTM figure contains a high base that has already disappeared.

That is why the TTM PE is 10.5x and the forward PE is 12.9x. The market is not naive; it uses forward earnings. Any analysis that calls 10.5x "cheap" must first check which period the denominator covers.

Disclosure risk: what the company no longer discloses

From 2026, Kuaishou will stop disclosing quarterly e-commerce GMV, reporting only merchant count and commission revenue. The implications: ① third parties can no longer cross-check the true growth rate of the e-commerce line; ② commission revenue is folded into "other services" and sits in the same table as Kling's RMB 850 million; ③ every GMV assumption in sell-side models is an estimate.

For researchers, narrower disclosure is itself information: what a company chooses not to disclose is usually what is deteriorating.

One additional sensitivity test I ran

The 51.6% gross margin is the most sensitivity-worthy item in this report. On revenue of RMB 35.5 billion, every 1pct change in gross margin equates to RMB 355 million of gross profit, or 9% of adjusted net profit. In 2026Q2 gross margin fell 4.1pct YoY, surrendering RMB 1.46 billion of gross profit and accounting for nine-tenths of the RMB 1.7 billion decline in net profit.

In other words, the profit decline needs no "AI investment" to explain it; rising revenue-share costs alone are enough. The roughly RMB 1.2 billion of extra R&D spending is the second layer; only the two combined make the -30.3% add up. This decomposition is critical to the call: if the decline comes mainly from revenue sharing, it is structural (you have to keep conceding margin to win creators); if mainly from R&D, it is temporary (it comes back once the investment period ends). The evidence so far points to the former.

Conclusion

Seven of the nine figures tie out. Both deviations above 1% reflect definitional differences, so the working-paper numbers can be used with confidence. Three points to note: gross profit is a derived figure (RMB 18.34 billion); net profit carries two definitions (31.52 and 39.13); and the TTM laps the high base from the second half of 2025.
