Core thesis

412 million people open Kuaishou every day, generating RMB 35.5 billion in revenue and RMB 3.9 billion in adjusted net profit in a single quarter. That sounds like a lot, until you put the two numbers together: revenue rose just 1.4% YoY, while net profit fell 30.3%.

The stock has fallen from 82.95 to 29.26, a drop of 65%. Many ask whether this is a buying opportunity. My answer is no, and the reason is not valuation — it is this subtraction.

One subtraction tells you what this company is really worth

Kuaishou reported revenue of RMB 35.535 billion this quarter, up RMB 495 million YoY.

Kling AI contributed RMB 850 million, up from roughly RMB 280 million — a gain of RMB 567 million.

495 minus 567 equals -0.7.In other words, strip out Kling and Kuaishou's revenue across all its other businesses combined was shrinking.

The arithmetic is simple enough; few bother to run it. But once you do, the claim that "total revenue is still growing" no longer holds up.

How big is the AI story? 2.4% of revenue

Kling is a genuinely good asset. Launched in June 2024, it has reached 100 million global users, nearly 50,000 enterprise customers and annualised revenue (ARR) approaching USD 500 million within two years, growing 200% YoY. In July it also closed USD 3 billion in financing at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion, with Kuaishou itself holding 83.3%.

It is the fastest-monetising Chinese AI application, without dispute.

But quarterly revenue is RMB 850 million, or 2.4% of total revenue. At a USD 18 billion valuation it is worth HKD 116.3 billion, equivalent to 92% of Kuaishou's current total market cap.

That is the problem: either the private-market price is wrong, or the market believes Kuaishou's core business is worth little. One of the two must hold.

Where did profit go? Revenue sharing and R&D each take a bite

Gross margin fell from 55.7% to 51.6%. One point is RMB 355 million; 4.1 points is RMB 1.46 billion. The company attributes this to "higher revenue-sharing costs" — in plain terms, the platform gave up more money to retain streamers and short-drama creators.

R&D rose from RMB 3.4 billion to RMB 4.6 billion, up nearly RMB 1.2 billion, mainly into AI training.

Together the two total RMB 2.6 billion, compressing adjusted net profit from RMB 5.6 billion to RMB 3.9 billion.

The interesting part: had the R&D expense ratio held at last year's level, net profit would have fallen only 9.7%.In other words, two-thirds of the profit decline was management's own choice; one-third was forced by competition.The voluntary part can be stopped; the involuntary part cannot.

Is 10.5x PE cheap? Depends on which denominator you use

A 10.5x P/E excites a lot of people. But the denominator of that P/E moves.

Consensus 2026 adjusted net profit has already been cut to RMB 8.99 billion. On that number, a market cap of roughly RMB 116 billion implies a forward P/E of 12.9x — more expensive than the 10.5x TTM.

A forward multiple above the TTM multiple has only one cause: earnings are heading down. This is not cheap; it is the classic value-trap pattern — cheap on a static basis, increasingly expensive on a dynamic one.

That said, the company holds RMB 24.27 billion in cash-like assets, 21% of market cap, or HKD 6.1 per share. That is a real buffer. But a buffer keeps you alive; it does not make you money.

Three scenarios: 45% downside, 57% upside

Bear case: advertising turns negative and Kling falls behind; 2027 net profit of RMB 5.5 billion at 7x, plus net cash, gives HKD 16, 45% below the current price.

Base case: advertising +3%, Kling doubles but remains loss-making, gives HKD 26.6, 9% below the current price.

Bull case: advertising back to +8% and Kling ARR breaks USD 1 billion, gives HKD 46, 57% above the current price.

Weighted at 35/45/20 probabilities, expected return is -8.4%. The upside looks larger, yet the math comes out negative—that is the difference between odds and probabilities.

What is the market betting on?

Two bets are embedded in the current share price.

The first is that "Kling wins." Video generation is the hottest technology theme of 2026 and Kling sits in the first tier. If it breaks out the way Douyin did, Kuaishou becomes the next ByteDance. The odds are long, but nobody knows the probability of success.

The second is that "advertising comes back." Chinese consumption recovers in 2027, ad budgets rebound, and Kuaishou, with 400 million DAU, benefits automatically. This bet has a higher hit rate but limited upside — if advertising returns, Kuaishou is still just a 12x P/E company.

One bet offers upside without certainty; the other offers certainty without upside. The market packages both and sells them to you at HKD 29.

Under what circumstances would I change my mind?

Two conditions need to be met, and neither is far off:

First, online marketing revenue growth returns above 8%, signalling that advertisers are willing to raise budgets on Kuaishou again and the core business is recovering.

Second, Kling discloses revenue and profit/loss on a comparable basis, with annualised revenue exceeding RMB 6 billion — the point at which AI turns from a story into a business.

Until either happens, a single quarter of negative advertising growth is all it takes for the share price to fall from 29 to 20. That is not a black swan; it is the base case.

One last thing.

Kuaishou is not a bad company: its 400 million users remain, its RMB 24.2 billion of cash remains, and Kling's technology remains.

But investing does not pay for what remains; it pays only for improvement.

This quarter's results offer no evidence of improvement.

Should such evidence emerge, I will not dismiss it simply because I was bearish today. But today there is none.

Conclusion

Avoid. Not because the stock has fallen 65%, but because the financials cannot verify the investment case. Until advertising growth returns to 8%, or Kling reaches an annualised RMB 6 billion with disclosed P&L, price alone is not a reason to own it.
