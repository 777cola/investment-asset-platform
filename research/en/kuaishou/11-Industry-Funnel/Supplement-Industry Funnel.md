## First-round screen: can it be bought

Three of the six are uninvestable: Douyin, Xiaohongshu and Hongguo are all unlisted. They drop out not because they are poor businesses but because they cannot be bought — and a good company you cannot own is worth nothing to a portfolio.

Tencent is buyable, but WeChat Channels is only a small slice of revenue; owning Tencent means paying 30x P/E for an 8x P/E business plus a WeChat Channels option. Bilibili is buyable, but its scale is an order of magnitude smaller than Kuaishou's: ad growth is faster, profitability weaker.

That leaves Kuaishou: the only Hong Kong-listed pure play on short video, livestreaming and AI video. It wins this round on scarcity, not on quality.

## Second-round screen: growth and profitability

Side by side, the three investable names stack up as follows: Kuaishou revenue +1.4%, adjusted net profit -30.3%; Bilibili's advertising grows faster but net margin is near zero; Tencent's Video Accounts grows fast but is diluted by the parent's size.

On "growth + profitability," none of the three passes. On "cash flow + valuation," Tencent ranks first, Kuaishou second, Bilibili worst. On "pure sector beta," Kuaishou ranks first.

Hence the awkwardness of the funnel: Kuaishou ranks first on no single dimension. It is simply irreplaceable on "purity."

## Final selection: why this one, and why not the others

Why it is the one: ① The only pure-play in Hong Kong — institutions seeking short-video exposure have no alternative; ② 412 million DAU is a hard asset, and after the fall to HKD 126.7 billion the EV is just RMB 91.8 billion, effectively handing investors an AI business for free; ③ Kling is the only asset in the global top tier of video generation to have raised independently priced financing.

Why it is not the one: ① Growth ranks last, and management itself guides to pressure in the second half; ② Margins are eroding (gross margin -4.1pct, net margin from 16.0% to 11.0%); ③ Kling's valuation cannot be verified and accounts for only 2.4% of revenue.

The funnel's conclusion: "No better choice" does not equal "a good choice." If it is the only option, the right move is to wait for price, not buy now.

## A note beyond the funnel: why valuation is not the first screen

Many investors screen on valuation first, keeping only the lowest P/E names. That order is wrong: low-valuation companies are often cheap precisely because their business is deteriorating. Kuaishou is a case in point. Its 10.5x TTM P/E looks low among Hong Kong internet stocks, yet its forward 12.9x is higher than Tencent's and higher than Bilibili's.

The correct order is to ask first whether you can buy it (accessibility), then whether the business is improving or deteriorating (trend), and only then about price. Kuaishou's funnel stalls at step two—it is buyable, but should not be bought while the trend is down, no matter how cheap. The same order explains why "low valuation + high dividend yield" screens often conceal a pile of declining companies.

## If one must allocate within this sector

Ranking the three options: Tencent > Kuaishou > Bilibili. Tencent offers cash flow, WeChat Channels optionality and AI applications (Yuanbao), with downside protection; Kuaishou offers pure beta and Kling optionality, with none; Bilibili offers small-cap elasticity, but no earnings support.

If Kuaishou is the only name you can buy, treat it as an option: size it small, buy only at extreme prices (below HKD 20), and accept it may not rise for a long time. This is not investing; it is a bet.
