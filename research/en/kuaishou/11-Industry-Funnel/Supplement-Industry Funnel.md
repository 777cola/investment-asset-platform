## First-round screen: can it be bought

Three of the six cannot be bought: Douyin, Xiaohongshu and Hongguo are all unlisted. This round of elimination is not because they are bad, but because they cannot be bought — and a good company you cannot buy means nothing to a portfolio.

Tencent can be bought, but WeChat Channels accounts for only a small share of its revenue; buying Tencent means paying 30x PE for an 8x PE business plus a WeChat Channels option. Bilibili can be bought, but its scale is an order of magnitude smaller than Kuaishou's; ad growth is faster but profitability is weaker.

What remains is Kuaishou: it is the only Hong Kong-listed pure play on short video + livestreaming + AI video. It won this round on scarcity, not on quality.

## Second-round screen: growth and profitability

Compare the three investable names side by side: Kuaishou revenue +1.4%, adjusted net profit -30.3%; Bilibili's advertising grows faster but net margin is near zero; Tencent's Video Accounts grows fast but is diluted by the parent's size.

If the standard is "growth + profitability," none of the three passes. If the standard is "cash flow + valuation," Tencent is best, Kuaishou second, Bilibili worst. If the standard is "pure sector beta," Kuaishou is best.

This is the awkwardness of the funnel: Kuaishou ranks first on no single dimension. It is simply irreplaceable on "purity."

## Final selection: why this one, and why not the others

Why it is the one: ① The only pure-play in Hong Kong; institutions wanting short-video exposure have no other option; ② 412 million DAU is a hard asset; after the fall to HKD 126.7 billion, EV is only RMB 91.8 billion, effectively giving away an AI business for free; ③ Kling is the only asset in the global top tier of video generation with independently priced financing.

Why it is not the one: ① Growth ranks last and the company itself guides to pressure in the second half; ② Margins are declining (gross margin -4.1pct, net margin from 16.0% to 11.0%); ③ Kling's valuation cannot be verified and accounts for only 2.4% of revenue.

The funnel's conclusion: "No better choice" does not equal "a good choice." Given it is the only option, the right move is to wait for price, not buy now.

## A note beyond the funnel: why valuation is not the first screen

Many investors cut valuation first in their funnel—keeping only the lowest PE names. That order is wrong, because low-valuation companies are often cheap precisely because their business is deteriorating. Kuaishou is a case in point: its 10.5x TTM PE looks low among Hong Kong internet stocks, but its forward 12.9x is higher than Tencent's and higher than Bilibili's.

The correct order is: first ask whether you can buy it (accessibility), then ask whether the business is improving or deteriorating (trend), and only then ask about price. Kuaishou's funnel end point stalls at step two—it is buyable, but should not be bought while the trend is down, no matter how cheap. This order also explains why "low valuation + high dividend yield" screens often conceal a pile of declining companies.

## If one must allocate within this sector

Ranking the three options: Tencent > Kuaishou > Bilibili. Tencent offers "cash flow + WeChat Channels optionality + AI applications (Yuanbao)," with downside protection; Kuaishou offers "pure beta + Kling optionality," with no downside protection; Bilibili offers "small-cap elasticity," but no earnings support.

If you can only buy Kuaishou, treat it as an option: small position, buy only at extreme prices (below HKD 20), and accept that it may not rise for a long time. This is not investing; it is a bet.
