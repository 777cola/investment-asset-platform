- Market cap：About HKD 126.7 billion

## Of the three criteria met, the quality differs

**Gross margin 51.6%: clears the bar but not comfortably.** The 40% threshold is set for manufacturing and retail; for an advertising platform, 51.6% is only mid-range, and the direction is down: -4.1pct means RMB 4.1 less earned per RMB 100 of revenue, or RMB 1.46 billion in a single quarter. The company attributes this to higher revenue-sharing costs, which translates to "the platform gave up more money to retain creators and streamers."

**Net cash of RMB 24.27 billion is the hardest of these seven items.** That is 20.9% of market cap, or about HKD 6.1 per share, with no near-term debt-servicing pressure. Note, however, that this is an aggregate "cash-like assets" measure (cash and equivalents, time deposits, wealth-management products), not free cash available in full for dividends.** Shareholder returns of HKD 880 million per quarter, or about HKD 3.5 billion annualized and 2.8% of market cap, are adequate, but were bought in at HKD 45.**

## Of the four that failed, only one is a real problem

R&D expense ratio of 12.9% (+3.2pct) fails, but that is a **choice**, not **deterioration**: of the RMB 4.6 billion, roughly RMB 1.2 billion of the increase went largely into AI training. A company raising investment while its core business stalls can be criticized for misjudgment, but that is a strategic disagreement, not poor financial quality.

Net margin of 11.0% fails; it is the arithmetic result of the preceding items, not a standalone problem. Revenue +1.4% is the real disease: **advertising +4.4% (below the consensus of 6.3%), livestreaming -13.5%, and only Kling +200%—while Kling is just 2.4% of revenue.** Strip out Kling and the remaining businesses are negative year on year in aggregate. A platform with 400 million DAU whose revenue stalls shows it cannot sell more advertising and cannot retain tippers.

The profit trend of -30.3% follows close behind. These two items only make sense together: revenue flat, profit down 30%, meaning operating leverage is working in reverse. Over the past three years the market's valuation logic for Kuaishou was "margin repair"; this hard metric failing means that logic has been disproven.

## One hidden indicator beyond the seven

Advertising revenue per DAU per quarter: 206 ÷ 4.12 = RMB 50.0; livestreaming revenue per DAU per quarter: 87 ÷ 4.12 = RMB 21.1, versus about RMB 24.6 in the same period last year (backed out from DAU of 408.7 million). **Ad revenue per user is rising, livestreaming is falling**—that is why total revenue still edges up while profit falls: advertising carries low marginal cost, livestreaming requires revenue sharing.

This structural shift is not bad in itself; the problem is that it is too small. Advertising adds RMB 2 per DAU per year, not enough to offset livestreaming's RMB 3.5 per DAU decline.

## Three conditions that will improve together

Of the four criteria not met, net margin and profit trend share the same root cause: both are squeezed between gross margin and R&D expense ratio. Run the sensitivity: every 1pct of gross margin recovery adds RMB 355 million to quarterly profit; every 1pct reduction in the R&D expense ratio adds the same RMB 355 million. Recover 3pct across the two, and quarterly adjusted net profit climbs from RMB 3.9 billion back above RMB 5 billion, clearing six of the seven criteria.

**So the question is not whether a recovery is hard, but whether the company wants one.** The AI spending was its own choice; the revenue-share concession was its own giveaway. Neither is an external shock. That means the timing of any recovery can only come from management, not from the industry cycle.
