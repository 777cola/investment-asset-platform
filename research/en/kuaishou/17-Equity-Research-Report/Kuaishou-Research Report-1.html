Investment summary

Kuaishou 2026Q2 revenue was RMB 35.535 billion, +1.4% YoY, with adjusted net profit of RMB 3.913 billion, -30.3% YoY. Within the three core business lines, advertising rose 4.4% while livestreaming fell 13.5%, and almost all incremental revenue came from Kling (RMB 850 million, 2.4% of total revenue). Our view: this is not an "AI transformation" story but a combination of "core business stalling + new business burning cash."

Our sum-of-the-parts valuation yields a neutral value of HKD 26.6, 9% below the October 12 close of HKD 29.26; bear case HKD 16, bull case HKD 46. Initiate at "Avoid."

I. Company Overview

Kuaishou began in 2011 as a GIF tool, pivoted to a short-video community in 2013 and listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in February 2021 (ticker 01024) at an offer price of HKD 115, raising about HKD 41.3 billion. As of Q2 2026, DAU stood at 412 million and MAU at 797 million, with revenue split across three lines: online marketing RMB 20.6 billion, live streaming RMB 8.7 billion and other services RMB 6.2 billion.

In July 2026, video-generation business Kling completed a USD 3 billion capital increase at a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion, cutting Kuaishou's stake to 83.3%. It is the company's biggest structural change in five years—its most imaginative asset now carries a separate price.

Market cap is currently HKD 126.7 billion, with P/E (TTM) at about 10.5x. The share price is down 64.7% from its 52-week high of HKD 82.95 and sits just 2.5% above its low of HKD 28.54.

On ownership, total share capital is 4.33 billion shares under a dual-class structure, with Class A shares carrying 10 votes each and the founder retaining control. The revenue mix has shifted fundamentally over five years: at the 2021 listing, live-streaming tips accounted for more than 40% of revenue; in Q2 2026, advertising was 58.0%, live streaming 24.5% and other services 17.5%. The company has gone from a "tipping company" to an "advertising company"—the first layer of context for understanding its valuation today.

II. Industry analysis

China's short-video industry has shifted from growth to a stock market. The overall advertising market is growing around 6% in 2026, while short-video advertising has fallen from 40%+ in 2021 to single digits. Livestream tipping is being squeezed by both regulation and trade-down consumption, and the entire industry is shrinking; livestream e-commerce growth has dropped from 30%+ to around 10%, and platforms have begun shifting traffic toward shelf-based commerce.

The only growth area is AI video. 2026 marks the second year of commercialization for global video generation, with ByteDance Seedance, Kuaishou Kling, Google Veo and Runway in the first tier. The economics of this track are "compute for revenue": every generation carries inference cost, and gross margin depends on chips and inference optimization—the opposite of advertising's "marginal cost approaching zero."

Advertiser structure determines its cyclical nature. Performance ads and e-commerce merchant spending account for the bulk of Kuaishou's advertising revenue, with brand advertising a low share. Performance ads are judged on ROI, brand ads on tone—the former is cut first when the macro weakens, the latter is more stable. This structure explains why its advertising growth is so sensitive to consumer sentiment, and why it has never captured Douyin's share of brand budgets.

III. Business model

Kuaishou is at heart an attention wholesaler: it sources content and creator supply upstream, then sells attention downstream to advertisers and merchants. Four monetisation streams: advertising (RMB 20.6 billion, auction-priced), livestreaming (RMB 8.7 billion, tipping revenue share), e-commerce commission (consolidated into other services) and Kling (RMB 850 million, subscription plus usage-based).

Unit economics: each DAU contributes RMB 86 per quarter (advertising 50, livestreaming 21, other 15), or RMB 344 annualised. That is low for the industry, reflecting a dual gap in user spending power and advertising product capability.

Kling is a different business: customers shift from advertisers to enterprises, revenue from auctions to subscriptions, costs from bandwidth to compute. The two share a brand but not a set of economics.

Breaking down the cost structure: revenue sharing is the largest item (livestreaming payouts, creator incentives, short-drama revenue splits), followed by bandwidth and servers, then content procurement and personnel. Revenue sharing is a variable cost — the more users and the stronger the creators, the faster it rises. This is the fundamental reason Kuaishou's gross margin is only 51.6% while a pure advertising platform can reach 80%. Scale is not a cure-all in this business.

One easily overlooked revenue characteristic: Kuaishou's ad inventory is "self-produced" and does not depend on external web pages the way search does. The upside is stable supply; the downside is that the inventory ceiling equals user time spent — if time spent does not grow, ad inventory caps out and only pricing can drive growth.

IV. Financial analysis

Revenue RMB 35.535 billion (+1.4%), gross profit RMB 18.34 billion (-6.0%), gross margin 51.6% (-4.1pct). R&D RMB 4.6 billion (+34.7%, expense ratio 12.9%), adjusted EBITDA RMB 7.1 billion (-7.7%), adjusted net profit RMB 3.913 billion (-30.3%), reported net profit RMB 3.152 billion (-36.0%).

One key subtraction: revenue increment RMB 495 million, Kling increment RMB 567 million, all businesses excluding Kling combined -RMB 70 million. On the profit side, gross profit down RMB 1.18 billion plus R&D up RMB 1.185 billion equals RMB 2.36 billion — the entire source of the RMB 1.7 billion net profit decline, with the remainder offset by expense compression and interest income.

Asset side: cash-like assets RMB 24.27 billion, 20.9% of market cap, capex down to RMB 5.9 billion. On accounting quality, all R&D is expensed, with no major capitalisation to flatter results, but disclosure has also narrowed (GMV no longer disclosed, Kling not broken out).

The three expense ratios total about 35%: sales and marketing about 20% (compressing), R&D 12.9% (rising), administrative about 2%. The shift in expense structure since 2024 is clear — money saved on marketing has moved into R&D. This is not cost cutting to lift efficiency; it is resource reallocation. Saving on marketing can improve a single quarter's profit but weakens user growth; raising R&D drags on current profit and bets on three years out.

On cash flow, adjusted EBITDA of RMB 7.1 billion less capex of RMB 5.9 billion gives roughly RMB 1.2 billion of free cash flow. That number is not high, but it is positive. For a company funneling cash into AI, self-funding is the floor, and Kuaishou has held that line.

V. Management

Cheng Yixiao took over as CEO in October 2021. A product man by background, his most important call in the role was betting on video generation in June 2024, building Kling to USD 500 million ARR and a post-money valuation of USD 18 billion in two years. That call deserves credit.

The capital allocation record, however, is poor: in 2026Q2 he bought back HKD 880 million of stock at an average price of about HKD 45, versus HKD 29.26 now, a 35% unrealized loss; no cash dividend has been paid since listing; and under the dual-class structure the founder holds control while minority shareholders lack checks and balances.

Verdict: credible on strategy, not credible on capital allocation. The former determines whether one is willing to buy at the right price; the latter determines not buying at this price.

On the organization side, three restructurings since 2023: commercialization merged with e-commerce, local life made independent, and the Kling team spun out into a subsidiary reporting directly to the CEO. The common thread is concentrating resources on the battlefields that can tell a story. The effect cuts both ways: Kling broke out, while the core advertising product team changed leaders twice in two years.

VI. Growth drivers

Four potential drivers, ranked by certainty: ① ad load and eCPM uplift (medium certainty, 20%–30% upside, but capped by user spending power); ② e-commerce commission rate uplift and shelf-based shopping (medium certainty, but GMV is no longer disclosed and cannot be verified); ③ Kling commercialization (low certainty, large upside, RMB 3.4 billion annualized; at RMB 15 billion in three years it would be equivalent to recreating half of Kuaishou); ④ overseas Kwai (low certainty, small scale, a free option).

Of the four, only the first can be verified within the next four quarters, which is why we treat ad growth as the core metric to watch.

Ad load is self-limiting. Kuaishou's feed ad load sits below Douyin's, so there is theoretical room to raise it, but each point of load costs a little user time — ad load and time spent are inversely related, and no solution raises load without hurting the experience. That is also why ad growth can only come from eCPM and advertiser count, not from more ad slots.

VII. Competitive landscape

Four-front squeeze: Douyin takes brand budgets and e-commerce merchants (across-the-board pressure), WeChat Channels takes social scenarios and older users, Hongguo short dramas take watch time and short-drama creators, and Xiaohongshu takes high-ticket seeding budgets.

Kuaishou's position is "the second choice in lower-tier markets": cheaper than Douyin, more authentic than Channels, more social than Hongguo. That position can hold 400 million DAU, but it cannot hold ad unit price gains.

In the AI video race, Kling faces ByteDance's Seedance and a price war already under way. Its rival's cash flow is an order of magnitude deeper — the type of opponent Kuaishou least wants to face.

User overlap is the backdrop to this competition. Kuaishou and Douyin overlap by more than 60%, meaning the same pool of people is contested by two platforms. In markets with high overlap, price wars are inevitable, because advertisers need only compare prices once between the two. Channels and Hongguo overlap less, but what they carve away is "scenarios" and "watch time" — equally lethal.

Hongguo deserves separate mention. It is not short video but free short drama, yet it competes for the same hours. Rivals of this kind — "different form, same purpose" — are the hardest to defend against, because you cannot counter with the same product; you can only launch a similar one, and following means burning another pile of cash.

VIII. Risk factors

① Core business decline risk: If ad growth turns negative, the valuation framework shifts from P/E to P/S or EV/EBITDA, implying a share price below HKD 18. ② AI investment with no return risk: R&D plus capex exceeds RMB 40 billion annually against Kling's annualised revenue of just RMB 3.4 billion — the input-output ratio is unbalanced. ③ Spin-off governance risk: Kling's losses stay on the consolidated statements while its valuation sits outside minority shareholders, so the parent's profit may be hit twice. ④ Valuation anchor break risk: If the USD 18 billion primary-market price is marked down to USD 8 billion, the parent's equity value falls by about HKD 64.6 billion. ⑤ Regulatory risk: Tipping limits, algorithm filing and AI content labelling are all tightening.

These five risks are not independent; they share a single factor — advertising budgets. In a macro downturn, advertisers cut budgets, merchants trim spend and AI clients reduce subscriptions, so all five deteriorate at once. Kuaishou's downside is therefore not linear but stepwise. Calculating expected value from single-risk probabilities understates it; a correlation adjustment is required.

Nine, Earnings Forecast

Assumption 1: advertising +3% in 2026 and +2% in 2027 (base case), -3% bear case, +8% bull case. Assumption 2: livestreaming -10% in both 2026 and 2027. Assumption 3: other services +20% in 2026 (Kling at RMB 4 billion annualised) and +25% in 2027 (Kling at RMB 6 billion).

This gives 2026E revenue of about RMB 145 billion (+2%) and adjusted net profit of RMB 8.99 billion (consensus); 2027E base-case revenue of RMB 148 billion and net profit of RMB 9 billion, a net margin of 6.1% — profit no longer declines, but it no longer grows either; this is the most likely steady state.

Bear case net profit of RMB 5.5 billion (advertising turns negative and Kling falls behind), bull case RMB 11 billion (advertising +8% and Kling breaks even).

Sensitivity: each 1pct change in advertising growth moves 2027E adjusted net profit by about RMB 800–900 million (rough calculation based on a 58% revenue share × 25% net margin contribution); each 1pct change in gross margin moves net profit by about RMB 1.4 billion. Net profit is more sensitive to gross margin than to revenue growth — which explains why the market reacts more sharply to "rising revenue-share costs" than to "slowing revenue growth."

X. Valuation

Sum-of-the-parts: core business at 7–10x 2027E adjusted net profit (China ADR advertising platforms trade at 8–12x; we take the low end given negative growth); Kling at 0 in the base case, and in the bull case at 50% of the USD 18 billion post-money valuation less a further 10% discount, or RMB 48 billion.

Valuation: bear case HKD 16, base case HKD 26.6, bull case HKD 46. Base-case target market cap HKD 115 billion, 9% below the current HKD 126.7 billion.

Reverse check: deduct base-case value from the current price and the HKD 11.7 billion residual is what the market is paying for Kling — 9% of the USD 18 billion post-money valuation. The market is not paying an AI premium; it is applying an AI discount.

Cross-check on three metrics: P/E 10.5x (TTM)/12.9x (forward); EV/EBITDA about 3.2x (EV RMB 91.8 billion ÷ annualised EBITDA RMB 28.4 billion); P/S 0.82x (market cap RMB 116 billion ÷ annualised revenue RMB 142.1 billion). All three say "cheap," and all three rest on the assumption that profit stops declining. Cheap and safe are not the same thing.

Kling is the biggest controversy in the sum-of-the-parts. We use 0 or a 50% discount because its valuation comes from the private market and lacks a listed-comparable anchor. If Kling lists independently, the market will assign it a real multiple and Kuaishou becomes "a holding company that owns a Kling stake" — the entire valuation approach would need to be rewritten.

XI. Investment recommendation

Rating: Avoid. Target price HKD 26.6 (Neutral). Three reasons we do not assign a Buy: core business growth of 1.4% with guidance under pressure; Keling's valuation is unverifiable and the unit is only 2.4% of revenue; forward P/E of 12.9x sits above the 10.5x TTM multiple.

Triggers for reassessment: ① online marketing YoY ≥8% for two consecutive quarters; ② Keling discloses P&L on a comparable basis with annualised revenue ≥RMB 6 billion; ③ share price falls below HKD 20 (7x 2027E + net cash). The first two are fundamental triggers; the third is a price trigger.

The time frame matters: this is a 2–3 year observation, not a 3-month trade. The nearest validation point is the November Q3 report — advertising growth back above 6% would support raising the neutral valuation to HKD 30, while a turn negative would put the bear case in play directly. Moves in between carry no information.

For existing holders the action is not "stop-loss" but "re-decide": assuming you were flat today, would you still buy at 29.26? If not, holding is equivalent to making a "buy" decision every day. That question is more useful than any target price.

Conclusion

Kuaishou's problem is not valuation; it is the business. A platform with 400 million DAU whose revenue growth has slowed to 1.4%, whose profit is down 30%, and whose incremental growth rests entirely on a new business accounting for 2.4% of revenue—that combination does not justify a buy at any multiple.

We assign a neutral value of HKD 26.6, 9% below the current price, and a bear-case value of HKD 16, which assumes advertising turns negative. Until two verifiable signals appear (advertising at 8%, Kling at RMB 6 billion annualized), no price justifies a buy.
