Core view

The income statement is the star pupil, the cash flow statement is the alarm, and the balance sheet is the explanation. Net profit RMB 13.65 billion, operating cash flow RMB 1.80 billion; receivables RMB 15.00 billion, inventory RMB 19.83 billion; total assets RMB 68.94 billion, debt-to-asset ratio 42.2%. All three statements say the same thing: in its expansion phase, this business must lay out cash first and wait for customers to pay.

Income statement: impeccable

RMB 41.78 billion revenue, RMB 13.65 billion net profit, 32.68% net margin, 37.62% weighted ROE (half-year). Quarterly looks smoother: Q1 RMB 19.50 billion, Q2 RMB 22.28 billion, +14.3% QoQ; net profit RMB 5.74 billion → RMB 7.92 billion, +38.0% QoQ. Profit growth QoQ is more than double revenue growth, indicating the share of high-priced products continued to rise in Q2.

Cost side is also healthy: R&D expense RMB 1.15 billion, +96.9% YoY; G&A expense RMB 767 million (RMB 291 million in the year-ago period); selling expense very low — a B2B business needs no advertising. R&D doubled while net margin still rose 5.7 percentage points, showing scale effects outweigh expense growth. No problem at this layer.

Cash flow statement: what does a net cash ratio of 13% mean?

Net profit RMB 13.651 billion, operating cash flow RMB 1.800 billion, net cash ratio 13.2% versus 80% in the year-ago period. Where did the RMB 11.85 billion gap go? Two main pieces: inventories up RMB 7.145 billion and receivables up RMB 8.724 billion, totaling RMB 15.87 billion, partly offset by non-cash items such as long-term payables.

Buffett would press two questions. First, was this voluntary or forced? The evidence leans voluntary: long-term payables rose about RMB 4.16 billion and prepayments about RMB 950 million in the half, both moves to lock in materials early; inventories of RMB 19.826 billion equal roughly 160 days of cost of sales, clearly above normal levels. Second, can it be collected? That depends on downstream pull — the customers are North American cloud providers with sound credit, but production schedules can shift.

The comparison with 2025 makes it clearer: full-year 2025 operating cash flow RMB 10.896 billion, net profit RMB 10.797 billion, net cash ratio 101%, up 244.31% year on year. The same company scored full marks on cash flow a year ago and 13 six months later — the variable is not operating capability but the pace of inventory build and payment terms.

Balance sheet: where does the 42.2% debt ratio come from?

Total assets RMB 68.94 billion (+52.23% from the start of the year), net assets attributable to parent RMB 39.86 billion (+33.91%), implying liabilities of RMB 29.08 billion and a debt-to-asset ratio of 42.2%, up 12 percentage points from 30.18% at end-2025. The increase came mainly not from bank borrowings but from long-term payables (about RMB 5.06 billion), accounts payable and notes payable.

This cuts both ways. The good: using suppliers' money to build inventory is effectively interest-free upstream financing, showing bargaining power on procurement (the half-year purchase scale speaks for itself). The bad: that money turns into cost on delivery in 2027, and if demand reverses, prepaid materials become a burden. Goodwill of RMB 1.94 billion (from the 2017 acquisition of Suzhou InnoLight) is a small share and poses no risk.

Once the roughly HKD 53.4 billion (about RMB 49 billion) raised in the H-share offering lands on 30 July, the debt ratio will fall markedly and the cash on hand will suffice to fund the 2027 capacity expansion. That money is both ammunition and evidence that internal cash generation is insufficient — both are true at once.

Conclusion

Financial conclusion: a business that must front cash during expansion—the financial risk lies not in debt service but in inventory realization. A 42.2% liability ratio is not high, interest-bearing debt is minimal, and cash is ample after the H-share financing; the real risk is RMB 19.8 billion of inventory and RMB 15 billion of receivables turning into impairment and bad debt at the same time when demand inflects.One metric is enough to watch: the net cash ratio in the third-quarter report.
