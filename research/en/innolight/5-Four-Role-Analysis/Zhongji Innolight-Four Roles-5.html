Core view

Four independent analyses reach the same conclusion for different reasons. They agree that the company is the industry leader, that the 1.6T volume ramp represents real earnings, and that the 53x P/E (TTM) already discounts 2027. They diverge on three points: how long gross margin can hold, whether cash flow reflects inventory build for components or channel stuffing, and the CPO timeline. Base case gross margin 42%, cash flow pending verification in the Q3 report, CPO impact after 2028. Action: 0% position, conditional order at RMB 700.

Four sources agree on a Hold: the call is not a negative view on the company

None of the four reports argues the company is a bad one. The commercial view derives per-unit gross profit of RMB 637 → 1,015; the financial view confirms a 2025 net cash ratio of 101%; the industry view confirms a 23% global share and about 50% in 800G silicon photonics; the risk view confirms it survived two downturns. By any measure, this is a company that has passed the "good company" test.

All four pause for the same reason: the price has already used up the good news. RMB 925.24 = 53.3x P/E (TTM) = 35x 2026E P/E. The neutral case of RMB 772 sits 17% below the current price, the bear case of RMB 407 is 56% below, and the bull case of RMB 1,131 offers only 22% upside. This risk-reward structure needs no debate; arithmetic alone rejects it.

Ruling 1: gross margin, use 42% rather than 46%

The industry camp expects a return to 38% by 2027, on the view that Eoptolink's 1.6T entry triggers a price war; the commercial camp expects 42% to hold, with 1.6T the mainstay through 2027 and a fresh premium when 3.2T takes over. We side with 42%: it acknowledges both that the 1.6T window is longer than 800G and that rivals are closing the gap fast.

The basis is one detail from 2026H1: Eoptolink's gross margin of 48.44% already exceeds Innolight's 46.4%. If rivals' pricing capability is no weaker, 46% cannot be an industry steady state — only a window reading. Using 42% as the neutral assumption leaves a margin of safety.

Ruling 2: Cash flow — wait for the Q3 report rather than judging now

The financial role built the evidence chain for "proactive stockpiling": long-term payables +RMB 4.16 billion (materials locked in for over a year), prepayments RMB 950 million, inventory RMB 19.826 billion. The risk role countered that the evidence only shows "materials were bought," not that "they can be sold." Both are right.

Verdict: do not prejudge; set observation points. Three numbers in the Q3 report will settle it — whether the net cash ratio returns above 50%, whether inventory falls quarter-on-quarter, and whether receivables growth is below revenue growth. If two of the three hit, the stockpiling thesis holds; if none hit, the channel-stuffing thesis holds. These are the validation nodes for the holding thesis (see folder 13).

Ruling 3: Neither CPO nor H-shares changes the current-period conclusion

CPO: Nvidia's March 2026 Spectrum-X full mass-production announcement is real, but it displaces short-reach interconnect on the switch side, not all pluggables. Verdict: no earnings impact before 2027; after 2028 it caps the valuation multiple. What it affects, then, is the multiple investors will pay, not whether to buy.

H-shares: issued on July 30 at HKD 980, raising HKD 53.4 billion; the stock broke issue price on day one and closed near HKD 950, diluting about 5.6%. Verdict: on the commercial side, swapping to RMB 49 billion of ammunition is worth it; on the financial side, it is evidence of insufficient cash generation — both hold at once. The financing itself is neutral, but it nails down the hard metric of "+47% share count expansion."

Final action: 0% position, conditional order at RMB 700

Together the four reports yield three execution rules. First, do not build a position now, and do not short: a 53x P/E invites a short on valuation, but 241% growth can squeeze at any moment, and the risk-reward does not allow it. Second, start building below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027 P/E), in three tranches, adding one tranche for every RMB 50 decline. Third, below RMB 600 offers a margin of safety (about 20x), where the position can be raised to 3%–5% of target.

The early-trigger conditions are also spelled out: if the Q3 report shows net cash flow to net profit back above 50%, the sequential average selling price of a single unit has not fallen, and the stock is still below RMB 800, you may skip waiting for RMB 700 and build one-third first. Reverse conditions: if customer concentration rises above 85%, or the sequential average selling price of 1.6T products falls more than 8%, cancel the buy plan and downgrade to avoid.

Conclusion

Team lead conclusion: Watch, wait for a pullback, 0% position. All four perspectives agree the company is good; where they diverge does nothing to change "don't buy now." RMB 700 is the first-tier entry price, RMB 600 the margin-of-safety price, and the Q3 report's net cash ratio the only key to entering early. Until all three conditions are met, any purchase means paying 53x P/E for someone else's capex budget.
