Core view

In one line: what it sells is not modules but "transmission cost per bit." Customers demand faster speeds every 18 months; whoever ships first and runs at lower power per watt captures a 45% gross margin in that generation. Zhongji Innolight sold 18.99 million units in 2026H1 at an average price of RMB 2,178 each, with gross profit of about RMB 1,015 per unit — that is a speed-tier windfall, not a cost advantage.

Nature of the business: selling speed, not components

When Duan Yongping asks about a business, his first question is always "what problem does this thing solve." Optical modules solve data movement between racks and between clusters: electrical signals cannot run beyond ten meters, so they must be converted to light. Customers buy them for one reason only—the same bandwidth at lower power, lower cost and more reliable lead times. So the essence of this business is selling the price-performance of "speed/power," not selling components.

This determines three things. First, price is set by speed, not cost-plus: the average unit price of 1.6T is RMB 2,178, nearly double that of 800G, because what customers save is the number of switch and fiber ports. Second, annual price declines are the norm, an industry convention of 3%–5% per year, offset by new products. Third, whoever reaches mass production first captures the premium; two quarters late and you can only accept the price.

Together, these three features amount to "technology-generation rent." Zhongji Innolight's 46.59% gross margin in 2026H1 is essentially the generation rent on 1.6T; the 39.96% in 2025H1 was the rent on 800G. Rent expires, so you have to win it again every year.

How gross profit per unit rose from RMB 637 to RMB 1,015

Breaking it down is simple: average price rose 36.6%, gross margin rose 6.63 percentage points; multiply the two and gross profit per unit rose 59.3%, from about RMB 637 to about RMB 1,015. Volume also doubled (18.99 million units), so total gross profit went from RMB 5.77 billion to RMB 19.27 billion, up 234%.

This is the best growth pattern—volume, price and margin all rising together, without relying on price cuts to grab share. Duan Yongping would acknowledge this as the result of "doing things right": betting correctly on 1.6T, locking in materials early, expanding across five locations—miss any one link and you don't get this number. He would also add a warning: volume, price and margin can only rise together in a window of demand exceeding supply; once the window closes, all that's left is volume up, price down, margin down.

Is this a good business: Duan Yongping's three questions

Question one: who is the customer? The answer is not flattering: 5 customers generate 81.9% of revenue, the largest 25.1%, and overseas accounts for 94.8%. This is not a to-C business; it is a business to five procurement departments. Customer orientation here means "follow these five companies' roadmaps" — there is no second path.

Question two: will it still be here in ten years? Most likely yes, but whether it will be this company with this gross margin is uncertain. The leader changes with every generation of optical modules; this company got its entry ticket through the 2017 acquisition of Suzhou InnoLight and stayed at the table by betting twice, on 800G and 1.6T. It has proven it can win; it has not proven it can keep winning.

Question three: at what price do you buy? Duan Yongping's answer is to wait for the moment "nobody wants it." At 53.3x TTM PE and 35x 2026E PE, this is the moment "everyone wants it." He would put this company on his watchlist and revisit when gross margin returns to around 35% and sell-side reports start writing "cyclical downturn."

Conclusion

Business conclusion: a business that collects rent on a technology gap — rent charged per generation, not per year.Zhongji Innolight is one of the most efficient rent collectors in this industry, but its rent is not a perpetual title. A Duan Yongping-style move: acknowledge the excellence, wait for the cycle low, and do not buy at RMB 925.
