## I. Company overview: from motor equipment to the world's top optical module maker

Zhongji Innolight (300308.SZ) listed on ChiNext in April 2012 as a maker of motor winding equipment. In September 2017 it acquired 100% of Suzhou InnoLight for RMB 2.8 billion in shares, reorienting its core business to high-speed optical transceiver modules and booking goodwill of RMB 1.939 billion. On 30 July 2026 it listed on the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (03308.HK), raising approximately HKD 53.4 billion and creating a dual A+H platform.

Wang Weixiu is the actual controller; Liu Sheng, founder of Suzhou InnoLight, serves as chairman and president. Capacity spans five sites: Suzhou, Tongling, Chengdu, Taiwan and Thailand. Optical module shipments reached 21.09 million units in 2025 (+44.6%), giving a global share of about 23% and the number one ranking; its global share of 800G silicon photonic modules is about 50%. Shipments totalled 18.99 million units in 2026H1 on revenue of RMB 41.778 billion, 94.8% of which was overseas.

## II. Industry analysis: a USD 14.6 billion high-end market, with growth slowing in 2027

The global 800G and 1.6T optical module market totals about USD 14.6 billion in 2026, 64% of the overall optical module market; 800G shipments reach 33.5 million units, 1.6T demand 8.6 million to 20 million units (LightCounting, 2026/09). Demand is driven by capex at the four major North American cloud providers, which totalled USD 164.9 billion in 2026Q2, up 86% YoY.

The key variable for the industry is the speed generation: 800G ramps in 2024–2025, 1.6T in 2026, and 3.2T is expected in 2027–2028.Each generation's price curve follows a "premium first, annual decline later" pattern, with annual declines of 3%–5%; whether gross margins hold depends on whether the next generation takes over in time. Industry growth is expected to slow from the doubling level of 2026 to 20%–30% in 2027.

## III. Business model: design and packaging capture rent from the process-node gap

The company sources optical chips (EML/CW) and electrical chips (DSP), designs its own optical path, and handles optical coupling, packaging and testing before selling modules to cloud providers and equipment vendors. It does not fabricate wafers, so capex stays relatively light (construction in progress of RMB 3.707 billion for the half-year), underpinning a high ROE (37.62% weighted for the half-year).

Profit derives from "technology-generation rent": when 1.6T was in short supply, average price per unit was RMB 2,178 at a 46.59% gross margin; in the 800G era (2025H1) it was RMB 1,595 at 39.96%. Rent is collected per generation, not per year, so the company must keep investing in R&D (2026H1 R&D expense RMB 1.153 billion, +96.9%) to stay a generation ahead.

## IV. Financial analysis: top-tier profit, tight cash

2026H1 revenue RMB 41.78 billion, net profit RMB 13.65 billion, gross margin 46.4%, net margin 32.7%. By quarter, Q2 revenue RMB 22.28 billion (+14.3% QoQ) and net profit RMB 7.92 billion (+38.0% QoQ); profit outgrew revenue, pointing to a further rise in the mix of high-priced products.

Cash flow is the weak spot: operating cash flow RMB 1.80 billion (−44.1%), net cash ratio 13.2%; inventory RMB 19.83 billion (+56.3%), receivables RMB 15.00 billion (+138.98%). Cash went into materials, capacity and payment terms. Full-year 2025 net cash ratio was 101% and operating cash flow RMB 10.90 billion, so the model itself generates cash; 2026H1 is a one-period anomaly driven by stockpiling.

Balance sheet: total assets RMB 68.94 billion, net assets attributable to parent RMB 39.86 billion, liability ratio 42.2% (30.18% at end-2025), low interest-bearing debt, long-term payables RMB 5.06 billion. With H-share proceeds received, cash is ample and solvency risk is negligible; the main risk is inventory realization.

## V. Management: technical team holds sway, capital allocation skews to expansion

Liu Sheng (born 1971, PhD) was an R&D engineer at Lucent, headed Pine Photonics' China R&D centre and served as a senior manager at Opnext before founding Suzhou InnoLight in 2008. He became president of the listed company in 2017 and chairman and president in August 2023. 2025 compensation RMB 4.1834 million (+14.71%), holding 3.652 million shares.

The capital allocation throughline is financing to fund capacity expansion: a private placement in 2021 and HKD 53.4 billion raised via H shares in 2026, lifting total shares 47% over five years (800 million to 1.178 billion shares). Returns are improving: an interim dividend for 2026 of RMB 12 per 10 shares (about RMB 1.4 billion) and roughly RMB 5 billion of buybacks in August–September. Governance shows no hard flaws; in July 2026 the controlling shareholder and concert parties announced a change in shareholding exceeding 1% (including passive dilution and reductions).

## Six, growth drivers: three legs, none dispensable

Driver one: rising 1.6T penetration. 2026 1.6T demand stands at 8.6–20 million units, and the company is a major supplier at an average price of RMB 2,178 per unit, with volume and price rising together. Driver two: 3.2T takes over. 3.2T NPO and 6.4T NPO are fully developed, while 2.4T silicon photonics + lithium niobate can iterate to 3.2T and is compatible with OCS; as of April the status was still "capacity in preparation, samples not yet shipped", with revenue expected to contribute in 2027.

Driver three: higher silicon photonics mix. Silicon photonics reduces reliance on EML and raises integration, providing structural support to gross margin. The risk across these three legs is a timing mismatch: the 1.6T premium narrows in 2027, and if 3.2T slips by two quarters, a gross-margin gap opens in between.

## VII. Competitive landscape: number one, but not the only one

Global landscape: Zhongji Innolight (about 23%), Eoptolink (rose to second place in 2025), Coherent, Lumentum, Accelink, T&S Communications (upstream). 2026H1 financial comparison: Zhongji revenue RMB 41.78 billion, gross margin 46.4%; Eoptolink RMB 20.91 billion, 48.44%; T&S RMB 2.83 billion, 60.87%; Accelink RMB 6.61 billion, 25.53%.

Gross margin, not share, decides this competition: share leader Zhongji's gross margin sits below second-place Eoptolink, which shows the industry has no technological monopoly, only window pricing. Customers generally dual-source, so any one supplier's share has a ceiling. The most dangerous rival is Eoptolink (same customers, same-generation products, more aggressive on cost); the longest-term threat is CPO.

## 8. Risks to watch: five variables that demand attention

① Price war: Eoptolink's 1.6T pricing has fallen since mass production began; an 8% decline in ASP implies a 4-5pct hit to gross margin.② Customer concentration: Top five customers account for 81.9% and the largest for 25.1%; a 20% order cut from a single customer would cost 5% of revenue.③ CPO substitution: Nvidia's Spectrum-X CPO entered full mass production in March 2026 and could capture 20% of new interconnect after 2028.

④ Upstream bottleneck: The largest supplier accounts for 38.3% of procurement; optical and electrical chips are sourced overseas, leaving lead times and pricing beyond the company's control.⑤ Inventory impairment: RMB 19.826 billion of inventory, equal to roughly 160 days of turnover — the largest one-off loss should demand reverse.Three secondary risks remain: FX (94.8% of revenue overseas), geopolitics (US over 60%), and further share dilution.

## 9. Earnings forecast: RMB 31bn in 2026, RMB 35bn in 2027

2026: H1 net profit already at RMB 13.651 billion. Assuming modest QoQ growth in Q3 and Q4 (Q3 about RMB 8.2 billion, Q4 about RMB 9.1 billion), full-year net profit of about RMB 31 billion, revenue of about RMB 94 billion, net margin 33%.

2027: We assume 1.6T shipments keep growing but ASP falls 5%–8% a year, 3.2T contributes at small volume, and gross margin retreats to 42%. Net profit RMB 35 billion, revenue RMB 115 billion, net margin 30.4%. Bear case (price war plus capex growth slowing to 20%): net profit RMB 24 billion; bull case (capex again beats expectations): RMB 43 billion.

The key assumption behind the forecast is a 42% gross margin, 4.4 percentage points below the 46.4% of 2026H1 — a move of that size has happened within a single quarter historically (2025Q2 gross margin +4.79pct QoQ), so this is neutral, not conservative.

## X. Valuation: 26x 2027 net profit, RMB 772

Basis for 26x 2027 P/E: ① growth remains high (+187% in 2026) but is unsustainable; ② the moat is speed-based rather than structural; ③ high customer concentration warrants a discount; ④ peers trade at roughly 40x H1 annualised P/E (Eoptolink) and roughly 126x (TFC).

Three scenarios: bear case RMB 24 billion × 20x = RMB 407; base case RMB 35 billion × 26x = RMB 772; bull case RMB 43 billion × 31x = RMB 1,131. The current RMB 925.24 implies 35.2x 2026E, 31.1x 2027E and 53.3x TTM. On TTM multiples Zhongji trades above Eoptolink (40x H1 annualised); the premium reflects its leadership position, but the magnitude is already excessive.

## 11. Investment recommendation: Watch (Neutral); wait for a pullback below RMB 700

Rating: Watch (Neutral). 12-month target price RMB 772, 17% downside from RMB 925.24. We would not build a position at the current price on three grounds: a three-scenario payoff of −56%/+22%, a net cash ratio of 13.2% and customer concentration of 81.9%.

Entry points: below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027E), first tranche 3%; below RMB 600 (about 20x), add to 5%; single-name cap 6%. Preconditions: Q3 net cash ratio ≥50% and share price Abandon conditions: net cash ratio 8% QoQ, customer concentration >85%.

Why "Watch" rather than "Avoid": this company's problems are entirely price problems, not business problems. Once valuation returns to 20–23x, it is one of the most holdable names in this chain.
