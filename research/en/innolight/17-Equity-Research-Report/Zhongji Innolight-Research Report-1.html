I. Company overview: from motor equipment to the world's top optical module maker

Zhongji Innolight (300308.SZ) listed on the ChiNext board in April 2012, originally focused on motor winding equipment. In September 2017 it acquired 100% of Suzhou InnoLight via a RMB 2.8 billion share issuance, shifting its core business to high-speed optical transceiver modules, with goodwill of RMB 1.939 billion. On 30 July 2026 it listed on the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (03308.HK), raising approximately HKD 53.4 billion, forming a dual A+H platform.

The company's actual controller is Wang Weixiu; the chairman and president is Liu Sheng (founder of Suzhou InnoLight). Production capacity is spread across five locations: Suzhou, Tongling, Chengdu, Taiwan and Thailand. In 2025, optical module shipments reached 21.09 million units (+44.6%), with a global share of about 23%, ranking first; global share of 800G silicon photonic modules is about 50%. In 2026H1, shipments were 18.99 million units and revenue was RMB 41.778 billion, of which overseas accounted for 94.8%.

II. Industry analysis: a USD 14.6 billion high-end market, with growth slowing in 2027

The global 800G and 1.6T optical module market totals about USD 14.6 billion in 2026, 64% of the overall optical module market; 800G shipments reach 33.5 million units, 1.6T demand 8.6 million to 20 million units (LightCounting, 2026/09). On the demand side, capex from the four major North American cloud providers drives the market, totaling USD 164.9 billion in 2026Q2, up 86% year on year.

The key variable for the industry is the speed generation: 800G ramps in 2024–2025, 1.6T in 2026, and 3.2T is expected in 2027–2028.Each generation's price curve follows a "premium first, annual decline later" pattern, with annual declines of 3%–5%; whether gross margins hold depends on whether the next generation takes over in time. Industry growth is expected to slow from the doubling level of 2026 to 20%–30% in 2027.

III. Business model: design and packaging, earning rent from the process-node gap

The company buys optical chips (EML/CW) and electrical chips (DSP), handles its own optical path design, optical coupling, packaging and testing, and sells modules to cloud providers and equipment vendors. It does not manufacture wafers, so capex is relatively light (construction in progress of RMB 3.707 billion for the half-year), which keeps ROE high (37.62% weighted for the half-year).

Profit comes from "technology-generation rent": when 1.6T was in short supply, average price per unit was RMB 2,178 with a 46.59% gross margin; in the 800G era (2025H1) it was RMB 1,595 and 39.96%. Rent is collected per generation, not per year, so the company must keep investing in R&D (2026H1 R&D expense RMB 1.153 billion, +96.9%) to stay a generation ahead.

IV. Financial analysis: top-tier profit, tight cash

2026H1 revenue RMB 41.78 billion, net profit RMB 13.65 billion, gross margin 46.4%, net margin 32.7%. By quarter, Q2 revenue RMB 22.28 billion (+14.3% QoQ), net profit RMB 7.92 billion (+38.0% QoQ); profit grew faster than revenue, indicating a continued rise in the share of high-priced products.

Cash flow is the weak spot: operating cash flow RMB 1.80 billion (−44.1%), net cash ratio 13.2%; inventory RMB 19.83 billion (+56.3%), receivables RMB 15.00 billion (+138.98%). Cash turned into materials, capacity and payment terms. Full-year 2025 net cash ratio was 101% and operating cash flow RMB 10.90 billion, showing the model itself generates cash; 2026H1 is a one-period anomaly caused by stockpiling.

Balance sheet: total assets RMB 68.94 billion, net assets attributable to parent RMB 39.86 billion, liability ratio 42.2% (30.18% at end-2025), low interest-bearing debt, long-term payables RMB 5.06 billion. With H-share proceeds received, cash is ample and solvency risk is negligible; the main risk is inventory realization.

V. Management: technical team holds power, capital allocation skews toward expansion

Liu Sheng (born 1971, PhD) served as an R&D engineer at Lucent, head of Pine Photonics' China R&D center and senior manager at Opnext; he founded Suzhou InnoLight in 2008, became president of the listed company in 2017, and chairman and president in August 2023. 2025 compensation RMB 4.1834 million (+14.71%), holding 3.652 million shares.

The capital allocation throughline is financing to expand capacity: a private placement in 2021 and HKD 53.4 billion raised via H shares in 2026, with total shares up 47% over five years (800 million shares to 1.178 billion shares). Returns are improving: an interim dividend for 2026 of RMB 12 per 10 shares (about RMB 1.4 billion) and roughly RMB 5 billion of buybacks in August–September. Governance shows no hard flaws; the controlling shareholder and concert parties announced in July 2026 a change in shareholding exceeding 1% (including passive dilution and reductions).

Six, growth drivers: three legs, none dispensable

Driver one: rising 1.6T penetration. 2026 1.6T demand is 8.6–20 million units; the company is a major supplier at an average price of RMB 2,178 per unit, with volume and price rising together. Driver two: 3.2T takes over. 3.2T NPO and 6.4T NPO are fully developed; 2.4T silicon photonics + lithium niobate can iterate to 3.2T and is compatible with OCS; as of April the status was still "capacity in preparation, samples not yet shipped", with revenue expected to contribute in 2027.

Driver three: higher silicon photonics mix. Silicon photonics reduces reliance on EML and raises integration, providing structural support to gross margin. The risk across these three legs is a timing mismatch: the 1.6T premium narrows in 2027, and if 3.2T slips by two quarters, a gross-margin gap opens in between.

VII. Competitive landscape: number one, but not the only one

Global landscape: Zhongji Innolight (about 23%), Eoptolink (rose to second place in 2025), Coherent, Lumentum, Accelink, T&S Communications (upstream). 2026H1 financial comparison: Zhongji revenue RMB 41.78 billion, gross margin 46.4%; Eoptolink RMB 20.91 billion, 48.44%; T&S RMB 2.83 billion, 60.87%; Accelink RMB 6.61 billion, 25.53%.

The key to competition is not share, but gross margin: share leader Zhongji's gross margin is lower than second-place Eoptolink, showing that this industry has no technological monopoly, only window pricing. Customers generally use dual suppliers, so any one supplier's share has a ceiling. The most dangerous rival is Eoptolink (same customers, same-generation products, more aggressive on cost); the longest-term threat is CPO.

8. Risks to watch: five variables that demand attention

① Price war: Eoptolink's 1.6T pricing has fallen since mass production; an 8% drop in average selling price implies gross margin −4 to −5pct.② Customer concentration: Top five at 81.9%, largest at 25.1%; a 20% order cut from a single customer hits revenue by 5%.③ CPO substitution: Nvidia Spectrum-X CPO announced full mass production in March 2026; after 2028 it could take 20% of new interconnect.

④ Upstream bottleneck: Largest supplier accounts for 38.3% of procurement; optical and electrical chips rely on overseas sources, with lead times and prices beyond control.⑤ Inventory impairment: RMB 19.826 billion of inventory, roughly 160 days of turnover — the largest one-off loss if demand reverses.Three secondary risks remain: FX (94.8% overseas revenue), geopolitics (US over 60%), and further share dilution.

9. Earnings forecast: RMB 31 billion in 2026, RMB 35 billion in 2027

2026: H1 net profit already at RMB 13.651 billion. Assuming Q3 and Q4 grow modestly quarter on quarter (Q3 about RMB 8.2 billion, Q4 about RMB 9.1 billion), full-year net profit of about RMB 31 billion, revenue of about RMB 94 billion, net margin 33%.

2027: Assume 1.6T shipments keep growing but ASP falls 5%–8% a year, 3.2T contributes at small volume, gross margin retreats to 42%. Net profit RMB 35 billion, revenue RMB 115 billion, net margin 30.4%. Bear case (price war plus capex growth slowing to 20%): net profit RMB 24 billion; bull case (capex again beats expectations): RMB 43 billion.

The key assumption behind the forecast is a 42% gross margin, 4.4 percentage points below the 46.4% of 2026H1 — a move of that size has happened within a single quarter historically (2025Q2 gross margin +4.79pct quarter on quarter), so this is neutral, not conservative.

X. Valuation: 26x 2027 net profit, RMB 772

Rationale for 26x 2027 PE: ① growth remains high (+187% in 2026) but is unsustainable; ② the moat is speed-based, not structural; ③ customer concentration is high and warrants a discount; ④ peers (Eoptolink) trade at roughly 40x H1 annualized PE, TFC at roughly 126x.

Three scenarios: bear case RMB 24 billion × 20x = RMB 407; base case RMB 35 billion × 26x = RMB 772; bull case RMB 43 billion × 31x = RMB 1,131. The current RMB 925.24 corresponds to 35.2x 2026E, 31.1x 2027E and 53.3x TTM. Against peers, Zhongji's TTM valuation exceeds Eoptolink's (40x H1 annualized); the premium stems from its leadership position, but the magnitude of the premium is already excessive.

11. Investment recommendation: Watch (Neutral), wait for below RMB 700

Rating: Watch (Neutral). 12-month target price RMB 772, 17% downside from RMB 925.24. We do not recommend building a position at the current price, for three reasons: three-scenario payoff −56%/+22%, net cash ratio 13.2%, customer concentration 81.9%.

Entry points: below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027E), first tranche 3%; below RMB 600 (about 20x), add to 5%; single-name cap 6%. Preconditions: Q3 net cash ratio ≥50% and share price Abandon conditions: net cash ratio <30% for two consecutive quarters, 1.6T ASP down >8% QoQ, customer concentration >85%.

Why "Watch" rather than "Avoid": this company's problems are entirely price problems, not business problems. Once valuation returns to 20–23x, it is one of the most holdable names in this chain.

Conclusion

Rating "Watch", target price RMB 772; start building the position below RMB 700, add to 5% below RMB 600, stop-loss on thesis conditions (net profit to operating cash flow ratio <30%, average price down 8%, customer concentration >85%).
