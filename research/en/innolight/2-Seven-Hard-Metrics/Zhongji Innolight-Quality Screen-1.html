Core thesis

Five of seven hard metrics pass; the stock fails on share dilution and single-period cash flow. Gross margin of 46.4% and net margin of 32.7% are the best readings in the set, and a 10-year average ROE of about 14% also clears the bar. The real problem is the seventh metric: total shares rose from 800 million in 2021 to 1.178 billion by September 2026, up 47% in five years, with expansion funded by financing rather than internal cash. The fifth metric "once full marks, now zero": net cash ratio was 101% in 2025 but only 13% in 2026H1. This is not a bad company; it is a good company showing its cyclical hand.

The two most important: pricing power and resilience

Criteria ④ and ⑥ are what this screen is really designed to filter for. A gross margin of 46.4% means the business has pricing power, and a net margin of 32.7% means it would not lose money even if revenue fell 30%. The longer view makes it clearer: gross margin was 32.99% in 2023, 33.80% in 2024, 42.04% in 2025 and 46.4% in 2026H1 — a 13 percentage point lift over three years, driven by product speed, not cost control.

Both criteria pass cleanly, and they explain why the working paper concludes "good company, bad price" rather than "avoid". A negative screen cannot identify a good price; it can only identify whether a company is worth a closer look. This one clearly is.

The cash flow line: a perfect score in 2025, zero in 2026H1

Item 5 warrants the closest scrutiny. In 2025, operating cash flow was RMB 10.896 billion against net profit of RMB 10.797 billion, a net cash ratio of 1.01 — flawless; the ratio was also up 244.31% YoY, which the company attributed to faster collection on high-speed products and lower investment spending after heavy capex the prior year. By 2026H1, operating cash flow was RMB 1.800 billion against net profit of RMB 13.651 billion, a net cash ratio of 13.2%, down 44.1% YoY.

What changed between the two periods? Inventory rose from RMB 12.681 billion to RMB 19.826 billion (+56.3%), receivables from RMB 6.277 billion to RMB 15.001 billion (+138.98%), and long-term payables increased by about RMB 4.16 billion to lock in key materials for more than a year. This is proactive stockpiling, not a collections problem. But "proactive" holds only if demand materializes; once downstream pull slows, the RMB 19.8 billion of inventory becomes a reserve for impairment. Hence a verdict of "borderline" rather than "pass" — and not "fail" either.

Share count rose 47% over five years, with the expansion funded by financing

Item 7 is the only outright failure. Total shares stood at 800 million after the November 2021 private placement, 1.115 billion on June 30, 2026 and 1.178 billion on September 1, 2026. That is +47% over five years, of which the July 30, 2026 H-share listing added 62.68 million shares in a single step (including the over-allotment), raising about HKD 53.4 billion.

The failure needs to be stated precisely: management is not issuing stock recklessly; the business is expanding faster than its own cash generation. Revenue nearly tripled in half a year, built on capacity laid down two years ahead and materials locked in six months ahead, and that money can only come from financing and payables. Shareholders' equity was diluted 47% in exchange for a 23% global share and a 46% gross margin — the math works, but it falls short of the standard of a first-rate company that can grow without dilution.

Are these fatal or not?

Not fatal, but it must be priced. Item 7 (dilution) is structural and will persist; Item 5 (cash flow) is cyclical and hinges on when stockpiled materials convert into revenue. Both point to the same conclusion: this company's growth is "borrowing to buy growth," not "compounding growth out of profit."

That limits how far the seven filters can take us: the stock qualifies for the deep-research list, not the "buy with eyes closed" list. What matters from here is not the metrics but the price—a 46% gross margin at 20x P/E is a good business; at 53x P/E it is a cyclical lottery ticket.

Conclusion

Conclusion: five of seven criteria pass, one is borderline and one fails. The two failures (share dilution, single-period cash flow) bear on valuation, not on whether this is a good company. On positioning, stay at "watch, wait for a pullback": revisit below RMB 700, and use the Q3 report's net cash ratio as a second confirmation — back above 50% means the inventory build is converting to cash; still below 30% means inventory is starting to pile up in the channel.
