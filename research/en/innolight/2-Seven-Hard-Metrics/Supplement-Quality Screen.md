## The two most important: pricing power and resilience

Criteria ④ and ⑥ are what this screen is really designed to filter for. A gross margin of 46.4% means the business has pricing power, and a net margin of 32.7% means it would not lose money even if revenue fell 30%. The longer view makes it clearer: gross margin was 32.99% in 2023, 33.80% in 2024, 42.04% in 2025 and 46.4% in 2026H1. That is a 13 percentage point lift over three years, driven by product speed, not cost control.

These two criteria pass cleanly, and they explain why the working paper's conclusion is "good company, bad price" rather than "avoid". A negative screen cannot identify a good price; it can only identify whether a company is worth a closer look. This one clearly is.

## The cash flow line: a perfect score in 2025, zero in 2026H1

Item 5 deserves the closest watch. In 2025, operating cash flow was RMB 10.896 billion and net profit RMB 10.797 billion, a net cash ratio of 1.01 — flawless; it was also up 244.31% year on year, which the company attributed to faster collection on high-speed products and slower investment spending after heavy capex the prior year. By 2026H1, operating cash flow was RMB 1.800 billion against net profit of RMB 13.651 billion, a net cash ratio of 13.2%, down 44.1% year on year.

What happened between the two quarters? Inventory rose from RMB 12.681 billion to RMB 19.826 billion (+56.3%), receivables from RMB 6.277 billion to RMB 15.001 billion (+138.98%), and long-term payables increased by about RMB 4.16 billion to lock in key materials for more than a year. This is proactive stockpiling, not a collections problem. But "proactive" holds only if demand materializes; once downstream pull slows, the RMB 19.8 billion of inventory becomes a reserve for impairment. Hence a verdict of "borderline" rather than "pass" — and not "fail" either.

## Share capital line: +47% over five years, expansion funded by financing

Item 7 is the only one that fails outright. Total shares were 800 million after the November 2021 private placement, 1.115 billion on June 30, 2026, and 1.178 billion on September 1, 2026. That is +47% over five years, of which the July 30, 2026 H-share listing added 62.68 million shares in one step (including the over-allotment), raising about HKD 53.4 billion.

The failure needs to be stated precisely: management is not issuing stock recklessly; the business is expanding faster than its own cash generation. Revenue nearly tripled in half a year, built on capacity laid down two years ahead and materials locked in six months ahead, and that money can only come from financing and payables. Shareholders' equity was diluted 47% in exchange for a 23% global share and a 46% gross margin — the math works, but it does not meet the standard of a first-rate company that can grow without dilution.

## Are these fatal or not?

Not fatal, but it must be priced. Item 7 (dilution) is structural and will persist; Item 5 (cash flow) is cyclical and depends on when stockpiled materials convert into revenue. Both point to the same thing: this company's growth is "borrowing to buy growth," not "compounding growth out of profit."

So the conclusion from the seven filters can only go this far: it qualifies for the deep-research list, not the "buy with eyes closed" list. What matters next is not the metrics but the price—a 46% gross margin at 20x PE is a good business; at 53x PE it is a cyclical lottery ticket.
