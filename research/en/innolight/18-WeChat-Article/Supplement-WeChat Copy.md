## Notes

All data in this article can be verified: the 2026 interim report (2026/08/21), the 2025 annual report (2026/03/30), closing quotes on 8/24 and 9/18, and September broker target prices (Goldman Sachs RMB 2,645, Nomura RMB 1,325, BOCOM International RMB 1,488). The "143x static PE" cited in the text = market cap of RMB 1.54 trillion on 22 June divided by 2025 net profit of RMB 10.797 billion, a rough calculation. Not investment advice.

## Half-year profit of RMB 13.6 billion, but only RMB 1.8 billion in cash collected

Start with the conclusion: Zhongji Innolight earned RMB 13.651 billion in the first half of 2026, while operating cash flow was only RMB 1.800 billion. Net profit was 7.6 times cash flow.

This is not financial fraud. It is the norm in the optical module business—but the extent still exceeds what most people imagine.

The interim report came out on the evening of Friday, August 21. On the next trading day, August 24, the stock opened at RMB 945, hit an intraday low of RMB 850, and closed at RMB 870.22, down 7.72%, with market cap briefly falling below RMB 1 trillion. The same day, Eoptolink fell 7.52%.

The market is not punishing the results. They were the best in history: revenue of RMB 41.778 billion, up 182.5% year on year; net profit of RMB 13.651 billion, up 241.7%; optical module gross margin of 46.59%, 6.63 percentage points higher than a year earlier. Profit for the half-year exceeded all of 2025.

What the market is punishing is something else: the cash did not come back.

## Where the money went: RMB 19.8 billion inventory and RMB 15 billion receivables

Open the cash flow statement. Inventory rose from RMB 12.681 billion at the start of the year to RMB 19.826 billion, an increase of RMB 7.145 billion; accounts receivable rose from RMB 6.277 billion to RMB 15.001 billion, an increase of RMB 8.724 billion. The two together total RMB 34.827 billion, equivalent to 83% of half-year revenue.

Over the same period, cash and cash equivalents fell from RMB 10.987 billion to RMB 6.397 billion.

The company's explanation: to match strong downstream orders, it stepped up raw material stocking. The explanation has evidence behind it — long-term payables rose about RMB 4.16 billion in the first half, earmarked for locking in key materials for more than a year; prepayments were about RMB 950 million. In other words, the company is using suppliers' money and its own to buy next year's materials in advance.

The problem: stocking up to 160 days of cost of goods sold is no longer "stocking" — it is a "bet." A bet that demand will be stronger next year, a bet that 3.2T hands off smoothly, a bet that customers will not cut orders.

## How good is this business, really: RMB 1,015 profit per module

To be fair, this is a good business, and one executed to perfection.

In the first half it sold 18.99 million modules at an average price of RMB 2,178 each, with a gross margin of 46.59%, implying gross profit of about RMB 1,015 per module. A year earlier the figures were RMB 1,595, 39.96% and about RMB 637. Gross profit per module rose 59%.

The driver is not cost savings but faster speeds. Customers want 1.6T, and only two or three companies worldwide can deliver reliably, so supply sets the price. That is the rent from a technology gap.

But rent has an expiry date. Gross margins across the industry: Eoptolink 48.44%, 2 points above Zhongji; TFC Optical (upstream components) 60.87%; Accelink 25.53%. The leader's gross margin trails the runner-up's, which shows the industry has no technological monopoly, only window pricing.

Customers are the bigger problem. The top five account for 81.9%, the largest alone for 25.1%, and overseas revenue is 94.8%. The procurement budgets of five customers determine the financial statements of a trillion-dollar company.

## Is RMB 925 expensive: three methods, one answer

Closed at RMB 925.24 on September 18, market cap about RMB 109 billion, corresponding to 53.3x TTM PE.

Method one, three scenarios for 2027. Bear case: cloud capex growth drops to 20%, 1.6T price war, net profit RMB 24 billion, 20x, worth RMB 407. Base case: gross margin holds at 42%, 3.2T takes over, net profit RMB 35 billion, 26x, worth RMB 772. Bull case: capex beats expectations again, net profit RMB 43 billion, 31x, worth RMB 1,131.

The base case is 17% below the current price, the bear case 56% below, the bull case only 22% above. This risk-reward needs no debate.

Method two, look at the path the stock has taken. Intraday RMB 1,416.88 on June 22, market cap RMB 154 billion, more expensive than Kweichow Moutai; RMB 925.24 on September 18, down 33%. Over these three months the company reported its best-ever interim results. All of the decline was valuation, not earnings.

Method three, look at the sell side. In the same week of September, Goldman Sachs put RMB 2,645, Nomura RMB 1,325, BOCOM International RMB 1,488. The high and the low differ by a factor of two. When professional institutions diverge this much, everyone is guessing.

## So, buy or not

My answer: don't buy now, but not because the company is bad.

This company deserves a place on the watchlist. Global share of about 23%, ranking first; 1.6T ahead of rivals by 2–3 quarters; 3.2T NPO and 6.4T NPO already developed; first-half revenue of RMB 41.78 billion and a first-half ROE of 37.62%. This is a company that passes the "good company" test.

There is only one reason not to buy: the price has already used up the good news.

At what price would it be worth discussing? Below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027 earnings); below RMB 600 (about 20x) offers a margin of safety.

There is also a precondition: wait for the third-quarter report in late October. If the net cash flow to net profit ratio returns above 50%, it means the RMB 19.8 billion of inventory is turning into revenue and the cash-flow strain was one-off; if the stock is still below RMB 800 then, buy a third of the position first.

Conversely, if the net cash flow to net profit ratio stays below 30%, or the average selling price of 1.6T units starts falling quarter on quarter, then it is no longer "wait for a pullback" but "change the thesis."

One last point that may not be welcome: a stock falling from RMB 1,416 to RMB 925, down 33%, will strike many as "cheaper." But in June it traded at 143x static PE, and it is still at 53x now. A 33% drop only means it went from "very expensive" to "expensive."

Good company, bad price. Wait — that is the whole answer.
