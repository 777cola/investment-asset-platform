## Notes

All figures in this article are verifiable against the 2026 interim report (2026/08/21), the 2025 annual report (2026/03/30), closing quotes on 8/24 and 9/18, and September broker target prices (Goldman Sachs RMB 2,645, Nomura RMB 1,325, BOCOM International RMB 1,488). The "143x static PE" cited in the text is a rough calculation: market cap of RMB 1.54 trillion on 22 June divided by 2025 net profit of RMB 10.797 billion. Not investment advice.

## Interim profit of RMB 13.6 billion, yet cash collected of only RMB 1.8 billion

Start with the conclusion: Zhongji Innolight earned RMB 13.651 billion in 1H26, while operating cash flow was only RMB 1.800 billion. Net profit was 7.6 times cash flow.

This is not financial fraud. It is the norm in the optical module business—but the extent still exceeds what most people imagine.

The interim report came out on the evening of Friday, August 21. On the next trading day, August 24, the stock opened at RMB 945, hit an intraday low of RMB 850 and closed at RMB 870.22, down 7.72%, with market cap briefly falling below RMB 1 trillion. Eoptolink fell 7.52% the same day.

The market is not punishing the results. They were the best in history: revenue of RMB 41.778 billion, up 182.5% YoY; net profit of RMB 13.651 billion, up 241.7%; optical module gross margin of 46.59%, 6.63pp higher than a year earlier. Profit for the half-year exceeded all of 2025.

What the market is punishing is something else: the cash did not come back.

## Where the money went: RMB 19.8 billion of inventory and RMB 15 billion of receivables

Turn to the cash flow statement. Inventory rose from RMB 12.681 billion at the start of the year to RMB 19.826 billion, up RMB 7.145 billion; accounts receivable rose from RMB 6.277 billion to RMB 15.001 billion, up RMB 8.724 billion. Together the two total RMB 34.827 billion, equivalent to 83% of half-year revenue.

Over the same period, cash and cash equivalents fell from RMB 10.987 billion to RMB 6.397 billion.

Management's explanation: the company stepped up raw material stocking to match strong downstream orders. The evidence backs this up — long-term payables rose about RMB 4.16 billion in the first half, earmarked for locking in key materials for more than a year, and prepayments were about RMB 950 million. In other words, the company is using both suppliers' money and its own to buy next year's materials in advance.

The problem: stocking up to 160 days of cost of goods sold is no longer "stocking" — it is a "bet." A bet that demand will be stronger next year, a bet that 3.2T hands off smoothly, a bet that customers will not cut orders.

## How good is this business, really: RMB 1,015 profit per module

To be fair, this is a good business, and one executed to perfection.

In the first half it sold 18.99 million modules at an average price of RMB 2,178 each, with a gross margin of 46.59%, implying gross profit of about RMB 1,015 per module. A year earlier the figures were RMB 1,595, 39.96% and about RMB 637. Gross profit per module rose 59%.

The driver is not cost savings but faster speeds. Customers want 1.6T, and only two or three companies worldwide can deliver reliably, so supply sets the price. That is the rent from a technology gap.

But rent has an expiry date. Gross margins across the industry: Eoptolink 48.44%, 2 points above Zhongji; TFC Optical (upstream components) 60.87%; Accelink 25.53%. The leader's gross margin trails the runner-up's, which shows the industry has no technological monopoly, only window pricing.

Customers are the bigger problem. The top five account for 81.9%, the largest alone for 25.1%, and overseas revenue is 94.8%. The procurement budgets of five customers determine the financial statements of a trillion-dollar company.

## Is RMB 925 expensive: three methods, one answer

Closed at RMB 925.24 on September 18, market cap about RMB 109 billion, equivalent to 53.3x P/E (TTM).

Method one: three 2027 scenarios. Bear case: cloud capex growth slows to 20%, a 1.6T price war breaks out, net profit RMB 24 billion, 20x, worth RMB 407. Base case: gross margin holds at 42%, 3.2T takes over, net profit RMB 35 billion, 26x, worth RMB 772. Bull case: capex beats expectations again, net profit RMB 43 billion, 31x, worth RMB 1,131.

The base case sits 17% below the current price, the bear case 56% below, and the bull case only 22% above. That risk-reward needs no debate.

Method two: the path the stock has taken. It hit an intraday RMB 1,416.88 on June 22, a market cap of RMB 154 billion, more expensive than Kweichow Moutai; by September 18 it was RMB 925.24, down 33%. Over those three months the company reported its best-ever interim results. The entire decline was valuation, not earnings.

Method three: the sell side. In the same week of September, Goldman Sachs set RMB 2,645, Nomura RMB 1,325 and BOCOM International RMB 1,488. The high and the low differ by a factor of two. When professional institutions diverge this much, everyone is guessing.

## So, buy or not

My answer: don't buy now — but not because the company is bad.

This company deserves a place on the watchlist. Roughly 23% global share, ranking first; 1.6T ahead of rivals by 2–3 quarters; 3.2T NPO and 6.4T NPO already developed; first-half revenue of RMB 41.78 billion and a first-half ROE of 37.62%. It passes the "good company" test.

There is only one reason not to buy: the price has already used up the good news.

At what price would it be worth discussing? Below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027 earnings); below RMB 600 (about 20x) offers a margin of safety.

There is also a precondition: wait for the third-quarter report in late October. If the net cash flow to net profit ratio returns above 50%, it means the RMB 19.8 billion of inventory is turning into revenue and the cash-flow strain was one-off; if the stock is still below RMB 800 then, buy a third of the position first.

Conversely, if the net cash flow to net profit ratio stays below 30%, or the average selling price of 1.6T units starts falling quarter on quarter, then it is no longer "wait for a pullback" but "change the thesis."

One last point that may not be welcome: a stock falling from RMB 1,416 to RMB 925, down 33%, will strike many as "cheaper." But in June it traded at 143x static PE, and it is still at 53x now. A 33% drop only means it went from "very expensive" to "expensive."

Good company, bad price. Wait — that is the whole answer.
