Core thesis

The 2026 interim report reads as a "perfect top half, tense bottom half". Revenue of RMB 41.78 billion (+182.5%), net profit of RMB 13.65 billion (+241.7%), optical module gross margin of 46.59% (+6.63pct) and weighted ROE of 37.62% — all four are the best in history. Operating cash flow, however, was just RMB 1.80 billion (−44.1%), a net cash ratio of 13.2%; inventory of RMB 19.83 billion and receivables of RMB 15.00 billion absorbed RMB 15.87 billion of cash between them. Over the same period long-term payables rose about RMB 4.16 billion and construction in progress rose RMB 2.29 billion — the money turned into materials, capacity and payment terms.

Layer one: revenue mix — 99% from a single business, 94.8% from overseas

Revenue splits into three lines: optical modules RMB 41.355 billion (99.0%), other businesses RMB 423 million, and overseas RMB 39.615 billion (94.8%). This is an extremely focused, extremely outward-facing company: one product line, five customers, one currency (USD).

The volume-price split of growth matters more: unit sales 18.99 million (+110% YoY), with average price per unit up from RMB 1,595 to RMB 2,178 (+36.6%). Volume doubled and price rose 36% — that accounts for the entire +182.5% revenue growth. On volume growth alone, with no price increase, revenue growth would fall to roughly 110% and the 46% gross margin would not hold.

Overseas share rose from 86.4% to 94.8%, so all incremental revenue came from North America. Upside: strong customer payment capacity and relatively disciplined payment terms. Downside: FX, tariffs and export controls all hang on a single line. The capacity buildout in Thailand and Taiwan hedges that line.

Layer two: gross margin — 46.59% reflects generational pricing power, not a cost advantage

Optical module gross margin reached 46.59%, up 6.63 percentage points YoY, with blended gross margin at about 46.4%. The three-year series: 32.99% in 2023, 33.80% in 2024, 42.04% in 2025 and 46.4% in 2026H1. That is a 13 percentage point lift over three years, driven by a single factor: a richer mix of high-speed products.

This is precisely what makes the gross margin fragile. 46.59% does not reflect "an efficient company"; it reflects the fact that only two or three players can deliver 1.6T. One telling comparison: Eoptolink posted a 48.44% gross margin in 2026H1, above Zhongji's 46.4%-plus. On the same product generation, rivals' pricing power is no weaker, which shows that 46% is the industry window price, not a premium unique to Zhongji.

Is there room to go higher? One positive: silicon photonics can reduce reliance on EML optical chips and should structurally lift gross margin. But the barrier to silicon photonics is falling and rivals are pursuing it too. The reasonable expectation is a retreat to 40%–43% in 2027; 46% is unlikely to become the norm.

Tier 3: Costs and profit — R&D doubles, net margin still rises

R&D expense RMB 1.153 billion, +96.9% YoY; G&A RMB 767 million, +164% YoY (RMB 291 million prior year); selling expense minimal. Total expenses grew slower than revenue (+182.5%), lifting net margin from 27.02% to 32.68% — the textbook shape of scale effects.

The 164% G&A increase warrants one line of explanation: half is personnel and site management costs tied to capacity expansion, half intermediary fees for the H-share listing. Both were spent in pursuit of larger revenue, not a loss of control. R&D +96.9% deserves more credit — 3.2T NPO, 6.4T NPO and 2.4T silicon photonics + lithium niobate were all advanced during the half-year.

Net profit RMB 13.651 billion, net margin 32.68%, weighted ROE 37.62% (half-year). That ROE is unusually high for manufacturing, and the driver is not leverage (debt ratio 42%, very little interest-bearing debt) but turnover — RMB 68.9 billion of total assets supports RMB 41.8 billion of half-year revenue.

Layer 4: Cash flow and balance sheet — where the money went

This is the single most important passage in the report. Net profit of RMB 13.651 billion, operating cash flow of RMB 1.800 billion, net cash ratio of 13.2%; the year-earlier figure was 80%, and full-year 2025 was 101%. Read the three together: 1H 2026 is an outlier, not the norm.

The cash went three ways: ① inventories +RMB 7.145 billion (126.81 → 198.26), equal to about 160 days of cost of sales, clearly above the normal level; ② receivables +RMB 8.724 billion (62.77 → 150.01), equal to 65 days of revenue (on a 181-day half-year basis), with payment terms lengthening; ③ construction in progress +RMB 2.285 billion (14.22 → 37.07), with capacity under construction at five sites simultaneously.

The sources of cash are equally clear: long-term payables up about RMB 4.16 billion (key materials locked in for more than a year, not yet paid in cash), accounts payable and notes payable up, and HKD 53.4 billion raised from the H-share offering on 30 July (about RMB 49.0 billion, after the balance sheet date). In other words, the 30 June balance sheet marks the tightest point; the cash position has improved markedly since July.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the income statement earns full marks, the cash flow statement loses points, and the balance sheet explains everything. The 46.59% gross margin is generational rent from 1.6T; the 32.68% net margin is scale effect; the 13.2% net cash ratio reflects inventory build and payment terms. Three things, in order of importance: whether the Q3 report's net cash ratio returns to 50%, whether inventory falls sequentially, and whether the 1.6T average selling price is still rising. These three numbers determine whether 2027 profit is real.
