Core view

Two of the four moats are hard: technology leadership (1.6T ahead by 2–3 quarters) and scale (18.99 million units in six months, about 23% global share); switching costs are moderate (12–18 months for cloud provider certification, but dual sourcing is common); brand and licenses are essentially non-existent. The most dangerous rival is Eoptolink — same customers, same product generation, and its 2026H1 gross margin of 48.44% is 2 points higher than Zhongji's. The long-term threat is CPO, which sets the valuation ceiling beyond 2028.

Position in the value chain: stuck at the "design and packaging" layer

Zhongji Innolight's position in the chain, in one sentence: buys chips, does design, controls packaging, sells modules. It does not make optical chips (EML, CW lasers), nor electrical chips (DSP); these two account for more than 50% of material cost. What it does is optical path design, optical coupling, packaging processes and delivery management—precisely the two metrics customers care about most: "yield" and "lead time."

The advantage of this position is extremely high capital efficiency: no investment in wafer fabs, total assets of RMB 68.94 billion support half-year revenue of RMB 41.78 billion, and half-year weighted ROE of 37.62%. The downside is bargaining power squeezed at both ends: the largest upstream supplier accounts for 38.3% of procurement, while the top five downstream customers account for 81.9% of revenue—concentration at both ends is higher than its own, and the only buffer in between is a 46% gross margin.

The truth about bargaining power: making money from the "irreplaceable three months"

Module makers have no long-term pricing power over customers, only "window pricing power." When 1.6T first ramped, only two or three players worldwide could deliver reliably, and customers accepted an average price of RMB 2,178; once Eoptolink's 1.6T ramp completes, the price will be pulled back to around RMB 1,900. That is why the +36.6% ASP gain is both meaningful and time-limited.

The same applies to suppliers: RMB 950 million in prepayments and RMB 5.06 billion in long-term payables to lock in more than a year of materials show that optical chips and electrical chips are a seller's market. The company can do only two things: lock in volume early and raise the silicon photonics mix (reducing reliance on EML). It is doing both, but neither changes its "middleman" nature.

Moat score: two hard, one medium, three soft

Two hard moats are technology lead and scale. Technology lead is worth 2–3 quarters, roughly 6–8 percentage points of gross margin—of the 46.59% in 2026H1, half is estimated to come from this time gap. Scale is worth procurement bargaining power and the yield learning curve; shipments of 18.99 million units make it cheaper than second-tier players on chip procurement and speed up yield ramp on new products.

The moderate one is switching cost. A cloud provider changing suppliers needs 12–18 months of re-certification, and that time cost is real; but cloud providers are institutionally required to dual-source, so any single vendor's share has a ceiling. The three soft ones are network effects, brand and licences—in a B2B hardware industry, these three should not be counted on in the first place.

What 6.5/10 combined means: this moat is enough to capture the 1.6T cycle's gains, not enough to guarantee it is still the one at 3.2T. It must be re-dug every year, rather than dug deeper.

The most dangerous rival: Eoptolink, then CPO

Eoptolink is the only rival competing head-to-head on the same customers and the same product generation. 2026H1 revenue RMB 20.91 billion (+100.3%), net profit RMB 7.53 billion (+90.98%), gross margin 48.44%, net cash ratio 21.5% — gross margin 2 points above Innolight and cash flow better. Its playbook is cost control plus fast follow; once 1.6T reaches volume, its first move will be to quote price.

The second threat comes from upstream players Coherent and Lumentum: they sell both chips and modules, and the stronger the industry cycle, the more profit they capture. Every module Innolight sells requires buying chips from them, and that structure cannot be changed.

The third threat is CPO. NVIDIA's Spectrum-X CPO switches were announced for full mass production in March 2026, entering production ramp in August, with second-generation 3.2T optical engines and 16 200G channels per engine. Near term it replaces pluggable modules on switch faceplates; longer term it could erode a slice of the short-reach market. This is not tomorrow's threat, but it determines what PE multiple the stock gets after 2028.

Conclusion

Conclusion: moat 6.5/10 — adequate but not deep. Technology leadership and scale are solid; switching costs are moderate; brand and licences are zero. Among competitors, the one to watch most is Eoptolink's 1.6T pricing, the one to guard against most is Coherent's vertical integration, and the one to model most is the pace of CPO penetration. None of the three affects whether it is a good company now — only whether it deserves 20x or 40x.
