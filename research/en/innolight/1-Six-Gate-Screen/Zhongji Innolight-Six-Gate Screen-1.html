Six-Gate Screen — Zhongji Innolight

Zhongji Innolight (300308) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen

Core view

Four of six gates passed; the last two are the sticking point. The business itself is fine: 1.6T silicon photonics has ramped, first-half revenue reached RMB 41.78 billion, +182.5% YoY, net profit RMB 13.65 billion, +241.7% YoY, and optical module gross margin 46.59%, up 6.63 percentage points YoY. The problem is price and cash: RMB 925.24 corresponds to 53.3x P/E (TTM), and our base-case target price of RMB 772 sits below the current price; meanwhile net profit of RMB 13.65 billion converted into only RMB 1.80 billion of operating cash flow, a net cash ratio of 13%. This is a textbook case of the best company in the industry, but not the best price.

The first three hurdles: the business is real, but its boundaries must be spelled out

It clears the first gate, but only halfway. The optical module logic in one sentence: customers want faster interconnect, and I convert electrical signals into optical signals and sell them. Demand and the competitive landscape are understandable; the technology route is not. As of September 2026, three routes coexist: pluggable modules, CPO co-packaging and OCS optical switching. Nvidia's Spectrum-X CPO switch was announced for full mass production in March 2026, and pluggables remain the shipment mainstay. Which route takes the lion's share in three years, I have no answer, so I give 7 points rather than 9.

The second gate gets 7 points. A 46.4% blended gross margin and a 32.7% net margin are top-tier for hardware manufacturing. But think through where that 46% comes from: 1.6T is in short supply, and the average selling price rose from RMB 1,595 to RMB 2,178, up 36.6%, while volume still reached 18.99 million units over the same period. Rising volume and price is good, and also the fragile part—as soon as a competitor's 1.6T arrives, the price-increase leg breaks.

The third gate gets 6 points, a marginal pass. The moat breaks into four layers: in-house silicon photonics (saves EML, raises integration), 1.6T first-mover advantage (2–3 quarters ahead of rivals), cloud-provider certification (switching suppliers requires 12–18 months of re-validation), and delivery scale (about 23% global share, 21.09 million units shipped in 2025). All four layers are real, and all four thin over time. This is a moat that exists only for those who run fast.

Stage four: management got the 1.6T call right

I award it 8.0 points, mainly for three calls made between the second half of 2025 and the first half of 2026: locking in long-term agreements for optical chips and electrical chips ahead of time, spreading 1.6T capacity across five sites — Suzhou, Tongling, Chengdu, Taiwan and Thailand — and daring to lift inventory from RMB 12.681 billion to RMB 19.826 billion when demand was tightest. In hindsight, all three were right — without the RMB 19.8 billion of stockpiled materials, there would be no RMB 41.778 billion of half-year revenue.

Two things cost it points. First, the H-share listing on 30 July 2026 at an offer price of HKD 980, raising about HKD 53.4 billion, broke issue on day one and closed near HKD 950 — raising capital near the share-price high dilutes existing shareholders and traps new ones. Second, the roughly RMB 5 billion of buybacks in August–September is a sizable amount, but it cannot change the cyclical nature of this business; it looks more like propping up a trillion-currency market cap. Management are excellent operators, not the kind of capital allocators who say "I'd be happier if the stock fell."

Level five: 53x TTM is the one hurdle it cannot clear

No debate needed here. TTM net profit RMB 20.45 billion (FY2025 RMB 10.797 billion − 2025H1 RMB 3.995 billion + 2026H1 RMB 13.651 billion), EPS RMB 17.36, and RMB 925.24 implies 53.3x. On a rough full-year 2026 figure of RMB 31 billion, forward P/E is about 35x. For a hardware company whose gross margin rests on a product cycle, 35x forward P/E means the market has already priced in 2027's 3.2T handoff.

The three scenarios make it clearer: bear case (cloud capex growth slows to 20%, price war in 1.6T, 2027 net profit RMB 24 billion, 20x) RMB 407; base case (gross margin holds at 45%, 3.2T handoff, net profit RMB 35 billion, 26x) RMB 772; bull case (capex beats expectations again, net profit RMB 43 billion, 31x) RMB 1,131. The base case sits 17% below the current price, so making money requires the bull case to materialize. That is the reason for the 3 score.

Level six: the buy case rests on someone else's budget

4 points. If buying today, the only rationale is that the four major North American cloud providers spent a combined USD 164.9 billion in capex in 2026Q2, up 86% year on year, and will spend more in the second half. The subject of that sentence is Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Google, not Zhongji Innolight. Building a position on guesses about someone else's budget is a discipline problem, not a judgment problem.

Conversely, the sell case is not in the company's hands either. That is the hardest part of a cyclical growth stock: you are paying 53x PE for a variable you cannot influence. If you must participate, position matters more than opinion — talk below RMB 700, and below RMB 600 there is a margin of safety.

Veto items and items to be verified

Red flag one: cash flow quality. 1H net profit was RMB 13.651 billion against operating cash flow of RMB 1.800 billion, a net cash ratio of 13% — an abrupt break from 101% for full-year 2025. Inventory of RMB 19.826 billion and receivables of RMB 15.001 billion total RMB 34.827 billion, equal to 83% of half-year revenue. The money is not lost; it is tied up in materials and payment terms — but how long it stays tied up, and whether it converts to cash, depends on the pace of downstream pull.

Red flag two: customer concentration. The top five customers account for 81.9% and the largest customer for 25.1% (2026Q1 basis), up from 75.98% in 2025 and still rising; overseas revenue is 94.8%, with the US market above 60%. A single customer's production schedule adjustment can rewrite a quarter's financials outright.

Four things to verify: ① whether the Q3 net cash ratio recovers above 50%; ② whether 1.6T gross margin holds at 45%, and how Eoptolink prices 1.6T after mass production; ③ whether top-five customer share keeps climbing past 85%; ④ the penetration pace of Nvidia's CPO switches, and whether they start taking pluggable share in 2027.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Watch, wait for a pullback. Four gates passed, two failed — and the two failures are the costliest: price and discipline. Triggers are fixed: start building the position below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027 earnings) and add below RMB 600; alternatively, if the Q3 net cash ratio returns above 50% while the share price is still below RMB 800, act early. If the Q3 net cash ratio stays below 30% and the top five customers' share rises above 85%, downgrade to avoid regardless of price.
