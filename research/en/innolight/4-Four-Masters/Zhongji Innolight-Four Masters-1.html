Core thesis

The four men's methodologies differ, yet their conclusions converge: this business's excess profit comes from a technology gap, not from structure. Duan Yongping accepts it as a top-tier player among difficult businesses but would not buy at a cyclical peak; Buffett exits outright on seeing 81.9% customer concentration; Munger treats the 46.4% gross margin as a temporary reading against a historical gross margin of 33%; Li Lu concedes that optical interconnect is the nervous system of computing power, yet states plainly that midstream assembly is not a position that collects rent. Four languages, one action: don't buy, wait for the price.

Duan Yongping: good companies, tough businesses — wait for the cycle low

Duan Yongping would start with praise: a tough business executed to perfection. A 46.4% gross margin, a 32.7% net margin, RMB 13.651 billion earned in half a year, 23% global share and 21.09 million units shipped in 2025 — by these numbers management has done everything it needed to do right. It bet on 1.6T silicon photonics, locked in materials a year ahead and expanded capacity across five sites, timing its moves more accurately than rivals.

Then comes his question: will this company still be here in ten years, and will it still make money? The history of optical modules is a history of leadership changes — the leaders of the 100G era and those of the 400G era were not entirely the same group. This business has to be won anew every 18 months, and the way to win is to give up the previous generation's gross margin in exchange for share. By his standard, Zhongji Innolight is a "good company you can understand," not one "you can hold for ten years without touching."

So his move is clear: don't chase highs, wait for the cycle low. What counts as a low? Not a 30% drop in the share price, but gross margin back below 35%, rivals starting to lose money and no one in the market talking about optical modules. At that point the P/E might be just 12x, but the business is still there. At RMB 925 and 53x P/E, this is where "others are greedy," not where he stands.

Buffett: Your customers are your destiny

The first two things Buffett looks at, this company fails both. First, customer concentration: the top five customers account for 81.9%, the largest customer 25.1%, overseas revenue 94.8%, and the US market more than 60%. Pricing power therefore sits with the buyer. Customers will pay a premium this year because only two or three players can deliver 1.6T; next year, when Eoptolink's 1.6T ramps up, that premium turns into a discount.

Second, product substitutability. What he likes are businesses where "customers can't switch even if they want to" — Coca-Cola's formula, GEICO's cost structure, Apple's ecosystem. For optical modules the switching cost is a 12–18 month qualification cycle, not an insurmountable wall, and cloud providers generally run a dual-supplier strategy, structurally denying you exclusivity. The qualification barrier protects "share of this generation of product," not "this company's long-term profit."

He would look more closely at the cash flow section. 2025 operating cash flow RMB 10.90 billion, net profit RMB 10.80 billion — one-to-one, very pretty; 2026H1 net profit RMB 13.65 billion, cash flow RMB 1.80 billion, net cash ratio 13%. He would not call this fraud; he would call it the essence of the business model: the faster the growth, the more working capital is fronted. A business that "runs short of cash the faster it grows" does not make his "great business" list, only his "cyclical growth stock" list — and the latter he touches only at extremely low valuations.

Munger: high gross margins at the top of the cycle are an illusion

Munger would strip out the emotion first and look only at mean reversion. Zhongji Innolight's gross margin: 32.99% in 2023, 33.80% in 2024, 42.04% in 2025, 46.4% in 2026H1. That is a 13-percentage-point rise over three years, driven by higher ASPs (RMB 1,595 → RMB 2,178) as 1.6T supply fell short of demand. His argument: this is not a new normal but excess profit, and excess profit inevitably draws in capacity.

He would list three triggers and say "you don't need all three — one is enough": ① Eoptolink and Coherent reach volume production of 1.6T, a price war breaks out and gross margin falls back to 38%; ② North American cloud capex growth drops from 86% to 20%, and order growth slows in tandem; ③ CPO penetrates early — Nvidia's Spectrum-X CPO switches were announced for full mass production in March 2026, and if they ramp in 2027, a chunk of pluggable module demand gets structurally cut away.

He would be bluntest about the market's problem: in September 2026, Goldman Sachs set a target price of RMB 2,645 for the A-share and HKD 3,267 for the H-share, Nomura RMB 1,325 and BOCOM International RMB 1,488 — same month, same set of results, target prices differing by a factor of two. This is not a question of analytical ability but of assumptions: whoever's 2027 net profit assumption is closer to reality is right. Munger would say: when sell-side views diverge this widely, it means nobody knows — they are all guessing.

Li Lu: optical interconnect is the nervous system, but midstream is no rent-collecting position

Li Lu's framework is one of civilizational evolution: the larger the compute cluster, the higher the value share captured by interconnect. The direction of travel from 800G to 1.6T to 3.2T is certain. Zhongji Innolight has genuine engineering capability here — 18.99 million units delivered in the half year, full mass production capability at its Thailand base, and 3.2T NPO and 6.4T NPO already developed.Only two or three companies worldwide can deliver at this scale; that is not luck.

His judgment, though, would turn on position in the chain: the certainty sits with the industry, not the company. The optical module landscape is a "duopoly plus a pack of chasers," with excess profit rotating among a handful of players. What durably locks in profit is upstream optical chips (Coherent and Lumentum control EML) and downstream cloud players (Nvidia and Google set the specs); midstream assembly has the weakest bargaining power. In 2026Q1 purchases from the company's largest supplier accounted for 38.3% of total procurement — evidence it has little negotiating room upstream either.

True to form, if he were to buy into this chain he would buy the "toll booth," not the fastest car. His verdict on Zhongji Innolight would therefore be: an excellent midstream manufacturer, worth researching, not worth holding long term at 53x P/E.

Odds assessment: −56% vs +22%, structurally unfavorable

Lay out the probabilities of the three scenarios and the odds become clear. Assume 30% pessimistic, 45% neutral and 25% optimistic; expected target price = 407×0.3 + 772×0.45 + 1,131×0.25 = RMB 749, 19% below the current price. For expectancy to turn positive, the probability of the optimistic scenario must rise above 45% — that is, you must believe cloud capex will beat expectations for a fourth consecutive year.

This is "good company, bad odds." It does not need to get worse to lose you money; it only needs to not get better, and the valuation gets killed. On 22 June 2026 the stock touched RMB 1,416.88 for a market cap of RMB 1.54 trillion; by 18 September it was RMB 925.24, down 33% in three months — a period in which the company reported its best interim results ever.

The four investors' consensus compresses into one line of action: do not buy at RMB 925; start talking below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027); there is a margin of safety below RMB 600 (about 20x). If the Q3 net cash ratio returns above 50% and the stock is still below RMB 800, you can move the entry level forward.

Conclusion

Conclusion: none of the four would buy, but for different reasons and with the same action. Duan Yongping waits for a cyclical low, Buffett for customer-mix improvement, Munger for gross margin to fall before buying, and Li Lu simply goes upstream to find toll booths. What they share is a rejection of 53x P/E (TTM). Maintain "watch, wait for a pullback," with a first tranche buy price of RMB 700 and a second tranche at RMB 600; if the Q3 report shows net cash ratio back above 50% and the share price below RMB 800, a one-third position can be built early.
