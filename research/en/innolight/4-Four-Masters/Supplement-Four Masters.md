## Duan Yongping: Good companies, tough businesses, wait for the cycle low

Duan Yongping would first offer a word of praise for this company: **a tough business executed to perfection.** A 46.4% gross margin, a 32.7% net margin, RMB 13.651 billion earned in half a year, 23% global share, and 21.09 million units shipped in 2025 — these numbers show management has done everything it needed to do right. Betting on 1.6T silicon photonics, locking in materials a year ahead, and expanding capacity across five sites, it has timed its moves more accurately than rivals.

Then he would ask that question: **will this company still be here in ten years, and will it still make money?** The history of optical modules is a history of leadership changes — the leaders of the 100G era and those of the 400G era were not entirely the same group. This business has to be won anew every 18 months, and the way to win is to give up the previous generation's gross margin in exchange for share. By his standard, Zhongji Innolight is a "good company you can understand," not a company "you can hold for ten years without touching."

So his move is clear: **don't chase highs, wait for the cycle low.** What counts as a low? Not a 30% drop in the share price, but gross margin back below 35%, rivals starting to lose money, and no one in the market talking about optical modules. At that point the PE might be just 12x, but the business is still there. At RMB 925 and 53x PE, this is where "others are greedy," not where he stands.

## Buffett: Your customers are your destiny

The first two things Buffett looks at, this company fails both. **First, customer concentration: the top five customers account for 81.9%, the largest customer 25.1%, overseas revenue 94.8%, and the US market more than 60%.** That means pricing power sits with the buyer. Customers are willing to pay a premium this year because only two or three players can deliver 1.6T; next year, when Eoptolink's 1.6T ramps up, the premium turns into a discount.

Second, product substitutability. What he likes are businesses where "customers can't switch even if they want to" — Coca-Cola's formula, GEICO's cost structure, Apple's ecosystem. The switching cost for optical modules is a 12–18 month qualification cycle, not an insurmountable wall, and cloud providers generally run a dual-supplier strategy, structurally denying you exclusivity. **The qualification barrier protects "share of this generation of product," not "this company's long-term profit."**

He would look more closely at the cash flow section. 2025 operating cash flow RMB 10.90 billion, net profit RMB 10.80 billion — one-to-one, very pretty; 2026H1 net profit RMB 13.65 billion, cash flow RMB 1.80 billion, net cash ratio 13%. **He would not call this fraud; he would call it the essence of the business model: the faster the growth, the more working capital is fronted.** A business that "runs short of cash the faster it grows" does not make his "great business" list, only his "cyclical growth stock" list — and the latter he touches only at extremely low valuations.

## Munger: High gross margins at the top of the cycle are an illusion

Munger would strip out emotion first and look only at mean reversion. **Zhongji Innolight's gross margin: 32.99% in 2023, 33.80% in 2024, 42.04% in 2025, 46.4% in 2026H1.** A 13-percentage-point rise over three years, driven by higher average selling prices per unit (RMB 1,595 → RMB 2,178) as 1.6T supply fell short of demand. He would argue this is not a new normal but excess profit, and excess profit inevitably draws in capacity.

He would list three triggers, and would say "you don't need all three — one is enough": ① Eoptolink and Coherent reach volume production of 1.6T, a price war breaks out, and gross margin falls back to 38%; ② North American cloud capex growth drops from 86% to 20%, and order growth slows in tandem; ③ CPO penetrates early — Nvidia's Spectrum-X CPO switches were announced for full mass production in March 2026, and if they ramp in 2027, a chunk of pluggable module demand gets structurally cut away.

He would also be bluntest about the market's problem: **in September 2026, Goldman Sachs set a target price of RMB 2,645 for the A-share and HKD 3,267 for the H-share, Nomura RMB 1,325, and BOCOM International RMB 1,488 — same month, same set of results, target prices differing by a factor of two.** This is not a question of analytical ability but of assumptions: whoever's 2027 net profit assumption is closer to reality is right. Munger would say: when sell-side views diverge this widely, it means nobody knows — they are all guessing.

## Li Lu: Optical interconnect is the nervous system, but the midstream is not a position for collecting rent

Li Lu's framework is civilizational evolution: the larger the compute cluster, the higher the value share of interconnect. From 800G to 1.6T to 3.2T, the direction is certain. Zhongji Innolight has real engineering capability in this direction — deliveries of 18.99 million units in the half year, full mass production capability at its Thailand base, and 3.2T NPO and 6.4T NPO already developed.**Only two or three companies worldwide can achieve this scale of delivery; this is not luck.**

But his judgment would land on position in the chain: **the certainty is in the industry, not in the company.** The optical module landscape is "duopoly plus a pack of chasers," with excess profit rotating among a few companies. What truly locks in profit long term is upstream optical chips (Coherent and Lumentum control EML) and downstream cloud players (Nvidia and Google define the specs); the midstream assembly segment has the weakest bargaining power. In 2026Q1 the company's purchases from its largest supplier accounted for 38.3% of total procurement — a figure that shows it has little negotiating room with upstream either.

By his habit, if he were to buy this chain, he would buy the "toll booth" — not the fastest car. So his assessment of Zhongji Innolight would be: **an excellent midstream manufacturer, worth researching, not worth holding long term at 53x PE.**

## Odds assessment: −56% vs +22%, structurally unfavorable

Lay out the probabilities of the three scenarios and the odds become clear. **Assume 30% pessimistic, 45% neutral and 25% optimistic; expected target price = 407×0.3 + 772×0.45 + 1,131×0.25 = RMB 749, 19% below the current price.** For it to turn into positive expectancy, the probability of the optimistic scenario must rise above 45% — that is, you must believe cloud capex will exceed expectations for a fourth consecutive year.

This is "good company, bad odds." **It does not need to get worse to lose you money; it only needs to not get better, and the valuation gets killed.** On 22 June 2026 the stock touched RMB 1,416.88 for a market cap of RMB 1.54 trillion; by 18 September it was RMB 925.24, down 33% in three months — a period in which the company reported its best interim results ever.

The four investors' consensus compresses into one line of action: **do not buy at RMB 925; start talking below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027); there is a margin of safety below RMB 600 (about 20x).** If the Q3 net cash ratio returns above 50% and the stock is still below RMB 800, you can move the entry level forward.
