## Why 0%: the risk-reward structure does not hold

The premise for building a position was "limited downside, ample upside." Now it is the reverse: 56% downside (bear case RMB 407) against 22% upside (bull case RMB 1,131). For this to carry positive expected value, the bull case probability must exceed 45%—tantamount to betting that cloud capex beats expectations for a fourth consecutive year.

Another angle: buying at RMB 925 requires 2027 net profit of RMB 35 billion and a 31x P/E just to break even. That is an "everything must go right" price, not a "room for error" price. The portfolio-management principle: never pay an "everything must go right" price for any single name.

## Why still set three tiers: because it is worth tracking

0% does not mean off the radar. This is the purest "speed upgrade" play in the AI compute chain, with 23% global share and a 2–3 quarter head start in 1.6T; a name like this earns a permanent place on the watchlist.

The three tiers exist to enforce "behavioural discipline." Buying 1% at RMB 750–800 is not about making money; it forces me to look at its data every day—only with a position will I seriously read the Q3 report. Buy 3% at RMB 700, because that is the break-even point under the neutral scenario. Buy 5% at RMB 600, because half of the bear case is already priced in there. Each tier maps to a calculable reason, not to "buy because it fell."

## Stop-loss: thesis-based, not price-based

Price stops on deep-cyclical stocks shake you out — optical modules can draw down 30% in a single quarter (they fell 33% from June 22 to September 18). So define the stop conditions in fundamental terms:

- Net cash flow to net profit below 30% for two consecutive quarters: inventory is not moving, writedowns loom — exit unconditionally

- 1.6T ASP down more than 8% QoQ: price war confirmed, gross margin assumptions invalid — halve the position

- Top five customers exceed 85% of revenue, or a single customer cuts orders by 20%: demand or mix deteriorating — exit

- CPO ports exceed 20% of new interconnect additions: the long-term thesis is being displaced — liquidate and do not re-enter

Keep a single price stop as a backstop: 35% below entry cost. It will most likely never trigger, because the fundamental stops will fire first.

## Correlation: shares the same risk factor as the AI hardware in the portfolio

This matters more than position size.Zhongji Innolight's earnings sit at the third link in the transmission chain from North American cloud capex to Nvidia GPU shipments to optical module orders, and are highly correlated with Nvidia, TSMC, SK hynix and WUS Printed Circuit. On August 24, 2026, the two leading optical module names fell more than 7% in unison (Eoptolink −7.52%), evidence of same-factor, same-direction moves.

Size positions by risk factor, then, not by number of stocks.If the portfolio already holds AI compute hardware (GPU, HBM, PCB, servers), the optical module allocation must come from the same pool; we suggest total "AI capex beta" not exceed 15%, with Zhongji as a single name not exceed 6%.

Hedging approach: to retain long-term optical module exposure without single-stock risk, wait for a pullback and split the allocation half in Zhongji, half in Eoptolink—the two overlap on customers and technology roadmaps but trade at different valuations, smoothing single-stock valuation swings.
