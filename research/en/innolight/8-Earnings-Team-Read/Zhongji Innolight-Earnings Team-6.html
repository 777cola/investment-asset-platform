Zhongji Innolight: RMB 13.6bn of 1H profit, only RMB 1.8bn collected — where did the cash go?

Core view

Editorial angle: weave the four close reads into one publishable article. The core narrative is "the gap between profit and cash," supported by three numbers — RMB 13.65 billion net profit, RMB 1.80 billion operating cash flow, RMB 19.83 billion inventory. No bull call, no bear call — just make the accounts clear.

Main text (1): A P&L statement with no flaws to pick

Zhongji Innolight's 1H26 income statement leaves little to fault. Revenue came in at RMB 41.78 billion, up 182.5% YoY, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 13.65 billion, up 241.7%. Optical module gross margin reached 46.59%, 6.63pp above the prior-year period, and first-half profit exceeded the whole of 2025. The drivers are clear: 1.6T silicon photonic modules ramped at scale, with 18.99 million units sold and ASP rising from RMB 1,595 to RMB 2,178 per unit.

Volume doubled, price rose 36% and gross margin expanded 6.6 points — three variables moving up at once. This is the healthiest growth pattern in manufacturing: not winning share by cutting prices, but capturing a premium through higher-speed products.

Main text (Part 2): A cash flow statement that makes you frown

Flip to the cash flow statement in the same report and the picture changes abruptly. Net cash flow from operating activities was RMB 1.80 billion, down 44.08% YoY; net profit was RMB 13.651 billion, a net cash ratio of 13.2%. A year earlier that figure was 80%, and for full-year 2025 it was 101%.

Where did the money go? Two places. Inventory rose from RMB 12.681 billion to RMB 19.826 billion, an increase of RMB 7.145 billion; accounts receivable rose from RMB 6.277 billion to RMB 15.001 billion, an increase of RMB 8.724 billion. Together the two total RMB 34.827 billion, equivalent to 83% of half-year revenue. Over the same period, cash and cash equivalents fell from RMB 10.987 billion to RMB 6.397 billion.

The company's own explanation: it stepped up raw material stocking to match strong downstream orders. The evidence is there — long-term payables rose about RMB 4.16 billion in the half-year, used to lock in key materials for more than a year; prepayments were about RMB 950 million. In other words, the company is using suppliers' money and shareholders' money to buy 2027's materials in advance.

Body (3): These RMB 19.8 billion in inventories — moat or barrier lake?

This is the crux of the whole story. If downstream customers keep pulling inventory, the RMB 19.8 billion of stock converts into revenue within two quarters and the 13% net cash ratio corrects on its own; if demand reverses, it becomes a reserve for impairments. Either outcome is possible, and both hinge on one variable: capex at North American cloud providers.

In 2026Q2, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Google spent a combined USD 164.9 billion on capex, up 86% YoY. That single variable explains first-half results and is the sole risk variable for the second half. Staking a position on someone else's budget sheet is the mistake investors make most often.

Two structural issues also need airing. First, customer concentration: the top five customers account for 81.9%, the largest single customer 25.1%, and overseas revenue 94.8%. Second, gross margin sustainability: the three-year average is 36.3% versus 46.4% now, 10 points higher, while rival Eoptolink runs a gross margin of 48.44% — evidence this is not a proprietary premium but an industry-wide window price.

Body (4): Is RMB 925 worth it?

Closed at RMB 925.24 on September 18, implying a market cap of about RMB 1.09 trillion and 53.3x P/E (TTM). Three 2027 scenarios: bear case RMB 407 (net profit RMB 24 billion, 20x), base case RMB 772 (net profit RMB 35 billion, 26x), bull case RMB 1,131 (net profit RMB 43 billion, 31x). The base case sits 17% below the current price.

At this price, the bull case has to be right — cloud capex beating expectations for a fourth straight year. Not impossible, but it should not be the base assumption.

The conclusion is plain: good company, bad price. Below RMB 700 (about 23x 2027) is the first level worth discussing; below RMB 600 there is a margin of safety. If the Q3 net cash ratio returns above 50% while the stock is still below RMB 800, you can act early. If none of the three conditions is met, just watch.

Conclusion

Bottom line: a "perfect profit, tight cash" print, and on 53x P/E the only answer is to wait. Wait for three numbers: the net cash ratio in the Q3 report, the QoQ inventory change, and the 1.6T average selling price. Until they give direction, any purchase is paying a premium for someone else's capex.
