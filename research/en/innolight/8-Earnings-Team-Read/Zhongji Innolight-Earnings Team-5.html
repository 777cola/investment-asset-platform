Core view

Four readers of the same interim report converged on three points: the statements are not fraudulent, the 46% gross margin is a cyclical peak, and the cash-flow strain reflects inventory build-up rather than bad debt. They split on two: whether owner earnings should be exempted on "expansion-phase" grounds, and whether the H-share financing is a plus or a minus. Net: an "honest but dangerous" set of financials.

Three consensus points: honest financials, peak gross margin, cash as stockpiled inventory

Consensus one: the financials are not fabricated. All four read them differently, yet none found traces of accounting manipulation: goodwill of RMB 1.939 billion was left untouched, R&D expenses of RMB 1.153 billion were fully expensed (+96.9% YoY), the gross margin improvement reconciles through a price-volume breakdown, and customer and supplier concentration is disclosed as it stands.

Consensus two: the 46% gross margin is a peak reading. The three-year average is 36.3%; it now stands at 46.4%. Peer Eoptolink's 48.44% shows this is no proprietary premium. All four expect it to fall back in 2027; they differ only on whether it lands at 38% or 42%.

Consensus three: the cash-flow tightness is inventory build-up. Inventory +RMB 7.145 billion, long-term payables +RMB 4.16 billion, prepayments RMB 950 million — three pieces of evidence point the same way. Li Lu cautions that "stockpiling can also mean betting wrong," but none see it as a collection problem — the customers are North American cloud providers, with very low credit risk.

Two disagreements: owner earnings and H-share financing

Disagreement one: whether the negative RMB 0.8 billion in owner earnings should be forgiven. On Buffett's definition this is a fatal flaw: "the more it grows, the more cash it lacks." Duan Yongping and Li Lu counter that working capital tied up during expansion is investment rather than cost, and that 2025 owner earnings of +RMB 8.1 billion prove the model can generate cash. Synthesis: on a cycle-average basis the business generates cash; on a single-quarter basis it does not. Valuation should therefore rest on cycle-average profit, not single-quarter annualised figures.

Disagreement two: whether the HKD 53.4 billion H-share raise is a plus or a minus. The bull case: it delivers RMB 49 billion of ammunition, funds 2027 capacity expansion and lowers the debt ratio. The bear case: share capital up 47% over five years points to insufficient internal cash generation and dilutes existing shareholders. Synthesis: neutral to slightly negative — the financing itself is reasonable, but it cements "growth through fundraising" as this company's label.

Synthesis in one sentence

Four close reads, one conclusion: "This is an honest but dangerous earnings report—honest because there is no window dressing, dangerous because it captures exactly the top of the cycle."

The trading implication: do not buy because the earnings look good, and do not sell because the cash flow looks bad. The only usable information the report provides is "validation points"—the Q3 net cash ratio, inventory, and the 1.6T average price. These three numbers will tell us whether the cycle keeps climbing or starts to turn.

Conclusion

Synthesis: high reporting quality, poor cycle position, stretched valuation. None of the four deep reads overturns the working-paper conclusions; all four harden the case to "watch, wait for a pullback": a 46% gross margin, a 13% net cash ratio and a 53x P/E point to the same action — wait.
