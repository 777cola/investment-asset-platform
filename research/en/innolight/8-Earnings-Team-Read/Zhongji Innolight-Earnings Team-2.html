Recomputing on owner earnings: of the RMB 13.651 billion net profit, how much is actual cash

Core view

Buffett sets net profit aside and focuses on "owner earnings"—net profit plus depreciation and amortisation, less maintenance capex and working capital build. On that basis, owner earnings were negative in 2026H1: a RMB 15.87 billion increase in inventory and receivables absorbed the RMB 13.65 billion of net profit, with a further RMB 2 billion-plus of capex still to deduct. That is inevitable in an expansion phase, but it needs to be quantified clearly.

Owner Earnings: An Ugly Recalculation

The numbers: net profit RMB 13.651 billion; working capital absorption (inventory +7.145, receivables +8.724, partly offset by payables and long-term payables) of roughly RMB 11.7 billion; capex of roughly RMB 2.5–3.0 billion. Owner earnings ≈ 136.51 − 117 − 27 ≈ −RMB 0.8 billion.

The negative figure does not mean the books are being cooked; it means the company is in a "cash for share" phase. On the same basis, 2025 was clean: net profit RMB 10.797 billion, operating cash flow RMB 10.896 billion, capex RMB 2.760 billion, owner earnings of about RMB 8.1 billion. In one year it swung from +RMB 8.1 billion to −RMB 0.8 billion, and the variable is the pace of inventory build.

The key question Buffett would ask: will this RMB 11.7 billion of working capital absorption turn into profit? If the RMB 19.8 billion of inventory is delivered smoothly in the second half of 2026 and in 2027, the answer is yes, principal plus interest; if demand reverses, the answer is impairment. That is why he only buys businesses with "predictable demand."

Dividends and buybacks: RMB 6.4 billion returned to shareholders — is that enough?

Interim dividend for 2026 of RMB 1.2 per 10 shares, roughly RMB 1.4 billion, plus about RMB 5 billion of buybacks in August–September — a total of some RMB 6.4 billion. Against interim net profit of RMB 13.651 billion that is a payout of about 47%; against a market cap of RMB 1.09 trillion, a return of 0.6%.

Buffett's test is the "one-dollar principle": every dollar retained must create more than one dollar of market value. This company retained large profits over the past five years to expand capacity, and its market cap rose from the RMB 100 billion level to the RMB 1 trillion level — value created per dollar retained far exceeds one dollar. He does not object to paying out less in dividends; he objects to the contradiction of "raising capital while paying dividends."

This company does both at once: HKD 53.4 billion raised via H shares in July, then in August an interim dividend of RMB 1.4 billion plus RMB 5 billion of buybacks. That is not financial irrationality but signal management — the fundraising funds capacity expansion, while the dividend and buybacks support the share price. Buffett's verdict on such moves is usually one line: spend money on the blade, not on PR.

Conclusion

Buffett-style conclusion: a good business that must keep investing to grow, not a money printer. Owner earnings were negative through expansion periods and positive at RMB 8.1 billion in 2025 — investing here means buying the capacity expansion cycle, not the cash flow. Paying 53x P/E for a machine that constantly needs refuelling is too high a price.
