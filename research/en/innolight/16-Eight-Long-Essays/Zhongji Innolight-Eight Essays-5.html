⑤ Industry and competition: a USD 14.6 billion market split among four players — Zhongji Innolight

Demand is visible; supply is running ahead of it; the cycle is in its mid-to-late stage

Core view

We size the global 800G+1.6T optical module market at about USD 14.6 billion in 2026, or 64% of the total optical module market, with 800G shipments of 33.5 million units and 1.6T demand of 8.6–20 million units. Innolight holds about 23% share and about 50% of 800G silicon photonics. The market is large enough; the problem is that four Chinese companies and two US companies are expanding capacity at the same time, against only five customers. This is a "certain demand, runaway supply" combination.

Market size: USD 14.6 billion, 64% share

LightCounting's numbers are worth keeping in mind: in 2026, 800G and 1.6T combined will total about USD 14.6 billion, or 64% of the overall optical module market; 800G shipments will reach 33.5 million units, with 1.6T demand at 8.6 million to 20 million units.

Against Zhongji's 2026H1 optical module revenue of RMB 41.355 billion (about USD 5.8 billion), six months alone absorbed four-tenths of that USD 14.6 billion market—far above its 23% annual share. Nearly all of 2026's incremental growth therefore landed in its pocket, and in 2027 it will struggle to grow by "absorbing incremental demand" again.

Competitive landscape: four Chinese and two US manufacturers

The competitive line-up: Zhongji Innolight (about 23% share), Eoptolink (rising to No. 2 globally in 2025), TFC Optical (upstream components), Accelink (full lineup but thin gross margin), Coherent (chips + modules) and Lumentum (mainly chips).

What defines this landscape is the absence of a technology monopolist. Zhongji can do 1.6T and so can Eoptolink; Zhongji has silicon photonics, Coherent has EML; customers require at least two suppliers, so no single vendor can command exclusive pricing. Hence share leader Zhongji posts a 46.4% gross margin against 48.44% for No. 2 Eoptolink — the leader trailing the runner-up, something that would never happen in a monopolistic industry.

Positioning: mid-to-late stage, three characteristics

Feature one: pricing still rising: average price per unit RMB 2,178, +36.6%. Feature two: capacity expansion under way: Zhongji construction in progress RMB 3.707 billion (+160.7%), with the whole industry expanding in step. Feature three: upstream the tightest link: optical chips account for over 50% of cost and carry long lead times; the company locked in supply with RMB 5.06 billion in long-term payables.

Where have these three features appeared historically? Second half of 2018 (end of the 100G cycle) and first half of 2021 (end of the 400G cycle) — both were points of "best earnings, peak share price." That is not to say this time will be the same, only that this level is not cheap.

One more timeline to remember: NVIDIA's Spectrum-X CPO switches were announced for full mass production in March 2026, with the mass production ramp in August. CPO will not replace all optical modules, but it will take the short-reach segment on the switch side. After 2028, this market may no longer belong to pluggable modules.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the market is big enough, the structure bad enough, and the position too far back. A USD 14.6 billion market can support four Chinese manufacturers, but not high gross margins—there are only five customers, and they actively cultivate second suppliers. Zhongji's 23% share is real; its 46% gross margin is temporary.
