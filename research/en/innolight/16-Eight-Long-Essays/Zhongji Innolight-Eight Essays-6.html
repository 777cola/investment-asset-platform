⑥ Management: technical team holds sway and prioritises financing — Zhongji Innolight

A successful handover, an aggressive capital allocation

Core view

Governance stands out as clean among A-share manufacturers: the Wang Weixiu family retains control (Zhongji Holdings 10.31% + Wang Weixiu 5.92%), while Liu Sheng of Suzhou InnoLight runs operations, serving as chairman and president since August 2023, with 2025 compensation of RMB 4.1834 million. Selling control for RMB 2.8 billion in 2017 and then ceding the chairmanship in 2023 is no easy move. The deduction is on capital allocation: share count up 47% over five years, plus a further HKD 53.4 billion raised via an H-share offering in July 2026.

Handover: entrusting the company to the acquirer's people

In 2016–2017, Shandong-based motor winding equipment maker Zhongji Equipment acquired Suzhou InnoLight, an optical module company founded in 2008 by Georgia Tech PhD Liu Sheng, for RMB 2.8 billion.The "snake swallows elephant" deal drew scepticism at the time: how could a traditional manufacturer run a technology company with Silicon Valley roots?

The answer: it didn't. The deal closed in September 2017 and Liu Sheng became president of the listed company that same year; in August 2023 he took over as chairman, with founder Wang Weixiu stepping back to controlling shareholder.Over nine years the company moved from 100G to 1.6T and from RMB 1.2 billion in revenue to RMB 41.778 billion in half-year revenue — a rare A-share case of control actually being handed over after an acquisition.

Capital allocation: three funding rounds, one expansion storyline

November 2021 private placement of 87.08 million shares (taking total share capital to 800 million shares) to build 800G capacity; H-share listing on July 30, 2026, issuing 54.50 million shares at HKD 980 per share to raise about HKD 53.4 billion (including the over-allotment), funding capacity and materials for 1.6T/3.2T; several equity incentive plans in between.

The result: total share capital rose from 800 million to 1.178 billion shares, up 47% in five years. Over the same period, dividends went from RMB 4 per 10 shares at the 2025 interim and RMB 10 per 10 shares for full-year 2025 (RMB 1.111 billion) to RMB 12 per 10 shares at the 2026 interim (about RMB 1.4 billion, rough estimate); buybacks in August–September 2026 totaled about RMB 5 billion. Returns are improving, but always rank behind capacity expansion.

A fair assessment: all three fundraisings came ahead of demand, and the money was spent effectively. The RMB 41.778 billion of revenue in 2026H1 largely reflects the capacity and materials bought with the 2021 and 2026 financings. For a growing manufacturer, that is not a mistake; but it pins the company in the "growth funded by equity issuance" category.

Governance details: two points to remember

1. Controlling shareholder stakes are falling. Zhongji Holdings holds 121,440,135 shares (10.31%) and Wang Weixiu 69,731,451 shares (5.92%). On July 30, 2026 the company disclosed that the combined holdings of the controlling shareholder and concert parties moved by more than 1%, reflecting the H-share issuance, equity incentive exercises and secondary-market sales. Passive dilution is understandable; active selling near a trillion-yuan market cap is not a good signal.

2. Wang Weixiu pledged 1.35 million shares, or 1.9% of his holding. The risk is negligible, but it points to funding arrangements at the family level. Overall: integrity is intact (no investigations, no censures, no restatements) and governance scores 7, with deductions entirely for "financing taking priority over returns".

Conclusion

Conclusion: management scores 7. Technical judgement has been validated across two windows, and the governance structure is clean. But this is an expansion-minded management team, not a return-minded one — buying the stock means accepting that dilution eats into part of earnings growth. That is one reason the 53x P/E warrants a discount.
