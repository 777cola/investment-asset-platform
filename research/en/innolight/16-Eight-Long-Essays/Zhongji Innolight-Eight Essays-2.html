Core view

Break the business down to its smallest unit: in 2026H1 it sold 18.99 million modules, at an average price of RMB 2,178 each, a gross margin of 46.59%, and gross profit of about RMB 1,015 per module. A year earlier the figures were RMB 1,595, 39.96% and about RMB 637. Gross profit per module rose 59.3%, and the driver was not cost savings but selling faster speeds. That is the essence of the business: customers are not buying modules, they are buying a lower transmission cost per bit.

Why customers pay RMB 2,178: it comes down to port economics

A single 1.6T module lets one switch port run 1.6T of bandwidth. Without it, customers would need twice the fiber, twice the ports and twice the rack space for the same bandwidth. So what customers calculate is not "how much does a module cost" but "how much per bit, how much per watt."

That is why modules command a premium when speeds upgrade: the average price of a single 1.6T module is nearly twice that of 800G, yet the customer's cost per bit actually falls. When supply is tight, that price gap becomes excess profit for module makers; when supply is loose, customers claw the gap back — annual price cuts of 3%–5% are industry practice.

Cost structure: half the money goes to chips

The cost of a module breaks down roughly as: optoelectronic chips over 50%, packaging and testing 20%, PCB and structural parts 10%–15%, labor and manufacturing overhead 10%. The most expensive chips are EML optical chips and DSP electrical chips; electrical chips at 200G and above come almost entirely from overseas suppliers.

This structure determines two things. First, the gross margin ceiling is set by chip prices — when upstream raises prices, module makers can pass through only part of the increase (in 2026Q1 the largest supplier accounted for 38.3% of purchases, so bargaining power sits upstream). Second, silicon photonics is the real cost-down path — using silicon photonics to cut EML usage and raise integration is one of the technical reasons Zhongji pushed gross margin from 39.96% to 46.59%.

Why this business is durable: the customer's roadmap is the order book

Optical module demand is not random; it follows customers' compute roadmaps: GPUs change generation by generation, switch bandwidth doubles in step, and optical module speeds double in step. 2026 will see 800G as the mainstay plus 1.6T ramping; 2027–2028 will see 1.6T as the mainstay plus 3.2T starting to scale.

This gives the business "visibility" and also "fragility." Visibility comes from a certain roadmap (1.6T is bound to happen); fragility comes from uncertain timing (a quarter early is windfall profit, a quarter late is inventory). Zhongji's RMB 19.8 billion of inventory in 2026H1 is essentially a bet on timing.

The business in one sentence: a business that makes money by "getting it right two quarters ahead." Get it right, and gross profit per unit is RMB 1,015; get it wrong, and RMB 19.8 billion of inventory becomes impairment. That is the dividing line between a 20x and a 40x valuation.

Conclusion

Conclusion: this is a "speed arbitrage" business; the RMB 1,015 gross profit per unit is a reading of the arbitrage window. The window opens with the customer's roadmap and closes when competitors reach mass production. The key to investing is not judging how hot AI is, but judging how many more quarters this window stays open.
