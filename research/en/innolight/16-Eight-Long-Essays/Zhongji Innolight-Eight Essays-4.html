Core view

One fact stands out: the profit is real, but cash generation is thin. Net profit was RMB 13.651 billion against operating cash flow of RMB 1.800 billion, a net cash ratio of 13.2%; inventory of RMB 19.826 billion and receivables of RMB 15.001 billion together total RMB 34.827 billion. The same company posted a net cash ratio of 101% in 2025, which points not to a problem with the model but to a timing issue — it bought two years' worth of materials in half a year.

Income statement: three numbers are all you need

Revenue RMB 41.78 billion (+182.5%), net profit RMB 13.65 billion (+241.7%), gross margin 46.4%. Net profit grew 59 percentage points faster than revenue, driven by +6.63pct of gross margin expansion and a lower expense ratio. R&D expense of RMB 1.15 billion (+96.9%) and G&A expense of RMB 767 million (+164%) both grew slower than revenue.

There is no padding in this half-year's profit: no asset disposal gains, no government subsidies propping it up (other business income of RMB 423 million, 1% of the total), and goodwill of RMB 1.94 billion left unchanged. Operating profit-to-net profit conversion is normal, as is the tax rate. At the income statement level, this is a clean set of accounts.

Cash flow statement: the path to a 13.2% net cash ratio

Bridge net profit to operating cash flow: RMB 13.65 billion net profit − RMB 7.15 billion increase in inventory − RMB 8.72 billion increase in receivables + about RMB 4.16 billion increase in long-term payables ≈ RMB 1.80 billion. Three big line items, two negative.

Whether those two negatives are bad turns on one judgment: is this RMB 19.8 billion of inventory "materials already backed by orders" or "a bet on next year's demand"? The evidence leans toward the former — long-term payables lock in materials for more than a year, prepayments of RMB 950 million, and customers are North American cloud providers with good credit. The latter cannot be ruled out, however: inventory turnover days of 160 are double the normal level.

Balance sheet: composition of the 42.2% debt ratio

Total assets RMB 68.942 billion, net assets attributable to shareholders RMB 39.859 billion, liabilities RMB 29.08 billion, debt ratio 42.2% (30.18% at end-2025). The increase came not from bank borrowings but from long-term payables of RMB 5.06 billion and accounts and notes payable. This is "using suppliers' money to build inventory," not "borrowing bank money to expand capacity."

After raising about HKD 53.4 billion (roughly RMB 49 billion) in its H-share offering on July 30, cash rose from RMB 6.397 billion to the RMB 55 billion level and the debt ratio fell below 25% (rough estimate). That money solves two things: capex for the 2027 capacity expansion, and a buffer if inventory cannot be sold.

The financial truth in one sentence: the company does not lack money now; what it lacks is evidence that "inventory turns into cash." The Q3 report is that evidence.

Conclusion

Conclusion: clean profit, tight cash, healthy balance sheet. The 13.2% net-cash ratio is a one-off, distorted by inventory build-up (2025's 101% proves the point), but 160-day inventory turnover is a genuine risk. One test matters: does the Q3 net-cash ratio recover above 50%? If yes, the inventory build is converting to cash; if not, the timing bet was wrong.
