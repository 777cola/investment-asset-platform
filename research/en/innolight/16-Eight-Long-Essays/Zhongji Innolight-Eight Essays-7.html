Core view

Seven risks, two potentially fatal. The fatal ones are the "1.6T price war" (medium-high probability, high impact) and "customer order cuts" (medium probability, extremely high impact). The other five (CPO, upstream bottlenecks, FX and geopolitics, inventory impairment, share dilution) affect valuation or profit but will not determine the company's survival. Each risk comes with an observable indicator, to avoid "worrying on gut feel."

Fatal risk one: a price war, and one that is "bound to happen"

This is not a question of "whether" but of "when." The price war in optical modules never unfolds slowly: the first thing Eoptolink does once its 1.6T reaches mass production is quote a price. In 2026H1 Eoptolink's gross margin was 48.44%, higher than Innolight's, which shows it has room to cut prices.

Gauging the impact: an 8% drop in average selling price would cut gross margin by roughly 4–5 percentage points; a 15% drop would take gross margin back to 41%, 2027 net profit from RMB 35 billion to about RMB 30 billion, and the neutral target price from RMB 772 to RMB 660. That is not a disaster, but it is enough to make the current price look more expensive.

Fatal risk two: customers cut orders — low probability, outsized impact

Five customers account for 81.9%, the largest for 25.1%. If the largest customer cuts orders by 20%, revenue drops 5% outright; if the top two adjust production schedules at the same time, revenue falls more than 10%, and the RMB 19.8 billion of inventory immediately becomes a problem.

What makes this risk frightening is that it is "unpredictable." Customers' capex decisions are driven by their own AI strategies, over which Zhongji has no influence. In 2026Q2, the four major cloud providers' capex rose 86%, a growth rate that cannot last forever—the day it falls to 20% is the day orders get cut.

The remaining five: they affect valuation, not survival

CPO: In March 2026 Nvidia announced full mass production of Spectrum-X, which near term replaces short-reach links on the switch side and over the long term could take 20% of new interconnect additions. This is a "valuation ceiling" risk, not an "earnings" risk.

Upstream bottlenecks: The largest supplier accounts for 38.3% of procurement, and optical chips and electrical chips rely on overseas sources. The company locked in supply with RMB 5.06 billion of long-term payables — buying insurance, not solving the problem.

FX and geopolitics: 94.8% of revenue is overseas, with the US accounting for more than 60%, and capacity in Thailand and Taiwan provides the hedge. If tariffs cover optical modules, gross margin drops 1–2 points, but orders will not disappear (customers need the capacity).

Inventory impairment: RMB 19.8 billion of inventory and 160 days of turnover make this the largest one-off loss if demand reverses. Share dilution: up 47% over five years, and every future large capacity expansion could bring another round.

Conclusion

Conclusion: of the seven risks, the price war is "certain to happen", order cuts are "impossible to predict", and the other five are "chronic conditions". Taken together, the risk for this company is not "will it collapse" but "will half of its earnings be eaten by the cycle". Corresponding action: cap the position at 6%, use a thesis-based stop-loss, and do not take a long-term heavy position.
