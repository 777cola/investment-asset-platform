Core thesis

Read together, the four close readings converge on one sentence: this is an earnings report with excellent demand and deteriorating quality. The disagreement is over how to price that deteriorating quality. Duan Yongping rejected 15x on a cash-flow multiple; Buffett rejected the entire company on circle of competence; Munger worked backward from gross margin ten years out and concluded it is already expensive today; Li Lu offered an equal-price solution via the financing structure.

Intersection one: this is a report of "no problem with demand, problems with money"

All four agree on demand: revenue of RMB 96.221 billion, data center RMB 89.023 billion, ACIE up 138% YoY, Q3 guidance of RMB 108 billion and FY2028 growth of 70% — the numbers show demand is real and broadening.

All four also agree on funding: operating cash flow of RMB 24.077 billion against net income of RMB 59.688 billion, receivables of RMB 63.059 billion, inventory of RMB 31.575 billion, guarantees of RMB 108.5 billion and equity investments of RMB 93.940 billion — the numbers show the funding structure of this business has grown more complex.

The value of the intersection: it replaces the question "is Nvidia doing well" with "where does Nvidia's money come from." The first already has an answer; the second does not.

Is the structure a risk or an uncertainty?

Buffett and Munger call this "uncertainty"—unmeasurable, and therefore not compensable through price; the only options are to walk away or hold a minimal position. Duan Yongping and Li Lu call it "risk"—probabilities can be estimated, so it can be bought at a discount.

The distinction matters: risk can be priced, uncertainty can only be avoided. Take the USD 108.5 billion guarantee. If you know the counterparty and the trigger conditions, it is risk and the expected loss can be calculated; if you know only a cap, it is uncertainty.

Verdict: treat it as uncertainty, but not by "walking away"—by "discount + position cap." I can size the worst case (a one-time provision of RMB 30–50 billion, net profit halved, share price down 40%–50%), and that is enough for position management.

One earnings report changes the valuation approach, not the rating

Four deep reads point to the same conclusion: NVIDIA should be priced on cash flow, not net income.

On net income, FY2028 EPS of USD 14.61 at 15x looks cheap. On cash flow, FY2027 free cash flow of about USD 140 billion against a market cap of USD 5.33 trillion is 38x; if FY2028 free cash flow doubles to USD 280 billion, that is 19x. The two measures differ by 4x, and the gap comes entirely from the 40% cash conversion rate.

So the rating is unchanged (Hold), but the valuation anchor shifts from "15x FY2028 net income" to "19x FY2028 free cash flow." After the switch, the USD 248 neutral target price is unchanged, but the definition of margin of safety changes: it is no longer waiting for the P/E to fall to 12x, but waiting for the cash conversion rate to return to 60%.

This is also why I set the add-on line at USD 180 rather than USD 200: USD 180 corresponds to 26x FY2028 free cash flow, and only at that level have you truly "paid for the uncertainty."

Conclusion

Synthesis: real demand, fake cash (low conversion), complex structure. All four deep reads agree on "price it on cash flow"; only the method divides them. Bottom line: hold, do not chase, cap the position at 8%, add on dips at USD 180, trim on receivables days.
