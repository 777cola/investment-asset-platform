Core view

Buffett's order for reading financial statements is: cash flow statement, balance sheet, and income statement last. Read Nvidia in that order: operating cash flow of USD 24.08 billion against net income of USD 59.69 billion (40%), a third of balance-sheet assets are customer equity, and the income statement is so pretty there is no need to look at it. His conclusion would be: this is a good company, but its income statement is far more optimistic than its cash flow statement.

Cash flow statement: this is why he distrusts the income statement most

"Profit is an opinion, cash is a fact." He has said this for decades. This quarter Nvidia's net profit was RMB 59.688 billion, operating cash flow RMB 24.077 billion, a gap of RMB 35.6 billion. Free cash flow RMB 21.341 billion, equal to 35.8% of net profit.

He would not call this fraud. He would go through the changes line by line: receivables +RMB 22.346 billion, inventory +RMB 5.784 billion, prepayments +RMB 5.497 billion, investment income -RMB 7.771 billion. Add the four together and the accounts reconcile. Then he would ask one question: can these receivables and this inventory turn into cash next quarter?

If yes, it is a payment-terms issue; if no, it is a demand issue. And the financial statements hold no answer to that question — only next quarter will. His favorite companies are those that "collect first, ship later," such as See's Candies, such as insurers. Nvidia ships first and collects later, and its payment terms are lengthening.

Balance sheet: the RMB 93.9 billion equity stake is the real story

The liabilities side is clean: total liabilities of RMB 91.29 billion, a debt-to-asset ratio of 28.5%, and net cash of RMB 23.22 billion. Although the company issued RMB 24.9 billion of debt this quarter, that is negligible against a market cap of RMB 5.33 trillion.

The asset side is problematic. Available-for-sale equity of RMB 42.78 billion plus unlisted equity of RMB 51.16 billion equals RMB 93.94 billion, or 29.3% of total assets, up from 16.3% six months ago. The counterparties to this equity are customers — companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic and CoreWeave.

This means three things hold true at once: the company invests in its own customers; rising customer valuations generate profit for the company; and customers use this money (along with the company's guarantees) to buy the company's products. Each of these is reasonable in isolation; together they form a closed loop.

Buffett has seen this structure before. This is exactly what 1960s conglomerates did: cross-holdings, reciprocal purchasing, and inflated valuations. He called it a "chain letter." He would not say Nvidia is a fraud; he would say this structure strips the financial statements of their reference value.

Income statement: so good he daren't use it

Gross margin 75.0%, operating margin 66.2%, net margin 62.0%. These three numbers have no precedent in manufacturing history.

He would acknowledge this as proof of a moat: the CUDA ecosystem, rack-scale systems, capacity priority. He would also point to the moat's limits: no technology-industry moat in history has lasted more than two decades. IBM looked invincible in the 1980s, Intel in the 1990s, Cisco in 2000. All three still exist, but none is what it was.

He would also note the Q4 gross margin guidance of 71%–72%. A company that can hit 75% gross margin is telling you unprompted that it will fall to 71%, which means it cannot control cost-side changes. However strong the pricing power, it is no match for memory price increases — that is the physical boundary of this business.

His final verdict would most likely be: "A great company, but I can't understand its off-balance-sheet items, and I'm not sure how long a 5 trillion moat can hold. Pass."

Conclusion

Buffett-style verdict: cash flow fails the test (40%), balance-sheet structure is suspect (29% of assets are customer equity), income statement is excellent but cannot be relied on independently. He would rate the business highly, then say "outside my circle of competence." This is not a rejection of Nvidia; it is a rejection of his ability to price it with this method.
