Core thesis

Munger's method for reading financial statements is to read them backwards: first find where things are most likely to go wrong, then check whether they are going wrong. The three places most likely to go wrong at Nvidia: inventory (RMB 31.6 billion), accounts receivable (RMB 63.1 billion), off-balance-sheet guarantees (RMB 108.5 billion). In this report, the first two have already started to move; the third has only a ceiling, no structure.

Inventory is always the first to deteriorate.

Inventory of RMB 31.575 billion, up RMB 5.784 billion in a single quarter, equal to 119 days of cost of revenue. Management says this is stockpiling for Vera Rubin, a reasonable explanation—new product ramps require channel fill ahead of time.

But Munger would press: if the Rubin ramp slips a quarter behind plan, what happens to this RMB 31.6 billion? The answer: no immediate impairment; first gross margin takes the hit (price cuts to clear inventory), then cash flow (more working capital tied up). In semiconductor history, inventory is always the first warning indicator—Intel in 1985, Cisco in 2001, Nvidia itself in 2018 (mining-card inventory) all followed this pattern.

Nvidia has a buffer others lack: the USD 279 billion in supply commitments is not dead inventory but a purchase cadence that can be adjusted. But the commitments are signed: FY2027 has USD 92 billion remaining to pay, FY2028 USD 87 billion, FY2029 USD 88 billion. That means its costs are locked for the next three years, while revenue is not.

The problem, then, is payment terms, and it is structural.

Accounts receivable RMB 63.06 billion, up 54.9% QoQ, while revenue rose only 18% QoQ. Payment terms stretched from 45 days to 60 days. Management attributes this to large orders from investment-grade customers being delivered across quarters.

That explanation can hold once, not four times. The Munger-style test is simple: if the terms lengthened because customers and orders got bigger, receivables should stabilize at some new level; if it is because customers cannot pay, they will keep climbing. Two numbers next quarter will settle it: whether payment terms exceed 65 days, and whether receivables growth continues to outpace revenue growth.

There is harder evidence still: the top five customers account for 70% of receivables. That means a single bad debt could hit more than 5% of receivables. If one of them is NeoCloud and its funding environment tightens in 2027, a bad debt is not a question of probability but of timing.

The customer list is the last thing to break, and it will not recover

What Munger valued most was not the numbers but the structure. The structure of this business: five customers, four of which are building their own chips.

Google's TPU has reached its seventh generation, Amazon's Trainium is used in-house at scale, OpenAI's Jalapeño is claimed to surpass Blackwell, and Meta has been experimenting with its own inference chips. Nvidia's response is to bring Groq in for dedicated inference (Groq 3 LPX is already in mass production), an admission that "general-purpose GPUs are not the optimal solution for every scenario."

His framework runs like this: near term (1–2 years), customers cannot break away, because of capacity and ecosystem; medium term (3–5 years), training still belongs to Nvidia, but inference will be partly carved away; long term (5+ years), if AI compute becomes a standardized commodity, gross margin will drift toward 50% and the valuation multiple toward 20x.

"Invert, always invert." — If ten years from now Nvidia's gross margin is 50% and growth is 15%, is today's USD 5.33 trillion market cap expensive? At 50% gross margin, 25% net margin and 15% growth, net profit ten years out is about USD 260 billion; at 20x that is USD 5.2 trillion — in other words, today's price has already fully priced in a decade of success.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: three things that could go wrong — two already moving (inventory, payment terms), one invisible (guarantees). He would not say "don't buy"; he would say "if you buy, know that you are buying a business shifting from a seller's market to a buyer-financing market." His phrasing would be roughly: "Tell them where the problems are, and they will decide for themselves."
