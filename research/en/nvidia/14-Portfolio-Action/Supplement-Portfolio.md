## Why 5%, not 10%

On business quality alone, Nvidia deserves a 10% weight. Gross margin 75%, net margin 62%, growth 106%, moat score 8.9 — the best asset in the portfolio.

Three reasons cap it at 5%, all structural. **First, cash conversion of 36%.** That means part of its "profit" is accounting figures; once the market starts pricing on cash flow, the valuation will be re-rated. **Second, off-balance-sheet guarantees of RMB 108.5 billion plus supply commitments of RMB 279 billion.** Neither sits on the balance sheet, but both come back when demand weakens. **Third, no succession plan.** Jensen Huang is 63; the company has no COO and no designated number two — a risk no valuation model can capture.

Taken together, 5% is a "participate but don't bet" position. Won't miss the upside, won't get hurt on the downside.

## Increase position, reduce position, no stop-loss

**Add line at USD 180.** That is 12.3x FY2028 EPS of USD 14.61 and 15.5x FY2028 free cash flow (about RMB 280 billion). The implication of this price: even if FY2028 grows only 30% (bear-case EPS 11.22), it is still 16x — not expensive. USD 180 is the price at which "you don't need to believe the guidance."

**Trim triggers:** DSO above 75 days, quarterly revenue turning negative QoQ, Q4 gross margin falling below 71%, or two cloud vendors cutting 2027 capex by more than 10%. Any one trigger trims the position to 3%.

**No mechanical stop-loss.** The stock has moved −0.9% over three months (222.82 → 220.78), yet its range has been 190–228, with intraday 5% swings the norm. Anyone using a 10% stop would have been stopped out three times in three months, each time buying back at a lower price. **For a company like this, the stop belongs on the fundamentals, not the price.**

**No shorting either.** Shorting a company with 75% gross margin, demand still accelerating and USD 99 billion in buyback authorization on the books is betting your own money that someone else will stumble. Get it right and you make 20%; get it wrong and buybacks plus earnings crush you from both sides.

## Correlation with other holdings: it is the highest multiple of AI beta

NVIDIA's real identity in this portfolio is not "a company" but "the highest beta on AI capex." Rough correlation coefficients:

- **With TSMC, Micron, Broadcom: 0.8–0.9.** Same chain, same driving variable (cloud capex). Holding NVIDIA + TSMC + Micron means buying the AI compute chain three times over.
- **With Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta: 0.5–0.6.** Their capex is NVIDIA's revenue, but their share prices are also driven by advertising, cloud operations and regulation.
- **With CoreWeave, Nebius: 0.7–0.8.** NeoClouds are NVIDIA's customers, guarantee recipients and equity holdings — a triple bind.
- **With Tencent, Meituan, Xiaomi: 0.2–0.3.** China consumer internet, different driving variables.
- **With Berkshire, cash: near 0 or slightly negative.** Safe-haven assets.

This means two things. **First, if the portfolio already holds 5% TSMC and 3% Micron, NVIDIA's 5% is effectively 13% exposure to the AI compute chain — already high.** Second, when cutting AI risk, the first name to trim is not TSMC but NVIDIA, because it has the highest beta, the richest valuation and the most complex off-balance-sheet structure.

One counterintuitive point: NVIDIA is highly positively correlated with its own customers, but the direction is asymmetric. **Cloud earnings beat, NVIDIA rises; cloud earnings miss but capex guidance is raised, NVIDIA also rises; only when cloud capex is cut does NVIDIA fall.** In other words, it absorbs all of its customers' good news and amplifies the bad — the signature of an asset with beta above 1.5, and position sizing must be calculated on that basis.
