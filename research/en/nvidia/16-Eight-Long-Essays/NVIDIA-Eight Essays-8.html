Core thesis

The previous seven pieces addressed "what this company is"; this one addresses "what you should do." The first step in any decision framework is not valuation but determining who you are — a long-term holder, a swing trader, or someone unwilling to bear volatility. Each maps to a completely different set of actions.

Long-term holders: hold 5%, add up to 8% below USD 180. Swing traders: the stock's three-month displacement is −0.9% against a 20% amplitude, which makes it unsuitable for trend trading. Those unwilling to bear volatility: do not buy, given its reasonable volatility range of ±40%.

Step one: first determine who you are

The same stock warrants three different actions from three types of investor.

Long-term holders (five years or more, able to withstand a 40% drawdown): Buy 5% now and add to 8% below USD 180. The business occupies a position that comes along once in twenty years; as long as AI capex does not collapse, it keeps making money.

Swing traders (three months to one year): This is not a trend-following stock. Over the past three months it has moved from 222.82 to 220.78, a displacement of −0.9%, within a 190–228 range (20%). Trend traders get stopped out repeatedly in this kind of pattern.

Those who cannot stomach volatility: Do not buy. Its reasonable volatility range is ±40%; any 5% intraday move will keep you up at night. Buying something like this requires both "understanding it" and "holding through it" — missing either one is disqualifying.

This step matters more than valuation, because valuation is objective while risk tolerance is subjective. The same USD 220 is an opportunity for A and a disaster for B.

Step two: define "cheap" clearly

"Cheap" has three definitions, each tied to a different price.

Definition one: cheap on net income. FY2028 EPS of USD 14.61 puts USD 220.78 at 15.1x. For a company growing 70%, that is cheap. But the denominator is the company's own guidance.

Definition two: cheap on cash flow. FY2027 free cash flow of about USD 140 billion against a market cap of USD 5.33 trillion is 38x; if FY2028 doubles to USD 280 billion, that is 19x. Paying 19x for a company whose growth may fall from 70% to 20% is not cheap.

Definition three: cheap on the worst case. Bear-case EPS of USD 11.22 at a demanded 15x gives USD 168. Only below USD 168 can you say "even if guidance is cut by 70%, you don't lose."

My approach: definition two (cash flow) as the primary basis, definition three as the floor. That yields two prices: USD 180 (add-to-position line, 15.5x FY2028 free cash flow) and USD 168 (heavy-position line, 15x the bear case).

Step 3: Hard-code the sell signal

There are only three reasons to sell. Everything else is noise.

Reason one: the thesis is disproven. Five signals: quarterly revenue turns negative QoQ, receivables days break 75, Q4 gross margin falls below 71% with no recovery guided for FY2028, the guarantee records its first compensation payment, and two cloud vendors cut 2027 capex by more than 10%. Two signals: trim to 3%. Three signals: exit entirely.

Reason two: valuation is stretched. Above USD 386 (23x FY2028), trim below 5%; above USD 450 (30x), trim to 3%. Even a good company can get too expensive to hold.

Reason three: a better opportunity emerges. If the same money offers better odds elsewhere — say TSMC falls to 18x — switching is fine. But do not sell to "lock in gains": this company's earnings are still growing.

Not reasons to sell: a 10% share-price drop, a big-name investor turning bearish, news out of China, AI bubble talk, a quarterly gross margin 1 point light. All five happened in the past three months, and anyone who sold on each one has already capitulated below USD 190.

Step 4: Timeline

Mapping the key milestones for the next twelve months is more useful than watching the tape every day.

  • September–October 2026: Watch Q3 supply-chain data (TSMC CoWoS capacity, SK hynix HBM shipments) — the leading indicators for whether Nvidia's Q3 guidance can be met.
  • 21 October 2026: GTC Berlin — watch the Rubin follow-on roadmap, LPU customer progress and sovereign AI orders.
  • 17 November 2026: FY2027Q3 results — the most important checkpoint. Three numbers: revenue vs RMB 108 billion, gross margin 74%, receivables days within 65.
  • December 2026–January 2027: 2027 cloud capex guidance (Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Meta report between October and February) — the leading indicator for Nvidia's FY2028 demand.
  • February 2027: FY2027Q4 results — the quarter gross margin bottoms; verify 71%–72%.
  • May 2027: FY2028Q1 results — the quarter price increases land; verify whether gross margin returns to 72%–73%.

One last point: the core of this framework is not prediction, it is preparation. I do not know whether AI capex will peak in 2028, but I know which three numbers to watch when it does and how far to cut the position. The only thing investors control is their own actions in each scenario.

Conclusion

Decision framework: first establish your investor profile (long-term / swing / cannot tolerate volatility), then define "cheap" precisely (15x net profit, 19x cash flow, worst case USD 168), then hard-code the sell signals (five thesis signals, two valuation lines), and finally track against a timetable.Current action: hold 5%, add on dips at USD 180, cap at 8%; November 17 is the nearest watershed.
