Core thesis

Nvidia's risk list is short but unusually structured: five items can be quantified (customer concentration, China revenue going to zero, valuation, supply commitments, technology roadmap) and one cannot (the counterparties and trigger conditions behind the USD 108.5 billion in guarantees). Quantifiable risks can be priced; unquantifiable ones can only be managed through position limits.

The value of this list lies not in acknowledging that the risks exist, but in pairing each one with an observable indicator and a falsification condition.

Five that can be calculated: none of the magnitudes is fatal

Customer concentration (three customers at 44%). Worst case: one customer brings all inference in-house, leaving a 10%–15% revenue gap and a 25% share price decline. Quantifiable, trackable, not fatal—these five are the world's best cash-flow companies and will not suddenly disappear.

China to zero (already happened). FY2026 China revenue USD 19.7 billion, down from about USD 25 billion the prior year, with a USD 4.5 billion charge on H20. Guidance now excludes China data-center compute revenue. This risk is already cleared; what remains is an option on "will it recover," not a risk of "will we lose it."

Valuation (15x FY2028). Worst case: FY2028 growth of just 30%, EPS USD 11.22, at 15x that is USD 168, down 24%. Quantifiable.

Supply commitments (USD 279 billion). Worst case: demand weakens, part of the USD 175 billion in FY2028/FY2029 purchase commitments becomes inventory impairment, a one-time charge of USD 20–40 billion, net income halves for a quarter. Quantifiable.

Technology route (ASIC). Worst case: 30% of inference shifts to in-house silicon, growth drops from 70% to 40%, valuation drops from 15x to 12x, down 35%. Quantifiable, but on a three-to-five-year timescale.

The one that cannot be counted: three outcomes of a guarantee

Against the USD 108.5 billion guarantee cap, the company disclosed only the total — not the counterparties, tenors or trigger conditions. Three possible outcomes:

Outcome one (highest probability): no trigger. The underlying is leases to investment-grade customers, compute demand is real, customers pay on schedule, and the guarantee expires unused. In this case the USD 108.5 billion is just a number and does not affect profit in any year.

Outcome two: partial compensation. One NeoCloud fails to raise financing and halts its project; Nvidia compensates its lease obligations, RMB 5–20 billion, taken as a one-time charge. The stock falls 15%–20%, but fundamentals are unaffected.

Outcome three: contagion. Financing conditions tighten in 2027 and two or three NeoClouds run into trouble at once; compensation plus impairment of supply commitments plus bad receivables stack up, producing a one-time charge of more than RMB 50 billion. In this case the stock falls 40%–50%, but the company survives — because its net cash is RMB 23.2 billion, free cash flow is positive, and RMB 99 billion of buyback authorization remains.

I assign probabilities of 70% / 22% / 8% to the three outcomes. Expected loss is modest, but variance is enormous. That is why it can only be handled with a position limit, not with a valuation discount — you cannot put a precise discount on an unknown distribution.

Four overrated risks

The market is worried about four things that I believe are overpriced.

First, the AI bubble bursting. The core argument behind the bubble thesis is "unclear returns on compute investment." But the five major cloud companies fund capex from operating cash flow and carry over USD 2 trillion in backlog — this is not the 2000 telecom bubble, which was built on debt-funded fibre no one used. The bubble may be at the NeoCloud layer, not the cloud company layer.

Second, AMD taking share. Three years of chasing, the same growth rate, no share movement. Its role is to pressure pricing, not to replace.

Third, China. It has already gone from "largest increment" to "zero" — and it is a zero the company itself wrote into guidance. The worst case is simply staying at zero.

Fourth, gross margin decline. A 71%–72% gross margin is still above TSMC's 67.7%. The real risk is not the margin dropping, but it not recovering after the drop — the company says it returns to 72%–73% in FY2028, which needs verification, but is not the current crisis.

Tail scenario: if three things happen at once

What happens when three negative variables stack up?

Take the second half of 2027: ① cloud capex growth slows from +60% to +5%; ② one NeoCloud defaults, triggering RMB 10 billion in compensation; ③ Q4 gross margin comes in at 71% and FY2028 fails to return to 72%.

Result: FY2028 revenue grows just 20% (RMB 580 billion), net margin falls to 48% on lower gross margin plus impairments, net profit RMB 278.4 billion, EPS USD 11.5. The market assigns 12x — growth below 20% forces a switch to a cyclical valuation — USD 138, down 37%.

I put a 15% probability on this scenario, and its trigger signals are observable: a downward revision to cloud capex guidance, widening NeoCloud bond spreads, Q4 gross margin below 71%. If two of the three appear, cut the position rather than wait for the earnings report.

To close with one number: in the worst case this company falls 40%–50%; in the best case it rises 75%. Probability-weighted it is positive expectancy, but the variance demands a position cap. That is where the 8% comes from.

Conclusion

Risk checklist conclusion: five risks are quantifiable (worst case down 35% combined), one is not (worst case down 50%) and four are overstated (bubble, AMD, China, gross margin). Price offsets the quantifiable risks, position limits cap the unquantifiable one, and the overstated risks do not inform our trading. Action: 5% position (8% cap); cut the position if two of the three tail signals appear.
