Core thesis

Four numbers in this report have been misread: net margin of 62% (the core figure is actually 55%), ROE of 124% (suppressed by buybacks), gross margin of 75% (already at the ceiling), and growth of 106% (converging quarter on quarter). Only one number has been ignored: free cash flow of USD 21.341 billion, just 35.8% of net profit.

That ignored number is the key to understanding Nvidia in 2026.

Misreading 1: Of the 62% net margin, RMB 7.8 billion came from "someone else"

Net profit of RMB 59.688 billion on revenue of RMB 96.221 billion, a net margin of 62.0%. That figure would be inconceivable in any industry.

But it includes USD 7.771 billion in gains on equity securities (RMB 23.707 billion cumulative in H1). That money comes from revaluing equity stakes in companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic and CoreWeave — as their valuations rise, Nvidia's profit rises; as they fall, profit falls.

Excluding that: pre-tax profit of RMB 63.736 billion, post-tax RMB 53.20 billion, net margin 55.3%. 55.3% is still an extreme in manufacturing history (TSMC's net margin was about 55.6% in Q2 2026 — roughly the same), but it is an order of magnitude less imaginative than 62%.

Why does this matter? Because investment gains are unsustainable, unpredictable and move in the same direction as the core business (client valuations rising signals AI heat; AI heat means Nvidia sells well). That means it amplifies losses in a downturn: client valuations fall plus orders fall — a double hit.

Misreading 2: the 124% ROE was bought, not earned

Annualized ROE of 124% (59,688×4 ÷ average equity of 193,139). That number will excite many people, but half of it is an accounting artifact.

Equity rose from RMB 157.29 billion (January 2026) to RMB 228.98 billion (July 2026), up RMB 71.7 billion in six months; over the same period buybacks totaled RMB 39.04 billion and dividends RMB 6.29 billion. Buybacks shrink the denominator, profit lifts the numerator, and the two together produce 124%.

The more accurate metric is ROIC: annualized NOPAT of about RMB 212.8 billion (operating profit of RMB 63.73 billion × 4 × (1−16.5%)), invested capital of about RMB 205.9 billion (equity 228.98 + debt 33.37 − cash and bonds 56.59), ROIC of about 103%.

What does 103% mean? TSMC is about 30%, Apple about 50%, Microsoft about 25%. Nvidia is 2–4x those levels. This is its true capital efficiency, and the confidence behind its willingness to issue USD 24.9 billion of debt and buy USD 42.4 billion of customer equity.

Misreading 3 and 4: gross margin is the peak, growth is converging

Gross margin of 75.0% is taken by the market as evidence of strong pricing power. True, but it is also the peak of this cycle. The company itself guided downhill: Q3 74.0%, Q4 71%–72%, back to 72%–73% by FY2028.

Revenue +106% is the same story. Doubling year on year sounds like acceleration, but the sequential rate is converging: data center revenue over the last four quarters was 51.2 → 62.3 → 75.2 → 89.0 billion, with sequential growth of 21.7%, 20.7%, 18.4%. The absolute number is getting bigger; the growth rate is getting slower.

Put the two numbers together and they point to the same conclusion: 2026 is the best quarter for this business, not the start of its best year. Peak gross margin + converging sequential growth + costs locked through FY2029 — these three together spell downside risk in the second half of 2027.

Granted, the company's FY2028 +70% guidance implies growth continues in 2027. But the quality of that growth is changing: from margin-driven to scale-driven, from cash-driven to financing-driven.

The one being overlooked: free cash flow is only 36% of net profit

Net profit RMB 59.688 billion, operating cash flow RMB 24.077 billion, free cash flow RMB 21.341 billion. Conversion rate 35.8%, versus 50.9% a year earlier.

This is the most important number in the entire report, because it determines the valuation method. A company converting 36% of earnings into cash cannot be priced on DCF (the cash flow is too ugly); only PE works (the earnings look good). And for a company priced on PE, once profit growth slows, the de-rating is double.

Where did the money go? Receivables +RMB 22.346 billion, inventory +RMB 5.784 billion, prepayments +RMB 5.497 billion — RMB 33.6 billion across the three; add investment income of RMB 7.771 billion that never hits cash. All of it is explainable, nothing suspicious.

But "explainable" is not the same as "no problem." A company whose revenue doubled for four straight quarters, whose payment terms stretched from 45 days to 60 days, and whose inventory rose from RMB 21.4 billion to RMB 31.6 billion — put those three numbers together and there is only one explanation: it is stocking ahead of larger demand, while customers are paying more slowly. Stocking ahead is growth if right and impairment if wrong; slower payment means customers got bigger, or customers got poorer.

This is Nvidia's real situation in 2026: on the books it is impeccable; in cash flow it is already overdrawing.

Conclusion

Financial reality: the 62% net margin should be discounted by 30% (55.3%); the 124% ROE should be halved (ROIC 103%); the 75% gross margin is a peak, not the norm; the 106% growth rate is converging. The only figure that needs no discount is free cash flow of RMB 21.341 billion — it is too low, so low that it changes the valuation method. Conclusion: at 15x net profit it looks cheap; at 19x cash flow it is not cheap. The market is switching from the first method to the second, and that switch is the source of the stock's volatility.
