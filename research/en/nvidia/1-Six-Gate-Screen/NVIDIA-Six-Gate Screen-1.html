Six-Gate Screen — NVIDIA

Core view

Six checks score 42 points, with deductions falling entirely on "price" and "role," not on the business itself. The first three checks are near full marks: FY2027Q2 revenue of USD 96.221 billion, +106% YoY; data center revenue of USD 89.023 billion, +117% YoY; gross margin 75.0%, net margin 62.0%. This is the best form manufacturing can achieve: customers queue up, capacity is bought out in advance, and there is no need to build fabs.

The fourth check loses points on the role change: supply and capacity commitments rose from USD 119 billion to USD 279 billion, it provides credit support of up to USD 108.5 billion for data center projects, and it has committed to purchase USD 36 billion of future cloud services. The fifth check scores 3 points, because the 15x FY2028 P/E implied by the USD 5.33 trillion market cap rests on a denominator of "growth of about 70%" that the company itself gave on August 26. Good company, good business, expensive certainty: the only action is to hold, not to buy.

The first three hurdles: a company where customers queue for goods

Start with the business. FY2027Q2 revenue was USD 96.221 billion, up 18% QoQ and 106% YoY — a fourth consecutive quarter of acceleration. Data center revenue was USD 89.023 billion, 92.5% of the total, split between hyperscale cloud at USD 48.710 billion (up 102% YoY) and AI cloud, industrial and enterprise at USD 40.313 billion (up 138% YoY). Edge computing was USD 7.2 billion, up 27% YoY.

The income statement is clean in a way that does not look like manufacturing: gross margin 75.0%, operating expenses USD 8.408 billion for an expense ratio of 8.7%, operating income USD 63.734 billion for an operating margin of 66.2%, and net income USD 59.688 billion. Every USD 100 of goods sold therefore drops USD 66 into operating profit — against a TSMC gross margin of 67.7% and Hon Hai at 6.12% over the same period, Nvidia sits at the very top of the entire chain.

Three moats. The first is CUDA: two decades of accumulated switching costs, which are not a matter of money but of code, talent and time. The second is rack-scale systems: Vera Rubin is not a GPU but a combination of Vera CPU + Rubin GPU + NVLink + Spectrum-6 networking + the full rack, and a competitor that wants to beat it must first build an entire system. The third is capacity priority: USD 279 billion in supply commitments, essentially cash used to buy up the next two years of HBM and advanced packaging capacity. On the call the company itself gave a quantified metric — the commercial value per gigawatt of compute: USD 18 billion in the Hopper era, USD 25 billion for Grace Blackwell, USD 40 billion for Vera Rubin. The money earned from the same kilowatt-hour is doubling, and that is the hardest evidence of the moat.

Stage four: from selling shovels to becoming half a developer

On management, the score is 7. The deduction is not about capability. Jensen Huang founded the company in 1993 and has spent thirty-three years on one thing: accelerated computing. He has never missed a beat: Hopper in 2023, Blackwell in 2024, Vera Rubin in 2026, generation after generation, each announced as the "fastest ever" in its fastest-ramping quarter. The company returned USD 26 billion to shareholders this quarter (USD 20 billion buyback + USD 6 billion dividend), with USD 99 billion remaining on the buyback authorization.

The deduction is about boundaries. Three lines of numbers disclosed this quarter changed the company's risk profile: supply and capacity commitments of USD 279 billion (USD 119 billion last quarter, +134%); credit guarantees for data center projects with maximum total exposure of USD 108.5 billion (including USD 105 billion of project guarantees and USD 3.5 billion for AI cloud customers); and committed purchases of certain future cloud services of USD 36 billion. According to the Australian Financial Review's tally of the 10-Q notes, six off-balance-sheet commitments total roughly USD 530.5 billion.

The company knows how outsiders will read this. On the earnings call, Jensen Huang volunteered: "We know that outsiders will interpret these arrangements as circular financing." His explanation: these AI labs have proven technology and real demand; the money Nvidia puts in is only a small fraction of free cash flow over the same period; compute is fungible and can be reallocated to other customers; the risk is controllable. Half of that is right—compute can indeed be resold; the other half cannot be verified—to whom, at what price, and how quickly.

One more critical sentence went largely unnoticed: next year, AI lab businesses that need support from Nvidia's balance sheet will account for roughly a quarter of the company's total business. When a quarter of a company's revenue requires its own credit support, this is no longer a "selling equipment" balance sheet.

Fifth and sixth hurdles: the 15x denominator is the company's own figure

USD 220.78, 24.19 billion shares, USD 5.33 trillion market cap, TTM P/E around 28x. Management guides FY2028 revenue growth of about 70%, implying revenue of roughly USD 641.3 billion; at a 55% net margin that is USD 352.7 billion of net income and EPS of USD 14.61 — 15.1x. On the multiple alone, this is one of the cheapest large-cap tech stocks in the market.

The problem is where that denominator comes from. Not historical results, not third-party consensus, but forward guidance more than a year out, given for the first time by CFO Colette Kress on the August 26 call — and she explicitly called the guidance "supply-constrained", with real demand "far above 70%". In other words, 70% is the capacity ceiling, not the demand ceiling. That sounds more optimistic, but it is also a trillion-dollar company forecasting itself two years out, without historical precedent.

Three scenarios: bear case USD 168 (FY2028 growth of only 30%, 15x), base case USD 248 (60% growth, 17x), bull case USD 386 (77% growth, 23x). Upside 12%, downside 24%, odds 1 : 0.5. That is why gate five scores only 3: not that it is expensive, but that it is not thoroughly expensive and only conditionally cheap.

Gate six scores 5: the buy premise can be stated clearly but takes too long to verify — FY2027Q3 revenue of USD 108 billion, FY2028 growth of 70%, gross margin bottoming at 71%–72% in Q4 before recovering. All three numbers can be checked in quarterly reports, but that means waiting for November 17, then February and May next year. Until then, any purchase is a faith vote on guidance.

Conclusion

42/60, overall rating Hold; action: do not chase the rally.Full marks on three gates speak to the business, doubts on two speak to the role, one failure speaks to the price. Existing holders have no reason to sell — demand, supply and ecosystem are all intact; new money has no reason to buy — USD 220 already discounts the company's own guidance.Triggers are fixed: the share price falling below USD 180 (about 12.3x FY2028), or FY2027Q3 (November 17) and FY2027Q4 both delivering the 70% slope for two consecutive quarters.Either one and the rating moves up one notch.
