## Layer one: revenue mix — demand is broadening, not narrowing

Data center revenue reached USD 89.023 billion, up 18% QoQ and 92.5% of total revenue. Hyperscale cloud contributed USD 48.710 billion (up 102% YoY, up 13% QoQ) and ACIE USD 40.313 billion (up 138% YoY, up 25% QoQ); edge computing was USD 7.200 billion (up 27% YoY). The two segments sum exactly to USD 89.023 billion, confirming the scope does not blend in edge business.

The key number is not the USD 89 billion but the structure. ACIE rose 25% QoQ and hyperscale cloud 13% QoQ — the faster-growing half is the half that was not on the customer list before. Per company disclosure, sovereign AI revenue rose 35% QoQ and more than doubled YoY, while NeoCloud installed capacity is targeted to go from 3GW at end-2025 to 8GW by end-2026.

QoQ growth is converging, however. Data center revenue over the last four quarters was 51.2 → 62.3 → 75.2 → 89.0 billion, with QoQ growth of 21.7%, 20.7% and 18.4%. Absolute dollars are accelerating while growth rates slow — the standard curve for a large platform entering its mature volume-ramp phase. On this curve, QoQ growth falling below 10% in some quarter of 2027 is inevitable, not a surprise — knowing this in advance means it will not be mistaken for evidence of a collapse.

The China treatment also deserves a note: H200 shipments under US government licenses in Q2 accounted for less than 1% of data center revenue, and Q3 guidance assumes zero China data center compute revenue. A company that takes the relevant revenue from its second-largest market to zero and still guides to +12% QoQ is telling you demand genuinely comes from elsewhere.

## Tier two: gross margin — 75% is the ceiling this cycle

Gross margin 75.0%, identical on a GAAP and non-GAAP basis, as non-GAAP no longer excludes stock-based compensation from FY2027Q1. Cost of revenue RMB 24.079 billion, +87% YoY, trailing revenue growth of +106%, so scale effects persist. Operating margin 66.2%, up from 65.6% last quarter.

Guidance, however, points down: Q3 74%±50bp, a trough of 71%–72% in Q4, then recovery to 72%–73% in FY2028 as price increases take effect. The driver: memory price hikes are "far exceeding prior expectations" and will keep rising next year.

The implication is clear: NVIDIA is trading gross margin for certainty. It locked in memory and manufacturing capacity early and paid for it with higher costs; the payoff is that in 2026, with HBM and advanced packaging in full shortage, it secured the most supply. That trade pays in a shortage cycle—selling an extra dollar of product matters far more than preserving an extra point of gross margin.

The risk is timing. If demand weakens in 2027H2 while costs are already locked through FY2029 (supply commitments of USD 87 billion in FY2028 and USD 88 billion in FY2029), then 71% is not the bottom—it is the starting point. This is the most easily overlooked line in the entire earnings report, because it sets the starting point for FY2028 EPS—a 2-point difference in gross margin means roughly USD 13 billion difference in FY2028 net income and USD 0.54 difference in EPS.

## Layer 3: Costs and profits — operating leverage is already maxed out

Operating expenses RMB 8.408 billion, +10% QoQ and +55% YoY, for an expense ratio of 8.7%. R&D RMB 7.054 billion (+64% YoY); sales and administrative RMB 1.354 billion (+21% YoY). At an 8.7% expense ratio, every RMB 10 billion of revenue adds only RMB 870 million of expenses, with the rest dropping through to operating profit.

That leverage, however, is now exhausted. Q3 guidance points to operating expenses of RMB 9.2 billion GAAP and RMB 9.0 billion non-GAAP, with full-year operating expense growth of "mid-to-high teens, around 50%". Expense growth will run well ahead of the past two years for two reasons: an expanded product matrix (five lines — GPU, CPU, networking, LPU, robotics), and the compute and headcount costs of adopting AI tools at scale.

Other income RMB 7.773 billion, including RMB 7.771 billion of gains on equity securities (H1 total RMB 23.707 billion). This lifted net profit from RMB 53.2 billion to RMB 59.7 billion and net margin from 55.3% to 62.0%. The gains are real but unstable — when the market falls, they bite back. Valuation should rest on the 55% net margin, not 62%.

Tax RMB 1.182 billion, an effective tax rate of 16.5%, within the 16%–18% guidance. Tax will not be a swing factor, which shows earnings are in fact highly predictable — as long as revenue and gross margin are predictable.

## Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — the layer that matters most

Operating cash flow of RMB 24.08 billion against net profit of RMB 59.69 billion leaves a RMB 35.6 billion gap, explained by three "+" items: accounts receivable +RMB 22.35 billion (balance RMB 63.06 billion, 60-day terms), inventory +RMB 5.78 billion (balance RMB 31.58 billion) and prepayments and other assets +RMB 5.50 billion, plus investment income of −RMB 7.77 billion with no cash impact.

On the investing side, capex was RMB 2.68 billion against RMB 15.82 billion of equity securities purchases. Cash spent buying other companies' equity is 6x the cash spent on its own equipment. H1 equity purchases total RMB 42.40 billion; available-for-sale equity of RMB 42.78 billion plus unlisted equity of RMB 51.16 billion comes to RMB 93.94 billion, or 29.3% of total assets (16.3% at the start of the year).

On the financing side, bond issuance of RMB 24.90 billion (long-term borrowings 7.47 → 32.37 billion), buybacks of RMB 19.73 billion and dividends of RMB 6.05 billion. RMB 25.78 billion was returned, exceeding free cash flow of RMB 21.34 billion for the quarter — the gap was borrowed. The quarter also included RMB 2.94 billion of cash paid for the Groq asset transaction.

The balance sheet itself is solid: total assets RMB 320.27 billion, total liabilities RMB 91.29 billion, debt-to-asset ratio 28.5% and net cash RMB 23.22 billion. But the nature of the assets has changed: three-tenths are equity in customers and potential customers, and a quarter of next year's revenue must be supported by its own balance sheet. In one sentence: it increasingly resembles a holding company, not a chip company.

## Layer five: how to read the three off-balance-sheet lines

Three lines of numbers, three ways to read them.

Supply and capacity commitments of RMB 279 billion: This is not debt but a purchase commitment to suppliers. The risk is not "cannot pay" but "bought it and cannot sell it." Remaining payments are RMB 92 billion in FY2027, RMB 87 billion in FY2028 and RMB 88 billion in FY2029—the cost curve is already drawn; the revenue curve is not. If demand falls 20% in 2027, the portion of the RMB 279 billion due in the latter two years will need impairment or renegotiation.

Credit guarantees of RMB 108.5 billion: A ceiling figure, triggered only by counterparty default or the satisfaction of agreed conditions. The company says this compute ultimately flows to investment-grade customers or customers backed by investment-grade entities—if true, real exposure is small; if not, it is a cohort of NeoClouds expanding on debt. The key point is that the company has not disclosed the counterparty list; this is "uncertainty," not "risk."

Cloud service purchase commitments of RMB 36 billion: Nvidia has committed to buying a portion of future cloud services. This line is the easiest to overlook, but it means the company appears on both the buy side and the sell side—buying cloud services for internal R&D and also to provide a floor for customers. The amount is not large, but the nature of it is worth noting.

## The three things to watch most closely

First, is the 71% gross margin the floor? Q4 will settle it. If Q4 lands at the low end of 71%–72% and FY2028 cannot recover above 72%, cost lock-in is starting to backfire and FY2028 EPS must be cut 5%–8%.

Second, will receivables days of 60 breach 70? This is the only dividing line between a "payment-terms problem" and a "demand problem." Holding within 65 days points to a change in customer mix; breaching 75 days means someone is stalling on payment.

Third, will inventory backing the RMB 279 billion supply commitment be impaired? Current inventory of RMB 31.575 billion, or 119 days of cost of sales, is a healthy stocking level. If revenue misses guidance next quarter while inventory keeps rising, that is the inflection point.

The first two of these three questions will have answers by November 17. That is also why I put the nearest checkpoint on the Q3 report rather than the share price—this company's problem is not in market sentiment; it is in the three financial statements and the three footnotes.
