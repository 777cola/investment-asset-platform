Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — NVIDIA

NVIDIA (NVDA) | A pretty income statement, but the cash flow statement tells the truth
Research date 2026/08/31  ·  Price $220.78 (+1.48%)  ·  Market cap Approximately USD 5.33 trillion (24.15 billion shares)  ·  PE(TTM) About 28x (roughly 15x forward FY2028)

Core thesis

The income statement is flawless: revenue of RMB 96.221 billion, gross margin 75.0%, operating margin 66.2%, net profit RMB 59.688 billion. The cash flow statement is not: operating cash flow RMB 24.077 billion, only 40.3% of net profit; free cash flow RMB 21.341 billion, only 35.8%. The gap is not fraud — it is accounts receivable +RMB 22.3 billion, inventory +RMB 5.8 billion, prepayments +RMB 5.5 billion, plus RMB 7.771 billion of investment income that does not enter cash.

Income statement: three numbers that tie together

Revenue 96,221, cost of revenue 24,079, gross profit 72,142, operating expenses 8,408, operating income 63,734. These five figures are internally consistent, with no one-off items inflating the result. Operating margin of 66.2% is higher than last quarter's 65.6%, showing economies of scale are still at work.

Net income of 59,688 needs to be broken down: other income, net was RMB 7.773 billion, of which gains on equity securities were RMB 7.771 billion (H1 cumulative RMB 23.707 billion). This money comes from revaluation of equity investments in companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic and CoreWeave, and will bite back when the market moves the other way. Excluding this, net income was RMB 53.2 billion and net margin 55.3% — that is the core profitability, and it is already absurdly high.

Per share: GAAP diluted USD 2.46, non-GAAP USD 2.22. Non-GAAP is lower than GAAP because from FY2027Q1 the company includes stock-based compensation in its non-GAAP measure, a more honest approach worth noting.

Cash flow statement: the money printer is still running, but at half speed

Operating cash flow was RMB 24.077 billion, versus RMB 15.365 billion a year earlier, up 57% in absolute terms. But net profit rose from RMB 26.422 billion to RMB 59.688 billion, up 126%. Cash growth trailed profit growth by 69 percentage points, and the conversion rate fell from 58.1% to 40.3%.

Where the money went is visible item by item: accounts receivable rose RMB 22.346 billion (balance RMB 63.059 billion, 60-day terms); inventory rose RMB 5.784 billion (balance RMB 31.575 billion); prepayments and other assets rose RMB 5.497 billion. The three items total RMB 33.6 billion, exactly equal to the gap between net profit and operating cash flow minus investment income.

Investing activities are more interesting: purchases of equity securities were RMB 15.822 billion in the quarter and RMB 42.404 billion cumulatively in H1; purchases of fixed assets were RMB 2.677 billion. Money spent buying others' equity was 6x that spent on its own equipment. Financing activities: RMB 24.896 billion in debt issued, RMB 19.732 billion in buybacks, RMB 6.047 billion in dividends. A net-cash company issuing RMB 25 billion of debt to buy others' equity is the question Nvidia most deserves to be asked in 2026.

Balance sheet: clean, but the nature of that cleanliness has changed

Total assets RMB 320.272 billion, total liabilities RMB 91.288 billion, debt-to-assets ratio 28.5%; equity RMB 228.984 billion, up RMB 71.7 billion in six months. Cash RMB 22.443 billion + marketable bonds RMB 34.143 billion − debt RMB 33.366 billion = net cash RMB 23.220 billion; still a net cash company.

What changed is the asset mix. Marketable equity securities RMB 42.783 billion (RMB 12.886 billion at the start of the year) + unlisted equity RMB 51.157 billion (RMB 22.251 billion at the start of the year) = USD 93.940 billion of equity investments, 29.3% of total assets. Six months ago that ratio was 16.3%. The company itself says it has invested nearly USD 50 billion in frontier AI labs.

A chip company holding 30% of its assets in the equity of customers and potential customers is fine accounting-wise, but commercially it means two things: the fair value of these stakes flows through the income statement (so profit swings with the market); and these customers are also revenue sources (so revenue quality is tied to the value of the equity). Buffett would stop here, because this is the kind of circularity he dislikes most.

Conclusion

In one sentence: the income statement tells you about demand; the cash flow statement tells you who is paying for that demand. Both statements are true, but the latter matters more. When a company's net profit growth is twice its cash growth, valuation should be based on cash flow, not net profit. Watch three numbers: receivables turnover days (60 days), operating cash flow/net profit (40%), and equity investments as a share of assets (29%). Deterioration in all three together is the signal that the nature of the business has changed.
