Risk assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — NVIDIA

NVIDIA (NVDA) | Three quantifiable risks, one that cannot be quantified

Core thesis

Nvidia's risk list is short, but one item defies calculation. What can be calculated: customer concentration (three customers at 44%), China going to zero (already happened), valuation (15x FY2028, whose denominator is the company's own guidance). What cannot: of the USD 108.5 billion guarantee ceiling, how much sits with the same cohort of debt-fueled NeoClouds. The most expensive thing in investing is not big risk, but unmeasurable risk.

Customer concentration is not a flaw; it defines this business

Three customers account for 44% of receivables and the top five for 70%. That sounds alarming, but look closer: those five are the five strongest cash generators in the world. Combined operating cash flow at Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta and Oracle is more than enough to buy out Nvidia's entire annual capacity.

The real risk is not concentration but the concentrated few starting to build their own. Google's TPU is already in its seventh generation, Amazon's Trainium is deployed at scale in-house on inference, and OpenAI's Jalapeño is claimed to outperform Blackwell.The risk of customer concentration is not that they stop buying, but that they buy less and less, and buy only for training, not inference.

Sizing that risk: if 30% of inference shifts to in-house chips, Nvidia's revenue growth falls from 70% to 40% and its valuation from 15x to 12x, taking the stock down 35%. Calculable, trackable, not fatal.

Credit guarantees: the one line item that cannot be pinned down

Company disclosure: credit guarantees on data centre projects, with maximum total exposure of USD 108.5 billion (USD 105 billion of project guarantees plus USD 3.5 billion for AI cloud customers); supply and capacity commitments of USD 279 billion; cloud service purchase commitments of USD 36 billion. Per AFR, the six off-balance-sheet commitments summarised in the 10-Q notes total approximately USD 530.5 billion.

Two points need to be clear. First, the USD 108.5 billion is a ceiling, not a liability: it is triggered only when agreed conditions are met or a counterparty defaults. Second, the company repeatedly stresses that "Nvidia does not lend directly"; instead it builds platforms with six partners—Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR—to mobilise USD 500 billion of third-party capital, and what it provides is a guaranteed payment commitment and credit enhancement.

Neither statement resolves the core questions: who the counterparties are, how concentrated they are, and what the trigger conditions are. The company disclosed a striking ratio—the AI lab business that will need support from Nvidia's balance sheet next year accounts for roughly one quarter of total business. One quarter, on FY2028 revenue of USD 641.3 billion, is USD 160 billion of revenue, backed by the same group of companies that are not yet profitable.

My approach: do not forecast default probability, only apply a valuation discount. Cutting the neutral scenario from 17x to 15x is the discount for this risk.

Technology roadmap and policy: trackable, no need for excessive concern

Technology roadmaps are slow variables. ASIC cost advantages in inference are real, but Nvidia has three buffers: no near-term replacement in training, reusable general-purpose compute, and the Groq LPU plugging the gap in interactive inference. The real watershed is 2027—if inference revenue then exceeds half of data center revenue and Nvidia's share starts to slip, substitution has taken hold.

Policy is a fast variable, but the clearing has already happened. China data center compute revenue has gone to zero (compliant H200 deliveries in Q2 were under 1%, and guidance excludes China), and the marginal impact of export controls is fading as the revenue mix has already shifted to non-China customers.The remaining policy risk is antitrust: a USD 500 billion financing platform, USD 93.9 billion in equity investments and USD 108.5 billion in guarantees—put the three together and any regulator will ask whether this amounts to vertical-integration-style market lock-in.

A Li Lu-style close: the essence of this AI cycle is turning intelligence into tradable compute, and Nvidia is the toll booth. The risk for a toll booth was never too many cars—it is charging so much that someone builds a second road.The second road (ASIC plus in-house silicon) is under construction, but not yet open to traffic.

Conclusion

Three of the risks can be quantified; one cannot. The quantifiable three (concentration, China, valuation) sum to a worst case of a 35% share-price decline. The one that cannot (the guarantee structure) carries a worst case of a one-off provision in a single quarter and a share-price drop of more than 50%. Position sizing therefore follows a simple rule: no single holding above 8%, no leverage on the "AI is the national destiny" thesis, and no liquidation on the "the bubble is about to burst" call.
