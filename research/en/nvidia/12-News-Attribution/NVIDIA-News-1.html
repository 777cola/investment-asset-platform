Core thesis

Of the seven notable moves over the past three months (June to August), only two carried real information: late July, when China ordered a halt to H20 purchases (shares fell to a quarterly low of USD 190.01), and the gross margin guidance issued with the August 26 earnings (August 27 +8.74%, August 28 −4.57%).

The other five were policy noise, sector beta and influencer commentary. The test is simple: did the move change any of the three numbers — revenue, gross margin, cash conversion? If none moved, it was noise.

Signal one: China shifts from "largest incremental driver" to "zero"

At the end of June the market was still cheering the H20 ban lift; by the end of July it was back to reality: Chinese regulators told domestic firms to stop buying H20, and Nvidia asked suppliers to halt related production. The stock fell from 206.84 to 190.01, down 8.1% and a three-month low.

The key point is that this changed how guidance is framed. Q3 guidance of USD 108 billion explicitly assumes zero China data-center compute revenue, and H200 shipped under licence in Q2 was less than 1% of data-center revenue. In other words, the China line has gone from an "option" to "zero" — and a zero the company itself wrote into guidance.

Many investors are still waiting for a "China recovery" catalyst. My view: stop treating it as an option. FY2026 China revenue was USD 19.7 billion, down from about 25 billion the prior year, with a 4.5 billion charge on H20 — the line has already contributed negative value. Valuing it at zero actually makes the valuation cleaner.

Signal two: gross margin has peaked — the only new information in this earnings report

Earnings landed after the close on August 26, and the stock rose 8.74% to USD 227.98 on August 27 as the market first took in revenue of USD 96.2 billion, Q3 guidance of USD 108 billion and 70% growth for FY2028.

By August 28 it had fallen back to USD 217.55, down 4.57%, as the market digested two other disclosures: Q4 gross margin bottoming at 71%–72% (prior market expectation 73%–74%), and the USD 108.5 billion credit guarantee.

Up first, then down, all within a day and a half — the market itself had not decided what multiple to assign. But one thing is certain: this is the first time the company has explicitly guided gross margin lower, and it gave a specific path (Q3 74%, Q4 71%–72%, FY2028 back to 72%–73%).

The value of this disclosure is that it resets the FY2028 EPS starting point. The market had been calculating FY2028 on a 74% gross margin; it must now use 72.5%, cutting EPS by about 4%–6%. The 4.57% share-price decline roughly matches that downward revision — the market was efficient this time.

Noise: five unusual moves, not a single number changed

The remaining five: why each is noise, point by point.

6/5 Senate hearing (−6.2%). Warren's invitation to Jensen Huang to testify was a political gesture. A hearing will not change export rules; those rules already hard-code zero China data-center revenue, so whether he testifies has no bearing on Q3 guidance.

6/26–6/30 lockup expiry (+3.9%). Looser H20 sales licenses sound like good news, but H20 is a previous-generation product and Chinese customers have already shifted to domestic alternatives. The rally lasted two days and faded.

8/18 Gundlach warning (−2.3%). He said AI chip financing models look like the eve of the 2007 subprime crisis. A good metaphor, but not data. A metaphor cannot be used for pricing, only to remind yourself to read the footnotes.

8/24 sector pullback (−2.9%). No company-level news; this was valuation digestion across the AI sector. Drops like this usually repair within two weeks.

8/31 rebound (+1.48%). No news; it retraced part of the post-earnings decline. Anyone treating it as a signal gets whipsawed twice within two weeks.

Two things we can learn from unusual moves

First, the stock is more sensitive to gross margin than to revenue. The 8.74% gain on 8/27 was driven by revenue and guidance; the 4.57% drop on 8/28 was driven by gross margin. Net gain over the one and a half days was 4%, but the swing was 13%. The market already treats revenue growth as a given and has begun pricing the quality of growth — the most typical feature of the transition from growth stock to value stock.

Second, China news is now negative alpha. Every "lockup expiry" rumour brings a rally, then gets disproven. The late-June wave of +3.9% was given back by late July, with an extra 8% decline on top. Anyone trading on China news has lost money over these three months.

So the focus for next quarter is clear: ignore China, ignore opinion leaders, ignore the sector. Watch only the three numbers on November 17 — whether Q4 gross margin lands in the 71%–72% range, whether receivables turnover days hold at 65, and whether FY2028 guidance is maintained at 70%.

Conclusion

Seven abnormal moves, only two informative: China revenue heading to zero (end-July) and gross margin peaking (28 August). The other five were policy noise, sector beta and metaphors. Over those three months the share price went from 222.82 (6/2) to 220.78 (8/31), barely moving, after passing through a trough of 190 and a peak of 228 — large swings, zero displacement: the textbook shape of "much noise, little signal."
