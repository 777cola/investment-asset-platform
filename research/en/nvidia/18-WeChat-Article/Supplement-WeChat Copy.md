## Here's the good news first: the company made RMB 59.7 billion in a single quarter

After the close on August 26, Nvidia reported its FY2027 second-quarter results.

Revenue was USD 96.221 billion, up 106% year on year and 18% quarter on quarter. Data center revenue was USD 89.023 billion, up 117% year on year. Gross margin was 75.0%. Net income was USD 59.688 billion, up 126% year on year.

Net income of USD 59.7 billion in a single quarter. How large is that? It exceeds the annual fiscal revenue of many countries.

Guidance for the next quarter is even more striking: USD 108 billion. Note the premise — assuming zero China data center compute revenue. Even after stripping out revenue from the world's second-largest market, the company still guides to 12% quarter-on-quarter growth.

More unusually, the company for the first time gave guidance more than a year out: FY2028 revenue growth of about 70%. The CFO said this guidance is "supply constrained." Jensen Huang put it more bluntly: "If we were not constrained, our growth would be much higher."

This is good news. The good news is solid.

## Now the bad news: the money has not all been collected

In the cash flow statement of the same filing, operating cash flow was USD 24.077 billion.

Net income USD 59.688 billion, operating cash flow USD 24.077 billion. Conversion rate 40.3%. A year earlier that figure was 58.1%. Free cash flow USD 21.341 billion, conversion rate 35.8%, versus 50.9% a year earlier.

Where did the money go? Three places.

Accounts receivable rose USD 22.346 billion to a balance of USD 63.059 billion, with days sales outstanding moving from 45 days to 60 days. Inventory rose USD 5.784 billion to a balance of USD 31.575 billion, building stock for new products. Prepayments rose USD 5.497 billion. A further USD 7.771 billion of net income came from revaluation of equity investments, which brings in no cash at all.

The company's explanation for the payment terms: large orders from investment-grade customers are delivered across multiple quarters, extending the payment cycle. The explanation is reasonable, but it also says one thing — customers are getting bigger, and they are paying more slowly.

## Three things it had never done before

This quarter Nvidia did three things it had never done before.

First, it issued USD 24.9 billion of debt. Long-term borrowings jumped from USD 7.469 billion to USD 32.366 billion. This is a company with USD 23.2 billion of net cash — it does not lack money; the bond sale was to keep cash available for elsewhere.

Second, it bought USD 15.8 billion of equity (USD 42.4 billion cumulative in the first half). It now holds USD 42.783 billion of marketable equity and USD 51.157 billion of non-marketable equity, USD 93.940 billion combined, or 29.3% of total assets. Six months ago that ratio was 16.3%. The company says it has invested nearly USD 50 billion in frontier AI labs.

Third, it is guaranteeing its customers. It provides credit guarantees for data center projects, with maximum total exposure of USD 108.5 billion. Add USD 279 billion of supply commitments and USD 36 billion of cloud service purchase commitments, and off-balance-sheet commitments total roughly USD 530.5 billion, per the Australian Financial Review's tally of the 10-Q notes.

Taken together, Nvidia is doing something new: it has started financing its own customers.

## How does the company explain it, and does the explanation hold up?

Jensen Huang addressed it directly: "We know the outside world will read arrangements like this as circular financing."

His explanation has three layers.

First, the technology at these AI labs is proven and the demand is real. This layer can be verified — revenue at these labs is indeed rising.

Second, the money invested is only a small fraction of free cash flow over the same period. This layer warrants the most caution. The company itself says that next year, AI lab business requiring support from Nvidia's balance sheet will account for roughly a quarter of total business.

A quarter. Not a number after the decimal point.

Third, compute is fungible and can be reallocated to other customers, so the risk is manageable. This layer holds partly — compute can indeed be resold, but if the entire industry is oversupplied at the same time, who do you sell it to?

He also described a more important shift: Nvidia no longer just sells hardware. It is partnering with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilize USD 500 billion of third-party capital to build AI infrastructure, providing guaranteed minimum payments on part of the compute in exchange for a share of the upside.

Sell hardware once, collect rent for twenty years. This is an attempt to swap one-time revenue for perpetual revenue. If it works, the valuation framework has to be rewritten; if it doesn't, that USD 500 billion is enormous leverage.

## So can we buy now?

USD 220.78, USD 5.33 trillion market cap, 28x TTM. On the company's own FY2028 guidance, 15x.

Is 15x expensive? On net income, it is unreasonably cheap. On cash flow: FY2027 free cash flow of about USD 140 billion, 38x; FY2028 doubles to USD 280 billion, 19x. The two measures differ fourfold, and that gap is the 40% cash conversion rate.

Three scenarios: bear case USD 168 (FY2028 growth of only 30%), base case USD 248, bull case USD 386. Upside 12%, downside 24%, odds 1 : 0.5.

So the answer is clear: hold, do not chase.

Keep existing positions, because demand, supply and ecosystem are all intact. New money waits for one of two signals: the stock back below USD 180, or the November 17 FY2027Q3 report showing the 70% slope for the first time while receivables days hold at 65.

One last aside.

In this AI cycle, the most misread phrase is "compute is revenue." Compute is indeed revenue, but between revenue and cash sits a customer. Everything Nvidia is doing now shortens that distance — with equity, with guarantees, with financing platforms.

That is not necessarily bad. But it means you are not buying a chip company; you are buying a chip company plus an infrastructure investment company.

The former deserves 25x, the latter 12x. Combined, 15x. That is what the market is pricing today, and why I think it is neither cheap nor expensive.

What is really worth waiting for is not a lower stock price, but the day cash conversion returns to 60%. On that day, 15x will genuinely be cheap.
