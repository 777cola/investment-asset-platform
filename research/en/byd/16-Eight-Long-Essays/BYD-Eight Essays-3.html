③ Moat anatomy: it defends cost, not customers — BYD

Six moats: two real, two half-real, two fake
Research date 2026/09/04  ·  Price ¥87.40 (+0.10%)  ·  Market cap Approximately RMB 796.8 billion (91.17 billion total shares, A+H)  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 27x (about 24x on the RMB 32.6 billion net profit reported for 2025)

Core view

Breaking the moat down item by item: vertical integration (9 points) and scale (8 points) are real; overseas localization (7 points) and channel energy replenishment (7 points) are half real; brand (5 points) and intelligent driving (5 points) are fake; switching costs (3 points) do not exist at all.

Two things are true: vertical integration and scale

The vertical-integration math is straightforward: over 80% of batteries are supplied in-house. At CATL's 20%–24% cell gross margin, a car priced at RMB 150,000 with RMB 50,000 of battery cost saves RMB 10,000–12,000 by self-supplying. That is why its gross margin still climbs during a price war.

Scale is real too: 1.81 million units sold in 2026H1, No. 1 globally in new energy vehicles. Scale delivers two things—procurement leverage (it is one of the world's largest buyers of lithium, nickel and graphite) and fixed-cost dilution (sales rebounded from 700,000 to 1.11 million in Q2, lifting net profit per vehicle from RMB 5,800 to RMB 7,200). These two are the foundation of all its advantages.

But note: these two moats defend against a "cost war", not a "share war". Low cost lets it outlast rivals in a price war, but it cannot stop customers from walking into the store next door. 2026H1 proves the point: gross margin rose, sales fell 15.7%.

Two false ones: brand and smart driving

The brand is fake. Denza plus Yangwang account for only 12.8% of sales; the market above RMB 300,000 has never been conquered. In consumers' minds, the three characters "BYD" mean "value for money," not "I want it." The gap between those two ideas is the gap in gross margin — cars above RMB 200,000 can reach gross margins above 25%, while those below RMB 150,000 reach only 15%.

Smart driving is also fake (at least not real for now). God's Eye is installed on 3.33 million vehicles, generating 210 million km of data per day — data volume is not lacking; but in urban NOA experience and algorithm iteration speed, it is not in the first tier. The competitive focus in 2026 is shifting from "how good the battery is" to "how good the smart driving is," and this will only grow more important.

The common consequence of these two fake moats: it can only fight in the most brutally competitive price band, forever having to re-prove every year that "I am cheaper than the guy next door and better than him too."

The boundaries of the moat: RMB 150,000 is the dividing line

Below RMB 100,000, its moat is widest—the cost advantage translates into a 5,000 yuan price gap that rivals cannot match. At RMB 100,000–200,000, the moat has been erased—Geely's Galaxy and Leapmotor's C series can both field comparable products. Above RMB 200,000, there is almost no moat—competition turns on smart driving, cockpit and brand.

This structure explains all of its strategic moves: why build Denza and Yangwang (to move above RMB 300,000), why build God's Eye (to close the smart-driving gap), why go overseas (to exit the most brutally competitive price band). All three moves are right, but all three fix weaknesses rather than extend strengths.

How long would it take a competitor to replicate this

The other side of the moat is the "cost to replicate." For Geely to build batteries, it takes at least three years from plant construction to mass production, and it must bear capacity-utilization risk; for Xiaomi to build batteries, it likewise takes three years, and it is still losing money.BYD's vertical integration is the result of twenty years of accumulation, not something money can buy—this is its hardest edge.

But the cost to replicate brand and intelligent driving is far lower. Geely's Galaxy produced a comparable product in two years; Xiaomi's intelligent driving reached the first tier in two years.So its "hard moat" lies on the cost side, and its "soft underbelly" lies in brand and intelligent driving—and the latter is becoming the main battleground of competition.This is the precise meaning of a moat that is "deep but narrow."

The conclusion can be put more bluntly:its moat can block "who is cheaper," but cannot block "who understands users better."And after 2026, what the auto industry competes on is precisely the latter.

Final judgment on the moat:deep on cost, shallow on customers, so it can only command a cost-type valuation.

Conclusion

Moat conclusion: cost moat 9 points, customer moat 3 points, weighted 6.5/10. It can defend "I won't lose my shirt," but not "customers won't leave." This structure supports "it won't collapse," not "deserves a premium."
