Three numbers, one report's true hand

Core view

This interim report holds three cards: the 18.85% gross margin is real skill, the RMB 6.3 billion saved on R&D was cut, and the RMB 37.3 billion of operating cash flow was squeezed out. Lay all three on the table, and the quality of earnings takes a 50% haircut.

First card: gross margin is real capability

18.85%, up 0.84 points year on year, rising against the trend in a year of price war — this number is clean. Breaking it down: overseas auto revenue +55.8% (high gross margin), second-quarter sales volume +58.6% quarter on quarter (scale dilution), in-house battery supply (cost internalization). All three sources are real, but the first two are mix, not capability.

To judge whether this is "real skill," look at a counterexample: if it were merely a mix dividend, domestic gross margin should be falling. That is exactly what is happening — domestic auto revenue -33.6%, with both volume and price declining. So the accurate statement is: overseas saved the gross margin, while domestic is bleeding.

The second card: RMB 6.3 billion less spent on R&D is money saved

R&D expense was RMB 23.3 billion, down 21.2% YoY, versus RMB 29.57 billion in the prior-year period. Net profit fell only 20.5%; without that RMB 6.3 billion, the decline would have exceeded 40%. In other words, half of the reported result of "earning only RMB 3.2 billion less" came from cost cuts.

The R&D expense ratio dropped from 7.97% to 6.76%. For any technology-driven company, that number is a red flag—especially during the industry's window of transition from electrification to intelligence, where cutting R&D is tantamount to decelerating while changing lanes.

Why did management do this? Because share is being lost, profit is falling and the stock is falling. This is a classic case of short-term pressure overwhelming long-term investment. Understandable, but it must be penalized.

The third card: cash flow is captured, not given

Operating cash flow of RMB 37.3 billion, up RMB 5.5 billion year on year, was 3x net profit. It looks impeccable until you read the company's own explanation: "a decrease in cash paid for goods purchased." Translation: payments to suppliers were delayed.

That cash flow is unsustainable and masks the real business — a company with volumes down 15.7% whose cash flow improves is itself a signal. Genuinely healthy cash flow comes from "selling more and collecting faster," not "paying slower."

One layer deeper: cumulative free cash flow over the past five years was negative, with all earnings plowed back into production lines. That means shareholders got only share-price gains over the past five years, no cash. Implication for valuation: DCF is unusable; only PE applies — and PE assumes sustainable profit, which loops back to "can it defend its share."

Depreciation: a hard cost over the next three years

Four overseas plants plus more than a dozen domestic bases are gradually being transferred to fixed assets, and depreciation will rise markedly over the next three years. By industry convention, a 150,000-unit vehicle plant carries fixed-asset investment of RMB 5–8 billion, depreciated over 10 years, or RMB 500–800 million a year; four plants together mean RMB 2–3 billion a year. That number is not fatal, but it will amplify profit volatility when sales decline.

This is the fate of asset-heavy companies: capacity is an asset when you build it, and a cost when you cannot sell what it produces. 2026H1 sales fell 15.7%, and depreciation pressure has already surfaced—it is merely masked by the overseas mix dividend and R&D cuts. If domestic volumes do not recover in 2027, both buffers will disappear.

One more counter-check: if profit really is improving, why did net margin fall from 4.18% to 3.58%? Because the 0.84-point rise in gross margin was eaten by 1.4 points of FX and investment income. The core business improvement is real, but not strong enough to offset the financial items.

So when reading this report, look at gross margin first, then R&D, and cash flow last—the order cannot be reversed.

Conclusion

Financial reality conclusion: Of the three cards, one is real (gross margin), one is saved (R&D), one is occupied (cash flow). Profits are not fabricated, but quality is halved; cash flow has not deteriorated, but the improvement comes from upstream. Such statements look good in an upcycle and ugly in a downcycle—2026H1 sits between the two.
