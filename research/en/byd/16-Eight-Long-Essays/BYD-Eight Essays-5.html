⑤ Industry and competition: once penetration passes 50%, the industry's playbook changes — BYD

From "replacing fuel vehicles" to "replacing each other"

Core view

The most important industry data point for the first half of 2026 is not the 7.3% growth rate, but penetration passing 50%. That means new energy vehicles are no longer a "new thing" — they have become the mainstream, and the growth logic of the mainstream market has shifted from "taking customers from fuel vehicles" to "taking customers from peers".

Three consequences of stock competition

Consequence one: the price war will not stop. Marginal cost in the vehicle industry sits far below average cost, so as long as capacity utilization is under 100%, every player has an incentive to cut prices and fill capacity. With 2026 industry-wide capacity far exceeding demand, that incentive only strengthens.

Consequence two: share concentrates with the lowest-cost players, but slowly. Cars are not standardized goods; brand, channel and after-sales all matter. So consolidation will be far slower than in home appliances or solar — home appliances took ten years, cars may take fifteen.

Consequence three: going overseas is the only answer. The domestic market is only so big; anyone who wants growth has to go abroad. In 2026H1 Chinese automakers' export growth was broadly above 50% — not a coincidence, but a common choice.

Competitors: Geely is the only real rival

Ranking competitors by threat level: Geely (most dangerous), Leapmotor (secondary), Xiaomi (medium-term), Tesla (structural), Great Wall (localized).

Geely is dangerous because it competes in the same price band with an upgraded version of the same playbook, and in 2026H1, while BYD fell -15.7%, its NEV sales still grew 10%. It does not carry battery-plant depreciation and can source batteries by price comparison from CATL, SVOLT and its own Yaoning, making it more flexible than BYD when demand declines.

Leapmotor enters the RMB 100,000–200,000 range on cost-performance, attacking the core base of the Dynasty/Ocean lineups, but its gross margin is too thin (on the order of 10%–15%) to sustain a prolonged war. Xiaomi competes at RMB 200,000–300,000, winning young users with its ecosystem and advanced driver assistance; the threat is medium-term. Tesla competes above RMB 200,000, with no overlap with BYD's core base, but it is a direct rival in overseas markets.

Clearing path: follow home appliances, not solar

Solar clears out through a "price collapse → industry-wide losses → hard capacity exit" because the product is fully commoditized. Autos clear out more like home appliances: brands retain residual value, channels carry sunk costs, and local governments weigh employment, so exits are slow and price wars drag on.

The home-appliance endgame: Gree and Midea together hold over 60% share, net margins of 8%–10%, valuations compressed from 40x to 10x, then a decade-long slow bull driven by profit growth. BYD will most likely follow this path — first in share, mid-range margins, a lower valuation center, and returns from profit growth rather than multiple expansion.

The investment implication: don't count on "valuation repair" to double your money; count on "profit growth + higher dividends" for 10%–15% annualized. That return is not bad, but you need to buy cheap.

Where did exports go: three markets, three different scripts

The structure of the 792,000-unit export volume breaks into roughly three blocks: Southeast Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia) — long-standing Japanese-brand territory, with high acceptance of Chinese brands and low tariffs, where its Thailand plant is already in production; Latin America (Brazil, Mexico) — tariffs are rising, and its Brazil plant starting production in July 2025 is precisely the response; Europe — highest unit price, greatest policy risk, and the Hungary plant starting production in 2026 is the key step.

What the three blocks share is "insufficient local supply"; what differs is "policy friendliness." Southeast Asia is the most friendly, Latin America is tightening, Europe is the strictest. So the quality of overseas growth depends on the mix: Southeast Asia is volume, Europe is profit. If Europe is shut off, exports can still grow, but the gross margin will drop by a notch.

So overseas is not "icing on the cake" — it is "the only incremental growth." If the European market closes, its growth story is left with only Southeast Asia and Latin America, and the unit price and gross margin of those two blocks cannot support the RMB 34 billion 2027 net profit.

One number to remember the industry position: industry +7.3%, it -15.7% — that is the whole story.

Conclusion

Industry conclusion: Once penetration passes 50%, the market shifts to competition for existing demand: price wars become structural, consolidation is slow, and overseas expansion is the only source of growth. BYD will become the "Midea of autos": No. 1 share, mid-range margins, a lower valuation center. That is not a bad outcome, but it means the entry price must be low enough.
