Why "Watch" rather than "Avoid"

Avoid means "this company is no good." BYD clearly is not — gross margin rose against the trend, per-vehicle net profit returned to RMB 0.72 million in the second quarter, overseas revenue approached half, and four plants are under construction. It is the only company in this industry with all four legs standing firm.

Watch means "the company is fine, the price is not." At 27x TTM, +3% base case and -37% bear case, the risk-reward does not hold. Both can be true at once: good company + bad price = watch.

Watching is not passive. It means checking sales on the 1st of every month, and gross margin and R&D expense ratio once a quarter. Wait for a reason you can calculate, not a price that excites you.

Two conditions — why these two

Condition A (price ≤ RMB 75) addresses the odds. RMB 75 corresponds to 23x 2027 neutral net profit of RMB 34 billion; upside to RMB 90 is 20%, downside to RMB 55 is 27%, odds near 1:1.3 — enough to build a position, not enough to go heavy.

Condition B (domestic sales volume positive year-on-year for two consecutive months) addresses the logic. A return to positive sales growth means neutral net profit can be revised up from RMB 34 billion to RMB 40 billion, and RMB 90 becomes RMB 105; at that point RMB 87 offers 20% upside. This matters more than price — it changes the numerator, not the denominator.

Both satisfied is a "price and fundamentals double-click," and justifies adding to 3%–4%. This is the only moment worth going heavy, and it may come just once a year.

Stop-loss: use logic, not price

Price-based stop-losses are the most common mistake. If a stock falls from RMB 87 to RMB 60 and the thesis is intact, that is an opportunity to add; if the thesis is broken, RMB 60 is still expensive. So stop-losses should track the thesis, not the price.

Three thesis checkpoints: auto gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters (cost advantage eroded), R&D expense ratio falling below 5.5% (management has gone fully short-term), export growth dropping below 20% (overseas window closing). If any one appears, exit the position regardless of price.

Conversely, if none of the three is broken and the stock falls to RMB 65, that is 20x neutral net profit — a signal to add, not to stop out. That is the difference between "stopping out on the thesis" and "stopping out on the price" — the former lets you make money in a decline, the latter makes you sell at the bottom.

A checklist: five questions to ask yourself before every purchase
  • Did domestic sales turn positive year on year? No — so buy only a starter position.
  • Is automotive gross margin still above 20%? Yes — the thesis holds.
  • Has the R&D expense ratio returned to 7%? No — the 2027 forecast needs a haircut.
  • What multiple of 2027 neutral net profit does my purchase price imply? Above 25x, don't buy.
  • If it drops 20% tomorrow, would I add or panic? Panic — the position is too heavy.

Answer these five questions and the action follows naturally. The hardest part of investing isn't judging a company; it's stopping yourself from acting at the wrong price.

Counterexample: when should you chase a rally

Every framework needs a counterexample. Under what circumstances would I buy above RMB 87? One is domestic sales turning positive year on year and accelerating for three consecutive months — that would mean the share war is won, neutral net profit must be revised up to above RMB 40 billion, and RMB 105 would be the fair price. The other is an overseas breakthrough on the scale of a "second China market" — for example, monthly sales in Europe steadying above 3.0 million units, in which case overseas would no longer be a "window" but a "second home market," and the valuation framework would have to be rewritten.

What both cases share: they would show up in the data in advance; no forecasting required. So "chasing highs" is not forbidden, provided you chase where the data supports it, not where sentiment does.

One last reminder: the value of a framework lies not in being right, but in knowing promptly when you are wrong. That is why every condition comes with an observable indicator — this is not caution, it is keeping yourself from getting lost in the noise.

If you remember only one line: good company, bad price, wait for the signal — three words, and you cannot act if any one is missing.

After reading all eight pieces, if you remember only one action: on the 1st of every month, check domestic sales year-on-year; the rest of the time, don't look.

Conclusion

Decision: 0 position, watch, two triggers (≤RMB 75 or domestic sales turning positive for two consecutive months), one stop-loss (any breach of the three lines: gross margin / R&D / exports). This company deserves the top slot on the list, but not a buy at RMB 87.4. Waiting is the hardest and most correct move here.
