① Cognitive reset: a cyclical manufacturing leader, not a growth stock — BYD

Tear off the label first, then talk valuation
Research date 2026/09/04  ·  Price ¥87.40 (+0.10%)  ·  Market cap RMB 796.8 billion market cap (91.17 billion total shares, A+H)  ·  PE(TTM) About 27x (roughly 24x on the RMB 32.6 billion net profit reported for 2025)

Core view

The market has always priced BYD on its "growth stock" label: rising NEV penetration, doubling sales and overseas expansion opening up upside, hence a multiple above 30x. The 2026 interim results tear that label off: revenue -7.1%, net profit -20.5%, total sales volume -15.7%.

A company with contracting revenue should not be priced as a growth stock. That is the starting point for every judgment.

Three commonly misread facts

Misreading one: as the "new-energy vehicle leader", the company is bound to capture industry growth. In fact, the industry grew 7.3% in 2026H1 while the company fell 15.7% — a 23pp shortfall. Leadership does not equal capturing growth, particularly once penetration exceeds 50% and competition shifts from incremental volume to the existing stock.

Misreading two: rising gross margin means strengthening competitiveness. In fact, gross margin rose from 18.01% to 18.85%, driven by overseas mix (overseas auto revenue +55.8%) and scale effects, not price increases. Domestic auto revenue fell 33.6% amid a brutal price war. Margin expansion and share loss can occur simultaneously — as they did in 2026H1.

Misreading three: Q2 net profit +29.6% means the inflection point has arrived. In fact, Q1 net profit was RMB 4.09 billion versus RMB 9.15 billion a year earlier, down about 55% YoY. Climbing back to ground level from a pit is not a new high. H1 net profit was RMB 12.33 billion, still below RMB 15.51 billion in the same period last year.

What is the right label: the world's strongest cost-driven manufacturer

Place BYD in the coordinate system of global manufacturing and its closest analogue is not Tesla but a combination of Toyota and Foxconn: Toyota's scale and cost control, Foxconn's vertical integration and contract-manufacturing capability (FinDreams Battery already supplies external customers).

The valuation profile of such companies is clear: a P/E of 10–25x fluctuating with the cycle, market-cap growth driven by profit growth rather than multiple expansion, and a payout ratio rising as capex declines. Toyota traded at 8–15x P/E over the past two decades, Midea at 10–20x and Gree at 8–15x.

BYD now trades at 27x, above that range. The excess is the premium the market pays for "high overseas growth." Whether that premium is justified depends on how many years the overseas high growth can last—the only thing in this piece that truly requires a judgment call.

After the cognitive reset, the question becomes simpler

For a cyclical manufacturing leader, only three questions matter: first, where is the cycle trough (when does domestic volume stop falling); second, how many years can the overseas second curve sustain (3–4 years); third, what price does 20–25x correspond to (RMB 70–105).

There is no need to debate "whether EVs are the future"—that is a certainty; nor "whether BYD's technology is strong"—that too is a certainty. What needs judging is the cycle position and the price—both can be tracked with data, no faith required.

One more comparison: how the last cycle rallied

Between 2021 and 2024, BYD's share price roughly tripled: half from net profit growth (RMB 3 billion to RMB 40 billion), half from re-rating (40x to 60x, then back to 25x). That was the era of the "penetration-rate dividend plus earnings explosion" double punch, and both drivers have now reversed — penetration has passed 50% and net profit is falling. The market's biggest cognitive inertia today is pricing this cycle off the valuation memory of the last one.

That also explains why RMB 87 looks like it "has fallen a lot but still isn't cheap": the absolute price is back to 2024 levels, but net profit then was RMB 40 billion versus TTM of only RMB 29.4 billion now. The price has returned to the past, the profit hasn't — so the valuation is actually higher.

One more often-overlooked fact: in its sales mix, the share of models priced below RMB 100,000 is rising, as the 2026 price war pushes consumers toward lower prices. Low-priced cars carry low gross margins, so a downmarket mix shift is negative for profit — which is why "sales recovery" does not equal "profit recovery."

Remember one ratio: its brand premium is almost zero and its cost advantage is roughly 10%–15% — that is the entirety of its competitive capital.

Give this piece a numerical anchor

If a single number is to settle whether "perceptions have reset," use this one: 27x P/E implies RMB 29.4 billion of TTM net profit, yet in 2024, when net profit was RMB 40 billion, the market still awarded around 20x. Profit fell 26% while the valuation rose 35%—quantitative evidence that "the label has not been torn off."

Contrast Toyota: net profit roughly 8x BYD's, market cap about 3x, P/E around 10x. The premium the market grants BYD rests entirely on the word "growth"—and growth has just turned negative. That is not an argument for a de-rating to 10x, but 27x demands evidence, and the evidence from 2026H1 points the other way.

Conclusion

Cognitive reset: reclassify the stock from "growth" to "leading cyclical manufacturer"; the valuation anchor falls from over 30x to 20–25x, implying RMB 70–105 per share. At RMB 87.4 the stock sits in the upper half of that range—neither cheap nor absurd—so the call is "watch," not "avoid."
