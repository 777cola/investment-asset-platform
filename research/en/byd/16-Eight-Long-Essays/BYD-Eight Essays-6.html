⑥ Management: a leader whose calls have almost never been wrong — but one move warrants caution — BYD

Wang Chuanfu's track record, capital allocation and the 2026 price cut

Core view

Wang Chuanfu is this company's scarcest asset: Blade Battery, DM-i, the e-Platform and flash charging — four technology calls, all made at industry inflection points. Yet a 21.2% cut to R&D spending in 2026H1 exposes a problem: his judgment is starting to tilt toward the short term.

Judgment: four market calls, four correct

2003: everyone thought a battery maker building cars was insane, yet he acquired Qinchuan Automobile. 2020: Blade Battery turned safety into a selling point using lithium iron phosphate, sidestepping the cobalt and nickel costs of NCM chemistry. 2021: DM-i broke through the RMB 100,000 price band on "electricity cheaper than oil," crushing joint-venture ICE vehicles outright. 2024: flash charging compressed charging time to single-digit minutes, targeting "charging anxiety," the last pain point.

Four judgments, four correct — and not one of them followership; all ahead of the curve. That kind of judgment is extremely rare in Chinese manufacturing, where most entrepreneurs succeed through execution, not judgment.

It is also why the stock has long commanded a "founder premium": the market believes Wang Chuanfu can see the next step.

Capital allocation: right direction, rushed pace

Over the past five years the money has gone mainly to three places: domestic capacity (a dozen or so bases), overseas plants (Thailand, Brazil, Hungary, Uzbekistan) and a fleet of eight ro-ro vessels. All three bets are right—capacity for scale, overseas plants for tariffs, the fleet for the shipping bottleneck.

The problem is pacing. Cumulative free cash flow over the five years was negative, so these investments were funded entirely from operating cash flow and financing. When domestic volume fell 15.7% in 2026H1, capacity was still expanding overseas, and fixed-cost pressure will persist for two years.

Toyota is the contrast: through its global expansion, Toyota's free cash flow was positive every year, because it added capacity only once demand had appeared. BYD builds capacity first and looks for demand second—an advantage in an upcycle, a risk in a downcycle.

The cut: R&D slashed 21.2%

2026H1 R&D expense was RMB 23.3 billion, down 21.2% YoY — RMB 6.3 billion less spent. Net profit over the same period fell only 20.5%. The cut saved the income statement, but it came at the expense of the company's most core asset.

The context: domestic share is being lost, profit is falling, and the stock has dropped from RMB 110 to RMB 71 (H shares). Management is under real pressure. But management with a genuine long-term money mindset would raise R&D when share is slipping, because that is the only way to win it back.

The message from this cut is therefore not "the company lacks money" — it is "the company chose to protect the short term." Implication for investors: if the R&D expense ratio keeps falling over the next two years, be prepared to cut 2028 earnings forecasts.

Succession: the biggest gap

Wang Chuanfu was born in 1966 and turns 60 in 2026, with no publicly disclosed succession plan. Management has no clear second-in-command: Li Ke runs overseas operations and each FinDreams unit has its own head, but strategic decisions concentrate in one person.

This is not a reason to sell today, but it caps the valuation. Systematised companies (Toyota, Volkswagen) are resilient to mistakes and can command a stability premium; companies dependent on a single decision-maker are less resilient and can only command a discount. BYD is still the latter.

How exactly does its governance fall short of peers?

Compare with Great Wall and Geely: all three are private and founder-controlled. The difference lies in decision-making concentration. Great Wall has Wei Jianjun, Geely has Li Shufu — both equally concentrated. So founder dependence is not a problem unique to BYD; it is common to all Chinese private automakers.

The real difference is incentives. Geely has independent equity structures for Volvo and Zeekr, giving management incentives across multiple platforms; BYD's Fudi entities are not yet spun off and listed, so core team incentives rely mainly on group-level share plans. BYD's management is therefore more integrated and has less internal entrepreneurship motivation. In new businesses requiring rapid trial and error (smart driving, overseas), this may be a hidden disadvantage.

In one sentence: His judgment hasn't changed; what has changed is the situation — from offense to defense, and a company playing defense should not be valued as if it were on the attack.

To assess management, look at only two things: where he spends money, and what he cuts when times are hard.

Conclusion

Management conclusion: capability 9, integrity 8, capital discipline 6, governance structure 6, overall 7/10. The founder is both this company's biggest asset and its biggest single-point risk; the 2026 R&D cut is the first clear signal of short-termism and bears watching.
