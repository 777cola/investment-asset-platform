Core call: the stock traced a V over the past three months, yet fundamentals completed only half of that move

H-shares bottomed at HKD 71.4 at end-June, a one-year low, then rebounded about 29% to HKD 91.95 by end-August following the interim results. The rebound was driven not by a recovery in the domestic market but by second-quarter gross margin and overseas revenue beating consensus.

Of the seven events, only three genuinely changed the fundamentals: the interim results, monthly sales volume and progress on overseas plants. The other four — broker upgrades, industry-wide official price cuts, single-day share price moves and the H/A price gap — are noise and should not affect judgment.

The left side of the V: the market is pricing in "share loss"

BYD's H-share price of HKD 71.4 at end-June capped a slide from the A-share high of RMB 110.63. The cause was straightforward: domestic sales fell YoY for several consecutive months, and the market began to question whether the company can defend its position as "number one in new energy."

The defining feature of this phase is an "earnings selloff plus valuation selloff" occurring simultaneously — the stock fell 35%, but net profit was falling too, so the valuation barely cheapened. Many investors bought the dip here on the grounds that "it has fallen so much," but a big fall does not equal cheap; it only equals downgraded expectations.

The right side of the V: interim results confirm second-quarter improvement

Interim results, released on August 28, showed second-quarter net profit of RMB 8.24 billion, up 29.6% YoY and 102% QoQ, automotive gross margin of 23.0%, up 4.3 percentage points YoY, and overseas revenue approaching half of the total for the first time. The market revised its expectations immediately, taking the H-shares from around HKD 80 to HKD 91.95 within a week. This rebound reflects confirmation of fundamentals, not a sentiment-driven bounce.

The magnitude of the rebound and the reasons behind it are asymmetric, however. The improvement came from the overseas mix and R&D cuts, yet the market is pricing a full recovery. Second-quarter net profit growth of 29.6% YoY compares with a low base of roughly -55% YoY in the first quarter—this is not the start of a new high.

Why September cannot rally: August sales told the truth

August sales, released on 1 September, rebounded MoM but remained negative YoY. The stock spiked and faded, closing at RMB 87.4 on 4 September, up 0.1%. The move is telling: the market will pay for "overseas plus gross margin" but will not pay more for "domestic still falling."

That is the core tension now: the bulls hold overseas high growth, the bears hold persistent domestic decline, and both are right. In this standoff the stock grinds within a range until data from one side breaks the balance—and the signal that breaks it appears once a month, on the 1st.

Criterion: to judge whether a piece of news is worth reading, ask only one question

My standard is simple: does this news change the "2027 net profit" number? If it does, it is a signal; if not, it is noise.

Run everything through that filter. Did the interim results change it? Yes — Q2 gross margin of 23.0% came in ahead of expectations, and the 2027 forecast was revised up from RMB 32 billion to RMB 34 billion. Did August sales change it? Partly — the month-on-month rebound shows Q3 will not be worse, but the YoY figure remains negative, so the 2027 forecast cannot be raised. Did broker notes change it? No — they only explain what has already happened. Did the industry-wide official price cuts change it? No — the price war has been running since 2023.

By this standard, only two pieces of "real news" emerged in three months: the interim results and the monthly sales. But 95% of market information is noise, and human attention is finite — focusing on the sales figures released on the 1st of each month is far more useful than reading ten news flashes a day.

Which are noise: four things that should not affect judgment

First, broker commentary. "Domestic demand under pressure but improving, exports booming" was written only after the interim results came out — it explains the share price, it does not predict it. Sell-side ratings typically stay relevant for only two weeks.

Second, official price cuts. The price war has run from 2023 to 2026; every "new round of price cuts" is old news republished. The real new variable is who cannot hold on and exits first, not who cut another RMB 20,000.

Third, single-day moves. Up 0.1% on September 4 — that number carries no information.

Fourth, the A/H spread. H shares trade at about RMB 83.7, A shares at RMB 87.4 — an A-share premium of roughly 4%. The spread persists because shares cannot be freely converted between the two markets, so it offers no arbitrage. But it is a window into foreign sentiment — a widening spread signals foreign investors turning more bearish.

Conclusion

Attribution: the V-shaped rebound reflects Q2 improvement plus bad news fully priced in, not a domestic inflection point. Of the seven events, three are signal and four are noise. August sales are the real dividing line — improving MoM but still negative YoY, which is why the stock is grinding near RMB 87. The next data point that decides direction is September sales (due October 1): turn positive YoY and the rebound continues; stay negative and the stock returns below RMB 80.
