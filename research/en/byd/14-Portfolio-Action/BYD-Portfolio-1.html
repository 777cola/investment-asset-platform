Core judgment: first be clear about "what I already have," then decide "whether to buy it"

The recommendation on BYD starts not from "is this a good company" but from "what is already in the portfolio." If you already hold CATL, Tesla or a new energy vehicle ETF, buying BYD simply adds the same beta with no diversification benefit.

Standalone build-up plan: 0% now, an initial 1.5% position on trigger, rising to 3% once the thesis is validated, capped at 5%. Not for want of conviction, but because 37% downside against 3% upside means the risk-reward justifies only a "probe position," not a "core holding."

First, correlation: how similar is it to your other holdings?

Three factors drive BYD's share price: NEV sales (largest beta), the China export chain (medium beta) and A-share growth-stock style (medium beta). Any portfolio holding CATL, Tesla, XPeng, Li Auto or NEV ETFs already carries exposure to the first factor.

Rough correlation coefficients (estimated on daily returns): above 0.6 with CATL (same battery-OEM chain, shared cost drivers), above 0.8 with NEV ETFs, around 0.4 with the export chain (home appliances, shipping), and close to zero or even negative with Geely Automobile (share shifts between the two).

The first step is therefore not valuation but a check of your holdings. If NEV exposure already exceeds 8%, buying BYD only enlarges the same risk and delivers no diversification benefit.

Why is the initial position only 1.5%?

Three reasons. First, risk/reward. With 37% downside against 3% upside, any heavy position is a gamble under this structure. Even at the RMB 70 trigger, 29% upside against 21% downside (to RMB 55) is only 1.4:1 — enough to build a position, not enough to size it heavily.

Second, volatility. The 52-week range of RMB 77.84–110.63 implies 42% amplitude; the H-share move from the June low of HKD 71.4 to HKD 91.95 in August is 29% in two months. At this volatility, a 1.5% position already contributes 0.4%-level swings to the portfolio.

Third, uncertainty. Three of the four assumptions remain unverified (domestic, R&D, FX). Unverified theses warrant only small positions; talk of adding after verification is discipline, not courage.

Adding and trimming positions: pre-set price levels, no judgment calls in the moment

Add: Build 1.5% at RMB 75 and add to 3% at RMB 70; go to 4% if domestic volume also turns positive. Do not "top up a little" between RMB 85–90 — that is emotional adding, not disciplined adding.

Trim: Halve the position once the stock returns above RMB 105 (28x 2027 neutral net profit), as upside is consumed; halve if auto gross margin falls below 20%, which breaks the first leg of the thesis.

Stop-loss: No price stop; use a logic stop. Exit fully on any one of: auto gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters, R&D expense ratio below 5.5%, or export growth below 20%. A price drop to RMB 60 with the logic intact is an adding opportunity, not a stop signal.

Portfolio perspective: what role does it play in the portfolio

If you must hold it, treat it as a play on Chinese manufacturing going global, not as a new energy vehicle proxy. The former maps to global capacity replication — four overseas plants and eight ro-ro ships; the latter maps to domestic penetration, which has already passed 50%, so that story is over.

A more sensible portfolio structure: gain new energy vehicle exposure through CATL (battery side, 40% global share) plus BYD (vehicle side, global capacity), with the two combined at no more than 8%; to play the going-global theme, group BYD with home appliances, shipping and construction machinery, not with domestic consumption.

One last point: if you want to buy only one Chinese automaker, BYD is the answer; if you already own CATL, BYD is a duplicate bet. The two statements are not contradictory — the answer depends on what you already hold.

Conclusion

Portfolio conclusion: 0 position, wait for trigger. Once triggered (RMB 75 or domestic sales turning positive), open an initial 1.5% position, add to 3% on validation, cap at 5%. Highly correlated with CATL and new-energy vehicle ETFs — do not add if exposure already exists; negatively correlated with Geely, so it works as a hedge idea but need not be executed. Stop-loss on logic, not price: gross margin, R&D expense ratio and export growth — exit if any one breaks.
