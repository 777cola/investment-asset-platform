# BYD（002594）— Industry Funnel Supplement

## Notes

The five coarse screens applied in this report are: two consecutive years of profitability; annual sales above 500,000 vehicles; overseas sales; core components self-supplied or deeply bound to suppliers; and a calculable valuation. BYD's figures are taken from its 2026 interim report; Geely's 2026H1 total sales +1% and new energy +10% come from public reports; market caps and gross margins for the remaining companies are order-of-magnitude descriptions rather than line-by-line verified, hence labelled "approximately/order of magnitude". The final selection conclusion is consistent with the working-paper rating (Watch).

## Step 1 Initial screen: five hard criteria eliminate 80% in one pass

Criterion one: profitable for two consecutive years. Rules out Leapmotor, Neta, Zeekr (pre-consolidation) and a batch of others. Criterion two: annual sales above 500,000 units. Rules out all second-tier new entrants. Criterion three: overseas sales. Rules out Li Auto and AITO (negligible export share). Criterion four: self-supply or deep binding of core components. Rules out pure assemblers. Criterion five: valuation can be calculated. Rules out loss-makers that cannot be valued.

Apply all five and only four remain: BYD, Geely, Great Wall and Tesla (Xiaomi fails criterion one because its auto business is not yet profitable). This step does not judge good or bad; it only screens out what is "unintelligible and unquantifiable."

## Step 2 Comparison: Why not Geely

Geely is the closest competitor. Its total sales rose 1% and NEV sales 10% in 2026H1 — still growing while BYD fell 15.7%, a sign that both product strength and channels are sound. Its weak spot is batteries: it relies mainly on external sourcing (CATL, SVOLT and its own Yaoning), leaving costs well above BYD's, with gross margin in the 16% range versus 18.85%.

In a price war that gap is fatal — BYD can cut prices down to an 18% gross margin, while Geely at 16% is on the edge of losses. Geely's share gains therefore rest on "the right products" rather than "strong costs", and such growth stalls first as the price war deepens.

It does have one advantage: no battery-plant depreciation, which makes it more flexible when demand falls. That is why it is the "second choice" rather than "eliminated" — it loses on cost and wins on flexibility.

## Step 3 Final selection: why BYD

All four legs stand up: profitability (RMB 12.33 billion, one of the few in the industry that actually makes money), scale (1.81 million vehicles, No. 1 in global NEVs), overseas (792,000 vehicles, +67.8% YoY, four plants under construction) and technology (over 80% in-house battery supply, in-house IGBT/SiC). Globally, the only automakers with all four at once are it and Tesla — and Tesla's valuation is a different species.

One under-discussed advantage: it is the only company to replicate "China manufacturing cost" at overseas plants. Thailand, Brazil, Hungary and Uzbekistan together total more than 750,000 units of capacity. This is not exporting; it is building capacity on other countries' soil. Once complete, tariffs cannot touch it.

So the funnel's answer is it. But note: "the best company in this industry" and "worth buying now" are two separate conclusions. The former holds; the latter does not.

## If you can't buy it, what should you buy?

If the judgment is that "the industry will win, but the leader may not be the most profitable," three alternatives present themselves. First, buy upstream in the supply chain — battery materials, power semiconductors; the upside beta is larger than for whole vehicles, but so is the cyclicality. Second, buy Geely — it is the share gainer, with NEV sales +10% in 2026H1; the drawback is a cost structure inferior to BYD's. Third, buy a new energy vehicle ETF — delegate the choice to the index, at the cost of earning the industry average return, which is currently a loss.

None of the three beats "wait for BYD to fall to RMB 70." The reason is simple: it is most likely the one that survives this industry, and its current price is merely "expensive," not "absurdly expensive." A 20% drop from RMB 87.4 to RMB 70 is the kind of opportunity that appears several times a year in the A-share market.

## After the Final Selection: It Fails the Valuation Test

Market cap RMB 796.8 billion, 27x TTM, 24x 2025 net profit. Base case 2027 net profit of RMB 34 billion at 24x gives RMB 90, or 3% upside; bear case RMB 55, 37% downside. The funnel screens for "worth researching"; valuation decides "worth buying" — and it fails that test.

Put another way, at RMB 70 (23x base-case net profit) the funnel's conclusion would be unchanged and the stock would clear the valuation test. For this company, then, the only action required is "wait", not "choose".
