Six-Gate Screen — BYD

BYD (002594) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen
Research date 2026/09/04  ·  Price ¥87.40 (+0.10%)  ·  Market cap Approximately RMB 796.8 billion (91.17 billion total shares, A+H)  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 27x (about 24x on the RMB 32.6 billion net profit reported for 2025)

Core call: good company, inflection unconfirmed, price not discounted

Six gates completed with a score of 36; the conclusion is watch. The first three gates pass: the business is understandable, vertical integration is a genuine strength, and gross margin can still rise from 18.01% to 18.85% in 2026H1, showing it has one more life than rivals in the price war. But the fifth gate, margin of safety, scores 3/10 and is the fatal flaw—a market cap of RMB 796.8 billion, 27x TTM, a neutral-case target price of RMB 90 implies just 3% upside, and the bear case of RMB 55 is -37%.

The most critical veto reason is not valuation but share: total sales of 1.81 million vehicles, -15.7% YoY, versus the industry at +7.3% over the same period. A company losing share should not command a growth-stock multiple.

Gate 1 Circle of Competence: 8/10 — the business is understandable, the competitive landscape is not

BYD's business can be summed up in one sentence: it makes its own batteries, motors, electronic controls and power semiconductors, builds its own vehicles, and sells them through five brands — Dynasty, Ocean, Fang Cheng Bao, Denza and Yangwang — via 4S dealerships plus direct sales at home, and via six plants and ro-ro ships overseas. Revenue structure, cost structure and profit sources are all calculable — no question on that front.

The 2-point deduction is about the competitive assessment, not the business itself. The RMB 80,000–200,000 price band is the most crowded stretch of China's auto market, where product differentiation lasts only six months and anyone can build a car with 500 km of range and driver assistance. You can understand how BYD makes money, but not how far the price war in this segment will go next year. That is the boundary of the circle of competence.

Duan Yongping's standard is "will this business still exist in ten years, and will it still be around". The first question is a yes; the second depends on whether it can defend a domestic share above 20%. Right now the answer to that question is "it is losing share", so 8 points, not 10.

Stage two, good business: 6/10 — even when a tough business is executed to perfection, it is still a tough business

Autos are a classic asset-heavy business: a full vehicle production line costs several billion, model changes require new tooling, and a ten-point drop in capacity utilization takes a point off gross margin. The long-run net margin for global leaders is 5%–8% — Toyota 8%, Volkswagen 4%, GM 5%. BYD exceeded 8% in 2025 and fell to 3.58% in 2026H1; that is the industry's normal fluctuation range, not a surprise.

Where it beats peers is capital efficiency: vertical integration means it does not have to leave profit for battery and electronic-control suppliers, and its per-vehicle cost is 10%–15% lower than rivals. In 2026H1, with revenue down 7.1%, gross margin still rose 0.8 points — proof of that system. But "better than peers" and "a good business" are two different things. The test of a good business is raising prices without losing volume; when BYD raises prices, it loses share.

Score: 6. Its earning power is first-rate within manufacturing, but cash flow is swallowed by capacity expansion (see Module 7), shareholder returns are thin, and the business's ceiling is set by industry structure, not by company effort.

Gate 3 Moat: 7/10 — wide enough, but the boundaries are clear

Four moats: two hard, two soft. The hard ones are vertical integration and scale: in-house battery supply exceeds 80%, IGBT and SiC are self-supplied, and tooling is made in-house; 2026H1 sales of 1.81 million vehicles rank first globally in new energy, and both procurement bargaining power and fixed-cost dilution are real. Second-quarter sales recovered from 700,000 to 1.11 million vehicles, and net profit per vehicle recovered from RMB 0.58 million to RMB 0.72 million — the scale effect is quantifiable.

The soft ones are brand and intelligent driving: Denza plus Yangwang account for only 12.8% of sales, and the company has yet to establish itself in the above-RMB 30.0 million market; God's Eye is installed on 3.33 million vehicles and generates 210 million km of data per day — the data volume is sufficient, but the algorithm experience is not top-tier. And the focus of competition in 2026 is shifting from "how good the battery is" to "how good the intelligent driving is."

Where is the boundary? Total sales -15.7% against an industry +7.3% shows that cost advantage can protect profit but not share. What the moat protects is "I won't lose money to death," not "others can't take my customers." 7 points: deep enough, but not wide enough.

Gate 4 Management: 7/10, full marks for capability, marks deducted for actions

Wang Chuanfu's technical judgment has rarely missed: Blade Battery, DM-i hybrid, e-Platform, flash charging — each landed right at an industry inflection point. This is the fundamental reason BYD could go from a battery maker to the world's largest automaker, and this deserves a score above 9.

The deduction comes from one specific move: 2026H1 R&D expense was RMB 23.3 billion, down 21.2% year on year — RMB 6.3 billion less — while net profit over the same period fell only 20.5%. In other words, without the R&D cut, profit would have fallen more than 40%. R&D is BYD's most core asset — Blade Battery and DM-i are both the result of more than a decade of investment — and using it to smooth the income statement makes the short-term numbers look good while mortgaging the product cycle over the long term.

This is not fraud; it is a trade-off. Facing share loss and profit decline, management chose to protect profit. That choice is understandable, but it tells you one thing: the company itself does not believe 2027 growth is certain. So 7 points: capability and integrity are both fine, but the motive bears watching.

Gate 5 Margin of safety: 3/10, no room for debate on this gate

The math is straightforward: market cap RMB 796.8 billion (9.117 billion shares × RMB 87.4), TTM net profit RMB 29.44 billion (RMB 32.619 billion in 2025, minus RMB 15.51 billion in 2025H1, plus RMB 12.33 billion in 2026H1), 27x; on 2025 net profit of RMB 32.619 billion, 24x.

Three scenarios: bear case 2027 net profit RMB 25 billion at 20x = RMB 55 (-37%); base case RMB 34 billion at 24x = RMB 90 (+3%); bull case RMB 42 billion at 27x = RMB 124 (+42%). Upside 3%, downside 37%, a 1:12 risk-reward — this is not "a bit expensive," it is "bet and lose expected value."

Some will argue the stock has already fallen 21% from RMB 110.63 and is now cheap. A 21% drop only takes the valuation from 34x to 27x, while earnings are falling at the same time — numerator down, denominator down, valuation has barely improved. This is the classic pattern of earnings de-rating without multiple de-rating — the most painful phase.

Level six, discipline: 5/10. The buy conditions are clearly stated, but only one is met

The buy case originally rested on two legs: a domestic volume inflection plus continued overseas ramp-up. The overseas leg is now solid—exports of 792,000 units, up 67.8% year on year, 43.8% of total volume, with overseas revenue above half; the domestic leg is weak, total volume -15.7% versus the industry's +7.3%.

Discipline means: do nothing while only one condition holds. Monthly sales are published on the 1st of each month—the most honest indicator in the whole market, no guessing and no need to listen to management guidance. Wait for it to turn positive before discussing a purchase; if it doesn't, RMB 55 wouldn't be surprising.

The 5 score reflects how easily this discipline breaks: on a rebound people think "the inflection may already be here," and on a decline they think "it's already fallen this much." Hard-coding the conditions—monthly year-on-year turning positive, or the stock returning to just above RMB 70—is the only way to protect yourself from yourself.

Veto items and items to be verified

One veto item: margin of safety. Buying an automaker at 27x TTM with revenue down 7% YoY and total volume down 15.7% YoY means the valuation has already priced in two things — "overseas growth continues at a high rate + domestic decline stops" — and only one of them has data behind it. When the risk-reward doesn't hold, no company is good enough to buy.

Four items to verify:

  • When domestic monthly sales turn positive YoY. This is the most critical item; it is visible on the 1st of every month, no need to wait for the quarterly report.
  • Whether the R&D expense ratio can return above 7%. H1 was 6.76% (233/3,448.2), versus 7.97% a year earlier; continued decline means pulling forward future spending.
  • Whether European sales can hold up after the Hungary plant starts production in 2026. This is the only way around tariffs and the key variable for 2027 profit.
  • FX. H1 financial expenses swung from a net gain of RMB 3.2 billion to a net expense of RMB 5.1 billion — an RMB 8.3 billion swing; the higher the overseas share, the more routine this volatility becomes.

Of these four, the first decides whether to buy; the last three decide whether you can hold on after buying.

Conclusion

Conclusion: watch, don't buy. 36/60. Four gates passed, margin of safety failed. No problem with company quality—gross margin rising against the cycle, per-vehicle net profit back to RMB 0.72 million in Q2, overseas revenue above half. Real capability. The issue is price and timing: 27x TTM prices in the perfect assumption of "domestic stabilization + high overseas growth," and only half of that holds today. Two triggers; act on either one: domestic monthly sales turn positive year on year, or the stock returns to just above RMB 70 (23x 2027 neutral net profit).
