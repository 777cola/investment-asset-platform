## Two markers of comfortable operations: ROE and gross margin

A ten-year average ROE of 13%–15% makes it a top performer among asset-heavy manufacturers. That return is not built on leverage—BYD's interest-bearing debt ratio is low, and capacity expansion is funded mainly from operating cash flow and equity financing—but on turnover: high capacity utilization, fast inventory turnover and a strong bargaining position with upstream suppliers. Operating cash flow of RMB 37.3 billion in 2026H1, twice net profit, is the evidence of that turnover capability.

A gross margin of 18.85%, up 0.8 points year on year, is the most telling point. In 2026H1, with the industry in a price war and BYD's revenue down 7.1%, gross margin still rose, driven by high-priced overseas markets (overseas revenue accounts for more than half) and the cost advantage of in-house parts. **Peers' gross margins generally fell over the same period while its margin rose—a direct monetization of vertical integration.**

## Two metrics that only just cleared the line: interest coverage and operating cash flow

Interest coverage of 6–8x is safe. But watch the direction: H1 financial expenses swung from a net gain of RMB 3.2 billion in the year-ago period to a net expense of RMB 5.1 billion, an RMB 8.3 billion swing.**This is not higher interest expense; it is FX loss.**The higher the overseas revenue share, the larger this volatility, and interest coverage will become an unpredictable metric going forward—not a solvency issue, but an income-statement issue.

Operating cash flow of RMB 37.3 billion, up RMB 5.5 billion year on year, looks strong on the surface. But the company's own explanation is "a decrease in cash paid for goods purchased," which translates to**lengthening payment terms across the supply chain**. The cash flow improvement comes from stretching upstream suppliers, not from earning more money. Read this alongside accounts payable: if payable days are already stretched to the limit, next year's cash flow will look ugly.

## Three metrics that fell short: net margin, free cash flow, share count

**Net margin 3.58%, 1.4 points short of the 5% threshold.** This is the most glaring item, but it needs to be viewed within the industry: in 2026H1 the average net margin of China's whole-vehicle industry was below 3%, and BYD is already a leader. Its above-8% in 2025 was the peak of scale effects plus overseas mix, not the norm. **Holding it to a 5% threshold amounts to demanding it sustain peak profitability amid a price war, which is unrealistic.**

**Cumulative free cash flow negative over five years — this cannot be forgiven.** 2021–2023 was the peak of capacity expansion, with battery plants, vehicle plants and overseas factories breaking ground simultaneously, and capex far exceeding operating cash flow; 2024–2025 turned positive but did not fill the hole from the earlier years. **This means all the money it earned over the past five years went back into production lines, and shareholders got nothing.** This is the fate of asset-heavy companies, and the reason it must rely on growth to sustain its valuation — once growth stops, these assets are worth only their depreciation.

**Share count rose from the 2.9 billion share level to 9.117 billion shares, nominally tripling, but this is a capitalization issue, not dilution.** EPS on an adjusted basis is unaffected; this is a different matter from raising money through share issuance. Failing this item is only formal; substantively no points are deducted.

## But is it fatal? Not fatal, but it determines the valuation approach.

None of the three holds up, but none is fatal. **Low net margin is an industry trait, negative free cash flow is a feature of the expansion phase, and share count inflation is an accounting formality.** What is truly fatal is what the three imply together: it must keep growing to justify its current assets and valuation.

That is why 27x TTM is a problem. Give a company with a 3.58% net margin, negative free cash flow and expansion-driven growth a 27x multiple, and you are effectively paying for "it can still grow 20% over the next three years." **Yet revenue for 2026H1 was -7.1%.** The two cannot both hold.

Conversely, if domestic volume turns positive in 2026Q4 and net profit returns to RMB 40 billion in 2027, 24x implies RMB 105. **So the seven hard metrics do not say "don't buy"; they say "don't buy at a growth multiple when growth has been disproven."**
