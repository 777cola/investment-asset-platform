## BYD earned RMB 3.2 billion less—why I still keep it first on my watchlist

Bottom line first: not buying, but watching every day.

BYD closed at RMB 87.4 on September 4, a market cap of RMB 796.8 billion, down 21% from its 52-week high of RMB 110.63. A week earlier it reported interim results: first-half revenue of RMB 344.8 billion, down 7.1% year on year; net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 12.33 billion, down 20.5% year on year.

This is its first simultaneous decline in revenue and profit in recent years. By any industry's standards, these are not pretty numbers.

But here is the interesting part: the same day, many institutions raised their target prices. Why? Because the report contains three counterintuitive figures.

## First counterintuitive point: revenue fell, yet gross margin rose

First-half gross margin was 18.85% versus 18.01% a year earlier, up 0.84 percentage points. In the year the price war hit hardest, that number stands out.

The breakdown explains it: domestic auto revenue was RMB 145.6 billion, down 33.6% year on year; overseas auto revenue was RMB 129.5 billion, up 55.8% year on year. Exports reached 792,000 units (79.2 万辆), up 67.8% year on year, accounting for 43.8% of total sales.

The same vehicle sells for more in Europe than at home, at a higher gross margin. So the margin gain comes not from raising prices but from selling elsewhere. That is mix improvement, not stronger competitiveness.

## Second counterintuitive point: sales fell 15.7%, yet the sector rose 7.3%

This is the ugliest number in the entire report. First-half total sales were 1.81 million vehicles, down 15.7% year on year; over the same period China's new energy vehicle industry grew 7.3%.

Underperforming by 23 percentage points is not an industry cycle — it is share loss.

Where did the share go? Most likely to Geely. Geely's first-half total sales edged up 1%, with new energy up 10%; the Galaxy series is attacking precisely the core base of BYD's Dynasty and Ocean lines. Same price band, same playbook, and Geely does not carry battery-plant depreciation, making it more flexible than BYD when demand is weak.

So the real threat is not Tesla — it is the neighbor.

## Third counterintuitive point: profit rose 29.6% in the second quarter, but half of that came from cost savings

Second-quarter net profit was RMB 8.24 billion, up 29.6% year on year and double the prior quarter; automotive gross margin was 23.0%, up 4.3 percentage points year on year; net profit per vehicle was about RMB 0.72 million, versus RMB 0.58 million in the first quarter.

Many call this an inflection point. But one number goes unmentioned: first-half R&D expense was RMB 23.3 billion, down from RMB 29.57 billion a year earlier — a cut of RMB 6.3 billion, or 21.2%.

Net profit fell only RMB 3.2 billion. In other words, without the RMB 6.3 billion R&D cut, profit would have fallen more than 40%.

R&D is the source of all of BYD's advantages. Blade Battery, DM-i hybrid, flash charging — all were built over more than a decade of heavy spending. Cutting R&D in a year of share loss flatters the near-term statements, but two years out the product cycle falls behind, and the price doubles.

Another item: financial expense swung from a net gain of RMB 3.2 billion a year earlier to a net cost of RMB 5.1 billion this year — an RMB 8.3 billion swing, equal to 67% of net profit. The more it sells overseas, the wider the FX hole.

## Doing the math: why we are not buying now

The company is fine; the price is the problem.

Market cap RMB 796.8 billion, trailing twelve-month net profit RMB 29.44 billion, P/E 27x. Three scenarios: bear case RMB 55, down 37%; base case RMB 90, up 3%; bull case RMB 124, up 42%. Weighted at 30%, 45% and 25% probabilities, expected value is RMB 88, almost identical to today's share price.

In plain terms: take this bet and the expected return is zero, while you carry 37% downside.

The 27x already prices in two things — continued high overseas growth and a halt in the domestic decline. The first has data behind it; the second does not, and is still moving the wrong way. Paying full price for a hypothesis that is only half true is not investing, it is praying.

One more point: the stock has fallen from RMB 110 to RMB 87, which looks like a big drop, but earnings are falling too, so valuation has barely cheapened. That kind of decline hurts most, because it adds nothing to your margin of safety.

## When to buy: two hard conditions

First condition: domestic monthly sales turn positive year on year for two consecutive months. This data is published on the 1st of each month, timelier than any quarterly report, and it is the most honest indicator in the entire market. Once sales turn positive, neutral-case net profit can be revised up from RMB 34 billion to RMB 40 billion, and RMB 90 becomes RMB 105.

Second condition: the stock returns to just above RMB 70. That corresponds to 23x neutral net profit, and upside expands from 3% to 29%.

Meet one, and you can open a probe position; meet both, and you can scale to full size.

The stop-loss line is equally clear: if the automotive business gross margin stays below 18% for two consecutive quarters, or the R&D expense ratio falls below 5.5%, the thesis is void and you exit regardless of the share price. Not because the numbers look ugly, but because that means management has slid from protecting profit to giving up on the future.

## The last sentence

BYD is the most formidable new-energy manufacturer in the world; of that I have no doubt. It makes its own batteries, its own chips and its own cars, and has built four plants in Thailand, Brazil, Hungary and Uzbekistan — the most complete landing of Chinese manufacturing overseas to date.

But it sits in the most brutally competitive market, in its ugliest stretch of the cycle, and the price is not yet cheap enough to justify a bet.

So my move is this: put it first on the watchlist, check sales on the 1st of every month, and wait for one number.

Waiting is the hardest and the most correct action here.
