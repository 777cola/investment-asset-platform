Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — BYD

Five risks, ranked by likelihood rather than by how alarming they sound
Research date 2026/09/04  ·  Price ¥87.40 (+0.10%)  ·  Market cap RMB 796.8 billion market cap (91.17 billion total shares, A+H)  ·  PE(TTM) About 27x (roughly 24x on the RMB 32.6 billion net profit reported for 2025)

Core view

Of the five risks, three are already materialising (share loss, profit mix propped up by cost cuts, adverse FX) and two are probabilistic (European policy, falling behind in smart driving). The real risk is not a "black swan" but "no improvement."

Three transitions are already underway. No new assumptions are required; the task is simply to extend them.

First, share. Total sales of 1.81 million vehicles, -15.7% YoY, against an industry up 7.3%. No forecast needed here; it is already in the interim report. In Li Lu's framework, the greatest danger is not "will it get worse" but "it is already getting worse while the market is still pricing in a recovery."

Second, profit structure. Net profit was RMB 3.2 billion lower, but R&D spend was RMB 6.3 billion lower. Strip out that RMB 6.3 billion and the profit decline would have exceeded 40%. This trades future product competitiveness for today's financial statements; the cost will surface in the 2027–2028 model cycle.

Third, FX. Financial expenses swung from net income of RMB 3.2 billion to net expense of RMB 5.1 billion, an RMB 8.3 billion round trip. With overseas revenue now over half the total, this is a recurring item, not an occasional one. The more it sells overseas, the larger the absolute value of FX swings.

Two of the probability events: not inevitable, but costly if they materialise

Europe policy is the biggest risk. Tariffs are only the first layer of cost; carbon footprint accounting, data compliance and exclusion from government procurement are the second. The Hungary plant starts production in 2026, and ramping 150,000 units of capacity to full utilisation will take two years; during that window exports depend on Thailand and Brazil, leaving limited resilience. If the European market is shut off and overseas growth falls from 67.8% to 20%, the RMB 34 billion net profit in the base case does not hold.

Smart driving is the chronic weakness. God's Eye is installed on 3.33 million vehicles, generating 210 million km of data per day; data volume is not the constraint, algorithmic experience is. The competitive focus in 2026 has shifted from "are the batteries good" to "is the smart driving good," and in the market above RMB 150,000, smart driving is decisive. BYD is not in the first tier in this segment, and this is the fundamental reason its brand uplift stands at only 12.8%.

What matters most is not the risk but the assumptions embedded in the valuation

Translate risk into valuation language: the 27x TTM embeds the assumption of "overseas growth continuing above 50% + domestic decline halting." Only one of these two currently has data behind it; the other is moving in the opposite direction.

Li Lu's approach is to "first work out how much you can lose in the worst case." The pessimistic scenario is RMB 55, which from RMB 87.4 is -37%. That magnitude is not a disaster, but paired with "only 3% upside," the expected value is negative. His conclusion would be: this company is within the circle of competence and worth long-term tracking, but now is not the buying point.

Conclusion

Risk rating: medium-high. Three risks are already materialising and two are probabilistic. What is truly unsettling is not that any single one blows up, but that all of them point the same way: profit improvement hinges on "cost-cutting" and "exchange rates," not on "selling more." Against that backdrop, a 27x valuation leaves no margin for error.
