## Chapter 1 Company Overview

BYD Company Limited, A-share code 002594 (SZSE Main Board), H-share 1211 (HKEX), total share capital 9.117 billion shares (A+H combined). On 2026/09/04 the A-share closed at RMB 87.4, market cap RMB 796.8 billion; the H-share closed at HKD 91.95 on 28 August, market cap HKD 838.3 billion.

Two business segments: **automobiles and related products** (2026H1 revenue RMB 275.3 billion, 79.8%) and **handset components and assembly, etc.** (mainly BYD Electronics, derived at roughly RMB 69.7 billion, 20.2%). The automotive segment holds five brands — Dynasty, Ocean, Fang Cheng Bao, Denza and Yangwang — covering the RMB 80,000 to RMB 1 million price band; it also achieves vertical self-supply of batteries, motors and electronic controls, and power semiconductors through FinDreams Battery, FinDreams Powertrain and BYD Semiconductor.

Ownership: Wang Chuanfu holds about 18% and is the actual controller; Lu Xiangyang about 5.5%, Xia Zuoquan about 3%. Listed in both A and H shares, with a high H-share proportion and dispersed institutional investors.

Valuation reference: on 2026E net profit of RMB 27.5 billion, the current 27x TTM corresponds to 29x 2026E PE, above its 2024 low of 20x and below its 2023 high of 30x. **In other words, the market is pricing a "mid-cycle" level, not a "cycle-bottom" level.**

Governance positives: **audit opinions have been standard unqualified in all years, related-party transactions are fully disclosed, and there is no record of controlling-shareholder fund appropriation.** Among private enterprises, this is not easy.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

China's new-energy passenger vehicle wholesale volume rose 7.3% year on year in 2026H1, with penetration exceeding 50%. **This marks the industry's shift from an incremental phase of "replacing fuel vehicles" to a stock phase of "replacing each other."** Growth slowing from above 30% in 2023–2024 to the single digits is the most direct evidence of this shift.

Stock competition brings three consequences: normalized price wars (industry net margin below 3%), slow consolidation (autos have brand and channel stickiness, slower than solar), and overseas expansion as the only source of growth (export growth at major automakers generally above 50% in 2026H1).

Compare China's home appliance path from 2005 to 2015: after penetration passed 50%, the sector went through roughly a decade of price wars, ending with two leaders holding more than 60% share and net margins of 8%–10%, while the valuation center fell from 40x to 10x. **Autos will most likely follow the same path, only over a longer period.**

Supply-demand gap: domestic new-energy passenger vehicle capacity utilization is estimated at 60%–70% in 2026, while capacity under construction is still rising. **At this ratio, price wars will run at least two more years, until second-tier capacity exits in a substantive way.**

By region, Southeast Asia contributes volume, Europe contributes profit, and Latin America contributes growth. **The imbalance among the three (Europe's share is too low) is one reason its profit margin cannot rise.**

## Chapter 3 Business model

The core is vertical integration: battery self-supply rate above 80%, in-house IGBT/SiC, in-house tooling, in-house vehicle assembly. This makes its per-vehicle cost 10%–15% lower than rivals — on a RMB 150,000 vehicle with RMB 50,000 of battery cost and a 20%–24% cell gross margin, self-supply alone saves RMB 10,000–12,000.

Revenue comes from two markets with completely different logic: **domestic (RMB 145.6 billion, -33.6%) is price-sensitive, trading value for volume; overseas (RMB 129.5 billion, +55.8%) is supply-scarce, trading "the only one with cars in stock, after-sales service and a local plant" for a premium.**

Unit economics (rough calculation for 2026Q2): net profit per vehicle about RMB 7,200 (broker estimate, excluding electronics), gross profit per vehicle about RMB 33,000 (at a 23.0% automotive gross margin), group net margin 4.2%. **Thin margins, heavy assets, diluted by scale — common to every automaker; BYD's difference is lower cost and faster turnover.**

Customer mix: domestic buyers are mainly first-time family buyers and trade-up buyers, highly price-sensitive; overseas buyers are mainly in first-electrification markets, insensitive to brand but sensitive to delivery certainty. **The two customer types differ in willingness to pay, which is the root cause of the gross margin gap.**

The business model in one sentence: **sell your own cost advantage into other people's markets, earning the "manufacturing efficiency spread", not "technology monopoly rent".**

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

Revenue: RMB 344.82 billion (-7.1%). Domestic vehicles -33.6%, overseas vehicles +55.8% — one falling, one rising, a net reduction of about RMB 27.3 billion that accounts for the entire decline.

Profit: gross profit RMB 65 billion (-2.8%), gross margin 18.85% (+0.84pct); net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 12.33 billion (-20.5%), net margin 3.58%. **The profit decline far exceeds the gross profit decline, mainly because financial expenses swung from net income of RMB 3.2 billion to net expense of RMB 5.1 billion (FX), and investment income swung from +RMB 1.38 billion to -RMB 180 million.** A RMB 6.3 billion reduction in R&D expenses offset most of it.

Cash flow and assets: operating cash flow RMB 37.3 billion (+RMB 5.5 billion), which the company attributes to lower cash paid for goods purchased (stretched payables); cumulative free cash flow over five years is negative; debt-to-asset ratio above 70%, mostly operating liabilities, with interest-bearing debt manageable. **The core flaw in financial quality: profit comes from cost-cutting, cash comes from occupying upstream, expansion comes from financing.**

Cross-checking the three statements: gross margin up, net margin down, operating cash flow up — only one explanation fits all three at once — **product mix is improving, but expenses and FX are deteriorating, and working capital is shifting upstream.** The statements are internally consistent.

An easily overlooked positive: **2026H1 net profit per vehicle recovered from RMB 0.58 million to RMB 0.72 million, showing economies of scale remain intact, merely offset by the price war.** Once the price war eases, profit elasticity will be substantial.

## Chapter 5 Management and Governance

Wang Chuanfu (Chairman and President, born 1966) holds about 18%. He founded the company in 1995 and entered vehicle manufacturing in 2003. His technology calls have an excellent record: Blade Battery (2020), DM-i (2021), e-Platform and flash charging (2024) all preceded the industry.

Capital allocation: mainly organic capacity expansion, very few acquisitions (Qinchuan Automobile in 2003 is the only major vehicle acquisition); an H-share placement of about HKD 29.9 billion in 2021; dividend payout has long been 20%–30%, leaving shareholder returns thin.**R&D expense fell 21.2% in 2026H1, the first clear signal of short-termism.**

Governance: audit opinions have been standard unqualified every year, with no record of controlling-shareholder fund appropriation. Two blemishes — Great Wall's public emissions complaint in May 2023 and labor disputes during construction of the Brazil plant in late 2024 — neither involves financial authenticity.**Succession arrangements remain undisclosed, and strategic decisions are highly concentrated in the founder, a valuation discount factor.**

Incentives and assessment: A-share buybacks in 2022 funded an employee stock ownership plan, with assessment metrics usually tied to net profit or sales volume.**This explains why profit protection came first in 2026H1 — the assessment cycle is out of step with the product cycle, a structural governance problem.**

Governance versus peers:**BYD's disclosure density exceeds that of most private automakers (complete monthly sales and quarterly segment data), which merits a premium.**

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

**Driver 1: Overseas capacity comes online.** Thailand 150,000 units already in production, Brazil 150,000 units (production started July 2025, planned 300,000), Hungary 150,000 units (production in 2026), Uzbekistan 300,000 units — over 750,000 units in total. This is the only way around tariffs and the main source of profit in 2027.

**Driver 2: Export mix upgrades.** 2026H1 exports 792,000 units (+67.8%), 43.8% of sales; overseas auto revenue 47.1% of the total, with gross margin above the domestic business. Europe, Southeast Asia and Latin America advance in parallel.

**Driver 3: Charging network.** 7,018 fast-charging stations, year-end target 20,000. Charging is the last pain point of electrification; executed well it is differentiation, executed poorly it is capex.

**Driver 4: Brand upmarket and smart driving.** Denza + Yangwang at 12.8% of sales; God's Eye installed on 3.33 million vehicles, adding 210 million km of data per day.**These two determine whether the company can enter the above-RMB 200,000 market, and neither counts as a success yet.**

Fourth driver: **vertical integration supplying external customers**. FinDreams Battery already supplies other automakers, and BYD Semiconductor sells externally. Revenue share is small today, but this is the path to turning a cost center into a profit center and merits separate tracking.

One thing to watch: **if FinDreams Battery's external supply revenue exceeds 15% of the total in 2027, its valuation logic shifts from "vehicle maker" to "vehicle maker + components" and warrants a re-rating.**

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

BYD ranks first in global NEV sales (1.81 million units in 2026H1) and leads share in China's RMB 80,000–300,000 price band, but its share eroded in 2026H1.

**Geely is the most direct competitor**: same price band, multi-brand approach, 2026H1 total sales +1%, NEV +10%, and it carries no battery-plant depreciation, making it more flexible when demand falls.**Leapmotor** attacks the RMB 100,000–200,000 segment on value for money, with thin gross margins that cannot sustain a protracted war.**Xiaomi** captures young users in the RMB 200,000–300,000 band through its ecosystem and smart driving; the threat is medium-term.**Tesla** poses structural competition above RMB 200,000 and in overseas markets.

BYD's relative strengths are cost and capacity; its relative weaknesses are brand and smart driving.**This makes it strongest below RMB 150,000 and weakest above RMB 200,000—and the latter is exactly where margins are fattest.**

Share data: in 2026H1 BYD's share of China's domestic NEV passenger vehicle market fell versus the same period in 2025, while the combined share of Geely, Leapmotor and Xiaomi rose.**This is direct evidence of a shift from one dominant player to multi-polar competition.**

The key variable in the competitive landscape is not who has better technology, but who exits first.**Second-tier brands have already begun trimming product lines in 2026—a signal that consolidation has started.**

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

- **Continued domestic share loss** (already occurring): total volume -15.7% vs industry +7.3%; if the decline does not stop by 2027, scale advantages will work in reverse.
- **Profit quality** (already occurring): R&D expense -21.2%; further cuts would pressure the 2027–2028 product cycle.
- **FX** (already occurring): H1 financial expenses swung by RMB 8.3 billion; the higher the overseas share, the greater the volatility.
- **European policy**: tariffs, carbon footprint, data compliance, public procurement restrictions; limited risk resilience during the ramp-up phase of the Hungary plant.
- **Falling behind in smart driving**: urban NOA is not in the first tier, hindering breakthroughs in the market above RMB 200,000.
- **Valuation risk**: 27x TTM; if domestic decline continues, pressure to revert toward 20x (corresponding to around RMB 65).

Policy risk addendum: domestic NEV purchase tax incentives are phasing out year by year and may tighten further from 2027. **This will suppress industry growth but is relatively favorable for the lowest-cost leader—the phase-out will accelerate the shakeout of second-tier players.**

Two other secondary risks: **rebound in raw material prices (lithium, copper) and rising ocean freight rates**. Both directly compress gross margin, but both are cyclical and recoverable.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026E: revenue of about RMB 715 billion (H1 344.8 + H2 about 370, roughly -3% YoY), net profit attributable to parent of about RMB 27.5 billion (H1 12.33 + H2 about 15.2), net margin 3.8%, EPS about RMB 3.02. **Assumptions: Q3/Q4 improve QoQ but still decline YoY; R&D expense ratio holds at about 6.8%.**

2027E: revenue of about RMB 780 billion (+9%), net profit attributable to parent of about RMB 34 billion, net margin 4.4%, EPS about RMB 3.73. **Assumptions: domestic volume stops falling (0% to +5% YoY), overseas growth slows to below 50%, R&D expense ratio recovers to 7%, and FX no longer turns sharply adverse.**

Sensitivity: every 5pp shortfall in domestic volume cuts 2027E net profit by about RMB 1.5 billion; every 10pp shortfall in overseas growth cuts it by about RMB 1.2 billion; every 1pp lower R&D expense ratio adds about RMB 2.5 billion. **Of the three variables, domestic volume matters most.**

Downside risk to forecasts: if Q3 auto gross margin falls below 20%, 2026E net profit must be cut to about RMB 25 billion and 2027E to RMB 30 billion, and the neutral target price drops from RMB 90 to RMB 79. **This is the number to watch most closely.**

Forecast logic in one sentence: **2026 is the “bottom year”, 2027 the “recovery year”, and 2028 the “validation year”.** So the time horizon for this investment is at least two years.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

Methodology: based on 2027E net profit, applying a 20–27x PE (the lower half of its 2023–2025 historical range of 20–30x), divided by 9.117 billion shares.

**Bear case RMB 55** (RMB 25 billion × 20x): continued domestic share loss, prolonged price war, export growth slowing below 20%. **Base case RMB 90** (RMB 34 billion × 24x): domestic decline halts, overseas sustains above 50% growth. **Bull case RMB 124** (RMB 42 billion × 27x): domestic returns to growth + Hungary ramp-up.

Versus RMB 87.4: -37% / +3% / +42%, probability-weighted (30/45/25) expected value RMB 88, implying +0.7%. **The risk-reward does not hold up — this is the core reason it is a "watch" rather than a "buy."**

Another reference for the valuation anchor: **on PB, the RMB 796.8 billion market cap corresponds to net assets of roughly RMB 250 billion (rough estimate), PB about 3.2x**, above traditional automakers (1–1.5x) and below growth automakers. This level likewise indicates "the market is pricing it at mid-cycle."

Methodological note: **no DCF, because free cash flow is negative over the long term; no PB, because heavy-asset depreciation distorts it; PE only, and it must be paired with a cycle-position judgment.**

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

**Rating: Watch (awaiting an inflection in domestic volume).** Three reasons: top-tier company quality (cost, scale, overseas capacity), real current-period improvement (Q2 net profit +29.6%, auto gross margin 23.0%), and a valuation with no margin for error (27x TTM, +3% base case, -37% bear case).

**Triggers:** (1) domestic monthly volume turns positive year-on-year for two consecutive months; (2) share price falls to RMB 70–75 (23x 2027E net profit). Meeting either opens a 1.5% initial position; meeting both allows adding to 3%–4%, capped at 5%.

**Falsification conditions:** auto gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters, R&D expense ratio falling below 5.5%, or export growth dropping below 20% — any one of these downgrades the rating to "Avoid".

Time frame: 12 months. Next decision point is the late-October Q3 report (watch auto gross margin and R&D expense ratio).

Portfolio level: **if you already hold CATL or a new-energy vehicle ETF, BYD's marginal diversification benefit is close to zero and doubling up is not recommended.** As a "Chinese manufacturing going global" thematic allocation, it makes more sense grouped with home appliances, shipping and construction machinery.

On the "Watch" rating: **it does not mean "unfavorable", it means "the favorable price has not arrived yet".** The two are entirely different in action — the former means dropping coverage, the latter means tracking monthly.
