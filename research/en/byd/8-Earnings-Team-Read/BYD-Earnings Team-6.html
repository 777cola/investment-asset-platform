I. First, the ugly side

BYD's 2026 interim results: revenue of RMB 344.82 billion, down 7.1% YoY, and net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 12.33 billion, down 20.5% YoY. It is the first simultaneous decline in revenue and profit in recent years. A RMB 3.2 billion shortfall is not crippling for a company that earned RMB 32.6 billion last year, but the direction of travel is wrong.

Volumes look worse: 1.81 million units in the first half, down 15.7% YoY, against 7.3% growth in China's new energy vehicle industry over the same period. The industry is growing and BYD is shrinking — the definition of share loss. Domestic automotive revenue was RMB 145.6 billion, down 33.6% YoY: not just fewer vehicles sold, but cheaper ones.

2. Now look at the side that gets overlooked

Three numbers in the same set of financials are moving in opposite directions. Gross margin came in at 18.85%, up 0.84pp YoY, while second-quarter automotive gross margin reached 23.0%, up 4.3pp YoY. Second-quarter net profit per vehicle was about RMB 0.72 million, against RMB 0.58 million in the first quarter. In a year of price wars, a rising gross margin is real capability.

Where does the money come from? Overseas. First-half exports were 79.2 million vehicles, up 67.8% YoY and 43.8% of total sales; overseas automotive revenue was RMB 129.5 billion, up 55.8% YoY and 47.1% of automotive revenue (52.6% on a group basis). The same vehicle sells for more in Europe than at home, and carries a higher gross margin.

Second-quarter net profit was RMB 8.24 billion, up 29.6% YoY and double the prior quarter. Many call this an "inflection point." But first-quarter net profit was only RMB 4.09 billion, down about 55% YoY. This is not a new high — it is a climb back to ground level from a pit.

III. The detail no one wants to mention

Why did profit fall only 20.5% rather than more? Because R&D spending dropped RMB 6.3 billion, from RMB 29.57 billion to RMB 23.3 billion — a 21.2% decline. Had R&D been spent as usual, the profit decline would have exceeded 40%.

R&D is the source of all of BYD's advantages. Blade Battery, DM-i hybrid, flash charging — all are the result of more than a decade of sustained investment. Cutting R&D in a year of share loss is like disarming in the middle of a war. The short-term statements look better; the 2027–2028 product cycle will pay the price.

The other item is FX. Finance costs swung from a net gain of RMB 3.2 billion in the year-earlier period to a net expense of RMB 5.1 billion this year — an RMB 8.3 billion round trip, equal to 67% of net profit. The more it sells overseas, the bigger this hole gets.

IV. So why not buy

The company is fine; the price is the problem. At the September 4 close of RMB 87.4, market cap is RMB 796.8 billion against TTM net profit of RMB 29.44 billion — 27x. Three scenarios: bear case RMB 55 (-37%), base case RMB 90 (+3%), bull case RMB 124 (+42%). Weighted at 30/45/25, expected value is RMB 88 — which tells you the expected return on this bet is zero.

The 27x already discounts two things: continued high overseas growth and a domestic bottoming. The first has data behind it; the second does not. Paying full price for a thesis that is only half-proven is not investing, it is praying.

V. When to buy

Two triggers — act on either. First, domestic monthly sales turn positive YoY for two consecutive months: reported on the 1st of each month, more timely than any quarterly report, this is the most honest indicator in the entire market. Second, the share price returns to just above RMB 70, equivalent to 23x neutral net profit, expanding upside from 3% to 29%.

The stop-loss line is equally clear: automotive gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters, or R&D expense ratio below 5.5%, and the thesis is void. Not because the numbers look ugly, but because that would mean management has slid from "protecting profit" to "abandoning the future."

In one sentence: This is the world's most formidable new-energy manufacturer, sitting in the most brutally competitive market, and the price is not yet cheap enough to bet on. Put it first on the watchlist and wait for one number.

Conclusion

Publishable conclusion: Good company, wrong price — wait for a signal. The buy trigger is a return to positive domestic monthly sales or the share price falling back to just above RMB 70; the thesis breaks if automotive gross margin stays below 18% for two consecutive quarters.
