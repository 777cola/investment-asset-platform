Core view

Duan Yongping cares about only one thing when reading financial reports: whether the way this company makes money can be repeated next year. Three details in this interim report answer that for him: gross margin rose but revenue fell, R&D spending was cut by RMB 6.3 billion, and overseas revenue accounts for nearly half.

Detail one: gross margin up 0.8 points, revenue down 7.1% — trading share for profit

Revenue RMB 344.82 billion, down 7.1% YoY; gross margin 18.85%, up 0.84 pp YoY. Put the two together and there is only one explanation: fewer vehicles sold, but more profit per vehicle. The auto segment's domestic revenue fell 33.6% while overseas revenue rose 55.8%; the mix shift lifted gross margin.

Duan Yongping would call this "a smart short-term choice, a dangerous long-term one." Smart because it protects the income statement and cash flow; dangerous because share is the only long-term asset in this industry, and share lost must be bought back at twice the price. "Price cuts can win back share, but share won back through price cuts does not make money; holding prices protects profit, but the profit protected has no future." This is the dilemma every manufacturing leader faces, and BYD ran into it in 2026.

Detail two: RMB 6.3 billion less on R&D, only RMB 3.2 billion less in profit

R&D expense RMB 23.3 billion, down 21.2% YoY, versus RMB 29.57 billion in the year-ago period. Net profit attributable to shareholders RMB 12.33 billion, down 20.5% YoY, versus RMB 15.51 billion in the year-ago period. The math is simple: without cutting that RMB 6.3 billion, the profit decline would have exceeded 40%.

This is exactly the kind of move Duan Yongping would find unacceptable. He often speaks of "benfen"—doing the right thing and doing it right. R&D investment is the source of all of BYD's advantages over the past two decades—Blade Battery, DM-i, and fast charging all came from sustained R&D. Cutting R&D in a year of share loss is like disarming in the middle of a war. The short-term numbers look better, but two years later the product cycle falls behind, and the price is doubled.

Detail 3: Overseas revenue accounts for nearly half — the only genuine good news

Overseas automotive revenue reached RMB 129.5 billion, up 55.8% YoY, accounting for 47.1% of automotive revenue; exports totaled 792,000 units, up 67.8% YoY, representing 43.8% of total sales.This is the only piece of good news in this report that requires no discount.

But Duan Yongping would press further: how long can this premium last? The overseas premium stems from "scarce supply," not brand. Once plants in Thailand, Brazil and Europe are built, Chinese automakers will fight overseas the same war they fought domestically."Today's overseas market is the domestic market of five years ago."So he would not treat overseas gross margin as permanent, granting it at most three years.

His conclusion: business B, price C

In Duan Yongping's evaluation framework, business quality and price are two separate things. This interim report proves the business quality is a B — the cost advantage is real, the scale is real, the overseas footprint is real, but there is no pricing power, capex is heavy, and share is being lost. The price is a C — 27x TTM, which assumes both "domestic decline halts + overseas growth stays high" hold at the same time.

"A good company is not the same as a good stock; a good stock is 'a good company + a good price.' Right now only the first half of that is in place." He would put BYD on the "understandable, waiting for the price" list, not the "buy now" list.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style conclusion: Three details together describe a company with first-class capabilities, difficult circumstances and short-termist actions. Gross margin rests on mix, profit rests on cutting R&D, growth rests on overseas markets—only one of these three legs holds over the long term. Rating B, wait for price.
