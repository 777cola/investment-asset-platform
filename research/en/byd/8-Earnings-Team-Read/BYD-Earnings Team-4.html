Core view

Li Lu cares about industrial change on a ten-year horizon. The most important thing in this interim report is not second-quarter profit but the shift in the overseas revenue mix: from "exporting finished vehicles" to "local manufacturing + local sales". This is the hardest step for Chinese manufacturing going global, and the most valuable one.

Exports of 792,000 vehicles are only the surface; behind them are four plants

2026H1 exports 792,000 vehicles, +67.8% YoY, 43.8% of total sales. That number is impressive, but the capacity footprint matters more: Thailand 150,000 vehicles already in production, Brazil 150,000 vehicles (production started July 2025, planned expansion to 300,000 vehicles), Hungary 150,000 vehicles starting production in 2026, Uzbekistan 300,000 vehicles, plus plans for Indonesia.

Exporting and local manufacturing are two entirely different businesses. Exporting earns the "price spread"; local manufacturing earns the "market" — the former can be shut down by tariffs at any time, while the latter, once built, is a long-term asset. What BYD is doing is replicating "China's manufacturing cost advantage" on other countries' soil. Japanese automakers did this in the 1980s and Korean automakers in the 2000s, and both succeeded.

But it is harder than Toyota's case back then: this time the rival is local

When Toyota entered the US, American automakers had no low-priced cars; when Hyundai entered the US, Japanese automakers were already there. As BYD enters Europe, Southeast Asia and Latin America, it faces Volkswagen, Toyota and Hyundai, which have already built local plants, and Changan, Great Wall and SAIC, which are building them.

So the high overseas gross margin is not a "permanent moat" but a "window of time". Li Lu would size that window: 2–3 years from plant construction to full capacity, 1–2 years from full capacity to a price war, so the high overseas gross margin has at most 3–4 years left. That judgment matches Duan Yongping's, but Li Lu is more willing to pay for those 3–4 years — because they are certain.

The real long-term question: what comes after electrification

In Li Lu's framework, industrial change unfolds in stages: electrification (2015–2025) is complete, and intelligence (2025–2035) has just begun. BYD won the first stage on batteries and cost; the second stage turns on chips, algorithms and data. It is not in the first tier on any of these three.

This is not to say it will be displaced. Intelligence in the auto industry is gradual, and the market below RMB 100,000 places low demands on smart driving—this is where BYD's base lies. But it means "moving the brand upmarket" will be very difficult—Denza plus Yangwang account for only 12.8% of sales, which is the evidence. The high-end market is the battleground for intelligence, and it cannot break in today.

Conclusion

Li Lu-style conclusion: Going overseas shifting from selling cars to building plants is the most valuable information in this interim report, worth 3–4 years of growth. But intelligentization is the next battleground, and it is not in the first tier. Conclusion: worth long-term tracking, but willing to pay only 20x valuation — paying for a certain 3 years, not an uncertain 10 years.
