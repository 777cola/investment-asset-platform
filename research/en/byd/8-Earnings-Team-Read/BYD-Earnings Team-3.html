Read the financials backwards: which three numbers are the most dangerous

Core view

Munger does not read growth; he reads deterioration. Three numbers in this interim report worsened at the same time: domestic automotive revenue -33.6%, financial expenses swinging from -RMB 3.2 billion to +RMB 5.1 billion, and R&D expenses -21.2%. Three deteriorations running in different directions point to the same cause: the core business is bleeding and external factors are plugging the hole.

First: domestic revenue -33.6%, the true state of the core business

Domestic automotive revenue was RMB 145.6 billion, down 33.6% YoY. That decline far exceeds the 15.7% drop in total volume, showing it is not just selling fewer cars, but selling them cheaper. The damage from the price war is already very clear on the revenue line.

Munger would say: "Don't look at how much it sells in new markets; look at how much it holds in old ones." The old market is its root — domestic revenue of RMB 145.6 billion is 1.12x the RMB 129.5 billion overseas and remains its largest single market. With this market shrinking by a third, any "overseas high-growth" story has to be discounted.

Second: the reversal of RMB 8.3 billion in financial expenses

From net income of RMB 3.2 billion to net expenditure of RMB 5.1 billion — an RMB 8.3 billion swing, equal to 67% of net profit.Munger detested this kind of structure, where profit is determined by things the company cannot control.

Worse, it persists. The higher the overseas revenue share, the more foreign-currency assets, and the greater the impact of FX swings on profit. And exchange rates are unpredictable — meaning every future quarter's profit will carry a layer of randomness, and investors cannot explain profit changes as "operational improvement." For valuation, that is a discount factor, not a premium factor.

Third: R&D -21.2%, the most dangerous one

The first two are environment; the third is choice. R&D spend cut 21.2%, RMB 6.3 billion less spent — a decision management made on its own initiative. Munger would say: this is "trading future competitiveness for today's financials," and companies that make this decision usually lack confidence in the future.

More alarming is the timing: 2026 is precisely the window when the industry shifts from electrification to intelligence. Smart driving, the cockpit, and electrical/electronic architecture all require heavy investment. Cutting R&D at this juncture is equivalent to decelerating while changing lanes. "Tell me what a company cuts in hard times, and I'll know what it truly believes in." BYD is cutting R&D, which shows it believes in short-term profit.

What the market is pricing

Put the three numbers together and the market's pricing logic becomes clear: it is paying for "high overseas growth," paying for "a domestic bottoming," and paying for a "second-quarter inflection." Of the three, the first has data behind it, the second has none, and the third is half cost-cutting.

Munger's verdict would be blunt: "If a case requires three assumptions to hold at once, it usually won't." 27x TTM is the price of three assumptions.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: Three numbers deteriorating at once is no coincidence — it is structural. The domestic cash bleed, the randomness of FX, and the short-termism of R&D all point to one thing: this company is playing defense, not offense. A defensive company deserves only a defensive valuation.
