## Tier 1 Revenue: the RMB 26.4 billion decline is entirely domestic

Revenue came in RMB 26.44 billion lower (RMB 344.82 billion vs RMB 371.26 billion). Breaking it down, the two segments moved in opposite directions: **domestic automotive revenue was RMB 145.6 billion, down 33.6% YoY, a decline of about RMB 73.7 billion; overseas automotive revenue was RMB 129.5 billion, up 55.8% YoY, an increase of about RMB 46.4 billion.** One fell, one rose, netting a decrease of RMB 27.3 billion; together with minor swings in the electronics business, that accounts for the entire RMB 26.4 billion decline.

This structure says one thing: **BYD's domestic business suffered a severe dual hit to volume and price in the first half of 2026.** Total sales volume fell 15.7% while the industry grew 7.3%, and the 33.6% revenue decline far exceeded the volume decline, meaning unit prices are also falling — the price war is not rumor, it is calculable from the financial statements.

Conversely, overseas revenue rose 55.8% against export volume growth of 67.8%, **indicating overseas unit prices also slipped slightly (volume growing faster than revenue), but by a far smaller margin than domestically.** Overseas is still a growth market; domestically it is already a fight over existing share.

## Second layer, gross profit: mix improvement, not price hikes

Gross profit of RMB 65 billion, down only 2.8% year on year, far less than the -7.1% decline in revenue, so gross margin rose 0.84 points to 18.85%. In Q2 alone, automotive gross margin was 23.0%, up 4.3 points year on year, a multi-year high. **Many commentaries frame this as "improving profitability" — that is inaccurate. It is not pricing power; it is selling in different places.**

Two sources: first, overseas revenue share rose to 47.1% (automotive basis), and per-vehicle selling prices and gross margins overseas are higher than domestic; second, scale effects, as Q2 volume recovered from 700,000 units to 1.11 million units, diluting fixed costs. **The former is structural, the latter cyclical — neither equates to a stronger brand premium.**

The key question is whether this gross margin can hold. If domestic volume keeps falling, capacity utilization drops and scale effects reverse; if overseas growth slows, the structural dividend narrows. **The 23% automotive gross margin is most likely the peak of this cycle, not the starting point.**

## Tier 3 Costs and Profit: Where Did the Missing RMB 3.2 Billion Go

Net profit attributable to parent fell RMB 3.18 billion. A rough bridge: **gross profit down RMB 1.9 billion (-19); R&D expense down RMB 6.3 billion (+63); finance costs swung from net income of RMB 3.2 billion to net expense of RMB 5.1 billion (-83); investment income swung from +RMB 1.38 billion to -RMB 180 million (-15.6).** The four items total -RMB 5.46 billion, yet the actual decline was only RMB 3.18 billion; the roughly +RMB 2.3 billion gap comes from selling expense, other income and income tax (derived as a residual).

The bridge shows three things. First, **the core business is not as bad as it looks** — gross profit fell only 2.8%; FX and investment income dragged profit down. Second, **the RMB 6.3 billion cut in R&D offset exactly three-quarters of the FX loss**, an ugly hedge: trading future product strength for today's reported numbers. Third, **FX is a new, structural variable** — with overseas revenue now over half the total, swings in the renminbi against the euro, real and baht will hit the income statement every year.

Look one layer deeper at the second-quarter "inflection". Q2 net profit was RMB 8.24 billion, +29.6% YoY, but back out Q1 2026's RMB 4.09 billion against RMB 9.15 billion a year earlier, roughly -55% YoY. **So the so-called inflection is "Q1 collapsed too hard, Q2 returned to normal", not "a new high".** The distinction matters: it means the YoY base turns friendlier in the second half, but the absolute level has not returned to 2025.

## Layer 4 Cash flow and balance sheet: the quality of RMB 37.3 billion

Operating cash flow of RMB 37.3 billion, up RMB 5.5 billion year on year, looks solid. But the company's explanation is "a decrease in cash paid for goods purchased"—**which is another way of saying payables were stretched.** Vertical integration makes it the largest buyer, able to push out payment cycles and shift working-capital pressure onto upstream suppliers.

This kind of cash flow has two problems: it is unsustainable (there is a limit to payable days), and it masks the true business. Genuinely healthy cash flow comes from "selling more, collecting faster," whereas 2026H1 sales volume was -15.7%.**So of this RMB 37.3 billion, how much is operational improvement and how much is squeezing upstream suppliers needs to be read alongside the payables balance.**

The asset side is a combination of heavy assets and high leverage: the debt-to-asset ratio has long been above 70%, but it is mainly operating liabilities (accounts payable, notes payable), interest-bearing debt is manageable, and monetary funds on the books are in the RMB 100 billion range, so there is no short-term solvency pressure.**The real pressure is not debt repayment but depreciation**—with four overseas plants plus more than a dozen domestic bases simultaneously transferred to fixed assets, depreciation over the next three years will be a hard cost.

## Three easily misread numbers

**The first is "58.6% QoQ volume growth."** Q2 volume of 1.11 million units, up 58.6% QoQ, looks like an explosion but is seasonal — Q1 is the slow Spring Festival quarter and Q2 is peak season, and 2025 followed the same rhythm. **QoQ should only be used to confirm a trend, not to draw conclusions.**

**The second is "52.6% overseas revenue share."** This is the group-level figure and includes BYD Electronics; on an auto-only basis it is 47.1%. Both numbers are correct, but using the wrong one inflates the "overseas is more than half" judgment by 5.5 percentage points. Use 47.1% when discussing the business and 52.6% when discussing structure — don't mix them.

**The third is "RMB 7,200 net profit per vehicle."** This is a brokerage estimate derived by subtracting the electronics business from auto-segment profit, not a company-disclosed figure. **It works for tracking trends (0.58 → 0.72) but not for absolute valuation.** The company has never disclosed per-vehicle profit on this basis.

## The three things to watch most closely

**1. Domestic monthly sales year-on-year.**This is the only indicator that tells you two quarters ahead what the financials will look like, published on the 1st of each month. The recovery from -15.7% to 0 is the first signal.

**2. R&D expense ratio.**H1 was 6.76%, versus 7.97% a year earlier. If management keeps cutting in H2, it is trading the future for the present, and the 2027–2028 model cycle will pay the price.

**3. Overseas per-vehicle profit and FX.**With overseas revenue now more than half of the total, exchange rates are a permanent variable. H1 financial expenses were a net outflow of RMB 5.1 billion; another such hit in H2 would swallow roughly RMB 10 billion of full-year profit.
