## Tier 1 revenue: the RMB 26.4 billion decline is entirely domestic

Revenue came in RMB 26.44 billion lower (RMB 344.82 billion vs RMB 371.26 billion). The two segments moved in opposite directions: domestic automotive revenue was RMB 145.6 billion, down 33.6% YoY, a decline of about RMB 73.7 billion, while overseas automotive revenue was RMB 129.5 billion, up 55.8% YoY, an increase of about RMB 46.4 billion. The net decrease of RMB 27.3 billion, together with minor swings in the electronics business, accounts for the entire RMB 26.4 billion decline.

The mix tells one story: BYD's domestic business suffered a severe dual hit to volume and price in the first half of 2026. Total sales volume fell 15.7% while the industry grew 7.3%, and the 33.6% revenue decline far exceeded the volume decline, implying unit prices are falling too — the price war is no rumor; it is calculable from the financial statements.

Conversely, overseas revenue rose 55.8% against export volume growth of 67.8%, indicating overseas unit prices also slipped slightly (volume growing faster than revenue), but by a far smaller margin than domestically. Overseas remains a growth market; domestically it is already a fight over existing share.

## Second layer, gross profit: mix improvement, not price hikes

Gross profit of RMB 65 billion, down just 2.8% YoY versus a 7.1% decline in revenue, lifted gross margin 0.84pp to 18.85%. Automotive gross margin alone reached 23.0% in Q2, up 4.3pp YoY and a multi-year high. Many commentaries frame this as "improving profitability" — that is inaccurate. It is not pricing power; it is selling in different places.

Two drivers: first, overseas revenue rose to 47.1% of the mix (automotive basis), and per-vehicle selling prices and gross margins are higher overseas than domestically; second, scale effects, as Q2 volume recovered from 700,000 units to 1.11 million units, diluting fixed costs. The former is structural, the latter cyclical — neither equates to a stronger brand premium.

The key question is whether this gross margin can hold. If domestic volume keeps falling, capacity utilisation drops and scale effects reverse; if overseas growth slows, the structural dividend narrows. The 23% automotive gross margin is most likely the peak of this cycle, not the starting point.

## Tier 3 Costs and Profit: Where Did the Missing RMB 3.2 Billion Go

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell RMB 3.18 billion. A rough bridge: gross profit down RMB 1.9 billion (-19); R&D expense down RMB 6.3 billion (+63); finance costs swung from net income of RMB 3.2 billion to net expense of RMB 5.1 billion (-83); investment income swung from +RMB 1.38 billion to -RMB 180 million (-15.6). Those four items sum to -RMB 5.46 billion, yet the actual decline was only RMB 3.18 billion; the roughly +RMB 2.3 billion gap comes from selling expense, other income and income tax (derived as a residual).

The bridge shows three things. First, the core business is not as bad as it looks: gross profit fell only 2.8%, with FX and investment income doing the damage. Second, the RMB 6.3 billion cut in R&D offset exactly three-quarters of the FX loss — an ugly hedge that trades future product strength for today's reported numbers. Third, FX is a new, structural variable: with overseas revenue now over half the total, swings in the renminbi against the euro, real and baht will hit the income statement every year.

Look one layer deeper at the second-quarter "inflection". Q2 net profit was RMB 8.24 billion, +29.6% YoY, but back out Q1 2026's RMB 4.09 billion against RMB 9.15 billion a year earlier, roughly -55% YoY. So the so-called inflection is "Q1 collapsed too hard, Q2 returned to normal", not "a new high". The distinction matters: the YoY base turns friendlier in the second half, but the absolute level has not returned to 2025.

## Layer 4 Cash flow and balance sheet: quality of the RMB 37.3 billion

Operating cash flow of RMB 37.3 billion, up RMB 5.5 billion YoY, looks solid. But the company attributes it to "a decrease in cash paid for goods purchased"—in other words, payables were stretched. Vertical integration makes it the largest buyer in its chain, able to extend payment cycles and push working-capital pressure onto upstream suppliers.

That kind of cash flow carries two problems: it is unsustainable (payable days have a ceiling), and it obscures the underlying business. Genuinely healthy cash flow comes from selling more and collecting faster, yet 2026H1 sales volume was -15.7%.How much of this RMB 37.3 billion reflects operational improvement and how much reflects squeezing upstream suppliers has to be read alongside the payables balance.

The asset side is heavy assets plus high leverage: the debt-to-asset ratio has long stood above 70%, but it consists mainly of operating liabilities (accounts payable, notes payable), interest-bearing debt is manageable, and cash on the books sits in the RMB 100 billion range, so near-term solvency pressure is absent.The real pressure is not debt repayment but depreciation—with four overseas plants and more than a dozen domestic bases simultaneously transferred to fixed assets, depreciation over the next three years will be a hard cost.

## Three easily misread numbers

First, "58.6% QoQ volume growth." Q2 volume of 1.11 million units, up 58.6% QoQ, looks explosive but is seasonal: Q1 is the slow Spring Festival quarter and Q2 is peak season, and 2025 followed the same rhythm. Use QoQ only to confirm a trend, not to draw conclusions.

Second, "52.6% overseas revenue share." That is the group-level figure and includes BYD Electronics; on an auto-only basis the share is 47.1%. Both numbers are correct, but using the wrong one overstates the "overseas is more than half" call by 5.5 percentage points. Cite 47.1% when discussing the business and 52.6% when discussing structure — don't mix them.

Third, "RMB 7,200 net profit per vehicle." This is a brokerage estimate, derived by subtracting the electronics business from auto-segment profit, not a company-disclosed figure. It works for tracking the trend (0.58 → 0.72) but not for absolute valuation. The company has never disclosed per-vehicle profit on this basis.

## The three things to watch most closely

1. Domestic monthly sales YoY.Published on the 1st of each month, this is the only indicator that tells you what the financials will look like two quarters ahead. The recovery from -15.7% to 0 is the first signal.

2. R&D expense ratio.H1 was 6.76%, versus 7.97% a year earlier. If management keeps cutting in H2, it is trading the future for the present, and the 2027–2028 model cycle will pay the price.

3. Overseas per-vehicle profit and FX.With overseas revenue now more than half of the total, exchange rates are a permanent variable. H1 financial expenses were a net outflow of RMB 5.1 billion; another such hit in H2 would swallow roughly RMB 10 billion of full-year profit.
