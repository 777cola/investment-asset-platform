Four Masters — BYD

Core call: four people would say the same thing, but for entirely different reasons

Duan Yongping would call the business too tough, though he would not deny its capabilities. Buffett would say he was right to sell in 2022, because the landscape ten years out is too hard to see. Munger would flip the question — "what would make it fall another 30%?" — and find that half the answer has already played out. Li Lu would acknowledge this is the most complete landing of Chinese manufacturing going global, but press the question of whether the winner can actually make money.

All four perspectives reach the same conclusion: the company deserves a place in the pool, but the current price does not justify a bet. The neutral case of RMB 90 implies just 3% upside; the bear case of RMB 55 implies -37%; probability-weighted at 30/45/25, the expected value is RMB 88 — almost exactly today's share price.

Duan Yongping: this is a hard business executed to the extreme, not a good business

Duan Yongping has always been reserved about autos, for a simple reason: "Autos are a tough business — asset-heavy, strongly cyclical, and you have to invest in new production lines every year." He would not dispute BYD's craft: batteries, motors, electronic controls and power semiconductors all made in-house, per-vehicle cost 10%–15% below rivals, and gross margin up 0.8 points in 2026H1 even with revenue down 7.1%. That is real capability, and it cannot be faked.

But he would immediately shift the topic from "capability" to "business": capex at Apple is 3% of revenue, at BYD persistently above 10%; Apple can raise prices without hurting volume, BYD loses share when it raises prices; Apple's profit rests on ecosystem lock-in, BYD's on scale dilution. Both are manufacturers, but the way they make money is entirely different. One earns from "others cannot switch away," the other from "others cannot learn to do it" — the former's profit persists, the latter must re-prove itself every year.

So how would he price it? As a manufacturer, around 20x, not 30x. "I can understand how it makes money, but not whether it can still make this much next year — I only buy companies like this when they are very cheap." At RMB 87.4 and 27x TTM, it is far from "very cheap."

Buffett bought in 2008 and sold in 2022 — and the reason for selling still holds today

In 2008, Buffett bought 225 million BYD shares at HKD 2.32 and held them for fourteen years before starting to trim in 2022 and exiting entirely, for a gain of roughly 30x. It ranks among Berkshire's most successful China investments — and its most misunderstood. Many assume he sold because the company deteriorated; in fact he sold because he "couldn't see the competitive landscape ten years out" and "the price was no longer cheap."

Apply those two reasons to September 2026 and not a word needs changing. BYD's competitive landscape is more complex now than in 2022: at home, Geely Galaxy, Leapmotor and Xiaomi are fighting head-on in the same price band; abroad, tariffs and localisation barriers apply; on technology, the industry is shifting from electrification to intelligence, and BYD is not in the first tier on smart driving. What Buffett fears most is a technology-route shift, because it forces the old moat to sit the exam again.

He would also look at one number: free cash flow. BYD has ploughed essentially all of the money it earned over the past five years back into production lines, and cumulative free cash flow over those five years is negative. "A company that must keep investing just to hold its position has real earnings that deserve a discount." Add 27x TTM, and his conclusion would be unambiguous: a good company, but not at this price.

Munger: invert the question — what would drive a further 30% decline?

Munger's approach is to invert the question: what would have to happen for the price to fall from RMB 87.4 to RMB 60? He can list the candidates, and the first is already underway.

First, the domestic price war runs another year. Dynasty and Ocean keep losing share, and net profit per vehicle falls from RMB 0.72 million in the second quarter back to RMB 0.5 million. In 2026H1 total sales fell 15.7% while the industry grew 7.3%; this requires no assumption, only continuation.

Second, Europe sets localisation thresholds. Tariffs are only the first layer; carbon footprint, data compliance and government procurement restrictions are the second. The Hungary plant starts production in 2026, and its 150,000-unit capacity will take at least two years to ramp to full utilisation; until then exports rely on Thailand and Brazil, with limited resilience.

Third, FX keeps working against it. In H1 financial expenses swung from net income of RMB 3.2 billion to net expense of RMB 5.1 billion, a RMB 8.3 billion round trip. Overseas revenue is already more than half the total, so currency swings are no longer an occasional item but a permanent one. None of the three requires a black swan, only "no improvement." Hence "invert, always invert" — a decline needs no reason, only the cessation of improvement.

Li Lu: the global electrification winner has emerged — the question now is whether it can make money

Li Lu's framework is industrial change at the civilisation level: 80 million passenger vehicles a year globally, electrification penetration still climbing, and BYD the only company that combines cost, technology, capacity and overseas channels. 2026H1 exports reached 792,000 units, +67.8% YoY; Thailand 150,000 units already in production, Brazil 150,000 units in production from July 2025 with plans to expand to 300,000 units, Hungary 150,000 units in production 2026, Uzbekistan 300,000 units—the most complete landing of Chinese manufacturing going global: not selling cars but moving factories.

But he would press a sharper question: winner-take-all presupposes barriers to entry. A hundred years of automotive history repeatedly show that scale does not equal profit—Toyota holds over 10% global share on a long-run net margin of 8%; Volkswagen has higher share and a 4% net margin. Even if BYD takes 15% global share, it may still be "a very large company earning hard money."

So what he offers is not "buy" but "enter the circle of competence, wait for price." He will pay 20x for "a certain globalisation winner," not 27x—because an EV is not software, has no winner-take-all network effect, and the number two can always buy share with price.

Price discipline: the 20–25x band is not arbitrary — it is derived from historical ranges and peer comparison

The multiple should not be a matter of feel; use two anchors. The first is BYD's own history: from 2023 to 2025 its P/E fluctuated between 20x and 30x, with the 2023 high of 30x corresponding to a phase of RMB 30 billion net profit and 80% growth, while the 2024 level of 20x corresponded to growth slowing to 30%.Growth is now negative, so a level in the lower half of the range is reasonable.

The second anchor is peers: Toyota has long traded around 10x, Volkswagen 5–6x, and Tesla above 50x (on narrative).BYD sits in the middle—it has more electrification and overseas expansion than traditional automakers, but less software and brand premium than Tesla, making 20–25x a reasonable midpoint between the two.To move up to 30x, there must be evidence of "a return to domestic growth," and the evidence in 2026H1 is the opposite.

The three scenarios therefore assign 20x, 24x and 27x—not 15x and not 35x.A 20x bear-case multiple is no longer "derating" but "pricing it as a manufacturer"; only the 27x bull case gives it growth-stock treatment, and that requires both a return to positive domestic growth and a European volume ramp to happen at the same time.

Odds assessment: expected value of RMB 88 — which is the same as telling you not to bet

Weighting the three scenarios at 30%/45%/25% gives 0.3×55 + 0.45×90 + 0.25×124 = RMB 88, +0.7% versus RMB 87.4. This is not "neutral-to-optimistic"; it is "betting gets you nowhere while still carrying 37% downside."

Why is the risk/reward so poor? The stock has already fallen 21% from RMB 110.63, but earnings are falling too — the de-rating is in earnings, not valuation. Market cap RMB 796.8 billion, TTM net profit RMB 29.44 billion, 27x; if 2027 net profit really is RMB 34 billion, 24x still only gets you RMB 90. To reach RMB 124, you need RMB 42 billion of net profit plus 27x — both at once.

What would improve the risk/reward? Two things. First, the price falling to just above RMB 70, which at 23x neutral net profit turns upside from 3% into 29%; second, domestic volume turning positive, which lifts the numerator in the neutral scenario from RMB 34 billion to RMB 40 billion and turns RMB 90 into RMB 105. Neither requires a forecast — only waiting for data. That is the entire meaning of the "Watch" rating.

Conclusion

Conclusion: four investors, one verdict—good company, bad price. Duan Yongping finds it a grind, Buffett finds it expensive, Munger can count three downside paths, and Li Lu concedes it is a globalisation winner but won't pay 27x. Probability-weighted across three scenarios, expected value is RMB 88, +0.7% versus the current price, with -37% downside. That is not "reasonably valued"—it is "the risk-reward doesn't hold." There is only one action: put it first on the watchlist and wait for monthly sales to turn positive, or for the stock to fall back to just above RMB 70.
