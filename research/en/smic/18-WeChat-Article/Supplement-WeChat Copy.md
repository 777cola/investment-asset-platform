## Consider a set of incongruous numbers first.

SMIC's 2026Q1 results looked odd at first glance.

Revenue came in at USD 2.506 billion, up 11.5% YoY and a record high, with capacity utilisation at 93.1% — 9.5 points higher than a year earlier and close to full capacity. Guidance for 2026Q2 is stronger still: revenue up another 14-16% QoQ.

Net profit, however, was just USD 197 million, up 5% YoY. On an A-share basis, net profit was RMB 1.361 billion, up 0.36% YoY — essentially flat.

Revenue up 11.5%, profit up 5%. Where did the 6.5-point gap go?

The answer is one word: depreciation.

## 1. For every RMB 100 collected, RMB 43 goes back to equipment first

Q1 depreciation and amortisation came to USD 1.088 billion, up 25.7% YoY and equal to 43.4% of revenue — for every 100 the company collects, 43 goes straight back into the machines.

The full year is harsher. On its February earnings call, management guided 2026 depreciation up about 30% YoY to roughly USD 5 billion. Full-year 2025 net profit? USD 685 million. Depreciation is seven times net profit.

This is not an operating problem but the structure of the business: a 12-inch line costs over USD 10 billion, depreciates over five years and takes three years to ramp. Once equipment enters the fab, depreciation ticks monthly regardless of whether orders exist.

SMIC's income statement is therefore essentially a function of depreciation: USD 100 million more cuts profit by 30%, USD 100 million less raises it by 30%. Earnings depend first on how accounting allocates the cost, and only second on how many wafers it sells.

## II. Who is making money: customers, equipment makers, local governments

The cash a fab generates has to be split four ways: customers want low prices, equipment makers want high prices, local governments want output value, and shareholders want returns.

At SMIC, shareholders rank last of the four.

Three facts: first, no dividend for 2025, on the grounds that "2026 will still require large-scale capital expenditure"; second, no buybacks ever — however far the share price falls, there is no company-level buying; third, more share issuance — in July this year it issued RMB 40.6 billion in shares to acquire 49% of SMIC North, expanding the share count from 7.96 billion to 8.561 billion shares and diluting existing shareholders by 7%.

No dividend, no buyback, more issuance. Put the three together and in shareholder language it is one sentence: your role at this company is to put money in, not to take money out.

This is not management greed; it is dictated by the ownership structure. State-owned shareholders including the Big Fund, Datang and Xinxin hold more than 20% combined, and the company's first goal is "to build up mainland China capacity"; shareholder returns are only the second.

## III. A 205x P/E — where does the premium come from?

SMIC's A-shares closed at RMB 126.25 on August 10, implying a market cap of RMB 1.08 trillion, 205x TTM P/E and 6.3x P/B.

The H-shares closed the same day at HKD 66.20, equivalent to RMB 57.2. Same company, same financials, same voting rights per share — yet the A-share trades 121% above the H-share.

The foreign stance is more telling. On August 9, UBS upgraded the H-share from Neutral to Buy and lifted its target price from HKD 76 to HKD 96.2, or about RMB 83. That target sits 34% below the current A-share price.

How to make sense of 205x? Three methods all fail to reconcile. At 4x P/B, the stock is worth RMB 81. At the 2026 consensus net profit of RMB 6.255 billion and 40x P/E, market cap is RMB 250 billion and the share price less than a quarter of today's. At the H-share price, RMB 57.

Three methods, three answers: 81, 29, 57. The current price is 126.25. The excess buys the "domestic substitution" story, not this year's earnings.

## IV. So is it a bad company?

No. It is the only foundry in mainland China that can deliver at scale, holding a 5% global share and ranked third — overtaking UMC and GlobalFoundries for the first time in 1Q of this year.

Its customers have no second option: Huawei's Ascend and Kirin, Cambricon's AI chips, Will Semiconductor's image sensors and GigaDevice's memory all have to tape out on its lines. Nobody can displace this "must-pass" position within five years.

Nor has it spent recklessly: a 33% debt-to-assets ratio, net debt of USD 6.6 billion and cash on hand of USD 72.8 billion mean it will not die from an inability to service debt through an industry downturn.

The problem is not the company but the price. A good company is not the same as a good stock; between them lies "how much you paid".

## V. At what price can you buy

Three scenarios: bear case at RMB 88 (price hikes peak in 2027, depreciation keeps climbing); base case at RMB 120 (full utilisation persists, ASP rises 5% a year); bull case at RMB 155 (domestic substitution accelerates, price hikes beat expectations).

Weighted 30%/50%/20%, that gives an expected return of -6.9% — in other words, buying at the current price carries a reasonable expectation of a 7% loss, with a further 30% of downside below that.

So my approach is: only talk below RMB 100, only cheap below RMB 80. Or wait for two signals — capex falling below USD 5 billion, or the company starting to pay dividends. That would mark a shift from "building capacity" to "returning capital to shareholders," and the valuation framework would be re-rated.

As for whether it rises now, I don't know and I don't guess. On July 9 it rose 13.74% in a single day to RMB 173, then a month later fell back to RMB 126. With a stock like this, even calling the direction right doesn't guarantee a profit, and calling it wrong guarantees a loss.
