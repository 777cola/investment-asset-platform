Divergence points and verdicts across four perspectives

Core view

After independent analysis, the four roles do not disagree on whether the company is good or bad—all recognize it as mainland China's only scaled foundry node. The disagreement centers on three points: the sustainability of price increases, the timing of peak depreciation, and which metric to use for valuation. All three point to the same action: do not buy, but keep it on the list.

Four-party conclusion comparison

Business (Duan Yongping): Position A, business C. Consumer electronics at 46.2% and industrial/automotive at 14.0% of the revenue mix reflect real demand, but the money ultimately accrues to capacity, not shareholders — the absence of a dividend in 2025 is clear evidence. Financials (Buffett): Safety 9, return 3. ROE 2.7%, ROA 1.3%, free cash flow negative; the core issue is the RMB 3.81 billion depreciation gap between EBITDA of RMB 5.256 billion and net profit of RMB 685 million. Industry (Munger): Third in share, mid-to-late upcycle, but mainland China capacity reaching 33% in 2027 will push prices back down. Risk (Li Lu): Depreciation and cutthroat competition are risks already unfolding, regulation is a tail risk, and premium and governance are slow variables.

The four perspectives do not overturn one another; each arrives at the same conclusion on a different time scale: over the next 12 months fundamentals improve while valuation compresses, and the race between the two decides the share price.

Disagreement one: how long can the price increases last

The commercial view is optimistic: the AI siphon effect lasts at least through 2027, no new 8-inch capacity is added globally, and price hikes have fundamental support. The risk view is cautious: price hikes cover only categories in short supply; mobile and panel driver ICs have not risen at all, showing the hikes are structural, not across the board.

Verdict: both are right, but they mean different things. Structural price hikes are enough to support revenue growth (Q2 guidance of +14%–16% QoQ), but not enough to support a sharp jump in gross margin (guidance of 20%–22%). In other words, price hikes can save revenue, not profit. On this point the risk view is closer to the facts; the commercial view's optimism requires "across-the-board price hikes" to materialize, and those require genuine supply shortage.

Divergence 2: When will depreciation peak?

The financial view stresses that 2026 depreciation +30% to 5 billion is a hard constraint; the industry view holds that once the new capacity ramp completes in 2027, depreciation growth will naturally slow; the company's own line is "it will still grow in 2027, and the peak depends on the pace of capacity expansion"—which amounts to no answer at all.

Verdict: do not assume a 2027 peak.The four new 12-inch fabs (SMIC Beijing, SMIC East, SMIC Shenzhen, SMIC West Qing) each have design capacity of about 10.0 million wafers/month, will gradually reach full production in 2026–2027, and reaching full production means depreciation continues to be accrued monthly. As long as capex stays at USD 8.1 billion, depreciation cannot peak.The precondition for depreciation to peak is capex falling first, and the precondition for capex to fall is completion of capacity targets—neither happens before 2027.

Divergence 3: Which metric should be used for pricing

The financial perspective favors EV/EBITDA (about 11x, which looks inexpensive); the risk perspective favors PB (6.3x, which looks expensive); the sell side favors PE or PS (target price RMB 160); this report uses PB.

Verdict: use PB, because earnings are distorted.2026 depreciation of USD 5 billion and net profit forecast at around RMB 6 billion mean the PE denominator is depressed by depreciation and unrepresentative; EV/EBITDA ignores maintenance capex—a large portion of a foundry's EBITDA must be reinvested in equipment immediately and cannot be treated as free cash flow. PB is the most stable because it corresponds to "how much it costs to rebuild a production line," and that is the true value anchor for a foundry.

Conclusion from using PB: 6.3x corresponds to ROE of 2.7%, implying ROE must rise above 15% to be justified. That assumption is open to debate, but it cannot be treated as a premise.

Verdict and division of labor

Verdict: watch, do not build a position. The consensus range across the four perspectives is "fundamentals improving, valuation falling": the neutral target price of RMB 120 is 5% below the current price, the bear case of RMB 88 is 30% below, and the bull case of RMB 155 is 23% above. Weighted 30/50/20, expected return is -6.9%. No negative-expectancy trades, however good the company.

Division of labour: the commercial perspective tracks ASP and product mix (quarterly, QoQ); the financial perspective tracks absolute depreciation and capex guidance (quarterly); the industry perspective tracks mainland China capacity and TSMC mature-node pricing (quarterly); the risk perspective tracks export controls and the A/H premium (weekly). If any one of the four indicators turns, convene another meeting.

Triggers: price below RMB 100, or gross margin above 24% for two consecutive quarters after the Q2 results. Abandon conditions: DUV ban takes effect, depreciation rises another 30% in 2027, or full-scale price war breaks out in domestic mature nodes.

Conclusion

TeamLead conclusion: Watch, wait for a pullback. All four perspectives converge on three points of divergence, all tied to the same thread of depreciation: price hikes rescue revenue but not profit; depreciation will not peak before 2027; valuation can only rely on PB. Together these three amount to one sentence — a good company at the wrong price; what is needed is not confidence, but price.
