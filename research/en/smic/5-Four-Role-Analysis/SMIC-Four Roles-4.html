Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — SMIC

Five risks, ranked by probability times impact
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 205x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Risk is not a checklist but a ranking. Ranked by probability × impact, SMIC's five risks stack up as follows: depreciation squeezing gross margin (high probability, medium impact); cutthroat competition in mature nodes (medium-high probability, high impact); further tightening of equipment controls (medium probability, extremely high impact); A/H premium convergence (high probability, medium impact); governance and dividend shortfalls (certain, low impact). The biggest danger is not TSMC; it is depreciation and cutthroat competition.

Risk one: depreciation — certain, and it happens every year

Depreciation and amortisation reached USD 3.81 billion in 2025, or 40.9% of revenue. Guidance for 2026 is roughly USD 5 billion, up 30% YoY, and management explicitly said 2027 "will still grow," with the peak timing dependent on the pace of capacity expansion. The numerator (price) of gross margin therefore has to race the denominator (depreciation), and depreciation is locked in ahead of time.

The arithmetic: the extra USD 1.2 billion of depreciation in 2026 equals 12.9% of 2025 revenue. Offsetting that through pricing would require a 13% ASP increase; first-quarter ASP rose just 2.5% QoQ, and second-quarter gross margin guidance of 20%–22% remains below the 22.5% posted in 2025Q2. Depreciation is outrunning price increases — that is the basic fact of 2026.

Risk 2: Involution — mainland China's capacity cannibalises itself

Mainland China's mature-node capacity is forecast to reach 33% of global capacity in 2027. Hua Hong posted Q1 2026 revenue of USD 661 million, up 22.2% YoY, with utilisation above 100% for an extended period; Nexchip and Smic-Integrated are both expanding. This capacity targets the same customer base: PMIC, DDI, CIS and power devices.

The signal came after Chinese New Year 2026: several Chinese foundries prepared to cut prices on general-purpose chips by 15%–20% to win orders. SMIC's response is not to follow — it chooses to defend gross margin rather than share, a short-term plus but one that means second-tier players will erode its share over time.

Li Lu would point to the nature of this layer: the risk is not cyclical but the product of industrial policy. Local governments want output value and jobs, so capacity will oversupply and prices will fall. The risk will not disappear; it will only erupt periodically.

Risks 3-5: regulation, premium, governance

Controls. In April 2026 the US plans a near-blanket ban on advanced wafer fabrication equipment at five companies, including SMIC, CXMT and Hua Hong, covering DUV lithography and etch tools. This is a tail risk: low probability, but if it lands, expansion timelines break and the four new fabs keep absorbing depreciation without revenue growth. In February 2026 Applied Materials was fined USD 252 million for supplying SMIC in breach of the rules — evidence that controls are enforced in earnest.

A/H premium. A-shares at RMB 126.25, H-shares at HKD 66.20, a premium of 121%. Narrowing that premium needs no change in fundamentals: from 9 July to 10 August, A-shares fell from 173 to 126 (-27%) and H-shares from 89.4 to 66.2 (-26%). Both fell in tandem and the premium did not narrow. Should southbound funds or foreign capital change direction, however, the premium compresses passively.

Governance and dividends. No dividend for fiscal 2025, citing capital expenditure. The Big Fund, Datang and Xinxin hold over 20% combined, and capital allocation serves industrial objectives first. For shareholders this means no downside protection — no one will buy back stock because the price falls.

Under what circumstances would these risks erupt simultaneously?

In the worst of the three scenarios, depreciation keeps climbing through 2027, domestic peers push mature-node prices down again and AI demand fades at the same time. Combined, gross margin falls below 15%, net profit returns to its 2023 level (full-year net profit that year was under RMB 1 billion), and the stock still trades at 6x PB.

That scenario is not highly probable, but it explains why the fifth gate scores only 1 point: not because a collapse is expected, but because the price leaves no buffer for "no collapse."

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: manageable but underpriced. Of the five risks, depreciation and cutthroat competition are high-probability and already unfolding; regulation is low-probability, high-damage; the premium and governance are slow variables. Taken together, the sensible response is not to liquidate but to avoid chasing highs and wait for the price to offer a buffer. At 205x P/E, the market is pricing in none of the five risks.
