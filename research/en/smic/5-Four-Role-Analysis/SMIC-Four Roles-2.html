Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — SMIC

Three statements tell the story: thick EBITDA, thin net profit
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 205x

Core view

One feature of SMIC's financials stands out: EBITDA of USD 5.256 billion in 2025 against net profit of USD 685 million — a multiple of 7.7x. Depreciation and amortisation of USD 3.81 billion accounts for the entire gap. This is a company you have to read on two sets of books at once — cheap on EV/EBITDA, absurdly expensive on P/E, and both conclusions hold.

Income statement: revenue growth without profit growth is structural

2026Q1 revenue USD 2.506 billion (+11.5% YoY, +0.7% QoQ), gross profit USD 504 million (-0.5% YoY), gross margin 20.1% (-2.4pct YoY, +0.9pct QoQ), net profit attributable to shareholders USD 197 million (+5.0% YoY), net margin 7.9%.

Put the four numbers together and only one conclusion holds: revenue rose 11.5%, gross profit did not rise at all. Three things consumed the incremental revenue — depreciation on new fabs (+25.7% YoY to USD 1.088 billion), rising wafer prices, and a higher mix of low-priced handset chips. Net profit grew 5% on other income (government subsidies) and lower finance costs, not on operating improvement.

Full-year 2025 tells the same story: revenue USD 9.327 billion (+16.2%), net profit USD 685 million (+39.0%), gross margin 21.0% (+3pct). Net profit outgrew revenue because the 2024 base was low (USD 493 million), not because earnings quality improved.

Cash flow: operating cash flow looks good, free cash flow looks bad

Net cash flow from operating activities was RMB 5.132 billion in 2026Q1 versus -RMB 1.172 billion a year earlier — a sharp-looking improvement. But capex in the same period was USD 1.563 billion (about RMB 10.871 billion), so operating cash flow covers less than a quarter of capex, with the gap funded by financing: net cash flow from financing activities was +RMB 16.044 billion in Q1.

The full-year picture is clearer. 2025 EBITDA was RMB 5.256 billion against RMB 8.1 billion of capex. Operating cash flow after adding back depreciation is around RMB 4 billion; deduct RMB 8.1 billion of capex and free cash flow is a net outflow of about RMB 4 billion. This has been the pattern for several consecutive years, not a one-year event.

Buffett's conclusion would be this: for a company that needs continuous external financing to sustain growth, that "growth" does not belong to shareholders — it belongs to creditors, local governments and equipment suppliers. A 33% debt ratio and net debt of only RMB 660 million show SMIC relies on equity financing (the STAR Market IPO, RMB 40.6 billion of share issuance) and subsidies rather than debt — safe for creditors, dilutive for existing shareholders.

Balance sheet: no shortage of cash, shortage of returns

At end-2025 total assets were USD 52.271 billion and total liabilities USD 17.250 billion, a debt-to-asset ratio of 33.0%. Cash and equivalents stood at USD 7.279 billion (Q1 2026), net debt at USD 660 million and net debt-to-equity at 1.9%. That is a very solid balance sheet: a foundry's biggest fear in a downturn is being unable to pay for equipment, and SMIC does not have that problem.

Solid, however, is not the same as efficient. Against total assets of USD 52.271 billion and 2025 net profit of USD 685 million, return on total assets is 1.3%; equity attributable to shareholders is about USD 25 billion, implying an ROE of about 2.7%. Put the same assets in TSMC's hands and the return is ten times higher.

The other side of this balance sheet is the RMB 40.6 billion acquisition of 49% of SMIC North. Buying back minority interests will thicken attributable profit, but total assets and return on total assets will not change. This is financial engineering, not operational improvement.

Conclusion

Financial verdict: healthy but unprofitable. Debt ratio 33%, cash of RMB 7.28 billion and net debt of RMB 660 million leave no near-term risk; ROE of 2.7%, ROA of 1.3% and negative free cash flow mean no long-term return. Buffett would score this balance sheet twice: 9 for safety, 3 for returns. He would not pay 6x PB for safety alone.
