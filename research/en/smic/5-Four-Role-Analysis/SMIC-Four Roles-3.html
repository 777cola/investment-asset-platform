Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — SMIC

Structure, cycle position and the most easily overlooked source of supply
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 205x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Global pure-play foundry share in 2026Q1: TSMC 73%, Samsung Foundry 6.4%, SMIC 5%, UMC 4%, GlobalFoundries 3%. SMIC overtook UMC and GlobalFoundries to rank third for the first time, driven by mainland China onshore demand rather than technology. The landscape is stable and its position has improved, but it remains an order of magnitude away from the top tier.

Structure: one dominant player, with the third-ranked player reliant on regional markets

TSMC reported 2026Q2 revenue of USD 40.2 billion, a 67.7% gross margin and a 60.3% operating margin, with 2nm in mass production. SMIC guides to USD 2.86–2.91 billion of revenue and a 20%–22% gross margin for the same period. Revenue differs 14x; gross margin differs 47 percentage points. These are not two players in the same industry — they are two different businesses.

SMIC's position is geographic: China accounts for 88.9% of revenue, and its customers — Huawei, Cambricon, Will Semiconductor, GigaDevice and SG Micro — must tape out domestically. That position holds as long as controls do not ease, not because SMIC is strong but because customers have no choice. Munger would caution: share won because "customers have no choice" disappears the day they do.

Samsung holds 6.4% foundry share and SMIC is closing in, but Samsung runs advanced nodes while SMIC runs mature nodes plus a small amount of advanced, so the overlap is small. SMIC's real competitors are UMC, GlobalFoundries, Hua Hong and Nexchip — the price-war opponents.

Cycle: third year of upcycle, 8-inch tighter than 12-inch

This upcycle began in the second half of 2023; 2026 is its third year. On the demand side, AI is siphoning capacity: AI servers absorb large volumes of advanced-node and memory capacity, pushing mature-node orders — power management, interfaces, drivers, power devices — back to mainland China. On the supply side, 8-inch capacity is barely expanding anywhere in the world, and the shortage is more severe than in 12-inch.

Price evidence: 8-inch BCD prices are up about 10%, with some orders up 20%; TSMC was reported in July 2026 to be raising mature-node quotes from early 2027, the first such move in more than three years. These are real signals, not hype.

Munger would ask the next question: when does supply come back? The answer lies in mainland China's expansion plans — institutions expect mainland China's share of global mature-node capacity to rise to 33% in 2027. A first sign appeared after Chinese New Year 2026: several Chinese foundries prepared to cut PMIC and DDI prices by 15%–20% to win orders. Prices rising and falling in the same market shows the structural shortage is confined to certain categories.

Positioning: midstream, earning toll fees

In the supply chain, SMIC sits between equipment and materials suppliers (ASML, Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron, Shin-Etsu, Shanghai Silicon) and design companies (HiSilicon, Cambricon, Will Semiconductor). It cannot access the most advanced equipment upstream, and downstream customers have no second source—it has pricing power over downstream customers but none over upstream suppliers. That is one structural reason its gross margin is 20% versus TSMC's 67.7%.

Where are we in the cycle? Three top signals: capex hits a new high (USD 8.1 billion, 87% of revenue), gross margin recovers (20.1%, but still far from the historical high of 30%+), and customers begin cutting orders (not happening). The first two have appeared; the third has not. So this is the "late upcycle," not the top.

The implication for the share price is subtle: fundamentals can keep improving for another two or three quarters, but the valuation has already reached 205x. The Munger-style answer: the good times are not over yet, but the good price is.

Conclusion

Industry view: position improved, structure unchanged. SMIC's rise to third globally is fact, driven by local demand rather than technology. The structural shortage in mature nodes lasts at least through 2027, but capacity expansion in mainland China will push prices back down in 2027–2028. The cycle is in its mid-to-late upswing and valuations are at peak levels — both are true at the same time.
