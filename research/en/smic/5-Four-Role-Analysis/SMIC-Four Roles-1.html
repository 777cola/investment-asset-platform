Business analysis (Duan Yongping's perspective) — SMIC

What makes this business profitable, and who ultimately captures the economics

Core view

SMIC sells "the right to use capacity" and collects processing fees. The model runs in one direction: customers place orders first, you spend first, depreciation runs first, profit arrives last. Revenue in 2026Q1 was USD 2.506 billion against depreciation and amortisation of USD 1.088 billion—for every 100 yuan collected, 43 yuan goes straight back to the equipment. This is not a bad business; it is a heavy one.

Where revenue comes from: five application types, three engines

2026Q1 revenue by application: smartphones USD 474 million (18.9% of total, -12.9% YoY), computers and tablets USD 341 million (13.6%), consumer electronics USD 1.158 billion (46.2%, +26.9% YoY), connectivity and wearables USD 183 million (7.3%), industrial and automotive USD 351 million (14.0%, +62.6% YoY, +25.8% QoQ).

Three engines stand out. Consumer electronics is the base, driven by spillover demand for AI server power supplies, interfaces and memory companion chips. Industrial and automotive is the fastest grower at +62.6% YoY, with automotive-grade BCD platforms running at full capacity. Smartphones is the drag at -12.9% YoY, and the company has not raised prices there either — Zhao Haijun said at the briefing that "smartphone and panel-driver products have not seen price increases to date; these customers are facing the same difficulties right now."

Pricing power comes from shortage, not from technology. In February–March 2026 the company negotiated price increases customer by customer, covering only categories in short supply: 8-inch BCD up about 10%, with some orders up 20%. The pattern shows it can collect "shortage rent" but not "technology rent."

Cost structure: depreciation is the only major variable

Foundry costs fall into three buckets: depreciation, materials (wafers, photoresist, specialty gases), and labour and energy. At SMIC, depreciation is 40.9% of revenue (RMB 3.81 billion / RMB 9.327 billion in 2025), materials about 20%, and the balance labour, energy and maintenance. Gross margin sensitivity to depreciation is therefore 1:1: every point of revenue absorbed by depreciation is a point off gross margin, with almost no buffer.

Depreciation rises from RMB 3.81 billion in 2026 to about RMB 5 billion, an increase of USD 1.2 billion, equal to 12.9% of 2025 revenue. To recover that 12.9% through pricing, ASP would have to rise more than 13%; so far the company has managed +2.5% QoQ in the first quarter. That is why revenue hit a record high while profit grew only 5%.

Materials are moving the other way: 12-inch wafers are up 15%–25% year to date, 8-inch up 8%–12%, with a further 5%–10% increase in the second quarter. Costs are rising, depreciation is rising, and only some products can raise prices — that is where the gross margin ceiling comes from.

Where the money ultimately goes: into capacity, not back to shareholders

Operating cash flow for 2025 — EBITDA of USD 5.256 billion less interest and tax, on our rough calculation — comes to roughly USD 4 billion, against capex of USD 8.1 billion, leaving free cash flow a net outflow of about USD 4 billion. No dividend for 2025; management's stated reason is that "2026 will still see relatively large-scale capital expenditure."

In shareholder terms: every dollar earned goes back into the fab, and what shareholders get is capacity appreciation, not cash. Capacity appreciation only matters to shareholders in two situations — capacity stays permanently tight (prices keep rising), or someone is willing to take it off their hands at a higher price (a valuation re-rating). The first depends on the industry cycle, the second on market sentiment; the company can promise neither.

This is where Duan Yongping's standard gets stuck: a company that cannot turn profit into cash and return it to shareholders must persuade people with "someday it will be able to." SMIC's "someday" depends on when the capex cycle ends — USD 8.1 billion in 2026, and in all likelihood still elevated in 2027.

Conclusion

Business conclusion: position A, business C. SMIC is the only node in mainland China that can deliver at scale, so customers have no alternative. But this business locks profit inside equipment, and shareholders must wait for the capex cycle to turn before they see cash. The right way to buy this combination is a price at the cycle bottom, not paying a premium at the peak for the position.
