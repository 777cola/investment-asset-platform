## Assumption behind the call: demand is the most solid leg

"AI spillover driving mature-node demand" is the most solid thesis right now, and the evidence chain is complete: AI servers are absorbing advanced-node and memory capacity, pushing orders for power management, interfaces, drivers and power devices back to mainland China. SMIC's consumer electronics revenue rose 26.9% YoY and industrial and automotive 62.6%, capacity utilisation reached 93.1%, and second-quarter guidance points to 14%–16% sequential growth. Four data points corroborate each other — this is not a single data point.

The value of this thesis is that it does not rely on the grand narrative of "domestic substitution," only on the physical transmission along one supply chain: AI chips need more power and interfaces, those chips use mature nodes, and mature-node capacity sits in mainland China. As long as AI capex holds up, this chain remains intact.

But it has boundaries: the sustainability of AI capex is itself an assumption. The four major North American cloud providers sharply raised capex in 2026; if questions about "circular financing" or order cuts emerge in 2027, this assumption will be the first to crack. So it holds, but not forever.

## Two unverified items: depreciation and gross margin

"Depreciation peaks in 2027" is a weak thesis for now. Management guides depreciation up c.30% YoY in 2026 to roughly USD 5 billion and says it "will continue to grow in 2027, with the peak depending on the pace of capacity expansion" — effectively no commitment to an inflection point. The four new 12-inch fabs (SMIC Beijing, SMIC East, SMIC Shenzhen, SMIC West Qing) each carry design capacity of about 100,000 wafers per month, or 10.0 billion wafers per month in total, and will ramp to full production through 2026–2027; full production means depreciation keeps being booked.

"Price increases can cover depreciation and wafer costs" is only half proven. First-quarter gross margin rose 0.9pp QoQ, showing price increases are working, but fell 2.4pp YoY, showing coverage is insufficient. The additional USD 1.2 billion of depreciation in 2026 equals 12.9% of 2025 revenue, while ASP rose only 2.5% in the first quarter — the gap remains wide.

If neither point holds, valuation has no upside. The signal that they do hold is the same for both: gross margin above 24% for two consecutive quarters, and the QoQ increase in depreciation begins to narrow.

## Two points in doubt: share and premium

The "no share loss" thesis has already cracked. Hua Hong's 2026Q1 revenue rose 22.2% YoY on capacity utilisation above 100%; Nexchip is expanding CIS capacity; and second-tier fabs are using low prices to grab commodity categories such as PMIC and DDI. SMIC chose not to match prices, protecting gross margin at the cost of share in commodity categories. Its strategic position (advanced process, automotive-grade) is intact; what erodes is the revenue base of mature process.

"The A/H premium holds" is the one assumption that should least be treated as a given. A premium of 121% means A-share investors pay twice as much for the same asset. It may persist, because capital does not flow freely between the two markets; it may also compress at any time, because sentiment can turn. Writing it into the thesis amounts to treating "market sentiment" as a fundamental.

## Falsification conditions: exit on any one of them

① Gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters, signalling that price hikes have completely failed; ② capacity utilisation falling below 85%, signalling a genuine drop in demand; ③ capex raised again for 2027 while revenue guidance is cut, signalling the onset of overcapacity; ④ the DUV ban taking effect with no alternative, signalling a hard cap on capacity expansion; ⑤ "no dividend + equity placement + major shareholder selling" all occurring at once, signalling a wholesale shift in capital allocation toward the financiers.

Of these five, ① and ② are the easiest to observe, ③ is the most critical, ④ the most lethal, and ⑤ does the most damage to sentiment.
