## The three items with a deviation >1%, explained one by one

**1. Net profit attributable to parent (2.5%).** IFRS statements are denominated in USD; A-share statements convert to RMB at the quarterly average exchange rate. The average rate in 2026Q1 was about 6.91, while the August 10 central parity had already reached 6.7367. RMB appreciation converts the same USD profit into fewer RMB, and vice versa, so the two bases naturally do not reconcile. Use the USD basis for analysis and convert back to RMB only for A-share valuation, and always state the exchange rate.

**2. PE (6.8%).** The 205x is what market software calculates as "total market cap ÷ net profit attributable to parent for the last four quarters," taking TTM net profit of RMB 5.2–5.3 billion; I use full-year 2025 of USD 685 million − 2025Q1 USD 188 million + 2026Q1 USD 197 million = USD 694 million, converted at 7.12 to RMB 4.94 billion, giving 219x. The gap comes from the exchange rate and whether non-recurring gains and losses are excluded. Both figures are "around 200x," so the conclusion is unchanged.

**3. A/H premium (14 percentage points).** This is the most important correction in this report. The working paper used an exchange rate of 0.92, converting HKD 66.20 to RMB 60.9, giving a premium of 107%; but by 2026 the RMB had appreciated to 6.7367, so 1 HKD is worth only RMB 0.865, and HKD 66.20 converts to RMB 57.2, making the premium 121%. **The same set of share prices differs by 14 percentage points on a different exchange rate**, which is why cross-border valuation must state the exchange rate date.

## Market cap basis: three numbers, three meanings

The same SMIC can be valued at three different market caps: **RMB 1.08 trillion** (A-share price 126.25 × 8.561 billion total shares, the quote-terminal convention, and the basis used on the cover of this report); **about RMB 690 billion** (A-shares at 126.25, H-shares at HKD 66.20 converted to RMB, sum-of-parts, rough calculation); **about RMB 500 billion** (all shares priced at the H-share price, i.e. SMIC as foreign investors see it).

Which one is right? For PE percentile and comparison with A-share peers, use RMB 1.08 trillion; for enterprise value and comparison with TSMC and UMC, use RMB 690 billion; to judge "what it would be worth if A/H were connected", use RMB 500 billion. The three numbers differ by a factor of two — that is the trouble with a dual A/H listing. **The 205x PE and 6.3x PB on the cover of this report are both based on RMB 1.08 trillion, the most conservative (most expensive) basis.**

## Final adopted basis

Revenue, gross margin, depreciation and capex are all in USD (the company's reporting currency), in USD billion; where A-share valuation is involved, convert at 6.7367 to RMB and note it; P/B uses book value per share of RMB 20.19 (A-share reporting basis); net profit uses the IFRS attributable-to-parent basis, not the A-share accounting basis for trend comparisons, because the two treat minority interests differently.

One caveat: SMIC completed in July 2026 the issuance of shares to acquire 49% of SMIC North for RMB 40.6 billion, raising total shares from about 7.96 billion shares to 8.561 billion shares, **so EPS and book value per share before July are not directly comparable with today's**. For any year-on-year data spanning July, use total net profit rather than per-share figures.

Two other points are easily confused. **First, the depreciation basis**: the company's "depreciation and amortization" includes depreciation of equipment and buildings plus amortization of land-use rights, at USD 3.81 billion in 2025 and guidance of about USD 5 billion for 2026; sell-side analysts sometimes count only equipment depreciation, which understates it by 5%–8%, so this report uses total D&amp;A throughout. **Second, government grants**: these flow through "other income," about RMB 350 million in Q1 2026, and are not netted against cost but go directly into operating profit; strip them out when normalizing profit, otherwise gross margin is unchanged while net margin is inflated.
