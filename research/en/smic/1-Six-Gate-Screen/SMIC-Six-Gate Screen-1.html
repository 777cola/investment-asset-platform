Six-Gate Screen — SMIC

SMIC (688981) | 01 · Six-Gate Screen

Core view: good company, expensive price

Four of six gates passed, scoring 31/60. The business is understandable, the moat is real and management does not oversell, clearing the first three gates. It fails the fifth: RMB 126.25 implies roughly 205x TTM P/E and 6.3x P/B, and the A-share trades at more than double the H-share price. The sixth gate also fails — the buy case requires three things to hold at once: price increases, full capacity utilisation and domestic substitution. This is a textbook "good company, real trend, expensive price": watch, don't buy.

Gate one: circle of competence — 8/10

The foundry business model fits on one page: buy equipment, build lines, turn design houses' layouts into wafers, charge per wafer. Profit hinges on four variables—capacity, utilization, ASP and depreciation—all four of which are disclosed in the 2026Q1 filing: monthly capacity of 1.078 million wafers (8-inch equivalent), utilization of 93.1%, ASP up 2.5% QoQ, and depreciation and amortization of USD 1.088 billion.

Revenue also splits across five end applications: smartphones 18.9%, computers and tablets 13.6%, consumer electronics 46.2%, connectivity and wearables 7.3%, and industrial and automotive 14.0%, with China at 88.9%. Which downstream segment is restocking is plain at a glance.

Two points off for the economics of advanced nodes. N+2 (7nm-class) and N+3 (5nm-class) rely on DUV multi-patterning, and the company never discloses yield or per-die cost. You can understand the business but cannot calculate what it is worth.

Gate two: good business — 5/10

Foundry is a capital-intensive, deeply cyclical grind of a business, and a score of 5 already credits its scarcity. 2025 revenue was USD 9.327 billion, net profit USD 685 million and depreciation and amortisation USD 3.81 billion — depreciation is 5.6x net profit. In 2026 depreciation rises a further 30% to about USD 5 billion, so every USD 1 of net profit absorbs USD 7 of equipment cost first.

Returns on capital are therefore persistently poor. On 2025 net profit attributable to shareholders and closing attributable equity, ROE is roughly 2.7%. Capex that year was USD 8.1 billion, or 87% of revenue, and operating cash flow less capex was a net outflow. At full utilisation it is a money printer; idle, it is a meat grinder — gross margin fell to around 16% in 2023.

A score of 5 is not a dismissal; it says the business model caps the valuation. For a company with single-digit ROE and persistently negative free cash flow, fair P/B should not exceed 2–3x.

Gate 3: Moat — 7/10

SMIC's moat is not technological leadership but its position as the only player in mainland China able to deliver at scale. That rests on three layers: 12-inch capacity (1.078 million 8-inch equivalent wafers per month, roughly 5% of global pure-play foundry share and third-largest globally); advanced processes (N+2 supports Huawei's Ascend and Kirin series, with no second domestic supplier); and customer certification (automotive and industrial qualification takes 18–24 months, and switching suppliers means redoing the entire validation chain). Add a fourth: policy. Equipment licences, capacity quotas and Big Fund capital injections amount to a de facto admission licence.

Three caveats. Equipment and materials remain constrained by others: in April 2026 the US plans to extend the DUV ban to China, so the pace of capacity expansion is not self-determined. The technology lags by more than two generations, with N+3 roughly comparable to the 5–6nm class while TSMC mass-produces 2nm. Most critically, this river protects revenue, not profit—profit is determined by depreciation and ASP.

Gate 4: Management — 7/10

Liu Xunfeng is chairman, with Zhao Haijun and Liang Mong Song as co-CEOs. Over the past two quarters they have largely been as good as their word: in February they guided to flat Q1 revenue QoQ, 18%–20% gross margin and capex flat with 2025; in May they reported revenue +0.7% QoQ and gross margin of 20.1%, above the top end, while raising Q2 guidance to +14%–16% QoQ and 20%–22% gross margin. The guidance is not padded—rare among A-share semis.

Nor do they dodge bad news. In February, finance head Wu Junfeng said plainly that depreciation from new fabs would lift 2026 depreciation about 30% YoY and pressure gross margin; in May, Zhao Haijun repeatedly stressed that depreciation, not demand, is the core variable.

Three points off for the capital-allocation stance: no dividend for 2025, and the RMB 40.6 billion share issuance to acquire 49% of SMIC North dilutes existing shareholders by about 7%. Both make industrial sense but deserve a discount from a shareholder-return standpoint—for this company, the money's first priority is national capacity.

Gate Five: Margin of Safety — 1/10

RMB 126.25 × 8.561 billion shares = RMB 1.08 trillion market cap; net assets per share RMB 20.19, PB 6.3x; TTM net profit attributable to parent of about RMB 5.2–5.3 billion, PE about 205x. Put the three numbers together and only one explanation holds: the market is pricing the stock on 2029 capacity and price hikes.

The H-share closed the same day at HKD 66.20, about RMB 57 — an A-share premium of about 121%. Same class of shares, same rights, same financial report, yet the two prices differ by more than double. On 9 August UBS upgraded the H-share to Buy with a target price of HKD 96.2 (3.3x average 2027–2028 PB), about RMB 83 — the foreign target price for the H-share is still 34% below the current A-share price.

A score of 1 acknowledges its resource value. At 4x PB that implies RMB 81; at the 2026 consensus net profit of RMB 6.255 billion and 40x PE, market cap would be less than a quarter of the current price.

Level six: Discipline — 3/10

The question is not whether the company is good or bad, but whether you can wait for the price. The buy case in one sentence: "price increases continue + capacity fully loaded + domestic substitution accelerates." None of the three is decided by the company alone: price increases depend on TSMC's mature-node quotes and domestic peers' capacity expansion, full loading on demand spillover from AI-adjacent chips, and substitution on the intensity of US controls.

The verifiable checkpoints are clear enough: the Q2 report on 13 August, Q3 gross margin guidance, absolute quarterly depreciation, and sequential ASP. The problem is the whole market is watching; by the time the metrics confirm, the price has already priced it in. A score of 3 means you can wait, but write the conditions down hard — don't wait while hunting for reasons to buy early.

Overall: what the 31/60 structure shows

Business quality 20/30, management 7/10, price and discipline 4/20. This is not a score for "the company has fundamental flaws"; it is a score for "the company is fine, the price is the problem." Such a score is most easily read as "bullish long term, wait and see short term," which then becomes an excuse to chase the stock higher.

To put it bluntly: 31/60 fails the six-gate framework, meaning do not buy now — not "buy a little less." Those already holding can stay put, because selling incurs an opportunity cost; those with no position chasing RMB 126 face negative expected return — the neutral case of RMB 120 is 5% below the current price, with 30% downside to the bear case.

Veto items and items to be verified

Deal-breaker: margin of safety. At 205x P/E (TTM), 6.3x PB and a 121% A/H premium, the market is already pricing in 2028–2029 capacity and price hikes. This is not "a bit expensive" — it is "no room for error".

Three items to verify: ① whether Q2 results on 13 August land at the top end of guidance (revenue USD 2.86–2.91 billion, gross margin 20%–22%), and the direction of Q3 gross margin; ② the timing of peak depreciation — the company says only that it rises 30% in 2026 and again in 2027, with no inflection point given; ③ whether ASP can cover wafer price increases (12-inch up 15%–25% cumulatively, 8-inch up 8%–12%), which determines the true elasticity of gross margin.

Conclusion

Verdict: fail, 31/60, watch. The first three gates say "worth researching"; the last three say "do not buy now". Buy triggers are fixed: the share price falls below RMB 100 (5x PB), or gross margin holds above 24% for two consecutive quarters after the Q2 results without the share price having risen. Abandon triggers are equally fixed: a DUV ban takes effect, depreciation rises a further 30% in 2027, or a domestic price war breaks out in mature-node processes.
