## Why no base position: volatility does not allow it

Over the past three months, single-day moves have included +18.78%, +13.74%, +12.62%, -9.97% and -7.85%. Annualised volatility is roughly above 60%, three times that of the CSI 300. At this level, a 1% position contributes as much volatility as a 3% position in a blue chip.

Implication for the portfolio: either hold none, or size it so that a 30% drop does not affect the NAV curve. Under this portfolio's risk budget, the maximum volatility contribution per single stock is set at 1.5%, corresponding to a position of about 2.5%. At the current price, it does not even deserve 2.5%.

## Add, trim, stop-loss: three price levels

Add: RMB 100 (3%), RMB 80 (raise to 5%–6%). These levels equate to roughly 5x and 4x P/B, both at the upper edge of the "reasonable range for cyclicals." If fundamentals improve in tandem (gross margin above 24%), the first tranche can be initiated at RMB 110. Trim: scale out above RMB 155 (bull-case target); exit fully above RMB 180 — that price already discounts 2030 capacity. Stop-loss: no price stop, a thesis stop — DUV ban enacted, utilization below 85%, gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters; any one triggers a cut to 0.0.

Why no price stop? Volatility is too high; a 10% stop would be knocked out by routine swings. For names like this, the only effective risk control is position sizing and thesis validation.

## Rebalancing: rising markets demand more discipline than falling ones

Returns on this kind of stock tend to cluster in a few weeks. On 9 July alone it rose 13.74%; a 5% position would have added 0.7% to NAV in a single day, but the swings in the week before and the week after give back half of that gain. So the discipline here is not "hold for the long term" but "trim once it hits target".

The specific rules: cut a third at RMB 155, another third at RMB 170, exit fully above RMB 180. Those three levels correspond to 7x, 7.7x and 8.2x PB, all above the reasonable range for a cyclical growth stock. Conversely, only consider adding below RMB 100, and add to the position cap below RMB 80. Between RMB 20 and RMB 100, do nothing.

Why no range grid? Because the driver is narrative and sentiment, not mean reversion. Running a grid between 100 and 155 means betting on volatility in an asset priced by headlines: the hit rate is low and the trading costs are not.

## Correlation: an amplifier of semiconductor positioning

SMIC's correlation with Hua Hong is about 0.85, with Cambricon about 0.8, with semiconductor equipment names (NAURA, AMEC) about 0.7 and with TSMC ADR about 0.5. Buying SMIC is therefore not diversification — it is leveraged exposure to the single "China semiconductors" factor.

If the portfolio already holds Cambricon, Hua Hong and equipment stocks, adding SMIC would push semiconductor factor exposure above 20%, breaching this portfolio's 15% sector cap. In that case, even if SMIC falls to RMB 100, the only option is to switch rather than add — sell the most highly correlated holding and rotate into SMIC.

Correlation with other sectors is low: about 0.1 with dividend names (China Yangtze Power, Shenhua) and about 0.15 with consumer names (Moutai, Midea). That is one source of value in portfolio allocation — but only at the right price.
