Core takeaway: a textbook case of "revenue up, profit flat"

2026Q1 revenue USD 2.506 billion, +11.5% YoY; gross profit USD 504 million, -0.5% YoY; gross margin 20.1%, -2.4 pts YoY; net profit attributable to shareholders USD 197 million, +5% YoY. The extra USD 258 million of revenue was fully absorbed by the extra USD 223 million of depreciation. Q2 guidance of +14%–16% QoQ revenue and 20%–22% gross margin is the only good news in this report.

Layer one: revenue mix — AI spillover, not a smartphone recovery

Breaking it down, what drove SMIC was not smartphones but consumer electronics (46.2%, +26.9% YoY) and industrial and automotive (14.0%, +62.6% YoY). Together these two accounted for 60.2%, mapping directly to demand for AI server power management, interface chips, memory peripherals and automotive-grade BCD. Smartphones fell to 18.9% (-12.9% YoY), and the company did not raise prices on them either — Zhao Haijun said "smartphone and display driver products have not seen price increases to date; these customers are equally struggling right now."

By wafer size, 12-inch accounted for 76.4%, down 1.7 points YoY, while 8-inch rose to 23.6%. This shift merits attention: 8-inch is the tightest capacity in this cycle, with almost no new global additions, and prices are rising faster than 12-inch (BCD up about 10%, some orders 20%). The revenue mix tilting toward 8-inch is a near-term positive for gross margin.

By region, China accounted for 88.9% and the US 9.3%. The former shows demand is domestically driven; the latter is what remains under export controls. This ratio also explains why US equipment bans are an existential risk for the company — its customers are all in China, its equipment all overseas.

Tier two: gross profit — the race between price hikes and depreciation, and depreciation is currently winning

Gross margin 20.1%, down 2.4 pts YoY and up 0.9 pts QoQ. The opposite YoY and QoQ directions show two forces offsetting each other: ASP is rising (QoQ +2.5%), shipments are falling (QoQ -0.2%), and depreciation is increasing (YoY +25.7%). The QoQ improvement reflects price hikes taking effect; the YoY decline reflects the low depreciation base in the year-ago period.

Adjusting for depreciation: 2026Q1 depreciation and amortization was USD 1.088 billion, 43.4% of revenue; 2025Q1 was USD 866 million, 38.5%. The depreciation ratio rose 4.9 percentage points over the year, while gross margin fell only 2.4 pts — meaning price hikes and product mix offset at least half of the depreciation pressure. This is the actual effect of what management called offsetting "through high utilization and cost and efficiency gains."

But one thing needs to be clear: the depreciation ratio will keep rising. Full-year depreciation of USD 5 billion against revenue of about USD 11.5 billion (annualized from Q2 guidance) puts depreciation at 43%–44% of revenue. For gross margin to move higher, ASP must keep rising, and by more than the depreciation ratio rises.

Tier 3: Costs and profit — net profit propped up by subsidies and financial expenses

Net profit attributable to parent USD 197 million, up 5% YoY, net margin 7.9%. Revenue rose 11.5% while net profit rose only 5%, the gap eaten by three items: depreciation (USD 223 million incremental), silicon wafer price increases (12-inch cumulative up 15%–25%), and R&D spend (USD 774 million for full-year 2025, 8.3% of revenue).

What actually held up net profit was two non-core items: other income (government subsidies) of about RMB 350 million, and lower finance costs (RMB appreciation + higher interest income). A-share net profit RMB 1.361 billion, up 0.36% YoY — essentially zero growth. That is the true operating result after stripping out FX effects.

EBITDA margin 57.3%, up 0.8 points YoY. This number matters: the operating level is improving, it is just being held down by depreciation. So the question is not whether it makes money, but whether the money it makes is enough to pay for the equipment.

One more easily overlooked item: R&D spend USD 774 million in 2025, 8.3% of revenue, down 1.2 points YoY. Trimming R&D during an expansion phase is common practice, but for a company that needs N+3 to catch up, falling R&D intensity is a signal to track — it means "fill capacity first" is taking priority over "push the process node up."

Layer four: cash flow and balance sheet — no shortage of money, but a shortage of free cash flow

Operating cash flow was RMB 5.132 billion, turning positive from -RMB 1.172 billion year on year — a huge improvement at first glance. But quarterly capex was USD 1.563 billion (about RMB 10.871 billion), and operating cash flow cannot cover even half a quarter of capex; the gap is filled by financing: net financing inflow in Q1 was RMB 16.044 billion.

The balance sheet is solid: total assets USD 52.271 billion, total liabilities USD 17.250 billion, debt ratio 33%; cash USD 7.279 billion, net debt USD 660 million, net debt-to-equity 1.9%. This structure means it will not blow up in a downturn, but it also means it must keep raising money from capital markets — the 2020 STAR Market IPO of RMB 53.2 billion and the 2026 share issuance of RMB 40.6 billion, both equity financings.

In one sentence, this report: operations are improving, the financial position is getting heavier, and shareholders are getting poorer. Revenue up, EBITDA up, utilization 93.1% — all real; net profit flat, free cash flow negative, share count up 7% — also real.

Add one layer: capacity and utilization — what 93.1% means

Monthly capacity rose from 1.059 million wafers (8-inch equivalent) in 2025Q4 to 1.078 million wafers in 2026Q1, a net increase of 19,500 wafers in the quarter, or about 8.7 thousand 12-inch equivalent wafers per month. Shipments were 2.5091 million wafers, utilization 93.1%, up 9.5 points year on year and down 2.6 points quarter on quarter.

Read the three numbers together: capacity is still being added, shipments edged down, utilization remains high. The 2.6-point quarter-on-quarter decline does not reflect falling demand; new capacity has just come online and is not yet fully loaded (the denominator grew). This explains why ASP rose 2.5% quarter on quarter while shipments fell 0.2%: the company is "shipping less, selling higher," allocating limited capacity to categories in short supply (BCD, industrial, automotive) and deliberately giving up low-priced smartphone orders.

This also answers half of the question "can price increases last": as long as utilization stays above 90%, the company can pick its customers; once it falls below 85%, price increases turn into price cuts. 93.1% is the leading indicator to watch most closely in this report.

August 13 Q2 results: just three numbers

The company has announced that its Q2 results will be released on 2026/08/13; this report does not cite its data. Three numbers matter: first, whether revenue reaches the top end of guidance (USD 2.86–2.91 billion), which determines whether the "AI spillover" is still intact; second, whether gross margin exceeds 21%, with the 21% midpoint the dividing line in the race between price increases and depreciation; third, the gross margin direction in Q3 guidance — if guidance is below 22%, depreciation is starting to outweigh price increases.

One hidden metric: the quarter-on-quarter change in absolute depreciation. Q1 was USD 1.088 billion; if Q2 exceeds USD 1.2 billion, the full-year USD 5 billion guidance risks an upward revision, and the gross margin ceiling must be lowered.

Another yardstick: the top end of guidance of USD 2.91 billion is just 3% away from "quarterly revenue breaking USD 3 billion." This threshold is the market's psychological marker for this upcycle — clearing it confirms the trend; failing to clear it means the guidance was an empty promise.

Conclusion

Conclusion: the results themselves pass; the valuation does not. Revenue above guidance, gross margin above the upper end of guidance, utilization at 93.1% — nothing to fault at the operating level; flat net profit stems from depreciation, not operational deterioration. But the results also confirm one thing: 2026 profits will not look good; what looks good is 2027–2028 capacity. Paying 205x PE for 2028 is the only warning this report delivers.
