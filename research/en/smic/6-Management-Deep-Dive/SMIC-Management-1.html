Core view: good engineers, but not fully accountable to shareholders

Split the management assessment in two. Operations merit an 8: the Zhao Haijun and Liang Mong Song team has taken 12-inch capacity from zero to 1.078 million wafers/month, and guidance has not been missed in eight consecutive quarters. Capital allocation merits only a 5: no dividend in 2025, a further USD 8.1 billion of investment in 2026, and the RMB 40.6 billion share issue to acquire SMIC North dilutes shareholders by 7%. This is less a governance judgement than a function of the ownership structure — the Big Fund, Datang and Xinxin hold more than 20% combined, and capacity build-out is the company's first objective.

Operations: guidance is not padded — this management team is credible

Take guidance delivery over the past four quarters. For Q4 2025 management guided to "revenue flat QoQ, gross margin 18%–20%"; actual revenue came in at USD 2.489 billion with gross margin of 19.2%, at the top of the range. Q1 2026 carried the same range; actual revenue was USD 2.506 billion and gross margin 20.1%, above the upper limit. At the Q1 2026 briefing, Q2 guidance was set at "+14%–16% QoQ, gross margin 20%–22%", 2 points above the prior quarter's guide. Three consecutive quarters without a miss — and no "low guidance, high actuals" financial engineering.

Rarer still is the candour on accounting basis. On the February earnings call, CFO Wu Junfeng volunteered that "depreciation from new fabs will lift total 2026 depreciation by about 30% YoY, pressuring gross margin, which we plan to offset by maintaining high utilisation and cutting costs and raising efficiency". Most semiconductor companies in an expansion phase frame depreciation as a "one-off factor"; SMIC frames it as "structural pressure".

The value of the Liang Mong-song line is not in the financials. Achieving mass production on N+2 and advancing N+3 to the 5nm-class without EUV is proof of engineering capability — the broad industry view is that both yield and cost suffer, but "being able to build it" is itself a barrier. He is 74, the single largest governance risk.

Capital allocation: the money went where it should have gone, just not into shareholders' pockets

Three major moves in five years, all in the same direction: convert every available dollar into capacity. The 2020 STAR Market IPO raised RMB 53.2 billion; 2025 capex was USD 8.1 billion, with another USD 8.1 billion in 2026; and in 2026 RMB 40.6 billion of shares were issued to buy the remaining 49% of SMIC North. Four new 12-inch lines (SMIC Beijing, SMIC East, SMIC Shenzhen, SMIC West Qing) are under construction simultaneously, each designed for about 100,000 wafers per month.

The logic is the time window: controls can tighten at any moment, every tool bought is one fewer available, and landing capacity first matters more than paying dividends first. Industrially, this is the optimal solution; on a DCF basis, it is swapping shareholder cash for assets that generate returns only 10 years out.

The cost is explicit: no dividend for 2025, on the grounds that "2026 will still entail relatively large-scale capital expenditure"; no buyback, ever; and the RMB 40.6 billion acquisition paid in shares, lifting total share count from about 7.96 billion to 8.561 billion and diluting existing shareholders by about 7%. For a company that pays no dividend, does no buyback and keeps issuing shares, the only source of shareholder return is the share price.

Governance and integrity: no scandals, but structural constraints

Ownership structure determines governance. HKSCC (H-share custodian) holds a nominal 56.29%, Datang Holdings (Hong Kong) 14.06%, Xinxin (Hong Kong) 4.50%, National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund Phase II 1.59% and China Information Communication Technologies 0.90%. The top three shareholders all have state-owned backgrounds and together hold over 20%; board and management appointments serve industrial policy objectives first and shareholder returns second.

The integrity record is clean at company level: the December 2020 addition to the US Entity List was a geopolitical event, not financial fraud. In February 2026 Applied Materials was fined USD 252 million for supplying SMIC in breach of rules during 2020–2022; the penalty fell on the supplier rather than on SMIC, but it shows supply-chain compliance risk persists over the long term.

The item to watch is related-party transactions. The RMB 40.6 billion acquisition of 49% of SMIC North involves 5 shareholders including the National Integrated Circuit Fund, making it a related-party transaction; the RMB 40.6 billion price implies an equity value of roughly RMB 82.9 billion for all of SMIC North. Whether that price is fair depends on SMIC North's capacity and profitability, and public disclosure is limited — this is the only major capital allocation in this report that cannot be independently verified.

Succession: the biggest single-point risk is not the chairman

Liu Xunfeng is 61 and Zhao Haijun about 62, both still within normal tenure; the real risk is Liang Mong Song at 74. Advanced-node roadmap decisions, process integration and joint development with customers have long rested on him alone, and public disclosure points to no clear second-in-command.

No technical succession plan has been disclosed. R&D headcount stands at 2,420, or 11.9% of employees — a sizable team, but "someone who can lead an N+3 team" and "having 2,420 R&D staff" are two different things. When Liang Mong Song left TSMC, it pulled Samsung's process progress forward by years; the replacement cost for people of this calibre is extremely high.

Implication for investors: an announcement of "Liang Mong Song retirement/departure" in the next two years with advanced-node customer orders holding up would be a sentiment shock; if orders also swing, it becomes a fundamental event.This risk cannot be quantified and can only be set as a watch item.

Conclusion

Conclusion: management passes, but that is not a positive. Operating execution 8, capital allocation 5, governance transparency 6, composite 7 — consistent with the six-gates score. Anyone buying this stock must accept one premise: you are buying a slice of national capacity, not a shareholder-return contract. If that premise is acceptable, look at the price; if not, it should not be bought at any price.
