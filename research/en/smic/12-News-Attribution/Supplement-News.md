## Three themes: price hikes, consolidation, narrative

Price hikes are a real signal. A May 20 report confirmed two things: Q1 ASP rose 2.5% QoQ even as shipments fell 0.2% QoQ, and "foundry prices for some products in advantageous segments rose steadily." That directly underpins Q2 guidance of 14%–16% QoQ growth; the A-share rose 12.62% that day on RMB 29 billion turnover.

Consolidation is a real signal. The RMB 40.6 billion acquisition of the remaining 49% of SMIC North cleared review on May 11, was registered on May 20 and closed in early July — the largest restructuring in STAR Market history. It buys in minority interests in a cash cow, accretive to net profit attributable to shareholders but 7% dilutive. The stock reacted positively because the deal was also read as "greater state support."

The narrative is sentiment. On May 25 Huawei unveiled the "Tao Law," proposing logic folding and time scaling in place of geometric scaling and claiming the equivalent of 1.4nm within five years. SMIC's A-share rose 18.78% that day; its H-share hit HKD 93 intraday the next day. The speech brought SMIC not one new order, but it gave the market a story that SMIC can bypass EUV.

## None of the three selloffs was company-specific, so the moves are noise

A-shares fell -7.85% and H-shares -9.97% on 17 July, -6.04% / -7.74% on 30 July and -6.04% on 3 August. All three selloffs had one thing in common: no company-specific bad news. The drivers were cooling in the global AI trade and profit-taking in semiconductors. Hua Hong fell 11.9% and over 8% over the same period, with Cambricon and Montage down in tandem.

On 9 August UBS upgraded the H-shares from Neutral to Buy and raised its target price from HKD 76 to HKD 96.2 — the only positive sell-side report in three months. The market's response: A-shares fell -1.75% and H-shares -1.05% on 10 August, with volume shrinking to 42.8 million shares against a July peak of 250 million. The report generated no buying, which tells us that whoever wanted to buy has already bought.

The test for noise is simple: once the news breaks, if the fundamental numbers (ASP, utilization, depreciation) have not changed, it is noise. Three selloffs and one analyst upgrade all meet that test.

## Valuation, not earnings, is fuelling this rally

Map three months of events against the share price and the correlation with earnings announcements is very low. The Q1 report (5/14) moved the stock just 0.95%, while a single technical talk (5/25) drove an 18.78% gain. Pricing power sits with sentiment, not with the numbers.

A sentiment-priced market has one characteristic: it rises fast, falls fast, and needs no reason. From July 9 to August 10 the stock fell 27%, yet the company issued no negative announcement in that window and left Q2 guidance unchanged. That decline was not fundamental deterioration but valuation mean reversion.

What it means for holders: if you bought on the domestic substitution thesis, these three months of volatility are irrelevant — what matters is 2027 capacity and depreciation; if you bought because "the stock will go up," you did not buy SMIC, you bought sentiment, and you need a stop-loss. What it means for those on the sidelines: wait for sentiment to clear, not for news to improve.

One more practical observation: volume in this rally clustered in late May and early July, both news-driven, with single-day turnover of RMB 25–37 billion, then rapidly shrinking to RMB 4–6 billion. This pulse-style volume expansion is typical of sentiment money — in fast, out fast, and no basis for long-term buying. Real long-term money entering usually shows up as weeks of moderate volume expansion, not a one-day spike.
