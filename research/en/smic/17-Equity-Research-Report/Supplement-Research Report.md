## Chapter 1 Company Overview

SMIC was founded in 2000, listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2004 and on the STAR Market in 2020 (688981); its H shares trade as 00981. Total share capital is 8.561 billion shares, comprising about 5.94 billion H shares and about 2.62 billion A shares. The top three shareholders are HKSCC (H-share custodian) at 56.29%, Datang Holdings (Hong Kong) at 14.06% and Xinxin (Hong Kong) at 4.50%.

Capacity: 2026Q1 monthly capacity of 1.078 million wafers in 8-inch equivalent, ranking third globally with about 5% of the pure-play foundry market; 12-inch accounts for 76.4% of revenue and 8-inch 23.6%, with China at 88.9% of revenue. Production bases span Shanghai, Beijing, Tianjin and Shenzhen, plus four new 12-inch lines (SMIC Beijing, SMIC Orient, SMIC Shenzhen, SMIC Xiqing), each designed for about 100,000 wafers per month and ramping.

Process nodes: mature nodes span 0.35 micron to 28nm; advanced N+2 (7nm-class) is in mass production and N+3 (5nm-class) is in progress, both based on DUV multi-patterning with no EUV.

History: founded in Shanghai in 2000, listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 2004, achieved 14nm mass production in 2019, raised RMB 53.2 billion in its July 2020 STAR Market IPO and was added to the US Entity List in December 2020; after 2022 it shifted to a dual strategy of "expanding mature-node capacity + securing supply of advanced nodes", and in 2024–2026 is concentrated on building four new 12-inch lines.

On share structure, H shares total about 5.94 billion and A shares about 2.62 billion; A shares are only 30% of total share capital yet have long traded higher volume than H shares, a technical reason the premium can persist.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

Global pure-play foundry share in Q1 2026: TSMC 73%, Samsung Foundry 6.4%, SMIC 5%, UMC 4%, GlobalFoundries 3%. The industry is worth about USD 200 billion a year and grew roughly 30% YoY in Q1 2026.

Demand: AI servers absorb advanced-node and memory capacity, pushing orders for power management, interface, driver and power discrete devices back to mature nodes, while 8-inch capacity is barely expanding globally, creating a structural shortage. Supply: China's mature-node capacity is expected to reach 33% of the global total by 2027, with four major new fabs ramping in concentrated fashion.

Cycle position: this upcycle began in the second half of 2023, and 2026 is its third year. Of the three top-of-cycle markers, record capex and recovering gross margins have appeared, while customer order cuts have not, pointing to a "late-stage upcycle".

On industry size, global semiconductors are worth about USD 700–800 billion, with pure-play foundry at about USD 200 billion, or a quarter of that. China imports more than USD 300 billion of chips a year and its local manufacturing self-sufficiency remains low, which underpins SMIC's long-term demand.

Foundry is a classic "capacity determines share" industry: whoever has capacity wins the orders. China is expanding capacity faster than anywhere else, which directly explains its share rising from 4% to 5%.

## Chapter 3 Business model

Revenue = capacity × utilisation × ASP. In 2026Q1 the three inputs were 1.078 million wafers/month, 93.1% and ASP up 2.5% QoQ. Revenue by application: smartphones 18.9%, PCs and tablets 13.6%, consumer electronics 46.2%, connectivity and wearables 7.3%, industrial and automotive 14.0%.

Pricing power stems from shortage rather than technology: 8-inch BCD prices rose about 10%, with some orders up 20%, while smartphone and panel driver prices were flat. The company negotiates with each customer individually rather than applying uniform increases.

On costs: depreciation is 43.4% of revenue (2026Q1), materials about 20%, and the balance labour, energy and R&D (R&D at 8.3% of revenue). Gross margin sensitivity to depreciation is close to 1:1.

By customer mix, advanced-node customers are mainly HiSilicon and Cambricon, while mature-node customers include Will Semiconductor, GigaDevice, SG Micro, Silan and BYD Semiconductor. Order visibility is about one quarter and the company gives no long-term order commitments, so revenue forecasts can only roll forward quarter by quarter.

An easily overlooked detail: price increases are negotiated customer by customer rather than set uniformly, so bargaining power varies widely across the customer base and the average ASP metric masks the structural divergence.

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

Revenue: RMB 6.322 billion in 2023, RMB 8.030 billion in 2024 and RMB 9.327 billion in 2025, a three-year CAGR of 21%; RMB 2.506 billion in Q1 2026, +11.5% YoY. Growth is driven by capacity expansion and domestic substitution rather than price increases.

Profit: net profit of USD 685 million in 2025, +39% YoY, though off a 2024 base of USD 493 million (-45% YoY), so on a two-year view profit remains at a low level. Q1 2026 net profit USD 197 million, +5% YoY; under A-share accounting, RMB 1.361 billion, +0.36% YoY.

Cash flow: 2025 EBITDA USD 5.256 billion, capex USD 8.1 billion, free cash flow net outflow of roughly USD 2.8–4.0 billion; Q1 2026 operating cash flow RMB 5.132 billion, capex RMB 10.871 billion, net financing inflow RMB 16.044 billion.

Balance sheet: total assets USD 52.271 billion, total liabilities USD 17.250 billion, debt ratio 33%, net debt USD 660 million, net debt-to-equity 1.9%. ROE about 2.7%, ROA about 1.3%.

The relationship between depreciation and EBITDA merits separate mention: 2025 EBITDA USD 5.256 billion less depreciation USD 3.81 billion leaves about USD 1.45 billion; deduct interest and tax and you get net profit of USD 685 million. So "EBITDA looks good" and "net profit looks bad" can both be true at once; the key is how heavy the depreciation cut is.

R&D spending USD 774 million in 2025, 8.3% of revenue, down 1.2 points YoY. Trimming R&D during a capacity build-out is common practice, but for a company that must catch up via N+3, this is a signal to track.

## Chapter 5 Management

Liu Xunfeng is chairman (age 61), with Zhao Haijun and Liang Mong Song as co-CEOs. Zhao oversees operations and customers; Liang oversees advanced-process technology (age 74, 2025 compensation RMB 28.81 million). Wu Junfeng is finance head.

Guidance delivery is strong: both Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 guidance landed within range or above the upper bound, and Q2 guidance was raised by 2 points. Disclosure is candid: the company proactively explains depreciation pressure without spin.

Capital allocation: RMB 53.2 billion STAR Market IPO in 2020, USD 8.1 billion capex in 2025, RMB 40.6 billion acquisition of 49% of SMIC North in 2026 (share issuance, 7% dilution), no dividend for fiscal 2025, no buyback.

On incentives and assessment, the company granted restricted shares and options in April 2024 covering directors and core technical personnel, with Liu Xunfeng and Liang Mong Song both on the list and a two-year vesting period. Performance metrics are mainly revenue and capacity; ROE and free cash flow are not set as targets—an indirect signal of the company's priorities.

On related-party transactions, the counterparties in the RMB 40.6 billion acquisition of SMIC North include five shareholders such as the National Integrated Circuit Fund, with pricing implying roughly RMB 82.9 billion for all of SMIC North's equity. Public information is insufficient to independently verify whether that price is fair.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Four drivers: capacity (four new 12-inch lines at 100,000 wafers/month each, ramping in 2026–2027), price hikes (8-inch BCD +10%; TSMC plans to raise mature-node quotes in early 2027), domestic substitution (China revenue at 88.9%, share rising to 5%) and advanced nodes (Huawei takes about 43% of N+2 capacity, Cambricon about 11%).

Of the four, capacity and substitution are certain medium-term trends, price hikes are cyclical and advanced nodes are constrained by equipment controls. 2026 revenue growth rests mainly on the first two; 2027 profit elasticity depends on price hikes and advanced nodes.

On structural opportunities, the divergence between 8-inch and 12-inch merits attention: globally there is almost no new 8-inch capacity, and this round of price hikes is steepest there, while new 12-inch lines release capacity in clusters, with potential oversupply in 2027. Revenue is 23.6% 8-inch and 76.4% 12-inch, so the company benefits near term from 8-inch and comes under pressure medium term from 12-inch.

The barrier in automotive-grade business deserves separate mention: AEC-Q100 certification, zero-defect requirements and 18–24 month qualification; once in the supply chain, replacement is very difficult. Industrial and automotive revenue is 14.0% of the total, +62.6% YoY — the business at SMIC that most resembles a moat.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

TSMC's 2nm mass production and 67.7% gross margin are the technology anchor. Samsung Foundry holds 6.4% share with yield under pressure, while UMC and GlobalFoundries stick to mature nodes at 32.5% and 28.3% gross margins respectively; Hua Hong pursues specialty processes at a 13.0% gross margin, trading capacity expansion for share.

SMIC ranks third by scale, with mid-range growth and a lower-middle gross margin of 20.1%. Its advantage is being "the only mainland player"; its disadvantage is "highest cost, two generations behind". Its real competitors are domestic peers (Hua Hong, Nexchip), not TSMC.

On cross-sectional valuation, UMC trades at about 12x P/E, GlobalFoundries about 20x, TSMC about 25–28x and SMIC 205x. Even allowing for a "domestic substitution premium" and an "A-share liquidity premium", fundamentals cannot explain this gap — only capital structure can.

Samsung Foundry is the only player capable of challenging TSMC over the medium to long term, but its yield problems recur, customer trust is lacking and the foundry business loses money at the group level. For SMIC, Samsung is not a direct rival but a "pricing reference for advanced nodes".

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

① Depreciation risk: 2026 depreciation of about USD 5 billion, +30% YoY and still rising in 2027, pressuring gross margin; ② Overcapacity: mainland China's mature-node capacity reaches 33% of global in 2027, potentially triggering a price war; ③ Equipment controls: in April 2026 the US plans to add DUV to China bans; ④ Valuation risk: 205x P/E, 6.3x P/B, A/H premium of 121%; ⑤ Governance risk: no dividend, no buyback, state-led; ⑥ Technology risk: without EUV, node progression faces a physical ceiling.

Ranking the six risks by probability: depreciation (certain), cutthroat competition (high probability in 2027), premium compression (high probability, timing uncertain), controls (medium probability), governance (certain but low impact) and technology roadmap (low probability, long cycle). The first two determine 2027 profit; the last four determine valuation.

The risks are not independent: depreciation and cutthroat competition amplify each other. A price war caps ASP while depreciation is still booked, squeezing gross margin from both sides — the combination to guard against most in 2027.

## Chapter 9 Earnings Forecast

2026E: revenue of about USD 11.4 billion (Q1 actual 25.06 + Q2 guidance midpoint 28.85 + Q3/Q4 up 2%–3% QoQ), gross margin of about 21% and net profit of about RMB 6 billion, against a consensus mean of RMB 6.255 billion.

2027E: base case revenue of USD 13.5 billion, gross margin of 26% and net profit of RMB 15 billion; bear case revenue of USD 11.8 billion, gross margin of 20% and net profit of RMB 10 billion. The gap comes mainly from ASP and depreciation.

Three key assumptions underpin the forecast: 1) Q2 guidance is met; 2) depreciation is not revised higher again in H2 2026; 3) 8-inch price increases extend into H1 2027.

Sensitivity: each 1 percentage point change in gross margin, on 2026E revenue of USD 11.4 billion, moves gross profit by about USD 114 million and net profit by about RMB 800 million, equal to 13% of 2026E net profit. Gross margin is therefore the most sensitive variable in this forecast, more so than revenue growth.

Downside risk to the forecast comes mainly from ASP: if Q3 prices rise only 2% and Q4 stays flat, 2026 net profit could be just RMB 5 billion, 20% below consensus. Upside comes from depreciation running below expectations.

The forecast's credibility rests on how stable the three assumptions are. The first (Q2 guidance is met) has the backing of a high hit rate in Q1 and is the most credible; the second (no further depreciation revisions) awaits company confirmation and is currently an open exposure; the third (price increases extending into H1 2027) depends on TSMC's mature-node pricing and the pace of domestic capacity release, and is the least certain. Credibility declines across the three, so this report sets a wide 2027 forecast range rather than a precise number — the honesty research demands.

## Chapter 10 Valuation

We use P/B: net assets per share of RMB 20.19 in Q1 2026, against a current 6.3x P/B. Applying 5.5x (neutral), 4x (bearish) and 7x (bullish) to expected 2027 net assets per share of RMB 21.8 gives target prices of RMB 120, RMB 88 and RMB 155.

Why not P/E: 2026 depreciation of USD 5 billion compresses net profit to around RMB 6 billion, distorting the P/E denominator; using 205x P/E as a valuation denominator and then multiplying by another multiple amplifies the error tenfold. P/B reflects "how much it costs to rebuild a production line", a closer anchor to a foundry's true value.

Cross-check: H shares at HKD 66.20 convert to RMB 57.2, implying an A-share premium of 121%; UBS's H-share target price of HKD 96.2 (3.3x average 2027–2028 P/B) converts to about RMB 83.

Valuation sensitivity: moving P/B from 4x to 7x implies a share price of RMB 81 to RMB 155, a span of nearly 100%. This is why we use P/B as the primary anchor—it acknowledges valuation uncertainty rather than pretending a precise P/E can settle the matter.

The H-share comparison is the valuation ceiling: as long as the A/H premium holds near 120%, A shares will struggle to decouple, because any rally would push the premium to more extreme levels and draw arbitrageurs to sell A shares.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Rating: Watch, wait for a pullback. Neutral target price RMB 120, 5% below the current price of RMB 126.25; bear case RMB 88 (-30%), bull case RMB 155 (+23%). Weighted 30/50/20, expected return -6.9%.

Buy conditions: share price below RMB 100 (5x PB), or gross margin above 24% for two consecutive quarters with capex falling below USD 5 billion. Abandon conditions: DUV ban enacted, utilization falling below 85%, or gross margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters.

Existing holders: hold, as selling incurs an opportunity cost, but trim in tranches above RMB 155. No position: do not chase, wait for the price.

Advice by investor type: long-term capital (three years or more) can wait to build positions in tranches below RMB 100 and accept two years of low returns; short-term capital should stay out, as daily swings often run 6%–19%; existing holders should not add and should trim in tranches above RMB 155.

A final note on timing: all judgments in this report rest on information available before 2026/08/10. Q2 results are due on August 13; if the figures deviate materially from guidance, the forecasts in this report will need to be recalculated in full.
