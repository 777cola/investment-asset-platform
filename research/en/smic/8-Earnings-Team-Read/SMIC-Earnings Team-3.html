Core view

Munger reads financial statements not for growth but for points of fragility. SMIC's report contains three: depreciation at 43.4% of revenue, capex at 87% of revenue, and customer concentration in China at 88.9%. All three point to the same problem—its operating flexibility is minimal, and if any one variable turns against it, profit has no buffer.

Vulnerability one: depreciation at 43.4% of the total

2026Q1 depreciation and amortization was USD 1.088 billion, 43.4% of revenue; full-year 2025 was USD 3.81 billion, 40.9%; the 2026 guidance is USD 5 billion, which on annualized Q2 revenue guidance of USD 11.5 billion puts the ratio at 43%–44%.

What does that ratio mean? For every 10% drop in revenue, depreciation does not fall by a cent, and gross margin drops 4.3 points. And gross margin is only 20.1%, so a 4-point drop puts it near the breakeven threshold. The fixed-cost structure of foundry determines that it is a "highly leveraged operation" — not financial leverage, but operating leverage.

Munger would say: this structure is an accelerator when demand rises and a guillotine when it falls. It demonstrated that once already in 2023: revenue declined, gross margin fell to around 16%, and net profit was almost zero.

Vulnerability two: 87% capex intensity

Capex of USD 8.1 billion against revenue of USD 9.327 billion — a ratio of 87%. Across global foundry, only TSMC has reached around 60% at the peak of its 3nm expansion; SMIC's ratio is an industry extreme.

High capex is not the problem in itself — the return on it is. USD 8.1 billion invested bought USD 685 million of net profit in 2025, an 8.5% return; over the same period TSMC invested USD 40 billion and got USD 35 billion of net profit, an 87% return. A tenfold gap.

Munger's question is blunt: if a business earns eight cents on every dollar invested, why invest? The answer is "because the country needs it." That is not a financial rationale; it is a political one. Investors need to know what they are paying for.

Vulnerability three: 88.9% of revenue comes from a single region

China accounts for 88.9% of revenue, the US 9.3%. In the "domestic substitution" narrative this structure is a strength; on a risk checklist it is a weakness — its customer base and its equipment sources sit at opposite ends of the same geopolitical conflict. Customers are entirely in China, equipment entirely overseas; a policy shift on either side hits revenue and capacity at the same time.

Munger would file this under "businesses a single political blow can kill." Such businesses deserve no valuation premium, only a discount — because business logic cannot forecast their next ten years.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: These results are not fraudulent, nor do they hide bad debt; the problem is entirely structural — depreciation 43.4%, capex 87%, single region 88.9%. Three numbers determine that it is powerful in a tailwind and fragile in a headwind. Paying 6.3x PB for this structure amounts to assuming the wind never turns.
