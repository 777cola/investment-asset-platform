Masterclass Close Read: Buffett — SMIC

When reading financial reports, ask one question: for every yuan invested, how many yuan can it grow into?

Core view

Buffett reads financial statements in this order: ROE → capex → free cash flow → debt. SMIC's scorecard on these four: ROE 2.7%, capex at 87% of revenue, negative free cash flow, debt ratio 33%. The first three fail; the fourth is excellent. That is why it is safe but not valuable.

ROE 2.7%: not weak operations, but an asset-heavy model

2025 net profit attributable to shareholders was USD 685 million, ending attributable equity about USD 25 billion and ROE 2.7%. Total assets were USD 52.271 billion, for an ROA of 1.3%. Both figures are low in any industry, but not abnormal in foundry — TSMC's 33% ROE is the exception, built on a 67.7% gross margin.

The three DuPont factors: net margin 7.9% (low), asset turnover about 0.18x (extremely low), equity multiplier about 1.5x (low). Not one of the three looks good. Net margin is low because of depreciation, turnover is low because a single line takes three years to reach full utilization, and the equity multiplier is low because the company funds itself with equity, not debt.

Buffett would draw a conclusion here: for a company with 2.7% ROE, fair PB is around 1.5x (assuming a 10% required return and 6% long-term growth). A 6.3x PB implies an ROE of 38% — higher than TSMC's. At this price you are buying a "miracle."

Capex 87%: he calls these companies "money-eating machines"

2025 capex was USD 8.1 billion, or 87% of revenue, with another USD 8.1 billion slated for 2026. In Buffett's language, this is "expansionary spending beyond maintenance capex" — maintenance capex being the money needed to keep existing capacity running, expansionary capex the money that adds new capacity. SMIC's problem is that both are large and the two cannot be separated.

That inability to separate them has a consequence: EBITDA looks strong (USD 5.256 billion, 57.3% margin), but a large chunk of it must be ploughed straight back into equipment, leaving nothing to pay out to shareholders or use to repay debt. The "cheap" 11x EV/EBITDA is therefore an illusion — free cash flow is negative.

When he bought TSMC in 2022, capex was about 50% of revenue, gross margin 60% and ROE 30%. All three metrics were a notch better than SMIC's, and the valuation was only 3x PB. On the same yardstick, SMIC is twice as expensive as TSMC was then and two notches worse.

33% debt: the one metric that reassures him

Total assets USD 52.271 billion, total liabilities USD 17.250 billion, debt ratio 33%; cash USD 7.279 billion, net debt USD 660 million, net debt-to-equity ratio 1.9%. This balance sheet can weather a downturn — when the industry bottomed in 2023, SMIC had no liquidity problems, and this structure is why.

But Buffett would caution: a safe balance sheet does not equal a good investment. For a company that does not lack cash but also does not make money, the margin of safety shows up only in "it won't go to zero," not in "it can make money." The reason to buy it must be the price, not the quality.

Conclusion

Buffett-style verdict: Safety 9, Return 3. He would file SMIC under "too hard" — not because he cannot understand it, but because he cannot compute a margin of safety: a 2.7% ROE against 6.3x PB leaves it expensive under any assumption. Only when ROE exceeds 10% and PB falls below 2x would he reopen this financial report.
