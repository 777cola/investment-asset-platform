SMIC: A Company That Turns All Its Money into Machines
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 205x

Core view

This is the publishable version of the team's close reading, with the framework jargon stripped out and only the numbers and judgments retained. About 1,000 words, ready for direct use in a WeChat public account post or an internal weekly note.

I. The conclusion first

SMIC closed at RMB 126.25 on August 10, down 27% from its July 9 high of RMB 173. Market cap is RMB 1.08 trillion, or 205x TTM earnings and 6.3x book. Its H shares traded at HKD 66.20 the same day, equivalent to RMB 57 — the same company, two markets, a price gap of more than double.

My conclusion: watch, not buy. Not because the company is bad, but because the price no longer leaves any room for error.

II. Financials: revenue rising, profit flat

Q1 2026 revenue USD 2.506 billion, +11.5% YoY; gross margin 20.1%, down 2.4 points YoY; net profit USD 197 million, up just 5% YoY. A-share net profit RMB 1.361 billion, +0.36% YoY — essentially flat.

Where did the money go? Depreciation. Q1 depreciation and amortisation USD 1.088 billion, +25.7% YoY, or 43.4% of revenue. Full-year guidance implies depreciation of about USD 5 billion, up roughly 30% YoY. The USD 258 million of incremental revenue was fully absorbed by the USD 223 million of incremental depreciation.

This is not an operating problem. EBITDA margin was 57.3% in the period, up 0.8 points YoY; capacity utilisation 93.1%, up 9.5 points YoY. Operations are improving — the money earned just isn't enough to pay for the equipment.

III. Demand: AI spillover, not a smartphone recovery

The revenue mix tells the story: consumer electronics at 46.2%, up 26.9% YoY; industrial and automotive at 14.0%, up 62.6% YoY; smartphones down to 18.9%, a decline of 12.9% YoY. What is driving SMIC is AI server power supplies, interface chips and automotive-grade BCD — not smartphones.

That also explains why the price increases are "structural": 8-inch BCD rose about 10%, with some orders up 20%, while smartphone and display driver prices did not move at all. Zhao Haijun said at the results briefing that these customers are "equally struggling right now." What can raise prices is what is in short supply; what cannot raise prices is what suffers from overcapacity. Two fates inside the same fab.

IV. Valuation: what are you paying for at 205x PE?

Three scenarios: bear case (price hikes peak in 2027, depreciation keeps climbing, 4x PB) gives a target price of RMB 88; base case (full capacity persists, ASP rises 5% a year, 5.5x PB) RMB 120; bull case (domestic substitution accelerates, 7x PB) RMB 155. Weighted 30/50/20, expected return is -6.9%.

In other words, buying at the current price reasonably implies a 7% loss, with a further 30% downside below that. This is not a level at which to "hold if you're bullish" — it is a level at which to "wait even if you're bullish."

V. When to buy

Two conditions, either of which would trigger a re-rating: first, the share price falls below RMB 100 (5x PB, around 160x P/E (TTM) — still expensive, but open to debate); second, capex drops below USD 5 billion or the company begins paying dividends — that would mark a shift from "building capacity" to "returning capital to shareholders" and prompt a re-rating of the valuation framework.

Until then, this is a company to respect but not to chase. Domestic substitution is real, the AI spillover is real, and Q2 guidance of +14%–16% QoQ is real. The only thing that isn't real is the price.

Conclusion

Published conclusion: A good company with a real trend behind it, but the price is expensive. Watch and wait for a pullback—below RMB 100, or until the capex cycle peaks.
