Synthesis of Views — SMIC

Four people, three things they see in the same earnings report
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 205x

Core view

Read together, the four deep dives overlap heavily: all agree the revenue mix is improving, all agree depreciation is suppressing profit, and all agree shareholder returns are absent. They diverge on one point only—whether this is "profit temporarily suppressed" or "profit that will never materialise." That single question determines the valuation.

Consensus one: revenue is real, and the mix is improving

Consumer electronics reached 46.2% (+26.9%) and industrial and automotive 14.0% (+62.6%). These two numbers are not financial engineering—they reflect customer orders: AI server power supplies, interface chips, automotive-grade BCD. The 12.9% decline in smartphones instead proves the company did not chase volume through low pricing.

The significance of the consensus: this is not a "cyclical rebound," it is a "structural shift." After the shift, SMIC's revenue volatility will be lower than in the past, because the downstream mix moves from smartphones alone to three legs: consumer, industrial and automotive.

Consensus 2: Depreciation caps earnings, and the squeeze will not ease near term

Depreciation: USD 3.81 billion in 2025, rising to about USD 5 billion in 2026, or 43%–44% of revenue. Four investors made the same point from four angles: Duan Yongping — "profit is calculated"; Buffett — "EBITDA is fake"; Munger — "a 10% drop in revenue takes 4.3 points off gross margin"; Li Lu — "rent goes to capacity."

The consensus takeaway: 2026 net profit will not look good, and this is not an operating problem but the overlap of accounting and the capacity cycle. Any valuation built on 2026 P/E must therefore be discounted.

Disagreement: are profits being suppressed, or simply failing to grow?

Duan Yongping and Munger lean toward the latter: low returns dictated by the business model will not turn into high ROE even once depreciation peaks, because the next round of capacity expansion begins immediately. Buffett sits in the middle: if capex stops and utilisation holds, ROE can reach 10%, but that is a 2029 story. Li Lu leans toward the former: industry position will eventually translate into pricing power, it just takes time.

Verdict: trade the short term (12 months) as "suppressed," track the long term (5 years) as "possible emergence."Near term, 2026 net profit of around RMB 6 billion against depreciation of RMB 5 billion means profit is indeed suppressed; long term, if 2028 capex falls to USD 5 billion and gross margin reaches 30%, ROE can exceed 10%, at which point 3x PB is reasonable.

What this verdict means for positioning: do not buy now, but set "capex falling below RMB 5 billion" as the trigger for reassessment.

Conclusion

Synthesis: The revenue-mix improvement is real, the depreciation drag is real, and the absence of shareholder returns is real. Together they add up to neither "buy" nor "sell" but "wait": for depreciation to peak, for capex to fall, for dividends to start. Until one of the three happens, 205x P/E is a price not worth paying.
