Masterclass Close Read: Duan Yongping — SMIC

Read financial reports by first asking "where the money went"

Core view

The line that matters most in this earnings report is not revenue but "capital expenditure USD 1.563 billion" against "operating cash flow RMB 5.132 billion". The cash generated in one quarter does not cover half a quarter's equipment spending. This is the order in which Duan Yongping reads financial statements: first how much cash comes in, how much goes out and who the remainder belongs to, then profit.

Revenue: good numbers, but not a good business

Revenue USD 2.506 billion, +11.5% YoY, above company guidance and slightly below the market consensus of USD 2.52 billion. By segment, consumer electronics 46.2% (+26.9%) and industrial/automotive 14.0% (+62.6%) reflect real demand, while smartphones 18.9% (-12.9%) were a drag. This mix shows SMIC captured AI spillover, not a consumer electronics recovery.

But Duan Yongping would press: how much investment does this growth require to sustain? The answer is USD 8.1 billion a year in capex. Revenue rose 11.5%, and capex equals 87% of revenue — every extra 1.0 dollar of revenue requires USD 0.87 spent on equipment first. The quality of this growth is low — it rests not on brand, patents or customer stickiness, but on continuously adding capacity.

Compare Apple, which he knows well: Apple's capex is 3% of revenue, and it earns on brand and ecosystem. SMIC is the reverse — 87% of revenue must be plowed back. Both are called "manufacturing," but one collects rent and the other hauls bricks.

Profit line: net income USD 197 million, a function of depreciation, not of operations

Net profit USD 197 million, up 5% YoY, net margin 7.9%. This figure is almost entirely determined by depreciation: depreciation was USD 1.088 billion, and had USD 100 million less been charged, net profit would have been more than 30% higher. In other words, SMIC's profit is not "earned" — it is "calculated."

Duan Yongping dislikes this kind of profit. He has said that "profit can be adjusted, but cash flow does not lie," and at SMIC the depreciation period, residual value assumptions and capitalization policy can each affect profit without affecting cash. The right way to look at this company is EBITDA (USD 5.256 billion, 2025) and capex (USD 8.1 billion); only what remains after subtracting the two is the money it truly has left.

That subtraction comes out negative. So he would not get excited about "net profit up 5% YoY," nor angry about "205x PE" — he would say the company should not be priced on PE at all right now.

Shareholder side: no dividend, no buyback, and a share issuance on top

No dividend for 2025, on the grounds that 2026 capex will require another USD 8.1 billion; never bought back stock; in July 2026 it issued RMB 40.6 billion in shares to acquire 49% of SMIC North, increasing share count by 7%. Taken together, shareholders' position in this company is clear: the people who put money in, not the people who take money out.

This is not management greed; it is dictated by the nature of the company. SMIC's first mission is to build up mainland China's capacity; shareholder returns are only the second. Anyone buying it must accept this ordering before discussing price.

Conclusion

Duan Yongping-style conclusion: This earnings report proves it is a company that "gets bigger and bigger while making less and less money" — revenue at a record high, net profit flat, cash flow negative, shareholders diluted. He would acknowledge this is a company the country needs, but he would not pay RMB 126 for an asset where "I cannot be sure I will get cash back in ten years."
