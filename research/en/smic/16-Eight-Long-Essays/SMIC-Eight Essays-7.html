⑦ Risk list — SMIC

Six risks, ranked by probability × impact

Core view

A risk list is only as good as its ranking. Ranked by probability × impact, SMIC's six risks are: depreciation (high probability, medium impact); cutthroat competition (medium-high probability, high impact); export controls (medium probability, extremely high impact); A/H premium convergence (high probability, medium impact); governance and dividends (certain, low impact); and technology roadmap (low probability, high impact).

Depreciation ranks first: certain, and recurring every year

2025 depreciation was USD 3.81 billion, or 40.9% of revenue. For 2026 it is about USD 5 billion, up 30% YoY and equal to 43%–44% of revenue; for 2027 management explicitly said it "will keep growing." The numerator of gross margin (price) therefore has to race against a denominator (depreciation) that is locked in ahead of time.

Quantified: the extra USD 1.2 billion of depreciation in 2026 equals 12.9% of 2025 revenue. Offsetting that through pricing would require a 13% ASP increase; in Q1 ASP rose just 2.5%. That 10-point gap explains the 2.4-point YoY decline in gross margin.

What defines this risk is its certainty: it needs no bad news, it simply recurs every year. That makes it the first constraint on valuation — as long as depreciation keeps rising, the P/E will not look good.

2. Involution: Chinese capacity cannibalising itself

Mainland China's mature-node capacity is expected to reach 33% of the global total in 2027. Hua Hong, Nexchip and SiEn are all expanding, and they are running the same playbook: win general-purpose categories on price. The first signal came after Chinese New Year in 2026, when several Chinese foundries began preparing 15%–20% price cuts on PMIC and DDI.

SMIC's response is to hold price rather than match the cuts, protecting gross margin at the cost of share. Financially correct in the short term, but share erodes over the long term. The bigger problem: this race to the bottom is not a market outcome but the product of local governments chasing output value and jobs—it will not stop simply because the industry is losing money.

The trigger point for this risk is most likely 2H27, when three conditions converge: the four major new fabs reach full production, domestic capacity is released in a concentrated wave, and demand growth slows.

Ranked third to sixth: regulation, premium, governance, technology

Export controls: In April 2026 the US plans to extend its China ban to DUV, covering five companies including SMIC. Probability is low, but enactment would stall expansion while depreciation continues — the ugliest combination. In February 2026 Applied Materials was fined USD 252 million for supplying SMIC in breach of the rules, a sign that enforcement is real.

A/H premium: The 121% premium can compress with no change in fundamentals. From 9 July to 10 August both listings fell 26%–27% in tandem and the premium did not narrow; once capital flows reverse, however, the premium compresses passively.

Governance: No dividends, no buybacks, state-led — falling share prices meet no company-level buying. That is the risk of "no downside protection".

Technology: What comes after N+3? Without EUV, the next step down (3nm equivalent) is orders of magnitude harder. That is a post-2029 problem, but no one can answer it now.

Worst-case scenario: the 2027 triple hit

Three risks compound: depreciation keeps rising into 2027 as capex stays elevated, mature-node pricing gets pushed back by domestic peers amid overcapacity, and the AI capex wave recedes, dragging demand. The result: revenue growth drops to single digits, gross margin falls below 15%, and net profit returns to 2023 levels — less than RMB 1 billion for the full year.

Where does that leave the stock? A de-rating from 6.3x PB back to 2x implies RMB 40, 68% below the current price. This is not a forecast; it is the floor of a stress test.

How likely is it? All three at once is not high probability, but two at once — depreciation plus cutthroat competition — is not low. That is why we assign a 30% weight to the bear case, and why RMB 126 is not a buy point.

Conclusion

Risk checklist: conclusion. The first two risks — depreciation and cutthroat competition — are high-probability and already unfolding; the remaining four are low-frequency or slow-moving variables. On balance, the most reasonable response is not to liquidate but to avoid chasing highs and wait for the price to offer a buffer. A 205x P/E embeds none of the six risks. One line to remember: the item at the top of the risk checklist is always the one that happens every year yet no one wants to calculate — because once it is calculated clearly, it becomes very hard to convince yourself to buy at a high level.
