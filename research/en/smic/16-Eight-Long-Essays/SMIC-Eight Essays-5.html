⑤ Industry and competition — SMIC

Third place, second-tier fate
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  PE(TTM) Approximately 205x
← Back to the hub

Core view

Q1 2026 global pure-play foundry share: TSMC 73%, Samsung Foundry 6.4%, SMIC 5%, UMC 4%, GlobalFoundries 3%. SMIC overtook UMC and GlobalFoundries to reach third place for the first time, a historic shift in position. But between "third" and "first" lie a 14x revenue gap, a 47-point gross margin gap and two process generations.

Structure: one dominant player, one weak player, four strong players

The standout is TSMC: USD 40.2 billion per quarter, 67.7% gross margin, 2nm in mass production, customers queuing to prepay for capacity. Its position rests not on price but on being the only one who can do it.

The weak player is Samsung Foundry: 6.4% share, 2nm ramp, inconsistent yields, and sustained foundry losses at the group level. It has technology and capital; what it lacks is customer trust.

The four strong players are UMC, GlobalFoundries, SMIC and Hua Hong: all in mature nodes or specialty processes, with gross margins of 13%–32.5%, surviving on regional markets and niche categories. SMIC is the largest, fastest-growing and most expensive of the four.

The key to this structure: the top two live on advanced nodes, the bottom four live on mature nodes, and there is no buffer in between. Advanced nodes have mild cyclical swings (customers are Apple and Nvidia); mature nodes swing widely (customers are consumer electronics and industrial). SMIC sits in the latter pool.

Cycle: AI muddied the waters and lifted prices

What makes 2026 unusual: AI servers are absorbing so much leading-edge process and memory capacity that orders for power management, interface, driver and power discrete devices are being pushed back to mature nodes. Global 8-inch capacity is barely expanding, so prices are rising — BCD up about 10%, some orders up 20%, and TSMC is reported to raise mature-node quotes in early 2027.

This is a spillover upcycle, not a demand-driven one. The difference: a demand-driven upcycle rests on end-market sell-through (phones and cars selling well); a spillover upcycle rests on capacity crowding-out (leading-edge is too tight, so orders are forced to spill elsewhere). The durability of the latter depends on AI capex, not on consumer electronics.

That also explains why handset chips have not risen a cent: handset demand itself has not recovered; it is merely that AI's capacity squeeze has incidentally benefited other mature-node categories.

Competition: the real rivals are domestic

TSMC does not compete with it for low-price orders in mature nodes, Samsung does not compete with it for domestic customers, and UMC and GlobalFoundries are contracting. The real competitors taking its business are Hua Hong, Nexchip and SiCarrier.

Hua Hong's 2026Q1 revenue was USD 6.61 billion, up 22.2% YoY, with a gross margin of 13.0% and capacity utilization above 100%; Nexchip is expanding CIS capacity; SiCarrier makes power devices. The common playbook of these fabs is "winning generic categories on low price" — after Chinese New Year 2026, several Chinese foundries plan to cut PMIC and DDI prices by 15%–20%.

SMIC's choice is not to match prices, protect gross margin and cede share. That choice is financially correct (gross margin 20.1% vs Hua Hong's 13.0%) but strategically costly (share in generic categories will erode). It is betting on "specialty processes + advanced nodes" rather than "scale of generic capacity."

Capacity map: four new lines will shape the next three years

SMIC's incremental capacity is entirely in 12-inch: SMIC Beijing Jingcheng, SMIC Orient in Shanghai Lingang, SMIC Shenzhen and SMIC Xiqing in Tianjin, four lines with designed capacity of about 100,000 wafers per month each, ramping through 2026–2027. These four lines are where the USD 8.1 billion of capex goes, and why depreciation keeps rising in 2027.

The four lines target different products: Jingcheng leans toward logic and advanced-node support, Orient toward 28nm and above, Shenzhen toward specialty processes, Xiqing toward analog and power. The benefit of this dispersed layout is a diversified customer base; the drawback is that each fab must ramp alone and absorb its own start-up losses.

Implication for investors: capacity is visible (construction progress can be tracked in local government announcements), yield is not (the company does not disclose it). So what can be calculated is when depreciation peaks; what cannot is when gross margin improves.

Conclusion

Industry and competition conclusion: The third-place position is real, but it is third place in "regional markets + mature nodes," not third place in "global technology." The 2026 upcycle comes from AI spillover; the 2027 oversupply comes from Chinese capacity coming online. The position has moved up a step; the nature of the business has not changed. In one sentence: its rivals are not in Taiwan, but Hua Hong and Nexchip on the same street. The opponent in a price war is never the strongest player, but the one most short of cash.
