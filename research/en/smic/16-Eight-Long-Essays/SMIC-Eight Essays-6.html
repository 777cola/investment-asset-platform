⑥ Management — SMIC

Capable engineers, a steward who does not crunch the numbers
Research date 2026/08/10  ·  Price ¥126.25 (-1.75%)  ·  Market cap About RMB 1.08 trillion  ·  P/E (TTM) Approximately 205x

Core view

Assessing management comes down to two things: operational execution and capital allocation. On the first, SMIC's current management deserves an 8: guidance delivered on schedule, capacity expansion on track, technology nodes hit on time. On the second, they deserve only a 5: no dividends, no buybacks, continuous share issuance. That gap between the two scores defines this company.

Operations: three quarters, three deliveries

Q4 2025 guidance was for flat revenue QoQ and a gross margin of 18%–20%; actual revenue came in at USD 2.489 billion with a gross margin of 19.2%. Q1 2026 guidance pointed to the same range; actual revenue was USD 2.506 billion with a gross margin of 20.1%, above the upper limit. Q2 guidance is +14%–16% QoQ with a gross margin of 20%–22%, raised 2 points from the prior quarter. Three rounds of guidance, three results within range or above the upper limit — not one miss.

That hit rate is unusual among A-share semiconductor names. Most companies in an expansion phase guide optimistically and deliver conservatively, using expectations to manage the share price; SMIC does the opposite, guiding conservatively and delivering above, which shows management has a firm grip on the pace of capacity ramp-up.

More noteworthy is the candor on accounting. CFO Wu Junfeng said back in February that depreciation from new fabs would lift total 2026 depreciation by about 30% YoY and pressure gross margin, without dressing depreciation up as a one-off. That style of disclosure earns institutional trust over the long run.

Capital allocation: three major moves in five years, all in the same direction

The 2020 STAR Market IPO raised RMB 53.2 billion, 2025 capex ran at USD 8.1 billion, and in 2026 the company will invest a further USD 8.1 billion while using RMB 40.6 billion of share issuance to buy 49% of SMIC North. All three moves point the same way: convert every dollar available into capacity.

The logic is the time window. Controls can tighten at any moment and every tool bought means one fewer available, so landing capacity matters more than paying dividends. From an industry standpoint this is optimal; from a shareholder standpoint it is "swapping cash for assets that pay off only a decade later."

The cost is explicit: no dividend for 2025 (capex takes priority), no buyback ever, and share count up from 7.96 billion to 8.561 billion (7% dilution). For a company that pays no dividend, buys back nothing and keeps issuing shares, the only source of shareholder return is the share price. That is not a criticism but a statement of fact—anyone buying the stock must accept that premise.

Governance: state capital dominance, single-point technology

Ownership: HKSCC (H-share custodian) 56.29%, Datang Holdings 14.06%, Xinxin 4.50%, Big Fund Phase II 1.59%, China Information and Communication Technologies 0.90%. All three top shareholders are state-backed and together hold over 20%; board and management appointments serve industrial policy objectives first.

The key single-point technical risk is Liang Mong-song, aged 74. Advanced-node roadmap decisions, process integration and joint customer development have long been led by him alone, with no clear second figure visible in public disclosures, and the company has not disclosed a technical succession plan.

Together these two facts define the real governance picture: strategy will not drift (state-led), but execution rests heavily on one individual (Liang Mong-song). The former makes the company stable; the latter makes it fragile.

Division of roles among the three: chairman, operations, technology

Liu Xunfeng is chairman, representing shareholders and policy; Zhao Haijun is co-CEO, running operations and customers; Liang Mong Song is co-CEO, running the technology roadmap. The division of labour among the three is clear, which is why the company has shown no strategic wavering over the past five years.

Zhao Haijun's style leans cautious: on every earnings call he discusses depreciation first, then demand, and on pricing he says only "selected categories." That style looks "insufficiently optimistic" during upcycles, but it protects gross margin during downturns — not chasing orders on price in 2026Q1 is a reflection of this style.

Liang Mong Song's value lies in the technology roadmap. Bringing N+2 to mass production and advancing N+3 to the 5nm-class without EUV is proof of engineering capability. But he is 74, and public information shows no clear successor — the biggest single-point governance risk.

Conclusion

Management verdict: Operations 8, capital allocation 5, governance 6, overall 7. This is a trustworthy team, but it serves national capacity targets first and shareholder returns second — a ranking that will not change with the share price. In one sentence: management is trustworthy, but it answers to the state before it answers to shareholders. Before buying, be clear with yourself whether you can accept that ranking.
