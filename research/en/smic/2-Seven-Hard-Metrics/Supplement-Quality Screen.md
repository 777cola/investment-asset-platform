## Which items failed, and why did they fail?

The four failures are essentially four facets of one thing: the company converted all of its earnings into equipment. 2025 revenue was USD 9.327 billion, depreciation USD 3.81 billion and capex USD 8.1 billion — depreciation at 40.9% of revenue, capex at 87%. Once equipment enters the fab, depreciation begins eating into profit, ROE is compressed to 2.7%, free cash flow is negative by construction, and nothing is left for dividends.

Gross margin of 20.1% falls short of the 30% threshold not because prices are low but because the cost structure is heavy. On the same 12-inch wafer, TSMC charges over USD 20,000 for 3nm at a 67.7% gross margin, whereas SMIC sells mature 28–40nm nodes at unit prices of a few hundred dollars to just over USD 1,000 — yet depreciation is amortised over the same five-year period. Unit prices differ tenfold while depreciation lives are identical, so the gross margin gap is structural.

## It passed three of them, a record that speaks to solid fundamentals

A 33% debt-to-asset ratio, USD 660 million of net debt and 1.9% net debt-to-equity: this is the financing advantage of its state-owned background, and it is what allows four new 12-inch fabs (SMIC Beijing, SMIC East, SMIC Shenzhen, SMIC West Qing) to be built simultaneously. In a downturn it will not die from an inability to repay debt — the most important survival metric for a foundry.

Operating cash flow is 3.8x net profit, which looks impressive but really reflects depreciation add-backs: EBITDA was USD 5.256 billion in 2025, 7.7x net profit.This is a company with thick EBITDA and thin net profit; on EV/EBITDA it is not expensive (about 11x), on P/E it is absurdly expensive (205x). The two valuation methods give completely opposite answers, and this is why.

A five-year revenue CAGR of 19%, the hardest of the seven points. Revenue rose from USD 3.9 billion in 2020 to USD 9.3 billion in 2025, driven not by price increases but by doubled capacity plus order migration from domestic substitution. The trend continues: Q1 2026 revenue +11.5% YoY, Q2 guidance +14%–16% QoQ.

## But whether it is fatal: two ways to die

Debt service will not kill it. Debt ratio 33%, cash on hand USD 7.28 billion, net debt USD 660 million, with national credit behind it. For a company like this, the worst outcome is long-term low returns, not bankruptcy.

Valuation will. A company with ROE of 2.7% is being given 6.3x PB; the implied assumption is that ROE doubles to above 15% within three years. For that to hold, gross margin must rise from 20% to above 30% — meaning ASP up more than 40%, or depreciation growth reversing. Neither has a clear path in 2026–2027.

So the conclusion from the seven hard metrics is not "avoid" — it is "do not buy at a first-tier company's price." Its fair positioning is "cyclical growth stock": 3–4x PB at the upcycle, 1.5–2x PB at the downcycle. At 6.3x now, it is priced at the cycle peak.

One line to close: what this framework really screens out is "buying a cyclical stock at a growth stock's price," not SMIC the company. It belongs on the cyclical table, not the growth table.
