## Three-scenario valuation (anchored on 2027 operating profit, 12-month target)

| Scenario | Core assumptions | Target price | Relative to the current price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear case (30% probability) | DRAM contract prices turn to a 20% decline in 2027; HBM premium narrows from 3x to 2x; full-year operating profit falls back to 110 trillion | KRW 880,000 | -52% |
| Neutral (45% probability) | 2026 operating profit of about 230 trillion, falling to 180 trillion in 2027, at 6x plus net cash of 95 trillion | KRW 1.65 million | -9% |
| Optimistic (25% probability) | HBM4 sole-supply arrangement intact; supply remains tight in 2027; operating profit holds above 250 trillion; assign 7x | KRW 2.6 million | +43% |

> Methodology: target market cap = 2027 operating profit × multiple + net cash, divided by 711 million shares. Bear case 110 × 4.6 + 120 = KRW 626trn → 880,000; base case 180 × 6 + 95 = KRW 1,175trn → 1.65 million; bull case 250 × 7 + 100 = KRW 1,850trn → 2.6 million. The multiple spread reflects uncertainty over the cycle position, with the more bearish view warranting the lower multiple. Probabilities are subjective, benchmarked to the drawdowns of the three memory cycles from 2015 to 2025.

## Duan Yongping: this is a spread trade, not a business

When Duan Yongping assesses a manufacturer, his first question is whether a price increase would lose customers. If it would, it is a spread business; if not, it is a good business. SK hynix raising commodity DRAM prices by 60% would lose customers — customers have no choice, not no desire to switch. Only the small HBM slice comes close to a good business: Nvidia is willing to sign multi-year long-term agreements and pay 3x the price to secure supply two years ahead.

He would not deny the company's strength, but he would deny its predictability. Q1 2026 revenue rose 60% quarter on quarter on flat shipments — all the profit came from price. The cycle always takes price gains back; the only question is which month. His conclusion would most likely be "wait until it falls to the point where nobody wants it", rather than calling it cheap after a 38% drop — a 38% drop only shows how much it had risen before.

He would add one point on management: in 2023, when the whole industry cut capex, it did not cut HBM — that is doing one's duty; in 2026, raising USD 26.5 billion at the peak while approving RMB 4 trillion of capex — that is going with the flow. Both are right, but only the first proves the people.

## Buffett: calculate owner earnings first, then talk multiples

Buffett's approach to a cyclical stock is simple: swap peak profit for normalised profit, then subtract maintenance capex. SK hynix's 2026 capex is KRW 38–40 trillion, most of it expansionary, but depreciation lands concentrated in 2028–2029 — the Yongin Phase 1 and M15X fabs built this cycle carry 5-year depreciation schedules, effectively pushing the cost into the next downturn. Peak profit and peak depreciation do not fall in the same year — this is where cyclical financials most easily deceive.

His read on the moat would be milder than Duan Yongping's: the HBM qualification barrier is real — a customer switching suppliers must redo months of joint testing — not brand premium, but genuine switching cost. The problem is that the rent from this barrier must be renegotiated every year — once all three players passed HBM4 qualification on 8 June, the rent SK hynix can command shrank by a notch.

Conclusion: a good company, not a good business. 17x TTM for an industry whose profit may halve offers no margin of safety. He would not buy, but would put the name on his list.

## Munger: invert the question — what would make it fall by another half?

Munger did not estimate target prices; he wrote death lists. Only three paths could halve SK hynix again from 1.816 million:

- General-purpose DRAM contract prices turn down in 2027 as CXMT's 320,000 wafers/month of capacity and bit growth of more than 20% at both Samsung and Micron close the gap;

- Samsung's HBM4 share jumps from 28% to more than 35% and the HBM premium falls from 3x to 1.5x, leaving the 71.5% operating margin without support;

- AI capex peaks — the six AI giants issued USD 244 billion of debt this year versus just USD 108 billion in all of last year, and once leveraged financing stops, order cuts follow immediately.

If any one of the three hits, 2027 operating profit falls from KRW 180 trillion to below KRW 100 trillion and a market cap of KRW 600 trillion would be reasonable. If none hits, the current price is clearly undervalued. This is a probability question, not a valuation question, and the market has now priced part of all three at once — hence the 38% de-rating.

## Li Lu: Structural demand holds, structural profit does not

Li Lu's frame is the ten-year view: is AI memory demand a step-change? It is. Model parameters, context length and inference concurrency all lift memory content per unit of compute simultaneously, and a single Vera Rubin rack carries 1.5–2x the HBM capacity of the prior generation; on the supply side, a wafer fab takes 3 years and a cleanroom 18 months. That mismatch is structural, not an inventory cycle.

His immediate caveat: structural demand does not guarantee structural profit. Panels, solar and LEDs all saw demand grow tenfold while profits went to zero. Shareholder returns are set not by the demand curve but by supply discipline — and discipline breaks down most easily when money is easiest to make. Industry capex is +40% YoY in 2026, and JPMorgan expects a further +74% to USD 144 billion in 2027, evidence that discipline is loosening.

The one piece of "structural" evidence he would watch is long-term agreement coverage: if half of 2027 capacity is locked into multi-year contracts with price floors and ceilings, the business has moved one step from a cyclical stock toward a contract stock. There should be clarity on this point on July 29.

## Odds assessment: expected value -9%, not on the buy side

Weighting the three scenarios at 30%/45%/25% gives 0.3 × (-52%) + 0.45 × (-9%) + 0.25 × (+43%) = -8.9%. In other words, buying at the current price carries an expected loss of 9% after one year.

That is unsurprising for a cyclical stock: on the way down, EPS and the multiple compress together (Davis double-kill); on the way up, both expand together. The real opportunity appears when "the pessimistic scenario is fully priced in" — that is, below 1.4 million — at which point the pessimistic scenario is only -37%, the neutral case becomes +18%, and expected value finally turns positive.

So the four partners' consensus boils down to one sentence: now is a time to wait, not to buy. There are two things to wait for: the July 29 numbers, and the first hard evidence on 2027 supply.

## The shared blind spot of four people

All four analyses rest on one assumption: 2027 operating profit comes in at either 180 trillion or 110 trillion, with no more extreme outcome. That assumption itself may be wrong.

Upside extreme: if HBM4E remains exclusive in 2027 and commodity DRAM stays in shortage as ChangXin's capacity ramp disappoints, operating profit could reach 280 trillion, implying a target price above 2.9 million. Downside extreme: if AI capex reverses abruptly in 2027 (not a slowdown, but order cuts), operating profit could be 60 trillion, implying a market cap of 400 trillion and a share price of 560,000.

None of the four models captures these two tails. The reason: the tail scenarios carry too low a probability, and including them would only distort the expected value. But holders must know where the tails lie—that is the point of position sizing, and valuation cannot solve it.
