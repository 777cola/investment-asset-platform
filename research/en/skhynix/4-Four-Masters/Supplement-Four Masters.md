> Methodology: target market cap = 2027 operating profit × multiple + net cash, divided by 711 million shares. Probabilities are subjective, benchmarked to the drawdowns of the three memory cycles from 2015 to 2025.

## Duan Yongping: This is a spread-trading craft, not a business

When Duan Yongping looks at a manufacturer, his first question is: will a price increase lose customers? If it will, it is a spread business; if it won't, it is a good business. SK hynix raising commodity DRAM prices by 60% would lose customers — customers simply have no choice, not no desire to switch. Only the small HBM slice comes close to a good business: Nvidia is willing to sign multi-year long-term agreements and pay 3x the price to secure supply two years ahead.

So he would not deny the company's strength, but he would deny its predictability. Q1 2026 revenue rose 60% quarter on quarter with flat shipments — all the profit came from price. The cycle always takes price gains back; the only question is which month. His conclusion would most likely be "wait until it falls to the point where nobody wants it", rather than calling it cheap after a 38% drop — a 38% drop only shows how much it had risen before.

He would add one point about management: in 2023, when the whole industry cut capex, it did not cut HBM — that is doing one's duty; in 2026, raising USD 26.5 billion at the peak while approving RMB 4 trillion of capex — that is going with the flow. Both are right, but only the first proves the people.

## Buffett: calculate owner earnings first, then talk multiples

When Buffett encounters a cyclical stock, he does one simple thing: swap peak profit for normalized profit, then subtract maintenance capital expenditure. SK hynix's 2026 capex is KRW 38–40 trillion, most of it expansionary, but depreciation will hit concentrated in 2028–2029 — the Yongin Phase 1 and M15X fabs built this cycle carry 5-year depreciation schedules, effectively pushing the cost into the next downturn. Peak profit and peak depreciation do not land in the same year — this is where cyclical-stock financials most easily deceive.

His view of the moat would be milder than Duan Yongping's: the HBM qualification barrier is real — a customer switching suppliers must redo months of joint testing — this is not brand premium, but it is genuine switching cost. The problem is that the rent from this barrier must be renegotiated every year — after all three players passed HBM4 qualification on 8 June, the rent SK hynix can command shrank by a notch.

Conclusion: a good company, not a good business. 17x TTM for an industry whose profit may halve offers no margin of safety. He would not buy, but would put the name on his list.

## Munger: invert the question — what would make it fall by another half?

Munger did not estimate target prices; he wrote death lists. Only three paths could halve SK hynix again from 1.816 million:

- General-purpose DRAM contract prices turn down in 2027, as CXMT's 320,000 wafers/month of capacity plus bit growth of more than 20% each at Samsung and Micron close the gap;

- Samsung's HBM4 share jumps from 28% to more than 35% and the HBM premium falls from 3x to 1.5x, leaving the 71.5% operating margin without support;

- AI capex peaks — the six AI giants issued USD 244 billion of debt this year versus just USD 108 billion in all of last year, and once leveraged financing stops, order cuts are immediate.

If one of the three hits, 2027 operating profit falls from KRW 180 trillion to below KRW 100 trillion, and a market cap of KRW 600 trillion would be reasonable. Conversely, if none hits, the current price is clearly undervalued. This is a probability question, not a valuation question, and the market has now priced part of all three at once — that is why the stock is down 38%.

## Li Lu: Structural demand holds, structural profit does not

What Li Lu cares about is the ten-year dimension: whether AI demand for memory is a step-change. It is. Model parameters, context length and inference concurrency all push up memory configuration per unit of compute at the same time; a single Vera Rubin rack carries 1.5–2x the HBM capacity of the previous generation, while on the supply side a wafer fab takes 3 years and a cleanroom 18 months. This mismatch is structural, not a inventory cycle.

But he would immediately add: structural demand does not guarantee structural profit. Panels, solar and LEDs all went through tenfold demand growth with profits going to zero. What determines shareholder returns is never the demand curve but supply discipline, and supply discipline breaks down most easily when money is easiest to make — industry capex is +40% year on year in 2026, and JPMorgan expects a further +74% to USD 144 billion in 2027, which is evidence that discipline is loosening.

The only "structural" evidence he would watch is long-term agreement coverage: if half of 2027 capacity is locked into multi-year contracts with price floors and ceilings, then this business has moved one step from a cyclical stock toward a contract stock. There should be clarity on this point on July 29.

## Odds assessment: expected value -9%, not on the buy side

Weighting the three scenarios at 30%/45%/25%: 0.3 × (-52%) + 0.45 × (-9%) + 0.25 × (+43%) = -8.9%. In other words, buying at the current price carries a mathematical expectation of a 9% loss after one year.

This result is unsurprising for a cyclical stock: on the way down, EPS and the multiple contract together (Davis double-kill); on the way up, both expand together. The real opportunity appears when "the pessimistic scenario is fully priced in" — that is, below 1.4 million — at which point the pessimistic scenario is only -37%, the neutral case becomes +18%, and expected value finally turns positive.

So the four partners' consensus is really one sentence: now is a time to wait, not to buy. There are two things to wait for: the July 29 numbers, and the first hard evidence on 2027 supply.

## The shared blind spot of four people

All four analyses rest on one assumption: 2027 operating profit will be either 180 trillion or 110 trillion, with no more extreme outcome. But that assumption itself may be wrong.

Upside extreme: if HBM4E remains exclusive in 2027 and commodity DRAM stays in shortage because ChangXin's capacity ramp disappoints, operating profit could reach 280 trillion, implying a target price above 2.9 million. Downside extreme: if AI capex reverses abruptly in 2027 (not a slowdown, but order cuts), operating profit could be 60 trillion, implying a market cap of 400 trillion and a share price of 560,000.

None of the four models these two tails. The reason: the tail scenarios carry too low a probability, and including them would only distort the expected value. But holders must know where the tails lie—that is the point of position sizing, and valuation cannot solve it.
