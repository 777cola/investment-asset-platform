## Q: Is this a good business?

Answer: a good industry and a good company, but not a good business. A good industry means only three players plus one chaser remain in memory and the ten-way melee is over for good; a good company means SK hynix has built a genuine HBM lead that others cannot close within two years; not a good business means raising prices loses customers — when commodity DRAM prices rose 60%, customers did not run because they had no choice, not because they could not do without it.

The real dividing line is the small HBM slice: prices there tripled and customers still scrambled to sign long-term agreements, so that segment counts as a good business. But that slice is only 30-40% of revenue, and its contracts run on annual terms. Applying a ten-tenths valuation to four-tenths of business quality is why many people lose money.

## Q: Will it still be around in ten years?

Answer: yes, it survives. Memory does not disappear, Korea will not let it fail, and Nvidia needs a non-US HBM supplier. The three-player oligopoly can hold for at least another five years.

But "still here" and "making money" are two different things. Over the past decade the company has earned a cumulative KRW 105 trillion in net profit and invested KRW 130 trillion in capex — it did not earn enough to build its fabs. The essence of this business is "trade profit for capacity, trade capacity for the next round of pricing."

So in ten years it will certainly still be here, but its operating margin in ten years could be 20% or 60%, depending on how the 2027–2030 capacity expansion round plays out. Anyone buying it must accept one thing: you are not buying "SK hynix ten years from now," you are buying "the remainder of this cycle."

## Q: Can you understand it?

Answer: The business is understandable; the inflection point is not. In three sentences: build fabs, make chips, sell at contract prices. Only three variables matter — inventory, capex and contract price — and all three can be checked monthly without insider information.

The inflection point is not understandable. The CEO said on July 10 that 2027 will be the tightest year in history; the market knocked the stock down 15% on July 13. Both sides have a point: one is talking about order bookings, the other about expectations. Duan Yongping's approach: if you cannot understand it, do not price it in. What can be calculated is a profit of 230 trillion in 2026; what cannot is whether 2027 profit will be 180 or 110.

## Q: At what price to buy?

Answer: below KRW 1.65 million — 6x 2027 operating profit plus net cash, with a 10% margin of safety. The stock is now KRW 1.816 million, 9% more expensive.

Why not wait for KRW 1.4 million? Because good companies are rarely "cheap enough," and KRW 1.4 million may never come. Scaling in makes sense: buy a third at KRW 1.65 million, add to half at KRW 1.4 million; if it falls further, the thesis is wrong, not the price.

One more point: if the July 29 earnings show operating profit of KRW 65 trillion and management maintains its tight-supply stance for 2027, then KRW 1.75 million is also acceptable — because the assumptions have been validated, the price can be slightly higher. That is the sequence: get it right first, then buy cheap.

## Q: What is the biggest fear?

Answer: The biggest mistake is treating "it has fallen a lot" as "it is cheap." A 37.8% decline from 2.919 million to 1.816 million feels like a lot, but on 2027 neutral profit the stock still trades at 7x, and our neutral target price sits 9% below the current price. A 38% drop only shows the prior rally was too big; it does not show the stock is cheap now.

The second mistake is "this time is different." AI demand is real, the HBM barrier is real, long-term agreement lock-in is real — three reals combined still do not overturn the thirty-year rule that "memory is a cyclical industry." Every historical top had a "this time is different" story: data centers in 2018, work-from-home in 2022, AI in 2026.

## Q: What if I already hold it?

Answer: Hold, do not add; add only on a break below 1.4 million. The sell trigger is not price but the thesis: DRAM contract prices falling QoQ for two consecutive quarters, or Samsung's HBM share exceeding 35%. If either signal appears, exit regardless of profit or loss.

If your cost is above 2.5 million, ask yourself one question: why did you buy it? If the reason was "long-term AI memory shortage," that reason has not yet been falsified, so you can hold; if the reason was "it's going up," that reason has already disappeared.

"Benfen" here means: do not forecast what you cannot forecast, and do not pay for risks you do not want to bear. At 1.816 million, you are paying for "no crash in 2027," and that is not a bill optimists should pay.
