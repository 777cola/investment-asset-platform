Core thesis

A "high-quality, high-volatility, high-beta" stock. It is an offensive position in the portfolio, not ballast: correlation with the AI compute chain is close to 1, and its diversification benefit against any defensive asset is poor.

Current recommended position 0%; build 1/3 at a trigger price of KRW 1.65 million; below KRW 1.40 million, add to 1/2. The cap for a single memory stock is 100% of the original target position for that name and 10% of portfolio equity.

Three price-level disciplines

First, 1.65 million is where you can start buying, not where you should be fully invested. That is 6x 2027 operating profit and leaves only a 10% margin of safety—not enough for a cyclical, given EPS halves in a downturn.

Second, 1.40 million is where you can build a heavy position. That is 5x 2027, and the bear case (110 trillion operating profit × 4.6x + net cash) works out to 880,000, at which point the bear case is only -37% against +18% base, and expected value turns positive.

Third, set falsification-based stops, not mechanical ones. Price stops on cyclicals are the easiest way to get shaken out—in the 2022 cycle the stock fell 60% from its high and staged three rebounds of more than 20% along the way. Tie stops to the thesis: contract prices rolling over and share loss are more reliable signals than price.

Correlation: this is the beta of the AI trade, not a diversification tool

SK hynix's daily correlation with Nvidia, AMD, TSMC and Micron exceeds 0.7, and stands at 0.5–0.6 with A-share memory names (GigaDevice, Longsys). It cannot diversify AI position risk; it is part of the AI position.

What genuinely diversifies: utilities with stable cash flow, consumer staples, and assets unrelated to AI capex (e.g. pharma, some financials). If the portfolio already holds more than 20% in the AI compute chain (GPUs, optical modules, memory, servers), adding SK hynix is putting all eggs in one basket.

July 13 was a good example: SK hynix -15.4%, Samsung -11%, A-share memory names limit-down, US memory stocks down 6%–9% premarket. Same supply chain, no diversification.

Rebalancing discipline: quarterly reviews, not monthly

Memory stocks move week to week, so monthly rebalancing gets dragged around by noise. Do three things each quarter: first, check the status of H1 and H2 assumptions (contract prices, share); second, recalculate 2027 operating profit and target price; third, if a position has risen above 1.5x its target weight, trim it back to target.

One more practical suggestion: if the only way to gain exposure is through an ETF (such as the CSOP 2x Long SK hynix), don't use it. Leveraged ETFs suffer heavy decay in choppy markets; the 30% single-day drop on 13 July is one example. If you buy, buy the common stock or the ADR.

If the portfolio already holds AI-chain exposure

If the portfolio already holds Nvidia, TSMC and optical modules, SK hynix adds almost no marginal diversification. The right move is not to "add another memory name" but to size total exposure across GPU, foundry, optical modules, memory and servers — five holdings that together constitute a single risk factor: AI capex.

Rule of thumb: no single risk factor should exceed 30% of equity holdings. If exposure is already close to that threshold, SK hynix should not be bought however cheap it looks — not because it is a poor company, but because buying it means doubling down on the same bet.

Conversely, if the portfolio holds no AI-chain names at all, SK hynix is the entry point offering "the best quality and the highest volatility." In that case, a one-third position below 1.65 million is meaningful.

Conclusion

Portfolio recommendation: target position 0%; build one-third at RMB 1.65 million and add to one-half at RMB 1.40 million; clear to zero unconditionally if the thesis is falsified. Cap single-name memory exposure at 10%–15% of equity, aggregated across Micron and Samsung. This is an offensive position, not a ballast holding—size it on the assumption of a possible 50% loss, not on the view that a 38% drawdown makes it cheap.
