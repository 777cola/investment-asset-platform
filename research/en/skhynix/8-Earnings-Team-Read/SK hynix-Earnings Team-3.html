Core view

Munger does not look at profit; he looks at commitments. Profit is the past; capex is the future — commitments of RMB 3.8–4.0 trillion mean a wave of new capacity will be waiting to be sold in 2027–2028.

Profit is the result; capacity expansion is the cause.

The best-looking item on the statements is operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion; the most dangerous is capex: KRW 30.2 trillion in 2025, KRW 38–40 trillion in 2026, and the Yongin Phase 1 budget raised to KRW 31 trillion. That money becomes capacity in 2027–2028, and once that capacity lands, price has to give way.

Munger's formulation: "the most profitable moment for any industry is when it begins to destroy itself." Memory has played out this pattern four times: 2000, 2008, 2018, 2022. Each time profits hit new highs, capex hit new highs, and two years later prices collapsed.

The signal hidden in inventory

Inventory appreciates during a price upcycle—carried at cost, its book value stays put as market prices rise. So inventory days this quarter look lower, but that is a price effect, not an efficiency gain. Conversely, once prices turn down, inventory instantly becomes a burden, and impairments hit profit for two consecutive quarters. That is how losses piled up in 2022–2023: not because goods could not be sold, but because selling them did not cover cost.

Receivables follow the same logic. Revenue up 60%, receivables rise with it, and payment terms have not stretched—meaning customers are still paying on schedule, a healthy signal for now. What to watch is the day payment terms start to stretch: that means customers are running short of cash.

One question: who is buying all this new capacity?

The world is adding that much new capacity in 2027 — who are the buyers? AI servers, phones, PCs. AI is the only certain growth, but the AI money is borrowed: the six giants issued USD 244 billion of debt this year. Phone and PC shipments have not grown for three years running.

So Munger would ask: if AI capex growth falls from 60% to 20% in 2027, who buys this new capacity? The answer is "sell it to the consumer market at lower prices," which means another downcycle. The filings do not show this risk, but the capex numbers write it in.

Conclusion

Munger-style conclusion: this earnings report is a "license for self-destruction." The better the profits, the more aggressive the capacity expansion, and the worse the next downturn. No denying the company is excellent, but excellence cannot change the cycle.
