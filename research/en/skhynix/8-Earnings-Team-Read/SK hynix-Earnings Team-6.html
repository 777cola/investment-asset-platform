Earns 37.6 trillion in a single quarter, stock down 38%: the two faces of the memory king — SK hynix

Core view

A company posts quarterly operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion and an operating margin of 71.5%, yet its share price falls 37.8% in five weeks. Both happened to SK hynix, and they are not contradictory.

I. First, look at that pretty face

On April 22, 2026, SK hynix delivered its strongest quarterly results ever: revenue of KRW 52.58 trillion, up 198% year on year; operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion, up 405%; operating margin of 71.5%. Profit in a single quarter was 1.6 times that of all of 2024.

The breakdown is more interesting: DRAM shipments were flat quarter on quarter, yet revenue rose 60% sequentially. The extra KRW 20 trillion came entirely from price increases — DRAM average selling prices rose more than 60% sequentially, NAND more than 70%. This is not one company selling more chips; it is the entire industry repricing memory.

There is only one reason: AI. An AI server needs 3 to 5 times the memory of an ordinary server, and only three companies can make HBM. SK hynix holds 54% share among those three, 12-layer HBM4 entered mass production in February this year with yields on par with the previous generation. Orders from Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom are booked into next year.

II. Now look at that ugly face again

On June 22, SK hynix's market cap reached KRW 2,072 trillion, briefly overtaking Samsung Electronics to become the most valuable company in Korea. That day it closed at KRW 2.919 million. Five weeks later, on July 27, it closed at KRW 1.816 million, down 37.8%.

The trigger was a July 13 report from Korean brokerage KIS: it cut its Q2 operating profit forecast to KRW 60.4 trillion, below the consensus of KRW 65 trillion, on the grounds that SK hynix's high HBM mix and long-term contract price locks meant it captured less of the commodity DRAM price increase than peers. The stock fell 15.4% that day, its largest single-day drop since listing, and the KOSPI fell 8.95%.

But one brokerage report cannot produce a 38% decline. The real selling pressure lies further out: 2026 industry capex is +40% year on year, with a further +74% expected in 2027; Samsung's HBM4 has passed certification and its share has risen from 20% to 28%; ChangXin's commodity DRAM capacity reaches 350,000 wafers/month by year-end. The market is selling 2027, not Q2.

III. What does the 71.5% figure mean?

The historical pattern in memory: the higher the operating margin, the closer the peak. The 2018 cycle peaked at a 55% margin, then lost 7.7 trillion over the following year; this cycle has run straight to 71.5%, as the HBM premium compounded a shortage of commodity DRAM.

A 79% gross margin has no room to rise—wafer cost is rigid, and a further 10% price increase would lift gross margin only to 81%. But there is plenty of downside: a 30% price decline would halve operating profit. This is the most counterintuitive thing about cyclicals: the best financials mark the worst share price.

A TTM P/E of 17.2x looks like a utility. But the denominator is peak-cycle profit. On 2027 mid-cycle profit of 180 trillion, the current price implies 7x operating profit—not expensive, and by no means cheap.

IV. Three numbers to watch on July 29

First, whether Q2 operating profit is KRW 60 trillion or KRW 65 trillion. A KRW 5 trillion gap equals 4% of market cap.

Second, the QoQ increase in blended DRAM ASP. Brokers say 30%, industry contract prices say 58%–63% — a 28 percentage point spread. The answer is in the earnings report.

Third, what management says about 2027. CEO Kwak Noh-jung said on July 10 that "2027 will be the tightest supply year in history, and demand may still exceed supply after 2030." If that stance holds, the selloff is a golden pit; if it shifts to "supply and demand trending toward balance," the numbers need reworking.

V. Conclusion: a good company at a bad time

SK hynix's quality is not in doubt: No. 1 in HBM share, net cash, positive free cash flow, and management that dared to invest in HBM in 2023 when the industry was at its worst. Its problem was never whether it is good, but at what price to buy.

The current price of KRW 1.816 million corresponds to 7x 2027 neutral earnings, leaving only a 10% margin of safety. To actually buy, two conditions: second-quarter operating profit of no less than KRW 60 trillion and management maintaining its tight-supply stance; and the price back below KRW 1.65 million.

Until then, the best move is to wait. Waiting two days costs nothing; betting on two days could lose 38%.
