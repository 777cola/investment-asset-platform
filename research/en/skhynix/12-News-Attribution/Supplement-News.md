## Valuation looks stretched — all it needs is a spark

On June 22, SK hynix's market cap reached KRW 2,072 trillion, overtaking Samsung as the largest stock in Korea. At 9x 2026 expected profit, the consensus at the time was already pricing in a "long-term HBM monopoly" — at that level, no bad news was needed, just a match.

The match was the KIS report, but the gunpowder was self-stacked: the stock had risen more than 600% year-to-date (April reports cited "nearly 90% YTD," and by June it had multiplied several times over), Korean retail and leveraged ETFs were deeply involved, and the CSOP 2x long SK hynix ETF fell more than 30% in a single day on July 13. Rising too fast + leverage too concentrated = any bad news gets amplified.

## Second-layer attribution: KIS flags a structural issue, not a numbers issue

KIS cuts Q2 operating profit to KRW 60.4 trillion, 7% below consensus. The driver is not weak demand but "a high HBM mix plus long-term contracts locking in prices, which leaves less of the price-hike windfall." That points to a real issue: within SK hynix's revenue structure, HBM, the most profitable segment, is precisely the one with the least price elasticity.

Generic DRAM contract prices rose 58%–63% in Q2 and NAND 70%–75%, while SK hynix's blended ASP rose only 30%. It locked in prices on its most profitable category and carries less exposure to the most speculative one. In an upcycle that is a drawback; in a downcycle it is an advantage—in July the market saw only the drawback.

## Third layer of attribution: Korean deleveraging and the AI debt narrative

KOSPI fell 8.95% on July 13, and SK hynix was not the whole story. Korean regulators tightened rules on leveraged products, while repeated swings in Fed rate-cut expectations triggered a concentrated unwinding of leveraged positions. The same day, the market began trading the narrative that "AI infrastructure runs on debt": the six AI giants issued USD 244 billion of bonds this year, against just USD 108 billion in all of last year.

SK hynix is the most sensitive name in this narrative, with 100% of demand tied to AI capex, so any tightening in financing is followed immediately by order cuts. That logic did not hold in July, when cloud capex was still being revised up, but the market priced it in early.

## Which are noise: four out of five

Noise one: the 15.4% down day. That reflects the speed of leverage unwinding, not a change in fundamentals. KIS cut its forecast by only 9%–11% while the stock fell 15%; the overshoot came from forced liquidation.

Noise two: +21% on the ADR's first trading day. This reflects the issuance discount and passive allocation, not a change in company value; what matters is the 2.5% dilution, which will take a year to show up in EPS.

Noise three: the +4.86% rebound on July 23. A sector β bounce, with no new information.

Noise four: the CEO's remarks on July 10. Directional statements carry no information ahead of earnings; the real evidence is the July 29 numbers and the 2027 long-term agreement terms.

The only real signal is the second item: Samsung's HBM4 share rising from 20% to 28%, and all three suppliers passing certification on June 8. The "sole supplier" pricing logic is over, and the market's valuation anchor for SK hynix must shift one notch from "core AI supplier" toward "memory leader."

## Whether a news item is a signal depends on whether it changes the assumptions

This report applies a simple test: if a piece of news cannot change any of the five assumptions in m13, it is noise.

KIS's downgrade changes the "Q2 profit number" but not "whether supply will be tight in 2027," so it is short-term noise only. The structural issue it exposes, however—"HBM long-term agreement price locking"—does change H3 (long-term agreement coverage ratio), so half of it is signal.

Samsung's share rising from 20% to 28% directly changes H2 (holding the 50% share), making it pure signal. The CEO's comment that "2027 will be the tightest" merely repeats known guidance and changes no assumption, so it is noise. Filtered by this standard, only two items over the past three months truly require a response.
