## Chapter 1 Company Overview

SK hynix (000660.KS) is the world's second-largest DRAM maker and largest HBM supplier. Headquartered in Icheon, South Korea, it is listed on KOSPI, with ADRs listed on Nasdaq on July 10, 2026 (ticker SKHY). Total shares outstanding are about 711 million, market cap KRW 1,291 trillion (about USD 920 billion), making it the world's most valuable memory company.

Capacity footprint: Icheon M10/M14 (DRAM), Cheongju M15/M15X/M11 (DRAM and HBM packaging), Wuxi C2/C2F (DRAM, serving the China market), Dalian (NAND, Solidigm), Yongin (first phase starting production in 2027). Product lines: DRAM (DDR5, LPDDR5X, GDDR7, HBM3E/HBM4), NAND (321-layer 4D NAND, enterprise SSD), and non-memory businesses such as CIS (a very small share).

Revenue mix (rough estimate for 2026Q1): DRAM about 80%, NAND about 20%; HBM accounts for around 40% of DRAM. Customers include Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta, Apple and Samsung Mobile.

## Chapter 2 Industry Analysis

DRAM has settled into a "three plus one challenger" structure: Samsung c.38%, SK hynix c.30%, Micron c.22% and ChangXin c.8% (2026Q1 revenue basis). NAND has five major players, with concentration one notch lower. Only three can make HBM, the tightest link in the entire chain.

In 2026 the industry sits mid-way through its third AI-driven super-cycle: general DRAM contract prices rose 100% in the first quarter (Samsung repricing) and a further 58%–63% in the second quarter; NAND rose 70%–75% in the second quarter; H2 SLC NAND is expected to rise 120%–170% versus the first half. Industry capex is +40% YoY in 2026, and JPMorgan expects a further +74% to USD 144 billion in 2027, yet actual bit supply grows only about 13%.

On the demand side, AI server memory capacity is 3–5x that of ordinary servers, and HBM usage is tied to GPU shipments. UBS expects global memory industry revenue of USD 992 billion in 2026 and USD 1.76 trillion in 2027.

## Chapter 3 Business model

Heavy-asset manufacturing with exogenous pricing. An advanced DRAM fab costs KRW 8–10 trillion, takes 18–24 months to build and depreciates over 5 years. Materials and manufacturing account for 20% of the cost structure and depreciation and expenses around 10%, so every 10% rise in price lifts operating profit by 15%–20%, while every 10% fall cuts it by more than 20%.

HBM changes one segment of this: standard products become custom parts, customers engage at the product-definition stage, qualification cycles run in quarters and prices are locked in multi-year long-term agreements. Unit prices in this segment run about 3x those of commodity DRAM and are the main source of the 71.5% operating margin.

The flip side of custom parts is asset specificity: HBM lines are built for specific GPUs and cannot be repurposed if the customer switches designs. That explains why the company would rather earn less on commodity DRAM and prioritise capacity for HBM and high-density server modules.

## Chapter 4 Financial Analysis

2026Q1: revenue KRW 52.58 trillion (+198% YoY, +60% QoQ), operating profit KRW 37.61 trillion (+405%), operating margin 71.5%, gross margin 79%, net profit KRW 40.35 trillion and free cash flow KRW 18.67 trillion — all four are all-time records.

Growth came almost entirely from price: DRAM shipments were flat QoQ with ASP up more than 60% QoQ, while NAND shipments fell 10% QoQ with ASP up more than 70%. The opex ratio was just 7.5%, implying extremely high operating leverage. Net profit exceeded operating profit by KRW 2.7 trillion, mainly on non-operating items such as the Kioxia equity revaluation.

Balance sheet: the company turned net cash in 2025, with net cash of about KRW 58 trillion in 2026Q1 (rough calculation) and about KRW 95 trillion after the USD 26.5 billion ADR offering in July. This is the biggest difference from the 2018 cycle — the prior peak was net debt. The risk sits in 2028: once Yongin Phase 1 and M15X ramp, annual depreciation will rise by KRW 5–8 trillion (rough calculation).

## Chapter 5 Management

CEO Kwak Noh-jung took office in 2022 after years overseeing DRAM technology and production lines. Three decisions have defined his tenure: holding HBM investment through the industry's worst period in 2023, a bet that paid off; pulling the Yongin Phase 1 cleanroom forward from May 2027 to February 2026, evidence of strong execution; and raising USD 26.5 billion via an ADR issuance in July 2026 at a share-price high, smart but dilutive.

On shareholder returns, the board approved a KRW 12.2 trillion buyback and cancellation in February 2026 (2.1% of share capital), alongside a 25% dividend increase. Cancellation-style buybacks are rare among Korean chaebol-affiliated companies and are a substantive signal of governance improvement.

Governance risk: Chairman Chey Tae-won's divorce asset-division case has been remanded for retrial several times since 2024, with amounts large enough to affect SK Group's control over SK Square (20.5% stake). A low-probability, high-impact event, isolated from the listed company's operations but not to be ignored.

## Chapter 6 Growth Drivers

Three drivers, ranked by certainty. First, HBM bit demand: AI server shipment growth plus higher HBM content per unit (the Vera Rubin rack carries 1.5–2x the HBM capacity of the prior generation), with HBM demand outpacing supply in 2026–2027. Second, mix upgrade: over half of 1c DRAM capacity converted within the year and 321-layer NAND becoming the mainstay, lowering unit costs. Third, capacity coming online: M15X reaches full production by end-2026 and Yongin Phase 1 starts up in February 2027, driving roughly 30% bit growth in 2027.

All three drivers land at once in 2027 — which is precisely why "supply loosens in 2027": growth and oversupply are two sides of the same coin.

## Chapter 7 Competitive Landscape

HBM market shares: SK hynix 54%, Samsung 28%, Micron about 18% (Counterpoint 2026 estimate). SK hynix's edge rests on its earlier 12-layer mass-production ramp, MR-MUF packaging yield and the depth of its joint development with Nvidia; its constraints are capacity scale (Samsung's DRAM capacity is 1.3 times its own) and group resources.

On June 8, 2026, Nvidia confirmed all three had passed HBM4 certification, ending exclusive supply. Samsung's share rose from 20% in 2025 to 28%, the largest marginal change. In commodity DRAM, CXMT is testing the price floor with an 8% share and 48% bit growth.

Our view: SK hynix can hold above 50% share through 2028, but the HBM premium will fall from 3x to 2x and operating margin will return to 45%–50%.

## Chapter 8 Risk factors

① Prices turn down: DRAM contract prices fall QoQ for a second straight quarter; operating profit could halve (30% probability, impact of -40% or more). ② Share loss: Samsung's HBM share exceeds 35% and the premium narrows (25% probability). ③ AI capex cut: cloud providers cut 2027 guidance by more than 15% (20% probability). ④ Depreciation step-up: an extra 5-8 trillion per year from 2028 (certain to happen, 3-5pct impact on operating margin). ⑤ Geopolitics: the US restricts equipment upgrades at plants in China (20% probability, limited impact). ⑥ Governance: change in group control (5% probability). ⑦ Leverage and sentiment: liquidation of Korean leveraged products amplifies volatility (already happened, -15.4% in a single day on July 13).

## Chapter 10 Valuation

P/E (TTM) is not the right metric: the denominator is peak-cycle profit, so 17.2x tells you nothing. Nor is a DCF: pricing beyond 2028 cannot be forecast. Use 2027 operating profit × a mid-cycle multiple, plus net cash.

Neutral: 180 trillion × 6x + 95 trillion = 1,175 trillion market cap = KRW 1.65 million/share, 9% downside to the current price. Bear: 110 × 4.6 + 120 = 626 trillion = KRW 880,000 (-52%). Bull: 250 × 7 + 100 = 1,850 trillion = KRW 2.6 million (+43%). Probability-weighted expected return: -9%.

Cross-check: the current price implies 5.4x 2026E net profit of 240 trillion and 13x owner earnings (roughly 100 trillion). That spread is the gap between cycle peak and normalized earnings.

## Chapter 11 Investment Recommendations

Rating "Under Review", target price KRW 1.65 million (-9%). Three reasons: first-class company quality (54% HBM share, net cash, management execution), an elevated cycle position (71.5% operating margin is a historical extreme) and poor timing (July 29 earnings; 7% gap between KIS and consensus unresolved).

Entry conditions: build a 1/3 position below KRW 1.65 million (6x 2027), add to 1/2 below KRW 1.40 million; or, if July 29 operating profit ≥ KRW 65 trillion and management maintains tight-supply guidance for 2027, build 1/3 below KRW 1.75 million. Falsification conditions: DRAM contract prices fall quarter-on-quarter for two consecutive quarters, or Samsung HBM share exceeds 35%; either triggers a full exit.
