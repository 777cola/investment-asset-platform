## Hypothetical ranking: H1 decides everything

The five assumptions are not parallel. H1 — supply still tight in 2027 — determines whether 2027 operating profit is 180 trillion or 110 trillion, and whether the stock trades at 6x or 4x. The other four are supports or modifiers for H1.

The evidence for H1 rests on "capex does not equal capacity": equipment lead times of 12-18 months and cleanroom build times of 18 months mean money spent in 2026 only creates supply in the second half of 2027. JPMorgan estimates 2027 bit supply growth of only 13%. The counter-evidence is equally hard: Samsung's HBM4 capacity doubles in 2027, ChangXin reaches 420,000 wafers/month, and Yongin Phase 1 comes online in February 2027. All three point to the second half of 2027, so H1's time window is the first half only.

## Falsification conditions: only two, both requiring hard evidence

Falsification one: DRAM contract prices fall QoQ for two consecutive quarters. Note this is the contract price, not the spot price — contract prices underpin company revenue. Historically, once this signal appears, a further 30%–40% decline follows, as EPS and multiples compress simultaneously. In the 2022 cycle, contract prices turned down in 2022Q3 while the stock peaked in 2022Q1, making this a lagging confirmation rather than an early warning.

Falsification two: Samsung's disclosed HBM share exceeds 35%. This is the killer of the valuation centre. A move from 28% to 35% would shift HBM from a duopoly to two players splitting the market evenly, taking the 3x premium below 2x and returning operating margin to 45%–50%. Data sources: quarterly share data from TrendForce and Counterpoint, and Nvidia's supplier allocation.

No signal other than these two counts as falsification. A 20% stock decline, brokerages cutting target prices, tighter Korean regulation — these are volatility, not falsification.

## Watch calendar: what to look for over the next six months

July 29: Q2 results. Watch three numbers: operating profit (60 or 65), DRAM blended ASP QoQ and management's 2027 guidance. This one sets the near-term direction.

September: Q3 contract price negotiations. If commodity DRAM price increases fall below 20%, H1 starts to crack.

October: Q3 results plus preliminary 2027 capex guidance. This is the single most important number of the year — if 2027 guidance exceeds KRW 50 trillion, capacity expansion is accelerating and H1 is at risk.

January–February 2027: Yongin Phase 1 cleanroom activation and equipment move-in. The first physical evidence of supply landing.

April 2027: Full-year 2026 results plus 2027 HBM4E pricing. Whether the HBM premium holds at 2.5x hinges on this negotiation.

## Failure scenarios for the thesis: when the framework itself breaks down

This framework rests on the premise that memory is a cyclical industry. Should any of the three scenarios below materialise, the framework itself must be rewritten.

First, long-term agreement coverage exceeds 70% and carries price floors and ceilings. Price would then be set by contract, the cycle smoothed out, and the valuation framework lifted from 6x to 10–12x, rendering all falsification conditions based on contract prices invalid.

Second, HBM accounts for more than 70% of DRAM revenue and the three players establish a stable technology gap (for example, SK hynix leading by a generation over the long term). It would then resemble TSMC more than a memory maker.

Third, AI demand suffers a cliff-edge decline (not a slowdown), leaving industry-wide capacity with nowhere to go. The cycle would then clear more violently than history, and the valuation method would have to revert to replacement cost.

None of the three has happened yet, but use them to check whether your assumptions have expired.
