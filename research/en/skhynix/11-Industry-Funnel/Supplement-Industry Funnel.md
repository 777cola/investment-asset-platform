## Coarse screen: four elimination criteria

First, business purity. Samsung Electronics' Q2 operating profit hit a record KRW 89.4 trillion, but that spans phones, displays, home appliances and foundry, with memory just one piece. Buying Samsung is buying a Korean Berkshire Hathaway, and the memory elasticity is diluted — it lags SK hynix on the way up and on the way down.

Second, HBM exposure. Micron holds about 18% of HBM share and is Nvidia's third supplier; Kioxia and YMTC have no HBM exposure at all, only NAND. Excess profit this cycle sits almost entirely in HBM and high-end DRAM; NAND is merely riding along.

Third, investability. ChangXin Memory passed its STAR Market IPO review on 27 May, and YMTC is unlisted, so A-share investors can only buy concept stocks, not the issuers themselves.

Fourth, valuation calculability. Samsung does not disclose memory segment P&L separately, so normalized-profit valuation is impossible; SK hynix and Micron are the only two companies where "operating profit × multiple" works.

## Final pick: SK hynix vs Micron — why SK hynix

Both are pure-play memory makers, both have passed HBM4 certification and both run gross margins around 80%. The differences come down to three things. First, share: SK hynix at 54% and Micron at 18%, so SK hynix captures three times the upside when HBM prices rise. Second, customers: SK hynix is tied to Nvidia (about 70% of HBM4 orders), while Micron is a second or third supplier with one notch less pricing power. Third, capacity timing: SK hynix's Yongin Phase 1 comes online in February 2027, while Micron's new US fab will not arrive until end-2027.

Micron's strengths run the other way: US domestic capacity (CHIPS Act subsidies) lowers geopolitical risk; a higher NAND mix means it benefits more when NAND prices rise; and its valuation has consistently been one notch cheaper than SK hynix's. If you want the one you can "sleep well" holding in this segment, it is Micron; if you want the one with the most upside elasticity, it is SK hynix.

## Why it, and why not buy now

Why this one: SK hynix is the purest, largest-share, highest-torque play in this AI memory cycle. For memory sector exposure it is the first choice, not the second.

Why not buy now: the market already knows. A KRW 1,291 trillion market cap equates to 7x 2027 neutral profit, against a neutral target price of KRW 1.65 million, 9% below the current price. The HBM premium, the share lead and AI demand are fully priced in; the only thing not priced is the option on tighter supply in 2027 — and that needs evidence.

So the end of the funnel is not "buy" but "put on the top pick list and wait for price" — the same conclusion across every module in this report collection.

## Reverse thinking: if you had to buy now, who would it be

For anyone unwilling to wait and wanting exposure to this theme now, the ranking is Micron > SK hynix > Samsung.

Micron ranks first not because it is the better company but because it is cheaper and safer: HBM share is only 18%, so narrowing HBM premiums hurt it less; it has US domestic capacity and low geopolitical risk; and its valuation has consistently sat a notch below SK hynix's. In a downturn, low share is an advantage.

SK hynix ranks second: it offers the most upside, but the current price already discounts that. Samsung ranks last: its business is too diversified, diluting memory upside, and the conglomerate governance discount is unlikely to close near term.

This ranking answers a different question from "who is the better company." The better company is SK hynix; the better stock at this price may be Micron.
