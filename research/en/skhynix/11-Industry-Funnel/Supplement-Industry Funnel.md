## Coarse screen: four elimination criteria

First, business purity. Samsung Electronics' Q2 operating profit hit a record KRW 89.4 trillion, but that spans phones, displays, home appliances and foundry, with memory just one piece. Buying Samsung is buying a Korean Berkshire Hathaway, and the memory elasticity is diluted — it lags SK hynix on the way up and on the way down.

Second, HBM exposure. Micron holds about 18% of HBM share and is Nvidia's third supplier; Kioxia and YMTC have no HBM exposure at all, only NAND. Excess profit this cycle sits almost entirely in HBM and high-end DRAM; NAND is merely riding along.

Third, investability. ChangXin Memory passed its STAR Market IPO review on 27 May, and YMTC is unlisted, so A-share investors can only buy concept stocks, not the issuers themselves.

Fourth, valuation calculability. Samsung does not disclose memory segment P&L separately, so normalized-profit valuation is impossible; SK hynix and Micron are the only two companies where "operating profit × multiple" works.

## Final pick: SK hynix vs Micron — why SK hynix

Both are pure-play memory makers, both have passed HBM4 certification, and both run gross margins around 80%. The differences come down to three things. First, share: SK hynix at 54% and Micron at 18%, so when HBM prices rise, SK hynix captures three times the upside. Second, customers: SK hynix is tied to Nvidia (about 70% of HBM4 orders), while Micron is a second or third supplier with one notch less pricing power. Third, capacity timing: SK hynix's Yongin Phase 1 comes online in February 2027, while Micron's new US fab will not arrive until end-2027.

Conversely, Micron's strengths: it has US domestic capacity (CHIPS Act subsidies), so geopolitical risk is lower; its higher NAND mix means it benefits more when NAND prices rise; and its valuation has consistently been one notch cheaper than SK hynix's. If you want the one you can "sleep well" holding in this segment, it is Micron; if you want the one with the most upside elasticity, it is SK hynix.

## Why it, and why not buy now

The answer to "why this one": SK hynix is the purest, largest-share, highest-torque play in this AI memory cycle. If you want to bet on the memory sector, it is the first choice, not the second.

The answer to "why not buy now": the market already knows. A market cap of KRW 1,291 trillion corresponds to 7x 2027 neutral profit; the neutral target price is KRW 1.65 million, 9% below the current price. The HBM premium, the share lead and AI demand are all fully priced in; the only thing not priced is the option on "tighter supply in 2027" — and that needs evidence.

So the end of the funnel is not "buy" but "put on the top pick list and wait for price." This is also the same conclusion across every module in this report collection.

## Reverse thinking: if you had to buy now, who would it be

Suppose someone rejects "wait" and wants to build a position in this theme now. Then the ranking is: Micron > SK hynix > Samsung.

Micron ranks first not because it is better, but because it is cheaper and safer: its HBM share is only 18%, so narrowing HBM premiums hurt it less; it has US domestic capacity and low geopolitical risk; and its valuation has consistently sat a notch below SK hynix's. In a downturn, low share is an advantage.

SK hynix ranks second: it has the most upside, but the current price already prices that in. Samsung ranks last: its business is too diversified, diluting memory upside, and the conglomerate governance discount will not disappear near term.

This ranking is a different question from "who is the better company." The better company is SK hynix; the better stock at this price may be Micron.
