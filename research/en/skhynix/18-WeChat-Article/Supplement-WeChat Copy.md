## Title and lead-in

Alternative headlines: ① "RMB 3.76 trillion profit in a single quarter, stock down 38%: the two faces of the memory king" ② "Profit at a record high, market cap down RMB 78 trillion: what happened to this company" ③ "The whole world is scrambling for its memory, yet its stock is crashing".

Lead: On July 27, 2026, SK hynix closed at KRW 1.816 million, down 37.8% from its high five weeks earlier. At the same time, it had just reported the strongest quarterly results in its history: operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion for the quarter, an operating margin of 71.5%. Both things happened at once, and they do not contradict each other. Understand this contradiction and you understand cyclical stocks.

## Main text 1: First, look at that pretty face

On April 22, SK hynix reported first-quarter 2026 results: revenue of KRW 52.58 trillion, up 198% year on year; operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion, up 405%; operating margin of 71.5%. The money made in a single quarter was 1.6 times its full-year 2024 total.

The breakdown is more interesting: DRAM shipments were flat quarter on quarter, yet revenue rose 60% over the same period. The extra KRW 20 trillion came entirely from price increases — average DRAM selling prices rose more than 60% and NAND more than 70%. It did not sell more chips; the entire industry is repricing memory.

There is only one reason: AI. A single AI server needs 3 to 5 times the memory of an ordinary server, and only three companies can make HBM. SK hynix holds a 54% share among those three, twelve-layer HBM4 entered mass production in February this year, and yields match the previous generation. Orders from Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom are booked into next year.

## Body 2: Look again at that ugly face

On June 22, SK hynix's market cap reached KRW 2,072 trillion, briefly overtaking Samsung Electronics to become the largest company in Korea by market value. It closed that day at KRW 2.919 million. Five weeks later, on July 27, it closed at KRW 1.816 million, down 37.8%.

The trigger was a July 13 report from Korean brokerage KIS: it cut its Q2 operating profit forecast to KRW 60.4 trillion, below the consensus of KRW 65 trillion. The reason: SK hynix has a high HBM mix and locked-in long-term contract prices, so it captured less of the commodity DRAM price increase than peers. The stock fell 15.4% that day, its largest single-day drop since listing, and the KOSPI fell 8.95%.

But a single brokerage report cannot produce a 38% decline. The real selling pressure lies further out: industry capex rises 40% year on year in 2026 and is expected to rise another 74% in 2027; Samsung's HBM4 has passed certification and its share has climbed from 20% to 28%; ChangXin's commodity DRAM capacity reaches 350,000 wafers per month by year-end. The market is selling 2027, not Q2.

## Body 3: What does the 71.5% figure mean?

The historical pattern in memory: the higher the operating margin, the closer the downturn. The 2018 peak margin was 55%, and a year later operating profit fell from 20.8 trillion to 2.7 trillion; this cycle it hit 71.5% outright, as the HBM premium compounded a shortage of commodity DRAM.

A 79% gross margin has no room left to rise—wafer cost is rigid, and a further 10% price increase would lift gross margin only to 81%. But there is plenty of downside: a 30% price decline halves operating profit. This is the most counterintuitive thing about cyclicals: share prices are worst when the financials look best.

A TTM P/E of 17.2x looks like a utility. But the denominator is peak-cycle profit. On 2027 mid-cycle profit of 180 trillion, the current price implies 7x operating profit—not expensive, and certainly not cheap.

## Main text 4: Three numbers to watch on July 29

First, whether Q2 operating profit is KRW 60 trillion or KRW 65 trillion. A KRW 5 trillion gap equals 4% of market cap.

Second, how much DRAM blended ASP rose quarter on quarter. Brokers say 30%; industry contract prices say 58% to 63% — a 28 percentage point spread. The answer is in the earnings report.

Third, what management says about 2027. CEO Kwak Noh-jung said on July 10 that "2027 will be the tightest supply year in history, and demand may still exceed supply after 2030." If that stance holds, the selloff is a golden pit; if it shifts to "supply and demand trending toward balance," the numbers need reworking.

## Body five: Conclusion — good company, bad timing

SK hynix's quality is not in doubt: No. 1 in HBM share, net cash, positive free cash flow, and management that dared to invest in HBM in 2023 when the industry was at its worst. Its problem was never whether it is good, but at what price to buy.

The current price of KRW 1.816 million corresponds to 7x 2027 neutral earnings, leaving only a 10% margin of safety. To actually buy, two conditions: second-quarter operating profit of no less than KRW 60 trillion with management maintaining its tight-supply stance; and the price back below KRW 1.65 million.

Until then, the best move is to wait. Waiting two days costs nothing; betting on two days could lose 38%.
