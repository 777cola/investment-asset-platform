Financial analysis (Buffett's perspective) — SK hynix

The profit is real, but owner earnings need a haircut
Research date 2026/07/27  ·  Price ₩1,816,000 (+3.24%)  ·  Market cap Approximately KRW 1,291 trillion (approximately USD 920 billion)  ·  PE (TTM, as of 2026Q1) Approximately 17.2x

Core view

2026Q1 net profit of RMB 40.35 trillion and free cash flow of RMB 18.67 trillion are impressive. But peak earnings need adjusting for two things: scheduled depreciation on expansionary capex, and non-operating equity revaluation gains.

Income statement: growth was entirely price-driven, with costs broadly flat

Revenue rose 60% QoQ on flat DRAM shipments and a 10% decline in NAND shipments, so the extra RMB 2 trillion of revenue came almost entirely from ASP. Costs tell a different story: depreciation, power, materials and labour do not move in a single quarter just because prices went up. Operating margin therefore jumped 13 percentage points, from 58.4% to 71.5% — this is operating leverage, and the same multiple works on the way down.

One detail Buffett would note: gross margin of 79% against an operating margin of 71.5% leaves only 7.5 percentage points of expenses in between. R&D, sales and G&A combined account for just 7.5%, which tells you this is a "produce it and sell it" business — no channel costs, no brand spending. The upside is extreme elasticity; the downside is that a 30% price decline would wipe out more than 70% of operating profit.

Net profit exceeds operating profit; we break down the discrepancy below

2026Q1 net profit came in at 40.35 trillion, 2.7 trillion above operating profit. The driver is not a tax benefit but the revaluation of equity investments: SK hynix holds an indirect stake in Kioxia through a consortium, and Kioxia's market capitalisation rose after listing, which accounting rules require to be recognised as a change in fair value.

That is genuine asset appreciation, but it was not earned by the chip business and generates no cash flow. Use operating profit to judge the business; use net profit to judge value per share. The two measures differ by 7% in the same report, and mixing them would overstate profitability.

Balance sheet and cash flow: this time it is real money

The balance sheet swung from net debt to net cash in 2025; free cash flow in Q1 2026 alone was KRW 18.67 trillion, and the July ADR offering raised a further USD 26.5 billion (about KRW 37 trillion). Net cash is roughly KRW 95 trillion, or 7% of market cap. That is the biggest difference versus the 2018 cycle: the prior peak came with net debt, this one comes with net cash, so downside resilience is far stronger.

The composition of that cash flow warrants scrutiny, however. The KRW 18.67 trillion was generated on capex of only about KRW 10 trillion; with full-year spending running at KRW 40 trillion, free cash flow will narrow markedly over the next three quarters. Management's decision to raise equity at this point suggests they know next year's cash flow will not look this good.

Conclusion

Financial verdict: real profit, real cash, a healthy balance sheet — the cleanest memory earnings report outside Samsung. But owner earnings of roughly KRW 25 trillion a quarter (our rough calculation) annualise to KRW 100 trillion, and the current market cap of KRW 1,291 trillion is 13x that — not expensive, and certainly not as cheap as "5x P/E." That is why Buffett would not buy at this price.
