Industry Research (Munger's Lens) — SK hynix

The pricing power of the three oligopolists is being diluted by their own capex

Core view

DRAM is an oligopoly of three plus one chaser; HBM is an oligopoly of three. Pricing power sits with the seller, true. But with industry capex up 40% in 2026 and set to rise another 74% in 2027, the monopolists are dismantling their own moat.

Industry structure: better than the last cycle, but not so much better that we can forget the cycle

Only four DRAM players remain globally: Samsung at about 38%, SK hynix at about 30%, Micron at about 22% and ChangXin at about 8% (2026Q1 revenue basis, Counterpoint estimates). NAND has six players, a notch lower in concentration. HBM has only three, the tightest link in the entire chain.

Versus the 2018 cycle, the structure has genuinely improved: no new entrants (a fab costs USD 20 billion and up), no second-tier disruptors (Elpida, ProMOS and that cohort have been cleared out), and one extra AI customer on the demand side that does not count cost. Munger would concede the point, then add: a monopolist's self-discipline never survives the test of profit.

Supply: everyone expands capacity in the same year

Industry capex rises 40% year on year in 2026, and JPMorgan expects a further 74% increase to USD 144 billion in 2027. SK hynix alone plans KRW 38–40 trillion (about USD 28 billion), Samsung is adding HBM and advanced-node capacity, Micron is building fabs in New York and Idaho, and ChangXin is expanding from 320,000 wafers per month to 420,000.

Capex is not capacity — equipment lead times run 12–18 months and cleanrooms 18 months, so money spent in 2026 becomes supply in the second half of 2027 and in 2028. JPMorgan calculates that actual bit supply will grow only 13% in 2027, below demand growth, and this is the core argument for "2027 will still be tight."

But this calculation rests on one premise: everyone's expansion goes smoothly. Historically, memory oversupply has never come from weak demand; it has come from supply running slightly ahead of plan. 13% is a model, not a fact.

Cycle position: sixth quarter, prices still rising, growth decelerating

This cycle began in late 2024; 2026Q2 is its sixth quarter. The pattern: prices are still rising (Q2 commodity DRAM +58%–63%), but the pace of gains is narrowing (Q1 included a one-off +100% at Samsung); company margins are still hitting new highs, but sequential growth is slowing (Q1 operating margin 71.5%, Q2 broker estimate 65%).

This is the classic third stage of a cycle — the "capacity expansion race." Stage one was inventory destocking (H1 2024), stage two was the price surge (2025), stage three is everyone announcing capacity expansion in the same year (2026), and stage four is supply landing and prices turning down (H2 2027 to 2028). Stocks typically peak in the middle of stage three, because the market starts trading stage four. The KRW 2.919 million price on June 22 is very likely that point.

Conclusion

Industry view: the structure is better than the last cycle, but the cycle position is later. Supply stays tight in the first half of 2027 with high probability; the second half depends on how fast Samsung's HBM4 and ChangXin's general-purpose DRAM ramp. When assigning valuation, do not assume "this time is different" — only assume "this time is the same, but the magnitude is smaller."
