Risk Assessment (Li Lu's perspective) — SK hynix

Structural demand is real; so are structural losses

Core view

The risk lies not in the company but in the price and the timing. Three death paths, two external variables, one layer of governance shadow — assign a probability to each.

Rule one: prices turn down — the only risk that can kill it

Every other risk is a scratch; this one is fatal. SK hynix's 71.5% operating margin means a 30% price decline cuts operating profit by 70%; a 40% decline takes operating profit close to zero. 2022–2023 already staged this once: revenue fell from KRW 44 trillion to 32 trillion, and operating profit went from KRW 6.8 trillion to -7.7 trillion.

Current price levels are a historical extreme. Commodity DRAM contract prices rose more than 130% in a year (Samsung Q1 +100%, Q2 +30%); further gains from this base are hard, and declines are fast. The trigger signal is clear: contract prices falling quarter on quarter for two consecutive quarters. Historically, once this signal appears, the stock has 30%–40% more downside, because EPS and multiples contract at the same time.

Article 2: The HBM lease expires

HBM now contributes the company's most profitable revenue, but its pricing power rests on being "the best of the three." Samsung holds 28% share in 2026 (20% a year earlier), Micron has secured the second-supplier slot with North American customers, and on June 8 all three passed HBM4 certification.

If HBM4E share in 2027 becomes SK hynix 45%, Samsung 35%, Micron 20%, and the HBM premium falls from 3x to 2x, the company's operating margin would drop from 71.5% back to 45%–50%. This is not a collapse; it is a reversion. The valuation the market is assigning now already partly reflects this reversion—which is exactly why it trades at 17x TTM rather than 30x.

Article 3: The AI money is borrowed

This is the most easily overlooked point. So far in 2026, six companies—Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle, Nvidia and SpaceX—have issued about USD 244 billion of debt globally, versus just USD 108 billion for all of last year. The cash-flow returns on AI infrastructure remain unproven, yet financing costs have already risen.

Storage demand is highly elastic to AI capex: if cloud providers cut server procurement by 20%, DRAM demand falls by more than 10%, while supply is rigid (fabs cannot stop). Once leveraged financing stops, order cuts are immediate while prices lag, and that time gap is the window for a stock-price plunge.

Two external variables and a shadow

External variable one: CXMT. In 2026Q1 it already accounted for 8% of global DRAM revenue, with bit growth of 48%, 1.6 times SK hynix's 30%. It makes commodity DRAM and does not touch HBM, but if commodity DRAM collapses, HBM's premium cannot hold either.

External variable two: geopolitics. The Wuxi fab makes DRAM and the Dalian fab makes NAND, and equipment upgrades are constrained by US export controls. The impact is chronic and will not change the landscape overnight, but it will limit its cost advantage in China capacity.

Shadow: Chey Tae-won's divorce asset-split case has been remanded for retrial several times, and the amount involved is enough to affect SK Group's control over SK Square. This is a low-probability, high-impact event that can only be guarded against through position sizing, not priced into valuation.

Conclusion

Risk assessment: this is a company that will not die, but a stock that will swing wildly. Of the three main risks, the probability of the cycle peaking is 30%, and if it happens the decline would exceed 40%. Position sizing must therefore be designed for "I could lose half," not for "it's already down 38%, so it's cheap." Li Lu's conclusion: worth owning, not worth owning ahead of earnings.
