Management — SK hynix

Core thesis

Management has made the right calls through this cycle: it held the line on HBM when the industry was at its worst in 2023, won first-mover position on HBM3E in 2024, and pulled the Yongin fab 1 cleanroom start forward from May to February in 2026. On capital allocation, it approved KRW 12.2 trillion of buyback and cancellation in February and raised the dividend 25% — rare among Korean chaebol-affiliated companies.

Two marks against it: on 10 July it raised USD 26.5 billion through an ADR offering near the stock's all-time high, diluting shareholders by about 2.5%; and the group-level control issue (the chairman's divorce case) sits only one layer of SK Square away from the listed company's operations.

Key people: a technically trained CEO and a hands-off major shareholder

When Kwak Noh-jung took over in 2022, SK hynix was only just emerging from the 2022 downturn, and HBM still had "the technology but not the capacity." Three moves built today's fundamentals: raising HBM's capex priority above general-purpose DRAM, lifting MR-MUF packaging yields to mass-production levels, and shifting customer relationships from "supply" to "co-definition." None of the three is sexy, but each required sign-off when the income statement looked its worst.

SK Square holds 20.5%—a control channel rather than an operator. The structure has an upside: the group does not interfere in fab decisions, giving professional managers room. It also has a downside: capital needs at the group level (such as SK Group's AI data center investments) will ultimately flow through to the listed company in some form—the July ADR offering being one example.

Capital allocation: three big bets, two wins

The first was the USD 9 billion acquisition of Intel's NAND business in 2021. At the time, the market widely saw it as overpaying — NAND was the weakest segment of the cycle, and Solidigm lost money for three straight years. In hindsight, the deal bought enterprise SSD customer relationships and QLC technology, which became an asset in 2026 when AI servers needed high-capacity storage.But it also shows management is willing to pay a premium for "strategic position" — both a strength and a risk.

The second was the contrarian bet on HBM in 2023, which paid off and was the single most critical step of the whole cycle. The third is the two-track move in 2026: approving KRW 12.2 trillion in buybacks and cancellation on one hand, while raising USD 26.5 billion at the July peak on the other. Financially the two are not contradictory (one boosts per-share value, the other replenishes expansion funding), but the signals conflict — the buyback says "the stock is cheap," while the offering says "the stock is high enough, a good time to raise capital."

Integrity and governance: clean record, though a group-level shadow persists

At the listed-company level the picture is clean: no financial fraud, no material related-party transaction disputes, no regulatory penalties on record. Disclosure cadence is disciplined too — the volume and price guidance given at quarterly calls (DRAM shipments flat, ASP up 60%) held up against actuals.

The shadow sits at group level. Chey Tae-won's divorce asset-division case ran from 2024 into 2026, went through repeated remands, and involves an amount large enough to affect SK Group's control over SK Square. None of this changes SK hynix's fabs or customers, but in an extreme scenario it could affect the stability of the ownership structure — a layer of non-operating risk anyone holding the stock must accept.

One further governance detail: opting to cancel treasury shares rather than hold them is a plus among Korean companies. Many chaebol-affiliated firms use treasury shares as a voting-right tool; SK hynix has not.

Succession: no public arrangement; neutral

Kwak Noh-jung took office in 2022 on a three-year term under corporate governance rules; whether he will be reappointed in 2026 is not public. None of Chey Tae-won's three children sits on SK hynix's board, and the group's succession plan has not been disclosed.

For investment judgment this is neutral: SK hynix's core assets are its production lines, process technology and customer qualifications, not any one person's charisma. A CEO change affects the pace of capex, but it will not make HBM4 yields disappear. What matters is not who succeeds, but the capex preferences of the person who does—whether they continue to pull the schedule forward or revert to waiting for the industry to recover before investing.

Incentive structure: why Korean executives do not rely on options

SK hynix's executive compensation is structured nothing like Silicon Valley's: base salary dominates, bonuses are tied to operating profit and capped, and there are no large option packages. Two consequences follow.

The upside: management won't take short-term actions to pump the share price, nor buy back stock to prop up the market for option exercise. The downside: the correlation between their personal payoff and shareholder returns is weak. Getting the HBM decision right may earn Kwak Noh-jung a bonus of just a few times his annual salary, not billions of won.

This also explains why capital allocation at large Korean companies is often "right on the industry, mediocre for shareholders": incentives are tied not to the share price but to scale and status. SK hynix's cancellation-style buyback in February 2026 is an exception within this incentive system, and worth noting.

Conclusion

Management score 7/10: strategic foresight (the 2023 bet on HBM) and shareholder-return awareness (share-cancellation buybacks) both rank ahead of the industry; marks deducted for equity issuance at the peak and the shadow of group governance. Our advice to holders: treat "capex discipline" as a tracking item, not an assumption—if capex steps up again in 2027 without long-term agreement coverage keeping pace, that is the signal of management sliding from "discipline" to "going with the flow".
