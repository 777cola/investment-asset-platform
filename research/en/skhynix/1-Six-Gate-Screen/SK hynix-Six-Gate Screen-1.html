Six-Gate Screen — SK hynix

Core view

Four of six gates passed, 36/60. SK hynix has just posted the strongest quarterly report in its history: 2026Q1 revenue of KRW 52.58 trillion, operating profit of KRW 37.61 trillion and an operating margin of 71.5%, with single-quarter profit 1.6x full-year 2024. Yet the stock has fallen from KRW 2.919 million on June 22 to KRW 1.816 million, a 37.8% drawdown in five weeks.

The first four gates passed cleanly: the business is explainable in three sentences, 12-layer HBM4 is already in mass production with yields on par with the prior generation, and management did not stop HBM investment through the industry's worst year in 2023. The fifth gate is where it stalls—the denominator of the 17.2x TTM is peak profit at a 71.5% operating margin. On 2027 neutral profit of KRW 180 trillion, the current price implies 7x operating profit, leaving only about 10% margin of safety. The sixth gate is more direct: Q2 results land on July 29, so building a position now is a bet on a report that has not yet been printed.

Conclusion: good company, reasonable price, wrong timing. Revisit position sizing below KRW 1.65 million.

Gate one: the business is understandable, the inflection point is not

Score: 7. The SK hynix model in three sentences: buy equipment and build cleanrooms, turn wafers into DRAM chips, sell them at contract prices to server makers and GPU makers. 2026Q1 revenue was KRW 52.58 trillion, up 60% QoQ, yet DRAM shipments were flat QoQ — the entire incremental KRW 20 trillion of revenue was price. The chain is short enough that you do not need to call the endgame of AI; you only need to call who is adding capacity over the next four quarters.

The 3 points deducted are for the second layer: the inflection. CEO Kwak Noh-jung said on July 10 that 2027 will be the tightest supply year on record and that demand may still exceed supply after 2030 — yet the stock fell 38% over the same period. Both judgments cannot be right. Understanding the business is not the same as understanding when the market will believe whom.

Stage two: only six quarters of evidence for a good business

A marginal pass at 6 points. The bull case is real: global DRAM has consolidated to just Samsung, SK hynix, Micron and CXMT, and in Q1 2026 the top three took roughly 90% of industry revenue; HBM is even tighter, with only three players able to manufacture it, leaving pricing power entirely with the seller. Operating margin climbed from 42% in Q1 2025 to 71.5%, with gross margin at 79%.

The bear case is equally real: memory has never strung together three consecutive years of high gross margin. Operating profit of 6.8 trillion in 2022 swung to an operating loss of 7.7 trillion in 2023—a 14 trillion reversal in a single year. The cash this business earns has to go straight back into capacity: 2026 capex is 38–40 trillion, 1.3x the 30.2 trillion of 2025. What the company generates is not free cash flow; it is the entry fee for the next round.

Gate three: the moat is real, but only process remains

Score 8, the highest in the entire company. HBM is not about shrinking DRAM; it is about stacking DRAM twelve layers high, connecting the layers with TSVs and tuning power consumption jointly with the GPU. SK hynix's twelve-layer HBM4 entered mass production in February–March this year with yields close to those of HBM3E, and Korean media estimate its twelve-layer wafer cost is about 12% below Samsung's — a genuine cost and engineering advantage.

Three legs of the story are thinning, however. First, exclusivity: at Seoul on June 8, Jensen Huang confirmed that Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have all passed HBM4 certification, and the premium from "only SK hynix can make it" has disappeared. Second, share: Counterpoint estimates SK hynix will hold 54% of HBM4 in 2026 and Samsung 28%, a slight decline from 56% in 2025. Third, customers: Nvidia alone accounts for more than half of HBM demand. The moat can defend share, but it cannot defend a 71.5% margin.

Stage four: management got the first bet right; now they are making a second

7 points. When the whole industry cut capex in 2023, SK hynix kept converting HBM lines and investing in MR-MUF packaging; in 2024 it was first to supply HBM3E. That was the most valuable decision of this cycle. In February this year it approved a KRW 12.2 trillion buyback and cancellation (2.1% of share capital) and raised dividend per share by 25% — among Korean listed companies, cancelling treasury shares is a rare move.

The deduction is on timing: on July 10 the ADR listed on Nasdaq at USD 149/ADR, raising USD 26.5 billion, the second-largest IPO in US history. Raising capital is not wrong in itself, but near an all-time high in the share price while approving KRW 40 trillion of capex is the standard combination of moves at a cycle top. The company calls it "expansion grounded in capex discipline" — whether that discipline holds through 2027 is the test.

Gate Five: the 17x scam sits in the denominator

5 points, a bare pass. Market cap of KRW 1,291 trillion divided by trailing four-quarter net profit of KRW 75.2 trillion gives 17.2x. On 2026 expectations (operating profit of about KRW 230 trillion) it is only a bit over 5x, and on P/E alone anyone would call it cheap.

But a low P/E on memory stocks is always a trap: the denominator is peak profit. Switch to a neutral 2027 scenario of KRW 180 trillion operating profit, a 6x multiple and KRW 95 trillion net cash, and you get a market cap of KRW 1,175 trillion, or KRW 1.65 million per share, 9% below the current price. In other words, even after a 38% decline, the neutral scenario still offers no margin of safety. The real safety zone is below KRW 1.4 million, which corresponds to 5x 2027.

Stage Six: the print two days out is not a probability, it is a bet

3 points, fail. Q2 results on July 29. KIS cut its forecast on July 13 to revenue of KRW 80.9 trillion and operating profit of KRW 60.4 trillion (65% operating margin), below the consensus of KRW 65 trillion; the rationale being a high HBM mix and long-term contracts locking in prices, so it captures less of the price-hike windfall than peers. The report knocked the stock down 15.4% that day, the largest single-day drop since listing.

The disagreement is clear: 60.4 or 65. A KRW 4.6 trillion gap equals 3.5% of market cap. Wait two days and the cost is zero; don't wait and you are betting on whether others have done the math right. That is the whole of discipline.

Veto items and items to be verified

Veto items: none. No risk of permanent capital loss: net cash, positive free cash flow, share leadership, and a customer base of Nvidia and the four major cloud providers mean there is no existential threat. The real risk is valuation compression, not the company disappearing.

Four items to verify:

  • Whether Q2 blended DRAM ASP holds at +30% QoQ (TrendForce estimates commodity DRAM contract prices at +58%–63%; with HBM long-term agreements a high share of the mix, the company's blended ASP will be markedly below that);
  • Management's framing for 2027: still "tightest in history," or starting to flag "supply and demand moving toward balance";
  • Samsung's actual HBM4 supply share — July market talk had it winning roughly 20% of Nvidia's initial allocation; if that rises above 30% by year-end, the landscape changes;
  • The final 2026 capex figure and 2027 guidance; brokers estimate 38–40 trillion this year.

Conclusion

Six checks passed 36/60; rating "Watch, await the July 29 earnings release." The company's quality needs no further validation; what needs validating is how much bad news is already priced in. The current price implies 7x 2027 neutral earnings, which is neither expensive nor cheap. Two preconditions for buying: second-quarter operating profit of no less than KRW 60 trillion, with management maintaining its 2027 tight-supply guidance; and the price falling back below KRW 1.65 million. If either condition is missing, keep waiting.
