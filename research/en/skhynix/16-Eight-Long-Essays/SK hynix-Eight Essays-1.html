Core view

Most people still see memory as a cyclical stock, a bad business, and poor Korean corporate governance. All three held true in 2018; in 2026 only half of that holds. This piece tears up the old map first.

Old impression one: memory is purely cyclical, profitable one year and loss-making the next

The sample behind that statement is 2000–2020. Over those two decades, the DRAM makers consolidated from a dozen-plus players to three, and every cycle ran the same script: price hikes, capacity expansion, collapse, shakeout. In the 2018 cycle, SK hynix posted operating profit of KRW 20.8 trillion; the following year it fell to KRW 2.7 trillion, and in 2023 the company lost KRW 7.7 trillion. The volatility is real.

But by 2026 the structure has changed in three ways. First, only three players remain plus CXMT, and a new entrant needs USD 20 billion just to start, plus an EUV allocation. Second, demand now includes a customer that does not count cost (AI), and its price sensitivity is far lower than that of phone makers. Third, HBM has turned part of the product line from "standard parts" into "custom parts," with prices set by long-term contracts rather than spot.

These three things do not abolish the cycle; they change its shape: the bottom will not be as deep, the top will not last as long, but the inflection points will still come. View it through a "pure cycle" framework and you miss 2024–2026; view it through a "growth stock" framework and you get trapped in 2027.

Old impression #2: Korean corporate governance is poor and shareholders do not get paid

The sample for this statement is the South Korean chaebols of 2015–2020. The typical playbook then: expand capacity with profits, cross-shareholdings, treasury shares left uncancelled, token dividends.

What SK hynix did in February 2026 runs counter to that image: it approved a KRW 12.2 trillion buyback, explicitly cancelling the shares (2.1% of share capital), and raised the per-share dividend by 25%. Cancelling treasury shares is a rare move in Korea, because treasury shares are often used as a voting-right tool; cancellation means giving up that tool.

That does not make it a US-style shareholder-friendly company. In July it raised USD 26.5 billion in new shares at a high, diluting 2.5%. Cancelling with one hand, issuing with the other — that is the real state of Korea's large companies today: better than five years ago, but still far from "shareholder primacy."

Old impression 3: HBM is hype and a small market

In 2023, HBM accounted for less than 5% of the DRAM market — calling it small was correct. By 2026, HBM makes up around 40% of SK hynix's DRAM revenue, carries a unit price 3x that of commodity DRAM, and customers are signing two-year long-term agreements in advance to lock in volume.

It is no longer "a niche high-end category" but a physical component of AI compute — however fast the GPU, it is scrap metal without data fed into it. On Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, HBM costs account for 15%–20% of the full-system BOM (rough estimate).

But "not hype" does not equal "permanently profitable." HBM's current premium rests on a temporary state: "I'm the best of the three." On June 8, 2026, all three passed HBM4 certification — the countdown on that state has begun.

New map: three numbers, one timeline

The new map needs only three numbers: 71.5% operating margin (cycle position), 54% HBM share (competitive position), KRW 38–40 trillion capex (next supply cycle). The first tells you how good things are now, the second tells you why they are this good, the third tells you when they turn worse.

On the timeline, 2026 is a year of "profit and share price divergence": Q1 profit hit a record high, while the stock fell from KRW 2.919 million on June 22 to KRW 1.816 million on July 27. The divergence is not because the market cannot read the financials; it is because the market is pricing 2027.

That is the core of the cognitive reset: memory stocks' share prices are never a function of current profit, but of the "next supply cycle". Only by grasping this can one understand why a 71.5% margin and a 37.8% drawdown can coexist.

Conclusion

Tear up three old maps: this is not a pure cyclical (the structure has improved, but the cycle has not disappeared); it is not governance garbage (governance is improving, but not yet where it should be); HBM is not hype (it is a bottleneck, but the premium has a lease period). The new map is three numbers plus one timeline, and the remaining seven pieces all elaborate on these three things.
