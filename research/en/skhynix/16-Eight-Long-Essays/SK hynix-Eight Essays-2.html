Core view

Break SK hynix down into a single production line: how much to invest, what each wafer sells for, what it costs, and how much depreciation. Once that math is done, the 71.5% operating margin is no longer mysterious, and no longer reliable.

The physical form of this business: one plant, five-year depreciation, 18 months to build

An advanced DRAM fab costs KRW 8–10 trillion to build, takes 18–24 months from piling to mass production, and depreciates its equipment over 5 years. These three numbers determine everything about the business: huge investment (barrier to entry), long build time (lagging supply), fast depreciation (rigid costs).

Fast depreciation is the key: a fab fully depreciates in five years, meaning it must be reinvested every five years. So this business forever swings between "just finished depreciating, best margins" and "just started depreciating, worst margins." In 2026, most of SK hynix's production lines were built in 2021–2022; depreciation is already four years in and costs are spread very thin—this is the other half of the explanation for the 71.5% operating margin, not just price increases.

Where the leverage comes from: prices rise 30%, profit rises 100%

Using the table above: revenue per die rises from USD 24,000 to USD 39,000 (+62%), cost per die barely moves (USD 9,000), and gross profit per die climbs from USD 15,000 to USD 30,000 (+100%). Deduct fixed depreciation and expenses, and operating profit growth reaches 400%.

That is why 2026Q1 revenue rose 198% year on year and operating profit 405%. It is not selling more units; it is earning more per die. The reverse works the same way: a 30% price decline cuts gross profit per die from USD 30,000 to USD 18,000 (-40%), and after fixed depreciation operating profit may halve.

So judging this business requires no forecast of shipments (capacity-constrained and largely calculable) — only a forecast of price. Price is set by supply and demand, supply and demand by capex, and capex by whether the business is making money now. This is a self-reinforcing loop, and the engine of the cycle.

Customer structure: five customers account for 70% of revenue

SK hynix's customer base is highly concentrated: Nvidia (its largest HBM buyer), AMD, Broadcom (ASIC), the four major cloud providers (via module makers), and Apple and Samsung Mobile (LPDDR). Roughly, the top five customers account for 60%–70% of revenue.

The upside of concentration: high unit prices, high qualification barriers, high order visibility (long-term agreements). The downside: any customer that shifts its roadmap forces the production line to change with it. HBM lines are custom-built for specific GPUs; when a customer switches solutions, the line cannot be repurposed.

This is also why it would rather earn a little less on commodity DRAM and prioritize capacity for HBM and server modules of 128GB and above—it is betting that "locking in major customers" is worth more than "chasing spot-price highs". That bet came under market scrutiny in July 2026: commodity DRAM rose 58%–63%, while its blended ASP rose only 30%.

The boundaries of the business: why it cannot price like TSMC

TSMC's gross margin is 58%, SK hynix's 79% (a cycle peak) — on the face of it, SK hynix is more profitable. But TSMC's 58% is stable; SK hynix's 79% is temporary. The difference lies in substitutability: only TSMC can make 3nm, while all three DRAM makers can make commodity DRAM.

HBM moves SK hynix one step closer to TSMC's model: customization, joint development, qualification barriers. But it has not gone all the way — TSMC's customers cannot switch suppliers; SK hynix's customers can add a second source. That is what "half a step" means: it has won a premium, not pricing power.

Conclusion

Business essence: a manufacturing business with "massive investment, rigid depreciation, exogenous pricing." Its profit elasticity comes from price, not volume, so it is forever cyclical. HBM moved it one step toward custom components, but not all the way—it gained a premium, not pricing power. This alone dictates that it can only be valued as a cyclical stock.
