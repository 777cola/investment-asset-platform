Core view

Of the six risks, five are scrapes; one is fatal: a turn to falling prices. Every other risk ultimately transmits to profit through price.

The fatal one: price

SK hynix's operating margin is 71.5%; of its cost base, 79% is gross profit and 7.5% is expenses—leaving almost no buffer. A 30% price decline halves operating profit; a 40% decline brings it close to zero. This played out once in 2022–2023: revenue fell from KRW 44 trillion to KRW 32 trillion, and operating profit swung from KRW 6.8 trillion to negative KRW 7.7 trillion.

This risk is lethal because it is not independent of the other five: a downward revision in AI capex pushes prices down, HBM share loss pushes prices down, and ChangXin's capacity expansion also pushes prices down. Every risk ultimately flows into the same river: price.

The one thing that is certain to happen: depreciation

Yongin Phase 1 comes online in 2027 and M15X reaches full production by end-2026, adding KRW 5–8 trillion a year in depreciation from 2028 (rough estimate). That means even with prices flat and volumes still rising, the 2028 operating margin drops 3–5 percentage points.

This is the one risk with a 90% probability of happening, and the one the market most easily overlooks—because it is not in the quarterly report, it is in the capex notes. Buying cyclicals requires two calculations: whether prices will fall, and whether costs will rise. The second calculation is already underway.

The most easily overestimated point: geopolitics

Wuxi makes DRAM and Dalian makes NAND, and equipment upgrades are constrained by US export controls. This risk sounds alarming but has limited real impact: China capacity mainly serves the China market and does not participate in the most advanced nodes; even if restricted, output can be reallocated from the company's Korean fabs.

The real geopolitical risk is on the demand side: if US-China tech decoupling intensifies, Chinese customers (phone makers, cloud providers) will prioritize purchasing from ChangXin and Yangtze Memory. This trend has already emerged in 2026, but is moving slowly.

The hardest risk to guard against: leverage and sentiment

SK hynix fell 15.4% on July 13 alone, KOSPI dropped 8.95%, and the CSOP 2x Long SK hynix ETF fell more than 30%. This is not fundamentals; it is leverage being flushed out. The scale of leveraged products in the Korean market hit a record in 2026, and once a decline triggers forced liquidation, the drop is amplified.

For long-term investors, this is an opportunity rather than a risk—provided you carry no leverage and can withstand a 40% drawdown.Buying cyclicals with leverage is a bet on timing; buying without leverage is a bet on direction. The former is a guaranteed loss; the latter still has odds of winning.

Supplementary to Article 7: if the risks occur simultaneously

The six risks above are calculated separately, but historically they have often occurred together: prices turn down → cloud providers cut orders → brokers downgrade → leveraged positions are liquidated → the stock overshoots to the downside. In the 2022 cycle, four of these happened in succession within two months, and the stock fell 60% from its high.

So a stress test cannot look at each item alone; it must look at the combination: prices down 30% + share down 5 points + depreciation stepping up. With all three together, 2028 operating profit could be only 100 trillion, corresponding to a market cap of 500 trillion and a share price of 70.0 — 61% below the current level.

The probability of this scenario is not high (around 15%), but it is the benchmark for position sizing. A position that can withstand this scenario is the appropriate position.

Conclusion

Risk conclusion: six risks — one fatal (price), one certain (depreciation), four manageable. The response is not forecasting but position sizing: size positions on the assumption of a possible 50% loss, and hard-code falsification signals into contract prices and share. This is the only way to survive a cyclical stock.
