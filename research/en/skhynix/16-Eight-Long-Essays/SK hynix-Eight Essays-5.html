Core view

The DRAM oligopoly has never been more stable: three players hold 90% share, a new entrant needs USD 20 billion, and equipment is quota-rationed. Yet oligopoly discipline is weakest when money is easiest to make — and in 2026 everyone is adding capacity.

The three pillars of an oligopoly

The first pillar is capital: a single advanced DRAM fab costs KRW 8–10 trillion, and once R&D and certification are included, a new entrant must raise USD 20 billion and secure an EUV allocation from ASML. The second is technology: the 1c process, 12-layer stacking and TSV each represent a decade of accumulated expertise. The third is customers: Nvidia certification takes a year and a half.

These three pillars confine the industry to three majors plus ChangXin. That is the fundamental reason memory operating margins can reach 71.5% in 2026 — not because demand is strong, but because supply cannot keep up.

An oligopoly, however, is not a cartel. The three have different agendas: SK hynix must defend its HBM share, Samsung wants to win it back, and Micron wants to hold its position as the second North American supplier. So-called "tacit understanding" means shared gains during upcycles and a scramble for survival during downcycles.

The 2027 supply ledger: three dates, one conclusion

Three checkpoints frame the supply picture. First, by end-2026 M15X reaches full production, lifting SK hynix's 1b capacity to a new level. Second, in February 2027 Yongin Phase 1 cleanroom comes online with equipment move-in, the first phase at 60,000 wafers/month. Third, in 2027 Samsung's HBM4 capacity doubles and CXMT's 420,000 wafers/month comes on stream.

All three supply streams point to the second half of 2027. JPMorgan estimates 2027 bit supply grows only 13%, below demand — the basis for the "2027 still tight" thesis. That estimate, however, assumes "smooth capacity expansion," whereas memory oversupply has historically always come in "a little more than planned." Conclusion: tight in the first half of 2027, watch Samsung in the second half, most likely loose in 2028.

The real battleground is certification and yield, not price

HBM competition is fought not on the quote sheet but in the customer's validation lab. Higher yield means lower cost; first certification wins the first allocation. Three facts define 2026: SK hynix is first to mass-produce 12-layer, Samsung has the fastest validation progress, and Micron has secured the second-supplier slot in North America.

No price war has broken out in HBM yet because supply falls short of demand. Once the two balance, the price war will take the form of customers squeezing the second supplier's price to force the first supplier to follow. SK hynix's 3x premium will compress to 2x within two years — an industry pattern, not a company misstep.

NAND: the overlooked other half

NAND is 20% of SK hynix revenue, with prices up more than 70% in Q1 2026 — a shortage-driven move, not a structural one. Five players compete in NAND (Samsung, Kioxia, SK hynix plus Solidigm, Micron, YMTC), products are homogeneous, and YMTC is closing the gap with its 294-layer product.

SK hynix's NAND secures its enterprise SSD position through Solidigm, and the value of this business lies in attach sales — cloud providers buy SSDs alongside HBM. It will not be the main profit driver, nor the core of the valuation. To look at SK hynix, look at DRAM and HBM; NAND is a supporting player.

An overlooked variable: the KRW exchange rate

SK hynix books revenue in USD (memory contract prices are negotiated in USD) but costs in KRW (labour, electricity, some materials). A 10% depreciation of the KRW converts the same USD revenue into 10% more KRW while costs stay flat, passively lifting operating margin by 3–5pp.

The KRW was weak in 1H26, an invisible tailwind in the financials. Should the KRW appreciate in 2027, KRW-denominated profit will shrink even if USD prices hold. This variable does not change the direction of the cycle, but it makes quarterly numbers harder to forecast.

Conclusion

Industry conclusion: the oligopoly structure holds, capacity additions cluster in the second half of 2027, and competition centers on certification and yield rather than pricing. The first half of 2027 is the last certain window of this upcycle; the second half marks the start of the verification period. This timetable is the tightest stretch of the "three-year lease."
