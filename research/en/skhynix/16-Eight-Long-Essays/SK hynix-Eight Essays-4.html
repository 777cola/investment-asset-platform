Core view

2026Q1 net profit was RMB 40.35 trillion, higher than operating profit of RMB 37.61 trillion. The extra RMB 2.7 trillion did not come from chips; it came from revaluation of equity investments. This piece breaks down the income statement until it is fully legible.

Layer one: the profit is real, but the structure needs scrutiny

Gross margin 79%, operating margin 71.5%, net margin 76.7%. The three figures step up cleanly, with almost no leakage in between — this is a "sell what you produce" business, with no channel costs, no brand spending, no receivables game.

But the fact that net margin exceeds operating margin needs unpacking: of the RMB 40.35 trillion in net profit, about RMB 2.7 trillion comes from revaluation of equity investments (an indirect stake in Kioxia held through a consortium), plus the effect of taxes. This portion generates no cash flow and cannot be used to judge the chip business. Use operating profit to judge the business, net profit to judge per-share value; the two measures must not be mixed.

Second layer: cash flow is real, but it will thin out in the second half

Single-quarter free cash flow was RMB 1.867 trillion, up 582% year on year. Capex this quarter was only about RMB 1 trillion; on a full-year pace of RMB 3.8–4.0 trillion, the remaining three quarters need RMB 1.0–1.1 trillion each, which would compress free cash flow to around RMB 1 trillion.

There is another layer: a meaningful portion of operating cash flow is "passive working-capital appreciation from price increases" — inventory is carried at cost, so book value does not move when market prices rise; receivables grow with revenue, but payment terms have not lengthened. This cash is real but unsustainable: once prices turn down, inventory becomes a burden and receivables become risk.

Tier three: the cleanest balance sheet of the three

It swung from net debt to net cash in 2025; net cash stood at roughly 58 trillion in Q1 2026 (rough estimate), rising to around 95 trillion after the USD 26.5 billion ADR raise in July, equivalent to 7% of market cap. Compare the 2018 peak — net debt then, so once prices turned down interest burdened the stock. This cycle's downside resilience is far stronger.

That is also what gives it the confidence to do three things at once in 2026: 40 trillion in capex, 12.2 trillion in buybacks and cancellation, and a USD 26.5 billion follow-on offering. Add all three together and the balance sheet is still in net cash.

Tier four: three line items that will only hit next year

First, depreciation. Yongin Phase 1 starts production in 2027 and M15X reaches full production by end-2026, so depreciation steps up from 2028, adding roughly 5–8 trillion per year on a rough estimate. That directly eats 3–5 percentage points of gross margin, even if prices do not fall.

Second, the tax rate. The effective tax rate in 2026Q1 was abnormally low (net profit close to operating profit); if that came from tax credits, it will normalize next year. Peak-cycle high profit plus trough-cycle tax rate is the most deceptive combination in financial statements.

Third, equity revaluation. When Kioxia's share price rises, the book gains; when it falls, the book loses. This will make net profit more volatile than operating profit, so strip it out when looking at year-on-year quarterly comparisons.

Layer five: the definitional traps behind three numbers

Three pitfalls lurk in this earnings report. First, "net profit of 40.35 trillion" includes equity revaluation and cannot be read as the profitability of the chip business. Second, "free cash flow of 18.67 trillion" is a single-quarter figure; for the full year, capex will compress it to within 100 trillion. Third, "gross margin of 79%" comes from a brokerage note and is not broken out in the company's press release, so cite the source when using it.

What the three pitfalls share: the numbers are all real, but placed in the wrong context they become misleading. Reading an earnings report is half arithmetic, half context.

Conclusion

Financial reality: real profit, real cash, a clean balance sheet — the healthiest set of financials of the three. But three line items will bite in 2027–2028: depreciation stepping up, the tax rate normalizing, and equity revaluation reversing. Financial "perfection" has a shelf life, and like the HBM premium, it is about three years.
