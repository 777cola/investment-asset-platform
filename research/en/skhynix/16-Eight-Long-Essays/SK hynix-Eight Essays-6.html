Management: a team that has won two big bets and is now making a third — SK hynix

Core view

Kwak Noh-jung took over in 2022 and made three key decisions: investing in HBM against the cycle, pulling forward the Yongin timeline, and pushing the ADR listing. The first two look like clear wins; the third is debatable.

Decision one: no HBM cuts in 2023

2023 was the worst year in memory-market history: SK hynix posted an operating loss of KRW 7.7 trillion, and the industry as a whole cut capex, laid off staff and shut production lines. SK hynix chose to "cut commodity DRAM, protect HBM" — line conversions continued, MR-MUF packaging investment continued, and joint debugging with customers continued.

At the time, that decision looked like "burning money against the cycle." In 2024, HBM3E beat rivals to supply Nvidia, and by 2025–2026 its share had reached 54%–56%. The single most valuable step in the entire cycle was this one. It also shows that management's logic is not "follow the cycle" but "buy position at the cycle bottom."

Second decision: pull the schedule forward

The Yongin Phase 1 cleanroom comes online in February 2027, three months ahead of the original May 2027 schedule, and equipment deliveries for M15X in Cheongju are being pulled forward at the company's request. The rationale is blunt: "Customer demand continues to outstrip our supply capacity, and building sufficient supply capacity is a core competitive advantage."

The decision is a plus for 2026–2027 revenue and a minus for 2028 pricing. Starting production three months early means adding supply to the market three months early. Management knows this, of course, but is betting on "win share first, talk price later" — the right bet in HBM competition; not necessarily in commodity DRAM.

Third decision: raise USD 26.5 billion at the peak

The ADR listed on Nasdaq on July 10, 2026 at an issue price of USD 149/ADR, raising USD 26.5 billion in the second-largest IPO in US stock market history. Timing was set near the stock's all-time high, having just hit a new high of KRW 2.919 million on June 22.

For the company, this was the optimal choice: raise capital when valuation is highest and money is cheapest. For shareholders, it is dilution of about 2.5% of share capital, equivalent to 2.5% EPS dilution per year. Both are true, depending on which side you stand on.

Note the sequence: the company approved KRW 12.2 trillion in buybacks and cancellation in February (2.1%), then issued new shares for 2.5% five months later. One out, one in, and share capital is barely changed, but the company has USD 26.5 billion in cash. The move is clever and telling: management's read on the share price is more accurate than most analysts'.

Governance and succession: a group-level shadow, an undisclosed arrangement

No scandal at the listed-company level: disclosure is disciplined, guidance is delivered, treasury shares are cancelled. The shadow sits at the group level. Choi Tae-won's divorce asset-split case ran from 2024 to 2026, was remanded for retrial several times, and involves an amount large enough to affect SK Group's control over SK Square (20.5% stake). This is a low-probability, high-impact risk that can only be guarded against through position sizing.

There is no public information on succession arrangements. For investment judgment this is neutral: SK hynix's core assets are its production lines, process technology and customer qualifications, not any individual's charisma. What really matters is the next CEO's capex preference — keep pulling schedules forward, or return to waiting for an industry recovery before investing.

One detail: the shareholder structure after the ADR

Following the July ADR listing, SK hynix's shareholder base has shifted from "SK Square 20.5% + National Pension Service 8.1% + foreign investors at roughly 50%" to include an additional layer of US institutional investors. This is not merely a change in funding sources but a change in governance expectations: US institutions demand more on buybacks, dividends and board independence.

Near term, the ADR has brought in passive fund buying; medium term, it may push the company to further raise shareholder returns. This is the most easily overlooked intangible asset in the USD 26.5 billion financing.

Conclusion

Management assessment: strategic vision A (the 2023 HBM bet), execution A (ahead of schedule), shareholder returns B+ (cancellation-style buybacks, offset by equity issuance at the peak), governance B (group shadow). This team deserves trust, but not the assumption of "always rational" — financing at the peak shows their read on the cycle, like the market's, was simply that "it is very expensive now."
